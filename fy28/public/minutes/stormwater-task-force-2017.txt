STORMWATER TASK FORCE (SWTF)  
MINUTES 
January 12, 2017 
 
The Stormwater Task Force (SWTF) met in the Ritter Building, first floor conference room, as 
scheduled, with Matt Allison, Phyllis Luck, and Jack Rabbitt, present. Also in attendance was 
Lunenburg Land Use Director, Adam Burney. The meeting opened at 6:32 pm.  
 
An announcement was made that the 28 
th  Annual Nonpoint Source Pollution Conference for the New 
England states and New York will be held on April 12 – 13, 2017, at the Hotel Northampton in 
Northampton, MA.  Coordinated by the New England Interstate Water Pollution Control Commission 
in partnership with member states and the EPA, the conference focuses on sharing information about 
nonpoint source pollution issues and projects in the region.  Sessions will reflect the theme: “Lesson s 
learned:  What Worked, and What Didn’t”? 
 
Phyllis Luck shared that Sarah Cammer has resigned from the SWTF, effective immediately, for 
personal reasons.  SWTF members will reach out to see if there are others interested in filling that void. 
 
Minutes of the December 8, 2016, meeting were accepted.   
 
It was noted that Jon O’Connor has offered to help with the website; he’s created one poster, too, and  
will place copies around Town for us.  When he placed the rain barrel at the Library, Library personnel 
were interested in hosting a SWTF presentation.  A discussion around if and when ensued.  There was 
also a discussion about work needed on the website. Further discussion will follow at our next meeting. 
 
Phyllis reported on the Bay State Roads workshop sh e attended on 12/14/16 “Getting the Most out of 
your snow & ice operations”.  Although no one from Lunenburg’s DPW was available to come along, 
Jack Rodriquenz sent her armed with information on Lunenburg’s current winter storm protocol, which 
was very helpful. The presenter had been the DPW di rector in Heath, in the Northwestern corner of the 
state, where they’d already received 16” of snow th is year and have elevation changes from 300 to 
2400 feet.  Presenter Mike Smith had successfully i mplemented these advanced techniques in his 
Town.  Practical suggestions include pretreating of  roadways before the storm, applying only salt 
products (liquid or rock) during the storm, and usi ng multi sectional articulating blades on plows to 
increase the amount of snow cleared. These newer te chniques result in improved safety and lower 
costs. For example, before making the switch to sal t, it took Heath’s DPW 3 years to clean all catch 
basins; it now takes under a week because there is so much less debris to remove. A salt/sand mix uses  
more salt than a salt only application. We are sett ing up a meeting with Mike Smith, Baystate Roads 
Technical Training Specialist and the workshop pres enter, to explore if any of the techniques promoted  
would be of interest to Lunenburg.  Tentative meeting date is 1/24/17. 
 
During this discussion, Adam Burney suggested that SWTF member and DPW Director Jack 
Rodriquenz make a presentation to the SWTF on Lunen burg’s current winter storm protocol.  SWTF 
members have been asked to forward specific questio ns that should be addressed in the presentation to 
the Chair, who will compile same & bring them to th e next SWTF meeting.  It is intended that this 
presentation be taped by Public Access, either live or for future distribution.

Prior to beginning the Education Workshop, the Task Force reviewed the meeting schedule for the first 
quarter of 2017. At the 1/26/17 meeting, the SWTF will meet with Design Consultants, Inc. (DCI) for

for the 2/14/17 Board of Selectmen (BOS) meeting, be available for the 1/26/17 meeting.  This 
includes those roads: 
•  paved as a part of the Pavement Management Debt Exclusion in 2016

Board & Public comment was also sought, so that focus could shift to the workshop topic. 
 
Phyllis opened the Education workshop by reporting that she’s working on a Lunenburg Public Schools 
Visioning Steering Committee, and has had the opportunity to interact with 5 
th  grade Math & Science 
teacher, Brian Chartrand, who felt this grade level would be the perfect fit for educational outreach.  
He’d like us to get in touch with him to work out d etails.  She then introduced the Enviroscape that was

show the effects of nonpoint source pollution. It may be borrowed any time for presentations; the

Jack Rabbitt is planning to reach out to Superintendent Calmes for further discussion on Educational

     STORMWATER TASK FORCE (SWTF)  
MINUTES 
January 26, 2017 
 
 
 
 
The Stormwater Task Force (SWTF) met in the Ritter Building, first floor conference room, as 
scheduled, with Phyllis Luck, Jack Rabbitt, and Jack Rodriquenz present. Also in attendance were 
Lunenburg Land Use Director, Adam Burney, as well as Ed Himlan of the Mass Watershed Coalition 
and Mike Clark of Design Consultants, Inc. (DCI).  The meeting opened at 6:30 pm.  
 
Minutes of the January 12, 2017, meeting were accepted.   
 
The SWTF welcomed our consultants, Ed Himlan and Mike Clark, to discuss their progress so far on 
work to complete Lunenburg’s MS4 (Municipal Separat e Storm Sewer System) permit.  Ed Himlan 
passed out a one page bulleted outline entitled: Stormwater Management Program (SWMP) 
Requirements Outline (due by 6/30/18), which articulated those requirements as well as dates 
compliance must be achieved, attached.  During the discussion it was mentioned that the Central Mass 
Regional Stormwater Coalition (CMRSWC) website contains much useful information and related 
forms, as well as Illicit Discharge Detection and Elimination (IDDE) tools which are available for the 
taking.  There is other proprietary information available to members of the CMRSWC; Lunenburg 
became a member in FY 17. 
 
The SWTF and consultants spent the next hour and 45 minutes reviewing these requirements. The 
Minimum Control Measures are required to be achieved over a 5 year period, with completion dates 
dictated by the MS 4 (Municipal Separate Storm Sewer System) Permit. It was clear that some items, 
such as Construction Site Stormwater Control Program requirements, due by 6/30/18, will be relatively 
inexpensive and easily achievable. However, many of the other requirements will be quite costly and 
require careful planning. Some items will need to be outsourced.  It was determined that the Board of 
Selectmen and Town Manager will need to be brought into the conversation when the MS4 Permit is 
ready to be filed, before 8/17/17,  as sources of funding will need to begin to be identified. We will 
next meet with our consultants on March 9, 2017, at 6:30 pm in the Ritter Building, upper conference 
room. 
 
DPW (Department of Public Works) Director Rodriquenz presented the pavement management 
program, including roads paves in 2016, and those to be paved in 2017 per the Debt Exclusion.  There 
was some discussion that optional roads may be included in 2017, while some roads may be excluded, 
depending on issues such as stormwater management requirements.  Director Rodriquenz will set up a 
meeting with Mike Clark of DCI to review the roads to be paved in 2017, to be able to discuss 
stormwater best management practices that may be utilized where appropriate, on these roads. 
 
It was determined that the next meeting of the SWTF will take place at the DPW facility on Chase Rd.  
Director Rodriquenz will explain what the DPW does and how its operations deal with areas of concern 
to the SWTF.  Task Force members may submit questions directly to the Director in advance of that 
meeting. 
 
===PAGE 2===
Motion to adjourn was made at 8:35 pm by Jack Rodriquenz, seconded by Jack Rabbitt, all voted in 
favor. 
 
Next meeting will be Thursday, February 9, 2017, at 6:30 pm, at the Department of Public Works 
Building on Chase Road, in the conference room.  Remaining meetings of the first quarter will be 
February 23, March 9 (with DCI Consultants) & 23.

     STORMWATER TASK FORCE (SWTF)  
MINUTES 
February 23, 2017 
 
 
The Stormwater Task Force (SWTF) met in the Ritter Building, first floor conference room, as 
scheduled, with Matt Allison, Phyllis Luck, and Jack Rabbitt present. Also in attendance was 
Lunenburg Land Use Director, Adam Burney. The meeting opened at 6:37 pm.  
 
It was announced that the Board of Selectmen have voted to apply to become a Massachusetts 
Community Compact Cabinet community by entering into the Best Practice Program.  The “best 
practices” chosen include: 
 
       Best Practice:   Implement Stormwater Management Measures so that land use regulations help 
promote infiltration, control flooding, and reduce pollution. 
       Best Practice:   Perform a Cyber Security Assessment to analyze whether there is risk of 
unauthorized access, implement or improve policies and procedures appropriate for the technology 
environment and identify steps to remediate any problems identified. 
       Best Practice:   Implement a Citizen Engagement Communication Plan that leverages technology 
to engage the public through basic electronic communication channels and ensures that internal staff is 
positioned to support these initiatives. 
 
In its submittal, the Town specified the following for Stormwater Best Practice: 
“The Town of Lunenburg has set up a Storm Water Tas k Force that, working with MA Watershed 
Coalition Director Ed Himlan, has identified a number of measures but require assistance in 
implementing these measures.  The Town is seeking assistance in the form of funding for 
implementation of a series of identified measures that will help promote “infiltration, control floodi ng, 
and reduce pollution”.  Examples of these identifie d measures that the Town requires assistance in 
implementing are “Construction Site Storm Water Con trol Program”, “Separate Storm Sewer System 
Map”, “SS Overflows inventory”, among many other me asures.  Our goal is to have a further 
discussion about what measures this Community Compact Best Practice can help us implement.” 
 
 Upon acceptance, the Town becomes eligible for grants to aid in the development of these best 
practices over the next two years. 
 
Minutes of the January 26, 2017, meeting were accepted.   
 
Phyllis reported on the Central Mass Regional Stormwater Coalition Steering Committee Meeting she 
attended on February 15. Several Communities reported that they no longer use sand/salt mixes for 
winter storm roadway treatment and have moved on to the use of salt.  Holden last used sand in the 
2011-2012 season.  When I inquired as to capital expenditures needed for this transition, the Holden 
DPW director said none.  In fact, he pointed out that since salt has less volume and weight, the roads 
are now treated in ½ the time it took to treat with a sand/salt mix.  Reducing the amount of sand used in 
Lunenburg would address Minimum Control “Good House  Keeping and Pollution Prevention” 
Measure of the MS4 Permit. 
 
===PAGE 2===
4 subcommittees have been established to improve the effectiveness of the coalition:  Education, 
Program, Technical, and Legislative.  Phyllis has joined the Education subcommittee. The Coalition is 
looking for member communities to provide additional hands for these important missions. Worcester 
Polytech (WPI) has established a Water Resource Project Center (WRPC).  Students in their junior 
year work with a group to complete a project; the WRPC now has an office in Boston, as WPI wants to 
extend their outreach statewide. 
 
Jack Rabbitt reported that he has met, for the second time, with Superintendent of Schools Calmes.  He 
will meet with her again and report at the SWTF April 13 meeting on the agreed upon specific 
educational outreach actions. 
 
There was a discussion on the overall mission of the SWTF, which is: 
“The Stormwater Task Force is charged with the deve lopment of a comprehensive stormwater 
management plan that complies with state and federal regulations, provides a storm and surface water 
system that controls damage from storms, protects surface water quality and protects the environment.    
The plan shall determine which local agency or agencies will be responsible for implementation, 
establish the estimated cost of the program, as well as provide for the creation of an ongoing funding 
source to enable its success.” 
 
During Public Comment, Reservoir Rd. resident Rhonda Lisio expressed concern over stormwater 
runoff and perceived poor “housekeeping” habits of the construction crew building new homes 
adjacent to her property on Reservoir Rd..  It was pointed out that the mission of the SWTF, as noted 
above and as discussed prior to her comments, does not deal with such specific issues. Further, it was 
suggested she take her concerns to DPW Director Rodriquenz and to the Planning Board for further 
action. 
 
Motion to adjourn was made at 7:40 pm by Matt Allison, seconded by Jack Rabbitt, all voted in favor. 
 
Next meeting will be Thursday, March 9, 2017, at 6:30 pm, at the Ritter building, upper conference 
room.  Remaining meeting of the first quarter will be March 30. Proposed meetings for the second 
quarter include April 13, 27, May 11, 25, June 8, 22.

STORMWATER TASK FORCE (SWTF) 
MINUTES 
June 8, 2017 
 
The Stormwater Task Force (SWTF) met in the Ritter Building, first floor conference room, as 
scheduled, with Matt Allison, Phyllis Luck, Jack Rabbitt, and Jack Rodriquenz present. Also in 
attendance was Lunenburg Land Use Director, Adam Burney. The meeting opened at 6:35 pm.  
 
Minutes of the May 25, 2017, meeting were accepted.   
 
There was a discussion of the revisions to the Stormwater Management Plan (SWMP) 
Minimum Control Measure (MCM) Notice of Intent (NOI) drafts 1 through 6, which the SWTF 
had labored over with Consultant Ed Himlan.  Various changes were made; Phyllis will enter 
revisions in the document.  It was discussed that there are Town entities listed as Responsible & 
Support Entity in this draft, and that there should be communication with these boards prior to 
the presentation of the NOI to the Board of Selectmen (BOS) for their review.  Phyllis offered 
to speak with the Town Manager as to how this should unfold. 
 
There was a discussion on the Board of Selectmen’s request for a review of the SWMP NOI.  
The SWTF felt it should be ready for review following the 6/22/17 meeting with Design 
Consultants, Inc. (DCI).  Phyllis will make a request of the Town Manager to put this on the 
agenda either 7/18 or 8/1/17.  Adam has agreed to create summaries of each MCM to facilitate 
this presentation. 
 
Matt Allison exited the meeting at approximately 7:30, as he had an Agricultural Committee 
meeting to attend. 
 
Heather Lemieux has informed the SWTF that the Community Compact grant contract 
paperwork has been received and filled out; it was to be returned to the State on 6/9/17.  The 
SWTF will be informed when the paperwork has been received back from the State, signed by 
them, so that the SWTF may proceed with the work outlined. 
 
There was a discussion on the Rain Barrel/Compost Bin sales policy, attached, which was 
approved by the BOS at their 6/6/17 meeting.  DPW Director Rodriquenz felt that tracking sales 
to insure that $1000 maximum is extended in reductions/give aways to Boards & Committees 
was arduous.  The SWTF approved the creation of a flyer to announce the sales & a mailing that 
could be placed in with the tax bills for $242.80. 
 
There was a discussion on what is needed to move the Illicit Discharge Detection and 
Elimination (IDDE) Zoning bylaw to the general bylaws.  It was determined that, without 
changes to this bylaw, it could be moved, as is, at the Fall Town Meeting.  Adam will work to 
make this happen. 
 
===PAGE 2===
There has been no progress on altering Central Mass Regional Stormwater Coalition forms for 
use by Lunenburg.  Phyllis offered to assist in this effort. 
 
The meeting schedule was then confirmed:   June 22 nd will be with DCI to review the NOI prior 
to meeting with the BOS, July 13, July 27 with DCI, August 24, September 14, 28.  All meetings 
will be held at 6:30 pm, in the Ritter Building, upper conference room, unless otherwise noted. 
 
Jack Rodriquenz made a motion to adjourn at 8 pm, Jack Rabbitt seconded, all voted in favor.

STORMWATER TASK FORCE (SWTF) 
MINUTES 
July 27, 2017 
 
The Stormwater Task Force (SWTF) met in the Ritter Building, first floor conference room, as 
scheduled, with Matt Allison, Phyllis Luck, Jack Rabbitt, and Jack Rodriquenz present. Also in 
attendance were Ed Himlan of the Mass Watershed Coalition (MWC), Mike Clark, of Design 
Consultants, Inc. (DCI), and Lunenburg Land Use Director, Adam Burney. The meeting opened 
at 6:35 pm.  
 
It was announced that the Municipal Separate Storm Sewer System (MS4) Permit, originally to 
commence 7 2017, will be stayed until 7 2018.  The Notice of Intent (NOI) will now be due 9 
2018. 
 
Minutes of the June 8, 2017, meeting were accepted.  
  
There was a discussion on the status of the 2003 MS4 Permit requirements, including: 
 MS4 System mapping 
The system mapping to this point has shown the existence of some sump pumps 
 Procedures for removing illicit discharges to stormwater system 
Dry weather mapping is the most accurate indicator of the existence of illicit discharges 
to the stormwater system. 
Procedures must be adopted, implemented, and documented. 
 Record keeping and reporting for 2003 permit 
DCI Consultants emphasized the importance of the creation of accurate documentation 
within a Central Data System to comply with the requirements of the current MS4 permit. 
 
There was a discussion about what the SWTF may accomplish beyond the requirements of the 
current MS4 Permit, as there is much that is worth doing to improve water quality in 
Lunenburg’s lakes and ponds that is spelled out in the new MS4 Permit which has been delayed 
one year.  It was discussed that the BOS first wants the SWTF to focus on the “must do’s” with 
the available funds.  However, should there be “low hanging fruit” that may be relatively easy 
to accomplish, provide good value, and move stormwater management in the right direction, 
those tasks will be undertaken. 
 
The final draft of the Notice of Intent (NOI), now due 9 2018, was reviewed.  A few minor 
changes were made, such as the replacement terminology of “Land Use Department” for 
“Planning Department”, as this would encompass boards other than Planning and more 
accurately reflect the responsible entities.  It was determined that, within Minimum Control 
Measure (MCM) 6, the Best Management Practice (BMP) for Operation and Maintenance 
(O&M) “Responsible Departments/Entities”  would include Town Departments in addition to 
the DPW Operations, as other Town Departments have responsibilities in these areas (for 
example Police, Fire, and School departments maintain, wash, store vehicles.  The School 
Department mows and fertilizes playing fields.  
===PAGE 2===
There was a discussion on the Board of Selectmen’s request for a review of the Stormwater 
Management Plan (SWMP) NOI.  It was determined that it would fit best to have a review with 
the BOS in October.  Phyllis will request that this be put on the agenda.  Adam has agreed to 
create summaries of each MCM to facilitate this presentation. 
 
Cost estimates for MCM’s (attached) were discussed.  It was acknowledged that these are 
estimates only, and do provide a first look at what the Town may be looking forward to in terms 
of costs of compliance. 
 
It was announced that Town Manager Lemieux has informed the SWTF that the fully executed 
contract documents for the $25,000 Community Compact Grant to implement the Stormwater 
Measures identified in the scope of work have been received.  She has advised the SWTF to 
proceed with hiring a consultant to perform the tasks identified in this scope of work.  The 
SWTF will discuss at the next meeting to be held on 8/24/17. 
 
Jack Rabbitt exited the meeting at 8:24 pm. 
 
The meeting schedule was then confirmed  for the third quarter :   August 24, September 14, 
28.  A schedule for October through December will be discussed at the next SWTF meeting.  All 
meetings will be held at 6:30 pm, in the Ritter Building, upper conference room , unless 
otherwise noted. 
 
Motion to adjourn was made by Matt Allison at 8:31 pm; it was  seconded by Jack Rodriquenz; 
all concurred.
