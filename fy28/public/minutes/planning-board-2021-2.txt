The Hearing , via Zoom video conference, was to hear and review an application under Chapter 325 Subdivisi on Regulations, 
Article III, Section 325-3.4. Definitive Plan of the Code of the Town of Lunenburg  for a six-lot subdivision consisting of six duplex 
buildings at 101 Pleasant Street (Map 095, Parcel 0003), Lunenburg, MA.  Owner/applicant was MA Pleasant LLC, P O Box 903, 
Ashburnham, MA.     
 
The applicant was represented by Trevor Fletcher, Graz Engineering.  The south side of the site was Residence A zoning while 
the north portion was Commercial.  The project would be completely contained within residenti al zoning.  The commercial portion 
would be subdivided off and attached to land on Massachusetts Avenue.  The proposed roadway would remain private with a 
HOA (Home Owners’ Association) created to maintain the road.  Each duplex would be served by an on -site septic system with 
the site being served by town water.  Utilities would be located underground.  There were extensive wetlands throughout the site, 
which affected the roadway design.  There was a wetland crossing at the entrance with water flowing unde r the gravel path via 
three stone wicks.  The roadway would be raised and lined with two retaining walls.  Water would flow under the roadway via 
three 12 inch diameter culverts.  Stormwater would be treated via catch basins, swales, sediment forebay, infi ltration basins and 
a detention basin.   
 
A review letter by Jeff Walsh, Graves Engineering, Inc.  (GEI), was issued this date.  As all involved parties had not reviewed the 
letter, GEI did not go through the letter in great detail, but did point out some i tems.  Lots 2, 5, and 6 had paved portions of the 
parking spaces and maneuvering aisle within the 25 foot parking setback.  There was only one streetlight proposed ; at the 
project’s entrance.  Drainage calculations needed to be submitted.  Sight distance e xiting the site needed to be improved.  The 
curb radius at the entrance partially blocked the driveway on the abutting property.  Retaining walls were proposed in the right-of-
way on each side of the proposed roadway; they should be moved outside the right -of-way allowing Lunenburg utilization of the 
shoulder as intended, e.g. possible future utilities, snow storage.  Drainage pipes required at least three (3) feet of cover ; the 
applicant proposed 2.3 feet.  Pre- and post - hydrology maps were not available for review.  Infiltration and detention basins 
needed to provide sufficient access for maintenance equipment.

The DPW Director’s review memorandum stressed that the DPW did not have the resources for mainte nance of the project’s 
stormwater collection system.  The O&M  (Operation and Maintenance Plan)  did not specify who would be responsible for 
maintaining the system.  A. Burney explained that an O&M puts forth the process and schedule for the maintenance, bu t would 
not necessarily identify the responsible party.  The O&M Plan needed to be updated and possibly reviewed by Town Counsel.   
 
The requested waivers were listed on the cover page of the subdivision plan; the applicant needed to submit a formal letter  
requesting those waivers and the benefit to the public.   
 
Gregory Bittner, 32 Sandy Cove Road -  Inquired if there would be an affordable housing component with the residential 
subdivision as there would be more than ten (10) units.  A. Burney expanded up on that comment, stating that  under the zoning 
bylaw, any residential development of nine (9) or more units required 10% of the units to be affordable.   
 
For a residential subdivision, a form of financial security would be required with the Town in the ev ent any of the work was not 
completed by the applicant.   
 
Both G. Bittner and representatives of 182 Massachusetts Avenue  voiced concern with the applicant keeping everything open 
and honest.   
 
Andrea Adams- She was representing her mother who resided at  39 Pleasant Street.  She was developing comments on the 
project, to include comments on the roadway.  She opined that the project was significantly encumbered by wetland issues.   
 
G. Bittner- Inquired if there was an active road cut moratorium that would have to be taken into consideration.   
 
The applicant previously stated that the proposed roadway would remain private with a n HOA created to maintain the road.  T. 
Cole inquired if that could be memorialized through the subdivision approval process.  A. Burney maintained that would have to 
be with the agreement of the applicant, but he would also verify with Town Counsel.  P. Bertram questioned if the Board did not 
have the authority to impose the roadway ownership through the approval process, would it b e able to require a different 
stormwater management system from the applicant.  As the development is only twelve (12) units, M. Brenner voiced concern 
with the maintenance coming under an HOA.   
 
The applicant requested three waivers; all three Board memb ers present were not in favor of waivers for new developments  
unless there was a compelling reason.   
 
T. Fletcher asked for confirmation as to whether the 25 foot setback was from the property line or the street line.  A. Burney 
responded that he would request an opinion from the Building Commissioner.   
 
A. Burney recommended the applicant submit justification for the requested waivers and when the Board next met on May 10 th 
they could vote on both the requested waivers and the setback.  This would let th e applicant know if he needed to redesign the 
subdivision. 
 
P. Bertram made Motion to continue the Hearing to May 10, 6:35 p.m., M. Brenner Seconded, Roll Call Vote- P. Bertram, 
aye; M. Brenner, aye; T. Cole, aye.  Vote passed 3-0. 
 
Hearing recessed 8:02 p.m.

The Hearing  continuation, via Zoom video conference, was to hear and review an application under Ch apter 325 Subdivision 
Regulations, Article III, Section 325 -3.4. Definitive Plan of the Code of the Town of Lunenburg  for a six-lot subdivision consisting 
of six duplex buildings at 101 Pleasant Street (Map 095, Parcel 0003), Lunenburg, MA.  Owner/applicant was MA Pleasant LLC, 
P O Box 903, Ashburnham, MA.     
 
The continuation this evening was specifically for review of the applicant’s requested waivers and a discussion of the parking 
setback. 
 
The applicant was represented by Paul Grasewicz, P.E., GRAZ Engineering, LLC, and David Plunkett, Esq. 
 
Three waivers were listed on the cover page of the site plan: 
325- 5.4. Sidewalk Requirement 
325- 4.2.B(5) Intersection Radius at Pleasant Street 
325- 4.2.E.(1) Dead End Street Maximum Length of 650’ 
 
At the initial hearing of April 26, the applicant was requested to formalize the requested waivers.   
 
Sidewalk Requirement- P. Grasewicz said the proposed road was a “minor” street, not requiring a sidewalk; therefore  he opined 
the waiver was not required.   
 
Intersection Radius- P. Grasewicz stated the applicant met the 30 foot radius requirement on the south side of the project, but 
the regulations required curb radius plus tangent on each end which placed the project within the driveway of an abutter.  The 
engineer would work to eliminate the need for that waiver by locating the road more northwest to gain the full curbing on the right 
hand side.      
 
Dead End Street- Chapter 325, Subdivision Regulations, have a maximum dead -end road length of 650 feet; the proposed  road 
had a length of 843 feet.  Due to wetlands the applicant would not reach useable dry land until 300 feet into the site.  Shortening  
===PAGE 2===
Minutes, 05.10.2021 
101 Pleasant Street 
Pg 2 
 
the road would result in the loss of lot(s).  It was noted that Butter fly Lane, a previously approved subdivision, had a road length 
of ~765 feet.   
 
M. Allison inquired if there was an advantage to Lunenburg to grant the waivers.  P. Grasewicz responded the availability of 
additional housing.  M. Allison opined there was no  public benefit and that the applicant(s) for Butterfly Lane provided access to 
conservation land.   
 
Attorney Plunkett emphasized the more housing available, the increase in housing affordability.  He went on to stress that the 
increase in the length of the road was not to increase the number of housing lots, but due to the layout of the land.   
 
T. Cole noted that under Chapter 250, Zoning Bylaw , 10% of the units would have to be affordable.  He opined that waivers 
should be reserved for pre -existing non-conforming applications.  He emphasized that the applicant was aware of the wetlands 
when he purchased the property and thus, the applicant should have proceeded accordingly.   
 
P. Bertram was concerned that the 200 additional feet of pavement would have a  significant stormwater impact.   
 
M. Brenner asked for confirmation for a waiver to be granted, in addition to a site/topographic condition, a public benefit had to 
result from the granting of a waiver.  A. Burney responded that the Board Rules and Regula tions for Site Plan Approval & Special 
Permits contained such, and would review the subdivision regulations for same.   
 
Andrea Adams, 118 Green Street, Woburn, MA - She spoke on behalf of her mother who resided at 39 Pleasant Street.  Noted 
she had worked a number of years in the planning field.  She viewed the subdivision regulations and noted guidance to the 
applicants specified it had to be in the public interest.  She went on to opine that the reason for the road length was to maximize 
the financial benefit to the applicant.   
 
Gregory Bittner, 32 Sandy Cove Road - Echoed A. Adams’ comment that the applicant wanted to maximize the financial benefit 
and that the project would not be in the interest of Lunenburg.   
 
Attorney Plunkett- Stressed that the proj ect was also undergoing a hearing with the Conservation Commission at which wetland 
issues would be addressed.  P. Bertram stated that the applicant would also have to be granted a stormwater management 
permit from the Planning Board.   
 
P. Bertram made Motion to deny the requested waiver from the Code of the Town of Lunenburg, Section 325-4.2.E.(1), T. 
Cole Seconded, Roll Call Vote- P. Bertram, aye; M. Brenner, aye; T. Cole, aye; M. Allison, aye.  Vote passed 4 -0.   
 
T. Cole brought to the applicant’s atte ntion the DPW memorandum that was discussed at the April 26 th Hearing.  The applicant 
had previously informed the Board that the road would remain privately owned and went on this evening to stress that in 
redesign the retaining walls within the right of way would be removed.   
 
A. Adams- She inquired if the applicant should withdraw its application due to denial of the waiver, and concerns from both the 
DPW Director and Graves Engineering, Inc.  Attorney Plunkett responded that the applicant would request a hearing 
continuance.  P. Grasewicz requested a continuance to June 28 th.  A. Burney would send him the appropriate paperwork to sign 
and return.  Once filed with the Town Clerk, the time clock would freeze between filing and June 28th.   
 
G. Bittner- Requested he be supplied with any new data that came into the Planning Office regarding this project.

The Hearing continuation was to hear and review applications for site plan approval and stormwater management permit under 
Ch. 250, Section 8.4. and Chapter 204 respectively of the Code of the Town of Lunenburg  for construction of four 2 -unit 
townhouse buildings totaling eight (8) units at 711 Massachusetts Avenue Lt 4  (Map 0 59, Parcel 00 30), Lunenburg, MA .  
Owner/applicant was EF Realty, LLC, 654 Elm Street, Leominster, MA.    
 
The owner/applicant was represented by Wesley Flis, Whitman & Bingham Associates, hereinafter “W&B” and Eric Fowler, EF 
Realty, LLC.   
 
Peer reviewer for the Planning Board was Jeffrey Walsh, Grave s Engineering, Inc., hereinafter “GEI”.  The Applicant was in 
receipt of GEI review letter dated June 22 nd.  The majority of concerns centered on drainage/stormwater management.  The 
Plans would be updated to addre ss those issues.  GEI was also looking for  more information on sight distance as it was  
inadequate for a driver exiting the site and looking left towards the center of Town.  A daycare center was also located across the 
street from the site and the site was located clo se to  the intersection of Che stnut Street and Massachusetts Avenue.   GEI 
suggested consideration be given to relocating the drive more to the west.  T. Cole noted the site was located in the watershed 
protection district.  The Applicant would have to meet the conditions set forth in Section 250-4.9. E. (6).   
 
M. Allison made Motion to continue the hearing for 711 Massachusetts Avenue to Ju ly 26th, 6:35 p.m., P. Bertram 
Seconded, all aye.
