government functioning and trying to make government work better falls under her committee. Currently we are
heavily into budget season. The House is working on the process. She has met with House Chairman Sanchez

those line items that directly touch the Town.
Governor Baker has included in his budget about a 3% increase in Chapter 70 funding for Lunenburg and about
a 3.5% increase in unrestricted general government aid. She believed that is a very safe number to use. She is

working to increase circuit breaker funding for this fiscal year as part of our additional revenues.

Middle/High School Building Project Closeout
Ms. Calmes stated we are responding back to you to answer your questions as we near closing out the building
project. She asked Mr. Malandrinos to address the issue with shaky projector screens. He stated this was in
several rooms in the building. Some were shaking even when there was no activity around. Everything was
installed to specifications but we did go through them with all the involved companies, who helped quite a bit.
We have remedies in place in most of the rooms where they are mounted via a rod to the ceiling instead of to
the wall. We did notice A130 does shake when the door in that room is closed with force or adjacent doors are
closed.
Mr. Londa stated we noticed staining on one of the tiles in a HS collaborative room. We don't see any active
leaks there. He believed it is attributed to the roof leak from last February due to sliding snow that broke the
roof membrane. To remedy that we had snow guards on the pitched roof over the area installed this past
summer. He was assuming they missed some water that spread at that time and the tile was stained then. We
replaced all the tiles in the hallway and probably overlooked the stained tile you saw. We haven't had any
staining since we replaced the tiles. We had a couple of small spots that appeared that were in a different part of
the same room and in the science lab near the exhaust pipes. We had some wild weather in December and we
found snow in places you wouldn't believe; but nothing since then. In February we replaced the ceiling tiles
when we had a lift available to install banners in the gym. Discussion ensued on possible solutions to prevent
any more leaking or water getting into the school from the roof.
Mr. Londa addressed the HVAC system next. We have been working on issues with it since fall of 2016 when
the temperature fell below 20 degrees. We were seeing two roof top units start up at the beginning of the day
and then fail later on. The protection mode was shutting them down. We had a manufacturer representative
come out through the warranty process. They looked at the unit and said it was performing correctly. We went
back to the engineers and they came up with a solution. They gave a recommendation and for some reason, it
was not tollowed up on. As we moved into this season we were seeing the same problem. Representatives
came out again; they made some adjustments to the damper settings on January 8". We held a conference call
with all the involved parties and came back with a recommendation to install reset curves. They came out last
week and did that. There is still one unit that has some issues and we are trying to resolve that.

Discussion on Economic Development Survey and Next Steps
Ms. Lemieux reported we received 202 responses. Some highlights of the survey are that a majority of the
respondents were between the ages of 35 and 49 years old. They feel that the small town feel and education
system were the most important qualities of Lunenburg. Almost 72% were in favor of modest business growth
in town, prefer focus on a farmers market and agricultural development and prefer to see development in the
Village District, Summer Street and Massachusetts area from the Fitchburg line to Chase Road. A number of
comments would like the sidewalks addressed as well as rising taxes.

Ms. Lemieux introduced Sue Shillue, President of Cook & Company and reported they have been meeting with
the Public Employees Committee (PEC) which is composed of representatives from each of the unions,
including the school's and one retiree representative. We have discussed possible plan changes in order to
mitigate health insurance costs. Ms. Shillue presented a rate proposal they had presented to the PEC to modify
the plan design. The rates would be in effect July 1, 2018. She addressed the HMO Blue or Network Blue orates
as these cover the bulk of the employees' coverage. If there are no changes to the plan, there would be a 6.82%
increase in rates. This plan does not have an upfront deductible; it does have an out of pocket maximum which
most people will not hit through the plan year. Primary office visit payments and hospital copayments are
tiered, based on cost and quality. Specialists are a flat $25 copayment. An emergency room visit is a $100
copayment. Hospital out-patient surgery is $150. High tech imaging (CAT scans) is $100 and there are two
prescription drug programs. One is if you go to the pharmacy and you get a 30 day supply. You can also order
through mail order and get a 90 day supply.
Ms. Shillue referred to the Group Insurance Commission (GIC) plan, which provides and administers health
insurance and other benefits to the Commonwealth's employees and retirees, their dependents and survivors;
specifically sections 21- 23 of c. 32B, to highlight the differences between this plan and the Town's. The
benchmark plan has the highest enrollment and is often used during coalition bargaining for comparison. She
presented copayment differences between the GIC plan and the current Town plan.
Mr. Ebersole entered the meeting.
Basically the town would be adding a calendar deductible of $250/person and capped at $750 per family.
Office visits under the benchmark plan do not have payment tiers for primary care office visits. The flat fee is
$20.00 visit. Specialist office visits would go from $25 to $35. The emergency room would be $100 after the
deductible is satisfied. Hospital admissions would go to $300/$700 after the deductible is satisfied. Out-patient
surgery would be the same but you have to satisfy the deductible; same with hi-tech imaging. Prescription drugs
co-payments would stay the same. Overall the biggest difference is adding in the calendar year deductible, not
having tiering on the primary care visit copayment and increasing co-payments for hospitalizations. She stated
they reviewed the benefit structure with the PEC in previous meetings but have not presented a proposal. Some
feedback from the teachers is they liked having the tiered primary care office co-payments. Another question
asked is there a possibility of keeping the hospital co-payments at the $200/$400 level. That is a possibility and
it would add .2 percent to the premium rates. We would be looking at a 3.61 percent increase.
