1. Policy on approval process for signage on town property
Mr. Alonzo stated this was discussed at the last meeting. He read the draft aloud:
TOWN OF LUNENBURG DRAFT SIGN POLICY
The purpose of this policy is to designate the authorized location, size, and organizations that can
request temporary placement of signs on town property.
1. This policy applies to non-profit and civic organizations advertising events on town property.
Municipal and School events are exempt from this policy but must adhere to the town's sign bylaw.
2. There are
designated sites within the Town of Lunenburg that temporary signs can be placed.
An organization can request only one of those locations at the same time to allow other
organizations the opportunity to advertise their events. A designated area along Main Street
adjacent to the Ritter Memorial Building will be assigned to your request. Requests will be made
on a first come, first serve basis and if all designated sites are being used, the request will not be
granted until there is available space.
3. Signs may be placed two weeks prior to an event and must be removed the day after the event.
4. Requests for placement of a temporary sign advertising an event must be for events thatare
located in the Town of Lunenburg.
5. Signage must meet all the requirements under the town's sign bylaw.
6. Any display of signage not following this policy or not approved by this office, the violator will
be immediately notified to remove the sign or if the party cannot be located the town will remove
the sign.
7. Sign requests will be approved by the Town Manager according to the guidelines of this
policy.

OVERALL PERFORMANCE SUMMARY
Best Achievement:
There are numerous to choose from but the Select Board has singled out two:
(1) Her ability to navigate the Town through uncharted and unsettled waters of the COVID-19
pandemic. Things were not as anticipated but, as a Town Manager, she moved us forward. What caught
everyone off-guard created an avalanche of new work for every department in Town, all of it to be
orchestrated by the Town Manager. Policies, procedures, legislation, Governor's Executive Orders,
health regulations and countless other recommendations were changing daily sometimes faster than
that) while she had to determine how to keep the Town running through it all.
The way we operated was changing at a moment's notice. It took incredible amounts of time and almost
Herculean effort to keep up, but she managed to do it. The effort and the achievement are both highly
commendable and surely need to be singled out and recognized.
(2) The financial management of the Town. Progress has been made on Town goals, like the Pavement
Management Plan, the Capital Planning Budget and adding strategic personnel as needs require. All
the while maintaining healthy reserves and the second highest bond rating. The annual audits of the

Meeting with Bright Farms to discuss Tax Increment Financing Agreement
Bright Farms representative Sean O'Neill joined the meeting. Ms. Lemieux noted in early July
Mr. O'Neill came before the Board to present the Bright Farms project and proposal, as well
as the concept of a Tax Increment Financing Agreement (TIF) with the Town. They had
submitted a timeline of the process. Tonight's appointment is to follow up on the TIF
agreement. Ms. Lemieux has been working with Principal Assessor Byrne and doing research
as the Town does not have any previous TIF agreements. She had been looking at other town's
TIFs as well as reviewing the benefits for both the Town and Bright Farms. The company is
willing to invest in Lunenburg's economy by adding job opportunities and the added value to
the tax revenues. From Ms. Lemieux's memo dated July 14th.
"Tax Increment Financing (TIF) agreements are meant to provide an incentive for large-scale
transformative investments by businesses and developers. The Town can negotiate a Tax Increment
Financing (TIF) agreement when the benefits of private investment are expected to exceed any
foregone tax revenue. One important feature of these negotiated agreements is that they temporarily
reduce taxes on the improvement to the property resulting from new investment (the "increment").
The Town does not lose existing revenue because it continues to collect taxes on pre-investment
property value. A negotiated and temporary reduction in taxes collected against the incremental value
is intended to encourage investment that may not otherwise occur. After the reduction period, the
Town gains all taxes against the baseline value-and all taxes gained against the increment. "
There are two phases that Bright Farms is proposing; the first phase of the project is an
estimated value of a ten million dollar investment. The proposal is a ten year TIF agreement
where year one would start at a 100% discount and then going down by 10% each year.
By the tenth year the Town would be receiving 90% of the value. Year eleven would be the
full tax assessment.

Coronavirus Response Act ("PFCRA"), which includes, among other things, Section 5101, et seq., known
as the Emergency Paid Sick Leave Act (the "EPSLA"), which provides for temporary emergency paid

governments, and political subdivisions thereof for purposes relating to COVID-19 and its effect on the

taken" which may be used by eligible employees before using other paid leave, if any, provided to such

Eviction Diversion Initiative:
The Baker-Polito Administration announced a comprehensive set of resources, known as the Eviction
Diversion Initiative, to support tenants and landlords during the financial challenges caused by the
pandemic. The goal of this initiative is to keep tenants safely in their homes and to support the ongoing
expenses of landlords once the Commonwealth's pause of evictions and foreclosures expires on
Saturday, October 17th.
The Administration is making a $171 million total commitment this fiscal year, with $112 million of
new funding to support new and expanded housing stability programs during the remainder of the
fiscal year, including:
$100 million commitment this fiscal year to expand the capacity of the Residential Assistance
for Families in Transition (RAFT) program to provide relief to renters and landlords
impacted by COVID- 19
•
$48.7 million to HomeBASE and other rapid rehousing programs for when tenants are evicted
and are at risk of homelessness
• $12.3 million to provide tenants and landlords with access to legal representation and related
services prior to and during the eviction process, as well as community mediation to help
tenants and landlords resolve cases outside of court
• $6.5 million for Housing Consumer Education Centers (HCECs), the "front door" for
those facing a housing emergency
• $3.8 million for the Tenancy Preservation Program (TPP), to provide case management
support and to act as a neutral party to help tenants and landlords come to agreement.
In order to ensure tenants are aware of available resources, the Administration has kicked off a public
information campaign, including a new option available to call the Massachusetts 2-1-1 information
hotline, effective Tuesday, October 13th. Operators for 2-1-1 are trained to answer questions and
connect residents to the agencies that administer RAFT and ERMA. An easier path to important
information has also been launched on the state's website: mass.gov/CovidHousingHelp.

Electronic Voting Equipment:
Thavebeencorresponding with the Town Clerk on quotes for electronic voting equipment that could be used for town
meetings. The sot of the equipment is an eligible expense under the CARES Act funding the Town can submit
for reimbursement. Theequipment does not require WiFi or internet to collect votes as thesystem iscompletely
separated from the web to guarantee security. The way the system works is that each voting member is handed a key
pad. The key pad has 5 voting buttons including buttons marked Yes, No, Abstain. Prior to the event, motions or
warrants are typed into the voting software that comes with the system. When it is time for a vote the warrant item
is displayed on a main screen, the moderator thenopensthevoteand the participantspressthe button on their voting
deviceto approveofor disapproveofthe warrant item. Theaudience can see within the softwarethat votes are being
registered in the system. When the moderator closes the vote, the system automatically calculates the votes
and displays if the warrant item has passed or failed. The cost is approximately $18,000 for 600 voting
devices.

Ms. Lemieux read aloud her memo regarding this appointment:
I am extremely pleased to make the recommendation to the Select Board requesting the Board ratify my
appointment of Interim Police Chief Thomas Gammel to the permanent position of the Chief of Police in
Lunenburg. Interim Police Chief Thomas Gammel has been a law enforcement officer for close to 30
years. Tom attended Curry College where he earned a Bachelor of Arts degree in Criminal Justice
Administration. He began his career in Law Enforcement with the Honolulu Police Department, then
moving closer to home taking a position with the Pepperell Police Department. Tom was later hired by the
Lunenburg Police Department where he has served for 20 years, 16 years as a Sergeant, 2 years as
Lieutenant and the last 2½ months as the Interim Police Chief. During the last couple of months Tom has
seamlessly stepped into the position of Interim Chief and this opportunity has provided proof of his
abilities to become the next Chief of Police.
I would like to discuss the process that led to the decision to offer Interim Police Chief Gammel the permanent
position. Due to former Chief Marino's retirement at the end of July 2020, the Assistant Town Manager/HR
Director advertised the position at the beginning of July on the town website, MMA, the local newspaper, and
Indeed.com and we received a total of 45 applications. The Assistant Town Manager pre-screened these
applications to eliminate any applicants that did not meet the advertised minimum qualifications, including a
Bachelors in Criminal Justice, a minimum of 10 years' experience in police work with 5 of those years in a
supervisory/command role, certification as a full time MA Police Officer, and having a Class A License to Carry
Firearms. For the next step in the process I asked a panel to review the remaining applications and participate in the
interview process. This panel included Fire Chief Pat Sullivan, Fitchburg Police Chief Ernest Martineau, Ayer
Police Chief and Lunenburg resident Bill Murray, and the Assistant Town Manager Julie Belliveau. The panel
reviewed the remaining pool of applicants, which were then ranked by the panelists according to the presentation
of their application materials, the level of their education, the skills that were identified in their application, their
related police work history, their references, and the overall completeness of their application. Based on these
results we agreed on interviewing 7 of the 12 candidates. Prior to the interview, each candidate was asked to
provide a two page written response to a question that addressed a hypothetical question about the positive and
negative impact of the use of body cameras on the citizens of Lunenburg and how to respond to concerns from the
administration and privacy concerns of the public. The in- person interviews were held on October 5*h and October
6th and all candidates were asked the same questions by the panelists and each panelist ranked the response to each
question on a scale of 1-5. Although we had qualified applicants, the panelists all agreed unanimously that Interim
Chief Gammel stood out from the rest and was the best person for this position. Tom's responses to the questions
highlighted his intimate knowledge about the town, our citizens, his staff, and how his years under former Chief
Marino have prepared him for this role. He spoke about collaboration, keeping the tradition of an open dialogue
with the community through a forum like Coffee with the Chief, spoke of recruiting candidates from the Step
Internship Program that has been successful, the positive impact of community events that the police attend, and
"coaching up" to help them be prepared to be future leaders in the department. Another comment Tom made in his
final closing remarks was that no matter what the decision was, he planned on remaining with the department as he
recognized that the Town has invested in him and he was invested in remaining in Lunenburg until the end of his
career. In my opinion, this comment demonstrated his level of commitment to not only his profession but in
particular to the Lunenburg Police Department and was a testament to his character.
I have discussed Tom's background but it is also important to highlight other attributes such as his professional
approach that is also welcoming, his love of football and the St. Bernard's High School Football team where he is
the Assistant Varsity Football Coach and Defensive Coordinator, and how highly he speaks of his wife and grown
children. To have an internal candidate that has worked with the Town for the last twenty years and under former
Chief Marino's leadership, we are fortunate to have an internal candidate that can continue the positive influence
on the department and within the community.

Post65Program:
Our Health Insurance account representative and the Health Trust Project Manager under MIIA
approached us in late September about participating in the Post 65 program that would move a
small subset of retirees over age 65 that are not eligible to receive free Medicare Part A, as they
were hired priorto 1986 and were not required to contribute to Social Security to a Medicare plan.
Therefore, this small group of retirees did not earn the required 40 quarters to qualify for free
Medicare Part A. These retirees became known as "Medicare ineligibles" and remained on the
HMO and PPO plans with the active employees and under 65 retirees.
The Post 65 program was a concept that originated with Blue Cross Blue Shield of MAquite a
few years ago and it was first introduced in the Town of Wareas a pilot program. MIlA has since
rolled it outto much larger groups with some hepfrom Social Security. MIIA has transitionedover
300 retireesto date and estimates that the program is now saving these municipalities closeto $10
million dollars annually combined.
Keepingthissmallsubsetofretirees in our HMOand PPO population isa contributing factortoour
overall claims costs. In general they require more medical care and are prescribed more
pharmaceuticals. Medical cost and prescription trends have increased dramatically overthe past
decade and MIIA estimates that this population incurs $2 in claims costs for every $1 of premium
dollars that is paid toward their coverage by the municipal entities and the retirees themselves.
MIIA has worked with BCBS on claims analysis and continues to monitor the cost impact
annually. On average each individual retiree and spouse in this population will incur about
$20,000 in medical and prescription costs per year, directly impacting the claims and premium
increases for the Town. This program allows us to shift these claims to Medicare.
As part of participating in the program, the Town would agree to pay the Part A premium, Part A
and B penalties for these retirees and MIIA would offer a 2% discount on our active health
insurance rate renewal for FY22 in order to offset the initial costs of making this transition on
July 1,2021. By moving these retirees from our active to retiree plans it is estimated this will shift

Fraudulent Unemployment Claims:
A number ofmunicipalities across the state have been receiving fraudulent unemployment
claims filed inthe names of current employees and according tothe Departmentof
Unemployment Assistance this is part of a national unemployment fraud scheme. As of
October 19th
, a total of21 unemployment claims have been identified as fraudulent. The
Payroll/Benefits Coordinator receives notice of new claims, contacts the employee to verify
if a valid claim and if it is fraudulent notifies the employee of the process they need to
follow. The Payroll/Benefits Coordinator also notifies the Department of Unemployment
Assistance that the claim is fraudulent, and the employee has been told to notify the Police
Department as well to file a police report. To datethe Town has not been charged forany of
these fraudulent claims but if we were to becharged it would be follow the standard appeal
process under the Department of Unemployment Assistance.

Preservation District Commission (APDC) and relevant historical groups. It was the Chair's
thought to have a small park in the front of the lot with parking in the middle and a larger
park in the rear.
Mr. Jeffreys raised questions about how many parking spaces would be necessary. Ms.
Adams pointed out some of the natural features of the lot, the importance of increased town
center parking and expressed hopes for a bandstand. At the beginning of the meeting she
related the history of the lot including the old Center School. She also noted the easement
granted to the Lunenburg Historical Society for some of the parking area.
Mr. Marino suggested having a landscape designer or artist provide a visual rendition of the
ideas. He also documented the site walk with some photographs. Mr. Dwyer talked about the
park as a good use of the space as it will preserve the land for other future municipal use.
The Select Board concurred that next steps involved communicating with other boards and
working on warrant articles for the 2021 annual town meeting to seek funds for initial
planning.
The meeting adjourned at 10:21 a.m.
