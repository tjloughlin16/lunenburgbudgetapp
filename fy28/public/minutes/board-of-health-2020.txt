January 6, 2020 
 
 
The Board of Health met at 7:00 pm at the Ritter Memorial Building 
 
Present: Perry Jewell, George Emond, Paul Fortin David Passios, and Warren Mayhew 
  
MINUTES APPROVED: 
 
December 16, 2019 
 
TITLE 5 INSPECTION REPORTS ACKNOWLEDGED: 
 
SEPTIC PERMITS SIGNED: 
 
322 FLAT HILL ROAD 
 
John Boardman, Engineer, met with the board on behalf of the owner.  This matter was continued from 
December 16, 2019 at the request of Mr. Boardman.  As a summary:  The owner is requesting a well 
variance from 50 feet to 14 feet.  The owner has a large parcel of land and is proposing dividing it into 
three lots.  One will contain the existing house and the two remaining lots will be to the rear of the 
property off of a common dr iveway.  The lot which has the original house on it is served by a private 
well.  Once subdivided, this well does not meet the offset requirement from the newly created common 
drive. 
 
George Emond stated that he does not believe that this is a variance tha t the board can grant.  The 
owner has created this situation by subdividing the property and believes that a new well will have to 
be drilled. 
 
Dave Passios stated that he agrees, the owner has created this hardship.  The board does grant 
variances in cases where there is no other alternative, but in this case it is possible to drill a new well 
and feels that a new well should be drilled. 
 
After discussion, and on motion by Passios and second by Emond the variance was not approved.  
Votes as follows: 
 
Mayhew: Approved 
Passios: Denied 
Emond: Denied 
Fortin:  Denied 
Jewell:  Denied 
 
Meeting adjourned at 8:15 pm

31 ROUND ROAD 
 
The existing well for this property is failing and does not produce enough water.  A new well was 
drilled under an emergency situation. 
 
Agent Jim Garreffi reviewed the plans and new well location.  Well driller Mike Sullivan verified the 
location of abutting septic systems.  A location for the new well was found that was 100 feet from all 
septic systems but not the required 50 feet from all property lines.  The well is 39 feet from the 
property line of the adjourning property to the right.  Agent Garreffi spoke with Chairman Perry Jewell  
who allowed the installing to go forward, with the request for the offset variance to be heard at the next 
regularly scheduled meeting of the board. 
 
Variance requested is offset to property line from 50 feet to 38 feet. 
 
===PAGE 2===
After discussion and on motion by Fortin and second by Emond, the variance was approved.  All in 
favor. 
 
 
134 YOUNGS ROAD 
 
Dan Proctor met with the board on behalf of the owner.  This system is in failure.  The existing system 
is pits in the groundwater.  The site is small, has high groundwater and is served by an on-site well. 
 
Variances requested are offset to well – 100 feet to 82 feet 
Offset to foundation – 10 feet to 8.5 feet 
 
The system, as designed, is a gravity system.  Abutter notification is required and green cards were 
submitted to the board. 
 
After discussion and on motion by Emond and second by Fortin, the variances were approved.  All in 
favor. 
 
66 GIBSON 
 
Dan Proctor met with the board on behalf of the owner.  The system is in failure.  The design for the 
original septic system is for a three (3) bedroom system.  The assessor records indicate that the current 
house has seven (7) bedrooms and a total of eleven (11) rooms.  In an effort to install a compliant 
system for the house, as it currently exists, a six (6) bedroom system is proposed. 
 
The system, as proposed, complies with all Title 5 Regulations as well as local regulations with the 
exception of allowing the reserve area as trenches within trenches.  Trenches within trenches is 
allowed by Title 5 Regulations.  Agent Jim Garreffi stated that maintaining the adequate spacing 
between the trenches for future use is challenging and is not “a fan” of granting this variance.   
 
The design for this system would be considered for increased flow and therefore should meet full 
compliance. 
 
The design contains a boulder slope wall with a very steep slope instead of the necessary fill for 
breakout.  The fill can’t be installed because of the location of the driveway.   
 
On motion by Mayhew and second by Fortin, the variance was approved for trenches between 
trenches.  Further, a boulder wall is allowed provided it is certified by an engineer.  All in favor. 
 
Meeting adjourned at 8:15 pm

the front of the property.  After extensiv ely testing the site, the required four (4) feet of pervious material does 
not exist anywhere on the site.  In addition, due to the high groundwater, a sieve analysis was performed.

COVID – 19 UPDATE 
 
Agent Jim Garreffi was present at the meeting to give an update.  Mr. Garreffi stated that there have 
been many complaints that have been addressed inc luding the need for masks and gloves for 
employees, keeping an appropriate social distance, the number of customers allows in stores. Agent 
Garreffi stated that he has worked with both Walmart and Hannaford to address these concerns. 
 
Contract tracing is being done through Nashoba Associated Board of Health (Nashoba) originally with 
two of the nursing staff, which is now six doing contact tracing. 
 
There is a requirement for isolation for anyone testing positive for Covid-19 for a period of 14 days.  In 
addition, anyone feeling sick is to isolate at home for a period of 14 days. 
 
A listing of contacts that are with 6 feet for more 15 minutes is created through contact tracing and 
quarantine for a period of 14 days, with follow-up by Nashoba.

the Commonwealth decides what the regulations and guidelines are.   
 
Currently Walmart and Hannaford should require masks. 
 
Plexiglass is being installed for Cashiers 
 
The addresses are being provided to police and fire of possible or confirmed Covid-19 cases. 
 
WHITE STREET PROPERTY

Passios doesn’t feel there is a strong reason to go beyond the State’s guidelines and that an advisory to 
the Housing Authority should be sent based on the State guidelines. 
 
Agent Garreffi will look at the wording and provide a document in accordance with board’s wishes and 
provide same to the Housing Authority. 
 
With regard to State reporting of Covid-19 cases.  Dave Passios stated that he feels the data is seriously 
flawed as it comes out of DPH notification system. 
 
RUBISH REGULATIONS 
 
Any further discussion of changing the rubbish regulations for Lunenburg are on hold until a later date.

Next Board of Health Meeting will be June 1, 2020.

COVID UPDATE 
 
Chairman George Emond commended Agent Jim Garreffi on a letter that he prepared and sent out to board 
members, as well as Town Administrator Heather Lemieux and members of the Selectboard  with regard to 
Covid.  Chairman Emond stated that he felt it was very well written and, if members of the board had not had an 
opportunity to read the letter, they should do so. 
 
Agent Jim Garreffi stated that there are numerous changes and amendments to Covid guideline daily.  Re-
opening is based on risk level, specifically “red” communities as noted on the statewide Covid map.  Lunenburg 
is currently red.  We have moved into Phase 3, Step 2 which allows the bar portion to open for the serving of 
food with social distancing provisions in place.  Bars opening for alcohol are still prohibited.   We are allowing 
greater number at events and more people, up to 10 people, can now sit a table together at a restaurant.   In 
addition, restaurants stop serving food by 9:30 pm.  Alcohol can only be served with food and all food must be 
prepared on site.  In addition, there is a stay at home order from 10 pm until 5 am. 
 
We are seeing more cases now than we did last spring.  Currently, Lunenburg is still seeing 45 to 50 new 
positive test cases a week.  We have more people  coming into contact with more people.  It is important to 
social distance whenever you can.  Wear a mask in all public locations, including parking lots, sidewalks and 
office buildings.  Carry a mask at all times. 
 
Agent Garreffi has recommended to the boa rd that they start enforcement for all non-compliant establishments 
for overcrowding, seating and serving alcohol without food service. 
 
Garreffi stated that, currently, our cases are out pacing our ability to do contact tracing.  The State Collaborative 
in now doing some contact tracing for us.  He does not know what happened in the last week to push Lunenburg 
numbers up so high. 
 
Garreffi reiterated that you need to stay home until you get the results of your test.  If you are a close contact to 
a known positive case of Covid you must quarantine, even if you receive a negative test.   You cannot test out of 
quarantine.  It is important to protect ourselves as we don’t know where others have been or who they have been 
around. 
 
We continue to address complaints received from the State as well as complaints that come into our local Board 
of Health office.  We have complaints on restaurants as well as concerns about the wearing of masks. 
===PAGE 3===
 
Contact tracing is showing that private gatherings are accounting for the biggest increase in C ovid numbers and 
leading to clusters within families. 
 
There was discussion as to the difference in numbers from Nashoba reporting to State reporting.  Agent Garreffi 
stated that the state “sorts” out the information and corrects misreporting such as not really living in town. 
 
Agent Garreffi stated that with Special Town meeting tomorrow, he believes that all precautions are being taken.  
At Spring Town meeting all distances and mask requirements were adhered to.  There is still an exemption for 
people who can’t wear a mask due to a medic al condition and he has had conversation with the Town Manager 
with regard to this. 
 
Member Evan Watters has been tracking Covid numbers and it makes it easier to understand the difference from 
week to week.  Chairman Emond asked that those spread sheets be sent to the members and Agent Garreffi. 
 
Having no further matters before the board and on  Motion by Emond and seconded by Fortin, the meeting was 
adjourned at 8:45 pm.  By roll call vote, all in favor.
