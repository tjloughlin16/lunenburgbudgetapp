a two-year contract.

SCHEDULED MEETINGS AND HEARINGS  7:05 PM Board of Assessor’s Presentation Action Re: Tax Classification Public Hearing  Clerk Amanda Moore read the public notice for the record.  Principal Assessor Lynda McQuade addressed the Board and presented the FY2025 Tax Classification.  Some of the data is estimated, the Board of Assessor’s do not set the tax rate. Residents who attend the annual town meeting decide on what services the Town of Lunenburg provides which determines how much the town will spend. These decisions impact the tax rate. Each year the tax rate is determined by the levy and the real and personal property total values. FY2025 valuations were based on calendar year 2023 sales.    Principal Assessor McQuade stated that the purpose of the hearing this evening is to adopt a residential factor and to decide whether or not to adopt three exemptions (1) residential exemption (2) open space discount and (3) a small commercial exemption.    Principal Assessor McQuade reviewed how a split tax rate would impact Lunenburg.  The residential tax rate would decline and the commercial tax rate would increase.  There are 112 communities in Massachusetts that have split tax rates.  Principal Assessor McQuade doesn’t feel it would benefit the town.   Principal Assessor McQuade reviewed the residential exemption and stated that it shifts the tax burden from the moderately valued homes to the higher valued homes and nonowner occupied homes.  Principal Assessor McQuade reviewed the open space discount and stated that the Select Board may discount up to 25% of the open space share of the tax levy but currently there is no designated open space.   Principal Assessor McQuade reviewed the small commercial exemption and stated that the Select Board may adopt an exemption of up to 10% of the value of eligible parcels.  It is designed to provide tax relief for small businesses that own real estate.  Leased business do not receive this discount.  The qualifying properties must be included on a list provided annually to the assessors by the Department of Labor and Workforce Development.  Qualifying properties must also be valued at less than $1M and have 10 employees or less. Currently, Lunenburg has not adopted this exemption.  Principal Assessor McQuade stated that the Board of Assessors recommends a single tax rate and does not recommend adopting any of these exemptions.  Member Renee Emkey stated that the tax rate provided is an estimate and asked when they will have the final number.  Interim Town Manager Terenzini stated that they will review the recap

Action Re: MHP/MBTA Technical Assistance Grant 
 
Chair Nuzzolo welcomed Land Use Director Christopher Reilly to come before the Board to talk 
about this agenda item. Mr. Reilly explained that this is a grant available to communities to assist 
with compliance with the MBTA Zoning Act. The grant is approximately $20,000 and is funded 
through the Mass Housing Partnership. The technical assistance funds are used to help communities 
achieve compliance by developing develop district options, community engagement, and drafting the 
bylaw.  
 
Mr. Reilly explained that the Act’s application to Lunenburg- Lunenburg is required to zone a district 
with a minimum of 15 dwelling units per acre (three times the density allowed by the current 
bylaws).  Mr. Reilly stated he believed the Town will have options to consider that will allow for 
compliance without having a negative impact on residential areas.  He also stated that if the Town 
does not comply, grant funding could be affected as well as potential legal consequences.   
 
Motion by Member Alonzo to authorize Interim Town Manager Terenzini to allow for the 
application of the MHP/MBTA Technical Assistance Grant as presented by the Land Use 
Director. Seconded by Vice-Chair Jeffreys. Call for the vote: Five-aye. The motion passed 
unanimously (5-0-0).

Discussion on Annual Climate Survey Policy 
Chair Nuzzolo stated that the rest of the Board can send notes and feedback, and he will put 
together a draft. He would like to review the draft next week or sometime in January. Vice-Chair 
Jeffreys stated that he thinks having the outline in case members are unfamiliar with a climate or 
engagement survey. He added that it is helpful to look at the existing and what he would like to 
see in this policy is not just the schedule of when they want this to come out, but also the kind of 
details of what they are looking for and a lot of that is answered by the questions that go out into 
the survey which look at a variety of different areas. Having the list of questions that came from 
the company initially before modifications were made would be helpful to see what the template 
looked like. Chair Nuzzolo added that his idea in thinking of it was that what they’re looking for 
might change year-to-year so ideally the Town Manager would draft and then they review and 
give questions that they want to add or remove. Vice-Chair Jeffreys said that it should tie into 
Town Manager evaluation in terms of some of this because there is a level of inappropriateness if 
they go out and directly engage with Department Heads and ask them what they think of their 
boss. He suggested having a neutral survey that captures the information, and they can pair it into 
the Town Manager evaluation.  
 
Member Alonzo stated that most of this should go to the Towns HR person and that is where 
most of the decision should be made. He added that the Board should be able to review what 
they’re going to do but he is not sure about how much input the Board should have. Chair 
Nuzzolo suggested inviting the HR Director in to discuss this when they talk about it next. 
 
Discussion on Select Board and Town Manager Goal-Setting Process 
 
Chair Nuzzolo stated the idea of doing quarterly check ins on goals throughout the year and give 
progress updates. Vice-Chair Jeffreys talked about getting an update on the capital plan every 
other month. He added that goal setting could be time-consuming, and they must be careful 
about creating a process that no one has time to implement. He said that an alignment of goals 
would be helpful to help things happen. Clerk Moore added that there are several communities 
that start with their Town Manager and Select Board goal setting and then employees work with 
their Department Heads to set goals, but it is tied to their pay. Member Alonzo stated that most 
people working in departments in whatever role have a running list of things they need to do so 
the goals should be self-determined throughout the year rather than coming up with goals in one 
point of time. He added that the Town Manager should be working with the Department Heads 
all year around about what things should be on the horizon. Vice-Chair Jeffreys discussed the 
challenges of goals for when a new member joins a Board as they may need to get acclimated 
and determine their own goals. Member Emkey stated that goals are not static, they evolve, and 
one goal may not make sense because something else came up that’s higher priority. Chair 
Nuzzolo suggested a mid-year check in and potential realignment of goals could be worthwhile.
