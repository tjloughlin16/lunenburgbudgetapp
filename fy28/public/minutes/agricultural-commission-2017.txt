Present: J. Mendoza, D. MacMillan, R. Traikimas, A. Snow, M. Dupuis
The meeting was called to order at 7pm. A motion was made by R. Traikimas to approve the minutes of
the February 16, 2017 meeting. M. Dupuis seconded and the minutes were approved.
During public comment Matthew Allison reported that the Farming Beyond Borders group held it's third
meeting and continue to meet at the Westminster Senior Center, next on Friday April 28
at 5pm.
Meetings are open to anyone as is the group's Facebook page - check 'em out!
Dawn Gearin, Science teacher at LHS, has asked the Commission to help support this year's trip to the
Boston Flower Show. The programs entire budget is covering her part time help in the greenhouse. The
greenhouse program is self-funded in that she has to raise funding for her trips, and her materials. This
particular program has allowed students of all backgrounds and abilities to bring successful, prize winning
entries to the show. Students learn to grow house plants as well as some edible plants such as lettuces,
broccoli, and tomatoes. D. MacMillan made a motion to donate $300 to the cost of the bus, to be
recouped at the 2017 Ag Com sponsored town band concert. A. Snow seconded and the motion passed
unanimously. We will ask that the students bring something to show/speak to/sell at a market or two this
izen informally made a complaint about the noise her neighbor's rooster makes during the earlaking it
hours. According to the LPD the noise level isn't enough for a complaint. The Ag Com suggests talking it
out like friendly neighbors.
The next meeting of the Agricultural Commission was scheduled for Thursday March 16, 2017.
D. MacMillan made a motion to adjourn, M. Dupuis seconded. Meeting adjourned.
Respectfully Submitted,
Maureen C. Dupuis

June 8, 2017 Meeting Minutes
RECEIVED & FILED
FED 0 7 2010
LUNENBURG TONS
Meeting Posted: Yes
Place: The Ritter Building
Time: 7:00 p.m.
Present: J. Mendoza, D. MacMillan, R. Traikimas, M. Dupuis
The meeting was called to order at 7pm by Chairman Mendoza. The minutes of the April meeting were
read and citing no errors or omissions, D. Macmillan motioned to approve the minutes, R. Traikimas
seconded, and the minutes were approved unanimously.
During Public Comment, Miss Jillian Philbrick, resident at 234 Chase Road, came to the commission with
concerns regarding a neighbors complaint about the noise her rooster makes during the early morning
hours. It was determined by Planning Board liaison Matthew Allison that her address is in fact in the
outlying district and according to the zoning bylaw 4.2.1.1, homesteading is permitted at this address.
Ms. Philbrick is well within her rights to raise a rooster though some suggestions were made to lessen the
effect of the noise neighbors, i.e. ensuring all birds are roosted safely and securely in a hen house until a
reasonable morning hour and/or moving the birds to a part of the property further from the complainant.
Miss Heather Bowen of H&M Farm on Flat Hill Road was in attendance to report that the Unifier Roots
Festival will in fact be happening at the Lane Property on Holman Street. The event organizers have
secured all necessary permits and have met all safety and security requirements set forth by the LPD,
LFD, and the LBoH. The weekend long festival uses earthy, cultural, worldly music as a backdrop for the
development, dance workshops, children's activities, and much more! The festival is drug and alcohol
free and family friendly. Chairman Mendoza noted that the support Unifier Roots Festical organizers
have shown to local farmers and producers highlights the many necessities available right here in
Lunenburg.
Janine LaValley of Maplecrest Farm on Elmwood Road, manager of the LCFM provided her report on the
market to the commission. Ms. LaValley has given promotional materials for the market a make-over and
is reaching out to a long list of local vendors to participate in this year's market. Chairman Mendoza
noted that when the BOS appointed the Ag Com to oversee the Farmers' Market, a LFM Administrator
position had been created, but with Ms. LaValley stepping forward to take the reigns, this may no longer
be necessary.
The Agricultural Commission sponsored refreshments at town Band Concert will be Monday July 315!. M.
Dupuis will gather supplies for root beer floats which were a tremendous hit last year, Miss Bowen and
Chairman Mendoza will be providing baked goods as well as assisting at the booth. There will be a 50/50
raffle and a fabulous half-time performance by our own lyrical leader! Everyone is encouraged to come
out to the Gazebo for a fun, family friendly evening. All proceeds raised from this event will continue to
support the LHS horticultural classes and agricultural programming throughout town.
In new business, commission restructuring was tabled to an evening when all members are present.

The meeting was called to order at 7:32pm by Chairman Mendoza. The minutes of the June meeting
were not available and approval was tabled for the next meeting.
During Public Comment, Miss Heather Bowen of H&M Farm on Flatt Hill Road shared about Farming
Beyond Borders. A group of North Central Mass famers and farm supporters started by the Westminster
Agricultural Commission. The group's goal is to join together folks in North Central Worcester County to
support each other in Agricultural endeavors. The next meeting is to be held Sunday August 20 at MLs
Greenery In Motion, Westminster MA and all are invited to attend!
Janine LaValley of Maplecrest Farm on Elmwood Road, manager of the LFM provided her report on the
market to the commission. Ms. LaValley reported that to date, $350 had been collected in vendor fees
with 10 permanent vendors. All fees had been deposited to a Farmers Market account with Town Hall.
She has a key from the Town Hall and is able to open and secure the facilities weekly. August 6-12 is
National Farmers Market week and the Ledger is hoping to write a "Locally Grown" column. Because
some vendors filed verbal grievances against another vendor for intimidation and misrepresentation, and
because four weeks into the season, said vendor was still not in compliance with multiple market policies
including, no 2017 season application filed, non-payment of 2017 vendor fee, resale of product without
required labeling, and using a scale that had not been certified by the Department of Weights and
Measures, the manager's recommendation was to remove the vendor from the market for the health and
success of the Market. M. Dupuis made a motion to have the LCFM Manager write a letter to the vendor
in question, citing the 2017 LFM policies, highlighting those listed above that are still delinquent, and
further noting that said vendor will not be permitted to participate in the LCFM until the LFM Manager
has reported back to the Commission that all concerns have been rectified to management requirements.
A. Snow seconded the motion and with no discussion on the topic, the motion passed unanimously, 3-0.
The Agricultural Commission sponsored refreshments at town Band Concert will be Monday July 31st.
Dupuis will gather supplies for root beer floats which were a tremendous hit last year, Miss Bowen and
Chairman Mendoza will be providing baked goods as well as assisting at the booth. There will be a 50/50
raffle and a fabulous half-time performance by our own lyrical leader! Everyone is encouraged to come
out to the Gazebo for a fun, family friendly evening. All proceeds raised from this event will continue to
support the LHS horticultural classes and agricultural programming throughout town.
In new business, commission restructuring was tabled to an evening when all members are present.

AGRICULTURAL COMMISSION
November 16, 2017 Meeting Minutes
The meeting was called to order at 7:03pm. The approval of minutes was tabled to the next
meeting. A discussion of the Lunenburg Community Farmers Market season report began with
M. Dupuis. She reported that this year went really well. We will be looking into accepting SNAP
option at Market as the equipment is provided by the program for. This information will be
forwarded to other produce vendors. The Healthy Incentive Program- HIP- is for SNAP,
participants could spend $40 at farmers market and get reimbursed. Vendors have to be signed
up for both to enable this.
It has come to our attention that the state has approved the sale of beer from local micro-
breweries at farmers markets. Discussion followed concerning moving the location and time of
Market due to the Sunday alcohol law. River Styx Brewery has expressed interest in having their
product in Lunenburg Farmers Market.
A. Steele mentioned that we could make money for our committee by selling bags and t shirts
with our logo. R. Stober recommended a one-time indoor winter market or craft fair each year.
H. Bowen drafted a letter to Heather Lemieux to request a walk-through of TC Passios kitchen.
Applying for funds to upgrade the kitchen from Green Communities Commission was
suggested. M. Allison informed us that he met with town council and found that we could add
alternate AgCom members without changing quorum requirements with an amendment to the
bylaw. The potential of Farming Beyond Borders hosting the annual MAAC meeting of 150
people in February was discussed.
A vote expressing unanimous support for Article 11 was taken following discussion of the
Article and upcoming Special Town Meeting. Looking for recommendations on topics. The
implications of Lunenburg's population exceeding 10,000 and the potential benefits afforded by
MGL was proposed as a topic for discussion. The next AgCom meeting is scheduled for
December 21$ 7pm. Motion to adjourn by A. Steele, second R. Stober. Meeting adjourned.
Respectfully submitted,
