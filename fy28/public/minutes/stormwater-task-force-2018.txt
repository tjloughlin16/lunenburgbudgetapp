Phyllis Luck announced that the State Stormwater Coalition, which Lunenburg is a part of as a
result of our membership in the Central Mass. Regional Stormwater Coalition (CMRSWC), has
received a $200,000 grant to create an education and outreach program for the State.
Following a presentation by the THINK BLUE MAINE organizers at the Tuesday, January 16
meeting of the State Coalition, it was determined that the group will adopt this program of
education and outreach, making materials available to communities this summer. This will
greatly enhance our community's education and outreach efforts by providing excellent, tried
and true materials for distribution.
Jack Rabbitt shared that he'd recently attended a conference where the impact of greater
precipitation in the Northeastern United States was forecast and discussed
Jack Rabbitt also asked about the amount of sand/salt that is used on Lunenburg's roadways; he
will forward an agenda item to be placed on a future agenda to discuss this topic further.
Minutes of the 10/12/17 SWTF meeting were accepted. As mentioned in those minutes, Adam
Burney will now put the 10/17/17 Power Point presentation to the BOS on the SWTF webpage.
This will provide a clear, yet simple, explanation of the Municipal Separate Storm Sewer
System (MS4) Permits, current and proposed.
DCI gave an update on the progress of their Community Compact Grant award project to
examine and prioritize outfalls in Lunenburg to determine the ranking of the 27 outfalls (out of
the approximately 500 total outfalls) which have risen to the top of the "needs improvement"
list to be upgraded using Best Management Practices (BMP's). This work will need to be
completed to comply with the requirements of the new MS4 Permit, due to be implemented by
the Environmental Protection Agency (EPA) in July 2018.
DCI presented individual maps of 5 sub-basins in Lunenburg, comprising approximately 75%
of the Town. These sub-basins include: Catacunemaug Brook; Easter Brook; Hickory Hills
Lake; Lake Shirley; and Upper Mulpus Brook. Identified on each of these sub-basin maps, per
the Legend, were 28 designations, from Sub-Basin boundary lines to subcatchment area
boundaries. High Priority Outfalls were highlighted. Also included was a Priority BMP's
Draft Summary map of the Town of Lunenburg with 25 Priority BMP's identified. Members
were given 8 ½ x 11 inch copies of the maps, and DCI provided larger scale maps for
discussion purposes.

It is projected that the tasks identified will be completed over the next 10 years. Task Force
members will review and finalize the suggested ranking at the next SWTF meeting to be held

for 319 Grant submission. There is a 319 Grant Pre-Request for Proposal (RFP) meeting to be
held at the Massachusetts Department of Environmental Protection (DEP) Worcester office on
March 6, 2018, which will be attended by Adam Burney, Phyllis Luck, and Ed Himlan.
Hopefully, they will receive guidance which will aid in the final consideration of which projects
to apply for 319 grant funding. Discussions will take place with DCI to consider their possible
contribution to creating the 319 grant application. It has been the experience of the SWTF
Chair that when a consultant has been tasked with helping to complete the 319 Grant proposal,
the result has been to improve the chances of the actual awarding of said grant.
There was a discussion of record keeping and reporting for the new Municipal Separate Storm
Sewer System Permit (MS4); how to create an accurate documentation within a Central
Electronic Data System to comply with the requirements of this permit. It was suggested by the
DCI consultants that there be established a "Keeper of all things Stormwater"

Intents (NOI's), Stormwater Handbook, Annual Reports, and any other documentation required
by the MS4 Permit. It was suggested that a summer discussion with Steve Malandrinos,
Lunenburg's head of IT, be held to discuss how this might be accomplished. Steve is very
active in the Lunenburg Schools, so may have more free time during the summer to work on
this.

Lunenburg is making good progress on complying with the requirements of the current Municipal Separate
Storm Sewer System Permit (MS4), as well as being prepared for the introduction of the new MS4 Permit, due
to become effective July 2018.
Phyllis Luck commented that at the session she attended, representatives from the Town of Woburn gave a
presentation on how they are using high school and college interns to complete a good deal of their field work;
it is proving to be an efficient and cost effective process for Woburn. Newton Tedder, from EPA, author of the
new MS4 Permit, discussed that the document underwent "legal" review prior to being made available to the
public, and that, as a result of the subsequent "legalese" introduced into the permit, it is not always obvious
what is actually being required. He urged anyone with questions to reach out to him directly so he may shed
light on what is actually being required of municipalities, should there be confusion.
Phyllis mentioned that the EPA and Consensus Building Institute (CBI) are offering a 4 session training for
municipal leaders on reaching community consensus on Stormwater Funding Solutions on 3/6, 3/30, 5/3, and

3 1 18 minutes for pertinent attachments). DCI had suggested that S WTF members review the ranking to see if
any changes were warranted. This summary included Sites, Priority Ranking, Soils identified using Hydrologic
Soils Group (HSG), suggested BMP's, approximate cost estimate range, Timing (which year targeted for
completion), possible funding sources, and pertinent notes. Phyllis Luck had gone out into the field to examine
the sites that were given Medium to High priority and suggested that LS: 54-57 on Sunset Lane be moved from
Medium to High priority, as the stormwater runoff flows directly into Lake Shirley. No other changes were
suggested
Ed Himlan informed the group that the next step will involve Mike Clark of DCI going out to look over these
27 ranked sites. Mike will suggest possible BMP techniques and provide refined cost estimates for these sites.

STORMWATER TASK FORCE (SWTF)

August 2, 2018 
 
The Stormwater Task Force (SWTF) met in the Ritter Building, first floor conference room, as 
scheduled, with Matt Allison, Phyllis Luck, and Consultant Ed Himlan. The meeting opened at 
6:35 pm.   
 
Minutes of the June 28, 2018 meeting were accepted. 
 
Ed Himlan of the Massachusetts Watershed Coalition conducted a review of the Notice of 
Intent (NOI) for coverage under the small Municipal Separate Storm Sewer System (MS4) 
General Permit, which permit became effective on July 1, 2018. The first annual report for this 
permit will be due on September 29, 2019, and every year following on the 29th of September. 
The NOI covering the MS4 permit must be submitted to the Mass Department of Environmental 
Protection (DEP) by September 29, 2018. This will cover the period commencing July 1, 2018 
through the life of the permit, and is intended to be iterative in nature, that is permittees are 
expected to complete tasks, evaluate the effectiveness of same, and adjust and improve methods 
of completion over the passing of time.  Hopefully, this evolution will yield better results.   
There was discussion around whether the NOI should be submitted in paper copy or 
electronically; either form is permissible.  This will be determined at the SWTF meeting of 
September 13, 2018. 
 
The group then began a review of the 19 page NOI document.  It was suggested to leave 
discussion of Minimum Control Measure (MCM) 1, Public Education and Outreach, to later in 
the meeting. 
 
In discussing MCM 2, Public Involvement and Participation, it was determined that a survey 
posted on the SWTF webpage, as well as an article in the Ledger which advertises the survey 
and invites more community participation, should be undertaken.  The Stormwater Management 
Plan (SWMP), which should be ready by October-November, according to Ed Himlan, should 
also be posted on the website. 
 
In discussing MCM 3, Illicit Discharge Detection and Elimination (IDDE), it was determined 
that the Storm Sewer Overflow (SSO) Inventory must be completed by June 30, 2019.  This 
inventory is intended to assure there is no cross contamination of the stormwater and sanitary 
sewer systems.  This work will involve Lunenburg’s Department of Public Works (DPW), 
Sewer Commission, and Board of Health in a combined effort to complete this inventory. 
There are other requirements shown in the NOI for written IDDE procedures and 
implementation of same.  Ed Himlan pointed out that the Central Mass Regional Stormwater 
Coalition (CMRSWC) has an IDDE Plan template on their website that may be easily adapted 
for this purpose. 
 
===PAGE 2===
In the discussion of MCM 4, Construction Site Stormwater Runoff Control, it was determined 
that the first 3 Best Management Practices (BPM’s) in this section are already in place.  What 
still must be completed within one year of the effective date of the permit, or by June 30, 2019, 
is BMP 4, Waste Control, which involves the adoption of requirements to control wastes for 
construction sites.  The Stormwater bylaws are in place, but need to be reviewed to insure they 
are all inclusive. 
 
The conversation moved to MCM 5, Post-Construction Stormwater Management in New 
Development and Redevelopment.  The first two BPM categories in the NOI have been 
completed.  It was determined that the last two:  Allow green infrastructure and Street design 
and parking lot guidelines, should be combined.   
 
The final MCM, 6, is Municipal Good Housekeeping and Pollution Prevention.  The first BMP 
to be addressed will be “Operation and Maintenance (O&M) Procedures”.  Ed Himlan pointed 
out that the CMRSWC has templates on their website which may be easily adapted to assist 
with this requirement.  The second BMP: Inventory of all permittee owned parks and open 
spaces, buildings and facilities, and vehicles and equipment, requires the participation of Town 
Departments and the SWTF to complete, and should be the second task tackled in this MCM. It 
was determined that the DPW has already established a program for repair and rehabilitation of 
MS4 infrastructure.  The BMP to establish a Catch basin Cleaning schedule as well as a Street 
Sweeping program, must be developed by June 2019.  The BMP which requires the DPW adopt 
a road salt use optimization program by June 2019, which means that the Town must establish 
procedures to reduce the amount of road salt used during the deicing season by adopting 
techniques to maximize safety while reducing what is placed on the roads. The BMP which 
calls for inspections and maintenance of stormwater treatment structures, must also be 
implemented in 2019.  The CMRSWC has templates for this on their website. 
The last BMP in MCM6, Phosphorous Source Identification Report:  Appendix H, must be 
implemented in 2022. 
 
The conversation then moved back to MCM 1, Public Education and Outreach.  The NOI may 
stay as written, or it may be altered to more directly reflect the use of materials in the Think 
Blue Mass campaign, just released by MA DEP.  If the NOI MCM 1 section remains the same, 
the SWTF will have the option to proceed as indicated or to adopt the Think Blue Mass 
campaign.  It was determined to leave the NOI as is for now, and to have further discussion on 
this topic at the September 13 SWTF meeting. 
 
Part III, Stormwater Management Program Summary, “Actions for Meeting Requirements 
Related to Water Quality Limited Waters”.  It targets the pollutant Phosphorus, and its effect on 
the Nashua River.  The Town must adhere to the requirements in part II of Appendix H.  The 
Town will need to complete a Phosphorus Control Plan by 2021, this may be completed by a 
consultant if the Town deems that advantageous. 
 
The SWTF will take a vote on submission of the new NOI at its September 13 meeting.  There 
is a meeting scheduled for September 27 in the event final tweaks are required.  The Town 
===PAGE 3===

with Ms. Lemieux, and then have this put on the Board of Selectmen’s agenda for their review;

The Stormwater Management Plan (SWMP) BMPs for MCM 1 and MCM 2 will be reviewed at 
the September 13 SWTF meeting. 
 
Land Use Director Burney was not able to attend the meeting, so agenda items c. and d. will be 
taken up at a future meeting. 
 
Conservation Commissioner Jack Rabbitt was unable to attend the meeting, so agenda item e. 
will be taken up at a future meeting. 
 
A template for the Activity Log for Education and Outreach efforts to comply with the new 
MS4 permit was presented to the group.  One column of information was added; the group will 
begin to use this, and refine as needed. 
 
Ed Himlan reported that the workshop with the Hickory Hills Gardening Club was a very 
interactive evening; there was a lot of discussion and everyone gained new knowledge about the 
installation of rain gardens and the need to get  stormwater into the ground rather than flowing

informed the BOS of this date and indicated the SWTF will be bringing the SWMP to them for review
after the first of the year. It was also pointed out by Consultant Mike Clark that the Town must
designate a SWMP Team Coordinator; this person would be charged with insuring that all tasks
outlined in the SWMP are carried out in a timely manner. Mike Clark offered that the Town Manager,
as the official who must sign the NOI as well as the annual report for the SWMP, would be the likely
choice. Adam Burney and Phyllis Luck will meet with Town Manager Lemieux to discuss this critical
role.
Regarding dates for task completion, it was shared that if a task is shown on the S WMP to be
completed in 2019, that actually means the task be completed by June 30, 2019, for inclusion in the
annual report to be submitted on September 29, 2019. This schedule of completion dates must be
followed throughout the life of the MS4 Permit for all actions completed to fulfill the Town's
obligations as outlined in that permit.
There was a review of Minimum Control Measure (MCM) 1, Public Outreach and Education. It was
determined that a minor change in terminology was required to insure that the SWMP's terminology is
consistent throughout.

will be a survey to assess public attitudes, interests, and foster community participation in the Town's

survey responses will be tracked. Ed Himlan provided a sample survey disseminated by the Town of
Harrison, New York. Ed also shared that he has engineered a simple display that the Town could use
as a model. Other Best Management Practices (BMP's) were briefly discussed.
