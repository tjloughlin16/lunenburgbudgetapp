Lunenburg Municipal Buildings - Michael-Ray Jeffreys, Chair of both the Select Board the Municipal Building Committee was 
available for Board discussion.  He gave a brief history of the Commi ttee and its exploration of uses of TC Passios.  The 
Committee had been looking at how to re -purpose TC Passios, Town Hall, Brooks House, 30 School Street, and Ritter Building.  
The focus was on the Hybrid Concept .  TC Passios would be used as a transition  space while the other buildings were be ing 
rehabilitated, with TC Passios’ final disposition being as a community center.  Town Hall would be turned into a meeting house.  
The Ritter Building, with an addition, would become school administrative offices a nd the new town hall.  30 School Street would 
be demolished in spring of 2023.  A n outcome for the Brooks House had not been determined.  Board members wanted to take 
some time to absorb the information provided.  M. Jeffreys would return to the January 23 rd meeting for additional discussion.

The Hearing was to hear and review  an application for site plan approval and st ormwater management permit under Chapters 
250 and 204 respectively of the Code of the Town of Lunenburg  for a contractor yard, personal improvement building, and office 
building at 1325 Massachusetts Avenue .  Owner- 1325 Mass Ave Realty Trust Daniel C. & Jean M. Proctor, Trustees, 1325 
Massachusetts Avenue, Lunenburg, MA; applicants- Daniel & Jean Proctor, 1529 Massachusetts Avenue, Lunenburg, MA .     
 
The owners/applicants were represented by Greg Roy, Dillis & Roy, 1  Main Street, Lunenburg, MA.  G. Roy summarized past 
permitting on the property; an earth removal permit by the Select Board, in conjunction with the earth removal permit, a 
stormwater management permit and site plan approval with the Planning Board ; Conservation Commission Order of Conditions ; 
MEPA certificate ; temporary curb cut access from DOT ; special permit from the Zoning Board of Appeals for the use of a 
contractor yard on the back of the site ; and a Zoning Board of Appeals special permit for dimensional variation for land 
disturbance within the water supply protection district.  The owners had filed for a permanent curb cut access, which was in 
review with DOT.  The owner had been working for approximately the last three years to remove the earth mat erial from the site.  
As the site was commercially zoned, the owners intended to develop it commer cially.  Toward the front of the site would be two 
buildings; an office and personal improvement.  The contractor storage yard would consist of leasable bay s/units.  It was 
stressed to the Board that the bays were intended for “tradesmen” use, small business owners, etc.  Not open storage of earth 
materials.   
 
A grading and drainage plan was reviewed.  A landscaping and lighting plan was also viewed.   
 
A letter (on file in the Planning Office)  was submitted by abutters Gerry and Dale Erskine, 1331 Massachusetts Avenue.  It was 
read into record by Kathy McWilliams.   
 
Mike Rash, 74 Townsend Harbor Road - He had concerns with the size of the building to the rear of the site; he felt it belonged in 
industrial zoning.  He also felt the hours of operation set by the Zoning Board of Appeals should be reduced.  M. Allison noted 
that operation hours were set by the Zoning Board of Appeals and did not fall under the juri sdiction of the Planning Board.  For 
the benefit of the audience, A. Burney summarized what would fall under the purview of the Planning Board.

M. Allison inquired if the contractor storage bays had vehicular access – yes.  Discussion would take place between the owners

M. Allison asked if D. Proctor was amenable to erecting the same along the property lines ; D. Proctor said he would prefer to 
plant arborvitae.  D. Proctor mentioned that the abutters at 68 Townsend Harbor Road asked him to extend the elevated berm

The pole lights would be downward facing, dark sky compliant. 
 
M. Allison asked if he would consider a smaller Morton building.  D. Procto r responded that he  went with what was most
