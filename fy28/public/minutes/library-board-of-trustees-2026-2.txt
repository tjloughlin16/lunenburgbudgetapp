   FY26 Budget Update  
 
   FY27 Budget Update  
FY2027 is open for purchase requisitions, and we have been introduced to an improved process for 
Accounts Payable and invoice processing.  
   Staff Update 
The Town offered a full-day training on June 9, with sections on CivicsPlus (Town website), Suzor IT 
Help, and MUNIS. I attended and will share the CivicsPlus details with Melanie, though we do not use 
the Town’s website for much. 
User’s Council occurred on June 2, with a handful of votes, including a renewal of by-laws. News of 
Clinton’s voting down their Library’s building project clouded the event. Moods were slightly buoyed 
with reports from MBLC and MLS about advocacy efforts. 
Marabeth and Cheryl have both returned to duty. Lydia and I discussed her return at the end of the 
month. Thanks again to Debbie for sticking around in her retirement and continuing to help us out.  
North End Subaru has committed to another year of support for the Monthly Music Series and 
increased their funding level to $400 per event. They have also approved use of their funds for an 
outside, off-site series during the summer, if I can work with Deb at COA and make that happen on

   Programs 
Summer Reading registration has launched with quite a lot of interest. We registered nearly 200 
children by the end of the second week that registrations have been open. Teen numbers have been 
slow to increase, perhaps related to the conclusion of the school year. We have a couple dozen 
adults already registered, too. A number of residents registering for new cards have also expressed 
interest in Summer Reading. We have added second sessions for nearly all summer events for 
children, too, as regisrations filled up in the first two days for almost everything. We will use State 
Aid funding to cover these extra sessions. 
Hello Summer Henna was again popular, drawing 48 attendees. Although a number of registrants 
did not show up, Joslyn’s Summer Book Bag Decoration program had 72 attendees! 32 attended 
Wacky Weather with Kosmic Kelly and 13 came to Caterpillar Day and our screening of Amazing 
Caterpillar, even though we had to release the butterflies we hatched early. For teens, one scheduled 
of the Yoga drew a dozen participants, but then the second was scheduled to close to the end of the 
year. There does seem to be interested so far in our Crochet and 3D printing clubs during SRP, too! 
We had a full workshop to make felted sheep on June 10th, and Stitchcraft and Book clubs have been 
steady for adults. Concerts have brought in solid numbers the last few months. North End Subaru 
committed to another year of funding at $400 per event (an increase of $100).  
Coming up: SRP full launch 6/22! 6/18 Monthly Music: Pop Rock of the 60s and 70s, 6/19 closed for 
Juneteenth. Teens: 3d printing club on Mondays, Crochet Tuesday afternoons. Children: Make-It 
Mondays, Lego Tuesdays, Therapy Dog Wednesdays, Big Event Thursdays. 6/25 SRP Kickoff: Ice 
Cream Social (after free lunch) 7/9 Animal Adventures (with a wallaby!). 7/15 Monthly Music Series: 
Irish Folk Music, 7/27 Make It Monday: Unicorn Snot, 7/30 LED Mini Golf and Games (Finale). 
   Outreach 
The Earth Day Info Fair was our high point for outreach this month, Herlin Jaime organized a 
fabulous event with some vendors traveling quite a distance, and the gym was very active with 
visitors.  
April 27 FSU Farmer’s Market and Info Fair and May 18 Seven Hills Transition to Adult Hood Info Fair. 
Both enabled us to gather some informal data on needs in our community. 
Upcoming Events: Artisan Market on 6/21, 7/19, 8/2, 8/30. Town Beach TBD. 
▪ Building & Maintenance 
A number of Facilities projects have seen delays this spring. Our water fountain is still awaiting 
service. The bathroom stalls that were ordered came in the wrong color. Don has been trying to find 
a replacement for the baby changing station in the Men’s Room, but sizing has been incorrect and 
the original is no longer made. Al is out on leave and will continue painting projects around the 
Library when he returns.

Highlights:  
MINDFUL YOGA FOR TEENS: We had such an unexpected number of teens show up that the 
instructor had to make a trip back to her car to get more yoga mats! There was a good mix of 
kids (gender, age, newbies vs frequenters, etc.) with the newbies giving rave reviews 
afterwards! Bonus: Kat, the instructor, was proud of some of the boys who have shown interest 
but had a couple of behavioral issues during past sessions. A great example of how consistency 
can build both interest and trust with the teens! 
 
SUMMER READING OFF TO A GREAT START: Minna and I had a fantastic visit to the Primary 
School! The kids were so full of energy that we had to hold for applause after everything we 
said! And it seems to have worked... We have had an overwhelming response to our summer 
reading events! We’ve even had to add second sessions to a few programs. I can’t blame 
everyone for wanting to do a craft with me, but we’re going to need a lot of glue for our 
Unicorn Snot slime... We are also up to 207 SRP registrations! 
 
 
WACKY WEATHER W/ KOSMIC KELLY: Kosmic Kelly is karismatic, kreative, and klever! The kids 
were thoroughly entertained by her weather show and, thankfully, very interested in both the 
spectacle and the science behind it. Hopefully it was a room full of future scientists!  
   
 
LAST TODDLER YOGA: We had some frequenters and some surprised patrons who just 
happened to show up on a Saturday and then we roped into doing yoga... But everyone was 
delighted! Every session has received very positive feedback with 2MindfulGirls’ being fantastic

LUNENBURG PUBLIC LIBRARY DIRECTOR’S REPORT 
   Staff Update 
The biggest news this month is that Lydia has decided to step away from the Library to focus on her 
family. Her resignation will be effective October 10. There is no overstating the impact of this loss. I 
am still working with Town Manager, HR, and Trustees to plan how we will proceed with this 
departure. As you all know and have experienced, Lydia has been one of the most professional and 
skilled staff this Library has ever employed. She has been the heart of our staff since she started and 
has enabled me to focus on things that we would never have been able to do otherwise. As it was 
with Debbie’s retirement, it is hard to imagine finding anyone to fill her role. Every effort and success 
that we have seen here in her time has been influenced and improved by Lydia’s presence. 
The staff and I have come up with a plan to staff the Teen Room and offer an activity each day of the 
week in that space. So far, we have had success focusing their energy and encouraging them to take 
a break outside before they get too rowdy. Engagement with the activities is still on the low end, but 
the teens have shown interest in journaling, 3d printing, and stickers.  
I joined the Massachusetts Library Aid Association at their meeting in August to help disburse 
scholarship funds to 18 applicants. It is a pleasure to help this organization support library workers 
further their education. 
Townwide staff appreciation was celebrated at the LAAC on August 27. This has become an annual 
event and seems to have a very positive effect on morale.  
   Programs 
This Summer Reading Program was by far our most successful ever. 450 children registered! Another  
62 teens, and almost a hundred adults participated in tracking their reading this year. We tracked 
more than 600,000 minutes between late June and the end of July. Similarly, we served just over 
1,700 free lunches to the community. Thank you, Friends, for funding this year’s lunch program. 
There was so much appreciation for this food! 
Our programming events for the summer were also especially successful. Many events received so 
much interest that we added second sessions to meet the demand. Even then, we had a number of 
people who were waitlisted and unable to attend. Joslyn’s “Make-It Mondays” were especially 
popular, and out Ice Cream Social had more 149 people enjoying frozen treats! The staff and I have 
been brainstorming for next summer’s theme and programs are already falling into place. 
The trustees and I have been working to enable the Rec Department to offer some of their programs 
at the Library. We hope to finalize an MOU to guide this policy exception this month. You will see 
some Rec programs happening in the Community Room. I anticipate these to be on Tuesdays, Friday 
evenings, Saturday after 2pm, and on Sundays. I applaud the Library programming staff for be 
accommodating to this unusual dynamic.  
Most of our Monthly Music Series is scheduled out until the end of the year. Lydia has been working 
with Kate Ravelette to plan our Holiday Showcase on December 6, 2026. The vendor showcase and 
concert will coincide and a local artist will serenade the crowds. Kate is hoping that all of the

Staffing Note 
I would like to start this report by acknowledging my resignation as Assistant Library 
Director, effective Saturday, October 10th. This was a very difficult decision to make, and 
one that I did not make impulsively or without many weeks of thought and discussion. I 
sincerely appreciate all the humbling comments, helpful advice, and so much more that 
have been shared with me during my seven years here. It has been an amazing opportunity 
that I do not take for granted and has been incredibly difficult to walk away from. However, 
I must step back from my career to prioritize caring for my family and myself. I will greatly 
miss everyone here, but I do look forward to taking on a new full-time role as a mom.  
Summary of Summer 
➢  In 2026, we had one of the most successful summer reading challenges for adults 
with 94 active readers logging 166,106 minutes from June 1st to August 1st. 
➢ Programming for adults was on the lighter side, as I was adjusting to returning from 
maternity leave, but we still had steady attendance at our book club meetings, and 
17 people enjoyed a wonderful Irish music concert in July.  
➢ In the month of August we had some other stellar events of note: 
o On August 5th there were 19 people and four teams who participated in a 
puzzle race 
o The summer puzzle swap was another success on Saturday August 15th with 
50 people attending 
o We closed out the month with a packed house for a truly fabulous concert by 
The Last Duo, who I would highly recommend booking again, on August 26th  
Recurring Program Updates 
➢ Book clubs continued without a hitch during my leave this spring/summer, and our 
July and August meetings were quite well attended.   
o 100 Books Bucket List is discussing A Heartbreaking Work of Staggering 
Genius by Dave Eggers at the next meeting on Tuesday October 6th  
o Between the Bookends is discussing The Book of Lost Names by Kristin 
Harmel at the next meeting on Wednesday October 14th 
===PAGE 14===
➢ Stitchcraft has been continuously growing! We now average 7-8 fiber workers every 
Tuesday afternoon, with new faces joining all the time. Though attendance has its 
fluctuations week to week, it has been really encouraging to see a core group of 
women return consistently and build this into a great community.  
September Programs 
➢ A new recurring program started on September 10th and will be running through the 
remainder of 2026. The bi-weekly Thursday early afternoon meetings for the Friendly 
Frets ukulele meet-up and open jam sessions. Meetings usually have a dozen or so 
people dropping in to practice, meet others who play, and learn a little more about 
the instrument. There are a variety of ages in attendance too, making this a great 
little intergenerational program run by a community volunteer! 
➢ On Wednesday, September 23rd we will have Emily Makrez of F-Word Farm returning 
to do a workshop on making your own fermented hot sauces.  
➢  The monthly music concert for September will be on Thursday, September 24th
