LUNENBURG CEMETERY COMMISSION
MEETING MINUTES
Thursday, August 24, 2023 – 2:00 PM
Lunenburg Public Library
1023 Massachusetts Ave., Lunenburg, MA
Suldonik Conference Room
9:00 AM – The Cemetery Commission Meeting was called to order at 2:07 PM by Chair, Nancy Foss-Yasko, other members present 
were David Dorren, Vice-Chair & Clerk.  Also present was Bill Bernard, DPW Director.
Public Comment – Nancy Foss-Yasko, Chair asked if there was any Public Comment?  There was none.
DPW Director Updates – Discussion on the length of the bench that was accepted by the Select Board on Tuesday, August 22, 2023. 
Bill Bernard, DPW Director wanted to suggest that the Cemetery Commission only accepts a 3 foot bench vs. a 4 foot bench. 
Unfortunately, the Cemetery Commission is not in agreement, as many benches that have been placed in the North Cemetery are 
between the 3 – 4 foot benches.
Discussions continue regarding the Beaulac Brothers, and thoughts going forward with recent pruning of the Arborvitae Hedges.  The 
Cemetery Commission requests moving forward, that the commission is informed ahead of time of any future trimming, pruning or 
tree removal in all cemeteries.  The DPW allocates $22K for work in the cemeteries.  Taking the amount out of the budget for the 
trimming/pruning, leaves an amount of $12K. In addition, allocation has been put aside for $18K for seasonal workers.  Note:  DPW 
has not been able to fill this role as of this date.
Brief discussion with DPW Director, Bill Bernard – holidays that all cemeteries need to look their best for.  They are as follows:  
Memorial Day, Fourth of July, Labor Day and Veterans Day.  A decision needs to be agreed upon regarding an outside 
contracting/landscaping company to take care of the mowing/trimming going forward.
The Cemetery Commission made a motion to not allow Beaulac Brothers into the North and South Cemeteries, which was approved 
unanimously.  The DPW Director, Bill Bernard was not in agreement, but asked the Cemetery Commission to e-mail him other 
landscaping companies to choose from.
A motion was presented to accept Chair, Nancy Foss-Yasko as primary contact and permission to speak on behalf for the Cemetery 
Commission.  Therefore, giving the Chair the authority to move forward within the Cemetery Commission and combined DPW 
projects.  The Cemetery Commission motion was approved and accepted unanimously.
Minutes were read/reviewed from the meeting of August 10, 2023.  Upon a motion by Chair, Nancy Foss-Yasko – to accept the 
minutes as written, with changes on spelling errors of Bernard, DPW Director; seconded by Vice-Chair and Clerk, David Dorren, 
minutes were approved unanimously.
Vice-Chair and Clerk, David Dorren had Fred Anderson from Anderson Landscaping attend our meeting to discuss findings and 
solutions for our 100+ year old hedges.  Several suggestions and recommendations were made during this cemetery meeting.  Fred’s 
professional opinion was that the Arborvitae Hedges were at their “end of life.”  The lack of maintenance and proper trimming 
throughout the years, have cause these hedges to grow somewhat to the side and not straight up.
Plan was discussed with replacement of these hedges, but water may be an issue – due to lack of water flow in the North Cemetery.  
These hedges will need three season’s of water trucked into the North Cemetery to keep them alive.  Planting 270+ hedges was 
suggested.  First estimate cost was:  $167 - $187K.

Lunenburg Cemetery Commission Presentation 
Restoration of North Cemetery Boundaries
Select Board Town Meeting
September 12, 2023
Presented by:  Nancy Foss-Yasko, Chair

===PAGE 2===
Lunenburg Cemetery Commission Presentation
The CEMETERY COMMISSION stands before the Select Board 
Members and Town Manager to Present Recovery Options to Repair 
the North Cemetery Boundary and Request Funding from the 
American Rescue Plan Act. 
===PAGE 3===
Lunenburg Cemetery Commission Presentation
▬ Bottom Line Up Front (BLUF):
● The current condition of the 100 year-old Arborvitae Hedge Boundary – needs to be 
restored to preserve the Peace, Tranquility and Honor contained within the North 
Cemetery.
● Replacement cost of Hemlock Hedges are estimated at approximately $185K.
● Replacement hedges will need additional electricity, water, and yearly maintenance, which 
will raise the life-cycle cost significantly.
● Preferred Solution – Permanent New England Rustic Field-Stone Wall – estimated cost at 
approximately $288K to $388K.  This reduces the life-cycle cost associated with this asset.
● CEMETERY COMMISSION recommends to the Select Board Members to move forward 
with securing from the American Rescue Plan Act – significant funding for a New England 
Rustic Field-Stone Wall vs. Hemlock Hedges.
===PAGE 4===
Lunenburg Cemetery Commission Presentation
▬ Facts:
● North Cemetery 100 year-old Arborvitae Hedges were severely pruned in 2023.
● Following the pruning, several licensed and professional arborists were called in by the 
Cemetery Commission to make recommendations regarding the recovery options to the 
100 year-old Arborvitae Hedges.
● Recommendations were simple – because of the lack of historical maintenance and 
limited access to water, the landmark hedges are likely beyond recovery.  Arborists 
advised not to put good money toward working to improve their health, due to an 
expected poor outcome, and instead recommend removing them.
===PAGE 5===
Lunenburg Cemetery Commission Presentation
▬ Facts:
● Replacement cost of Hemlock Hedges are estimated at approximately $185K.
● Anderson Landscape Construction Horticulture Preservation walked the Cemetery 
Commission and DPW Director through this process.
● Recommendation and estimate provided to install and plant 173 young trees up to 6 – 7 
feet tall.  Extraction and disposal of stumps, set in new planting bed with mulch and 
watering allowance for one season.  Key Note:  three seasons of water needs to be 
complete, which is going to be cost prohibitive – due to lack of water and irrigation at 
the North Cemetery.
● Complete estimate from Anderson Landscape Construction Horticulture Preservation 
has been provided and given to each Select Board Member and Town Manager for 
review.
===PAGE 6===
Lunenburg Cemetery Commission Presentation
▬ Facts and Concerns:
● Additional Cost for newly planted Hemlock trees.
● Having 173 newly planted Hemlock trees will cost a significant amount of money for 
both the watering and maintenance care of these trees for three years, and beyond.  One 
year of watering will cost the Cemetery Commission and DPW – estimated at $3K.  This

▬ Life Cycle Cost (LCC) Analysis:
● Definition – Life Cycle Cost means considering all the costs that will be incurred during the lifetime of 
the product, work or service:  Purchase price and all associated costs.
● Life cycle costing provides an estimate of the cost that an asset will incur in its lifetime.  Life cycle 
costing calculation generally involves adding six types of costs; purchase costs, maintenance costs, 
operational costs, financing costs, depreciation costs, and end-of-life costs.
● Life Cycle Cost incorporates a model to help examine the total cost of the Wall or Hedge installation.
● Life Cycle Cost is using a 3.0% annual inflation factor based on historical run rates, current data and 
estimated future factors.
● Then Year Costs or Future Dollars – is a financial term that means the cost reflects the money as it is 
spent including the eﬀects of inflation.  For this model purposes the base year is 2024.
● Current Dollars – income in the year in which a person, household, or family receives it.  For example, 
the income someone received in 1989 UN-adjusted for inflation is in current dollars.

LUNENBURG CEMETERY COMMISSION
MEETING MINUTES
Thursday, November 30, 2023 – 9:00 AM
Lunenburg Public Library
Suldonik Conference Room
The agenda lists all the topics which may be discussed at the meeting and are those reasonably anticipated by the Chair.  Votes may be 
taken as a result of these discussions.  Not all items listed may in fact be discussed and other items not listed may also be brought up for 
discussion to the extent permitted by Open Meeting Law.  Public participation is encouraged and public comments may be welcome 
during committee member discussions at the discretion of the Chair.
9:00 AM – Call to Order – The Cemetery Commission Meeting was called to order at 9:06 AM – by Chair, Nancy Foss-Yasko, other 
members present were David Dorren, Vice-Chair & Clerk.  Bill Bernard, DPW Director and Todd Holman, Cemetery Superintendent 
were unable to attend and not present.  Public attendance were John Bowen and Jeanmarie Price.
1. Public Comment – John Bowen stood up to make a motion that the Cemetery Commission should be on time – we were six 
minutes late.  Made another motion about the clarification on the words – “watch out” – Cemetery Commission, Chair, Nancy 
Foss-Yasko stated as Mr. Bowen’s demeanor was confrontational toward the Chair. The Cemetery Commission Chair, advised 
Mr. Bowen that he had three minutes to make his Public Comment.  He asked for an extension, which was not granted the first 
time.  Mr. Bowen keep arguing and the second extension was granted.  Mr. Bowen spoke rapidly to the Commission, regarding 
many topic’s that he expressed his concern with.  They are as follows:  oil spill (Spring of 2022), local 39 bargaining agreement 
for the DPW, abolishing the Cemetery Commission, and Cemetery Commission should meet at night and at Town Hall.  Many 
allegations were made to the Cemetery Commission, Chair.  Starting with: calling the State on the possible oil/gas leak into the 
Cemetery at North Cemetery; issues with the DPW members; hiring a third-party landscaping company; bench placed into the 
North Cemetery; dog issues within the cemetery – owner lives on Highland Street; putting camera’s into place; abuse by the 
Cemetery Commission on building and putting an article in the Lunenburg Ledger for support with a New England Rustic Field-
Stone Wall – low maintenance; taking work away from DPW employee’s because of low maintenance; Rules and Regulations 
are not part of the bylaws; the word sole should not be used in our Charter and Cemetery Commission Rules and Regulations. 
The Cemetery Commission Chair, allowed Mr. Bowen to speak from 9:06AM – 9:20AM – 14 minutes.  The Cemetery 
Commission Chair, asked Mr. Bowen to leave the meeting, because the Chair and the Commission felt unsafe with Mr. Bowen’s 
demeanor toward each member. The Chair stepped out to the hallway to call the Select Board Assistant at the Town, which then 
called the police.  When the Chair returned to the room, a motion was made to adjourned the meeting at 9:25AM – Chair was 
gone for two minutes to make the call.  Mr. Bowen quickly got up from his chair to leave the adjourned Cemetery Commission 
Meeting – once Mr. Bowen was told that the police were on their way to the Cemetery Commission Meeting.  The Cemetery 
Commission had a brief recess before Reconvening at 9:54 AM.
2. DPW Director Updates – were sent in a prior e-mail and will be further discussed at our next meeting on January 04, 2024 – 
Sudolnik Conference Room – 9:00 AM – 12:00 AM
3. Discuss Open Items – Cemetery Commission
a. Burials Information from North and South Cemeteries.
b. Copy of Burial Rights Agreement.
c. Validation of mowing hours – 3,952 (stated at Town Meeting) vs. 2,770 (DPW revised).
d. Leaf/raking blowing hours – North and South Cemeteries. Break-out of numbers from DPW vs. outside participants.
e. Cemetery closing protocol.
f. Next Steps for the 2024 Season.
g. Capital Planning Meetings.
h. Ramaker updates and concerns.
