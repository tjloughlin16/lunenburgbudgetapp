Meeting Minutes 
September 9, 2024 5:30pm 
 
 
Members Present: Brian Lehtinen, Michael-Ray Jeffreys, Greg Roy, David Blatt, Matthew 
Allison 
 
Members absent: Kristina Masaitis, Tom Gray 
 
Town Employees: na  
 
Community Guests: Anthony Sculimbrene  
 
Meeting called to order at 5:30pm. 
 
This is the first meeting of the year and the first time we've met since last November.  
Mr. Jeffries gave a quick recap. We just did a space needs assessment, we met with multiple 
departments, the architect met with multiple departments, we met with different community 
organizations. 
We came up with multiple different plans, and then landed on a hybrid plan that would use 
the current Town Hall as a meeting house. It would have three meeting rooms on the first 
floor, and the primary meeting space remaining on the second floor. 
We looked at the Ritter building, which would be repurposed as the new kind of office for the 
superintendent for the Ace Program and district offices related to the school department. We 
also looked at building a new Town Hall, which would be collocated with the Ritter building. 
Our mission was to go forward with the town in 2023, but due to tax issues we were advised 
to not pursue that. We were advised toward the end of last fiscal year that May would not be 
a good year to bring this project forward due to concerns about a potential override. We have 
$225,000 allocated to us from ARPA, but we need to have everything under contract by 
October. 
The purpose of our meeting tonight is to go over the RFP that was sent out to the committee. 
Discussion took place on the $225,000 cost. The town manager and Mr. Jeffries reached out 
to an architectural team. 

===PAGE 2===
This is a competitive process, and we intend to select not only on qualifications but also on 
price.  
Mr. Allison talked about getting the software to create a conceptual plan for the Ritter 
building, the new Town Hall, and the current Town Hall. 
The school committee is in the process of moving forward with a needs assessment of Turkey 
Hill and a Municipal Building Design product. The town manager wants to allocate capital 
funds to both projects and the select board fully agrees with that strategy. 
This RFP is similar to the one that we published before, in that we outline what the criterias 
are of how we are going to evaluate the different proposals, and then we go with the vendor 
that meets all of our needs and that comes out as kind of the highest rated. 
We have to allocate funds for the project before the RFP can be published, but we are 
projecting a release in October. As we walk through this RFP, we will identify things that 
need to be fine-tuned. The release date is determined by the day that this gets published, so 
the sooner we get it to Julie, the sooner it can get published. 
The Town Manager was clear that they wanted to keep the cost as low as possible, so we are 
going to adjust the goal year and then hit the key points where we want to talk about. 
Discussion took place on adding that they should provide a digital copy, and Julie agreed to 
scan the paper copy. 
The Town of Lunenburg has been working for several years to identify a municipal building 
solution that allows for the consolidation of school and administrative offices and public 
meeting spaces. The new Town Hall will be located on the same parcel as the Ritter 
Administration building. 
The Municipal Building Design Committee met with staff, Community stakeholders, and 
various Town departments or officials to work through space needs and finalize schematic 
designs. 
The vendor firm selected will be expected to meet with the Municipal Building Design 
committee to develop and review plans and estimates. Matthew Allison said that he was not 
happy to see the previous Town manager go, and that the contract seemed rushed through. 
Mr. Allison and Mr. Jeffries were trying to work together to come up with CAD drawings to 
push this along, but we haven't made any progress.  
Some design firms may want to meet with the group twice a month, but others might have a 
different take on it. If we leave that final call up to the selected firm, they might have that 
worked into their price. 
Anthony Sculimbrene thinks that being more specific about what the deliverables will be will 
help keep the architect engineering firms on the same page in terms of pricing and that it 
would be better to let them decide how long it would take to do those deliverables. 
The designer will be required to prepare detailed room data sheets and diagrams, establish 
necessary adjacencies and proximities as appropriate, and evaluate any state or Municipal 
zoning or regulation, including local design guidelines, that might impact the concept. 
===PAGE 3===
For schematic level drawings, you probably don't need to have field evaluations, although if 
you're looking for a real cost, you might want to know if you have to do some exotic 
Foundation on the site to get something that will fit your plan. 
Soil borings and geophysical work could use up a big chunk of the money, but it's a pretty 
small area. If we are talking about a 4,000 foot addition to the north of the Ritter building, we 
probably won't need a lot of soil boring work at this point in time. 
We don't necessarily need to go and get a detailed survey yet, or a detailed civil engineering 
civil engineered parking plan, if we use publicly available information, and get a preliminary 
site plan based on publicly available information. 
The meeting adjourned at 6:15pm due to loss of quorum.

Meeting Minutes 
September 23, 2024 5:30pm 
 
 
Members Present: Brian Lehtinen, Michael-Ray Jeffreys, Greg Roy, David Blatt, Kristina 
Masaitis, Matthew Allison 
 
Members absent: Tom Gray 
 
Town Employees: na  
 
Community Guests: Anthony Sculimbrene  
 
Meeting called to order at 5:30pm. 
 
The meeting resumed with a review of the RFP, starting with Section 3 and continuing into 
Section 4, which covers the minimum quality requirements for architecture and engineering 
services.  
It was suggested including a question on the firm’s familiarity with historic preservation and 
the Secretary of the Interior Standards for Historic Preservation. Concerns were raised about 
referencing the standards without full awareness, but it was agreed that firms familiar with 
historic tax credits likely have experience with these standards. It was discussed whether 
requiring historic preservation experience might narrow the field too much. The committee 
agreed that setting a baseline requirement of familiarity and experience with historic 
preservation would ensure that the firms considered had the relevant expertise. The 
committee discussed an appropriate minimum for project experience.  
The committee focused on refining the evaluation criteria for proposals. 
 
The committee discussed the importance of ensuring the project leader’s involvement 
throughout the project, emphasizing that the project leader must be present at all meetings 
and directly involved in project oversight. The committee suggested adding language 
regarding long-term operation and maintenance costs, as well as energy efficiency analysis, 
as part of the design requirements. The need for subcontractors should be minimal, and firms 
must demonstrate stability and capacity. The selection process will involve reviewing the 
non-price proposals first, followed by opening the price proposals. The highest-ranking firm 
will be awarded the contract based on qualifications and price. 
Michael Ray Jeffreys will review with Julie and get back to the committee. 
Greg Roy made a motion to approved the Rfp and gave the chair permission to make minor 
modifications with the consultation of the town procurement officer can make non-

===PAGE 2===
substantive changes to the document as required by law. The motion was seconded by Brian 
Lehtinen, unanimously passed.  It was recommended that we meet with other boards during 
the process to keep everyone informed. 
 
Motion to adjourn by Brian Lehtinen at 6:50pm, seconded by Greg Roy, unanimously passed.

Meeting Minutes 
October 21, 2024 5:30pm 
 
 
Members Present: Brian Lehtinen, Michael-Ray Jeffreys, David Blatt, Kristina Masaitis, 
Matthew Allison, Tom Gray 
 
Members absent: David Blatt, Greg Roy 
 
Town Employees: Chris Ruth 
 
Community Guests: Anthony Sculimbrene  
 
Meeting called to order at 5:30pm. 
 
Michael-Ray Jeffreys received an email regarding the RFP questioning the process. The RFP 
is framed that everything happens under the supervision of the town manager and that 
interviews are followed by the decision of the procurement officer which is the interim town 
manager and select board and we will comply with the process. 
 
Chris Ruth, the facilities manager gave an update on the status of the TC Passios building. 
The option to use the building for temporary offices is no longer viable; the leaks and heating 
system are the main concerns. Closing off the back section would be ideal and then 
abatement process could start. The likely scenario for office space would be to add the 
addition to the Ritter building first, then renovating town hall and Ritter. Anthony 
Sculimbrine asked about the approximately $300,000 for roof repairs.  Due to the schools 
looking to leave the building, the town decided to put the money on hold. The vast majority 
of the 38 year old roof does not have asbestos. The gymnasium and cafeteria will stay open as 
long as possible.   There were concerns that the cost to renovate TCP will limit the possibility 
to renovate Ritter, Town Hall, add to the Ritter, and to build a new Turkey Hill School. TCP 
is also at an operating loss and potentially a rent increase for the schools. The schools will 
likely leave the building and it will be vacant and at a total loss on operating costs.  
APDC article on action on the Brooks house was discussed. 
13 companies have requested documents. Questions from firms came in and they asked about 
whether there could be a site visit. The committee said we should allow for site visits. Some 
firms asked about previous studies, Michael-Ray Jeffreys stated he would prefer to wait until 
after a firm was picked due to the amount of material. The ranges of costs of projects were 
discussed. The committee agreed to keep it the same.  

===PAGE 2===
There will be a brief update at fall special town meeting. Michael-Ray Jeffries will reach out 
to Finance committee on the 24th of October for an update. Other committees will also need 
to have updates.  
 
 
Motion to adjourn by Thomas Gray at 6:42pm, seconded by Matthew Allison, unanimously 
passed.

Meeting called to order at 5:32pm. 
 
The meeting was called to order at 5:32 PM. The agenda for the evening included public 
comments, introductions, an interview with the Taylor & Burns team, and a post-interview 
discussion to assess the firm's qualifications. 
The committee welcomed the Taylor & Burns team, led by Robert Taylor, an experienced 
architect who would serve as project designer and bid manager. Accompanying him were 
Marley Lynch, Ken Beck, and Bruce Gorman, specialists in HVAC, plumbing, fire 
protection, and electrical design for historic buildings. The team expressed enthusiasm for the 
opportunity to work on the Town Hall and Ritter building project. 
During the interview, Taylor & Burns emphasized their approach to historic preservation, 
rooted in the Secretary of the Interior’s Standards. They highlighted several notable projects, 
including the Trinity Episcopal Church in Concord, where they successfully balanced the 
integration of new structures with existing historic buildings. Another example, the Cassidy 
Fieldhouse in Boston, demonstrated their ability to incorporate cost-effective, modern 
materials that maintained the appearance of traditional construction. 
The discussion also explored how the firm addresses challenges such as ADA compliance 
and modernization while preserving historic character. They outlined their focus on 
weatherization, improvements to the building envelope, and the use of minimally invasive 
systems to enhance energy efficiency. Sustainability was also a priority, with the team 
highlighting their expertise in designing efficient HVAC systems and utilizing incentives like 
Inflation Reduction Act grants to offset costs. 

===PAGE 2===
Taylor & Burns emphasized the importance of thorough evaluation and planning when 
working with historic structures. Their approach includes robust contingency planning, 
investigative demolition, and detailed assessments to address unforeseen structural issues. 
They also stressed the value of community input, using clear visuals and regular meetings to 
align their designs with the town’s vision. 
In terms of budget and schedule, the firm demonstrated a commitment to value design, 
focusing on cost-effective solutions without compromising quality. They highlighted their 
ability to monitor market trends and use detailed cost estimates to ensure projects remain on 
track. 
The committee members were impressed with the Taylor & Burns team, noting their 
expertise and articulate presentation. There was interest in further details about the firm’s 
structural engineer and their experience with wood-frame buildings similar to Town Hall. 
The committee planned to reconvene on Monday to review updated space needs assessments 
and finalize next steps. The meeting adjourned at 6:01 PM, following a motion by Matthew 
Allison, seconded by Tom Gray, and passed unanimously.

Meeting Minutes 
December 2, 2024 5:30pm 
 
 
Members Present: Brian Lehtinen, Michael-Ray Jeffreys, Matthew Allison, Tom Gray, Greg 
Roy, Kristina Masaitis 
 
Members absent: David Blatt 
 
Town Employees: Chris Ruth 
 
Community Guests: Anthony Sculimbrine  
 
Meeting called to order at 5:30pm. 
 
 
The Chair noted that public participation is encouraged and public comments may be 
welcomed during committee discussions at the Chair’s discretion. 
 
The committee discussed the town’s long-standing efforts to address municipal building 
needs. Initial planning began in the 1980s, followed by a Building Reuse Committee in the 
early 2010s. Recommendations for Town Hall did not move forward, and prior project costs 
(e.g., the TC Passios project) were deemed unviable for addressing all municipal building 
needs.  The Select Board reorganized the committee to focus on a comprehensive solution, 
including renovations to Town Hall, the Ritter Building, and the Middle School. 
 
The committee has developed a conceptual plan to repurpose Town Hall, the Ritter Building, 
and construct a new Town Hall annex. Measurements will be taken at municipal offices for 
improved accuracy. Focus will be on record storage solutions, favoring digitization to 
minimize physical storage costs. Jason Varney and Mark Webster from Simpson Gumpertz 
Hager introduced themselves, highlighting their expertise in historic buildings. 
 
Discussion included the Ritter Building’s limited parking and the potential for parking on 
nearby parcels. Historical Society easements and land use at 30 School Street were also 
reviewed.  Departments requiring space in the new design include the Treasurer/Tax 
Collector, Building Department, Board of Health, Planning, Recreation, and Veterans Affairs. 
The historical commission will require an office and a transaction counter. 
The committee discussed whether PACC (Public Access Channel) should be included in the 
project. PACC has unique needs, such as first-floor access and proximity for vehicle 

===PAGE 2===
loading/unloading. Concerns were raised regarding PACC’s budget and the potential costs of 
building a new studio. 
The committee will meet on December 16, 2024, and subsequently on January 6, January 22, 
and February 3, 2025. 
Soil and utility assessments at the Ritter parcel will be reviewed. 
Reports, drawings, and other materials will be distributed before upcoming meetings. 
The Chair thanked committee members for their time and dedication. 
A reminder was issued regarding the Chair’s term ending in May, with a decision about 
future service pending. 
 
Motion to adjourn by Anthony Sculimbrine at 6:44pm, seconded by Michael-Ray Jeffreys, 
unanimously passed.

Meeting Minutes 
December 16, 2024 5:30pm 
 
 
Members Present: Brian Lehtinen, Michael-Ray Jeffreys, Matthew Allison, Tom Gray 
 
Members absent: David Blatt, Kristina Masaitis, Greg Roy 
 
Town Employees: NA 
 
Community Guests: NA  
 
Meeting called to order at 5:30pm. 
 
The Committee reviewed updates from the Taylor and Burns team on the new building 
design progress, focusing on: program elements based on prior Committee input, existing 
building framework, integration of components into a cohesive design matrix, proposed 
layout visualizations. 
The presentation highlighted key aspects of the project. Lunenburg Village Center Vision: 
incorporation of several municipal buildings, public parking highlighted on diagrams. 
Proposed pedestrian pathway connecting town hall, the new town hall addition, and 
surrounding areas, with a primary route running past Bad Larry’s and across Route 2A to a 
new wing entrance. Building Naming Clarification: suggested referring to the new town hall 
as the Meeting House and the Ritter Building as the School District Offices. Space Allocation 
Overview: The new town hall design includes a U-shaped relationship between spaces, with 
the Town Manager’s office positioned separately. The Ritter Building program includes 
2,700 square feet for the School District. Additional offices for the State Representative, 
Select Board, Veteran Affairs, and storage on the second floor. Structural Connections: 
The Ritter Building and the new town hall will be connected via an umbilical cord structure. 
Public access planned at the ground level of the Ritter Building, with split-level navigation 
between the second floor and lower floor. 
Clarifications on Design and Elevation: Concerns raised regarding the connection between 
the Ritter Building and the new town hall, particularly due to differences in elevation. The 
new town hall entrance will be at sidewalk level, with a sloped corridor leading to the lower 
level of the Ritter Building. Access to Offices: Questions were raised about how accessible 
the lower level offices (e.g., Treasurer, Assessor) would be from the Town Manager’s office 
given differing elevations. Survey Data Needs: The team emphasized the importance of 
precise survey data to finalize connection details between buildings. New Town Hall 
Entrance: Tony’s concern about the new town hall entrance being accessible was addressed. 

===PAGE 2===
The entrance will be at sidewalk level at the corner of the library and Ritter properties. The 
design allows for internal floor-level transitions to accommodate elevation changes. Old 
Town Hall Programmatic Spaces: The first floor primarily houses programmatic spaces, with 
the middle corridor highlighted in yellow. The second floor is designated as hearing room 
space with minimal programmatic use, including cable access facilities. Site Plan 
Considerations: Most parking will be located at 30 School Street. The path from the parking 
area to the Meeting House should be emphasized. Suggestions included evaluating the 
possibility of a new entrance near the main road and considering a crosswalk there. 
Crosswalks: There were differing opinions on adding a second crosswalk near the existing 
one. While some saw potential in this, others expressed caution about its proximity. 
PACC Building: PACC was discussed as a standalone structure that should remain 
functionally connected but not physically integrated with the main buildings. Layout 
Adjustments: Color-coding improvements for the new town hall spaces were suggested. 
The idea of grouping Town Clerk, Treasurer, Collector, Assessor, Accounting, and Town 
Manager’s offices on the same floor was proposed. Ritter Building Usage: Suggestions 
included expanding the ground floor northward to provide additional space for the Town 
Manager’s office.  Using the basement for programs like ACE and less-utilized services such 
as State Representative and Veterans Affairs offices. Positioning the Recreation Director and 
Facilities Director offices on the second floor of the Ritter. Concerns were raised about the 
school superintendent’s office being located on the lower floor of Ritter. Suggestions 
included moving it closer to the new town hall or to an expanded Town Manager’s office 
area.  Structural Considerations: the design must account for the Ritter building’s north wall 
issues, including energy inefficiency due to leaks. Expanding and possibly adding a new level 
to the Ritter to maximize usable space was recommended. 
It was emphasized that clear, accessible pathways should connect the buildings, with 
attention to preserving distinct aesthetics between the Ritter and the new town hall. 
General consensus highlighted the importance of prioritizing accessibility, functional space 
allocation, and energy efficiency while addressing programmatic needs. 
The architects were encouraged to incorporate these suggestions into updated plans for 
further review. 
Architects to revise layouts based on the feedback, especially concerning programmatic space 
adjustments, crosswalk considerations, and structural enhancements. 
Further analysis of space needs for school-related programs and updates to the proposed 
connections between the buildings. 
The committee discussed the limited space for parking, particularly at the southwest corner 
and along School Street for handicap spots. Tony emphasized that parking should not 
dominate the design, and that the available space should be better utilized for building 
purposes. Committee members agreed to review the parking requirements based on code. 
The new town hall design includes a connector to Ritter. It was discussed that the connector 
should be on two levels to accommodate changes in level and maintain smooth accessibility, 
particularly from the sidewalk into the new town hall. 
Concerns were raised about the need for the new building's front to mirror the current Town 
Hall or Ritter building, particularly to maintain the historical aesthetic of the area. Members 
suggested that the side entrance from the parking lot should feature architectural elements 
signaling "Town Hall." 
Robert confirmed that the elevator would be placed in the center of the new town hall’s 
footprint. Members voiced concerns about the addition to the Ritter building, specifically that 
the design didn’t enhance its appearance.   A few members also expressed dissatisfaction

It was noted that PACC requires a significant amount of space for their operations. The 
committee discussed their growing space needs and the possibility of relocating them to a 
new building separate from the town hall. There was concern about the cost and feasibility of 
including PACC in the current town hall project due to space constraints. It was suggested 
that PACC should educate the public about their operations and how much space they truly 
need. 
The committee discussed the projected cost for building out 2,700 square feet for PACC, with 
estimates suggesting a cost of approximately $1.6 million. There were concerns about 
whether this investment would be justified, given that some members felt the community 
might not view PACC's studio as critical for the town’s operations. 
Members discussed options for shortening the connector or adjusting the number of floors in 
the connector and that the connector could be more cost-effective if limited to a single floor. 
The committee debated whether the entrance should be on Main Street or School Street. 
While Robert suggested an entrance on the narrower side facing Main Street, Tony proposed 
an L-shaped design that would face Mass Avenue. Some members felt the current entrance 
design needed to better reflect the town hall’s traditional architecture. 
Tony raised concerns about adding the Brooks House project to the committee's agenda, 
suggesting that focusing on the town hall should remain the committee's priority. Some 
members agreed that the Brooks House issue should be handled separately and not interfere 
with the ongoing town hall project. 
The next meetings are scheduled for January 6th and 22nd. The committee will review revised 
renderings and continue refining the design. The goal is to make significant progress by mid-
January. 
Robert thanked the committee for their constructive input. He mentioned that they would 
return to the drawing board and provide updated plans for review. 
Tony reiterated the need to stay focused on the town hall project and expressed hope that 
future meetings would bring positive responses to the revised designs. 
 
A motion to approve meeting minutes from November 14th, and 20th  and 25th 2024 was made 
by Tom Gray, seconded by Brian Lehtinen, approved unanimously. 
  
Michael-Ray Jeffreys thanked all the members for their work on the committee.  
 
Motion to adjourn by Matthew Allison at 7:34pm, seconded by Tom Gray, unanimously
