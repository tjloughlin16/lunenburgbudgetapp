Steps in Setting Tax Rates
Pre-classification Hearing Steps

Step 7: Sign the LA-5 Classification Form (Board of Selectmen)

Terminology
The following are definitions of the terms frequently used in the discussion of tax rates.
Levy: The tax levy (or levy) is the amount of property taxes to be raised. The levy amount
is determined by the budget. The total amount of the approved budget less revenues from
other sources like motor vehicle excise, municipal fees, and state aid is the amount to be
raised from property taxation.
Levy Ceiling: The levy ceiling is 2.5 percent of the full value of the Town. Based on the
Lunenburg aggregate valuation of $1,312,058,224 the town cannot levy taxes in excess of
$32,801,456.
New Growth Revenue: Property taxes derived from newly taxable properties like new
construction, additions, renovations, subdivisions, and personal property. The assessors
have tabulated new growth revenues of $481,496.
Levy Limit: Also referred to as the "maximum allowable levy", this is calculated by adding
2.5 percent of the previous year's levy limit plus new growth revenue for the present fiscal
year to last year's levy limit. Exceeding the levy limit requires an override of Proposition 2

Tax Impacts
The residential classes, including single family homes, condominiums, and land will realize
modest valuation increases, typically in the 5-6% range. Commercial and industrial
assessments did not show evidence of a market value increase. Individual parcel valuations
may vary from the class average due to data updates and corrections, new construction,
•and the effects of market trends on their specific characteristics.
The average single family home value increased from $267,155 in FY2017 to $282,996 for
FY2018 and the average tax bill increased by 4.4% from $5,337.76 in FY2017 to $5,575.02
in FY2018.

3. Designer Selection Procedures (from Town Manager Report)
The designer selection law M.G.L. c. 7C, S§ 44-57, requires municipalities to adopt written designer selection
procedures which must be used when contracting for design services for any building construction,
reconstruction, alteration, remodeling or a repair project that has an estimated construction cost of more than
$100,000 and an estimated design fee of $10,000 or more (must meet both criteria).
Design services include the preparation of master plans, feasibility and other studies, surveys, soil tests, cost
estimates and programs, preparation of drawings, plans and specifications; supervision or administration of a
construction contract; and construction management and scheduling. The Office of the Inspector General has
model procedures, which the Designer Selection Procedures before the Board tonight are based upon. The
Town last updated these procedures in 2005 and there have been changes in the statute which are reflected in
the updated version. There are three basic steps of design services, 1) Planning Stage (feasibility study, value
engineering), 2) the Design Stage (schematic design, preparation of final plans, specifications and other bid
documents, updated project cost estimates), 3) Contract documents, contract administration services.

Town Facilities Charrette:
Town Manager Lemieux thanked everyone for coming. She noted Land Use Director Adam Burney would be
assisting in the facilitating of the charrette this evening. She reported the Building Reuse Committee was
formed in 2013. Their goal was to look at the reuse and future use of certain town buildings; the Town Hall,
T.C. Passios, Brooks House, Eagle House, Ritter Memorial Building and the old Primary School building. The
Building Reuse Committee dissolved earlier this year. The Board of Selectmen from that point made one of
their goals to look at an infrastructure plan for Town facilities, including buildings and land. This past August
they held their first workshop and had another one in November. We received a detailed report on Town
buildings in September. Since that time we have had many discussions on the buildings including one at the
Special Town Meeting at which seven of the twenty articles was based on Town buildings and land in one way
or another.
A charrette is a planning session where citizens, officials and other stakeholders collaborate on a vision for
development. It provides a forum for ideas and offers the unique advantage of giving immediate feedback.
Participants become included in the process for developing a vision for the final design.
