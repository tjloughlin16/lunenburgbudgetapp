LUNENBURG BOARD OF HEALTH 
MEETING MINUTES 
Monday, January 4, 2021 
 
 
The Board of Health met at 7:00 pm at the Ritter Memorial Building 
 
Roll Call conducted by Vice-Chairman, Perry Jewell:  Perry Jewell, George Emond, Paul Fortin and 
Evan Watters.  Agent, Jim Garreffi, was also present. 
 
Vice-Chairman Perry Jewell prefaced the meeting with the following reading.  In accordance with the 
requirements of the Open Meeting Law, please be advised that this meeting is being recorded and 
broadcast over Lunenburg Pub lic Access and this meeting of the Board of Health  is being conducted 
remotely.  The Town of Lunenburg, in response to the COVID -19 (Corona Virus), is currently 
following the guidance from the Lunenburg Board of Health, Massachusetts Department of Public 
Health and the CDC regarding the virus and steps communities can take to prevent the spread and all 
town facilities are currently closed to the public.  In accordance with the Governor’s Order Suspending 
Certain Provisions of the Open Meeting Law, G.L.c.30A , Section 20, all public meetings are being 
conducted remotely.  The Governor’s Order, which you can find posted on the Town’s website 
COVID-19 Information Center page, accessed through the Town Manager’s webpage allows public 
bodies to meet entirely remot ely so long as reasonable public access is afforded so that the public can 
follow along with the deliberations of the meeting.  Ensuring public access does not ensure public 
participation unless such participation is required by law.  This meeting will fea ture public comment.  
For this meeting the Board of Health is convening by video conferencing via Zoom and FacebookLive 
as posted on the Town’s website identifying how the public may join.  For Zoom meetings, please note 
that this meeting is being recorded  and that some attendees are participating by video conference.  
Accordingly please be aware that other folks may be able to see you and take care not to screen share 
your computer.  Anything that you broadcast may be captured by the recording.   
 
Perry Je well then covered the ground rules for effective business and to ensure accurate meeting 
minutes.  All voting by Roll Call Votes.   
 
33 BOUTWELL 
 
Dan Proctor was present to represent the owner.  This syste m is in failure, the property is served by town water.  
A variance for groundwater offset to 4 feet is requested, soils tested at 2MPI. In addition, a variance is requested 
for distance to wetland from 100 feet to 68.5 feet.  There are no wells within 100 feet of the proposed system.   
 
Agent Jim Garreffi stated that this is a strange lot and is made up of the leftover portions of the adjoining lots.  
He stated that the variances make sense and he had no issues with the requests. 
 
After discussion and on Motion by Fortin and second by Jewell, the variances were unanimously approved by 
roll call vote. 
 
31 SOUTH ROW ROAD 
 
Dan Proctor met with the board on behalf of the owner.  This system is in failure , but the owner is not selling .  
Variances being request are offset to groundwater to 4 feet, distance to wate r line from 20 feet to 5 feet and 
distance to foundation from 20 feet to 13 feet.   
 
===PAGE 2===
This lots fronts on the lake and is a small, tight lot.  The property is connected to public water.   
 
Agent Jim Garreffi stated that it is a small lake lot with a high water table.   
 
After discussion and on motion by Jewell and second by Emond, the board voted to approve the variances.   All 
in favor by roll call vote. 
 
There is a well on the property , that was previously used as a potable source of water.  This property h as been 
connected to public water and  the owner would like to retain the use of  this well for irrigation purposes .  Dan 
Proctor stated that there are a lot of well still in service in this area. 
 
George Emond stated that typically wells are destroyed when  connected to public wat er to avoid cross 
contamination with the public water supply. 
 
Inasmuch the matter of retaining the well was not on the agenda for discussion, the owner would have to come 
back to the board to retain the agricultural well. 
 
In doing so, a request, by letter would need to be submitted to the board and the particulars as to location, depth, 
etc. would have to be provided so that the board would be able to make a decision. 
 
17 WEST STREET TERRACE 
 
Dan Proctor met with the board on behalf of the owner.  This system is in failure.  Testing showed 27 MPI soils.  
A Presby system with a pump chamber has been designed for this lot.  This site is connected to public water, has 
high groundwater and is a fairly small in size. 
 
Variances requested are offset to groundwater from 4 feet to 2 feet, less than 12” on inlet and outlet tees. 
 
Jim Garreffi stated the design of a Presby system allows the groundwater offset of 2 feet.  Because the level of 
the house is set, the inlet and out let tee variance  is necessary.  The system, as designed, is much better than the 
system that failed.  There are no wetland or wells and he had no issues. 
 
After discussion and on Motion by Fortin and second by Watters, these variances were unanimously approved 
by roll call vote. 
 
MEETING MINUTES 
 
On motion by Jewell and second by Watters all minutes were unanimously approved by roll call vote. 
 
BUDGET 
 
Perry Jew ell stated that the Board has had an ongoing request for additional office hours for Administrative 
Assistant Andrea Schnepf.  Mrs. Schnepf began with the board with  30 hours a week, but was reduced to 26 
hours a week some years ago due to town wide budget constraints .  In this last budget cycle, additional hours 
were included for Mrs. Schnepf but, due to Covid, were  not acted on at Annual Town meeting in May.  They 
were included in the November Special Town meeting.  This is an increase of 6 hours per week. 
 
Andrea Schnepf stated that in addition to the hours for herself, a 5% increase was included for Nashoba 
Associated Boards of Health, both Nursing and Environmental.  Nashoba has not had their meeting to determine 
the actual amount and won’t until the end of January  The 5% increase is a “guesstamate”. 
 
In addition, line item amounts were moved, while keeping the overall budget at level funding of $1,750.00 
 
===PAGE 3===
After discussion and on motion by Watters and second by Jewell the budget was una nimously approved by roll 
call vote. 
 
RUBBISH PERMITS 
 
Administrative Assistant advised that board that the annual Rubbish Permits , which are due January 1 st, have 
been prepared.  Typically, the members of the board sign the permits.  Mrs. Schnepf is asking that she be able to 
sign the permits on behalf of the board, or type their name. 
 
Chairman George Emond stated that he would rather have the permits signed rather than the names typed. 
 
After discussion and on motion by Jewell and second by Fortin, the board unanimously approve d authorization 
to sign permits for the duration of the Covid crisis by roll call vote. 
 
299 PAGE STREET 
 
Jim Morin met with the board on behalf of the owner.  This system is in failure.  The soils  on this site are 
strange and they had difficulty getting good depth of soil.  They tested by the old system but the soils were not 
desirable and ledge was found.   Decent soils were found at the rear of the property with a 9 MPI Perc rate.  
There was also good depth of soil.  Variance requested is offset to groundwater from four feet to three feet.   
 
Jim Garreffi stated that granting this variance helps with grading with the requirement of a poly barrier. 
 
After discussion and on motion by Jewell and second by Fortin, the septic variance was approved by unanimous 
roll call vote. 
 
The house is served by an artisan well.  The owner of the house is asking to keep two wells as irrigation wells on 
the opposite side of the property.  These wells were marked on the septic plan.  These well appear to be concrete 
lined and do have covers although they are covered with brambles. 
 
After discussion and on motion by Jewell and second by Fortin, the request to keep the two wells as noted on the 
septic plan was approved with the condition that a deed restriction be place on the property, the wells have 
covers and are fully disconnected from the residence and are not to be used as a so urce of potable water. 
Unanimously approved by roll call vote. 
 
COVID UPDATE 
 
Jim Garreffi stated that there seems to be a spirt of complaints, including one from the state.   Some of the 
complaints are second violations and Mr. Garreffi will be writing letters. 
 
The number of cases is up to about 51 for this last reporting week.  Mr. Garreffi is seeing a lot of multiple cases 
within the same address.  40% to 45% of reported cases are home clusters. 
 
Mr. Garreffi has been meeting with the school superintendent and the schools are doing a great job of keeping 
the kids in school safe. 
 
Nashoba has will be receiving doses of  the Moderna vaccine which will be given to first responders at a clinic 
on January 21 st or January 22nd.  This will include fire, rescue a nd EMS.  In order to provide this vaccine, new 
software was provided to keep track of scheduling, billing and second doses. 
 
Mr. Garreffi stated that as this point the distribution of the vaccine to the general public if very vague.  As stated 
by Governor Baker, distribution will be in Phases with the first doses going to health care workers and those at 
greater risk. 
===PAGE 4===
 
It might be late spring before vaccine is available to the general public.  It is possible that vaccine distribution 
with be through pharmacies such as CVS and Walgreens. 
 
Currently the vaccination rate is poor, between 25% and 30% of what was expected.  Many are waiting to see 
what other experience as far a side effects are. 
 
There are some business that are not adhering to the guidelines as put forth by the Department of Public Heath 
with regard to capacity, mask wearing and reporting.  When Mr. Garreffi was asked if any business are at a 
point where they should come before the board, Mr. Garreffi stated no. 
 
Chairman Emond, as well as other board members commented on what a great job member Even Watters is 
doing charting Covid numbers for the board. 
 
 
Having no further matters before the board and on  Motion by Watters and seconded by Jewell, the meeting was 
adjourned at 9:00 pm.  By roll call vote, all in favor.

466 MASS AVENUE 
 
Dan Proctor was present to represent the owner.  This syste m is in failure, the property is served by town water.  
A variance for groundwater offset to 4 feet is requested  to maintain grading , soils tested at 2MPI .  The 
surrounding properties are served by public water. 
 
Agent Jim Garreffi st ated that this is a fairly large lot with good soil.  The groundwater w as higher than 
expected.  He stated that the variances make sense and he had no issues with the requests. 
 
After discussion and on Motion by Jewell and second by Fortin, the variances were unanimously approved by 
roll call vote. 
 
75 RUTH STREET 
 
Jack Maloney met with the board on behalf of the owner.  This system is in failure.  This property is going from 
a 2 bedroom system to a 3 bedroom system and meets all Title 5 requirements.   
 
===PAGE 2===
Currently, the property is served by a shallow well with a well pump in the house.  As proposed, a new well will 
be drilled to meet the 100 foot requirement from a septic system.  Chairman Edmond stated that the new well 
will need to be drilled and tested before COC for the septic system can be issued. 
 
Variances requested are only one deep hole (2 are required) and distance to Lake Shirley from 100 feet to 57.4 
feet. 
 
Agent Jim Garreffi stated that it is a good size lot at 1.75 acres and the soil is good.  The topography slopes 
toward Lake Shirley.  The system is more shallow and a new well will provide better water quality. 
 
Member Evan Watters questioned the difference in a 50 foot distance from a wetland as opposed to a 100 foot 
distance and the need for a variance.  Chairman George Emond stated that the state allows a 50 foo t offset, 
whereas Lunenburg has a regulation that requires 100 feet.  By having this regulation, it allows the board to take 
a closer look at what is being proposed. 
 
After discussion and on motio n by Jewell and second by Emond, the board voted to approve the variances with 
the condition that the well be installed and tested prior to the issuance of a Certificate of Compliance for the 
septic system.  All in favor by roll call vote. 
 
60 WOODLAND DRIVE 
 
Jack Maloney met with the board on behalf of the owner.  This system is in failure.  This house is close to the 
road with the existing system behind the house and the rear portion of this lot goes up in grade.  Testing showed 
9 MPI soils.  Variances requested are less than 12” on inlet and outlet tees  and distance to well from 100 feet to 
79 feet. 
 
Jim Garreffi stated that due to this house being on a slab the plumbing is set so an inlet and outlet tee variance is 
necessary.  This will be a raised system with a tank and pump chamber to a Presby system.   The system, as 
designed, is much better than the system that failed.   
 
Agent Garreffi further stated that he does not recommend granting the well variance.  Public water is available 
in the street and he recommended that this property be connected to public water. 
 
Jack Maloney was asked if he had discussed retaining the well as opposed to connecting to public water with the 
homeowner.  He stated that he had not. 
 
Vice Chairman asked that this matter be continued until the next Board of Health meeting on Ma rch 15, 2021 to 
give Mr. Maloney an opportunity to speak with the homeowner.  All Parties were in agreement. 
 
This matter was continued until March 15, 2021 at 7:15 pm by agreement of the parties.  (Later changed to 6:15 
pm due to Caucus) 
 
MEETING MINUTES 
 
On motion by Jewell and second by Emond, the January 4, 2021 minutes were unanimously approved by roll 
call vote. 
 
COVID UPDATE 
 
Jim Garreffi stated that the State will be moving into Phase 3 Step 2 because of the improvement in Covid 
numbers. 
 
===PAGE 3===
The state feels that this will be good for business and that retail stores can go to a capacity of 50%.  Restaurants 
have no capacity limit other than the distance between tables and a limit of 1.5 hours and 6 people at a table.  
The Governor will be looking at movi ng to Phase 4 Step 1 beginning March 22 nd and the vaccine continues to 
roll out. 
 
Agent Garreffi stated that although the numbers for Lunenburg are going down, Lunenburg has been stubborn 
with cases and is going down more slowly. 
 
Agent Garreffi stated tha t they are pushing mass vaccination sites.  The thinking being that mass vaccination 
sites can administer the vaccine quicker, possibly bring more vaccine into the state.  All of Nashoba vaccine has 
been reallocated to Mass Vaccination sites.  Nashoba will be able to administer the second shot to those that got 
a first shot through Nashoba on either March 11 or March 25 th but there will be no more clinics unless more 
vaccine becomes available. 
 
Agent Garreffi stated that there are 11 statewide collaboratives that have been approved.  In order to be a part of 
a collaborative, you need to be able to administer 750  doses a day/5 days a week and must be open to all 
residents of the state. 
 
Chairman Emond stated that the lack of vaccine in local communities is most bothersome.  No matter how much 
vaccine the state receives, the local communities do not receive vaccine.  Mr. Emond feels that 1/3 of the 
population of Massachusetts has been disenfranchised.  The State has completely cut Nashoba’s allotment of 
vaccine and re-allocated it to mass vaccination sites. 
 
Mr. Garreffi stated that the difference in the State’s number and Nashoba is due to counting probable cases.  
Probable cases are cases that are diagnosed by other than a PCR test. 
 
224 TOWNSEND HARBOR ROAD 
 
Doug Smith met with the board on behalf of the owner.  This is an A-Frame house that was built in 
approximately 1955.  Mr. Smith designed a plan for replacement in 2000 but it was never submitted.  Mr. Smith 
is reactivating the plan.  All properties but on e are on public water.  The closest well is 65 feet away.  This plan 
was before the Conservation Commission and it was approved. 
 
Variances requested are offset to well - 100 feet to 65 feet 
Offset to crawl space – 10 feet to 5 feet 
Offset to wetlands – 100 feet to 50 feet 
 
Jim Garreffi stated that this is a small lot and that location of the wetland knocks off the back portion of the lot.  
There is an existing cesspool and the system, as designed, is an improvement over what is there. 
 
After discussion and on motion by Jewell and second by Fortin, the septic variance was approved by unanimous 
roll call vote. 
 
310 SUNNY HILL ROAD 
 
Dan Proctor met with the board on behalf of the owner.  A new, fully compliant system has been designed so 
there is no need for a hearing. 
 
Having no further matters before the board and on  Motion by Watters and seconded by Jewell, the meeting was 
adjourned at 9:00 pm.  By roll call vote, all in favor.

LUNENBURG BOARD OF HEALTH 
MEETING MINUTES 
Monday, September 20, 2021 
 
The Board of Health met at 7:00 pm at the Ritter Memorial Building  
 
Members Present:  George Emond, Perry Jewell, Paul Fortin, Evan Watters  and Lisa Normandin .   
Agent Jim Garreffi was also present. 
 
MINUTES APPROVED 
 
August 16, 2021 
 
On motion by Normandin and second by Jewell, minutes were approved.  All in favor. 
 
42 PEARL STREET 
 
Brandon Ducharme met with the board together with the owners, Joanne and Butch Magurn .   A new 
well is be ing proposed.  Currently, the existing well at 37 feet is too close to the septic system  and 
needs to be relocated.  This is a very tight lake front lot.   A new drilled well, steel cased and is sealed 
to bedrock is being proposed.  Offset variances are requested as follows: 
 
Leach field – 100 feet to 61 feet 
Septic Tank – 50 feet to 45 feet 
Sewer Line – 50 feet to 30 feet 
Property Line – 50 feet to 41 feet. 
 
Jim Garreffi stated that what is being proposed is better than what is there and he has no issues with the 
variances being approved. 
 
Lisa Normandin asked wh at would happen to the old well.  Chairman Emond stated that it will be 
decommissioned. 
 
After discussion and on motion by Normandin and second by Jewell, the board unanimously approved 
the requested variances.  All in favor. 
 
284 LANCASTER AVENUE 
 
Tom Alonzo and Christine Foltz, owners, met with the board.  The owners are proposing construction 
of a garage on a slab foundation on their property.  It was always a plan to include a garage on this 
property. 
 
The septic system on this site has a septic tank and pump chamber, which are downslope from where 
they are proposing to build the garage.  The garage will be attached to the home and accessed through 
an already existing door.   
 
Regulations require 10 feet distance from the proposed garage to the septic tank.  They are proposing a 
6.5 foot offset. 
 
===PAGE 2===
Chairman Emond stated that he has no issues with the request inasmuch as the septic tank and pump 
chamber are down slope from the proposed garage and the c ost of moving the tank and pump chamber 
would be substantial and create an undue hardship. 
 
Vice-Chair Perry Jewell stated that he does not feel that the construction of a garage with the reduction 
in offset would pose an increase in environmental concern and further the moving of the tanks is not 
easily feasible and would be costly. 
 
After further discussion and on motion by Fortin and second by Normandin, the board voted to 
approve the variance.  All in favor. 
 
30 WEST STREET TERRACE 
 
Dan Proctor met with  the board on behalf of the owner.  This system is in failure.  Testing was done 
with a 5 MPI perc rate.  The lot is small and is served by public water.  A Presby system is proposed.  
Variance request is for groundwater offset from 4 feet to 2 feet. 
 
Jim Garreffi stated that he has no issues with the request. 
 
After discussion and on motion by Normandin and second by Fortin, the variance was approved.  All 
in favor. 
 
COVID UPDATE 
 
Agent Jim Garreffi gave a Covid Update. 
 
Lunenburg is seeing a large number of positive cases and an increase in cases in family groups.   This 
past week there were 12 cases at 4 addresses.  Mr. Garreffi stated that he does not have an explanation 
for the increase in Lunenburg.  Labor Day there were 6 cases in one household.  We are seeing cases in 
churches, daycares and large gatherings.  Some are not answering the call from contact tracers. 
 
Currently there are no mandates in Lunenburg. 
 
5 WALLIS PARK 
 
Jim Garreffi stated that the tenant contacted him in February for a housing inspection.  The landlord 
had put in a mini split for heat, but it couldn’t keep up.  There were also mice. 
 
The Landlord asked for an extension to complete the work but did not respond to Mr. Garreffi when 
asked for an update.  A letter was sent and was responded to today stating that the repairs have been 
completed and is ready for inspection.  Mr. Garreffi will report back after inspection. 
 
441 TOWNSEND HARBOR ROAD 
 
Jack Maloney met with the board on behalf of the owner.  The house is being torn down and rebuilt on 
the same footprint as trees came down and crushed the house. 
 
A Presby system is proposed.  Testing showed a perc rate of 2 MPI.  The existing house was a two 
bedroom, and the plan will be amended for a new two bedroom to be re-built.  The property is a lake 
===PAGE 3===
front lot served by public water.  Variances requested are offset to wetland from 100 feet to 59 feet and 
groundwater offset from 5 feet to 3 feet. 
 
Jim Garreffi stated that this is a dog leg shaped lot with a high water table.  A 2 bedroom deed 
restriction will need to be placed on the property. 
 
After discussion and on motion by Jewell and second by Normandin, the variances were approved with 
the need for a 2 bedroom deed restriction.  All in favor. 
 
360 FLAT HILL 
 
Jim Garreffi stated that this property is a lot that is being split off of Flatt Hill orchard.  The purchaser 
is proposing 8 building lots on this 22 acre site. 
 
The concerns moving forward are what contaminants are in the soil as this property was utilized as an 
apple orchard with sustained pesticide use. 
 
Perry Jewell asked if this area is served by public water.  Jim Garreffi said no, that private wells would 
need to be utilized.  The houses in this area with wells have low water quality and low water capacity. 
 
A letter was sent to the developer stating that soil testing would be required to determine if there are 
any contaminants including arsenic.  If contaminants are found, mitigation would need to take place. 
 
35 PLEASANTVIEW  
 
Joe Brogna, owner, met with the board.  He is proposing to construct a new 2 car garage with a 
greenhouse above it.  There is a 4 bedroom septic permit on the property which would allow a total of 
9 rooms.  Currently there is also an in-law apt above the existing garage. 
 
Jim Garreffi explained the definition of bedrooms and total rooms by Title 5 definition and explained 
that Mr. Brogna’s house was currently (without any additional space) at or above the allowable number 
rooms in accordance with the existing septic permit. 
 
Mr. Brogna shared pictures of his house and the Board of Health and Building Department files were 
reviewed. 
 
After further discussion it was determined that Mr. Brogna’s house was already at the maximum 
number of rooms.  Unless the number of rooms in the house can be reduced, a building permit for the 
additional living space cannot be approved unless the septic system was expanded to allow for the 
additional rooms. 
 
The board rejected the request without prejudice and advised Mr. Brogna that he could resubmit his 
request with either the allowable number of rooms or an expansion of the septic system. 
 
71 WILDWOOD 
 
Nichole Kirouac met with the board on behalf of the owner.  An addition to an existing house is being 
proposed that exceeds both the allowable bedrooms as well as the total number of rooms.  This house 
has a 3 bedroom septic. 
===PAGE 4===
 
As currently presented, even with the changes that were made to present for the meeting, the addition 
exceeds what is allowable for both bedrooms and total number of rooms.  Possible solutions were 
discussed such as removing walls.  A revised plan will be submitted for review in keeping with this 
discussion and a reduction in room count. 
 
Having no further matters before the board and on Motion by Normandin and seconded by Watters, the 
meeting was adjourned at 9:05 pm, all in favor.

LUNENBURG BOARD OF HEALTH 
MEETING MINUTES 
Monday, October 18, 2021 
 
The Board of Health met at 7:00 pm at the Ritter Memorial Building  
 
Members Present:  George Emond, Perry Jewell, Evan Watters and Paul Fortin.    
 
MINUTES APPROVED 
 
30 JOHNSON STREET 
 
Chris MacKenzie met with the board on behalf of the owner.  This system is likely in failure as it was 
installed in 1985 and is a leaching pit .  This is a small lot and has a perc rate of 2 MPI .  What is being 
proposed is a gravity system with a pump chamber .  Variances requested are offset to wetland from 
100 feet to 70 feet and a groundwater offset from 5 feet to 4 feet. 
 
The existing house on the property is to be torn down and a new structure will be built on the same 
footprint.  There will be no change in the number of rooms. 
 
Jim Garreffi stated that the proposed system is more compliant that what is existing at this site now.  It 
will be greater than 100 feet from the existing well and have a four foot offset to ground water .  He has 
no issues with the proposed plan. 
 
After discussion and on motion by Jewell and second by  Fortin, the board unanimously approved the 
requested variances. All in favor. 
 
195 ROLLING ACRES 
 
Dan Proctor met with the board on behalf of the owner.  The septic system would most li kely be in 
failure due to high groundwater.    This is a large lot served by public water.  Because of the rainy 
summer, the soil was too wet to perc so a sieve analysis was used.  A Presby system is being proposed. 
 
Variances requested are offset to groun dwater from four feet to three feet, sieve analysis and inlet and 
outlet tees at less than 12 inches. 
 
Agent Jim Garreffi requested changes to the plan.  He requested that the back of the slope be stabilized 
by moving the tank downhill .  This would also a llow the tanks to move out of the groundwater.   
Provided the changes were made to the plan, he had no objections. 
 
After discussion and on motion by Watters and second by Jewell, the board voted to approve the 
variances, with the changes to the plan.  All in favor. 
 
186 WHITE STREET 
 
Kevin Ritchie met with the board on behalf of the  owner.  This system is in failure and a Presby 
system is being proposed.  The property is served by public water. 
 
===PAGE 2===
Variance being requested in accordance with DEP Presby approval is groundwater offset from 5 feet to 
3 feet. 
 
After discussion and on motion by Fortin and second by Watters, the board unanimously approved the 
request.  All in favor. 
 
61 UPLAND AVENUE 
 
Jim Moran met with the board on behalf of the owner.  The system is  in failure.  Soil testing was done 
on the site and there is no area where there is sufficient pervious soil (2+ feet) to design a system.  Title 
5 Regulations do not allow for a system design where the minimum cannot be met. 
 
Mr. Moran is proposing the use of a Tight Tank as the only alternative for a replacement system on this 
property. 
 
Agent Jim Garreffi said that after doing substantial testing on the site, there is no area to place a septic 
system.  The only alternative is a tight tank.  A tight tank requires the approval by the board as well as 
a deed notification and an operation and maintenance contract. 
 
After discussion and on motion by Fortin and second by Watters the request was approved.  All in 
favor. 
 
68 LAUREL LANE 
 
A housing inspection was performed at this address at the request of the tenant , which request was 
made on September 18, 2021 .  An Order to Correct was issued to Elizabeth McColligan, the owner of 
the property. 
 
Ms. McColligan requested a hearing before the board in accordance wit h the Order.  Ms. McColligan  
met with the board to discuss and request and extension of time to repair the issues noted on the Order.  
The Summary Process Agreemen t signed in Housing Court by the Tenants on Sept ember 17, 2021 
states that tenant will pay rent for the months of October, November and December and vacate the 
property on or before December 31, 2021.  Ms. McColligan is requesting an extension until after the 
tenants vacate to make the repairs with the exception of smoke detectors, which had been scheduled. 
 
One of the violations requires application for a variance to Housing Code for ceiling height, which was 
discussed at the meeting. 
 
Chairman George Emond told Ms. McColligan that the board understood her position and a letter 
requesting the variance would be sufficient and she would not need to appear before the board again. 
 
After discussion and on motion by Jewell and second by Fortin, an extension to complete repairs was 
given until January 21, 2022.  All in favor. 
 
COVID UPDATE 
 
Agent Jim Garreffi gave a brief Covid Update. 
 
===PAGE 3===

majority of the cluster cases in town.  A cluster is defined as two or more cases at a single address.   
 
Having no furt her matters before the board and on  Motion Jewell and seconded by  Watters, the 
meeting was adjourned at 8:10 pm, all in favor.

LUNENBURG BOARD OF HEALTH 
MEETING MINUTES 
Monday, November 1, 2021 
 
The Board of Health met at 7:00 pm at the Ritter Memorial Building  
 
Members Present:  George Emond, Perry Jewell, Evan Watters and Paul Fortin.    
 
MINUTES APPROVED 
 
398 SUNSET LANE 
 
Greg Roy met with the board on behalf of the owner.  A well variance is being requested.  This is a 
demolish and rebuild.  There is an existing shallow well.  What is being proposed is a new artisan well.  
Currently, the well is 16 feet from the property line.   What is being proposed is an artis an well 34 feet 
from the property line (shore line of the lake).  Variance requested is property line offset to well from 
50 feet to 34 feet. 
 
Jim Garreffi stated that the existing leach area will be used for the new hou se (3 bedroom installed in 
1985) and a small retaining wall is proposed between the leaching area and the driveway.  In order to 
maintain 100 feet from the leaching area and the proposed new  well, the well will be less tha n 50 feet 
from the property line (lake).  The granting of this variance will not affect any abutters.  The proposed 
well is an improvement over what is presently existing. 
 
After discussion and on motion by Jewell and second by Watters, the board unanimously approved the 
requested variance. All in favor. 
 
326 FLAT HILL ROAD 
 
Ken Matson, owner,  met with the board.  When the as -built was submitted for the well at the above 
location, it was drilled too close to the property line (less than 50 feet).  Mr. Matson’s explanation is 
that there was an error made as to where the property line was separating the two lots.  Mr. Matson 
owns both lots.  Mr. Matson stated that the location of the well does not have an effect on either lot.  
Mr. Matson is requesting an offset to property line for the well from 50 feet to 35 feet.   
 
 Agent Jim Garreffi stated that the lot is large and the well location should not affect abutting lots and 
does not see a problem with granting the requested variance. 
 
After discussion and on motion by Fortin and second by Watters, the board voted to approve the 
variance.  All in favor. 
 
909 NORTHFIELD ROAD 
 
James and Irene Rivers met with the board.  They are proposing an addition to their residence.  The 
addition, as proposed, will  exceed the total number of bedroom allows as we ll the total number of 
rooms allowed in the structure. 
 
===PAGE 2===
The board advised that the septic system on this property is in excess of 50 years old.  In addition, 
because of soil testing on the adjacent lot, it would appear that the system at this house would b e in the 
groundwater. 
 
Chairman George Edmond told the Mr. and Mrs. Rivers that the board could not further consider 
signing the building permit without a Title 5 Report being completed.  Should the report pass, they 
would still need to reduce the room cou nt to meet requirement and a deed restriction would need to be 
placed on the property. 
 
After discussion, Mr. & Mrs. Rivers advised that they would be discussing putting an addition on their 
house before moving forward. 
 
MARSHALL PARK 
 
Greg Roy met with the board to discuss the replacement septic system at Marshall Park.  Dave Passios 
was also present. 
 
Mr. Roy stated that testing had been completed at the park and the most suitable place found was in the 
location of the old bath house.  In designing a syst em, in the space available, he has determined that 
using a Presby System that would fit into the topography as well as the groundwater, a 1,000 gallon a 
day system can be designed.  A 1,000 gallon day system would support 200 people per day using the 
facilities. 
 
After discussion and a consensus of the board, Mr. Roy is moving forward with designing a 1,000 
gallon per day Presby System. 
 
68 LAUREL LANE 
 
A hearing on this matter was continued until after the first of the year. 
 
Having no further matters befo re the board and on  Motion Jewell and seconded by  Watters, the 
meeting was adjourned at 8:10 pm, all in favor.
