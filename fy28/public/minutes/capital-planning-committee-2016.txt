Attachment (1):
E Mail from John Londa re. Compliance to AHERA: Asbestos hazard Emergency Response Act.
The School Department must comply with federal AHERA regulations (Asbestos Hazard Emergency Response
Act) that became law in 1986. I am the school department Designated Person with respect to compliance. The
State Department of Labor has oversight on our compliance. The School department had to do an initial
AHERA inspection and subsequent re-inspections every three years. The last 3 year inspection was perform by
Cardno ATC, a liscenced asbestos planner in April 2013. We had a DOL compliance inspection in September
2012 that cited 21 items in a Notice of Non Compliance dated November 19, 2012. While most items were
citing failures of the Asbestos Planer (we had been using A-1 Spectrum, Inc since 1997), item 20 required that
we update the management plan with the consultant identify the resources to complete response actions
successfully and carry out re-inspection, operations and maintenance activity. The notice of non-compliance
spurred the change in asbestos planner. The re-inspection by Cardno ATC listed the removal as a high priority
removal of the spray on fireproofing. The Response Action Recommendation was "Remove spray-on fire
proofing as soon as economically feasible".
I have not seen any grant funding or other programs that support removal or remediation of asbestos in
schools in the 18 years I have been the Director of Facilities.
This spring we will have Cardno ATC conduct a re-inspection as required by AHERA and will have them update
projected cost for removal to match the experience we have had over the last two years with bidding this
work.

1. Call to Order: Jamie Toale called the meeting to order at 5:00pm.

3. Superintendent Loxi Calmes and Director of Facilities John Londa presented the Lunenburg Public
Schools FY2018 Capital Planning Requests.
A. Turkey Hill Elementary School Asbestos Abatement- Superintendent Calmes said the plan
would be for this project to be done in three phases - FY18 ($145,000), FY19($127,000)
and FY20 ($162,000). John Londa explained that the asbestos is mainly above the ceiling
tiles. Jamie Toale asked if the work was being done in multiple phases to spread out the
cost or because of time constraints. John Londa said that it was a bit of both but mainly
because of time constraints. Work needs to be done over the summer when school is not
in session and it would be a challenge to complete all the work in one summer. John Hen-
shaw asked if it could be split up further. John Londa explained that it could not as once
one area was started it needed to be completed as there was no way to stop mid way.
John Henshaw also asked about the urgency of completing it. John Londa responded that
it was tested 10 years ago and that the ceiling tiles installed around 1969 are very brittle.
Failure of the tiles would could cause asbestos exposure. The request for FY18 and FY19
would take care of the first floor and FY20 would take care of the second floor.
B. FY18 Technology requests - Replace teachers laptops $18,000 - 4 years old and out of
warranty. Replacement of server $6,000 - server is currently 5 years old and is being
dwarfed by their requirements. 30 replacement iPads $12,000 - current ones are 5 years
old and new operating systems won't install due to outdated equipment which is limiting
applications. Steve Maladrinos will provide further information when he presents his re-
quests.
C. Air Cooling System at Primary School - FY20 ($10,000) and FY21 ($300,000). Two large
air handlers with cooling coils are already on the roof. These two requests would be run-
===PAGE 2===
ning piping and installation of compressors. Jamie asked if this would be similar to the sys-
tem at the high school John Londa said they would be similar. John Henshaw asked if the
300k is something that could be spread over a number of years. John Londa explained that
he didn't think it could and that the biggest costs will be the condenser that is installed with
a crane that needs to be done in one shot.
D. Video Surveillance at Primary School ($52,370) and Turkey Hill Elementary School FY21
($53,000)- John Londa said one contractor to do both installations and would be cost ben-
eficial to have them down at the same time.
E. Roof replacement FY24 - John Londa explained that in the agreement with Nexamp, they
will remove the panels one time at no cost for roof maintenance. The roof replacement
would be done just before that agreement expires and the school district takes over own-
ership of the panels. John Londa said they would like to see the panels operate for at least
ten years after they take ownership so it would make sense to do the roof while Nexamp is
required to absorb the cost of removing and the reinstalling the panels. This would be a
project that may be eligible for MSBA assistance and the monetary projection is a worst
case scenario. He also said there may be other options other than complete roof replace-
ment and they will be investigate closer to FY24. John further explained that this would
need to fit into the long term plans for the building so things are fluid. In FY19 THES will be
50 years old and starting to push the envelope of typical systems.
