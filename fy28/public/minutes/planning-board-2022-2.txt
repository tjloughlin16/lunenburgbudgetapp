The Hearing  was to hear and review an application for a stormwater management permit under Chapter 204 of the Code of the Town of 
Lunenburg for six lots at 245 Holman Street . Applicant- Harbor Classic Homes, LLC, Leominster, MA.  Owner- Bray Ave 9, LLC, Leominster, 
MA.   
 
The applicant was represented by Brian Marchetti, McCarty Engineering.  This was a 10 -acre parcel on the corner of Holman and Northfield  
(aka 245 Holman).  The parcels had been designed for, and received approval from the Board of Health for septic systems.  Lots 1 and 5 would 
be served by a common driveway, as would Lots 3 and 4.  The applicant was meeting all the required MA DEP standards for stormwater 
management except for the peak flow rate.  Lunenburg’s bylaw required 60% total phosphorus (TP) removal and retention of runoff of 1.0 inch.  
Lot 5 was the only Lot that did not meet those requirements as the groundwater level was really high.  Even with rain gardens  the applicant 
could not attain a two foot separation to groundwater.  A waiver was requested for Lot 5 for the 60% TP removal.  A pea stone diaphragm was 
provided for Lot 5.  Water would sheet flow off the driveway, be treated through the pea stone diaphragm and continue to discharge to the west 
and north to an  existing drainage channel.  Lots A, 1, 2, 3 and 4 would each have a pea stone diaphragm and rain garden.   Peer review was 
provided by Graves Engineering, Inc. (GEI), Worcester, MA.   
 
M. Allison asked for the overages on not meeting the rates for Lot 5.  Two different points of analysis were used for the site.  There was no 
drainage infrastructure in either Northfield Road or Holman Street .  The majority of runoff from Lots A, 1 and 2 would flow to Northfield Road 
then to the drainage ditch at the southwes t.  Runoff from Lots 3, 4, and 5 would flow to a drainage swale to the north and then to a river.   T. 
Cole asked if there was any alternative to pea stone diaphragm and rain garden for Lot 5 to allow the applicant to meet the groundwater offset.  
B. Marchetti responded the only other solution would be to raise the entire site.  Of concern to the Board were the pre- and post- development 
peak runoff and the waiver request from the 60% treatment of TP on Lot 5.  Also of concern was the project’s proximity to Mulpus Brook 
causing a possible detrimental  effect to Hickory Hills.  B. Marchetti emphasized that it still met both DEP and Lunenburg stormwater 
regulations.  T. Cole remembered doing the ANR Plan and a common drive but did not recall if both common drive s were approved by the 
Board as neither split the lot line.  Both T. Cole and A. Burney recalled the Board approving the common drive for Lots 1 and 5.   Board 
members were interested in viewing the previously Board-endorsed ANR Plan.   
 
M. Allison made Motion to continue the hearing to June 13, 6:05 p.m., M. Brenner Seconded, Motion passed.   
Hearing recessed 7:51 p.m.

Section 8.4. and Chapter 204 respectively of the Code of the Town of Lunenburg  for construction of a 367,000 sq. ft. warehouse

lower than the underground storage system.  Any groundwater that was captured would be transmitted under the building to the 
opposite side of the site and follow the same general path as the original ditch.  Both requested changes were reviewed and

APPOINTMENT:  Karen Chapman, MRPC - With the assistance of MRPC, Lunenburg had applied for a grant through the state 
for compliance with MBTA Communitie s zoning requirements.  After input from rural communities, DHCD (Department of 
Housing and Community Development) made some change s to the compliance guidelines and issued final guidance in August.  
The requirement for 750 units no longer applies.  The De finitions also changed with the addition of “adjacent small town”, which 
is the category for Lunenburg.  Only 5% of the community’s housing count would have to be multi -family units; this would be 240 
units.  Lunenburg would have to zone for 240 units at 15 units per acre.  Up to 10% of the units could be affordable housing.  
(That would be superseded by Lunenburg’s inclusionary housing of 15 %.)  K. Chapman believed that if a community had a 40R 
District, which Lunenburg does, th en it could have up to 20  %.  The Action Plan deadline is January 31, 2023 with the District 
Compliance deadline being December 31, 2025.  MRPC w ould be trained on the compliance model.  There is also a draft model 
bylaw on the MHP (MassHousing Partnership) website.  The requirement could also be met through an overlay district.   
 
A. Burney felt that Lunenburg’s challenge would be the infrastructure outside of a sewered area that goes along with this density.
