3. Discussion with Tax Collector Regarding Tax Title Process  -  Myleen Mallari, Tax Collector, provided the 
Committee with an overview of the Tax Title Process.  Ms. Mallari explained that in accordance with MGL 
Chapter 60, if taxes are not paid within 14 days of demand, the Collector can then give 14 days’ notice of 
intention to exercise such power of taking.  In Lunenburg notice is published in the newspaper.  If taxes are 
not paid following notice, the notice of taking (lien) is recorded in the registry of deeds.    At that point in 
time the Tax Collector can then enter into an agreement with the property owner for payment.   
 
Six months after the taking, if the taxes are not paid, the Town can petition the land court to foreclose on 
the property.  The court then conducts a title examination and notifies all interested parties of the petition 
and provides 45 days to redeem or to challenge the legality of the taking.  If the taking is not redeemed or 
successfully challenged the Court will issue a decree which vests interest in the parcel to the Town. Notice 
of Foreclosure must be recorded. 
Once the decree/foreclosure has been recorded, the Town can then sell the property at auction or can sell 
a bundle of tax title properties to another entity through an RFP process within one year of the 
foreclosure.  The Town has the option of setting a reserve price and is not obligated to accept bids if they 
are not satisfactory.  If the Town wishes to retain the property, that can be announced at the auction and 
the auction for that parcel adjourned.   
The Committee questioned the status of numerous properties that are listed as “owners unknown”.  
Myleen responded that those parcels must be researched further and suggested that we reach out to the 
Assessors to get more information.   
Myleen informed the Committee that parcels classified as land of low value is a different process; the 
threshold is $15,000.  In the case of land of low value, the requested is submitted to the Commissioner of 
the Department of Revenue.   
Bob Pease asked if there is a manual that outlines the process, Myleen responded affirmatively and will 
investigate if the manual is available as public information.

3. Lake Shirley Improvement Corporation Discussion Regarding Parmenter Road Parcel (Out of Order) 
– Joanna Bilotta of the Lake Shirley Improvement Corporation (LSIC) explained that the LSIC is looking 
for a place to hold recreational lake events, they used to use Camp David and since it closed they have 
been looking for another venue.  Joanna became aware that the Open Space Committee was looking 
at the Parmenter Parcel owned by the Town and the LSIC would be interested in utilizing this property.  
Brandon explained that the Committee is in the process of looking at the entire list of Town owned 
general use properties to see if public use makes sense or if use by other Town Departments is  
appropriate.  He added that the Committee is looking at the Parmenter Parcel but has not yet put 
forth a recommendation.  Joanna said that the LSIC is looking for a beach for lake residents similar to 
the beach at Island Road on Hickory Hills Lake .  Currently the LSIC is paying $40,000 -50,0000 a year 
on treatments to maintain the lake and they would like to continue swimming lessons for children , 
which used to be held at Camp David.  Ana pointed out that although LSIC does pay for lake treatment 
the Town pays to maintain other aspects of the Lake and the only public access is through Shady Point 
which is very limited and individuals have to pay.  Sarah indicated that while she understands that the 
Corporation puts a lot of effort into the lake , access to the lake would be of interest to everyone in 
the Town.  Joanna explained that LSIC is looking for a parcel for use by the Association, not the public.   
Paula noted that Hickory Hills Lake is private land owned by Hickory Hills Landowners Inc. and all costs 
for maintenance are paid for by the residents.  Repairs to the Dam on Lake Shirley were funded by 
the State with the stipulation that there be public access, the only “access” currently is through Stump 
Cove which is not easily accessible.  Ownership of the Lake is unclear as there is a cloudy title.  Paula 
added that giving a town owned parcel to a private group is not an option without a public auction, 
she noted that public access would not exclude LSIC.  Brandon reiterated that the Committee is 
currently doing an inventory and coming up with recommendations.  
 
4. Chase Road and Northfield Road, Map 33, Parcel 6, and Map 38, Parcels 17 and 24.2, Agricultural 
Preservation Restriction (APR) Update – Brandon explained that Pierce Tree Farm is looking to put 
an Agricultural Preservation Restriction on this parcel at Spring Town Meeting.  This would be a 
permanent restriction and the land would remain in private ownership.  The APR would be sold to the 
State and then ownership would be transferred to Dick Violette  of Dicks Market Garden.  The APR 
program provides the bulk of the funding for the parcel which is approximately 99 acres.  Cost of the

Lynne Man seconded.   Motion passed unanimously by roll call vote. 
 
3. Presentation from Whalom residents regarding conservation of general municipal lands at 671 West Street and 225 
Pleasant Street –  David Prokowiew and Eric Hinds presented a PowerPoint presentation to the Committee which is 
attached and made part of these minutes.   Brandon Kibbe explained that these 2 parcels of land are some of the 
largest town owned parcels in a heavily populated area.  The Open Space plan identifies the need for equity of access 
to recreational open space in all areas of the Town.   
The West Street parcel is approx. 40 acres of wetland and woodland with a pond.  There are several existing trails on 
this parcel.  Currently there is adequate room for a parking area for 3 or more vehicles at the trail head.  This area 
could be expanded with the removal of dead trees.  There are several rustic crossings (planks, tires etc.) on the 
property that need updating. Approximately 10 years ago some abutters were able to purchase a portion of this parcel 
to add to their existing lots.  There are 2 possible access points, 1 at Pratt Street and the other at Pleasant St. Ext.  Bob 
Pease noted it will be important to ensure access before transferring responsibility.  The area identified in the 
presentation as “The Western Loop” is actively traveled by snowmobiles, mountain bikes and four wheelers.  There is 
one certified vernal pool in the area of West Street, it is unclear as to whether or not it is on the Town owned property.   
The Pleasant Street parcel is 66.55 acres and abuts the solar field.  Approximated 40 acres of the property is fields.  
According to Mr. Prokowiew, the owner of the solar fields is amenable to discussing the creation of trails to allow 
connectivity to the West Street Parcel.  This parcel lends itself to rectangular fields, identified as a need in the open 
space plan.  A portion of this property is currently hayed.  Mr. Prokwiew noted that since the pandemic many walkers 
are utilizing this land and the West Street parcel.   
Eric and Dave indicated they would be willing to provide labor to clean up the properties and enhance access to the 
land. Bob Pease noted that the residents need to take an active role in transferring property to other departments 
and applauded Dave & Erics efforts.  Brandon Kibbe stated that first the Committee should reach out to the Selectmen 
and then to other departments to determine if the parcels would fill a need.  If no other department sees an active 
need the recommendation would be to place these parcels under Conservation or Parks, or a combination thereof.   
Paula Bertram made a motion to reach out to the Board of Selectmen through the Town Manager, the Planning 
Director and the various committees represented on the open space committee to consider these 2 parcels for open 
space and recreation, committee members will report back to the Open Space Committee at the next meeting.  
Seconded by Bob Pease.  Passed unanimously by roll call vote. 
4. Update on general municipal lands at 860 Reservoir Road/725 Goodrich Street/0 Shirley Reservoir Road –  Paula 
informed the Committee that she has been researching town owned land on Lake Shirley.  860 Reservoir Road is 
clearly owned by the Town as it went through the taking process, the tax title process was begun in 1970 on the 725 
Goodrich Street parcel however it does not appear it was completed.  Previous ownership of several of the adjacent 
“owner unknown” parcels is unclear.  Paula will do further research and report back to the Committee.

5. Revision of Land Use Plans for Lane & Table Rock properties – Bob Pease explained the Conservation Commission 
was looking at updating the land use policies on conservation parcels it was discovered that both these parcels indicate 
that bow hunting is only allowed by special permit.  Bow Hunting is allowed by right on all other conservation property.  
The Commission will seek to revise this policy in the future which will require a public hearing. 
 
6. Questions for Town Clerk about minutes and town meeting actions – Bob Pease told the Committee that he will be 
meeting with the Town Clerk to research Town meeting actions on parcels listed as general municipal land.  Brandon 
requested that Bob look for the actual town meeting vote on the Woodruff parcel and the Town Meeting actions for 
225 Pleasant Street.   
 
7. Future Meeting with Assessor - Bob noted that several of the parcels currently identified as owner unknown could be 
taken as land of low value and he will be meeting with the Assessor to discuss.    The Committee will continue the 
process of reviewing and prioritizing parcels.  
 
8. MassGIS Training  – Lynne Man has signed up for the MassGIS training session and requested that any member has 
specific questions that they would like to ask, please email them to her. 
 
9. Committee administration (terms, membership) – Brandon said that he reached out to Adam Burney and currently 
there are not terms for open space members.  Some of the committee members are appointed by their various 
commissions/committees/boards (i.e. Planning Board, Conservation, Parks).  Current at large members are Brandon, 
Mike, Lynne and Sarah.   Sarah questioned if we want to establish a more formal process with terms, it may be prudent 
to see if other people would like to serve.  Ana noted that the Board of Selectmen have been discussing the nominating 
and reappointment process.  Paula commented that she believes continuity is important.  Brandon informed the 
Committee that Eric Hinds has submitted a talent bank expressing interest in servicing on Open Space.  Bob noted that 
the Committee would welcome Eric’s input as a non-voting member. 
 
10. Adjourn – Paula made a motion to adjourn at 8:34 p.m., seconded by Lynne motion passed unanimously by roll call 
vote. 
 
Respectfully submitted, 
 
Paula Bertram
