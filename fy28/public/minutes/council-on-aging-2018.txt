Lunenburg Council on Aging                                             
Meeting Minutes  
June 12, 2018 Annual Meeting 
 
CALL TO ORDER: 9:30 a.m. 
 
Present:  Chairperson Pete Lincoln, Director Sue Doherty, Cheryl Moisan, Betty DiGiacomo, Diane Nowd, Jane Rabbitt, 
Jacquelyn Dwyer, Deb Lincoln, Kevin McNally, Judy Tarbell, Sarah Grant 
 
Absent: Brian Guenard   
 
GUESTS:  Board of Selectman liaison Jamie Toale 
 
ANNOUNCEMENTS:  Mr. Lincoln congratulated the board for having six members with perfect attendance this year, and 
also commented that there are currently no Talent Bank forms on file at Town Hall that indicate interest in volunteering 
on the Lunenburg Council on Aging. 
 
Mr. Toale thanked the board for the opportunity to serve as liaison between the Board of Selectmen and the Council on 
Aging, and asked for feedback to bring to the Selectmen in order to assist in clearly defining the role of a liaison, as well 
as attracting more people to volunteer, training of volunteers, and improving communication between boards and 
committees and the Board of Selectmen. Mr. Toale explained that he thinks the relationship between the COA and the 
Selectmen is a good one, and can serve as an example for the rest of town to follow.  Mr. Lincoln pointed out that the 
skill sets of the different boards in town vary, and inquired as to how to advertise what a member of the COA actually 
does. Mr. Toale responded that a common place to find out about the various boards and committees in town is not 
available currently.  
The COA, as a whole, welcomed Mr. Toale to come back and serve as liaison for the upcoming fiscal year. Ms. Lincoln 
expressed her gratitude to Mr. Toale, and pointed out that not all boards and committees in town take advantage 
having a liaison to the Board of Selectmen. 
 
Ms. Lincoln announced that she attended an Eagle House Supporters Inc. meeting as the COA’s liaison, and while going 
over the minutes from their previous meeting, one member suggested that Ms. Lincoln should’ve been given permission 
to speak. Ms. Lincoln asked the COA to support her by making a request that the Eagle House Supporters Inc. include on 
their agenda a place for a COA liaison report. Mr. Lincoln commented that he thought the Supporters and the COA were 
on the same team. Mr. McNally pointed out that if the Supporters’ liaison attended the COA meetings regularly they 
might realize the two groups are on the same page. Mr. McNally suggested inviting anyone who has issues to a COA 
meeting. Ms. Doherty pointed out that issues between COAs and supporter groups are not unique to Lunenburg. The 
annual meeting of the Eagle House Supporters is not well attended, according to Ms. Doherty. Ms. Moisan commented 
that the Lunenburg Friendly Seniors’ monthly meeting is always well attended because their members see what their 
dues pay for at each meeting.  Ms. Doherty said that the Eagle House Supporters sponsor most of the programs at the 
Eagle House, and they’re recognized on posters, in press releases, and during the programs they sponsor. 
 
MINUTES APPROVAL:  Ms. DiGiacomo moved to approve the minutes from the May 14, 2018 meeting, Ms. Dwyer 
seconded. On vote, motion carried unanimously. 
 
CURRENT BUSINESS:  
1. Director’s Report:  
 
 Patron Complaint about Senior Center Scheduling:  A Stitch and Chat participant has expressed that 
she feels Stitch and Chat is getting bumped too often between programs that take up the room 
where Stitch and Chat usually meets, and Monday holidays. Ms. Doherty commented that programs 
are always shifting at the Eagle House. Stretch and Tone gets canceled once a month because of the 
Pancake Breakfast, Book Club meets on the Tuesday following any Monday holiday that falls in the 
third week of the month. On Some Tuesdays even Ms. Doherty gives up her own office for the grief

support councilor that comes once a month. Ms. Grant, who attends Stitch and Chat regularly,

schedule and urged her to bring it up to the COA. Mr. Lincoln determined the person has a personal 
problem and the board agreed they would support Ms. Doherty however she decides to deal with

 Meal Site Manager Position: Ms. Doherty reported that three people applied for the position, and 
Meals on Wheels Driver Sandra Ricci was hired. Kitchen Aide Judith Alario will fill in until July 1, 
2018. 
 Formula Grant: The grant money for FY 18 has all been spent, according to Ms. Doherty. Any 
mileage money not collected by Meals on Wheels drivers will be turned back to the town. The 
Formula Grant for FY 19 has been projected for $11.00 per elder, which is still a raise from last year’s 
grant; however, the budget has not been signed by Governor Charlie Baker, so that number could 
still change. Ms. Doherty is ready for three likely scenarios where funding is concerned, and also 
pointed out that two meal site assistants are paid out of the Formula Grant.  
 State Capital Plan 25K designated to Eagle House Senior Community Center for HVAC Repair:  
Senator Dean Tran advocated for the Eagle House and designated $25,000 to repair the HVAC 
system in the older part of the building.

Jacquelyn Dwyer as Vice Chairperson for a term of one year.  Ms. Grant seconded. On vote, motion 
carried unanimously.  “We are all chairmen on this committee. Everyone participates,” Mr. Lincoln 
said. 
 Recommendation to Board of Selectmen to reappoint Deb Lincoln and Betty DiGiacomo: Ms. 
Doherty reported that the Selectmen already reappointed Ms. Lincoln and Ms. DiGiacomo to the 
COA. Mr. Toale commented that the Selectmen are working toward improving the reappointment 
process. 
 Bridges Together: Ms. Nowd reported that the program was successful, and that the fifth graders 
involved now have a whole new perspective of “seniors.”  When asked about what it means to be 
old, the kids said nothing of wrinkles or slowing down, but talked about being wise and having 
experience instead. Ms. Nowd said after hearing about the program, it’s likely more people will 
volunteer for the fall session. The goal is to get enough volunteers to span the entire fifth grade class 
at Turkey Hill Elementary School.  
 Tri Town Landing Proposed Senior Housing: Ms. Doherty said the Tri-Town senior housing project 
was recently approved by the Lunenburg Planning Board after appeasing abutters who were 
concerned about the height of the buildings. Mr. Lincoln suggested members of the COA go to the 
complex when it’s finished so new residents can learn about the Eagle House, and what the COA has 
to offer. 
 Age/Dementia Friendly Community: Ms. Doherty and Ms. Rabbitt attended a seminar on Dementia 
Friendly Communities, which featured a speaker who has dementia. The speaker talked about how 
welcomed she felt, at 53 years old, to a senior center exercise class. Ms. Doherty plans to attend a 
“Percolator Meeting” in Waltham about Memory Cafes. Ms. Doherty said she also spoke with the 
Lunenburg Business Association about Lunenburg becoming a Dementia Friendly Community at the 
Arts Festival in Lunenburg over the weekend. Ms. Doherty also reported that Emily Kearns 
coordinator for Dementia Friendly Massachusetts would attend a Department Head meeting to talk 
about prepping the community to become Dementia Friendly. 
 SNAP: Ms. Doherty reported she received $71.00 from the first quarter, and hasn’t heard back 
about second or third quarter reports. Ms. Doherty expressed her frustration due to the lack of 
communication from UMass, and is considering leaving the UMass program. The Eagle House will 
still be able to provide assistance with SNAP applications, as anyone in the general public can access 
the SNAP website and apply on their own.  
 MART: No report 
 Building Report: The side door of the main entrance now has an accessible push-button door 
opener. Ms. Doherty purchased new porch furniture with Formula Grant funds. Landscapers,

BOARD COMMENT:  None 
 
PUBLIC COMMENT: None

Lunenburg Council on Aging                                             
Meeting Minutes  
August 14, 2018 
 
CALL TO ORDER: 9:30 a.m. 
 
Present:  Chairperson Pete Lincoln, Director Sue Doherty, Cheryl Moisan, Betty DiGiacomo, Diane Nowd, Jane Rabbitt, 
Deb Lincoln, Kevin McNally, Judy Tarbell, and Sarah Grant. 
 
Absent:  Jacquelyn Dwyer 
 
GUESTS:  Selectman Jamie Toale, Eagle House Supporters Liaison Marie Burchfield, prospective COA board member 
Barbara Brown, prospective COA board member David Dorren 
 
ANNOUNCEMENTS: Mr. Lincoln informed the board that Eagle House Supporters Board Member Ron Sinclair passed 
away, as well as Helen Fortier, who was an avid BINGO player at the Eagle House.  
 
MINUTES APPROVAL:  Ms. DiGiacomo motioned to approve the minutes from the June 12, 2018 meeting, Ms. Grant 
seconded. On vote, motion carried unanimously. 
 
CURRENT BUSINESS:  
 Board Member Vacancy: Barbara Brown and David Dorren were invited to the meeting after they submitted 
Talent Bank Forms to Town Hall. Ms. Brown told the board she is a former member of the COA, and left the 
board some years ago, when her term expired. Ms. Brown said that she’s lived in Lunenburg for fifty years, and 
is looking to bring more younger seniors into the building. Mr. Dorren said he’s lived in Lunenburg since 1996, 
and started getting involved in the community in 2011. His wife was ill, and she passed away in 2017, and it was 
in that time he realized that the senior population needs to be aware of the services available to them. Mr. 
Dorren said he learned a lot about how insurance companies work when dealing with his wife’s illness, and 
wants to learn the true needs of Lunenburg seniors, and wants to be sure they are not forgotten. Mr. Lincoln 
informed both candidates that the current board has been together for four years, and they work very well 
together, and also talked about other volunteer opportunities such as the Eagle House Supporters, and the 
Friendly Visitor Program.  
 
 Director’s Report:  
o FY18 End of Year Director’s Report: Ms. Doherty explained that she is working on compiling the 
FY18 end of year numbers for the Executive Office of Elder Affairs. The end of year report to the 
Board of Selectmen went well, and is looking forward to going in front of them for a mid-term 
report, and at the end of the year as well.  
o COA Volunteer Recognition Cookout/Stillman Fair: The police and fire departments have 
volunteered to cook hamburgers and hot dogs on the grill for the volunteer cookout. Ms. Lincoln 
commented that she was at the Pepperell Senior Center and found out that they have different 
awards recognizing specific attributes for their volunteers, such as longest time volunteering, the 
youngest volunteer, etc.  Ms. Doherty said she invited State Senator Dean Tran, and he RSVP’d he’d 
attend. Mr. Toale offered to do whatever the COA needed for the event. Mr. Lincoln encouraged all 
to attend. Ms. Doherty explained that she will go to the Stillman Fair site the night before to set up 
the booth. Ms. DiGiacomo, Ms. Tarbell, and Ms. Burchfield offered to tend the booth at the fair. Ms. 
Rabbitt, and Ms. Moisan were unsure whether they’d be available, Mr. Lincoln and Ms. Lincoln 
figured they could probably attend. 
o Meal Site Manager Position: Ms. Doherty reported that Ms. Ricci is doing well in her new position. 
The Meal Site Manager position is funded through the municipal budget, while the kitchen aides are 
now all funded through formula grant funding.  
o Formula Grant: Ms. Doherty reported that Governor Baker has signed the Commonwealth’s budget 
at $12.00 per elder; therefore, she does not expect a raise in those funds any time soon. Ms.

Doherty said she will use those funds for programs geared toward people over 60 years old. Ms. 
Doherty brought up the outdoor pickle ball program run by Sandy Laserte. Some funding will go

CORI checks are completed for those running the program, and documentation of attendance for 
those over 60. Ms. Doherty said she would also like to use Formula Grant funds toward an instructor

who want to apply.

Board of Selectmen at their August 14, 2018 meeting.  
o Building Report: The HVAC in the newer section of the building leaked into the computer room, as 
the technicians who serviced the unit stripped the fittings on one of the pipes. The painter sent to 
repair the damage to the ceiling will also touch up the damage left by ice dams in the foyer. Ms. 
Doherty also reported that the wheel chair ramp from the upper parking lot will be repainted by 
United Way volunteers on the Day of Caring, which is September 21, 2018. 
o Upcoming Events: Ms. Doherty talked about the upcoming events scheduled at the Eagle House. 
 
EAGLE HOUSE SUPPORTERS REPORT:  Ms. Burchfield introduced herself as the new liaison between the Supporters and 
the COA, and invited everyone to come to the Supporters’ Annual Meeting on September 7, 2018 at 9:30 a.m. Ms. 
Burchfield reported that the Supporters have gained a new member, Gaynor Grieci, and have another vacant seat due to 
the passing of Ron Sinclair. Ms. Burchfield said she strongly supports Ms. Doherty in all her endeavors, and will 
cooperate in every way possible to be sure that the goals for the Eagle House are met. 
 
NOTICES AND COMMUNICATIONS:  none 
 
BOARD COMMENT: Ms. Tarbell said she has heard many people say they have a hard time finding the Eagle House. 
Other members agreed that the signs on the road directing people are very small. Ms. Doherty said she’d speak to the

PUBLIC COMMENT: Mr. Toale thanked COA members who attended the Selectmen meeting. He explained that the role 
of liaison will probably change in the future, but that he would come to any meeting the board wants him to attend.

unanimously.

Lunenburg Council on Aging                                             
Meeting Minutes  
September 11, 2018 
 
CALL TO ORDER: 9:30 a.m. 
 
Present:  Chairperson Pete Lincoln, Director Sue Doherty, Cheryl Moisan, Betty DiGiacomo, Diane Nowd, Jane Rabbitt, 
Jacquelyn Dwyer, Deb Lincoln, Kevin McNally, Judy Tarbell, Sarah Grant, Barbara Brown 
Absent:   
 
GUESTS:  Selectmen Jamie Toale, Eagle House Supporters Liaison Marie Burchfield 
 
ANNOUNCEMENTS:  None  
 
MINUTES APPROVAL:  Ms. DiGiacomo motioned to approve the minutes from the August 16, 2018 meeting of the COA, 
Ms. Dwyer seconded. On vote, motion carried unanimously. Ms. DiGiacomo motioned to approve the minutes of the 
August 14, 2018 meeting of the COA, Ms. Grant seconded. On vote, motion carried unanimously. 
 
CURRENT BUSINESS:  
 Board Member Vacancy: Ms. Brown has been sworn in at Town Hall. A brief introduction among board 
members took place. 
 Director Performance Evaluation Review from Town Manager: Ms. Doherty received a glowing evaluation from 
Town Manager Heather Lemieux, where on a scale of one to five, one being the worst, and five being best, 
received all fives in all eleven categories. Mr. Lincoln commented that for creativity and initiative, Ms. Doherty, 
“should be a six.” 
 
1. Director’s Report:  
 Formula Grant: Ms. Doherty reported she still hasn’t received an official form from the Controller’s 
office at the Executive Office of Elder Affairs, but the grant will be for about $25,000, which is $12.00 
per elder. Ms. Doherty plans to use the funds for the two kitchen assistants, as well as for 
independent contractors for various activities at the Eagle House, such as a coordinator for the 
Memory Café, and for a Pickle Ball instructor, and Zumba instructor.  
 State Capital Plan $25,000 designated for Eagle House HVAC: Ms. Doherty reported she discussed 
the funds with the town manager, but it is unclear when the money will be received as it is bonded, 
and a request needs to be made.  There is a possibility the funds for repair could come from the 
town’s budget, which would be reimbursed when the bond is released. 
 Age/Dementia Friendly Community: Ms. Doherty reported that Pam MacLeod from Elder Affairs 
came to a Department Head meeting and talked about dementia friendly communities to the group.  
Ms. Doherty will go before the Board of Selectmen on September 21, 2018, to talk about Lunenburg 
becoming a “Dementia Friendly Community.” Ms. Doherty is still putting together a focus group, 
which so far includes: Selectman Bob Ebersole, Reverend Ernest Farrah, Pastor from United Parish of 
Lunenburg, Daron Massey, who chairs the Lunenburg Business Association, and Keith Lanzilotti, who 
is the coordinator for the Eagle House Memory Café, and also runs a home care business.  Ms. 
Dwyer volunteered to sit in.  Ms. Doherty said she plans to attend meetings at the Worcester Senior 
Center every other month to assist in the expansion of services and resources available for Memory 
Cafes in the Worcester County area.

Lincoln suggested the seniors assign the homework, as he was unsure whether the teachers were 
assigning it. Mr. Lincoln asked whether the students had a choice in participating in the program, 
and opined that they should have a choice. Mr. Lincoln’s fifth concern was the lack of a closing 
meeting.  Ms. Roy commented that the point of the program is to bring children and seniors 
together to build relationships, and that the curriculum is just the starting point, and also added that 
students would not be graded on their participation. Ms. Roy also agreed that everyone involved
