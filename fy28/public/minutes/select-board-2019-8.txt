Meeting with Lake Shirley Improvement Corporation's Aquatic Ecologist re: new Notice of Intent
filing and approval from Board of Selectmen to file Notice of Intent application
Joanna Bilotta, 362 Sunset Lane, Lake Shirley Improvement Corp. (LSIC), presented Wendy Gendron,
Aquatic Ecologist, of Aquatic Restoration Consulting, LLC, who prepared the Notice of Intent (NOI) for
Lake Shirley.
Ms. Gendron stated the NOI was for control of aggressive aquatic plants on Lake Shirley through the
use of winter water drawdown and herbicide/algaecide applications. They have been using this
technique for several years now.
Mr. Alonzo noted the Town, by lack of ability to determine otherwise, is the owner of Lake Shirley, and
LSIC, acting on behalf of residents living on Lake Shirley, are requesting permission to file the NOI
with the Conservation Commission. Our role is not evaluating the science of it but to see if it is in order
and can be forwarded to the Conservation Commission.
Mr. Alonzo stated on August 27, Ms. Lemieux reached out to Ms. Bilotta reminding her that the NOI
needed to be filed and with the Conservation Commission identified seven bullet items.
That the NOI should include:
• Water quality tracking data
• Weed control thresholds and targets
• Drawdown goals and planning
• Plan to address native weed decline
• Plans for fanwort
• Alternatives to chemical treatments
• Long range goals
He asked Ms. Gendron, "Do you believe those seven items were addressed in this NOI?"
Ms. Gendron responded to each item:
• Water quality tracking data-yes
• Weed control thresholds and targets- not actual numbers but targeting certain growths, yes, she
did not believe there was a number on x%
• Drawdown goals and planning- there is a drawdown depth and data from past years, which
would be followed in the future
• Plan to address native weed decline - there are zones set aside as no treatment areas to
preserve native plants
• Plans for fanwort- specifically there is a potential application for it on a case by case basis if
funds allow

"Please review the following concerns about the recently received draft of the Lake Shirley Notice of
Intent. In my opinion, this puts us back at square one. The application does not contain any of the
current conditions or concerns laid out by the commission. My concern in the BOS endorsing this
application in its current form will place the commission back having to dictate the scope and breadth
of the proposed activities. This will put the LSIC back on having to defend and argue every point the
commission puts forth. This application has no specific descriptions of any of the activities requested.
No goals. No plan. No research for alternatives. No water quality testing
There is no proposed review by the commission prior to the use of pesticides, no public notice, no
data collection, and no criteria on where, when or how to apply pesticides into the waters of Lake
Shirley. There is nothing in the application to support the use of a drawdown.
There is no description of the process to be followed. There is no method to determine if this
activity is successful. The narrative makes several assumptions based on a 2018 WRS report. The
WRS report should be included, at least the conclusions. Many of the excerpts from the report are quoted
as arguments against the concerns of the commission. The full report needs to be reviewed for context.
There is no mention of several concerns that the commission has, Fanwort, Fishkill, Drawdown and it
only argues "against" plant species concerns. It argues against pesticide residue using only the least
treated year totals.
There should be a discussion between the Board of Selectmen (Town) and the agent (LSIC) prior to
this application being submitted to determine what the overall goals are for Lake Shirley.
This application puts the commission right back where it all started. The commission should not be
having to determine the scope of this activity. To view in a very general manner, this application is to
perform specific activities to manage Lake Shirley. It needs to include,
1. The current conditions and what is wrong with these conditions
2. What the target conditions should be
3. The specific activity requested and how it will achieve the target conditions
4. Mechanism to measure the activities to prove they are successful

1. Town Manager Performance Evaluation Procedure
Mr. Alonzo presented in writing what had been discussed at a previous meeting. He read aloud the
eleven steps of the procedure:
1. Each member of the Selectboard completes the Selectboard-approved Town Manager Evaluation Form.
2. Each member can, at the individual member's discretion, obtain feedback by speaking with individuals who
work for and/or with the Town Manager to be used when completing the Organizational Leadership and
Personnel Management segment of the evaluation instrument.
===PAGE 8===
Manager.
5. Once all the individual Selectboard members have met with the Town Manager, the completed evaluation
forms will be submitted to the Chair of the Selectboard for the purpose of writing the composite evaluation.
6. At an open meeting of the Selectboard, at which the Town Manager's evaluation is on the agenda, the
completed composite Town Manager Evaluation Form will be distributed to the entire Selectboard, the
Town Manager and entered into the public record.
7. The period between the release of the composite Town Manager Evaluation Form into the public record and
the discussion of said evaluation by the Selectboard at a subsequent, open Selectboard meeting, the
Selectboard members may review, in a controlled environment, the other members' individual evaluation
forms for the purpose of ascertaining whether the composite evaluation fairly and adequately reflects the
viewpoints of all the individual evaluations.
8. At no time are the individual evaluations to be entered into the public record either by having it read, in total
or in part, into the record OR by direct reference at an open meeting of the Selectboard.
9. At a later open meeting of the Selectboard, at which the Town Manager's evaluation is again on the agenda,
the composite Town Manager Evaluation Form will be discussed, modified (if necessary) and its acceptance
voted upon by the entire Selectboard.
10. Once approved, the composite evaluation will be discussed with the Town Manager and the Selectboard
members at an open meeting.
11. The approved composite evaluation will be placed in the Town Manager's personnel file and be part of the
public record.
