PUBLIC COMMENT:
Mr. Alonzo thanked the state delegation; Representative Sena, Representative Kushmerek and
Senator Cronin for a $25,000 check presentation ceremony at the Primary School for public
safety and fire alarm upgrades for that building. They also announced the state budget has
secured $50,000 for materials abatement at the old Primary School.
He thanked everyone who came out for the Salvation Army tollbooth fundraiser last Saturday.
The generosity was incredible, we were able to raise $6,500 during the challenge; enough to
provide over 800 children and 1200 families in Lunenburg, Leominster and Fitchburg with
toys, clothes and food this holiday season.

concerns about ratifying the appointment of Rob Thompson as Recreation Director later this

appointment should be put on hold until they are certain they have a way for things to be
successful.
Terri Burchfield, 1 Rose Garden Lane, via ZOOM, in regards to the alcohol violation hearing
for On the Rocks this evening, had concerns about issues that have been going on at the bar.
One issue, predating Covid-19 there was excessive noise on the outside patio, especially on
the weekends. She requested that as part of the licensing process this be addressed.
Ms. Lemieux announced the Nashoba Associated Boards of Health will be holding a booster
vaccine clinic on January 5* from 1- 5 p.m. at St. Anthony's Church Hall at 33 Chapel St. in
Shirley. Town buildings will be closed this Friday due to observance of Christmas Eve and
Monday in observance of Christmas Day, as well as Friday, December 31s' in observance of
New Year's Day.

2:05 PM All Alcohol License Violation Hearing for JK Waterfront dba On the Rocks, 96
Lakefront Ave.
Mr. Alonzo noted the licensee/owner was not present. Chief Gammel reported they were served
notice of the hearing by hand delivery on December 13'h and notified by telephone. Mr. Alonzo
announced this was a hearing and anyone testifying must be sworn in. The order of the
proceedings will be as follows; we will hear from the complainant (the Town), the Town will
present its testimony and any evidence they have and then the license holder will present their
own testimony. The board can ask questions of either side and then we will deliberate. Following
that we will make a finding and depending on what the finding is, that no violation occurred or if
we find that one did, we may issue a penalty. The notice of the finding is sent by certified,
receipt requested mail to the licensee. He requested anyone who is going to testify to please
stand, raise their right hand and swear that all the testimony they will be presenting is the truth
and nothing but the truth, so help you God. Voices were heard stating, "I do."
Chief Gammel, acting as the Licensing Authority Agent, presented a brief summary of what
occurred over the Thanksgiving Day weekend at On the Rocks, 96 Lakefront Avenue. The first
incident was on Wednesday, November 24' that involved overcrowding at the bar and the
second incident was on Friday, November 26 that involved an assault and battery that occurred
inside the bar and then outside the bar.
Officer Broc was on patrol on November 24' at approximately 9:14 pm when he noticed there
were a lot of vehicles in front of On the Rocks. He walked up to the door, opened it and found
the bar was overcrowded by his estimation of 200 people. He announced he was there and
instructed the two bartenders to shut down the bar, turn up the lights and had people start
leaving. He called for a Fire Captain to come down and assist him and count the occupancy at
the bar which is rated for 99 people. At that time they counted 137 people in the bar. By his
estimations about 100 patrons had left before the Fire Captain arrived. When he observed the
employees, there were only two bartenders with no manager on site nor anyone at the door
checking IDs, or checking counts at the bar. The owner, Ken Ricker, was notified and requested
to respond at the bar. He readily admitted he was caught off guard as there was social media that
was blasted by patrons and they ended up all coming to the bar. He appreciated Officer Broc
shutting down and cleaning out the bar. They began a count and allowed people back in to about
90 patrons when proper staff was appropriately situated. All the incident activity from that night
was recorded by body camera one camera. Gammel had provided the video for viewing. When
you watch the footage the bar was extremely crowded. It presented a very dangerous situation; if
there was some sort of disturbance in the bar, there was no way it was going to be controlled.
The second concern was if there was a fire or an emergency in the bar there, it would have been
hard to get to.
The second incident occurred on Friday the 26 of that week. We were called for the report of a
man lying on the sidewalk who had just been involved in a fight. When Officer Cappucci arrived
he found the victim lying on the sidewalk and he had been involved in a fight in the bar and then
again outside. The fight that occurred outside originated from patrons that were originally inside.
When they were outside another incident occurred. Two combatants started fighting and four
===PAGE 3===
other people in a car jumped out, picked up the victim and body slammed him to the ground. We
reviewed the video and a fight did occur inside the bar. The doorman immediately broke it up

incident could have been prevented. It appears that when the staff put them outside that was the
end of it and ended their responsibility. With our rules and regulations set forth by the Select
Board, that is not true. They have a responsibility for anything that happens outside the bar and

I did make a note in the report to the Select Board that there have been several incidents and

Meeting with Town Counsel Regarding Discussion on Carrying Firearms on Town
Property and Hunting on Conservation Land
Attorney Costa and Conservation Commissioners Bob Pease and Richard Bursch were also
present for this discussion. Mr. Alonzo referred to town counsel's opinion on the RIGHT TO
POSSESS FIREARMS ON TOWN-OWNED PROPERTY.
Attorney Costa summarized his written opinion.
"Lunenburg would likely be within its rights to adopt a restriction on or prohibition of the possession
of firearms on certain properties owned by the Town. (I note the Town has already adopted a law
regarding the discharge of firearms: General Code, § 145-1, providing that "Inlo person shall fire or
discharge any firearms or explosives of any kind within the limits of any highway, park or other
public property, except with the consent of the Board of Selectmen... ") Any ban on firearm
possession should expressly exempt law enforcement officers engaged in the performance of their
official duties, as is probably effected anyway by G.L. c. 41, $ 98 ("police officers of all cities and
towns may carry within the commonwealth such weapons as the chief of police or the board or
officer having control of the police in a city or town shall determine.")
===PAGE 6===
If land has been conveyed to the Town
"in the care, custody and control of the Conservation
Commission," the Commission may regulate its use consistent with any Town laws, bylaws, rules and
regulations of general applicability. At present, and to my knowledge, a restriction on or prohibition
of firearms would not run afoul of any Town Bylaw or other rule, regulation or policy.
Lands in the care, custody and control of public bodies other than the Conservation Commission
(and School Committee), e.g. the Board of Park Commissioners, present a quandary of sorts,
regarding jurisdiction and the authority to regulate activities thereon, e.g. possession of firearms.
That is because, on the one hand, G.L. c. 40, § 3, quoted above, applies, as does, for example, the
Park Commissioners' authority, per the Charter, to "make rules for the... use and government" of
public parks. See Town Charter, § 3-9(b). On the other hand, however, the Charter vests you, as
Town Manager, with "full jurisdiction over the rental and use of all town facilities and property
except property under the control of the school committee and the conservation commission." See
Town Charter, § 4-2(1). It is difficult to reconcile these Charter provisions, especially in the context
of the underlying statutory provision. I believe that the Park Commissioners' policymaking authority
probably includes the right to dictate permitted and prohibited use(s) of park lands (again consistent
with any laws of general applicability; per the Town Charter, § 3- 9(b), the role of the Town
Manager is more about "day-to-day care and maintenance." But I am unprepared to say so with
absolute certainty, as the language lends itself to multiple interpretations."
