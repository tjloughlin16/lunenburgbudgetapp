Shirley Road.  – Greg Roy (Dillis & Roy) – shared a Google Earth image of the site with the existing 
well and pump house. He stated he was contacted to do a site plan per the Commission’s requests and 
they sent a wetland’s specialist to delineate the wetlands. They identified 7 flags (A1-A7) and noted there 
is a large bordering vegetated wetland between Lake Shirley, the train tracks and the well site. An erosion 
barrier has been constructed along the brush line and no vegetation will be taken down.  The proposed 
addition will be outside of the 50’, except for 6’ area for tying into the existing building and will be built 
in a previously disturbed area. Greg added that they filed for an exemption with the NHESP. Sophie stated 
that she has not visited the site but they have provided the needed details. Greg noted their well is about 
1,000’ from the town well. Rich asked that construction materials and gravel not be stored within 100’ of

-- Lake Shirley  Aquatic Management -Pre-Treatment Survey Report and Treatment Plan –  Dominic 
Meringolo  (SOLitude) reported that the biologists did a survey on 6/24/24  of all 66 data points.  They 
recommen ded treatment of 58.7 acres of the lake . Dominic screen-shared the survey report, which noted 
Fan wort was the most dominant species present at 56% of the points, with Leafy Pondweed being the 
second most dominate species. He shared a map of the lake indicating the plants present and the treatment
areas. Treatment will be done on 7/17 with Diquat, flumi and copper. (Mike L. and Chris S. joined the 
meeting. ) Mary asked how long it takes for the chemicals to dissipate  and Dominic replied Diquat and 
flumi dissipate in 48 hours. Copper is gone in 24 hours. The lake will be closed the day of treatment , with 
no drinking of water for 3 days or use for irrigation for 5 days.

To approve the treatment plan  for Lake Shirley.
