Bob Proctor met with the board on behalf of the owner.  This system would be considered a failure due 
to the estimated high ground water.  The site has high ground water, is flat and the location of the 
structures on the site made designing a replacement system a challenge.  Variances requested are offset

245 WEST STREET 
 
Gerald Lane met with the board.  There have been ongoing issues with an outdoor wood fired burner 
on the site.  The smoke from this device has been migrating into the neighbor’s yard and getting into 
their garage.  Unlike woodstoves these devices work on a thermostat and have a tendency to smolder 
between demand for either heat or hot water.  Once a call for heat, the fire reignite and the emission for 
the smoldering fire exit the unit until and normal burn is achieved. 
 
Agent Garreffi has viewed the site on a number of occasions and has not been able to document an 
issue with the opacity of the smoke but this unit meets the offset requirements in the regulations 
enacted in 2008. 
 
The affected owner’s complaint is based on 310CMR Section 7.00 – Air Pollution 
 
A cease and desist order was issued by Agent Garreffi after discussions with DEP.  Before the cease 
and desist could be lifted the unit would have to operate so as not to cause a condition of air pollution. 
===PAGE 2===
 
After discussion, the board agreed to lift the cease and desist under the condition that it would only be 
for a limited time to ascertain whether an attempt to solve the problem with air pollution had been 
achieved.  Should the burner be moved, stack raised or trees trimmed in an effort to remove the issue 
of air pollution, a request would be made to Agent Jim Garreffi for approval.  Once completed Agent 
Garreffi would be present when the burner was lit to determine if the change in situation eliminated the 
condition of air pollution. 
 
TC PASSIOS SCHOOL CAFITERIA 
 
Agent Jim Garreffi provided an update on the possible use of the TC Passios School Cafeteria as a 
community kitchen.  His response follows. 
 
Since your meeting on this matter I have spoken to the Town Administrator and a selectman.  I 
emphasized the need for more information on what is envisioned for the site. Once the Town had a 
plan for the site the Board could provide a more complete review of the proposed operation with 
regards to the regulatory requirements. 
Listed below are my previous comments to the Board. 
Bridgette and I visited with the Manager of the Worcester Food Hub (regional kitchen facility); they 
have a fully functional kitchen that can be used by the public as a commercial kitchen.  Here are some 
major points regarding their operation. 
1.) It is a managed facility, the Manager schedules individuals to use the kitchen and is present 
during all hours the facility is open (9-5, M-F). 
2.) Only one individual is allowed to work in the kitchen at a time. 
3.) The facility is licensed by the Board of Health and the individuals are licensed by the Board of 
Health. 
4.) Individuals go through an application process with the Food Hub prior to getting permitted by 
the Board of Health; the Board of Health does a preopening inspection before issuing 
individual licenses.  The facility sends a quarterly report of all permittees using the facility; he 
mentioned that they have had individuals who go through the permitting process, obtain a 
permit but never use the kitchen – they work out of their house (the kitchen has a nominal cost 
($25/hour)). 
5.) Any on-site food storage by individuals is in locked cages.

143 ROLLING ACRES
Jason Spinosa, the owner of the property as well as Jim Lavasseur, builder, were present at the
meeting. This property currently has a four bedroom septic system that complies with 310CMR 15
for the four bedroom home. Mr.Spinosa wishes to create an additional 3 rooms in an unfinished open
===PAGE 2===
The owner of this home would like to add three rooms to the basement. The current house has a
sewage disposal system designed for four bedrooms; currently the house has 8 rooms and four
bedrooms.
In a single family dwelling the size of a sewage disposal system is based on the number of bedrooms
and the definition in Title 5 (provided below) describes how to determine the number of bedrooms in a
single family dwelling. Based on this definition the proposed construction would require a septic
system designed to handle 5 bedrooms.
Bedroom - A room providing privacy, intended primarily for sleeping and consisting of all of the
following: (a) floor space of no less than 70 square feet; (b) for new construction, a ceiling height of
no less than seven feet three inches;
(c) for existing houses and for mobile homes, a ceiling height of no less than seven feet zero inches;
(d) an electrical service and ventilation; and (e) at least one window. Living rooms, dining rooms,
kitchens, halls, bathrooms, unfinished cellars and unheated storage areas over garages are not
considered bedrooms. Single family dwellings shall be presumed to have at least three bedrooms.
Where the total mumber of rooms for single family dwellings exceeds eight. not including hathrooms.
hallways, unfinished cellars and unheated storage areas, the number of bedrooms presumed shall be
calculated by dividing the total number of rooms by two then rounding down to the next lowest whole
number. The applicant may design a system using design flows for a smaller number of bedrooms than
are presumed in this definition by granting to the Approving Authority a deed restriction limiting the
number of bedrooms to the smaller mumber.
There are a number of options for this site:
1.) Reduce the number of rooms to be added to the house; a house with 9 rooms and 4 bedrooms
would still comply with the size of the existing sewage disposal system.
2.) The applicant could consider the installation of a sewage disposal system to handle 5
bedrooms.
Chairman Emond explained how we determine a bedroom by local definition as well as the fact that
with a 4 bedroom septic permit the dwelling would be limited to a total of 9 rooms. Inasmuch as the
home currently has four bedrooms the addition of three more rooms would not be possible unless the
septic system was expanded to accommodate the additional room.
Mr. Spinosa stated that he had read Title 5 Regulations and it was his opinion as well as his attorney's
opinion that if Mr. Spinosa executed and recorded a deed restriction on the property limiting the home
to four bedrooms that is would be able to add the three additional rooms.
===PAGE 3===
Mr. Spinosa indicated that he did not believe that he would be able to reconfigure the existing rooms to
reduce them to a level to allow the addition of three rooms in the basement and that he could be
contacting his attorney.
Mr. Spinosa was advised that if he wanted to speak with agent Jim Garreffi or meet back with the
board should there be a change in circumstance he was welcome to do.
After further discussion and on motion by Jewell and second by Passions the request to further develop
this property with additional rooms was denied even if a bedroom deed restriction was in place. All in
favor.

11 PENINSULA DRIVE 
 
Kirk Fitzpatrick from Civil Solutions met with the Board of behalf of the owner.  This system is in 
failure due to high ground water.  The lot is small and irregular.  A variance for a reduction in offset to 
groundwater is being requested from 5 feet to 4 feet is being requested.  This will allow better grading 
on the site. 
 
After discussion and on Motion by Pa ssios and second by Fortin, the variance was approved.  All in 
favor. 
 
1190 NORTHFIELD ROAD 
 
Neil Gorman met with the board on behalf of the owner.  This system is in failure due to high ground 
water.  This lot also has a steep slope.  A Presby system is p roposed.  DEP allows a groundwater 
reduction of up to 2 feet for replacement system.  Variances being requested are reduction in offset to 
groundwater from 4 feet to 2 feet and offset to wetlands from 100 feet to 69 feet.   
 
After much discussion and on motion by Mayhew and second by Jewell, the variances were approved.  
All in favor. 
 
157 TOWNSEND HARBOR ROAD 
 
Doug Smith met with the board on behalf of the owner.  This system is currently in failure due to the 
high ground water. 
 
===PAGE 2===
In addition to high ground  water this site has a steep slope.  The plan proposes a variance in reduction 
to offset to groundwater from four feet to 3 feet and less than 12 inches for inlet and outlet tees. 
 
After discussion and on motion by Fortin and second by Mayhew the variance was granted.  All in 
favor. 
 
71 HIGHLAND STREET 
 
Doug Smith met with the board on behalf of the owner.  This system is in failure.  There were very few 
test holes on the lot that provided the required 4 feet of pervious soil.  The area where the proposed 
leaching area is located provides a consistent 2 feet of pervious soil, the minimum allowed by DEP.  In 
addition, due to the high ground water a reduction to less than 12 inches for inlet and outlet tees is 
being requested. 
 
After discussion and on motion b y Passios and second by Mayhew, the variances were approved.  All 
in favor. 
 
113 SANDY COVE ROAD 
 
Doug Smith met with the board on behalf of the owner.  The system on this property is in failure.  This 
lot has high ground water and the existing sewer pipe exits the back of the house.  In addition, the 
adjoining lot has irrigation well. 
 
A variance for reduction in groundwater offset from 4 feet to 3 feet is being requested, as well as a 
reduction in less than 12 inches for inlet and outlet tees. 
 
After discussion and on motion by Fortin and second by Mayhew, the variances were approved.  All in 
favor. 
 
LANNI ORCHARD 
 
The owners of Lanni Orchard , Jerry Lanni Sr., Tony Lanni, Mario Lanni and Attorney Cella were 
present at the meeting. 
 
Lanni Orchard has filed for an APR (Agricultural Preservation Restriction) on their property.  This 
would mean that the property located within the APR could only be used for agriculture and no other 
purpose.  The plan was prepared by MDAR .  There  was an article at Town Meeting a nd this was 
approved for funding.  The plan was submitted to the Planning Board who has view this as and ANR 
subdivision of land.  Based on that determination, it was questioned as to whether the remainder land 
or land left outside the APR designation was sufficient to support a repair of the septic system on the 
commercial building, also outside the APR designation.  There is also a utility easement running 
through the remainder land. 
 
After much discussion and on motion by Passios and second by Mayhew, th e board voted to allow the 
APR to proceed, however, if, in the future, DEP determines that this division of  land falls under 
subdivision r ules, Lanni Orchard would have to fully comply with all current reg ulations and are 
proceeding with the project at their own risk.   
 
Meeting adjourned

217 RESERVOIR ROAD 
 
Neil Gorman of Ross Associates met with the board on behalf of the owner.  This is a voluntary 
upgrade.  Based on the high groundwater level this system would be in failure.  This property is 
connected to town water. 
 
Variances being requested are groundwater offset from 5 feet to 4 feet and the system less than 100 
feet from a wetland, proposed 57 feet.  This system is not in the River Front Zone.  Plumbing is on the 
side of the house where the new system is being proposed. 
 
After discussion and on Motion by Jewell and second by Fortin, the variance was approved.  All in 
favor. 
 
64 ARBOR STREET 
 
Matthew Woodward, owner, met with the Board.   
 
===PAGE 2===
Chairman George Emond stated that this matter was placed on the agenda beca use at their meeting on 
June 4, 2018, the board did error in stating that an existing system has to be four feet above the water 
table. Mr. Woodward was correct in that an existing system just need s to be above the high 
groundwater level and not 4 feet above groundwater. 
 
Mr. Woodward stated that he did not feel that the previous minutes as approved and posted reflected 
what transpired at the meeting. 
 
Mr. Emond further stated that the error made does not change the outcome with regard to this property.  
Member Perry Jewell also stated that this error did not change the situation with this property.  There is 
an old 500 gallon septic tank and a newer d -box as well as a d iscrepancy in the configuration of the 
leach field. 
 
Chairman Emond stated that previousl y, the board gave Mr. Woodward the option of having a 
witnessed Title 5 as an alternative to replacing the system.  Mr. Woodward has not availed himself of 
that option. 
 
145 WOODLAND 
 
Bob Proctor met with the board on behalf of the owner.  This system is i n failure.  The property is 
served by public water.  This lot is small and has high ground water.   
 
Variances being requested are a reduction in groundwater from 4 feet to 3 feet as well as less than 12 
inch separation between the inlet and outlet tees.  The proposed system is designed for two (2) 
bedrooms. 
 
After discussion and on motion by Passios and second by Mayhew, the variances were approved with 
the condition that a two (2) bedroom deed restriction be placed on the property.  All in favor. 
 
169 PENNINSULA DRIVE 
 
Bob Proctor met with the board on behalf of the owner.  The system on this site is in failure.  This lot 
is small, has a significant amount of wetlands and two buildings that take up a large portion of the site.   
 
Variances being requested are offset to groundwater from five feet to four feet , less than 12 inch 
separation between inlet and outlet tees, offset to garage slab from 10 feet to 8 feet and offset to house 
slab from 10 feet to 5.3 feet. 
 
After discussion and on motion by Mayhew and second by Fortin, the variances were approved.  All in 
favor. 
 
950 CHASE ROAD 
 
The property located above is without water.  The owner has made arrangement to have a new well 
drilled.  The exact location from the property line is unknown although it is m ore than 15 feet but less 
than 30 feet. 
 
===PAGE 3===
After discussion and on motion by Passios and second by Fortin, the board voted to allow the 
installation of the well to go forward.  A variance for the exact placement will be grated once the 
distance is known.  All in favor. 
 
SEPTIC LOAN PROGRAM 
 
Members of the board would like to consider using the money currently in the Septic Loan Program 
for other than the repair of private, low income, residential dwellings.  The board would only consider 
expanding the use of this money if they retain control over the funds. 
 
Both Chairman George Emond and member Dave Passios have met with Town Counsel to discuss this. 
 
After discussion, the board would like to move forward with discussing the possibility of using these 
funds in an expanded way and will be speaking with Town Counsel to ascertain how that might be 
accomplished. 
 
Meeting adjourned

34 CHARLTON STREET
Jack Maloney met with the board of behalf of the owner. This system is in failure. Because of the
small lot size, location of the house on the lot and sloping topography, designing a new system was
more difficult.
Variances being requested are reduction in groundwater from 4 feet to 3 feet and offset from tank to
cellar wall from 10 teet to 6 feet. The reduction in groundwater will allow the fill to remain on the lot
without the need for a retaining wall.
After discussion and on motion by Mayhew and second by Passios, the variances were approved. All
in favor.
748 PAGE STREET
A prospective owner, also the neighbor, of the above property contacted the Board office with regard
to the above property. The property failed Title 5 due to the proximity of the existing well to the septic
system. Due to this, either the well or septic system would need to be replaced. Both the well and the
septic, separately, passed Title 5. If there was not the issue of distance between the two, this would
have been a passing report.
After discussion, and on motion by Jewell and second by Mayhew, it was decided that either the
current owner or a prospective purchaser could make the determination as to which is to be replaced,
the well or the septic system. Once this property is vacated, it cannot be re-occupied until either the
well or the septic system was replaced. All in favor.
