"As you are aware our Reserve Officer program as it was known in the past has drastically changed due to Police
Reform, and the creation of the Peace Officer Standards and Training Commission (POSTC). The training
requirements for a reserve officer has to be equal to a full time officer, or that standard has to be met through the
Bridge Academy program. As a result of these requirements our reserve ranks have significantly been reduced.
With that being said, we are constantly attempting to recruit qualified candidates for our Reserve Officer ranks. I
am pleased to announce that we have found a Reserve Officer candidate who meets the criteria set forth by
POSTC, and would be a good fit for our Community and Department. I am pleased to recommend Paul C.
Theodoulou to an appointment as Reserve Police Officer. Paul grew up in the City of Fitchburg, graduating from

2015, Paul was hired by the Fitchburg Police Department as a full time police officer. Paul attended the
Municipal Police Training Committee (MPTC) Basic Recruit Officer Course (ROC) Academy at Reading,

Meeting with Sewer Commission Representative on Article 25 Sewer Bylaw Amendment
Commissioner Dave Macdonald and Business Mgr. Lefevbre presented the proposed changes to the bylaw:
Article 25 is to revise the current sewer bylaw including:
• the clarification that inclusion of a property in the Sewer Service Area (SSA) does not constitute
entitlement to a sewer connection and must still be subject to available capacity, capacity
limitations and Sewer Commission approval
• creating an exception to prohibiting sewer connections via easement provided that, and only if, the
following criteria are satisfied:
• the property must still be in the Sewer Service Area (which can only be expanded by Town
Meeting vote)
• connections through an easement must be across adjoining properties
• the process requires the issuance of a waiver (to be added as Section
10.0 of the Bylaw in Article 25) upon findings by the Commission:
• of emergency circumstances justifying the easement;
• that the inability to connect to public sewer is causing undue hardship; or
• that allowing the easement is deemed to be in the best interest of Town and consistent
with the purpose and objectives of the sewer bylaw
• a further clarification of the Commission's authorization to adopt rules and regulations under the
bylaw and to amend them following a public hearing with notice published at least 5 business days
in advance, with subsequent posting on the Commission's website
Discussion about some of the wording ensued. Suggestion was made to that in the event of an emergency
waiver rather than having a majority vote to have a super majority vote; that it should require a public
hearing. Other wording allows the commission to waive the rules when in the best interest of the town; it
should only be in the event of an emergency. Discussion continued on the waiver wording in its current form

Mr. Jeffreys read aloud the following proposed amendment to the Sign Policy:
Discussion ensued about the wording. The following is what was decided upon:
Election Day Exception
On any day that the Town conducts an election, administered by the Town Clerk, the following policy shall apply:
1) Campaign signs and/or ballot question signs may be placed on Town property along Massachusetts Avenue and Oak
Avenue, as follows:
i.
On Massachusetts Avenue, signage must be located between the driveway to the Lunenburg Public Library and
the eastern entrance to the middle/high school parking lot
il. On Oak Avenue, signage must be located within 50 feet of the entrance to the middle/high school parking lot
2) All signs and rockers must be less than 24 inches tall and 24 inches wide. Metal brackets shall be used to stake signs
into the ground, and when staked, the top of the sign shall be less than 40 inches above the ground.
Signs may be placed on a first-come, first serve basis, within 10 hours of polls opening, and must be removed within 2
hours of polls closing.
4)
Signs shall be placed within 10 feet of the public right of way and cannot obscure the point of view of vehicles exiting
each parking lot
5)
Each campaign or ballot question is limited to a maximum of 50 signs on Town property.
Candidates and their supporters may personally hold an unlimited amount of signs, provided that they remain at least
150 feet from the polling place.
7) Prior approval from any Town agent is not required.
8) Any sign in violation of this policy, will be removed by the Town Clerk or his/her designee.

Update on Public Records Retention Project
I received new information last week that we would be able to digitize records that would replace paper
copies, if we can document that we will continue to keep them in a format that is accessible (i.e. at one
point floppy disks were widely used for storage, perhaps in the future cloud storage would not be the right
storage method). Preparing for this, a formalized digital storage plan will need to be established and this
will incorporate input from all of our departments and IT department. The public records retention storage
project will first look at destroying all the records that we no longer have to retain, then look at all the
records that we can destroy the paper copies if we have them digitally stored, put in place proper storage
of any paper records, and catalogue all our records for retention and location. Digitization of what
records we can eliminate paper copies of will reduce our overall square foot needs for storage. There will
be more to come once I look more into what is involved the digital plan we would need to put in place.
On a related note, the Municipal Building Design Committee asked if off site storage was an option. I
responded that that would not be optimal for the Town. Records that are not digital need to be easily
accessible in order to respond to public records request within the required timeframe and there are times
that departments need to look back into records for information. It doesn't need to be in the same
building as the town offices, but in my opinion, it should be within another town owned property that is
easily accessible (first floor or space that is handicapped accessible).

Ms. Lemieux reported Police Chief Gammel has requested to hire four full-time firefighters due to
difficulty hiring Detail officers. In a memo to the Town Clerk:
"As Detail season is in full swing, we are finding it difficult to fill "road details." To address this ongoing issue the
Police Department created a Civilian Traffic Member Unit. This unit is dedicated to traffic control on various details
throughout Town. Due to the number of detail request we receive we would like to add four (4) Full Time Lunenburg
Firefighters to our Civilian Traffic Member Unit.
I am requesting the following Lunenburg Firefighters:
1. Christos Lekaditis
2. James Ricci
3. Melissa Buchanan
4. Olivia Hayes
receive an exemption under G.L. c. 268Aş 20, (f), which states: "(f to a municipal employee if the contract is for
personal services in a part time, call or volunteer capacity with the police, fire, rescue or ambulance department of a
fire district, town or any city with a population of less than thirty-five thousand inhabitants; provided, however, that the
head of the contracting agency makes and files with the clerk of the city, district or town a written certification that no
employee of said agency is available to perform such services as part of his regular duties, and the city council, board of
selectmen, board of aldermen or district prudential committee approve the exemption of his interest from this section."
This proposal has been addressed with the Lunenburg Police Officers Union, I.B.P.O. Local 353, and the Lunenburg
Firefighters Union, P.F.F.M. Local 5358A. Both unions are in agreement to allow the above firefighters to work details
for the Lunenburg Police Department upon completion of a certified Traffic Control class."

Best Achievement

Morale will be very important. Addressing these will all take additional time and attention.

up recreational cannabis businesses. Another provision of this bill is to require that the fees
charged by communities would need to be directly linked to costs. The Senate bill is
prospective only on the HCA's but it is unclear on whether the House bill would affect both
past and future Host Community Agreements. The argument against is that the
communities that have signed HA's have binding, legal agreements.
MBTA Housing Choice Act: This Act has attracted a lot ofattention from affected
