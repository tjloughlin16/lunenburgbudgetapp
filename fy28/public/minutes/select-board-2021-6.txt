Proclamation Honoring Alice D. Quinn
On Her 100th Birthday
WHEREAS, Alice D. Quinn will be honored on the occasion of her 100th birthday and
is deeply appreciated and loved by her friends and family as they join
together to wish her a happy birthday and many more wonderful years
ahead; and
WHEREAS, Upon the occasion of her 100th birthday, the Lunenburg Select Board
wishes to express their sincere congratulations to Alice D. Quinn, born
Alice Dealia LeBlanc on June 16, 1921 in Moncton, New Brunswick,
Canada to Hector & Amelia LeBlanc; and
WHEREAS, Alice's family moved to Fitchburg, MA in 1927, Alice met her husband,
Thomas Quinn in 1938; they dated and were wed in 1940 and enjoyed
life together for 66 years, and
WHEREAS, Alice worked evenings at the Foster Grant Company and after retiring took
a part-time kitchen job at Burbank Hospital until full retirement, and
WHEREAS, Alice has been a citizen of the United States since 1939 and a resident
of Fitchburg and Westminster, MA before moving to Lunenburg in
2007, and
WHEREAS, Alice is the proud Mother of two daughters, Grandmother of 4
grandsons, Great-Grandmother of 10 great-grandchildren and Great
Great-Grandmother of 3 great great grandchildren, and
WHEREAS, Longevity of life is a blessing for an individual and for a community which
benefits from the knowledge, creativity, and experiences this individual
brings to all; and
Therefore, BE IT PROCLAIMED, We, the Lunenburg Select Board extends to Mrs.
Alice D. Quinn best wishes for many more years of good health and happiness.

TOWN OF LUNENBURG
GREEN COMMUNITIES COMMITTEE CHARGE
Section 1: General
The Lunenburg Green Communities Committee will work with the Massachusetts Department of Energy
Resources to learn of grant opportunities for energy conservation and renewable energy generation, and
will apply for appropriate grants. The Committee will ensure the Town retains its status as a designated
Green Community.

Town's goals;
2. Develop action steps to ensure Lunenburg remains designated as a Department of
Energy Resources Green Community;
3. Facilitate town participation in DOER programs, grants, and incentives;
4. Serve as an information resource for town residents and business owners on the
benefits of energy and resource conservation;
5. Increase constituents' awareness of and promote resource conservation methods,
energy efficiency programs, utility rebates, and state/federal programs to promote
energy efficiency, waste reduction, recycling, and overall enhancements to long term
sustainability and resilience in response to environmental, resource, and energy
challenges;
6. Report at least once annually on its activities to the Select Board.
Section 3: Membership and Meetings
1. The Committee shall be composed of five (5) regular members serving three year terms
appointed by the Select Board and two (2) associate members. Two members shall initially be
appointed to a three year term, two members shall initially be appointed to a two year term, and
one member shall initially be appointed to a one year term. After the initial term, all regular
members shall serve a three year term. The associate members shall be appointed to a three year
term.
2. In addition to the regular members, the Committee shall consult with the Town Manager, the
town departments that oversee the facilities and energy accounts, the School
Superintendent, and the School Facilities Director that oversees the school facilities and school energy
accounts in order to ascertain future energy projects, areas where energy savings are needed, and
replacement schedule for vehicles.
1. Voting Power: All duly appointed regular members shall have full voting power.
2. Resignation: Members of the Committee shall notify the Chairman of the Committee, the
Town Manager, and the Select Board of their resignation.
3. Vacancies: Any vacancies shall be filled in the same manner as the original appointment.
4. Representations:
Members shall get approval of the Committee prior to making statements,
presentations, or joining activities on behalf of the Committee.
5. The election of officers including at least a Chairman, Vice-Chairman and Clerk and shall be
elected annually by a majority vote of the Committee.
6. A quorum shall consist of three (3) members.
7.
The recording of minutes and votes of all meetings shall be conducted as prescribed by the Open
Meeting Law. Copies of the minutes shall be submitted to the Town Clerk as prescribed in the
Town Bylaw and according to Open Meeting Law.
8. The Committee shall comply with all state laws and local bylaws.

Governor Files Legislation to Extend Certain COVID-19 Emergency Measures
On May 25th, Governor Baker filed legislation to extend certain emergency measures currently in place
via executive orders that are set to expire on June 15th when the State of Emergency will be rescinded.
This legislation proposes to extend measures providing for a temporary suspension of certain open
meeting law requirements, special permits for expanded outside dining at restaurants, and billing
protections for COVID-19 patients. When the State of Emergency ends, these orders will expire, and
temporarily extending these measures will allow for time to transition. Extending these measures, which
===PAGE 11===
Law to provide for greater flexibility in conducting open meetings through reliance on electronic
streaming and similar measures.
The bill will also grant municipalities authority to extend special permits for restaurants offering
outdoor dining issued under the State of Emergency through November 29, 2021. Under an Executive
Order issued in 2020, municipalities were permitted to use an expedited process to approve temporary
permits for new or expanded outdoor dining and alcohol service. Without a legislative extension, special
permits granted under the Governor's Order will expire 60 days after the end of the State of Emergency.
The legislation will also extend a protection adopted in an executive order that prohibits medical
providers from billing patients who have received COVID-related emergency and inpatient services for
charges in excess of costs paid by their insurers. As filed, the protection would extend until January 1,
2022, at which time recently passed federal legislation that included protections for both emergency and
non-emergency cases will become effective. Earlier this year, Governor Baker signed legislation
establishing surprise billing protections for patients for non-emergency services.

Mr. Kibbe stated the Upen Space Committee continues to work on various goals and objectives
that we have in our Open Space plan. One of the goals is to make sure we are using the existing
resources that we have in town. We have existing recreational, open space, etc. in town, but what
they may not know is the Town owns a lot of other properties, scattered all over the town. A lot
of them were acquired through the tax title process and there are other parcels that were acquired
and are sitting there vacant. Some were acquired for one use and then deemed not suitable for
that use. Some are conservation areas that are under the purview of the Conservation
Commission. Tonight he would be addressing 225 Pleasant Street and 671 West Street.
Access to the West Street area is limited due to being densely populated. He moved on to his
Power Point presentation:
The West Street parcel is 66.5 acres of wetland, woodland, a pond, trails and wildlife enjoyed
by local residents from around the Whalom district. The trailhead is currently located off the
south-westerly side of the West Street S-curve, where there is seasonal parking for three or
more vehicles. The trailhead is currently located at the west end of the West Street S-curve. It
follows the west side of the pond and wetlands before dividing into east and west loops.
The trail heads south around the westerly side of a large picturesque wetland and pond with
most of the woodlands to the west of the trail. The hiking is easy with very little slope, but
several rustic crossings and tricky areas around seasonal pools of storm water offer some
challenge. The parcel is divided and dominated by the eastern wetlands and the mixed
woodlands to the west. A large natural opening provides hikers with a grand view of the
wetland from the edge of the woods; a natural pause in the trek, and a perfect spot to observe
the landscape or listen to the rustling of the trees and the calls of the blackbirds, hawks and
bluebirds who share the marsh with deer, turkey and other wildlife. There are several downed
trees in the area which might be arranged for rustic seating.
Adventure and new discoveries await families undertaking this brisk walk with crossing loops
to the east and west. There are one or two large puddles along the trail way which may require
remediation or course changes to protect these natural features and improve walkability. The
trail continues south toward a fork in the system. Hikers may choose an eastern or western
loop. The Eastern Loop crosses the brook connecting the northern and southern wetlands. It
skirts the northern wetlands and circles around on itself. A turn to the left will take you on the
eastern loop trail, through a mixed forest with large stands of Mountain Laurel. Here, the trail
heads down a short slope toward a stone crossing of the brook connecting the southern and
northern wetlands. As hikers continue, they'll complete the eastern loop which runs behind the
homes along Whalom Road. The western loop is characterized by a large segmented loop with
smaller loops contained within it. To the north, a deep woodland separates the trails system
from homes on West Street and Pratt Street. There are several access points along Pratt Street
which could be converted to trailheads for people coming from the south. Heading off on the
western loop, the trails are more heavily trodden from dirt-bike and four wheeler use. Travelers
are surrounded by deep woodlands and ancient stone walls from the agricultural period.
Trails are easy to follow and the walking is not difficult.

related to the effects of the COVID-19 pandemic.
The Act is effective May 28, 2021 through September 30, 2021 or until State funding is
exhausted prior to this date.
ELIGIBILITY
Under the Act, employees are entitled to leave as follows:
• 40 hours of emergency paid sick leave for employees who work 40 or more hours
per week.
• For employees who regularly work fewer than 40 hours per week, leave in an amount
that is equal to the average number of hours that such employee works per week.
• For employees whose schedule and weekly hours vary from week to week, leave
that is equal to the average number of hours that the employee was scheduled to
work per week over the previous 6 months.
• If an employee with a variable schedule has not worked for the employer for 6
months, leave that is equal to the number of hours per week that the employee
reasonably expected to work when hired.
An employee may use emergency paid sick leave on an intermittent basis and in hourly
increments.
PURPOSES FOR WHICH LEAVE MAY BE TAKEN
Leave is available to employees who are unable to work for the following reasons:
1. An employee's need to:
• self-isolate and care for themselves because they have been diagnosed with
COVID-19;
• get a medical diagnosis, care, or treatment for COVID-19 symptoms; or
• get or recover from a COVID-19 immunization;
2. An employee's need to care for a family member who:
• must self-isolate due to a COVID-19 diagnosis; or
• needs medical diagnosis, care, or treatment for COVID-19 symptoms;

state, or federal public official, a health authority having jurisdiction, or a health
care provider;

Governor Baker's Executive Urder that suspended certain provisions of the Upen Meeting Law,
which altered the way public meetings were legally held during the duration of the State of
Emergency by allowing a public body to meet without the public body being physically present
and allowed fully remote participation, will be rescinded on June 15"h.
This means that
beginning on June 15th, to comply with the Open Meeting Law, all public bodies must meet in
places physically open to the public, pursuant to G.L. C. 30A, § 20.
Beginning on June 15th, members of a public body must be physically present at the meeting to
participate and satisfy quorum requirements. This means remote participation by members of
the public body is not allowed, unless a quorum of the board is present.
June 15" will be the first day that "in-person" public meetings will be held. Public Access
and our IT department have set-up the Town Hall meeting room to be able to conduct both
an in-person and Zoom meeting simultaneously. At this time, this will be limited to those
boards/committees that had "live broadcast" meetings in the Town Hall meeting room prior
to March 12, 2020. The agendas for these "hybrid" meetings reflect the in-person location
(Town Hall) and the Zoom meeting information. All other meetings will only need to reflect
the physical location of the in-person meeting.
We understand that the ability to have fully remote meetings allowed for greater flexibility in
meeting times for boards and committees that did not require reserving a physical space.
Given that there are 28 different boards, committees, and commissions that require meeting
space, and we have a limited number of meeting rooms, we ask for everyone's patience as we
transition back to in-person meetings.
In terms of face coverings and other COVID-19 safety requirements, board/committee
members and meeting attendees that have not been fully vaccinated (have received two doses of
either the Moderna or Pfizer vaccine or a single dose of the Johnson & Johnson more than 14
days ago) are asked to continue wearing a face covering and socially distance themselves from
their fellow board/committee members and members of the public when in-person, indoor
meetings resume. We ask that everyone continue to adhere to these safety protocols in order to
keep our staff and the public safe.
It is noteworthy to add that Governor Baker filed legislation on May 25, 2021 to extend certain
emergency measures currently in place via executive orders that are set to expire on June 15th.
This legislation, if enacted, would extend the authority for municipal boards to conduct virtual
meetings. However, until this legislation is passed and enacted as law, the above provisions
govern. If the legislation does pass a public notification will be sent out and a new plan
devised.

1. Discussion on Pay As You Throw Bag Program and Fees for FY22
Ms. Lemieux following up to her Town Manager's report from last week provided an update on
the costs to operate the PAYT bag program and information related to a suggestion at the June
8, 2021 meeting to offer discounted bags to senior citizens in Fiscal Year 2022. There is still
pending information as well. Due to a substantial increase in the costs to operate the Pay-As-
You-Throw curbside trash and recycling program related to new contract terms with E.L.
Harvey that reflects the increased market costs for collection and disposal of trash and recycling
and the increase in manufacturing and distribution of the PAYT bags, bag costs will be
increasing in Fiscal Year 2022. The price increase will be dependent on two main variables.
These variables include the estimate used to project the number of cases of the different sized
bags sold next fiscal year and whether the Town offers relief to senior citizens for PAYT bags.
The estimate used to project the number of cases of the different sized bags affects the cost of the
bags; as more cases of bags are sold, it decreases the operational costs for collection because this
is a flat rate annually. We received updated sales figures from our bag manufacturer for the time
period of June 1, 2020 until May 31, 2021 and there was a 38% increase in the actual number of
===PAGE 6===
After discussing this with the Finance Director, we anticipate that due to the actual number of
cases sold compared to our estimates, we will have a surplus in the Solid Waste Enterprise
account of between $40,000 and $50,000 at the end of the fiscal year.
Understanding that our collection and disposal costs that are covered under the Enterprise are
estimated to increase from $318,014.00 to $419,850.00 and the costs of manufacturing and
supplying the bags is also increasing, we will not see a surplus in Fiscal Year 2022 if the
estimated number of bags decreases and the price of the bags remains the same.
Ms. Lemieux stated she was waiting on additional information on Fiscal Year 2020 trash bags
sold versus Fiscal Year 2021 and trying to project knowing that due to people being home the
past year that more people were generating more trash.
The other variable is whether the Town offers relief to senior citizens for PAYT bags. At the
June 8" meeting Selectman Jeffreys inquired about offering discounted bags to senior citizens
and how we could implement a program for seniors in Fiscal Year 2022. In the last week I have
corresponded with Selectman Jeffreys on this question and made inquiries of my own on the
feasibility of such an initiative. In order to control the financial impact, maintain a simple
operational process, and not create burdensome additional responsibilities on different parties,
the Town could purchase a set number of cases that are directly shipped to the Town to disburse
to seniors for free through a tracked system. The purchase of the bags is an eligible expense
using our Recycling Dividends Program funds and the Senior Center could be the location that
seniors could pick up their allotted number of free bags. The logistics would need to be worked
out such as number of bags per household and how this information is tracked that I would
work out with the Senior Center Director and base the estimated number of cases on the total
number of eligible seniors in Lunenburg. We would also have to supplement the pickup and
disposal of the bags with the surplus Enterprise funds from this year. Ms. Lemieux spoke to
COA Director Doherty and she is agreeable to handing out those bags. This could encourage
more people to visit the Senior Center as well. It could be tracked through the My Senior Center
software used at Eagle House to see which households receive them so they would be
distributed evenly.
I am still awaiting data that will assist me in making a recommendation on the proposed
increase in bag price and the logistics of working out a senior citizen PAYT bag relief program.
This will be a recommendation made at a future meeting.
Mr. Alonzo wanted to ensure that there is not more work and time involved tracking that would
negate the savings.
Mr. Jeffreys added one issue discussed with Ms. Lemieux over the past week was the ability
to administer it seamlessly and not create a hardship on local vendors. Further discussion
ensued on what criteria would qualify getting the free bags as well as the number of households
that would receive them. This will be discussed more at a future meeting.

1. Pay As You Throw Bag Fee and Proposed Increase for FY22
Ms. Lemieux reported from her memorandum:
As mentioned in my memorandum to the Board last week, there are two main variables that will
impact the cost of the PAYT bags, the estimated number of cases sold for FY 2022 and offering
free bags to senior citizens for FY 2022. After reviewing the bag sales for the last three years,
the estimate that will be used to project the number of cases sold of the different sized bags for
FY 2022 will be based on FY2020 bag sales. Comparing FY2020 bag sales to FY2021 bag
sales, there was an 8% increase in cases of small bags sold, a 12% increase in cases of large
bags sold, and a 45% increase in cases of extra-large bags sold. Using the more conservative
figures will not negatively impact the Solid Waste Enterprise Account.
The other variable, providing free bags to senior citizens for FY 2022, required determining the
estimated number of senior households in Lunenburg that are not in multi-family complexes
that do not receive collection services through the PAYT bag program. The estimate I am using
is 2,200 households. My recommendation, if the Board does decide to offer free bags to seniors
in Fiscal Year 2022, would be to offer one roll of small bags to each household that is owned or
rented by a senior citizen. In order to track the supply, bags would be picked up at the Senior
Center. The overall projected cost of this provided an estimated 2,200 affected households
would be approximately $40,000 for collection and disposal costs and approximately $5,000
for the bags. The $5,000 could be paid out of the Recycling Dividends Grant funds and the
$40,000 would be covered by the projected surplus at the close of FY 2021.
