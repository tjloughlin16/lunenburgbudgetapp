The site approval process is offered to project sponsors who intend to apply for a Comprehensive Permit

needs and strategies. As part of our review, we are soliciting comments from the local community and we

Chapter 40B regulations (760 CMR 56.00) your comments may include information regarding municipal
actions previously taken to meet affordable housing needs such as inclusionary zoning, multifamily

be considered as part of our review.

plans for Pond View Commons. Please inform us of any issues that have been raised or are anticipated in
the Town's review of this application. We request that you submit your comments to this office by
Friday, July 30, 2021, so we may process this application in a timely manner.

760 CMR 56.02, may attend. The site visit for Pond View Commons has been tentatively scheduled for
Thursday, July 8th at 1:00 p.m. Please notify me promptly if any representatives of your office or if other
Local Boards plan to attend the scheduled site visit.

available to the Zoning Board of Appeals (ZBA) to review the permit application. The Massachusetts
Housing Partnership's (MHP) Ch. 40B Technical Assistance Program administers grants to municipalities

site, www.mhp.net/40B or e-mail MHP at kbosse@mhp.net.

Thank you for your assistance. Sincerely, Michael Busby Relationship Manager"

Mr: Alonzo stated this meeting is to continue a dialogue with the APDC concerning the old
Primary School building. There have been several town meeting warrant articles submitted for the
property. One article was submitted to authorize the Select Board to sell and /or lease the parcel
and/or buildings situated on it and it was defeated. Another article for the actual demolition of the
building was also defeated at town meeting. It appears that the townspeople do not want to sell the
land. Mr. Alonzo added, in his opinion, a renovation to the building would be cost prohibitive to
the town to fund it.

APDC Chairman McGrath called their meeting to order at 7:22 p.m. and stated they were not
seeking to be adversarial but are charged with enforcing the APD bylaw. He has been on the

and done many restoration projects.
The APD was designed to protect the rural character and history of the district agaist unbridled
development. In 2018 the APDC visited the site and wrote a letter to the Select Board noting the
neglect of the building and citing a violation of the bylaw. They sent periodic reminders to the
Select Board following the initial letter. The building remains in violation of the bylaw.
Mr. McGrath noted seven years ago he spoke to Building Commissioner Mike Savageau regarding
the Town Hall chimney condition; that it was rotten and in danger of collapsing. He reported back
to me after speaking with the former Town Manager that they were not ready to fix it at that time.

moved on to describe several old school houses in town that have been refurbished for use as
residences. He noted several communities that have converted old schools into senior housing,
some funded by non-profits and state money.
Mr. Alonzo stated liability is a primary concern for the town. He reiterated if the town has to fund
refurbishing the building, he did not see it passing at town meeting, therefore the liability takes a
larger life than the building itself. As far as the demolition by neglect, the APDC did not even exist
until this building was vacant for around eight years. When it first became vacant the idea of
elderly housing was presented to us but the HUD funding disappeared due to the economy at the
time. The proposed green space certainly prevents unbridled development.

state's concern about lack of housing. There are smaller organizations that would contribute
$5,000 - $20,000, but not the milions needed to restore the school building.
APDC Vice Chair Cullen Dwyer reiterated it is the opinion of the APDC that the old Primary
School has historical value due to the architectural style. This needs to be considered other than
the amount to restore it.

expenses, behavioral healthcare, and certain public health and safety staff;

public sector;

have borne and will bear the greatest health risks because of their service in critical
infrastructure sectors; and,

improve access to clean drinking water, support vital wastewater and stormwater
infrastructure, and to expand access to broadband internet.

CHAPTER 40B WORKSHOP
Mr. Alonzo stated Attorney Adam Costa would be conducting the 40B workshop and it would be
followed by a question and answer period.
Attorney Costa stated he would be referring to his syllabus and the Chapter 40B Handbook for
Zoning Boards of Appeals (2017) issued by the Massachusetts Housing Partnership. He noted this
state law is often referred to as the Comprehensive Permit Law (G.L. c. 40B, §§ 20-23), enacted in
1969 to facilitate construction of low to moderate income housing or "affordable housing." It
establishes a consolidated local review and approval process that empowers the Zoning Board of
Appeals (ZBA) to hold hearings and make binding decisions that encompass all local ordinances or
Under Chapter 40B3, the 7B3,A has auchority to grant all approvals that would otherwise require
individual applications under local bylaws or ordinances. The ZBA also has authority to grant
waivers of local requirements if requested by the developer and necessary to construct the proposed
project. The ZBA uses a single permit to expedite the approval process and construction of the
housing. The ZBA's jurisdiction includes zoning, subdivision regulations, and other types of local
bylaws or ordinances and regulations. The ZBA does not have the authority to waive state
requirements. Therefore, Conservation Commission and the Board of Health retain jurisdiction
when a project requires permits under the Massachusetts Wetlands Protection Act, G.L. c. 131, § 40
and Title V of the State Environmental Code, 310 CMR 15.00, respectively.
The Housing Appeals Committee (HAC), under c. 40B, has authority to consider appeals arising
from the ZBA's denial or conditional approval of comprehensive permits. However, the HAC's
ability to overturn local decisions applies only to cases involving a city or town that has not met its
regional fair-share obligations under the statute.
Costa moved on to project eligibility. Before the comprehensive permit application is reviewed the
town receives notice from the developer that it will be applying for comprehensive permit under c.
40B. The developer must meet certain prerequisites such as they are an eligible organization; that a
housing subsidizing agency has determined the project is fundable and the developer has site
control. The community has 30 days to provide public comment on the proposed project. A site
visit is also recommended. Unce the comprehensive permit application is received, the ZBA has 30
days to hold the public hearing.
The application to the ZBA must include the following:
===PAGE 2===
• Other information required by local rules and regulations
• Applicable fees
• Application processing
Consultant reviews)
Mr. Costa referred to the 40B hearing protocol listed in the Mass Housing Development handbook:
Basic Public Hearing Protocol
• Open the hearing and read the public hearing notice.
Introduce the ZBA members sitting on the case, including any associate member appointed
pursuant to G.L. c. 40A, § 12.
• Explain public hearing "basics" so the applicant and public can anticipate how the ZBA will
conduct the hearing and know what to expect along the way. (Some ZBAs have a short "code of
conduct" poster at the front of the hearing room as a reminder, and also as an aid to people who
arrive after the hearing has opened). If the ZBA customarily ends meetings at a certain time, the
public should be told at the outset so they are not surprised later.
Read correspondence into the record. (If town staff or members of boards that submitted
comments are in the hearing room, acknowledge them.)
• Invite the developer to present the project.
After the developer's presentation, give ZBA members time to ask questions.
Open the hearing to questions and comments from the public. Some ZBAs routinely set
time limits on individual comments; others take a less formal approach and hold off on imposing
time limits unless it becomes necessary. The board needs to strike a balance between providing
enough time for people to be heard and avoiding needless repetition. Also, some questions from the
public may need to be deferred until the ZBA receives peer review reports. For this reason, it
makes sense to let abutters know as soon as possible what the ZBA's project review schedule will
be.
Schedule a site visit. Note that site visits should be open to the public, but they are not a
"meeting" under the Open Meeting Law as long as the ZBA does not deliberate during the visit.
Decide on a date to continue the public hearing (assuming it does not open and close on the
same night), in consultation with the developer. Though some comprehensive permit hearings can
be completed in one evening, most proposed developments need several sessions. For example,
developers will often propose some changes to their original plans in response to concerns raised
during the hearing. Furthermore, developers can use time between hearing sessions to obtain
additional information for the ZBA and have informal discussions with city or town staff,
consultants, or abutters to the site. Hearing continuances and extensions of the decision deadline
must be approved in writing by the applicant and filed with the city or town clerk.
===PAGE 3===
There is the potential for peer review needed to evaluate the above issues and the developer is
responsible for reasonable monetary costs for them. The developer is not responsible for peer review
or studies on any issues not directly related to the project.
Work sessions/informal meetings can be conducted in between the public hearing sessions but a
quorum of the ZBA is not permitted. During the hearing process the developer may request waivers
necessary to build the project.

Building Commissioner re: Renewal of Annual Earth Removal Permits
Mr. Burlingame reported he had conducted a site visit on August 3r° to the PJ Keating earth
removal permit areas with two of the company's representatives. There are no issues to report;
everything was in line with the conditions. He is recommending renewal on all.
On August 4'h he conducted a site visit at 1325 Massachusetts Avenue site owned by Jean and
Dan Proctor. They are about 60% through the work and he is also recommending renewal. There
were no violations in any of the locations.
Ms. Lemieux noted the owners of 1325 Mass Ave, Dan and Jean Proctor, would like to change
their hours of operation from Monday- Friday (8:00 AM to 4:30 PM) to Monday- Friday (7:30
AM to 4:00 PM) and to add Saturday hours from 8:00 AM to NOON (no trucking on Sat.).
Mr. Alonzo asked what type of activity would occur in regards to the Saturday hours?
Daniel Proctor stated Saturday would be only for site work such as setting up transits, grading
and consolidating piles.
Pam Anzaldi and Clifford Smith Sr., 70 Townsend Harbor Road, via Zoom, stated from a
residential viewpoint asking to start earlier is asking a lot and adding starting early on Saturdays
is as well. Mr. Jeffreys sympathized with the abutters concerns. Typically construction hours are
7:00 am to 3:30 pm, so he understood that is the standard, but Saturdays is out of the norm. He
encouraged the time change to Saturday to be closer to 9:00 am. Mr. Proctor was fine with that.
Mr. Alonzo had no issues with the request but wanted to continue the discussion to next week to
allow other abutters to comment. Mr. Proctor stated the equipment being run on Saturday would
be an excavator, bulldozer and backhoe. After some discussion it was decided to give abutters
one week to provide comment on the requested changes to the hours of operation.

Cowdrey Property Survey of Boundaries on Townsend Harbor Road and Woodland Drive
As a follow-up to the July 13th meeting on the Conservation Commission requesting the boundary
between the Cowdrey Conservation property and 192 Townsend Harbor Road be surveyed to rectify a
possible encroachment and the identification of other possible encroachments onto the Cowdrey property
from another property on Townsend Harbor Road and Woodland Drive, the DPW Director sought an
estimate for the survey costs. We received an estimate of $6,500 to perform a partial boundary survey of
the three possible encroachments boundaries. For an additional $1,500 the property lines could be staked
in the encroachment areas. I have notified the Conservation Chairman and Agent and this will be an
agenda topic for the August 24th meeting.

CURRENT BUSINESS
1. Vote on Town Manager Contract for FY22-FY24
Mr. Alonzo summarized the terms of the new agreement between the Town and Town Manager.
It is a three year term. We have agreed to pay out up to five vacation days in each of the three
years of the contract if they are not used.
Termination severance pay has been increased from two to four months. We have agreed to offer
4% of the base salary for a deferred compensation plan and we have increased her cellphone
stipend from $60 to $85/month. There were clarifications made to language about vacation in the
agreement. For compensation we agreed on the following: an increase of 6% in year one
(FY22), 5% increase in the fiscal year 2023 and a 5% increase in fiscal year 2024. This brings
the Town Manager's compensation into the same range of other Town Managers in the extended
region of neighboring towns. We all felt the Town Manager is doing an excellent job and
believe this is a fair contract and addresses the needs of the Town and Town Manager.
