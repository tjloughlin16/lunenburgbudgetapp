WORKSHOP: OLD PRIMARY SCHOOL
• Meeting with Ray Dunetz on preferred concept for Landscape Design for 30 School
Street
Vil. Dunetz noted this is the 3rd community meeting regarding the 30 School Street site design.
The process started in December 2021 with a site walk with the Select Board and town officials
to get input on what might happen at this park. A community meeting was held in early
December to generate comments and was followed up with an online survey. Based on the
feedback we drafted two options and presented those in mid-January and issued another online
survey. Between then and now we have refined the Master Plan to capture everyone's input and
are looking to finish everything by the end of the month.
At community meeting #1 we talked about the history and some inspiration about what this park
could be. We also talked about the condition of the old primary school and generated a lot of
feedback from the first online survey. It was surprising how well it matched the requests for
proposals for this project. Folks really wanted a town green, community space for the farmers
market and concerts, to enjoy nature with pollinator gardens and to have an opportunity to picnic
and have enough parking to accommodate all those new uses.
===PAGE 2===
bit of sense that you are in the building. A large 40 x 70 foot pavilion shade structure will
provide programming opportunities and a place for the farmers market. One big design element
requested was a strolling path. We created a handicap parking area at the entrance to the park
with an accessible path that goes all the way around. We added picnic tables close to the parking
area and away from the pollinator meadow that we wanted to incorporate into the plan for some
nature interpretation and learning. A large lawn area can be used for a variety of purposes. A
heavily favored concept was an amphitheater with a band shell that would match the pavilion
with granite seating that is nestled into the hillside with shade trees on either side. We would
buffer the southern property from sound and from the park itself with plantings. We placed two
restroom facilities near the entrance that would require utility connections such as water service,
electrical and sewer. At the forefront of the building there will be an ornamental gate that allows
vehicles to drop off their wares or allows emergency vehicles access. We have an interpretive
sign that talks about the old Primary School. The parking area was reorganized from 25 to 48
spaces and 12 new spaces were redeveloped behind the Ritter Building. We created accessible
walkways and crosswalks and narrowed the wide opening from Massachusetts Avenue to a 24
foot wide two-way lane. Also incorporated is pedestrian level, dark sky compliant lighting. We
also considered placing ambient lighting under the pavilion and running electric service to the
band shell for performances. While doing the estimate we included utility upgrades. He asked,
would the Town want to install irrigation there?
Mr. Dunetz showed some conceptual drawings on how things might look. He stated it is a big
project and suggested construction phases over a time period in order to help finance it.
The first phase would be hazardous materials removal and demolition. The second phase would
be incorporation of some of the park elements at the upper level. The third phase would be to
bring in some more amenities with the concert area and restrooms and the final phase is the
roadwork. He had distributed the cost estimate to the Select Board. We have taken the cost
estimate and added the design contingencies, permitting and soft costs and portioned it to two to
four phases. The estimator expects a 4% increase per annum in costs due to inflation.

Lemieux noted placing a warrant article for the demolition costs. Hazmat costs would be
covered under the Capital Plan. Mr. Alonzo added the second article would be the design
costs. Mr. Dunetz stated that would be a conservative cost and in some ways it is better to go
out to bid for the full design and then bid out as separate documents because everything kind
of ties together. Mr. Alonzo asked about the Portland Blues being tied into the existing septic
rather that sewer. Mr. Dunetz pointed out the sewer hookup is very close to the area where
they would be placed. We don't know the condition of the septic as of yet.
Mr. Jeffreys asked Mr. Dunetz if he saw any other areas of opportunities for changes that
would result in savings. Mr. Dunetz stated the restrooms in the alternative pre-manufactured
wooden structure would be a significant savings versus the Portland Blues. But they would be
less vandal proof. Also less lighting could be another option and the curb work and paving
could be done through the Town's paving contract. We did cost out pervious paving for the
site to address storm water runoff off the top of the hill but we could just do asphalt. Unit
pavers inside the building footprint could provide opportunity to solicit donations and donor
bricks, but we could go to concrete. Concrete would give you another impervious surface. Mr.
Jeffreys noted there was opportunity for people to donate trees and some benches as he saw a
community approach to help furnish the park. He asked about water sources throughout the
park other than the irrigation. Mr. Dunetz did not plan water sources as the plantings should
be sustainable. Grass needs irrigation but we can do drought tolerant plantings as well as
native trees. Dunetz stated it could be incorporated into the next level as there is water on the
site.
Dave Passios, 56 Whiting Street, stated the efficiencies are in doing this project all at once.
He asked the Select Board to not cheapen the project by finding ways to save money because
five years later you are spending money to repair of renovate something you spent less on.
Regarding the pavilion roof, he noted solar panels could be installed for offset power use.
The warrant articles will be discussed up until the warrant closes and through budget
planning. Mr. Dunetz thanked the board for the opportunity to work with the Town.

Meadow Woods Trailer Park

delinquent taxes until the delinquent tax amount is paid off, while keeping current with
their current tax bills. The $400,000 payment was provided to the Tax Collector today upon
execution of this agreement.

2. Review of Tax Lien Sale List and Request from Open Space Committee
Ms. Lemieux reported the Select Board has a list of the tax lien sale properties in their packets
this evening. This is a public auction of tax liens used to recover delinquent property taxes. The
Tax Collector advertises the properties in the local newspaper and a company or individuals can
bid on the listed property at the auction. The bidder in turn pays off all the delinquent taxes and
late fees, interest, etc. to the Town and the property owner must pay the bidder or risk
foreclosure. The properties continue to owe taxes to the town on an annual basis going forward.
Mike Toohill, Open Space Committee Chair, reported the committee is asking for four properties
to be removed from the list as they have potential for open space for the community.
Mr. Jeffreys inquired about the tax lien process, does the Select Board have to approve the list or
is this within the Tax Collector's function? Ms. Lemieux stated the SB has the ability to request
she remove them from the auction list.
Mr. Toohill stated 356 Hollis Road, 250 Hollis Road, 56 Carr Avenue are all adjacent to each
other. They are 4.7 acres, 19 acres and 11.5 acres. 101 Pleasant Street is separate property that is
adjacent to existing town open space. Further discussion about the properties ensued.

PUBLIC COMMENT
Mr. Jeffreys stated on March 10th there is a public hearing with the Architectural Preservation
District Commission (APDC) at the Ritter Building at 7:30 p.m. regarding the Select Board's
application to demolish the old Primary School at 30 School Street. He will not participate in
that meeting and had comments about what he perceives to be unethical and unacceptable
behaviors by certain appointed members of the committee. He asked his colleagues to
consider the following concerns and act on them.
Our government structure provides the Select Board with the responsibility of appointing
members to some boards such as the Zoning Board of Appeals, Conservation Commission
and the APDC. It is expected for the Select Board to at times submit applications on behalf of
the Town and appear before those boards. Every member of every municipal board or
committee should vigorously perform their duties and provide the same level of inquiry to all
applicants regardless of who they are. A public hearing should be just that but here is what
happened and it is outrageous.
Several years ago the Town made the decision to create an Architectural Preservation District
in the center of our Town and created a new process requiring those with property in the
district to get the guidance and consent of a new committee, the APDC, to ensure that the
exterior modifications remain as visually similar to their condition as in the past as possible in
a bylaw. It required the APDC to conduct a public hearing within 45 days of receiving an
application and issue a determination within 70 days.
On October 27" the Select Board submitted an application to the APDC to demolish the old
Primary School. One week later on November 4"h
', a Facebook page titled the Lunenburg
Architectural Preservation District was created. In November and December there were two
===PAGE 2===
posts concerning the Methodist Church. On December 15", 49 days after receiving the
application, the APDC advised the Select Board that the wrong form was submitted. On
December the 30" we submitted the correct form. On January 9'" the APDC posted a copy of
that application on Facebook and noted the application had not been accepted. On the same
day they also posted to facebook a photo of the old Primary School with the statement, "The
old Primary School has been badly neglected since it was vacated in 2005. It is the APDC's
opinion that the Town should preserve and restore the building. "On January 11" their
Facebook page posted a photo of a classroom of the old Primary School with a caption that
noted that the APDC would rather see the building sold or leased to a private developer that
could restore it.
On January 13 the APDC posted a photo of the old Primary School with the caption, "Don't
let this building be demolished." On January 31", 32 days after receiving the application and
weeks after publicly advocating against the application, the APDC informed the Select Board
that our application was incomplete. On February 8" at a Select Board meeting we extended
an invitation to the APDC to meet with us and this was communicated to them on February
gth. On February 10' they declined our invitation in an email that essentially said there is
nothing to discuss. On February 11"
", 43 days after submitting the application, the APDC
changed their mind and determined that the application was accepted. They scheduled a public
hearing for March 10'" which is 71 days after submitting the correct application and 136 days
alter submitting the first application. We can all disagree on solutions and results and as I say
often, reasonable people will disagree. Differing opinions and individual talents offered for
the public good, make us better. But we all have to agree on the ground rules. Our bylaws are
our ground rules and the rules say by a public hearing by the APDC must be held within 45
days, not 71 days, and certainly not 136 days later. So what happens when boards don't follow
certain timelines? They constructively grant approvals. The APDC effectively approved the
town's application to demolish the old Primary School when they failed to meet the
requirements of the bylaw and hold a public hearing within 45 days. No one has to deduce or
assume what the APDC's opinions and reflections are about the application we submitted.
They have already told us and everyone else, which is antithetical to the purpose of a fair and
impartial public hearing. This is overreach and obstruction, plain and simple. I have a hard
time accepting that if we are proposing to do something that the APDC supported it would
take 71 or 136 days to even get to a public hearing. If this series of events happened to a
private citizen and if that private citizen sued the town, I would not expect this board to waste
taxpayers' dollars defending what is overtly wrong. Right is right and wrong is wrong and the
appearance of wrong doing which is hard to deny here is the same as actually doing something
wrong. We all have to hold ourselves to the highest standards, so in my view certain members
of the APDC egregiously disqualified themselves from fairly and impartially executing the
duties of their office and should be excluded from being appointed to positions of trust in the
future. It would have been okay for the APDC to hold a public hearing and then deny the
application; that is completely fine but weaponizing process through delays in action and then
publicly advocating as a town agent against an application submitted to them is a serious
dereliction of duty and none of this should be ignored.

unbelievable conditions and unspeakable horrific acts against them in their fight for their own
sovereignty and their freedom. We all see it from all different kinds of media. I can't imagine
