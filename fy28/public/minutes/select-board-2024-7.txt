As a follow-up to the revised agenda for this evening, you have asked for a plan to ensure we use 
all ARPA dollars as required by statute.  At this point I can only give you my thoughts in broad 
strokes as there are still numerous unknowns.  Those are primarily the results of project bidding: 
 
 Tree Trimming    $25,000  Due   10/10 
 
DPW Roof     $65,000  Due   10/23 
 
 DPW Floor     $60,000  Due  10/23 
 
Sewer Manhole Repair $100,000  Due  10/23 
 
Mun. Bldng. Design Comm. $225,000  Target  10/28 
 
Library Teen Room  $120,000  Target  11/04 
 
 Library Exterior Envelope   $60,000  Bid Result $38,000 (w/+15%) 
 
Some of these (see Library) may have underruns which then need to be applied elsewhere and 
the wildcard of getting to contract for the municipal building design committee.  While steering a 
bit off ARPA projects, I do want to also remind you that also have a number of Capital Projects 
which appear to be underfunded (Adult Activity Center roof by $10k+/-). 
 
In order to ensure we fully expend the funds it is my proposal that, to the extent needed, we: 
 
1.) Purchase all DPW trucks w/ARPA reallocating unused funds to capital projects; 
2.) Apply ARPA underruns to ARPA project over-runs and then capital over-runs; 
3.) Adjust any quantity contracts upwards (Tree Trimming and Manhole Repairs); and 
4.) Consider directing funding to foodstuffs for the local food pantry.

APDC Warrant Article for Brooks House with Summary 
September 22, 2024 
Summary: 
The purpose of this article is to save the Brooks House from demolition without 
using taxpayer money.  
 
The Brooks House is an attractive, historic building located at 1033 
Massachusetts Avenue. The article seeks to create the conditions for the 
building’s restoration, renovation, and valuable reuse, using private funds and 
initiative. In order to achieve this, the article asks Town Meeting to instruct the 
Selectboard to sell the building along with a minimal lot footprint. 
 
The Brooks House has a rich history, pre-dating the Civil War (built in 1835). It is 
an architecturally significant building, located at a prominent vista approaching 
the historic Lunenburg Town Center. Its present condition is habitable, but in 
need of significant repair.  
 
The Town of Lunenburg acquired the property in 1946 and it is still owned by the 
town. In recent times, until around 2011, the Brooks House was used as the 
office for the Superintendent of Schools, who relocated to the current office in 
the TC Passios Building. Following that, the property was partially leased to The 
Lunenburg Ledger for a brief period, until roughly five years ago. More recently, 
the main area of the house was occupied by the Lunenburg Public Schools 
Facilities Director, who has since also moved to the TC Passios Building. As a 
result, the Brooks House is now vacant, though the town continues to incur costs 
to heat the building, to maintain fire protection, and maintain the envelope of 
the building. 
 
The Brooks House is one of the buildings that the Town Building Reuse 
Committee, with varying memberships over about 10 years, has been 
considering for town government purposes. The general consensus has been 
that it is not well suited for the requirements of town departments. 
 
The Brooks House is in the Architectural Preservation District as well as the 
Village Center District, and falls under the protections and zoning of those 
bylaws. As such, it cannot be demolished without the consent of the APDC or a 
===PAGE 135===
vote of town meeting. These protections are necessary but not sufficient for 
preservation of this building. Absent a compelling use for the building, the town 
is unlikely, out of fiscal responsibility, to continue to allocate the funds needed to 
prevent the building from falling into disrepair. Without private reuse, the 
building will likely end up being demolished due to progressive neglect by the 
town, much like the Old Primary School building before it. 
 
At present, the building is still overall structurally sound. It is attractive for 
private mixed-use repurposing due to several factors: 
● Permissive mixed-use zoning of the Village Center District. 
● The building possesses a fire-prevention system that is required for many 
business uses. 
● Potential for good foot traffic and convenient location with visibility on 
Mass Ave in Town Center. 
 
Therefore, given the following facts: 
● The building is desirable for private, mixed-use repurposing. 
● The town does not have a public use for the building at this time. 
● The size and location of the parcel are in no way essential for further 
redevelopment of the town’s property for other uses. 
● Residents of the Town have consistently prioritized retention of the 
town’s historic character and structures, both by passing the APD bylaw 
in 2015 and as recently as 2024 in surveys and public working sessions 
related to updating the master plan. 
● Needed protective repairs and routine maintenance and upkeep are 
costly to the town. 
● Renovating the building would be costly to the town. 
● Demolishing the building would be costly to the town. 
● Conveying the building and lot for private use would produce tax 
revenue. 
===PAGE 136===
● Solving the future of this building further simplifies the overall equation 
for building reuse.

RE: FY’25 Goals 
Goal #1: Establish policies in collaboration with the Town Manager and Assistant Town 
Manager/HR Director to improve the quality of the Town as an employer, addressing the issues 
of recruitment, retention, and overall work environment by: 
• Objective I: Creating a schedule for the Town Manager and Assistant Town Manager/HR 
Director to conduct regular listening sessions and staff meetings. 
• Objective II: Implementing an Annual Climate Survey for regular feedback on job 
satisfaction, workplace environment, and communication with management. 
• Objective III: Creating a town-wide employee recognition program to recognize and 
reward staff achievements and milestones through formal recognition programs and events 
to boost morale and encourage high performance. 
• Objective IV: Encouraging professional development to support continuous learning and 
career growth through training programs, workshops, and educational opportunities to 
facilitate staff development. 
• Objective V: Organizing annual off-site employee events. 
Goal #2: Revamp the Select Board and Town Manager Goal-Setting Process 
• Objective I: Establish a formal annual goal-setting process by creating a structured timeline 
that aligns with the fiscal planning cycle to ensure timely and effective setting and 
reviewing of goals. 
• Objective II: Gather input from department heads and Town personnel, as well as elected 
and appointed Town bodies to collect insights and feedback to inform the goal-setting 
process. 
• Objective III: Set measurable and transparent goals by implementing clear, quantifiable 
benchmarks for success, ensuring goals are transparent and progress can be regularly 
reviewed and communicated. 
• Objective IV: Engage the public in goal-setting by utilizing public forums, surveys, and 
digital platforms to involve community members in the goal-setting process.

Town of Lunenburg Select Board 
 
Goal #3: Increase Transparency and Availability of Information to the Public 
• Objective I:  Ensure all public meetings are recorded for future reference, regardless of 
whether or not those meetings are broadcast. 
• Objective II: Maintain up-to-date town data, financial reports, and project updates on the 
Town website, allowing residents to track progress and understand town operations. 
• Objective III: Review policies regarding standards of public meeting agendas and 
minutes. 
• Objective IV: Make Select Board meeting materials available online to provide advance 
access to the public. 
• Objective V: Collaborate with PACC to create "Lunenburg 101" informational sessions 
designed to educate residents about municipal operations, services, and how they can get 
involved in local government, improving civic engagement and understanding. 
 
Goal #4: Work through the Town Manager, Assistant Town Manager/HR Director and the 
department heads to initially create, to periodically review and to develop and secure 
plans, policies and procedures to keep current the following: 
• Objective I: a complete and thorough Organization Chart of the entire Town personnel 
(excluding Schools), including defined areas for each department and any other 
subdivision(s) thereof. 
 
• Objective II: a complete and thorough listing of each job title of each Town employee 
and a full job description of each.  
 
• Objective III: a comprehensive and concise set of Standard Operating Procedures (SOPs) 
for each department consisting of (but not necessarily limited to) their recurring processes, 
daily/monthly/quarterly operations, the personnel and resources involved in those 
processes and escalation path for each. 
 
• Objective IV: a list of technological resources critical to staff performance and their 
cyclical maintenance schedule. 
The first order of business in this goal is for the Town Manager, Assistant Town Manager/HR 
Director and department heads to confer and establish a timeline for all to follow and schedule 
touch points not fewer than one per quarter at which the Select Board will hear progress on this 
goal and provide feedback.

foreclosure process and were outside of the one-year redemption period. The Interim Town 
Manager asked the Treasurer to provide a list and the current process for properties in this 
situation. 
 
The Interim Town Manager stated that there are changes in the auction process because of recent 
State legislation. He stated that it is now required that if the property is sold for more than what 
they owe in taxes, the municipality must find the owner and remit access to them, and if the 
owner is not found, the funds must be given to the State’s unclaimed property division. The 
Interim Town Manager stated that this is not currently within the Town’s policies and procedures. 
 
The Interim Town Manager proposed two changes to s. 3.08 Tax Title Policy and Procedure. He 
stated that the changes outline that if a board or committee has an interest in the Town taking 
ownership of a property, there would now be a more formal process for that entity to express 
interest and describe the potential use of the property. He stated that the second change would be 
to formalize the current procedure by which the Treasurer notifies all departments of the 
property. 
 
Member Renee Emkey asked if the Treasurer has a draft form for boards and committees to fill 
out if they have any interest in a property. The Interim Town Manager stated that he will get the 
form from the Treasurer for the Board to review. The Board provided the Interim Town Manager 
with some ministerial changes for the amended policy. The Interim Town Manager stated that he 
will provide the Board with a red-lined copy of the policy for the Board’s official vote at the next 
meeting.

CALL TO ORDER  
The meeting was called to order at 6:05 PM by Chair Nuzzolo
. Chair Nuzzolo noted that it was 
being recorded and broadcast live on Lunenburg Public Access Channel, Facebook Live, and 
would be uploaded to the Lunenburg Access YouTube channel. the agenda listed all topics which 
may be discussed, but not all items listed may be discussed, and other items not listed may be 
brought up for discussion to the extent permitted by the open meeting law. (Chair did not recite 
pledge due to Flag not being present in meeting room).  
 
PUBLIC COMMENT 
 
Glenn McLeod, 436 Reservoir Road:, a resident, addressed the board regarding the previous 
night's town meeting. He encouraged the board to bring up two issues at the next town meeting: 
pay and vacation time for new employees. He emphasized the importance of these issues for 
recruitment. McLeod also suggested obtaining a report on why there was a mass resignation 
from the Personnel Committee. 
 
Action Re: ARPA Reprogramming 
Chair Nuzzolo began by discussing the reallocation of ARPA funds. He went through the current 
requests that have money allocated, starting with the municipal building design committee, 
which came in under $40,000. Member Jeffreys provided an update, stating that interviews were 
likely to be pushed back to November 20th, with the intention of bringing them under contract 
by the end of November. 
other projects: 
• Water district and Sewer Commission were noted as being all set. 
• Public exterior envelope had $27,000 remaining. 
• Procter Memorial Garden was all set with $1 remaining. 
• Teen room came in at $107,000 according to estimate. 
• Parks Commission was returning approximately $800,000 out of the $1,000,000 
allocated. 
 
Mr. Terenzini explained that the Parks Commission project had been broken into multiple phases, 
and the current allocation would cover 60% design for phases 1 and 2, including cost estimates 
and public presentations. 
The board then discussed the Munis web deployment, for which they were waiting on a quote for 
a two-year contract.

Chair Nuzzolo went through several other projects, including IV pumps, furniture for public 
safety, historical society tree removal, DPW garage floor replacements, and partial roof 
replacement, DPW Tree Removal, and Council on Aging kiosk and Adirondack chairs. 
The board then discussed how to reallocate surpluses to cover capital overruns, particularly for 
the town hall and senior center roofs. Mr. Terenzini explained the need for contingencies in these 
projects. 
 
A motion was made to reallocate surpluses to cover capital overruns: 
Member Jeffreys moved to reallocate surpluses to cover capital overruns as presented. Tom 
Alonzo seconded the motion. The motion passed unanimously. (5-0-0) 
 
The board then discussed a list of potential projects to fund with the remaining ARPA money.  
 
Member Jeffreys proposed a motion to allocate funds for specific items: 
Member Jeffreys moved for the select board to commit $387,565 of ARPA funds for the 
purpose of purchasing body camera upgrades for the police department ($45,000), power 
stretcher and loader ($74,000), replacement of 15 portable radios ($50,000), a stair chair 
($23,000), gas powered cement mixer ($4,720), mini excavator ($72,500), trailer for the mini 
excavator ($14,700), a pickup truck with plow for the school department ($64,780.05), 
Middle High School window safety film ($26,865), and a fence for the council on aging 
generator ($12,000). Member Moore Seconded. The motion passed unanimously (5-0-0). 
 
The board agreed to revisit the remaining requests and get updated information for their next 
meeting on Tuesday.
