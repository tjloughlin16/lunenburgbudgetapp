TOWN OF LUNENBURG
Lunenburg Historical Commission
Meeting Minutes:
March 2, 2015
Joint Meeting with the Planning Board and the Historical Commission
Members Present:
Planning Board Members: Joanna Bilotta-Simeone, Nathan Lockwood, Damon
McQuaid, Kenneth Chenis and Matthew Allison
Historical Commission: Rebecca Lantry, Amory Phelps, Lois Lewis, Richard McGrath
and Cullen Dwyer.
Members Absent:
None.
Discussion of the Village Center District Bylaw (VCD) and Architectural
Preservation District Bylaw (APD)
Rebecca Lantry, Chairperson, of the Historical Commission expressed concern the VCD
Bylaw would encourage commercial growth in the village center district and change the
Damon McQuaid responded the VCD bylaw would actually promote a sense of
community and encourage walkability despite the Route 2A's running through the center
of the Town.
Matthew Allison opined the effect of the VCD Bylaw would be centered on new
structures whereas the APD Bylaw would be on the reuse of already existing buildings.
Rebecca Lantry gave an overview of the APD Bylaw. The APD Bylaw was in response
to the VCD Bylaw and had to be crafted very quickly by the Historical Commission.
The APD would overlie the VCD and encompass the Historical District which is the
center of the Town down to Leominster Road to Fairview Road then down Lancaster
Avenue to Rolling Acres Road then to Massachusetts Avenue and include the Brooks
House, down Oak Avenue to just before Passios and then down to Massachusetts Avenue
in the opposite direction just missing St. Boniface, but not include the old primary school
(high school).
The APD Bylaw provides for a five member commission to be appointed by the Town
Board of Selectmen, and, the Commission would be composed of a member of the
Historical Commission, a design professional, a crafts person familiar with historical
restoration and two residents of the APD.

There was a discussion as to when review should be recommended versus being required.
It was suggested that Section 7 and Section 8 be combined and note alterations that
would require review. Review, if required, would be triggered when a building permit
was pulled. Section 9 (a) change "should be compatible" to "shall be compatible".
There was discussion regarding enforcement. The Historical Commission wanted the
APD to specify that enforcement shall be compatible with the demolition delay bylaw,
and that if violated, no building permit would be issued for two years. Various other
opinions were discussed for enforcement, including fines. The Historical Commission
will discuss further options.
Adjournment:
Meeting adjourned at 9:45

Planning Board: Nathan Lockwood, Damon McQuaid, Kenneth Chenis, Matthew Allison
Adam Burney, the Land Use Director, was also present.
Call to Order: 7:10 p.m.
Present Business/Unfinished Business
The Architectural Preservation District Bylaw draft was reviewed.
Comments were received from Bob Ebersole, Damon McQuaid and Nathan Lockwood.
Amory Phelps from the Historical Commission stated the purpose of this Bylaw was to
preserve the historical treasures in the proposed District and to retain the character of the
Town.
There was a discussion of substantial alteration and minor alteration and the manner in
which these alterations would be treated under the Architectural Preservation District
Bylaw.
Changes: (based partially on the March 2, 2015 meeting with the Planning Board)
Page 1: Section 1 Purpose (d), (e) and Section 2 Substantial Alteration
Page 2: Certificate to Alter: Contributing Property revised to encompass more than just
buildings and to include stone walls.
Page 3: Substitute Siding deleted and Section 6(h) deleted as to substitute siding and (i).
Section 5 Powers and Duties edited to include both minor/substantial alteration and
demolition.
Page 4: Section 7 Administrative Review/Determination of Applicability to include
minor alterations. A Determination of Applicability will either be for a binding review or
for a negative determination.
Page 5: Binding Review. The written Determination of Applicability shall be no later
than 90 days after the application is filed instead of 60 days as previously provided. (The
===PAGE 2===
same applies in Section 10: Procedures for Issuance and Filing of Architectural
Preservation District Commission Determinations).
Page 6: Section 11: Enforcement changed to include demolition by neglect and no
building permit for up to two years.
A motion to submit the Architectural Preservation District Bylaw as amended with
changes as discussed was passed unanimously.

Luoma, Executrix for the Estate of Edward H. Riley and Alan Luoma, her husband, were

Members Absent:
None.
Call to Order:
Meeting called to order at 7:00 p.m.
New Business/Present Business:
The applicant/petitioner, Marcia K. Luoma, Executrix for the Estate of Edward H. Riley
Jr., requested demolition of a property at 790-792 Massachusetts Avenue (known as the
Stone Farm Package Store) owned by the beneficiaries of the Estate of Edward H. Riley.
This demolition included a store front (1730 barn), back of a store (1830 house), the right
side of an addition (1962) and the left side of the redemption building (1979) all at 790-
792 Massachusetts Avenue.
The 1730 barn and the 1830 back of the store automatically qualified for demolition
delay due to their age of over 75 years.
After discussion it was voted the Demolition Delay Bylaw Section 22 (the six month
delay provision) was applicable for this property.
