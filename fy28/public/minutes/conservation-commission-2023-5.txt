single family home and associated site work at 255 Sunny Hill Road. (DEP 208-XXXX)- Justin LeClair, 
Ed McSweeney, and Brian Marchetti of McCarty Engineering. Justin screen-shared the site plan for the 
proposed project at Lot 5 of the ANR just approved by the Planning Board. He pointed out the proposed 
house and driveway to Leominster Rd., indicating the delineated edge of wetlands, the 30’ no-touch, 50’ 
no-structure and 100’ buffer zone, as well as the pea stone diaphragm and rain garden. It is a limited 
project under 10.5 33E for a construction of a driveway from a public way to an upland area. They are 
requesting a waiver for work within the 30’ buffer for a section of the driveway, rain garden and work in 
the 100’ riparian. Justin said they do not have a number from Mass DEP but did get comments pertaining 
to the Stormwater Handbook and the proposed rain garden bmp. He noted Graves Engineering (3rd party 
review) found the project to be in compliance with the Stormwater Handbook. The proposed house will 
be connected to Town Water and Sewer in the driveway. Mary asked if there will be compensation for the 
work in the 30’ no-touch zone and Justin replied the Rain Garden would be planted with native species, 
but they are open to conversation if the Commission wanted more.  Mary asked to clarify if they are not 
altering the wetland, are they clearing within the 30’ no-touch. Justin pointed out the wetland limit and 
said they will be outside of the actual BBW. They are not filling any wetlands but will be just impacting 
the 30’ no-touch buffer. Mary asked if they will be taking trees down within the 30’ or 50’ areas and Justin 
replied yes. He indicated where the erosion control silt fence will be and said clearing will be kept tight to 
the limit of the grading until they get to the Leominster Rd. access. Mary asked how many trees would be 
cleared in that area and Justin said he did not have a number but the forester would mark the trees. Mary

understand the impact so they can argue for compensation. Rich T. said he would like to see the trees 
marked on the map and have a projection on how many will come down. He asked if the grades would be 
changed for the driveway and Justin said it was planned to maintain the current natural drainage path. Bob

drain to the subject lot. Justin indicated the contours on the site plan that allow water to drain to the pea 
stone diaphragm and would not cause a dam situation. He said the swale may have been done a long time 
ago, but it has grown in with natural vegetation and they are not altering it. Bob said they will need to 
continue the hearing because they do not have a DEP number yet and they will need to return with a 
survey locating all the trees to be cut that are greater than 4” or 6” diameter, breast height. They will also 
need to indicate how they will compensate for all the trees removed in the 30’ no-disturb zone, per the 
regulations. Justin asked if the Commission wanted to do a site walk before the next meeting and Mary 
said she would. Mike L. asked to see a larger version of the rain garden planting list and Justin went over 
the list, stating the selection is coordinated with their landscape architect. Bob asked if they have staked 
the house location, 30’ no-touch, limit of work and driveway. Justin replied not yet but they will make 
sure it is staked prior to the site walk. Bob noted the aerial photo indicated the abutter’s driveway run-off

Pursuant to MGL ch 131, s 40 as amended and the Town of Lunenburg Wetlands Protection and 
Municipal Bylaws a public hearing on a Notice of Intent by Steve Powell for the construction of a single 
family home and associated site work at 171 Arbor Street. (DEP 208-1308) – Brian Marchetti (McCarty 
Engineering), on behalf of PSG Realty, screen-shared the site plan and stated this is a 21.3 acre 
undeveloped parcel in the Residence A zoning. The parcel had earth removal activity 30 years ago, long 
before the applicant bought it in 2016. They had the wetlands flagged in 2017 and reflagged in 2023.  The 
project is a 4 lot subdivision and Brian pointed out the wetlands border and the buffer zones. There is a 
priority habitat line bisecting the property, with just over 2 acres in that habitat. Also 2 house lots will 
impact the buffer zones. Lot 2 has a portion of the house, dry well and rain garden between the 50’ and 
100’ buffer zone. It has some miscellaneous grading inside the 50’.  Lot 3 shows the house and 
stormwater infrastructure outside of the 100’ buffer zone. There will be some grading between the 50’ and 
30’. Stormwater will be collected by catch basins and discharged to the infiltration basin, which will 
become and remain town property for maintenance. Nothing will take place within the 30’ zone.  They are 
providing everything to meet and exceed the DEP and Lunenburg standards for Stormwater control. Brian 
noted they are before the Commission for just Lots 2 & 3. All lots have the pea stone diaphragm along the 
===PAGE 4===
driveways to filter stormwater to the rain gardens. Due to being within priority habitat, they were required 
to file with the National Heritage Endangered Species Program and to hire a state approved 
environmental scientist to do a habitat assessment on the property which was just submitted for review by 
National Heritage.  The property was confirmed as habitat for the Blanding’s turtle, with the prime habitat 
located north of the development in the wetlands area. Brian pointed out a sandy area (less than an acre) 
within the habitat which is considered a prime nesting area for the turtles. In order for the turtles to access 
it, they would have to fall down a 20’ slope to lay eggs.  The recommendation is to reestablish a sandy 
area closer to the wetlands by excavating a quarter acre flat landing pad. Mass DEP reviewed it, provided 
a DEP number, and had no comments. Brian stated they are continuing with the Planning Board. Graves 
Engineering did a 3rd party review for the Planning Board and agreed the Stormwater design was 
compliant with state and town regulations. There were no comments in regard to wetlands—only 
regarding subdivision design. Mary stated she had done a site walk with Chris and said they did not see 
any flags. She asked to do a site walk with their environmental scientist and Brian agreed. Mary asked if 
they are taking down trees in the 30’ or 50’ zones and Brian replied yes in the 50’, but Mary stated she did 
not see any on the plan. Brian pointed out the tree line and noted they will be removing trees in the 30’ to 
50’ area in the northern portion of the lot and the applicant will be removing gravel in that area, which 
was included in the NHESP submittal. Chris asked if there will be any town water or sewer and Brian 
replied it will be private wells and septic’s. The town will only provide electricity.  Mary stated she is not 
in favor of removing trees all along the 30’ to 50’ area in order to protect the habitat and resource area. 
Brian said the activities proposed follow along with what is stated in the by-laws. She agreed but said tree 
removal would be to protect house structure or for access to a house structure. Bob noted he has a 
different point of view on the tree removal and asked why they are continuing the hearing. Mary replied 
she would like to do a site walk to verify the delineations.

Pursuant to MGL ch 131, s 40 as amended and the Town of Lunenburg Wetlands Protection and 
Municipal Bylaws a public hearing on a Notice of Intent by Timric Realty Trust for construction of a 
single family home and septic tank at 20 Kilburn St-Lot 2. (DEP 208-XXXX)- Greg Roy (Dillis & Roy) 
noted the lot is adjacent to Lot 1 on Leominster Rd. The buildable area of the lot is segregated by a 
wetland and the proposed septic system would be located on the other side of the vegetated wetland. He 
screen-shared the site plan and noted the wetlands boundaries which have been flagged. The house, 
driveway and septic system will be located outside of the 50’ no build boundary. However, they will need 
to install a sewer force main under the vegetated wetlands. Greg is suggesting a temporary disturbance to 
the wetland resource area to install the force main in an open trench. The septic tank and pump would be 
installed behind the house. The proposed disturbance in the wetlands is approximately 2,100 sq.ft. and 
would be re-vegetated after. The force main will be installed in a sleeve so future repairs can be done 
without disturbance to the wetland area. The species and size of trees in the work area have been noted on 
the site plan. They are positioning the location of the wetland crossing that would not require any 
significant tree and canopy removal. It would be understory disturbance that would be mitigated through 
replicated style plantings. The rest of the work would be outside of the statutory buffer zones and the 
septic system outside of the 100’ buffer zone as required by Board of Health. Mary stated she did a site 
walk and noted there are a lot of trees there that are not shown. Greg replied that they were able to find an 
avenue through that did not result in loss of any trees. They will save the removed wetland soils and 
reinstall it after the force main is installed. Then will overseed with a New England wetland mix and plant 
with anchor wetland shrubs. Greg added they will be removing the trees upgradient of the erosion control 
barrier in a portion of the 50’, to grade to accommodate the house. He did not locate specific trees in that 
area. Anything within the 30’ would not be removed. Mary asked Greg to put both straw wattle and silt 
fence around the limit of work. Jeff V . asked how deep the pipe would be and Greg replied it would be 
below the frost line at 4’. In areas that are shallower than 4’, such as coming out of the pump chamber and 
entering the D-Box, they will put insulation.  Jeff asked how they will excavate a 4’ hole in the wetland 
area. Greg replied they will have a silt fence for limit of work, track the machine in and be careful to 
translocate the soil, install the pipe in one shot, and replace the soil. They will restore the area to the 
original grade. If the ground is not stable, they can use track mats. Jeff asked what type of soils were 
found in the tests and Greg screen-shared the chart for the soil tests and perk tests.  
Public Comment- Susan Brownell, 710 Leominster Rd- stated this is a very wet area and noted there is 
no town water on that section of Leominster Rd. Greg replied they would need to drill a well and be 50’ 
from the wetlands. Jeff V . noted the water main goes to the corner of Kilburn and looks like it would 
service those lots. Bob P. noted he has seen work done under roads using horizontal drilling and Greg said 
it is possible but not economically equivalent to what they are proposing.
