January 10, 2017
The Board of Selectmen met in the Lunenburg Town Hall, Joseph F. Bilotta Meeting Room, as scheduled with
Jamie Toale, Chairman; Robert Ebersole, Vice Chairman; Tom Alonzo, Clerk; Paula Bertram, Member and
Acting Town Manager Adam Burney
Not present: Phyllis Luck, Member
The meeting commenced at 7:00 p.m. with the Pledge of Allegiance.
PUBLIC COMMENT:
Mr. Toale announced the Lions Club Pancake Breakfast is tomorrow from 8-11 a.m. at the Eagle House Senior
Center. Cost is $2 per person. Take-out is available.
There is a member at large position vacancy on the Senior Citizen Property Tax Work Off Committee. Anyone
with tax knowledge is an added plus but this is not necessary to fill the position. If you are interested in serving
please submit a talent bank form to the Board of Selectmen office. Forms are in Town Hall or on the website.
John Baker, 53 Chase Road, presented to express opposition to any kind of a marijuana store being opened in
Lunenburg following the passing of Question #4.
Sue Doherty, Council on Aging Director, came in to clarify the Sand for Seniors Program. This was started by
Outreach Coordinator Faith Anderson about three or four years ago. The purpose was to provide 5 gallon
buckets of sand to elders aged 60 years and older and disabled people who find it difficult to get out and sand
their driveways and walkways. This is being done in conjunction with the Fire Department which received a
grant for smoke/carbon monoxide detectors. They deliver the sand and while there inspect the detectors to make
sure they are in working order. The grant will pay for new detectors for those in need. This program does not
replace the public access sand suspension at Public Works. She stated if you can't get out and get your sand,
contact the Senior Center. Anyone looking for sand can contact all local sand and gravel businesses.
APPOINTMENTS
Cannabis Cultivation Discussion
Brian Striar of Striar Development Corporation and Four Daughters Compassionate Care appeared about a
proposal for a cannabis cultivation project. Mr. Striar primarily a real estate developer, was awarded a license
in Sharon in June 2016 to construct a 31,000 square foot building and medical marijuana dispensary.
He stated he is exploring constructing a greenhouse for marijuana cultivation in Lunenburg and was seeking
feedback from the Board. The project would be a 200,000-400,000 square foot greenhouse with a 30,000
square feet warehouse for processing and storage.
Ms. Bertram asked if the facility would be for cultivation of medical or recreational marijuana. Mr. Briar stated
he would prefer to do both. She asked if the processing would include edibles. He stated there would be a
processing element in the warehouse for drying, curing and processing/packaging for edibles and for shipping to
other locations.
Mr. Alonzo asked if the Town medical dispensary bylaw allows for something of this size and is it just for
medical use. Mr. Burney stated according to section 4.1642, cultivation and/or agricultural processing is
allowed in all districts by right if the applicant is eligible for protection under section 4.13(b), the agricultural
===PAGE 2===
exemption under the town bylaw and M.G.L. c. 40A § 3. Any cultivation in a building requires a special permit
for the construction of the indoor facility. He can grow the marijuana outside without a special permit.
Mr. Alonzo asked since the Town has no bylaw to address Question #4, can the Board limit where those
medical and recreational products go from the Town's perspective.
Mr. Burney stated due to the language of the law, no special permitting for retail sales can be instituted. He did
not believe there is any way to regulate where the products go. Mr. Alonzo asked Mr, Striar if he had any
experience in growing. He replied he did not but he has hired professionals from California. Mr. Striar stated he
did not want to lock himself into just medical marijuana. He stated if Lunenburg was not interested in this, he
will go away. He stated this project could potentially employ 50-100 people if they are able to build it out to
the maximum capacity of 400,000 square feet. We would offer $1.00 per square foot to the Town annually for
allowing him to do the project. He stated he was trying to get out ahead of the opportunity. There's going to be
a lot of tax money and benefits. Mr. Alonzo stated because the town doesn't have anything in place he would
be hesitant to move forward. But if Mr. Striar wanted to go forward with just cultivation he would not be
opposed to it.
Mr. Ebersole asked if warehouse processing is allowed under our current bylaw. Mr. Burney stated the bylaw
states agricultural cultivation and processing; he would have to do further research on the definition of
agricultural processing. Mr. Ebersole stated he was fine with the cultivation part under the current bylaw but
was concerned about the warehouse and when it turns into an industrial use in a residential area.
Ms. Bertram stated she wasn't opposed to the cultivation and drying as well but is concerned about the
processing type and equipment being operated in a residential area.
Mr. Toale stated as a Right to Farm community he was excited in a way about it as a cultivation opportunity.
He was concerned about unintended consequences. He was supportive of the cultivation aspect.
Mr. Alonzo stated he took Mr. Striar's offer of a revenue source seriously. Mr. Striar stated it was an offer.
He could show the Board more information on the processing aspect, but he was willing to take that off the
table tonight. He would want a warehouse structure to process the removal of plant material, drying, curing and
packaging to move to the next facility.
Ms. Bertram asked if he was willing to enter a host community agreement. Mr. Striar stated absolutely but
would want to keep it out of medical marijuana/Dept. of Public Health jurisdiction and just let the Town decide
what they want. Ms. Bertram asked what would be the Selectmen's role in this. Mr. Burney stated the Board is
the representative of the town. Mr. Burney added if down the road there is a medical marijuana aspect of this,
the DPH would be looking for the Town's endorsement. Mr. Striar appreciated the Board listening to him and
asked how to proceed. Mr. Burney suggested formalizing the plan or a draft before going to the Planning Board.
Mr. Striar thanked the Board and exited the meeting.

property lines. He would expect that to be part of the review process. Mr. Toale appreciated Mr. Jeffreys
coming forward; this is why we heard Mr. Striar's proposal last week, to get a feel from the town. Mr. Jeffreys

Ms. Lemieux continued with her Town Manager report, specifically on the Community Compact Program.
This Best Practice Program is a voluntary, mutual agreement between the Town and the Administration
(Commonwealth). In a best practice compact, a community will agree to implement at least one best practice
that they select from a variety of areas. Applications to enter into the Community Compact program are
accepted on a rolling basis. It would be preferable to enter into the Community Compact by February 15* as

best practices. After reviewing a variety of things in Town and the ideas that align best with the Board's goals,

(regionalization of a service or other collaborative effort). She stated a need that has been identified as a
possible application under this program is a permitting software that would be utilized by all applicable town
departments (Planning, Building, Conservation, Sewer, Highway, Board of Health, Board of Selectmen, etc).

$100,000, which is the approximate cost of the implementation of that software.

an application for the regionalization of Animal Control Services. The advantage of applying for Regional

$200,000 and the Town could be eligible for the first year's costs, building of a new kennel, and possibly a new
Animal Control vehicle. Fitchburg would be the lead community with Lunenburg. Townsend is also interested
in participating.

Not present: Paula Bertram, Member
The meeting commenced at 7:53 p.m.
Attorney Bowen updated the Board on the Town of Lunenburg v. Fitchburg Gas and Electric Light Company
(Unitil) suit regarding the December 2008 ice storm.
The Town alleges that Unitil caused it to suffer significant expenditures during the storm; violation of the
Public Utility's tariff; negligence, gross negligence, or willful conduct; negligent and/or intentional
misrepresentation; breach of contract or quasi-contract and violations of M.G.L. c.93A. In 2011 the Town filed
the lawsuit seeking recovery for two sets of damages; the cost of public services the Town provided in the wake
of the storm, the majority of costs related to tree/vegetation removal from roads and public property and damage
to town-owned property.
The Court decision is as follows:
• the court found no private right of action under the tariff; there is no case law for this particular statute,
• no negligence claims and no duty for the gross negligence claims (the court found the tariff bars
negligence claims) the court also found no duty to trim trees to keep them off public ways,
• no damages identified flowing from the alleged misrepresentations,
• damages are speculative and not recoverable under the Municipal Cost Recovery rule
• property damages and c. 93A claims were dismissed.
Mr. Bowen will do further research and contact Unitil. He has also noticed an appeal as a placeholder on behalf
of the Town to preserve the Town's position.

To exit Executive Session and adjourn

Town Manager Fiscal Year 2018 Preliminary Budget Presentation
Ms. Lemieux stated this budget was a challenge to put together in such a short time. She thanked the department
heads for their cooperation. She stated there are some disclaimers and a number of assumptions in this
recommended budget that will be firmed up as we get closer to Annual Town Meeting such as state
assessments, insurance renewals, state aid, free cash estimate when certified, some budgets not submitted yet,
the Monty Tech assessment and the FY'17 ice and snow deficit spending. The goal in preparing this
recommendation was to create a balanced budget that reflected a minimum of level services with certain above
level service expenditures. Those reflect the Town's need for increased public safety services. The
recommendation covers all contractual salary increases, as well as employees covered under the salary
administration plan. It meets the Town's financial policies including maintaining a minimum amount of 5% in
stabilization and is a balanced budget based on the use of recurring revenues for operating costs. One of the
challenges we face is the state aid figure; right now we only have the Governor's proposal for state aid.
On the expenditure side, the liability, workers compensation, police/fire injured on duty insurance coverage
figures are all estimates given by our insurance providers. The budgets that were not received by the deadline
were level funded.
Revenue sources include property tax, state aid, local receipts and other revenues totalling an estimated
$39,850,595. Ms. Lemieux noted that under State Aid, the Smart Growth 40S amount was kept in the budget
but this number may have to be removed from revenue and expenditure if not awarded. Free cash will not be
used for the operating budget but will be used to offset capital items, the contribution to Other Post
Employment Benefits (OPEB) with the balance to be placed in a special purpose stabilization fund.
Expenditures are a reflection of the cost of providing a level of services to residents of all ages in the
community, to visitors travelling and doing business in Town and maintaining and improving our facilities. As a
town we provide services to students, provide health and human services to senior citizens and Veterans,
recreational services and public safety to all. The focus is how we provide these services not on the number of
employees we have. The balance is to provide the best level of services within our available resources.
Expenditures include departments, retirement assessment, non-appropriated costs such as cherry sheet offset
charges, allowance for abatements, tax title and state and county charges. Also included is the Sewer Enterprise,
solid waste disposal services and cable access. All of which are offset by sewer betterments, bad fees or
Comcast subscriber fees. The capital requests and other warrant articles that have a financial impact also have to
be included on the expenditure side but are proposed to be offset by free cash.
The above level services requests we have received this year include the following:
$31,000 for general government for services to recruit new growth costs, small claims court costs and to
increase the band width for data backup,
$228,000 for Police Department request to hire three additional police officers;

$36,000 for DPW for a mechanic's assistant;

$3,000 for Library reclassification and an additional three hours for one staff person
$1.1 million for the Lunenburg Public Schools to fund the Superintendent's/School Committee recommended
budget.

Right now for Annual Town Meeting warrant articles we have the Capital Planning Committee's
recommendations amounting to $633,317. Ms. Lemieux is recommending to proceed with the priority 1 capital
requests to be funded with free cash and using the balance to be used for any general fund articles, placing
$100,000 into the OPEB trust fund and depositing the balance into a stabilization fund account that will fund
future capital items.
Other FY2018 Expenditures include Non-Discretionary Debt Service and Unclassified such as Insurance

background in appellate tax board, open meeting law, public records to name a few. Attorney Hucksom has
been practicing law for over thirty years concentrating in municipal law and land use permitting law. We try to
be proactive to prevent problems. He also attends public hearings on more contentious or complex applications
to avoid problems from occurring in the final decision. I often draft decisions for various boards. We have an

issues from all different perspectives.
Mr. Ebersole asked how you would handle a board that takes a particular position other than your legal opinion
and on the other side, a board that doesn't know what they want to do and would be looking to you for a legal
and possibly business related opinion. Attorney DeRensis stated our job is to be advisors; our job is not to tell a
board what it has to do. Our job is to figure out how to do it legally. So if a board wants to do something against
our advice we'll still help them get it done with one caveat. If they are going to do something illegal or criminal,
we will not do that and we will bring that to the attention of the Board of Selectmen, the appointing authority or
whoever has to be notified. We have zero tolerance for corruption. Attorney Hucksom added the first part of
your question is a situation I have encountered many times. One thing that he tries to do is to make them
sensitive to the issue of how the advice is received by the board and how they use it. Mr. Alonzo asked how
many municipalities you represent and how many attorneys are in your firm. Mr. DeRensis stated he tries to
limit it to four or five municipalities because he believes he is going to be involved. You will have a very active
town counsel that is there for you. There are twenty-four attorneys in this firm. Mr. Alonzo asked him to expand
on loyalty and practicality. Attorney DeRensis stated we will be there for you; we will cover your back and
carry you through whatever struggle you are in. Mr. DeRensis affirmed he would be the lead attorney and
would be available twenty-four hours, seven days a week. Mr. Toale thanked them for coming in tonight.

The split was due to two of the firm's lawyers moving toward retirement and private practice. All of our towns
and eight of our attorneys came with us. Our goal is to create a municipal law firm providing counsel from the
senior attorneys in the firm. Attorney Costa stated his roots are in land use, planning and zoning law. He began
practicing with the former firm in 2004. He has also worked on 40B projects across the state as well as 40R
smart growth initiatives. They have also assisted with zoning rewrites as well as codification projects.
Our firm has eight attorneys and we pride ourselves on all of them being partner level attorneys. We hire
experienced attorneys who have practiced municipal law. We have an experienced litigator. We take a proactive
role in the communities we represent. We also hold office hours as part of putting the counsel back in legal
counsel. This is an opportunity for facetime with the municipalities' public officials. This provides the
opportunity to get ahead of problems. We represent the Town of Maynard and Hanson both around the same
population-wise to Lunenburg. We also represent Deerfield, on a land use kind of scale similar to Lunenburg.
We have represented Lancaster. Mr. Ebersole asked how you would handle a board that takes a particular

would be looking to you for a legal and possibly business related opinion. Ms. Mead stated where the board is
taking a legally untenable position, I would advise the board what the risks are one way or the other. But when

that decision yourself. On the other hand, a board that doesn't know, is unsure, I would politely suggest or find
a way to work into the conversation about what their rights are. One of the reasons our office hours are so

problems. We have eight attorneys and are general counsel to thirteen communities. We represent different
sized communities and are not always doing legal work for them. We don't believe we are over taxed because
