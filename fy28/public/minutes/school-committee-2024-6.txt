4. Public Comment –Mr Beardmore would like to congratulate and thank Mr. Sculimbrene, Dr. Burnham, and Dr. Gilson for 
their great work on the budget task force team, which Mr. Sculimbrene reported last night at the Select Board meeting.  This 
turned into an hour and half of a fairly extensive conversation.  You beat my record for standing up at the podium.  I think 
that all the recommendations, while I don’t agree with all of them, I do think that they are all things that should be pursued in 
part of a narrative to driving greater fiscal leadership and responsibility in Lunenburg.  All that being said I think we als o 
need to keep our eye on the ball, which is, we have a number of things that need to move forward over the course of the 
coming years including dealing with the turf field, dealing with Turkey Hill, and dealing with Town Offices, all of which are 
going to need support of the voters, and now if we are adding a CPA and a number of other things like that, this is an awful 
lot for us to have to articulate, campaign and build consensus around.  Finally in terms of the budget issue, the big issue 
around inflation and proposition 2 ½, the delta between the two, while there are a lot of things that were discussed last night 
that can have an impact the game, is still Chapter 70.  When you run a $25 million budget and $10 million of that comes from 
the state and it does not expand at the same rate as the rest of your budget.  The town has to pick up the state’s share and we 
need to continue to demand leadership from our legislators and our other state stakeholder organizations to make that case 
and right that wrong.

13. Reports Mr. Beardmore reports the Town Manager screening committee met twice last week for about 8 hours, we 
interviewed 6 candidates and have put forward 3 finalists to the select board and will be interviewed in early January.  Last

prioritization list for the FY26.  There were 25 items on the list, number 2 was the Primary School feasibility study for ent ry 
and driveway expansion, number 6 was the Primary School design services for playground equipment, number 7 is the 
Turkey Hill water tank, number 14 is the Primary School generator, number 15 is the replacement of security cameras and 
central server, and number 19 was the Primary School window safety film.  From a f unding perspective, originally we were 
told funding would be around 1.1 million, however, an error was found in free cash and is not anticipating another quarter of 
a million to go to capital planning. Capital Planning will be meeting in January to discuss  long term plans.  High School 
Advisory met last Thursday, they discussed items including cell phone use, to which they recommended no formal policy be 
adopted into the handbook. 
Ms. Archambault reports the Middle School advisory council met on the 10 th and their thought for the school calendar for 
next year is that we should go to school on December 22 a full day and a half day on the 23 rd, they discussed the cell phone 
policy as well, the middle school has a zero tolerance policy, it is supposed to be off  and in your backpack, it was felt by the 
group this decision should be made on a school level not a policy.  There was a discussion on the DC, so far they have about 
40 students signed up, and January 15th is the deadline.  There are 9 families requesting a need for scholarship help, the PTO 
said they would help out with some money as well.  We talked about the guidance staffing, they guidance counselor was on 
leave, she is back and now the social worker is on leave, but has resigned, so they are hiring fo r that position.  Fincom is 
meeting tomorrow night. 
Mr. Sculimbrene The budget task force concluded its work on Monday, Ezequiel and I attended the select board meeting 
and presented our findings. There was lots of interest in CPA (Community Preservation Act), this is a program to match fund 
program where the state uses revenue generated by the registry of deeds to match funds expended by towns, as long as the 
town expends at least 10% in three different areas which are preservation of historic buildings or parks, and updating and 
maintenance of parks, and affordable housing.  There are 200 towns in the state that do CPA.  We did recommend and 
everyone seemed to agree with, is that we need some type of diagnostic audit for the town, there are too many Finance and 
Accounting practices and too many things that go unaccounted for.
