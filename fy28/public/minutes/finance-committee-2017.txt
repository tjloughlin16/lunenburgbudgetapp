2. CPC presentation:
The Facilities Maintenance had requested $248,050 to fund five projects. Three of the projects were
rated a priority one by the CPC. The CPC recommended $216,050 for three priority one projects.
The DPW requested $108,891 for one project and two vehicles. The project and one vehicle were
rated as a priority one. The CPC recommend $65,767 for the two priority one items.
The Fire Dept. requested $1,565,000 for three vehicles: a command car, one engine and one ladder
truck. The command car was rated a priority one. The CPC recommended $60K for the command car.
The Police Dept. requested $ 158K for two cruisers, eight tasers, one ATV and riot gear. The cruisers and
the Tasers were rated a priority one. The CPC recommended $121,500 for the cruisers and the tasers.
The Schools requested $197,370 for two projects. One project was rated a priority one. The CPC
recommended $145K for the one priority project.
The Technology Dept. requested $71,300 for three items. One item, the Police Fingerprint system was
rated a priority. The CPC recommended $25,000 for the Fingerprint system.
The CPC is recommending moving smaller items to operating budgets of individual departments to allow
for trade offs.
Allot any available cash over Capital recommendation to a "reserve" fund to fund large expense items in
the future.
Total Capital Request for FY2018 is $2,349,111
CPC recommended $633,317 in request.

3. Town Manager Report: Budget recommendations FY18: The Town manager is new to Lunenburg. She just
started in her new position on January 17th of this year.
The TM started the budget presentation with some disclaimers: There are a number of assumptions
that are include with this preliminary budget. Some of these numbers will firm up as we get closer to
Town Meeting. State assessments, Insurance renewals, State aid, free cash estimate, Monty Tech
assessment, budgets that have not been received to date and the snow and ice deficit.
The DPW Director reported as of today, the snow and ice deficit is at $86,647. This balanced budget
recommendation reflects a minimum of level service, but contains a certain above level service
expenditures and those reflect the Town's need increased public safety services. Free cash to be used
to fund capital items, with the balance of free cash to go to a special stabilization fund to fund large
expense capital items. The budget meets the Towns financial policy on keeping the minimum five
percent in the stabilization fund. This balanced budget proposal is based on use of reoccurring
revenues for operating cost. No free cash or stabilization funds are used for the operating budget.
FY2018 Revenue Estimates are:
Property Tax: $25,639,730. No new overrides or Exclusions. New Growth estimated at $300K.
State Aid: $8,877,879. There is significant increase State aid which includes $883K in Chapter 70
funds, a 13.9% increase. $39K in General Government Aid, a 3.8% increase.
Local Receipts: $2,913,168. Estimated 2.5% increase in Motor Vehicle Excise tax and $140K in Solar
revenues.
Available Funds $2,419,818. MSBA Reimbursement of$534,198. Free Cash estimated at $900K.
Expenditures: $36,891,930. Expenditures reflect the cost of providing services to the Town. There
were above level service request from Police, Fire, DPW, COA, Library, and Lunenburg Public Schools.
Not all request will be funded.
Expenditures include $633,317 to fund the capital budget, and the Debt Service at $4,796,858.
Unclassified Expenditures includes estimated 6% increase in Health Insurance. 10% increase in
Police/Fire Injured on Duty (IOD) and Workers Comp.
The complete budget recommendation is available at:
http://www.lunenburgma.gov/filestorage/77/1234/FY2018 Town Manager Preliminary Budget Presenta
tion.paf

Council on Aging (COA): The Director is requesting to move Outreach Coordinator salary from the
State Formula Grant funding to a Town funded position. The State Grant Formula has not been
consistent. The State keeps changing the cost per elder. Currently the State covers 13 hours per
week, and the Town covers 14 hours per week. The State Formula Grant is based on the Town
Census from 2010. The census is not scheduled to be done again until 2020. There has been a
33% increase in elder population in Lunenburg from 2010 to 2016, based on the Federal Census.
There are approximately 2800 elders in Lunenburg. The FY17 Grant funding was $21,060. The
Grant funds could be used for other purposes, like renovations to kitchen. The kitchen has not
been updated since 1998.
The COA receives a lot of in kind donations of services from outside vendors.
The COA manages their own drivers and vehicles. The drivers have been sent to skid school training.
MART reimburses the Town thru the Cherry sheet. MART pays for 12 hours of the van dispatcher, the
drivers, fuel and maintenance on the vans. The Town turns over all collected fares to MART.

Planning Board, Conservation Commission, Building Inspections, Zoning Board of Appeals. All are
remaining at level service.

being carried forward as if he was continuing his employment as it has been budgeted for. His salary was at the
maximum on the pay scale. This will allow for negotiating room for hiring a qualified candidate. The position has
been advertised. Presently a retired Building Commissioner who worked for the Town of Lexington has been
contracted until the position is filled.

2. Presentation by Parker Elmore on GASB 75 and Other Post Employment Benefits (OPEB).
GASB75 mirrors what we see on the pension side. GASB75 use accrual base accounting method. The
Rating companies are more focused on the OPEB debt numbers. People are living longer which has
an effect on the OPEB figures. The Town had a small increase in retires, 226 to 240. Liability depends
on number of retires and the age they retire and how long the live. The longer they live, the higher
The Town just added $100K to the OPEB budget.
There were changes in assumptions because people are living longer. Normal cost of the plan about
$400 per person. Slightly higher than State average. Unfunded liability as percentage of payroll is at
about $250 vs the State average of $180. Benefit payments per retire is average. Two key element
that drive the Towns OPEB cost are: 15 covered people over age 65 that are not in a Medicare plan.
The Town reimburses a share of Medicare part B for retires. Most Towns don't offer this.
Normal cost of benefits per employee: $4400 per employee per year. The Town side is average,
School side is a little less. Public Safety cost is higher. They usually have a shorter career, retire earlier
and use benefits longer.
Liability went from $41M to $48M due to change on interest rate. A discount rate is calculated on how
OPEB is funded. Because the Town doesn't fund much, the 20 year Municipal bond index rate is used. 4% vs the
previous 3.25%. As the interest rate changes, as the numbers change. The Financial Institutions are aware of
Expense numbers are about the same, just calculated in a different way. Empioyer share cost: $1.179M to
