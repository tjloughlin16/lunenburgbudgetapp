Ms. Lemieux read aloud a press release:  IMPORTANT UPDATES RELATED TO FY 2023 
REAL ESTATE TAXES 
 
On January 17, 2023, the Lunenburg Select Board voted to initiate an investigation as authorized by Section 3-2(e) of 
the Lunenburg Town Charter into the process that contributed to the FY23 property valuation and tax assessments. 
During this process the date the tax bills were mailed was investigated due to reports it may have been mailed after 
December 31, 2022. Due to documentation from the Postal Service that contradicts the Affidavit of Mailing from 
company that handles the mailing of the Town’s tax bills and their associated postage statements, it has been 
determined there is enough doubt for town officials to determine that tax bills may have been mailed after December 31, 
2022. Based on this information it has been determined the Town will rely on the Postal Service date of processing, 
January 6, 2023, as the date of mailing for the third and fourth quarter tax bills. Following this determination, and per 
M.G.L. c. 59, Section 57C, the due date for the third and fourth quarter tax bills will be May 1, 2023 and no interest is 
due on the third quarter taxes between the period of February 1, 2023 and May 1, 2023. The Tax Collector will be 
adjusting any interest amounts or interest paid on tax payments that were made after the original February 1, 2023 
deadline. Any interest on 3rd quarter tax bills paid after February 1, 2023 will be applied as a partial payment towards 
the fourth quarter tax payments. If both the third and fourth quarters have been paid and the adjustment of interest 
results in an overpayment based on the total amount due for the fiscal year, the Collector’s Office will initiate refunds 
after June 30, 2023. If you have questions related to your FY 2023 property tax bills, please contact the Tax Collector’s 
Office at (978) 582-4130. 
Under M.G.L. c. 59, Section 59, the Lunenburg Board of Assessors has voted that based on the reliance of a tax bill 
mailing that is later than December 31, 2022, the deadline for anyone who has not already submitted a tax abatement 
application is now May 1, 2023. Tax abatement applications can be found on the town website at: 
https://www.lunenburgma.gov/departments/assessors-office/real-estatepersonal-property-abatement-forms-procedure 
For anyone who has previously submitted a tax abatement application to the Board of Assessors and is approaching 
the 90-day deadline, the Assessor’s Office has mailed out extension form letters. The Board of Assessors, and the 
consultant the Town has hired to assist the Town in conducting all the necessary inspections and valuation 
recommendations to the Board of Assessors, is making all efforts to meet the 90-day deadline from the date the 
applicant filed the tax abatement request to the Assessors, but due to the sheer volume of applications, applications 
may require an extension in order not to be deemed denied. Written consent from property owners is required for 
extensions and the length of the extension is at the discretion of the property owner. The property owner also has the 
right to request more than one extension if the length of the first extension is not sufficient. Extension forms can be 
found on the town website at: https://www.lunenburgma.gov/departments/assessors-office/real-estatepersonal- 
property-abatement-forms-procedure 
Questions related to tax bill amounts due or interest amount adjustments should be directed to the Tax Collector’s

Meeting with Municipal Aggregation Broker Colonial Power Group re Municipal Aggregation Rate 
Amendment due to Clean Energy Standard Expansion 
Mark Capedona of Colonial Power Group was here to speak about a small increase coming to the program. 
As the result of a change in law, the Town of Lunenburg’s current aggregation rate will increase by 
$0.00128 per kWh effective with the May 2023 meter reads. The new rate (increasing from $0.11740 per kWh 
to $0.11868 per kWh) will be fixed through the remainder of the term, ending November 2024. 
Mr. Alonzo stated it’s been great working with your company and this partnership has been productive 
for our residents. Given the average usage of 600 kWh per month, the new aggregation rate represents an 
increase of $0.77 per month versus the current rate per customer. Mr. Capedona stated this increase will 
go into effect with the May meter readings and will be seen on the June bills.  
Mr. Jeffreys asked what percentage of the power is from renewable sources of energy. Mr. Capedona stated  
starting in 2023 the required renewables are 59.2%. 
The board thanked Mr. Capedona for coming in tonight.  
 
Meeting with the Fire Chief/Emergency Management Director Patrick Sullivan re: updated 
Comprehensive Emergency Management Plan 
Chief Sullivan reported this plan was updated last May that you accepted. Tonight is a smaller update.  
The Town had gotten support from the UMass Collins Center to develop a continuity of operations plan for a 
major disaster. We started that in January of 2020 and like a lot of other things in life that went off the rails 
fairly quickly. A few months later after we had developed this plan, they reached back out to us. We 
reconnected and developed that plan and their suggestion was to add that plan into the comprehensive 
emergency management plan, so that's now section 21. It is basically a plan for if something affected a lot of 
department heads, management personnel, how we would keep things operating in town; what the chains are 
through each department stuff like that. We also added a little bit of language in the sheltering section that they 
suggested. On the update page, section three, there's a couple of typos that I found in there that I updated and 
changed also.  
Motion: T. Alonzo                                                                                                               2nd: L. Franco

Cost Timeline 
The conceptual design has been created and paid for in the amount of $36,000. The property survey of about 
$18,000 has been completed. The schematic design, costing $45,000, has just begun and was funded at the last 
town meeting. We got a rough estimate for the next phase from the group that's doing this work, it'll probably 
be about the same amount which would be 50 000. There will be another phase in between which is the bidding 
and bid management that's not quoted here but we should have enough from what was funded last time at the 
fall town meeting. The last part of the presentation is the breakdown of the phases and what they are, but the 
way it was conceptualized was the first phase would be cost about $3.3 million and that's the bulk of the 
construction. That's where we are going to build a pavilion, two pickle ball courts, a basketball court, the 
playground and the biggest part of the parking which will be the entrance off Mass. Ave.  Also within the 
pavilion there will be bathrooms. The second phase would be the 90 foot diamond along with the softball field 
and that's $1.5 million. The third phase would be the dog park with the path that's going through the woods 
through Marshall Pond with the overlook. The last little phase will be the overlook on Highland Street; kind of a 
little pocket park there and that's estimated at $86,000. 
As we go through the schematic design and start doing the engineering, the phases might get shifted around a 
little based on what's the best decision for engineering.

Flat Hill Road Drainage Discussion
Ms. Lemieux reported this is a follow-up item from previous ongoing discussions. She noted there has been
work done and meetings with our consultant, Brian Marchetti of McCarty Engineering. He did a study for the
town that was partially funded through town meeting and we had a meeting with the Trustees at Flat Hill to
discuss those results. The DPW Director and the Land Use Director were present as well.
Mr. Marchetti stated he did some preliminary investigations up and down Flat Hill. We had the right-of-way
surveyed, we did some test pits along the shoulders of the road trying to figure out if there are areas
within the town's right away where we could try to collect, intercept and infiltrate the runoff that's been
problematic. The preliminary findings of those investigations showed that groundwater on the shoulders of the
road was high. We did test pits and it was about 12 inches to 18 inches below grade, so it didn't bode well for us
to have a solution within the right of way. We looked at the actual drainage within the subdivision; the
detention basins, the infiltration basins, we did site inspections at different times of year to try to determine
from where this runoff was coming. It was evident during springtime there was a lot of runoff flowing straight
over the grassy areas into the roadway. There was runoff exiting the detention basins through their outlet
structures towards the right of way. We went back last fall and I was did an in-depth study of the actual
subdivision approval back in the 1990s/2000s. I reviewed the plans, drainage calculations, all requirements that
were supposed to be followed at the time. All of the documents that the town had were in compliance with
industry standard, so nothing was designed inappropriately. I found documentation that there was flooding in
that location before the subdivision was built and flooding was exacerbated after it was constructed. So what
can we do at this point! Maintenance of the existing storm water best management practices, and cleaning the
catch basins. Are they functioning properly? Have the infiltration basins located at both entrances to Cortland
Circle been maintained? They still have the same volume that was designed at the time when it was installed.
Those basins if not maintained, collect a lot of sediment which affects storage capacity. When we were out there
I noted in the outlet control structure that there wasn't much water in the basin, but it was flowing so it seemed
there was groundwater infiltrating from underneath the structure. A low flow pipe in that outlet appeared to be
separated, so water was seeping through cracks that probably shouldn't have been. Due to the groundwater
situation, being able to detain it somewhere is really the only way to collect this and let it release it slowly
somewhere else. But we can't just move it downstream to somebody else's backyard. He noted the drainage
system is on private land of the Trustees of Flat Hill, and based on what I saw, maintenance was required. The
inlet pipes were partially buried which tells me that a lot of sediment has built up in the basin. The maintenance
that I saw according to their inspection reports was just vegetation clearing, mowing the banks, cleaning the
bottom of the basin out; that's not restoring the flood capacity of the basin or taking all that silt and material
that's built up over 15-20 years. Digging it out to restore that volume in the pond would go a long way.

Review and Approve the Town Manager Evaluation
POLICY AND PURPOSE
The purpose of this evaluation instrument is to formally appraise the Town Manager's performance on an annual basis. The
document allows the Select Board to assess the Town Manager's performance in management practices and in the
accomplishment of goals. Through the evaluation procedure, Board members will recognize strengths, offer positive
feedback, and suggest any areas that require corrective action.
Individual performance evaluation forms prepared by members of the Select Board are considered work product/personnel
documents, and shall not be publicly released. The only document to be released will be this overview document, compiling
the ratings and comments, prepared by the Chair.
PROCEDURE
1. The Town Manager will be reviewed annually on the achievement of the agreed upon goals established the previous
year and performance standards. The performance standards will be measured using a rating scale of "consistently
exceeds expectations, "often exceeds expectations", "meets expectations", "needs improvement" and "unsatisfactory".
In addition to annual goal(s) ten categories of performance standards and core responsibilities will be used to evaluate
the Town Manager:
2. The Town Manager will submit a narrative self-evaluation including a summary of the Town Manager's performance
against goals and objectives for the year.
3. Board members will complete their evaluations, meet and discuss their conclusions with the Town Manager and give
their input to the Chairman by a date set by the Chairman.
4. The Chairman will compile ratings from individual reviews and develop a composite overview document.
5. Copies of the individual reviews will be given to the Town Manager and placed in the Town Manager's personnel file.
6. The Chairman will provide the other Board members and the Town Manager with the composite evaluation overview
document at a scheduled Select Board meeting. Publicly discuss the evaluation at a subsequent meeting(s).

and most annual goals were met.
2
Meets Expectations:
Performance consistently meets expectations and the overall quality of work is good. The most critical goals were met.
Needs Improvement:
Performance has not met expectations and improvement is needed. One or more critical goals were not met.
