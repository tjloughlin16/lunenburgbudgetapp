PUBLIC COMMENT:
Mr. Toale announced tonight was Selectman Ebersole's last meeting as he is stepping down and moving
out of town to take an employment position on Cape Cod. He has been involved in Town government
his entire life and the concept of giving to the town is part of who he is. He was part of the process to
hire a new Town Manager, Town Counsel and School Superintendent. He has also played a major role
in supporting the building of a new Middle/High School and major pavement improvements through a
debt exclusion. His experience in town government began when he joined the Fire Department as an on-
call firefighter at age 16. He also served as a reserve officer and continued to serve in the following
capacities over the years; Planning Board member (1977 - 1982), MRPC Representative (1978), 250th
Anniversary Committee member (Treasurer) (1978), Board of Health agent (1978-1989), Computer
Advisory Committee (1987-1989), Town Treasurer/Collector (1983- 1989); Registrar of Voters (1983-
1988), Building Committee (1987-1990), Sewer Commission (2010-2013, Chairman), Board of
Selectmen (2013-present, Vice Chairman) and the Charter Review Committee (2017-present,
Chairman). He is a wealth of information on why and how things are done in our town, and he clearly is
an asset that the Town will miss. Thank you, Bob Ebersole.
Mr. McQuaid stated it truly has been a pleasure to serve with you and your dedication and service to the
Town has been an inspiration to me.
Ms. Adams thanked Mr. Ebersole for being a bit of a mentor and for taking the time to understand me
and for being kind. You will be missed.
Town Manager Lemieux shared some archived photos from 1978, 1979 and 1980 of Mr. Ebersole from
old town reports. She stated we are all going to feel a void with Mr. Ebersole's departure. He has
dedicated his life to public service and has taken the true meaning of public service seriously; that it is to
make a difference in people's lives by serving the common good and improving Lunenburg by giving
back. We will miss your wealth of knowledge about every aspect of Lunenburg and beyond. We will
miss your ability to propose solutions that brought about compromise, your fairness, your sense of

Update from the Conservation Commission:
The following is an update from the Conservation Commission Charman Richard Bursch about a
grant the Commission applied for through DCR and were awarded to develop a Forestry for the Birds
Stewardship Plan at the Hollis Road property. There is no cost to the town for this service as a
consultant will perform the work at the cost of the
grant. Under this type of plan, the forester will initially walk the property to develop a site map with
land features and characteristics, as well as an inventory of the woodlands, over story and understory
torest growth, wetlands, streams or other items needed for the bird habitat assessment. The certified
forester will assess the forests looking for the unique characteristics needed for bird habitats. Once the
initial assessment is performed by the forester, a bird biologist from MassAudubon will review the
data and perform his own on-site assessment of the property and develop recommendations for
developing areas of the property best suited for the different migratory birds. The biologist will then
work with the forester to develop a plan based on his recommendations. Once completed, the biologist
will perform a public walk on site to explain his recommendations and how best to achieve them. He
and the forester will present the plan at a public meeting. There is no cutting involved in this plan, it is
just a review of the property by professionals and their recommendations for use of the property. The
plan must be submitted to DCR by March 1st.
Charman Richard Bursch shared these sentiments as well: "The Commission is very excited about
moving forward on this property. It currently has a pollinator habitat being developed on this property
with native shrubs and flowers planted to help attract bees, butterflies, birds and hummingbirds. The
goal of the Commission is to develop the entire property into a diverse wildlife park. They envision
adding forest walking path loops and wildlife blinds for residents to walk and enjoy the fields,
wildlife, and forests. This 60+ acre parcel will allow residents to get out and walk varying terrain
based on time, physical abilities and viewing preference all within just a few minutes from their
homes, apartments and condominiums in the Whalom area as well as all townspeople."

A. Ballot question regarding prohibition of sale of recreational marijuana
Land Use Director Adam Burney reported in March of this year, the Cannabis Control
Commission promulgated their final regulations. In doing so, they solidified the position of c.
94G, §1 which is the law for adult use of marijuana and that communities that wish to prohibit
marijuana establishments, any or all within their community who voted yes on the ballot
question #4 in 2016, are required to memorialize by town meeting vote and ballot vote. In the
Planning Board's initial conversations and research into what people in the community want,
they started reaching out and asking people where they want to regulate it and want it located.
There was a reasonable amount of people who came out and said they would like to prohibit
marijuana establishments in the community for a variety of different purposes. The Planning
Board took that under advisement and discussed and felt both prohibition and regulation was a
prudent course of action. What they would do was have a regulatory bylaw. Both the prohibitior
and the regulations will be presented at Town meeting. The adoption of the regulations is
imperative, even with the passage of prohibition, as should the ballot initiatives fail, the zoning
bylaws would govern the location of marijuana establishments in Lunenburg.
The Board is here tonight to discuss with the Selectmen the option of placing five ballot questions
that could prohibit each type of "marijuana establishment" that could be permitted. The five
questions cover cultivation, manufacturing, testing facilities, retail, and transportation.

economic development.

they obtain it legally or not. If through a retailer, one knows the product has been tested and the
Town will benefit financially and ensures the money is not going to drug cartels.

we are recommending to the voters.

Adult Use Marijuana Prohibition Ballot Questions
Article 16 on the 18 November 2018 Special Town Meeting was proposed by the Planning Board to extend the
Moratorium on Non-Medical Adult Use Marijuana Establishments until the end of May 2019. This article was the
result of the Planning Board's approach to the question of "are non-medical adult use marijuana establishments
desired in the Town of Lunenburg?"
As you are aware the Planning Board initially proposed the moratorium in May of 2017 in response to the passage of
Massachusetts Legalization, Regulation, and Taxation of Marijuana Initiative. The initial moratorium was
intended to provide the Planning Board with adequate time to reasonably review the regulations promulgated by the
yet to be established Cannabis Control Commission. At the time the moratorium was established the timeline being
proposed had final regulations being released in March or April of 2018. This would not allow the Planning Board
adequate time to review these regulations, establish a reasonable approach to how these regulations would apply to
Lunenburg and carry out a planning process to create zoning regulations. For this reason, the Planning Board
proposed the moratorium be in place until the end of November 2018.
the Planning Board discussed the merits of prohibition and regulation there was a thoughtful decision that the
citizens of Inmenbug should be offered both options. As Lunenburg was a "jes community" in the 2016 ballot it is
required to hold a local ballot vote on the prohibition of any or all marijuana establishments. It was initially
thought that the November ballot would be an ideal time to do this as it was in close proximity to when Special Town
Meeting is held and would allow for the citizens to voice their support or opposition to the location of marijuana
establishments in Lunenburg. In discussing this option with the Town Clerk the Board was informed that the
placement of local initiatives was not permitted on statewide ballots.
With the information that it would not be permitted to include the question on marijuana establishments on the
ballot the Planning Board discussed the matter and determined that it would more appropriate to extend the
moratorium with the intent of presenting regulation and prohibition concurrently at the Annual Town Meeting in
May of 2019. This was buttressed by the Board scheduling specific discussion forums on the proposed regulations for
each of the license classes currently being issued by the Cannabis Control Commission. Draft regulations are slated to
be released in January of 2019 and will be the subject of a series of public meetings culminating in a Publie Hearing
in early March as in required under M. G.L. 40A.

2. Discussion on the Contract with Casella Waste
Ms. Lemieux reported in response to the global recycling crisis that began in 2017 when China
enacted the National Sword program which banned materials that were previously recyclable, the
Town has been in discussion with Casella about addressing those recyclables, especially mixed
paper and glass. A glass recycling plant in Franklin, MA has closed and there is no longer a
market in the state for these items. Casella would like the Town to amend the contract to begin
paying for recycling. According to Casella, their cost, for at cost recycled materials is $55/ton.
The Town has countered by going from paying $0/ton to $25/ton. Casella has responded that is
not possible as they would still be losing money. Although our contract with them ends in June
2021, we could face the possibility of being dropped as a customer. Currently the going rate for
single-stream recycling runs between $60- $70 per ton. She believed $55/ton is a fair assessment
for that cost.
She presented a spreadsheet and noted the highlighted section from the Finance Director
showing the impact to the budget. If we were to start this on July 1, 2019, the total impact to the
General Fund would be $58,930. We will need to increase the price of the Pay as You Throw
trash bags, but that is the cost of the bags themselves increasing and the terms of our contract
increasing every year, which could be up to 3%. Discussion about public outreach and education
concerning the single stream recycling and contamination ensued.

of no greater than $55/ton in recognition of the fact that the Town will need to pay for
recycling as of July 1, 2019
