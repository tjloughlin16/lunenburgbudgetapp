Lunenburg Finance Committee 
Meeting Minutes 
Thursday, January 26, 2023 
 
Location: In-person at Lunenburg Town Hall and broadcast on Zoom 
 
Present: Chairperson Evan Watters, Tom Balish, Tom Gray, Caesar Nuzzolo, Jay Simeone (remote) 
 
Guests: Town Manager Heather Lemieux, Chairperson of the Lunenburg School Committee Carol 
Archambault, School Committee and Capital Planning Committee member Peter Beardmore 
1. Call to Order 
Mr. Watters called the meeting to order at 7p.m. and read a statement concerning the 
Open Meeting Law, and how all public meetings are legally being held remotely. 
This meeting was recorded and is viewable on YouTube at https://youtu.be/f3mPfSY148c 
2. Pledge of Allegiance 
 
3. Announcements: none 
 
4. Public Comment: none 
 
5. Town Manager/Finance Director Report: none 
 
6. Capital Plan Presentation: Ms. Lemieux explained the Capital Planning Process, and also 
explained that without knowing the amount of money needed to fund abatements, she has 
reduced FY24 Capital Plan requests from 29 items to 6. The $2,860,000 in free cash, which 
would normally fund capital plan requests, will be reserved to fund abatements. The Capital 
Plan will use $986,000 out of the Special Purpose Stabilization Fund to purchase the very much 
needed ambulance for the fire department, and a pair of dump trucks for the DPW. Also 
included on the Capital Plan are security upgrades for the schools, which will be funded by 
unexpended capital and tax revenue. Ms. Lemieux also explained that the capital plan amount 
can be amended as more information regarding abatements is learned. Ms. Lemieux will 
continue to re-evaluate the FY24 Capital Plan from now until Town Meeting, and will look to 
appropriate funds as they become available. Ms. Lemieux also pointed out that decisions made 
on municipal buildings moving forward will have critical impact on items included on the plan in 
the future. Ms. Lemieux also listed 10-year capital plan requests. 
Mr. Watters expressed his concern over the fire alarm system at the elementary school not 
being on the FY24 Capital Plan. Mr. Gray agreed there should at least be a contingency plan if 
the system does fail at the school.  
7. Tax Abatement/Assessment Discussion: Mr. Watters explained that several Lunenburg 
residents owning waterfront property received real estate tax bills that are much higher than 
anticipated. The Select Board has opened an investigation to find how waterfront property taxes 
could suddenly rise so high. Mr. Watters explained that the Finance Committee does not play a 
Unanimously approved by Finance 
Committee at February 16, 2023 meeting. 
===PAGE 2===
role in property taxes, however their duties will be impacted nonetheless. The town’s overlay 
account will probably need to be increased at Town Meeting in order to cover abatements. 
Without knowing the amount needed for the overlay, how to decide on other financial articles 
could pose a challenge. Mr. Watters suggested members of the committee, and the public to go 
to the Mass.gov website and look at Division of Local Services (DLS) data, to learn more about 
property values and assessments.  
 
8. MMA Trade Show Re-Cap: Mr. Watters attended the tradeshow, and learned about economic 
and fiscal outlook, and heard recently elected Governor Maura Healy talk about Chapter 70 
funding, Chapter 90 funding, housing, and economic development priorities. Governor Healy 
aims to fully fund the circuit breaker, and take a closer look at how small towns are treated 
regarding Chapter 70 funds. Mr. Watters will write up a report on the show and distribute it 
amongst committee members. 
  
9. Minutes: Mr. Balish motioned to approve the minutes from the January 12, 2023 meeting, Mr. 
Gray seconded. On vote, motion carried 4-0-1 (Mr. Simeone abstained).  
 
10. Committee Reports Committee members updated each other on their respective liaison 
assignments.  
 
11. Public Comment: Mr. Beardmore made clear he was commenting as a resident and not 
representing any board. Mr. Beardmore expressed his concern over how the latest property 
value assessments will affect the town in its entirety. Mr. Beardmore is also concerned that

discussion to make the right decisions at Annual Town Meeting. Mr. Beardmore advised the

Lunenburg Finance Committee 
Meeting Minutes 
Thursday, February 9, 2023 
 
Location: In-person at Lunenburg Town Hall and broadcast on Zoom 
 
Present: Chairperson Evan Watters, Vice-chair Chris Menard, Jay Simeone, Tom Balish, Caesar Nuzzolo 
 
Guests: Town Manager Heather Lemieux, Town Accountant Karen Brochu, School Committee and 
Capital Planning Committee member Peter Beardmore  
1. Call to Order 
Mr. Watters called the meeting to order at 7p.m. and read a statement concerning the 
Open Meeting Law, and how all public meetings are legally being held remotely. 
This meeting was recorded and is viewable on YouTube at https://youtu.be/olcG99iRSew 
2. Pledge of Allegiance 
 
3. Announcements: Mr. Watters informed the committee they have been invited by the Select 
Board to attend their March 7, 2023 meeting with the town’s state delegation. 
 
4. Public Comment: Mr. Beardmore corrected a statement he made about the FY24 Capital Plan 
requests for two dump trucks for the DPW at the last Finance Committee meeting. Mr. 
Beardmore also pointed out that the Town Manager has reprioritized several of the original 
requests that fell in the middle of the FY24 Capital Plan. 
 
5. Town Manager/Finance Director Report: Ms. Lemieux reported the Town Accountant 
discovered a projected deficit regarding health insurance, but they’ve found surpluses in other 
areas of the budget to cover the deficit.  
 
Snow and ice has gone over budget by $170,000 due to the increase in cost of salt, as well as the 
cost of hiring contractors to clean up after storms. Money to cover the increased cost of the 
snow and ice line will come from the pavement management line in the DPW’s budget. Ms. 
Lemieux made clear that the money would not come from the excluded debt that funds the 
pavement management plan. There also appears to be a $60,000 surplus in the salary line for 
the DPW that could also go toward snow and ice.  
 
6. Capital Plan 
a. Possible use of ARPA funding for DPW and Public Safety requests: If ARPA funds are 
used to fund the two dump trucks, and the ambulance listed on the FY24 Capital Plan, it 
will reduce the number of items on the plan from six to three. More items couldn’t be 
moved up in priority in the plan because the purchases of those vehicles are slated to be 
funded from the Special Purpose Stabilization Fund. No other items on the capital plan 
are eligible for that funding source, according to Ms. Lemieux.  
Unanimously Approved by Finance 
Committee at February 23, 2023 
meeting. 
===PAGE 2===
7. Tax Abatement/Assessment Discussion: Ms. Lemieux has recommended to reserve all free cash 
in order to fund the overlay account which covers abatements. About 427 abatements have 
been filed. Whatever surplus is left from funding the abatements should be voted on by the 
Board of Assessors to turn over to the town to be appropriated. The Town Warrant closes March 
20, 2023. The Board of Assessors has 90 days (until May 1) to approve or deny abatement 
applications, and then if denied, the people can appeal. Mr. Watters expressed concern over the 
amount of time needed to make the decisions regarding the budget at Town Meeting.  
 
Mr. Watters created and presented a presentation describing Proposition 2 ½ , and explained 
that the levy limit is based on the total value of all property in town. Mr. Watters suggested 
people go to the mass.gov Proposition 2 ½ Primer, as well as mass.gov Division of Local Services 
to learn more.  
 
8. Public Comment Rhonda Lissio informed the committee that she is against using money from 
the pavement management line to fund the snow and ice deficit.  
 
9. Upcoming Meetings Preliminary Budget Presentation 2/16, Department Budget Presentations – 
COA, Land Use, Library 2/23, Police and Fire departments 3/2, joint meeting with Select Board 
and state delegation 3/7 
 
10. Adjourn At 8:13 p.m. Mr. Menard motioned to adjourn, Mr. Nuzzolo seconded, on vote, motion 
carried unanimously.

Location: In-person at Lunenburg Town Hall and broadcast on Zoom

Nuzzolo  
 
Guests: Town Manager Heather Lemieux, Lunenburg residents John Fortune, and Joanna Bilotta 
1. Call to Order 
Mr. Menard called the meeting to order at 7p.m. and read a statement concerning the Open Meeting 
Law, and how all public meetings are legally being held remotely. 
This meeting was recorded and is viewable on YouTube at https://youtu.be/G_gNfRE8my4 
2. Public Comment: none 
 
3. Town Manager Preliminary Budget Presentation: In her presentation, Ms. Lemieux explained that the FY2024 
Target Budget includes revenues for property taxes included adding 2 ½ percent to the previous year’s levy, 
$200,000 for New Growth, and debt exclusion amounts. State Aid was level funded based on FY23 State Aid 
figures approved in July of last year. Local receipts included a $50,000 increase over last fiscal year’s estimate for 
Motor Vehicle Excise tax, but level funded other receipts.  
 
Projected Revenues totaling $47,321,389 for FY24 include: $31,422,519 in property tax (66%), $10, 500,128 in 
State Aid (22%), $3,160,153 in Local Receipts (7%), and $2,238,588 in other available funds (5%). 
For Expenditures, Ms. Lemieux used FY23 amounts regarding cherry sheet offsets, state and county charges, and 
choice/charter assessments. FY24 will use stabilization funds to offset the 8.5 percent increase in health 
insurance, as well as a 5 percent rate increase for retirees. Expenditures also include an additional $50,000 for 
pavement management, known principal and interest debt payments for FY24, a 5 percent increase for general 
insurances for the police and fire departments, a 13.55 percent increase for Worcester County Retirement, and 
a 2.5 percent increase for Lunenburg Public Schools and Monty Tech. Unknown expenditures for the Overlay 
Account, which covers abatements, will affect how certified free cash is spent for FY24. More information on 
expenditures as follows:  
Unanimously Approved by Finance 
Committee at February 23, 2023 
meeting.

4. Tax Abatement/Assessment Discussion: Mr. Menard explained that the finance committee is the only 
legislative body in town, and its job is to advise Town Meeting on the budget. Mr. Fortune informed the 
committee that those with waterfront property have formed the Lunenburg Lakes Action Committee, and 
around 420 people have filed abatements after the real estate tax bills went out. Mr. Fortune opined that if no 
mistake has been made by the assessor’s office, then the people with waterfront property have been singled out 
as their property values have gone up 77 percent on average, while other properties in town have gone down, 
or only risen incrementally. Mr. Fortune explained that no one foresees the tax rate going down in FY24, and 
property owners will have to file for abatements again. Mr. Fortune added that if a clerical error has been made 
by the Board of Assessors, they can admit the error, and go to the Department of Revenue to have that error 
corrected. Ms. Bilotta added that she has spoken with four other assessors in the area, and all explained that 
when property values increase, taxes should increase incrementall. Ms. Bilotta talked about the people who 
have been living in town for several years, and are now on a fixed income, and asked how they’re supposed to 
afford a 135 percent increase. Ms. Bilotta also added that all who’ve filed abatements could attend Town 
Meeting and vote no on all the articles. Mr. Menard urged the Lakes Action Committee to send any ideas or 
questions to the Finance Committee and Select Board to discuss with the state delegation at the March 7 
meeting.  
 
5. State Representative Meeting Preparation: Topics raised include: funding formulas, such as Chapter 70, and 
vocational schools, and real estate appraisal. Mr. Watters suggested sending questions out to the delegation 
before the meeting so they can prepare responses.  
 
6. Minutes: Mr. Watters motioned to approve the minutes from the January 26, 2023 meeting. Mr. Nuzzolo 
seconded. On vote, motion carried unanimously.  
 
7. Future Meetings: March 30 Public Hearing on FY2024 budget.  
 
8. Public Comment: 
 
a. Board Comment:  Mr. Gray commented that he hopes 420 people don’t show up to Town Meeting to 
vote everything down, and suggested that taking that kind of action won’t solve any problems.

Appendix A: 
 
===PAGE 3===
Lunenburg continues to operate with solid fiscal policy, allowing for the weathering of unexpected storms. Under sound 
management and conservative practices in prior years, the Town stands poised to fund the needs of all departments and 
the residents within. The process of developing and presenting a budget to the Annual Town Meeting begins in the early 
fall with the Town Manager meeting with department heads to discuss expenses, wants, and needs. The Town Manager 
then presents the proposed budget to the Finance Committee in mid-February. Over the following 90 days, the Finance 
Committee meets with the various department heads to discuss their individual line-item budget requests. The Finance 
Committee then votes on the recommendation of the finalized omnibus budget provided by the Town Manager and 
reports to the Annual Town Meeting. As the Legislative body acting on the Town’s behalf, the Finance Committee 
recommends the approval of the budget as presented at the Annual Town Meeting.  
Historically, the Capital Plan has been funded though free cash, taxes revenues, and unspent capital appropriation from 
prior years. For FY24, the available free cash is substantially lower due to the need to fill the overlay account with 
enough funds to offset a worst-case scenario with resident tax abatements. Without the use of ARPA funds, the 
proposed Capital Plan would fund $1,039,010, a 52% decrease from FY23. With the inclusion of the ARPA funds, the 
proposed Capital Plan funds $2,025,010, a 6.4% decrease from FY23. This conservative approach allows the Town to 
fund critical items in the Capital Plan while reserving enough money to cover potential abatements.  
Understandably, a significant issue on most resident’s minds revolves around the current assessments and subsequent 
tax levy. While the personal positions, understanding, and potential outcomes of the taxpayers may widely vary, there 
are a fair number of certainties.   
1. The increases felt by some taxpayers do not indicate a windfall of cash for the Town. Lunenburg is restricted by 
Proposition 2½ (Mass. Gen. L. c. 59, § 21C) and, as such, is unable to raise the overall levy of the Town by anymore 
that 2½% of the previous year’s levy limit plus any New Growth and Excludible Debt. The table below outlines the 
changes in taxes, showing the modest increase from year to year.

2. Many Town residents have filled abatements, disputing the assessments made of their homes. Should all 
abatements be granted, the Town would under-levy approximately $1,500,000. To best cover this potential 
outcome, the Town Manager has recommended the transfer of $1,500,000 from Free Cash to the Overlay 
Account.  The Town residents would not be responsible for any abatements granted.  
3. The abatement process and Overlay Account are the proper methods of both addressing the issue and reversing 
the financial burden placed on some of the Town residents.  
Noteworthy topics:  
• The end of FY23 will see a 14.6% reduction of our Debt Service and Excludible Debt, from $3,174,257.53 (FY23) 
to $2,712,069.90 (FY24). This decrease is the result of the retiring of excludible debt from the Public Safety Building 
and the Primary School as well as a completed MSBA reimbursement for the Primary School. As a result, debt 
capacity is made available for future projects. An analysis, run by Unibank, projected a variety of scenarios to guide 
the Town in debt capacity spending over the next 30 years. In total, the Town has $42,000,000 that will be useable 
while remaining under debt capacity limits. With this number in mind, the Select Board, Municipal Building Design 
Committee, and the Town will be better prepared to plan for the future.  
• Federal relief, during the COVID-19 pandemic, provided Lunenburg with $3,500,000 in ARPA funds and 
$1,900,000 in ESSER funds. ARPA funding is to be spent at the discretion of the Select Board. In an attempt to 
minimize the delivery time, the Select Board voted to spend $986,000 in ARPA funds to order three items from the 
Capital Plan; a replacement Rescue Ambulance ($506,000) for the Fire Department and two-6 wheel dump trucks 
($240,000 each) for the DPW. The remaining $2,514,000 has yet to be allocated as the Select Board considers 
recommendations from various department heads and Town citizens. ESSER funds allowed the School Department 
to hire 13 new positions across the district. Social workers, guidance counselors, subject specialists, tutors, and 
technicians were all brought into the school to provide much needed support to the Town’s youth. The School 
===PAGE 4===
Department will need to look for additional funding or other ways to maintain the new staff as the last of the ESSER 
funding is required to be spent by the end of September 2024.   
• Cherry Sheet offsets and other State monies have historically, on average, underwhelmed the Town. While FY23 
did see a larger than expected increase (11.5%) in Chapter 70 (public school funding) funds, the outlook for FY24 is 
the likely mandatory minimum increase resulting in a 0.6% increase. In FY23, the Ch. 70 increase was more than 
enough to cover the 20.4% increase in the Monty Tech assessment. For FY24, the 0.6% increase will not be enough 
to offset the 12% increase in the Monty Tech Assessment. The Finance Committee met with our State Delegation 
and pushed for a reassessment of the formulas used to determine funding for small municipalities.

department heads that gave their time and effort to produce the budget presented at the Annual Town Meeting. We 
recommend the passage of the Town Manager’s omnibus budget and the funding of Lunenburg’s FY24.
