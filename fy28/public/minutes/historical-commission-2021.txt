LUNENBURG HISTORICAL COMMISSION 
MINUTES OF MEETING 
THURSDAY JUNE 23, 2021 
Meeting called to order by Chairman Richard McGrath at 7:00  pm with a quorum present.  Also present was 
Cemetery Commissioner William Tyler. Minutes from the May meeting were submitted by William Lakso, and 
accepted.  Copies of the minutes from March and May have been published and submitted to committee 
members. 
CORRESPONDANCE Matt Allison has been in touch with Richard McGrath regarding the barn at 188 Flat 
Hill, which is a K-House.  The carpenter consulted thinks it can be saved, and Mr. Allison is amenable to the 
repairs. 
William Tyler reports a gaping hole in the roof of the Battle House on Elm Street. 
NO NEW BUSINESS 
OLD BUSINESS: Curbing has been donated by Powell’s for the Arnold-Litch Cemetery. 
Question on availability of funding for a wall at Goodrich family cemetery this year. 
William Lakso reported the publication on May 16 of the Jones House, with two other articles appearing soon. 
APDC UPDATE: The Primary School was recently vandalized, and remains in violation. Windows need to be 
boarded up, shingles replaced. This is now three years of demolition by neglect. 
The owner of the former Methodist Church has ignored letters sent regarding the deteriorating condition of the 
steeple. 
The Town Hall requires repairs before the weathervane can be erected. 
The idea of a preservation award for people restoring older homes was revisited.  
The Flat Hill and Page St. directional stones need to be cleaned and replaced.  They are currently located at the 
Historical Society. 
William Lakso will announce Megan Lordan’s appointment to the Historical Commission in the Lunenburg 
Ledger.   
Next meeting is Tuesday September 21, 2021.   
Meeting adjourned at 7:50. 
 
Jennifer Sanderson 
Clerk

LUNENBURG HISTORICAL COMMISSION 
SEPTEMBER 7, 2021 
MINUTES 
 
Chairman Richard McGrath opened the monthly meeting at the Ritter Memorial Building at 7:01 p.m. with a 
quorum present. 
Minutes of the June 23, 2021 meeting we read and amended.  On motion regularly made and seconded, it was 
unanimously VOTED:  To approve the minutes as amended. 
No correspondence was introduced.  Public Comment was passed by, there being no public present. 
New business introduced:  The Pond View 40B project of Mr. Kevin O’Brien was introduced by Mr. McGrath 
and discussed. Mr. Lakso was requested for formulate a response to the town manager, citing the commission’s 
experience in working with Mr. O’Brien during his application for demolition of the former Aro Poultry Farm 
home at 400 Howard Street. O’Brien’s failure to properly secure the house and lack of response to the 
demolition delay requirements of Chapter 134-4 of town bylaws will be included in the response. 
That same discussion segued into the APDC update listed in the agenda.  Mr. McGrath indicated that to reach 
the required percentage of Chapter 40B housing, eighteen units are required.  The Primary School, he stated, 
could accommodate low-income housing to satisfy the requirement, if renovated for that purpose.  It is apparent 
that the select board favors demolishing the building at present. Should the select board appeal a negative ruling 
by the APDC, a vote of the people at town meeting will be the deciding factor. 
Jennifer Sanderson and Megan Lordan were asked to research the residency if African Americans in town.  It 
was noted by Mr. McGrath that historically, there were “indentured servants” engaged here.  It was suggested 
that the Fitchburg Historical Society may have additional information. 
Demolition Delay Bylaw amendments were sent to the town manager March 30 by Mr. Lakso.  He was asked to 
follow up to determine the current status.  
Mr. McGrath reported the memorial stone and plaque for the Arnold-Litch burial ground are ready for 
installation, tentatively on September 20.  A proper base has been prepared. Kevin Boussom is invited to be 
present to video record the installation. 
Additional restoration work on the Goodrich family burial ground requires the removal of brush, to be 
accomplished by the DPW. Mr. McGrath has obtained a blank marble headstone and will recreate the badly 
damaged Captain Goodrich stone. 
The Cemetery Commission, with the assistance of Mr. McGrath, is working on preparing a Form E for the town 
tomb at North Cemetery, which will be submitted to the Massachusetts Historical Commission. 
Mr. Lakso reported that articles for the “Historically Speaking” are completed and ready for publishing, ending 
in January 2022. 
The next meeting of the commission was established as October 5, 2021 at 7:00 p.m. 
With no further business at hand, the meeting was adjourned at 7:55 p.m.

LUNENBURG HISTORICAL COMMISION 
DECEMBER 14, 2021 
MINUTES 
A regular meeting of the Commission was held this date at the Ritter Memorial Building, 
Lunenburg, Massachusetts. Chairman Richard McGrath opened the meeting at 7:00 p.m. with a 
quorum present. Members Sanderson and Lordan were absent 
Reading of the minutes of November 9 was deferred to the January meeting. 
A request of the town manager seeking opinions related to repairs to the Ritter Memorial 
building and town hall resulted in the following: 
1. Repair of shutters for the Ritter building – not required 
2. Town hall chimneys – should remain 
3. Ventilator on town hall – remove 
Mr. McGrath reported funds of $3,000.00 are available as the commission’s budget until July. 
A commemorative plaque listing names of sponsors of the memorial at the Arnold/Litch burial 
ground site was discussed. One motion was made and seconded, it was unanimously voted to 
procure a cast bronze plaque following Mr. McGraths determination of the cost. 
Mr. Lakso will contact the town manager relative to finalizing the language of bylaw 
amendments approved at town meeting, and if approval is required from the office of the 
attorney general. 
Mr. McGrath reported that due to lack of manpower, there is a delay in installing granite curbs at 
the Arnold/Litch burial site memorial. Also, a missing architectural preservation district sign has 
been replaced and it is in the hands of the DPW. 
Mr. McGrath’s efforts to preserve the primary school continue. Entry has been denied by the 
town manager due to the existence of black mold. No update on African Americans in 
Lunenburg project as both members were not present. In the meantime, Mr. McGrath plans to 
visit other communities where building re-use and preservation of old school buildings have 
been successful. 
 
There being no further business, the meeting was adjourned at 7:30 PM. 
 
Prepared by Bill Lakso  Edited by Kevin Boussom
