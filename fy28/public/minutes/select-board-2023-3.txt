Ana Lockwood, 91 Main Street: Refuted claims of a "backdoor deal" between the Cemetery

inside deals, and emphasized the need for respectful discourse.

residents about public discourse at town meetings, stressing the importance of collaboration.

COMMUNICATIONS FROM OTHER BOARDS 
 
Council on Aging: Deb Lincoln updated on the Council on Aging (COA), noting new members, 
rebranding initiatives, programs like the Century Club for centenarians, and volunteering efforts. 
She urged for increased funding for positions at the Lunenburg Adult Activity Center, 
highlighting the current difficulty in attracting applicants due to the unappealing wages and 
hours. 
300
th Anniversary Committee: Pete Lincoln shared the committee's objectives for the 300th 
Anniversary celebration, including proposed activities and encouragement for volunteers to join. 
Scheduled events include a First Night celebration on December 31, 2027, an anniversary party 
on July 28, 2028, and a grand parade the first weekend after Labor Day in 2028. 
Public Access Cable Committee (PACC): Steve Walker presented an update on PACC's 
current undertakings, which encompass financial and budget evaluations, studio tours, 
technology assistance, policy formulation, and the ARPA funding proposal. The committee has 
recommended to the Select Board the necessity for establishing a specific location and timeline 
for the new studio. 
Conservation Commission: Mary Wilson elaborated on the Commission's initiatives to manage 
and enhance conservation lands. She detailed the Commission's achievements, such as fostering 
a thriving pollinator habitat that supports native flora and fauna, coordinating a month-long 
series of Earth Day events that improved 13 properties with the help of 161 volunteer hours, and 
securing a mass trails grant to enhance access to local conservation areas. Collaborations with 
residents around Lake Shirley and Hickory Hills were also highlighted as part of their robust lake 
management plans aimed at preserving these vital ecosystems. Despite facing challenges from 
notable staffing changes this year, the Commission has persevered, focusing on sustaining their 
endeavors. New proactive measures, including the distribution of informational flyers to educate 
the community about wetland protection and conservation practices, have also been introduced 
to promote environmental awareness and engage residents. 
Architectural Preservation District Commission (written report submitted by Anthony 
Sculimbrene): The Architectural Preservation District Commission (APDC) handled certificates 
to alter (CTAs) for 993 and 912 Massachusetts Avenue, related to roofing work approved as 
routine maintenance. The APDC reviewed a CTA for 1025 Massachusetts Avenue (Ritter 
Memorial Building), which included design changes to the handicap entry and window designs. 
The CTA was approved on September 12, 2023. Nathan Lockwood joined APDC, while Alyssa 
Dwyer's position remains vacant. APDC is revising procedures and creating a brochure on 
bylaws to help district property owners. As part of the master plan project, APDC ensures the 
preservation district's historical assets are recognized in future town guidelines. 
Recommendations include aligning reporting to fiscal years and offering digital training for 
volunteers. 
Zoning Board of Appeals (written report): The Zoning Board of Appeals (ZBA) has five 
members and three associate members appointed by the Select Board under MGL Chapter 40A,

The ZBA hears appeals per MGL Chapter 40A, Section 8, involving grievances from denied

acreage requirements with state guidelines, featured in Article 13 at the 2023 town meeting. 
Community engagement was bolstered by creating market identity through market bags, with 
proceeds supporting Lunenburg 4-H. The Commission advised raising awareness of Chapter 143 
of the Lunenburg Code to enhance transparency and support for agriculture.

their exemplary response to the first storm of the season, emphasizing their effective road

and roadways. Additionally, the sewer business manager demonstrated proactiveness by 
identifying open manholes through social media monitoring. Highway Superintendent addressed 
this situation efficiently on Thanksgiving by replacing the covers and later securing them to 
prevent future issues, thereby ensuring public safety during critical times.

COMMITTEE REPORTS 
Planning Board: Member Alonzo noted the Planning Board has been actively engaged in 
finalizing their goals for the year, with a strong focus on advancing crucial projects like the water 
protection map update. They aim to address feedback from the recent Special Town Meeting, 
where the proposal did not pass, by refining the plan to gain broad consensus. Interim support by

School Committee: The School Committee recently announced a significant appointment, 
naming Ellen Whittemore the new Director of Administration and Finance. Set to commence her 
duties in February, following the expiration of her current contract, Whittemore's entry marks a 
notable staff addition, promising leadership in financial planning and operation management at 
the district level. Her appointment is anticipated to lend strategic direction in furthering the 
School Committee's objectives, including enhancing educational infrastructure, staffing, and 
policymaking in line with modern pedagogic standards. 
Economic Development Committee: Set to meet this Thursday, the pending quorum 
underscores the importance of regularly congregating to progress strategic economic plans.

exploring new opportunities, maximizing available resources, and encouraging business growth 
and sustainability within Lunenburg. The committee remains proactive in evaluating upcoming 
trends and aligning with community-centric development ideals that promise to foster fiscal 
health and communal prosperity.

Mr. McDonald spoke expressing deep appreciation and support for troops deployed abroad. He 
shared a personal connection to the military, highlighting that his family is part of a Gold Star

input to inform future town development strategies. The survey's design prioritizes accessibility,

their perspectives are heard. Additionally, the Board expressed gratitude towards the community

individuals who participated in the town meeting, encouraging more residents to engage in civic 
matters. Board members collectively stressed the need for continuous public involvement in 
local governance, reinforcing the importance of resident contributions in shaping Lunenburg's 
future.

Presentation and Review of New Fee Schedules for the Building Department – Brian 
Gingras, Building Commissioner. The building commissioner presented a review of the new 
fee schedules for the building department, explaining adjustments based on surveys conducted of 
neighboring towns. Listings included residential and commercial building fee changes, with set 
rates of $12.75 per $1,000 for commercial and $10 per $1,000 for residential projects, as well as 
a minimum permit fee for commercial projects set at $150. The schedules also established rates 
for work orders and occupancy permits, such as $50 per unit for occupancy and a $75 fee for 
work without a permit. The presentation was an in-depth comparison with surrounding 
communities showcasing how the proposed increases generally aligned with median rates for the 
area. 
 
The board decided to postpone the vote on the new fee schedules to provide the public with an 
opportunity for comments and to ensure ample review, planning to revisit the decision at a future 
meeting.

CALL TO ORDER AND PLEDGE OF ALLEGIANCE 
 
The meeting was called to order at 7:00 PM by Chair Jeffreys. The Pledge of Allegiance was 
recited. Chair Jeffreys noted that it was being recorded and broadcast live on Lunenburg Public 
Access Channel, Facebook Live, and would be uploaded to the Lunenburg Access YouTube 
channel. 
 
BOARD COMMENTS 
Selectman Tom Alonzo thanked the Council on Aging and Lunenburg Activity Center for hosting 
a 100th birthday celebration for Mario Patacchiola. He noted it was the town's third centenarian 
and expressed amazement at knowing all three residents who have reached 100 years old. 
Selectman Lou Franco added that he attended the event as well and presented a letter from the 
Select Board to Mario. 
 
PUBLIC COMMENTS 
Guy Gauvin of 389 Highland Street inquired about the process for the public hearing on propane 
tanks later in the agenda. Chairman Jeffreys clarified there would be a presentation followed by 
an opportunity for public comments. 
 
COMMUNICATIONS FROM OTHER BOARDS 
Chairman Michael-Ray Jeffreys reported receiving correspondence from John Reynolds, Chair 
of the Sewer Commissioners, expressing concerns about the Munis software conversion and its 
impact on billing and staff hours. Town Manager Heather Lemieux addressed ongoing issues 
with Munis, the town's accounting software for receivables, payables, and utility billing. The 
conversion impacted the Sewer Commission over billing inaccuracies, tied to resource 
constraints from Munis staff. The town’s IT and business manager are actively seeking 
resolution, and the absence of a dedicated project manager. They have since appointed a 
regularly consulted technician. Future testing will involve pre-production environments to 
preempt issues. Selectman Tom Alonzo expressed concern about the length of time (6+ weeks) it 
has taken to resolve the billing issues, given their impact on town revenue. He suggested 
requesting a root cause analysis report from Munis and regular progress updates. Member Renee 
Emkey, who works with enterprise-level software, suggested implementing more thorough 
testing procedures for future upgrades, including test servers and bill generation tests. Town 
Manager Lemieux clarified that the real estate tax bills were not affected by these issues and 
reviewed them with the tax collector to ensure accuracy.

APPOINTMENTS 
7:10 PM Public Hearing for the application for license(s) for keeping & storage of four 
(1000 gallon) propane tanks to be located at 647 Northfield Road and four (1000 gallon) 
propane tanks to be located at 677 Northfield Road by DiLeo Gas Co. on behalf of owner 
Stephen Violette 
Chairman Michael-Ray Jeffreys opened the public hearing at 7:16 PM. Paul DiLeo of DiLeo Gas 
presented the application on behalf of owner Steven Violette. He explained the request was for 
four 1,000-gallon propane tanks at each of the two addresses to provide cleaner, more efficient 
heating for Dick's Market Garden greenhouses. DiLeo outlined safety measures including 
vehicle crash protection, underground gas lines, leak mitigation systems, and regular inspections. 
He noted the fire department had reviewed and approved the plans. The Select Board asked 
questions about:  
• Efficiency compared to current oil heating (propane heaters will be 93% efficient)  
• Distance to nearest residences (over 200 feet)  
• Refill frequency (every 2 weeks in winter, not at all in summer)  
• Safety regulations and blast radius  
• Above-ground vs underground tanks  
Guy Gauvin, an abutter, asked questions about:  
• Above vs underground tank safety  
• Potential for asphyxiation from leaks  
• Visibility of tanks from his property  
Steven Violette provided additional details on tank locations and existing visual barriers. He 
expressed willingness to add landscaping screening if neighbors had concerns. The Board 
discussed conditions around screening and neighbor communication. Chairman Jeffreys closed 
the public hearing at 8:09 PM.  
Member Alonzo made Motion to approve the application for the licenses of four 1,000 
gallon propane tanks at 647 Northfield Road and four 1,000 gallon propane tanks to be 
located at 677 Northfield Road as presented by DiLeo Gas on behalf of Steven Violette. 
Member Franco seconded. The motion passed unanimously by roll call vote (5-0-0): 
Member Nuzzolo: Aye; Member Emkey: Aye; Member Alonzo: Aye; Member Franco: Aye; 
Chairman Jeffreys: Aye.

community:

sufficient response.

• Board and committee vacancies: Architectural Preservation District Commission, 
Cemetery Commission, Agricultural Commission, Green Communities Committee, Open 
Space Committee. Interested individuals can apply via the town website or by contacting 
the Town Manager's office.
