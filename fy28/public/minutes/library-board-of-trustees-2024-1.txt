Trustees of Lunenburg Public Library, Meeting minutes January 18, 2024  
Attending: Bob Conroy, Harry Kubetz, Dick Mailloux, Donna Saiia, Muir Haman – Director LPL, Laura Boussom - remote, Maia 
Wentrup, Jana-Lee Balish.   
 
The LPLBT meeting was called to order at 6:45 p.m. by Chairperson Harry Kubetz.  
 
Public Comment 
• Harry received a Freedom of Information Act request for security camera footage from the library. He forwarded the 
request to Muir and the Town Manager. Elaine from Town Hall responded and said she would handle it. Harry said the 
library would provide the footage. 
 
Agenda Items 
 
• Approval of Minutes 
o Dick made a motion to accept the minutes of November 16, 2023 as amended, seconded by Donna. The 
motion passed unanimously by roll call vote. 
 
• Director’s Report 
o January is month 7 out of 12. We are 58% through the year and the budget is 46% expended.  
o The library staff salary line item is low but we still have two vacancies. 
o We are doing well with electricity charges, have had credits the last three statements. January is usually a big 
month for heating. 
o State Aid will be up this year and that will help with materials costs. The Mango subscription was renewed and 
a subscription for sign language was added. 
o Expenses for programs are currently down a bit but there are a lot of programs coming up i n the spring. 
o Second interview rounds have been completed and Muir has made some offers. The offer for the Digital 
Services position has been accepted. 
o Budget requests were submitted this morning. The total requested is $613k. This includes $14k for technology 
and $2k for professional development, as we discussed in our previous meeting. 
o Lydia, Marabeth and Muir attended coordinator training for the Aspen Discovery layer of the ILS. This sits on 
top of the catalog software and allows administrative changes to be made. The new catalog version will be 
rolling out in March. 
o Muir met with Chris Ruth and the architect to discuss the teen renovation project. Muir presented a 3D model 
of the current layout that the architect produced. 
o December programs were very successful and well attended. 
o Chris and Muir continue to discuss a number of maintenance projects. The door to the community room is 
currently broken. The replacement hardware is expensive - $2500. Muir said this is a public safety issue and 
Harry suggested he elevate to the Town Manager. 
o Muir had his second meeting with the IT dept. to discuss the ongoing phone problems.  
o The thin client replacements are all installed and licensed. 
o $31k is currently in the capital plan to replace the security cameras. Muir would like to find a way to do it 
sooner and cheaper. 
 
• Green Communities Update 
o Muir sent an email to Dave Passios and Dave Blatt about the upcoming library projects but he hasn’t had a 
reply back yet. 
 
• Library Invested Funds Update 
o The funds are up to $93k.

● Director’s Report 
o February is month 8 out of 12. We are 67% through the year and the budget is 54% expended.  
o Payment made to Ingram for books. 
o The library was closed on Tuesday, the 13th, due to the anticipated snow storm. 
o Two new employees started on the 14th.  Melanie King is the new Digital Services Librarian and Outreach 
Coordinator. She worked part time at the Leominster and Lancaster libraries, and has web and digital 
experience. Kaitee Himmelberger is the new Young Adult Services Librarian.  She previous ly worked part time 
at the Holy Cross and Worcester Art Museum libraries. 
o Interlibrary Loan deliveries have been erratic recently, but a new driver has been hired by Optima Courier and 
Muir is optimistic that deliveries will return to normal. 
o Muir is presenting his proposed FY25 budget to the Finance Committee next Thursday.  Muir requested 
$613,099.77, and Heather's preliminary budget is $589,099.77.  Heather's budget is a 6% increase from last 
year. Muir requested 14k for technology that Heather is funding through the IT department.  
o Muir is uncertain how the school's budget shortfall of $1.5 million will affect other departments..  
o The CWMARS Bylaws Committee finished a draft of the Internal Privacy Policy for the network and sent it to 
the executive committee for review and potential legal feedback. 
o The Friends book donation sorting location has been moved out of the staff work area and relocated  to the 
storage closet at the entrance to the Children's Room. This change is to prevent access to non-public areas of 
the library in case we ever have a visit from a First Amendment audit group. 
o A member of the Master Plan Steering Committee asked Muir if the library might permanently house a 3D 
topographical map of the town during and after the master planning process. Muir will keep the conversation 
open and is trying to think of a solution for this request. 
o Programs included a virtual tea tasting, an introduction to Tarot, and a video game night.  A virtual garden tour 
had some technical difficulties, but was still well attended. The puzzle race was canceled based on feedback 
that the timing wasn't good for interested participants. 
o STEM kits have finally launched.  The Children's Room staff are trained and kits are being promoted.  
o Muir asked our opinion about replacements for the security cameras.  He has two options.  Verkada will cost 
36k, and if approved through the Capital Plan at Town Meeting, the money would be available 7/1/24. The 
Lunenburg Police already use Verkada, and Casey of Suzor IT supports going with Verkada. A Millennium 
Alarm system could be completed quicker and cheaper (under 10k).  We would not need to wait for it to be 
approved through the Capital Plan. Maya suggested that Muir investigate and compare the long -term costs of 
the two systems. Harry asked Muir to draft a letter to Michael-Ray Jeffries and ask him for his opinion. 
o The missing phone has been located.  It was installed under a desk in the Children’s Work Office.  The main 
line can now be picked up from most extensions, which is very helpful. 
o The backup systems for the server malfunctioned, and Dan has not been able to restore that yet.

A few outstanding programs we hosted in late April and May include: 
➢ The world hand drumming workshop on April 17th had 6 attendees who brought their own hand 
drums to practice Balkan rhythms along with the instructor’s clarinet melodies. It was a beautiful 
program that every participant enjoyed and would love to have again.  
➢ A stellar music concert by Creeque Alley brought one of the largest audiences to the library this year, 
with 68 people grooving to folk music of the 60’s and 70’s in the community room. Hearing the 
audience sing-along, laugh, and reminisce about songs from past years was a wonderful experience. 
➢ One of the biggest surprises for spring programming was the crowd we pulled in for the first 
community supported meditation night on May 7th. Emily Zaffino approached me earlier in the year to 
request a meditation group at the library, and after several discussions we refined our goals and 
ideas to this bi-weekly semi-guided community supported meditation circle. Emily leads the group in 
some basics of meditation to start each session, but it ends with quiet time for individuals to practice 
on their own. We had 30 people of varied experience join us, and we are looking forward to seeing 
how it grows over time. 
➢ The spring puzzle swap was another success for community driven programming. Held on Saturday 
May 11th, we had 45 people come to choose from over 500 puzzles during the swap. Despite being 
held quarterly, the swaps continue to maintain popularity in both attendance and donations.

Trustee Report 
Adult Services Department 
September 2024 
 
Summer Reading 2024 was a major success for the adult services department. We had 74 readers log 
135,315 minutes and enter 309 tickets into prizes on Beanstack. This was a substantial increase from 
previous years, which is incredibly encouraging for sustainable growth in the coming years.  
 
A few outstanding programs we hosted this summer include: 
➢ A nature themed talk about animals who can be seen on Tanzanian Safari by The Joys of Nature 
was held June 24th and attended by 11 patrons of various ages. 
➢ 22 crafty attendees made beaded dream tree sculptures at our craft night program on July 24th 
➢ The summer puzzle swap had 60 people exchange 400 tickets for new to them puzzles from the 
550 puzzles that were donated prior to August 17th making this puzzle swap our best attended 
summer swap event since starting the program! 
➢ A Caribbean steelpan player entertained 21 patrons on August 19th as part of our monthly music 
series programs to great acclaim and rave reviews. 
➢ Finishing out the summer, Kaitee and I hosted our first Friday evening craft & chill program on 
August 30th for pairs of participants to paint their partners while snacking on charcuterie 
selections and sipping on a fruit juice mocktail. We had 17 people join us for this first event; many 
of whom fit in the difficult to reach "new adult” age bracket of 20–40-year-olds. This will be a 
monthly program that we hope to grow over time. 
Looking ahead, here are the September events at the library: 
➢ The monthly music series presents the Alex Minasian Jazz Trio on Monday September 9th 
➢ A September puzzle race for teams of four to complete a 500-piece puzzle the fastest will be on 
Monday September 16th  
➢ Greg Burns from Oak Tree Forge will be onsite for a forging demonstration on Wednesday 
September 18th 
➢ Patrons will learn about the history and music of the street organ with Tony Gangi on Wednesday 
September 25th 
➢ The September meeting for the Friday night craft & chill group will be on September 27th for patrons 
to make fermented salsa, snack on chips and dips, and sip on virgin margarita mocktails 
➢ We are also participating in Monday evening zoom author talks for the month of September, hosted 
by the Ashland Public Library 
➢ Book clubs continue to be successful with 9-13 people attending the 100 Books Bucket List meetings 
on the first Tuesday morning or the Brighter Days meetings on the second Wednesday evening. The 
baking book club will be moving to the library community room every first Wednesday evening 
starting October 2nd 
 
Respectfully submitted, 
Lydia Gravell
