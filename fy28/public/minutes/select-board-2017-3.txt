Follow up on Legal Counsel Interviews: Reference Checks and Review of Ratings (non-priced)
Ms. Lemieux referred to compiled evaluation ratings for the four legal counsel firms. The ratings were based on
scoring by the Board, Ms. Lemieux and Mr. Burney. Ratings ranged from not advantageous, unacceptable,
somewhat advantageous, advantageous or highly advantageous. Miyares & Harrington's overall rating results
showed 4 out of 7 evaluators rated them highly advantageous; Murphy, Hess, Toomey and Lehane showed 3
out of 7 evaluators rated them as highly advantageous; Deutsch Williams showed 5 out of 7 rated them as
highly advantageous and Mead, Talerman & Costa rated 5 out of 7 as highly advantageous. The next goal is to
match the most advantageous non-priced proposal with the best price proposal.
Ms. Lemieux received 13 out of 16 reference checks back on the firms and presented them as well as their fee
schedules. The Board requested Ms. Lemieux separate the total hours over one year from KP-Law invoices of
general services and litigation services and then project with each of the four proposals what the estimated cost
would be for the Town. The next phase is reinterviewing the four finalists on April 18'h

Complete Streets Policy Review and Possible Vote to Adopt
Ms. Lemieux referred to the draft Complete Streets policy. Chairman Toale, DPW Director Rodriquenz., Land
Use Director Burney and she had met and put together the draft policy. The policy takes into account
MassDOT's criteria guidelines for the Complete Streets program. Once the Board votes to adopt the policy, it
will be uploaded into the MassDOT's website online portal and we would receive notification within three
weeks on our score. A score of 80 or above would generate funding of $50,000 for technical assistance which
would aid in the prioritization plan for our Complete Streets projects. The next step would be to solicit bids for
technical assistance and putting together that plan. After some discussion the wording under Implementation
(1) was changed to an advisory group of relevant stakeholders will be appointed by the Board of Selectmen
within six months of approval of this policy by MassDOT." Mr. Ebersole suggested waiting for approval of this
policy until Ms. Bertram returns next week as she had some concerns. He thanked Ms. Lemieux for all her
work. He asked if the Planning Board needs to revise their subdivision rules and regulations or policies to be
consistent with this. Land Use Director Burney stated yes there will need to be some work done. The discussion
will be continued.

Town Manager Report:
Ms. Lemieux reported the Board has received a copy of an indoor air quality report for Town Hall conducted by
the Department of Public Health in December 2016. They came to perform air quality testing and make visual
observations of the building. Results of the air quality test indicated the carbon dioxide levels, temperature,
humidity and particulate matter concentrations were below the recommended level and there was no detection
of carbon monoxide. However, the visual observations include bird waste in the attic, high levels of moisture in
the basement that have led to mold and a ventilation system that is not working properly. Mr. Lemieux and
DPW Director Rodriquenz have been working together and taken the following steps to address the issues. A
commercial restoration and cleaning company has been contacted to look at the basement and attic. They
provided an estimate for cleaning the attic bird waste of $7,400. She contacted Massachusetts Interlocal
Insurance Association (MIIA) to file a claim for mold remediation in the basement but unfortunately learned
today from the claim adjuster that it will not be a covered incident. If there had been a recent incident that
caused water damage, such as a broken pipe or structure that led to the mold, it would have been covered.
At this time we do not have a cost estimate for the mold remediation. Outside of the mold remediation which
includes restoration of records that we cannot destroy, there are a number of issues to get the basement to be
weathertight. Staff has been advised not to enter the basement until further notice. The DPW Director has
contacted a pest control company to control the rodent problem in the building; they will be working on sealing
all holes to prevent future access by squirrels or other critters. The ventilation system and other building repairs
will need to be addressed separately and a scope of work will need to be put together to establish a basis for
getting bids for performing that work. Overall we are at a preliminary stage of assessment. We will address the
attic and basement situations first.
Ms. Lemieux reported she has received notification today from the Department of Revenue regarding the
Community Compact Program and the submittal of our three best practices submitted. We will receive a grant
up to $10,000 for the cost of a cyber security assessment; a grant up to $10,000 to implement a citizen
communication engagement plan and a grant up to $25,000 for the stormwater measures to be implemented.
She is working with the IT Director to finalize the scope of work for the cyber security assessment and the
citizen communication engagement plan. She has contacted the Storm Water Task Force to request they provide
a scope of work to implement the stormwater measures.

Mr. Toale reported the four articles grant to the Board of Selectmen care, custody and control of the four

"conveyance." The Building Reuse Committee feels very strongly that our ability as a town to market,
negotiate, utilize buildings that are or may become surplus is enhanced by the ability of people who are
responsible for that and who have a knowledge of all the associated data to be able to make that decision. That
does not preclude in any way from involving the town; having public and marketing sessions, charrettes, etc.
The Building Reuse Committee believes the Selectmen should have the ability to convey these properties if it

Preservation District Commission (APDC) would be the appropriate boards that decide that initially and get
peoples' feedback on what they want rather than at the mad rush of Town Meeting floor.
The Village Center bylaw uses allowed are retail with a maximum 5,000 square feet floor area, professional

allowed by right with a floor area greater that 5,000 square feet included in a multiple commercial use or mixed
use development.

and a half holding public hearings, charrettes and doing research with the Historical Commission and Historical
Society as to what's appropriate in the town center. Site design includes interior streets, walkways, and access,
parking and loading areas, sidewalks, landscaping and street furniture.

Tax Title and Foreclosure discussion (cont.)
Ms. Lemieux presented an update from the previous discussion in March. A list of 63 tax title properties that
might go to foreclosure or tax lien sale was presented by Treasurer/Collector Mallari.
Ms. Lemieux stated a tax title assignment sale is the sale of tax liens that have gone through the tax-taking
process but have not been foreclosed. Selling the lien does not transfer ownership. Under M.G.L. Chapter 60, §
52, the Treasurer has the authority to assign and transfer tax title(s), individually or bundled to the highest
bidder. At the time of tax title sale, the Town recoups all the delinquent taxes, interest and all associated costs
(legal, recording costs, advertising).
Ms. Lemieux noted the Treasurer/Collector has committed to send a letter to the property owners 30 days in
advance of the sale so they have the opportunity to pay the delinquent taxes. The average minimum cost to
move a property through the Land Court foreclosure process is $3,500. Currently the Town only budgets $8,000
per year for tax title foreclosures (approximately two properties).
Based on the list of properties the Conservation Commission has expressed interest in, it would cost the Town
$56,000 to foreclose, create a deficit of approximately $180,000 in abated taxes and reduction of $1,208,400
from our Tax Levy. Foreclosure can take up to two years in Land Court. The original owner has a one year
redemption period after final court ruling to pay the amount owed on the property. Land Court protects owners
by allowing all available options to set up payment agreements). During the foreclosure process, it is not
accruing the 16% interest. The percentage of Tax Titles that actually end up foreclosed is .2%.
The public auction will attempt to recoup the value of all delinquent taxes and get them back on the tax rolls.
Auctioning off foreclosed properties does not cost the Town money. The auctioneer adds a percentage of the
sale on top of the auctioned price that the buyer is responsible for paying.
Ms. Lemieux stated if a tax title company bought all the delinquent properties, they would bring all the taxes
owed to the Town up to date.
Currently the Town owns four foreclosed properties:

Quarterly Financial Report
Town Accountant Karen Brochu presented a summary of revenues and expenditures up to March 31$l.
Everything is on target; revenues were at 77.06% of the estimates for FY'17. Through March 31, we have
collected 85.43% of the FY'17 local receipt estimates. She presented a breakdown of the local receipts.
School departmental revenues, licenses and permits, motor vehicle excise taxes, special assessments and
miscellaneous non-recurring revenue collected were all above what was estimated.
Fines and forfeits and the local option meals tax were below estimates. We have collected 75.93% of the levy.
We have collected what we anticipated under state aid at 74%.
Under expenditures, 68.4% of what was budgeted has been expended or encumbered. Most issues were
addressed at annual town meeting for the FY'17 budget. The Reserve Fund was reduced by $98,000, Town
Manager salary line was reduced by $7,000 and we had a small surplus of $1750 in the annual audit line.
We increased the Council on Aging budget by $3,100 due to changes in the MART reimbursement process and
contract changes. We increased the animal control officer budget by $6,100 due to extended care of two abused
pit bulls. There is going to be a surplus in the Monty Tech assessment of $9,209 at the close of the fiscal year
due to changes in the assessment. In snow removal we did a supplemental appropriation of $166,000 at town
meeting. There is still a small deficit that can be addressed internally in the DPW appropriation. Veterans
benefits were reduced by $45,000 at annual town meeting. Health insurance was reduced by $31,100 and
unemployment was reduced by $8,000. The majority of our reductions were used to offset snow and ice deficit.
