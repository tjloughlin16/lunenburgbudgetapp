Lunenburg Council on Aging                                             
Meeting Minutes  
January 14, 2020 
 
CALL TO ORDER: 9:30 a.m. 
 
Present:  Director Sue Doherty, Chairperson Deb Lincoln, Cheryl Moisan, Betty DiGiacomo, Diane Nowd, Judy Tarbell, 
Sarah Grant, Susan O’Sullivan 
 
Absent:  Jane Rabbitt, Pete Lincoln, Barbara Brown, John Pesenti 
 
GUESTS:  Selectman Michael-Ray Jeffreys, Council on Aging Liaison to the Eagle House Supporters Marie Burchfield 
 
ANNOUNCEMENTS:  Ms. Lincoln wished a happy birthday to those members born in January. Ms. Lincoln and Ms. Nowd 
gave updates on COA members struggling with their health. 
Ms. Lincoln suggested holding the discussion of COA board member orientation as well as policies and procedures until 
there are more board members present. 
Ms. Doherty informed the board that the Town is very close to hiring a new COA Van Driver for Thursdays and Fridays, 
and that the Kitchen Aide position has been advertised. 
Ms. Doherty also informed the board that Lunenburg’s Boston Post Cane holder Lucy Chartrand has passed away. 
 
MINUTES APPROVAL:  Ms. DiGiacomo motioned to approve the minutes of the December 10, 2019 meeting, Ms. Grant 
seconded. On vote, motion carried unanimously. 
 
CURRENT BUSINESS:  
1. Director’s Report:  
 Building Report- HVAC: Ms. Doherty reported that the boiler and air conditioning units are 
being replaced. 
 
 FY21 Council on Aging Budget: The COA FY21 budget from the Town Manager totals $159,813 
to cover salaries, mileage, and supplies. Ms. Doherty is asking for an additional $5,000 from the 
Town this year for program expenses. Ms. Doherty explained that she won’t have the same 
grant opportunities this year as she has had in the past. Ms. Doherty will go before the Finance 
Committee on February 20, 2020 at Town Hall. Mr. Jeffreys inquired about Ms. Doherty’s ideas 
of a nutritionist at the Eagle House, which was discussed at a Board of Selectmen meeting 
concerning the Town’s five-year financial forecast. Ms. Doherty replied that she is still looking 
into job descriptions in order to create the position, that it would be a full-time, benefitted 
position, and that the creation of the position is in its infant stages at this time. Mr. Jeffreys also 
asked about what additional services could be provided at the Eagle House for people who 
aren’t completely independent, but also are not ready to move to an assisted living or nursing 
home facility. Ms. O’Sullivan commented that there is a certification process to go through in 
order to provide additional services, along with additional employees with salaries. Ms. Doherty 
added that people who come to the senior center are welcome to come with personal care 
attendants and home health aides if they need assistance with activities of daily living.  Ms. 
Doherty added that sometimes when outside companies hold programs at the Eagle House they 
offer respite care, and that currently, the senior center’s role in health care is for information 
and referral to places that provide the services that seniors need.  
 
 Town of Lunenburg Property Tax Work-off Program: Ms. Doherty reported the committee has 
received seven applications, and approved five.  Despite the increase in income eligibility, 
earnings, and positions for the program, not many people have applied. Ms. Doherty added that 
there is one person in the program that’s filling three positions. Ms. DiGiacomo commented that 
there may be many seniors who would be interested in volunteering in those positions, but they 
Unanimously approved by COA at 
February 10, 2020 meeting, 
===PAGE 2===
are not property owners and therefore not eligible for the program. Mr. Jeffreys added that he 
asked Town Manager Heather Lemieux to add more positons in the schools to give seniors an 
opportunity to work around students. 
 
 Bridges Together: The Bridges Together Program, designed to bridge the gap between seniors 
and school age children, has been put on hold this session for lack of volunteers, according to 
Ms. Doherty.  Ms. Nowd commented that January is a tough time to get people to commit, given 
the New England weather.  
 
 Upcoming Events: Ms. Doherty listed scheduled upcoming events at the Eagle House.  
 
EAGLE HOUSE SUPPORTERS REPORT: Ms. Lincoln reported the Supporters have nominated Lori Ann Tessier to serve on 
their board, and there is still one vacancy. Ms. Lincoln added that Pat Kennedy is doing an excellent job sending out 
cards to those who are ill, grieving, etc. Ms. Burchfield encouraged people to let Ms. Kennedy know who might like a 
card from the Supporters, and added that it’s a nice change to get something in the mail other than bills and sales ads.  
 
NOTICES AND COMMUNICATIONS:   
 
BOARD COMMENT: Ms. Lincoln thanked Mr. Jeffreys for suggesting to keep the warrant article as written at Special 
Town Meeting on November 12, 2019. As a result of Mr. Jeffreys’s suggestion, the director’s grade and step increase 
boosted the position from a Step 11 to a Step 14. 
Ms. Lincoln also asked Mr. Jeffreys if he would bring the boulders on Memorial Dr. to the Selectmen’s attention in hope 
of getting them removed. 
 
PUBLIC COMMENT none 
 
ADJOURNMENT At 10:16 a.m., Ms. O’Sullivan motioned to adjourn, Ms. DiGiacomo seconded. On vote, motion carried 
unanimously. 
 
UPCOMING MEETING SCHEDULE 
Tuesday, February 11, 2020 at 9:30 a.m.

Lunenburg Council on Aging                                             
Meeting Minutes  
February 11, 2020 
 
CALL TO ORDER: 9:33 a.m. 
 
Present:  Director Susan Doherty, Chairperson Deb Lincoln, Cheryl Moisan, Diane Nowd, Jane Rabbitt, 
Judy Tarbell, Sarah Grant 
 
Absent:  Betty DiGiacomo, Pete Lincoln, Barbara Brown, John Pesenti, Susan O’Sullivan 
 
GUESTS:  Council on Aging Liaison to the Eagle House Supporters Marie Burchfield 
 
ANNOUNCEMENTS:  Ms. Lincoln wished a happy birthday to board members who have birthdays in 
February. 
 
MINUTES APPROVAL: Ms. Rabbitt motioned to approve the minutes from the January 14, 2020 
meeting, Ms. Tarbell seconded. On vote, motion carried unanimously. 
 
CURRENT BUSINESS:  
1. New Council on Aging Board Member Orientation: Ms. Lincoln suggested tabling the topic 
until more board members can be in attendance for the discussion. 
 
2. COA Bylaws vs. Polices & Procedures: The board reviewed and updated a document titled 
“Lunenburg Council on Aging Bylaws.” Ms. Lincoln suggested deducting the formal legal 
language from the document, as well as taking out the word bylaws, and replacing it with 
procedures, and the board agreed. Ms. Doherty suggested adding MART regulations 
regarding COA Van policies, as well as developing a building and visitation policy to address 
potential issues regarding patrons and their ability to take responsibility for their activities 
of daily living.  The board agreed to delete some clauses, as well as add new language to the 
newly titled “Lunenburg Council on Aging Procedures.” Ms. Lincoln said she would type the 
new document and that it would be distributed with next month’s meeting agenda.  
 
3. Director’s Report:  
 Building Report: Ms. Doherty informed the board that the splits in the old 
section of the building that used to control only the air conditioning have been 
replaced by newer models that control both heat and cool air. 
 
 Town of Lunenburg Senior Tax Work-Off Program: Ms. Doherty reported that 
not many applications have been received by the committee, and that the last 
meeting was postponed due to lack of quorum.  
 
 Heart to Home Meals: Ms. Doherty reported that a new company focusing on 
frozen home delivered meals for senior citizens is coming to the Eagle House to 
give some information about their menu and ordering options. 
 
Unanimously approved by COA at 
March 10, 2020 meeting. 
===PAGE 2===
 Discovery Center/Empower Success Corps: Ms. Doherty reported she’s applying 
to become a pilot site host for this program which is designed to connect  area 
volunteers with opportunities that match their skills. 
 
 Americans with Disabilities Act Meeting: Ms. Doherty informed the board she 
would be attending a meeting at Town Hall regarding recent studies done on 
the town center and other areas to make them more accessible.  
 
 Memory Café Lending Library: Ms. Doherty reported the lending library is going 
well, and that she is adding books for caregivers to borrow as well.    
 
 Upcoming Events: Ms. Doherty summarized upcoming events at the Eagle 
House. 
 
EAGLE HOUSE SUPPORTERS REPORT: The Supporters will meet on March 6, 2020 
 
NOTICES AND COMMUNICATIONS:  Ms. Doherty distributed a letter and information from the 
Lunenburg Historical Commission on their proposed Community Preservation by-law. 
 
BOARD COMMENT: none 
 
PUBLIC COMMENT none

vote, motion carried unanimously.

Lunenburg Council on Aging                                             
Meeting Minutes  
March 10, 2020 
 
CALL TO ORDER: 9:30 
 
Present:  Chairperson Deb Lincoln, Director Sue Doherty, Betty DiGiacomo, Diane Nowd, Pete Lincoln, 
Judy Tarbell, Barbara Brown, Sarah Grant, Susan O’Sullivan 
Absent:  Cheryl Moisan, Jane Rabbitt, John Pesenti 
 
GUESTS:  Selectman Michael Ray Jeffreys, Council on Aging Liaison to the Eagle House Supporters Pat 
Kennedy 
 
ANNOUNCEMENTS:  Ms. Lincoln announced that the Eagle House was chosen as a pilot site by the 
Massachusetts Council on Aging with Empower Success Corps Fellows to become a “Discovery Site,” 
where recently retired people will be trained to use their professional skills as volunteers to assist the 
community in various ways. Ms. Doherty explained that the program requires two “transition 
navigators” to serve as job coaches, and to market the program, and has Lori-Ann Tessier and Susan 
O’Sullivan in mind to fill those spots.  
Ms. Lincoln also announced that Pat Kennedy will succeed Marie Burchfield as Council on Aging Liaison 
to the Eagle House Supporters. 
 
MINUTES APPROVAL:  Ms. DiGiacomo moved to approve the minutes of the February 10, 2020 COA 
meeting, Mr. Lincoln seconded. On vote, motion carried unanimously. 
 
CURRENT BUSINESS:  
1. Rebecca Lantry, Lunenburg Historical Commission, Community Preservation Law: Topic 
was not discussed as no one from the Historical Commission was in attendance. 
2. New Council on Aging Board Member Orientation: Topic not discussed due to lack of 
attendance. 
3. COA Bylaws vs. Policies and Procedures: Mr. Lincoln found some grammatical errors in the 
updated policies. Ms. Brown recalled a discussion from prior years she had been on the 
board about decreasing the amount of members on the board. Ms. Doherty handed out the 
van driver policies and a draft of guidelines for Eagle House Participation, which the board is 
to read and review for the next meeting. Ms. Doherty added that she would also like to 
submit the guidelines to Town Council to review.  
4. Director’s Report:  
 Ms. Doherty informed the board she has hired Property Tax Work-off participant 
Ann Penney to serve as a kitchen aide. Ann will be on both the town’s payroll as well 
as continue to participate in the property tax work-off program. 
 
 Ms. Doherty also informed the board that Eagle Scout candidate Alex Powers has 
completed his project and supplied the Eagle House with two handmade octagon 
shaped picnic tables, which allow wheelchair access. According to Ms. Doherty, Mr. 
Powers raised the money to build the tables by making dog biscuits and selling 
them, and also donated the surplus money he raised to the Eagle House Supporters.  
Mr. Jeffreys asked for Mr. Powers’s contact information so the Board of Selectmen 
could send along their appreciation for his good deed. 
Unanimously approved by COA at 
6/9/2020 meeting via Zoom. 
===PAGE 2===
 Building Report: Ms. Doherty informed the board that the former AC units in the old 
section of the building have been replaced with new units which provide AC as well 
as heat.   
Ms. Doherty reported that the rotted porch boards are to be replaced with Trex 
composite decking, and have been ordered and received, but the color of the Trex 
does not match the existing painted porch floor. The floor will be painted to match 
the Trex.  Mr. Lincoln asked if grit could be added to the paint to provide a non-slip 
surface to the porch.  
 
Ms. Doherty reported that she recently learned the renovation of the newer kitchen 
is being funded by the FY21 Capital Plan. 
 
Ms. Doherty informed the board that the green bench that used to sit on the porch 
has been discarded as it has rotted away after 22 years.  
 
Ms. Doherty also reported that she has moved the large retractable presentation 
shade from the old section of the building to the big room.  
 
Mr. Lincoln added that the shed will need a second coat of stain.  
 
Ms. Doherty also reported she’s spoken with Lunenburg Police Chief Jim Marino, 
and has found that Tanner Cole’s mother is against removing the small boulders 
along Memorial Drive. Mr. Jeffreys said that he is looking for a friendly way to treat 
the issue, and pointed out that the small boulders also pose a risk when plowing 
Memorial Drive after snow storms. Mr. Jeffreys also suggested the Council on Aging 
send a letter to Mr. Cole’s mother requesting removal of the small boulders. Ms. 
Doherty pointed out that when the Complete Streets project starts on Memorial 
Drive, there will probably be no room for the boulders anymore.  
 
Mr. Lincoln mentioned the idea of the new parking lot, and how much backlash the 
Supporters received when they tried to make it happen years ago, and asked about 
drainage for storm water runoff, and the issues it would cause for the schools when 
it is built. Ms. Doherty commented that it’s possible that grant money is available to 
take care of drainage as she’s been chosen to serve on a committee that addresses 
the risk associated with natural disasters, and they’ve been discussing how to 
prepare for storm water run-off drainage problems. There will be another meeting 
of that committee at the end of March. 
 
 Ms. Doherty also reported that she met with the new Parks and Recreation Director, 
and they discussed a few town-wide event ideas, as well as painting pickle ball 
regulation lines on the old tennis courts. 
 
 Senior Tax Work-Off: Ms. Doherty reported the committee has decided to raise the 
hourly wage of property tax work-off participants to minimum wage; however the 
maximum amount of money they can earn remains the same at $1,500 per year. 
Although the income eligibility requirement for the program has risen, applications 
are still being denied because the applicants, incomes are still too high, according to 
Ms. Doherty.

 Upcoming Events:  Ms. Doherty highlighted special events going on at the Eagle 
House for the month of March, and added that the Bridges Together program was 
canceled because there weren’t enough volunteers.  
 
EAGLE HOUSE SUPPORTERS REPORT: Ms. Lincoln reported that the Supporters have cleared the 
cabinets of all outdated material in the old kitchen, and will keep one cabinet to store various items of 
general interest. Ms. Lincoln also reported that many monetary donations have been received in 
Lawrence (Lawrie) Montuori’s memory. Ms. Lincoln also thanked Ms. Kennedy for sending out sunshine 
letters to seniors who are ill, or going through challenging times. Ms. Kennedy urged all board members 
to let her know of anyone who is ailing, or who may be in need of a card in the mail to cheer them up. 
 
NOTICES AND COMMUNICATIONS:  None 
 
BOARD COMMENT: Mr. Lincoln volunteered to write a letter to be read at the next meeting addressing 
the small boulders on Memorial Drive. Mr. Lincoln also commented that it’s time to make preparations 
for the next recipient of the Boston Post Cane.   
 
PUBLIC COMMENT Ms. Lincoln thanked Mr. Jeffreys for standing up for the town’s seniors at Board of 
Selectmen meetings.  
Mr. Jeffreys commented that he has short and long-term goals. As for short-term, he’s looking for ways 
to make the town work environment better for employees, and that he’s working toward the 
development of a Citizen’s Award to recognize the members of the community who exemplify service to 
the community.  As far as long-term, Mr. Jeffreys is aiming to increase revenues, and provided using the 
old town dump for community solar as an example. Mr. Jeffreys also informed the board of a statewide 
program involving eighth graders and civic duty. And rhetorically inquired about what is the appropriate 
age to teach someone about civic involvement.

Lunenburg Council on Aging                                            
Meeting Minutes  
June 9, 2020 
 
CALL TO ORDER: 9:30 a.m. Chairperson Deb Lincoln read a statement concerning the Open Meeting Law, and how all 
public meetings in Massachusetts are legally being held remotely.  This meeting was held via Zoom. 
 
Present:  Director Susan Doherty, Chairperson Deb Lincoln, Jane Rabbitt, Diane Nowd, Betty DiGiacomo, Peter Lincoln, 
Judy Tarbell, Sarah Grant, Susan O’Sullivan 
 
Absent:  John Pesenti, Cheryl Moisan, Barbara Brown 
 
GUESTS:  none 
 
ANNOUNCEMENTS:   
 
MINUTES APPROVAL:  Ms. DiGiacomo motioned to approve the minutes from the March 10, 2020 meeting, Mr. Lincoln 
seconded. On vote, motion carried unanimously.  
 
CURRENT BUSINESS:  
1. COA Re-appointments: Cheryl Moisan, Diane Nowd, Sarah Grant: Ms. Lincoln reported that all three 
members have expressed interest in being reappointed, and two out of three have submitted their 
completed reappointment forms to the Office of the Board of Selectmen.  Mr. Lincoln moved to recommend 
reappointment of Ms. Grant, Ms. Moisan, and Ms. Nowd. Ms. O’Sullivan seconded. On vote, motion carried 
unanimously. 
2. Vote for FY21 COA Chairperson and Vice Chairperson: Mr. Lincoln moved to nominate Ms. Lincoln as 
chairperson and Ms. Rabbitt as chairperson, noting that the two officers are still in the process of 
accomplishing their goals. Ms. DiGiacomo seconded. On vote, motion carried unanimously.  
3. New Council on Aging Board Member Orientation: Tabled until the council can meet in person. 
4. COA Bylaws vs. Policies and Procedures: Tabled until the council can meet in person. 
Ms. O’Sullivan moved to resume discussion of orientation and policies and procedures until the council 
can meet in person. Mr. Lincoln seconded. On vote, motion carried unanimously. 
5. Director’s Report:  
 Covid-19 Report: Ms. Doherty reported that the St. Patrick’s Day luncheon was canceled the same 
morning it was scheduled, and has been postponed to a date to be determined. The office staff has 
been delivering meals on wheels since March, and also coming into the office on alternate days to 
keep down any possible exposure to Covid-19. The meal site manager has also been coming in to 
expedite meals on wheels, and the van has been running at a limited capacity. Ms. Doherty also 
reported that all town employees have been required to complete a Covid-19 web seminar, and that 
several signs have been posted throughout the building displaying proper virus protection protocol. 
Ms. Doherty also reported that the Eagle House has begun hosting “Drive Through Only” programs, 
in which coffee and donuts are being served curbside on Tuesdays, sandwiches on Thursdays, and 
ice cream treats on Fridays. Patrons will not have to leave their cars, and those who are serving food 
and beverages will be wearing a mask and gloves.  These programs are being funded by the 
Executive Office of Elder Affairs formula grant fund in lieu of programs missed because of 
cancelations due to Covid-19.  
Ms. Doherty also reported that the building will open toward the end of July to host ten people who 
reserve places for the Stronger Seniors Chair Yoga DVD.  Participants will be expected to cooperate 
with having their temperature taken with a “no-touch” thermometer, and wearing a mask to cover 
their nose and mouth, as well as keeping at least six feet away from other patrons.  Those who 
cannot wear a mask due to medical reasons will be excused. The Eagle House will also host movies 
on Wednesdays, and a grief support meeting once a month. Those events will be limited to ten 
people and Covid-19 guidelines will apply. Ms. Lincoln commented that the daily updates from the

 Resignation of COA Van Driver: Ms. Doherty read a letter of resignation from Paul Grunditz. Ms. 
Doherty said she wouldn’t be advertising the position any time soon, considering the van is only 
transporting people to medical and dental appointments, and to the grocery store. Only one 
passenger is allowed on the van at a time, and the driver must wear a mask, and gloves, and sanitize 
all surfaces between each stop.  
 Outreach Coordinator/SHINE Counselor: Ms. Doherty informed the board that Outreach 
Coordinator Pauline Roy is training to become a SHINE (Serving Health Insurance Needs of Everyone) 
Counselor.  Ms. Doherty said she’s pleased that the Eagle House will have its own SHINE Counselor 
in house to help answer questions as they come in. 
 Town of Lunenburg Senior Tax Work-Off Program: Ms. Doherty reported that since most town 
offices have been closed it’s been challenging for participants in the program to accumulate hours.  
 Ms. Lincoln asked for an update on the Discovery Site, which is a program that was scheduled to 
take place before the pandemic, aiming to train recent retirees for volunteer positions that could 
utilize their professional backgrounds.  Ms. Doherty responded that the Transition Navigators have 
been contacted and will be participating in training for the program in an online format.  
 
EAGLE HOUSE SUPPORTERS REPORT: Ms. Lincoln informed the board that the Eagle House Supporters had not met 
therefore there would be no report.

midst of Covid-19. Ms. Doherty also thanked each employee for coming in and handling whatever tasks were thrown 
their way during what has proved to be a very trying time for the whole community.
