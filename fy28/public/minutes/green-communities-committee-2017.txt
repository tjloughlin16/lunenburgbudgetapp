Minutes for March 29, 2017
Present: David Blatt, Greg Bittner, Neal Sullivan, Liz Murphy, John Londa, Heather Lemieux
Meeting was Called to Order at 6:30 PM.
Minutes from December 20, 2016 meeting were reviewed and approved unanimously.
No Public Comment
First Funding Round Projects were reviewed, J. Londa reported the last projects were fully
complete, inspected by Unitil and release of energy efficiency incentives would follow. There
was one change order-for 8 primary school and 8 library light fixtures.
Two lighting issues are still outstanding at the library. One, lighting in one room is too bright and
needs dimmer switch, and two, retrofit kits were installed but had a loud fan, they were returned.
DMH, the electrical contractor is likely willing to work with us, but funding deadline is end of
March and Library Trustees would like to make the decisions on the projects. J. Londa thought
he found a bulb that is quiet and may satisfy the Trustees, at a cost of $220 per bulb. We have
enough funding left to purchase one bulb. D.Blatt will ask the Dept. of Energy Resources if we
can use the remaining funding to purchase a bulb as a trial.
Discussion was had regarding the decision making process and authority to implement projects
in various public buildings. D. Blatt proposed either selectmen give support and formal authority
to this board to implement green projects, or the board awards funding based on scope of work
proposed by the body representing the public building in question.
Next steps for First Round Projects- DOER visits projects and gives final approval. We will
return the balance of $382.13 in this year's funding round if DOER does not allow us to use
funds to try one bulb that may suit the library's needs.
L. Murphy shared information from the City of Fitchburg regarding their costs and experiences in
converting streetlights to LED. Fitchburg purchased 3080 lights from Unitil at a cost of
$300,000, ($17 per light) in order to convert to LED. $1,000,000 was spent to convert the lights
to LED, including networking the lights. A $275,000 rebate was awarded by Unitil. The annual
costs went from $157 per light when Unitil owned, to $90 when the City owned them, to $35
after LED conversion.
Heather was able to report Lunenburg's street light count at 333
lights of varying wattage with annual costs of $122 per light. Our FY 18 budget projects a cost of
$129 per light. J. Londa reminded the board that asking for a price for the acquisition of street
===PAGE 2===
lights with Unitil starts a negotiation clock and we should not begin that process before we are
ready.
It is likely to cost the equivalent of one years lighting bill to purchase the lights. It was
suggested that net metering credits be used for the purchase. If that is allowable, grant funds
could then be used to do the conversion. Motion was made by G. Bittner, seconded by N.
Sullivan to ask selectmen to investigate whether net metering credits can be used to purchase
the street lights.
New Projects for FY18- Discussion was had regarding future projects at specific buildings.
There was some question about return of investment for oil to gas conversion at the public
service building The Board could seek suggestions from the Building Reuse Committee as well.
Brief discussion was also had regarding bringing in a consultant to help develop projects for the
next grant round. Project expeditors through utility companies will complete assessments, we
can seek this assistance to inform decisions. Below is a list of potential projects for 2018
funding:
Turkey Hill Middle School envelope project-ready to proceed
Lighting conversion at the schools- ready to proceed
~ Town Hall and the Ritter Building,- heating and air sealing- need energy assessment first
-Street light conversion- need to acquire first
- Senior Center air sealing and attic insulation- talk to MassSave first
A motion was made by D. Blatt, seconded by N. Sullivan to request energy audits by Unitil for all
town buildings. H. Lemieux will contact John DiNapoli for energy assessments.
John Londa presented an Annual Report from the Building Inspector of New Construction and
HERS ratings for each new project. Based on this, our Energy Conservation Measure, Criteria
3- we are down 6.3% from our 2013 baseline with a weather normalized basis if 4.4%. Criteria 4
involves the purchase of fuel efficient vehicles. We received feedback from Fire but not Police,
and all vehicles purchased to comply with the policy.
G. Bittner motioned to adjourn, L. Murphy seconded the motion at 7:45 PM.
Respectfully Submitted,
Elizabeth Murphy

Minutes for October 4, 2017
Present: David Blatt- Chair, Greg Bittner, Neal Sullivan, Liz Murphy, John Londa, Heather
Lemieux - Town Manager
Meeting was Called to Order at 6:30 PM.
Minutes from March 29, 2017 meeting were reviewed. G.B. made a motion to approve, N.S.
seconded.
No Public Comment
J. Londa provided a copy of the draft annual report. The report was reviewed and areas to be
updated were discussed. The report needs to be submitted by December 11, 2017 to be eligible
for the next grant round. It was noted that the only issue we might have related to the report is
our list of new residential should have HERS ratings and some of this data is missing, from the
Building Department report. J. Londa reported he will work on vehicle listing updates, following
up with the Building Department report and updating baseline data for the annual report.
Recommendations for upcoming projects for the next grant round were discussed. Turkey Hill
envelope and roof projects as well as lighting at the school were raised as well developed-
shovel ready projects with an estimated $70,000 cost. Town Hall also has significant potential
for energy improvements. J. Londa also recommended pursuing a public safety building
assessment, either with the CADMAS(sp?) or perhaps MRPC could develop a similar project.
H. Lemieux will compile a "wish list" for town buildings with department input for the committee
to review with return on investment in mind. List will need to be available by early November.
MRPC has reached out and reported they have a grant to develop new projects. H. Lemieux
volunteered to act as liaison with MRPC and invite them to the next task force meeting.
Discussion was had regarding the Task Force's status as a task force or committee. The Chair
suggested that selectman develop a charter for the task force to become a committee. Our
charge is to reduce energy consumption by 20% but this is not clearly stated in a charter.
Having support of selectmen can help convey the importance of this work to town departments
and changing the charter provides an opportunity to articulate this support. J. Londa suggested
the Task Force draft a charter for review by the selectmen. Any ideas for the charter should be
===PAGE 2===
emailed to the Chair. It was suggested the Town Manager could act as liaison to other
departments to assist in this work, as well as developing a presentation of the work to inform
others.
The next meeting date was set as November 8th, 2017 at 6:30 PM. MRPC will be invited to
attend.
L.Murphy motioned to adjourn, N. Sullival seconded the motion at 7:45 PM.
Respectfully Submitted,
Elizabeth Murphy
