Pursuant to MGL ch 131, s 40 as amended and the Town of Lunenburg Wetlands Protection and 
Municipal Bylaws a public hearing on a Notice of Intent by Raymond & Gloria Clouthier for the 
replacement of a single family dwelling and associated structures at 37 Pearl Street (DEP 208-1315) - 
Ryan Proctor (Dillis & Roy), representing the owners, screen-shared the locus map to indicate the 
location of the property. He stated that Pearl Street is a dead end street on a peninsula on Lake Shirley. 
The property currently consists of a cottage, detached garage, detached shed and a screened in gazebo 
near the lake. The existing cottage lies entirely within the 100’ wetland buffer zone and has a well under 
the drain manhole in the existing driveway. It is also served by a private sewage disposal septic system 
which lies within the 50’ wetland offset, with potentially a portion within 30’ no-touch zone. Ryan stated 
the owners are proposing to demo the cottage and garage. They will construct a new single family house 
with an attached garage and new driveway, with access on Pearl St. They will continue to use the existing 
well but replace the septic system with a new geo-mat pressure system with a treatment tank, which will 
be entirely outside of the 50’ wetland offset. The new house will be further away from Lake Shirley, with 
a significant decrease in the impervious area of the buffer zone, as indicated in his impervious area 
summary table- decreasing that area from 2,591 sq. ft to 1,284 sq. ft. There will be no trees removed from 
within the buffer zone and 2 trees removed about 150 ft from the lake. Other than landscape beds, they 
are not proposing any vegetation removal from the site. Erosion control of straw wattles and silt fence 
will be placed around the limit of work and on each property line. Ryan noted they appeared before the 
Board of Health, which granted their special approvals for local upgrades. Also last week, the ZBA 
granted their special permit for demolition of the existing cottage and construction of the new house on 
the property. Ryan added that they have a file number from the DEP and there were no comments 
associated with it. Mary W. noted that Ryan’s plan hit all the points and she appreciated the addition of the 
silt fencing, with the straw wattles. She asked Ryan about the existing gazabo, which he replied will 
remain in place. Mary said when they did the site walk, the Commissioners questioned whether the 
gazabo was permitted to be in place and asked Ryan when it was permitted. Ryan replied he did not know 
===PAGE 2===
and the owners purchased it in March 2023. Mary noted the earlier plan indicated the gazebo would be 
removed and Ryan replied it was changed on the revised plan sent this morning. Ray Clouthier stated the 
property has been in the family for 13 years and they inherited it 3.5 years ago from the owner who 
passed away. He had been before the Conservation Commission a couple of years ago regarding the 
gazebo. They allowed him to install a non-permanent screen room, which is used for storage of lawn 
furniture now. The Commission told him to flatten the land and take down the retaining wall, which he 
did.  Jeff stated that a couple years ago, the Commissioners were on a site walk in that area and Matt 
Marro noticed some stonework being done at 37 Pearl St. He added that the property owners were 
installing a stone walkway and a patio where the gazebo now sits. Jeff believes they had to remove the 
stones and prior to that there was not a gazebo there.   
Motion: Jeff V ., Second: Mike L. 
To approve 37 Pearl St. with the Standard Order of Conditions.

73 Horizon Island Road – Greg Roy (Dillis & Roy) screen-shared an aerial view of the property owned 
by Mr. & Mrs. Levesque. They are working with architect Ron Albert, as well as Dillis & Roy for 
engineering permitting and consulting. They are proposing an addition at the site, which is near the 
causeway on Horizon Island Road. Greg suggested to the property owners that it would be prudent to 
informally discuss the proposed project with the Conservation Commission before proceeding. He noted 
that they have not done any surveying work yet but have taken public information and assessor’s map to 
determine the 30’ no touch line and 50’ no build line from the Hickory Hills Lake. Greg indicated the 
property lines and the existing dwelling which appears to be outside the 30’ zone but is partially in the 50’ 
no build. It is a very tight lot and in addition to Conservation requirements, they also have underlying 
zoning requirements, which will require at least a special permit from the ZBA. He noted that the area 
under the proposed addition is previously approved yard area and is not impervious.  The proposed 
addition would be built within the 50’ no-structure zone which would require a waiver from regulations. 
They would propose keeping it out of the 30’ no-structure zone and keep it no closer than the existing 
building. Greg said he is looking from feedback from the Commission. Chris asked about the foundation 
and Greg replied he believes a full foundation but is not certain. Mary said she does not think they can 
allow it because the property already comes encumbered with a lot of incursions in the 30’ no-touch, 
which were grandfathered.  Jeff agreed with Mary and said the lot is just too small to really add any 
structure to unless they did a tear-down to build up. Mike L. agreed that this would open the floodgates to 
more construction in places where they have said no in the past. Chris said it is a tough spot to be adding 
to. Greg said he appreciated their feedback and candor.

New Business: Lake Shirley Improvement Corp. Annual Report- Joanna Bilotta, Dominic Meringolo 
(Solitude) and Wendy Gendron (Aquatic Restoration Consultants) – Joanna noted that Dominick and 
Wendy run their chemical treatment and water management program at Lake Shirley. Wendy stated that 
her company works with Lake Shirley to make sure they maintain compliance with their Order of 
Conditions and as a 3rd party reviewer to ensure there is little or no impact to non-target organisms. 
Wendy screen-shared the Annual Report. She noted that the 2022-23 winter was not a desirable year for 
drawdown and average drawdown was 0.6 ft. Heavy rain was an issue but there were no fish kills 
reported. The refill was completed early on 3/12. With a few exceptions, they maintained clarity above 5”. 
She noted that the upper north basin has poorer water quality than the other 2 because that is where the 
main tributary comes into the lake. Wendy shared graphs comparing Secchi Disc readings for the last 4 
years. She also shared a graph for measurements of temperature and dissolved oxygen, which showed the 
lake maintained good readings above the state required 5.0mg/L. They are generally concerned about 
rising lake temperatures at all lakes. Wendy stated phosphorus levels are important to control and higher 
levels encourage algae growth. Lake Shirley’s phosphorus numbers were good, with some elevation in the 
upper north basin. Nitrogen levels were moderate to slightly elevated at the bottom. Measurements of 
phytoplankton showed lower algae numbers in July but more elevated in August with higher density of 
cyanobacteria (blue-green algae) in the lower basin. Although elevated, the numbers were lower than 
those requiring treatment. Wendy noted that the end of season plant survey showed a net decrease in 
species richness, with 4 species of native pondweeds not observed, although possibly in other areas. They 
observed less of the native Robbin’s pondweed and coontail, so they do not treat in areas that they are 
===PAGE 4===
observed. A higher presence of non-native European naiad and fanwort was observed, which was 
concerning because they are impacting the natives even with treatment. Mike L. asked if Lake Shirley had 
any invasives removal programs in mind and Wendy replied they do not have any hand-pulling programs 
now. However, if they did observe a new concerning species introduced, they would go after it 
aggressively to prevent spreading. Dominic gave a review of the herbicide treatment with  
Tribune and Nautique in mid-July when they treated 56.5 acres out of the 350 acres of the lake.  There 
were no adverse effects on fish. The plan for the 2023-24 winter is to target a 6’ drawdown, continue 
monitoring water quality and a mid-season survey in June/July with treatments as warranted. They are 
also seeking an extension of the Order of Conditions (OOC), which expires soon. Joanna noted that when 
they started the treatment program in 2008, they would start the survey in June, but now Lunenburg wants 
them to go in July, which is when Dominic is busy doing treatments. However Joanna noted that Shirley 
adopted their own Order of Conditions in addition to Lunenburg’s OOC. In the past, they would treat the 
first week of July, but if they need to meet with Lunenburg and Shirley, it will move treatment to the end 
of July/early August when things are at their highest. Dominic clarified that if the survey for specific 
criteria is done in June, they may not see the cover in biomass that they would see later in the season. He 
suggested if they do the survey at end of June/early July, they could treat in mid-July. Mary asked what 
data they needed to collect and would want them to come to a meeting prior to treatment. Wendy stated 
they survey 60 points to collect data on the plants they are going to treat and create a treatment map. The 
problem is they would need to come before Lunenburg’s and Shirley’s Commissions and there can be 
delays. She suggested that if he can submit a report instead, it would save time with the scheduling. Jeff 
V . suggested having the Administrator look over the plan. Bob and Rich B suggested they could cut down 
on the number of days before a meeting for submittal of the plan but the plan should be reviewed by the 
Commission before they do a treatment. Sophie screen-shared the letter from ARC requesting an 
extension of the OOC and Wendy screen-shared the existing OOC. Rich B. noted that as long as the OOC 
is active, they can act on them in perpetuity but it needs to be listed on the agenda as a hearing. The

Hickory Hills Lake Annual Report – John Fortune (50 Hemlock Dr.), representing Hickory Hills 
Landowners Inc, screen-shared a slide show presentation. He stated per their OOC, DEP # 208-1266, 
issued 7/25/22, there are 13 conditions that he will discuss.  
1.) Survey and Fanwort Removal – They have organized surface monitoring looking for fanwort and 
other issues. They also have divers removing fanwort and they do Secchi disk measurements. Ten 
certified divers spent just under 250 hours in the water observing the surface and bottom of the lake.  In 
2021, they pulled 3,800 plants but that number jumped to 23,000 because they missed a chemical 
treatment.  In 2023, 24,000 plants were pulled and they did a chemical treatment in some big patches. 
John pointed out the 7 areas of concern for fanwort and noted that there is less area to treat than last year.  
2.) Water Quality & Algae Data – V olunteers collect samples and do simple analysis, while complex 
analysis is done by a certified lab. They check in Spring, Summer, and Fall. John shared a map of the 
tested areas and the data chart. He noted that when phosphorus goes over .02, you start to get problems 
and if over .05, you get algae bloom. Last year the numbers for phosphorus and nitrogen got high in front 
of the dam, 6’ down. The Algae Testing in 2023 showed no blue green algae in April and July but a high 
number in August. Testing in September and October showed significantly lower numbers. Little Hickory 
had no algae.  
3. Drawdown of 48” was done in the Fall 2022 but heavy rain in December brought it back up. In Fall 
2023 the drawdown was 47” and again December brought it back up. A 6’ siphon in the bottom of the 
lake is left on to try to pull phosphorus out.  
4). Chemical treatment. – In 2023, SOLitude performed all Chemical treatments. Poly-Aluminum 
Chloride treatment was done at Little Hickory and the Main Lake for algae. Flumioxazin treatment was 
done in the Main Lake for Fanwort. John shared the map with treatment areas noted and the notes by 
SOLitude for the treatment.

5.) Stormwater Testing Program was started in 2021, with testing during 3 storms in 2022 and 2 storms
