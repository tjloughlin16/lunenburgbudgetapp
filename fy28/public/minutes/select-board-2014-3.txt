1. Review/ Discussion/ Recommendations of Warrant Articles for Annual Town Meeting:
Article 27 - Registered Marijuana Dispensaries: Joanna Bilotta Simeone, Chairperson of the Lunenburg Planning Board reviewed
the Medical Marijuana Bylaw with the board touching on the following items:
•
The steps the Planning Board took to develop this Bylaw
• Why this Bylaw was developed
• The Definition of Registered Marijuana Dispensary (RD)
• Background Information
• Municipalities Options
• RMD's Purpose; Applicability (explaining section 4.16.2.2); Definition; Location; Procedure for Submittal; Criteria; Conditional
Standards, Distance (to be measured from the building not the lot line); Setbacks & Buffer Strips; Design Standards; Waivers:
Security; & Documentation
Next Steps/Conclusions
Any changes made this evening will be book marked for Town Meeting floor
Discussion: BOS discussed the following regarding the Bylaw with the Planning Board Chair:
4.16.10. Documentation: Clerical change to the proposed language to be put forward by Mr. Ebersole
• 4.16.5.1.a Criteria 2): This will be enforced by making findings using all four points included in the criteria
• 4.16.6.2. Setbacks and Buffer Strips (residential): This will be enforced by having a 200' buffer strip consisting of either
screening, fencing, or plantings with abutters input
• 4.16.6.4 Waivers: This would only pertain to an applicant who did not have the 200' buffer strip in a residential area and would
require input from abutters
The BOS complimented the Planning Board on their efforts in developing this Bylaw.
Ms. Bertram moved for the BOS to recommend approval of Warrant Article 27, Registered Marijuana Dispensaries, at Annual Town
Meeting. Mr. Matthews seconded. On vote, motion carried, 5 in favor, 0 opposed.
Article 28 - Right to Farm Bylaw: Doug MacMillan & Jeffrey Mendoza representing the Agricultural Commission came before the
BOS seeking approval of Warrant Article 28 and discussed the following changes, approved by the Agricultural Commission, to be
brought forth on Town Meeting floor:
Section 3. Right-to-Farm Declaration - Strike the word "of" and replace with "on" in the second sentence
Strike the words "exclusively" and "commercial" in the 4* sentence
• Section 4. Disclosure Notification - Strike the word "duct" and replace with "dust" in the last sentence
Addition of the words "and practices" after the word "uses" in the first sentence
Mr. Mendoza pointed out the importance of having an Agricultural Commission, the significance of the farmland pictured on the Town
Flag and Town Seal, and asked for the BOS support of this Bylaw and Commission.
Discussion: BOS recommended the following regarding changes to the Right to Farm Bylaw:
===PAGE 2===
• Agricultural Commission should discuss changes with the Moderator before Annual Town Meeting

vs. "parcel" along with the further research of other community's size requirements for farming on smaller portions of land, in

Article 19 - Administrative Reorganization, Land Use Departments: Ms. Speidel commented to say that after further review she
does not believe that this Article requires an Administrative change under the Town Charter, which does not mean that it cannot be
presented here or at Annual Town Meeting for full disclosure. She has submitted this to Town Counsel for review but has not heard
back from them yet. She intends to move forward with the Public Hearing for next week and hopes to have an opinion from Town
Counsel by then. This document has been submitted to the Land Use Departments for comment and the Board of Health (BOH) has
been the only department to reply. They voiced their support of the expanded concept but are concerned about a potential conflict of
interest with having a consolidated department.
Ms. Speidel reiterated the fact that the BOH is specifically excluded because State Law gives them a Separation of Duty & Authority
under Massachusetts General Law (MGL).
The intent is for a coordinated effort and not to supercede the role of any individual staff member or any board or committee that is a
part of Land Use.
There has been some interest in this position and if this is approved it will be advertised within the Town for approximately 3-4 weeks in
the month of May to be followed by interviews conducted in June, with hiring in July.
Discussion among the board included the following:
•
BOS members voicing support for the Land Use Director position and acknowledged the fact that there may not be a need for
a Bylaw but transparency through presenting at Public Hearings & Annual Town Meeting is important
Adding the Sewer Commission to the list of departments along with coordinating activities with the DPW
• Town Manager will present this at Annual Town Meeting and it cannot be amended on the floor
•
This does not require a changing of agencies, but rather extending the use of Planning Director to become Land Use Director
• If it is determined that a vote by the people at Annual Town Meeting should not be required, then we should not ask them to do
SO
• Intent of this position is not to delegate appointment authority or to delegate disciplinary action
• The BOS will review further the last paragraph on page 3 of the draft document of the Administrative Organization Plan for
potential change in language

An upgrade of an existing pipeline within New York, Massachusetts, Connecticut, and New Hampshire which will result in the
construction of approximately 250 miles of pipeline
They are looking to locate a significant portion within existing utility easements
• They are in the process of surveying for the purpose of soil testing and have contacted some individual homeowners
• Homeowners have the right to refuse entrance onto their property
This project is slated to begin in the year 2018
Ms. Speidel added that the Town of Lunenburg cannot review any agreements or provide legal advice on any document that a

3. Request to Review/ approve Middlesex County Interagency Mutual Aid Agreement (Police): Chief Marino reviewed the
Middlesex County Interagency Mutual Aid Agreement with the board. He explained that Lunenburg currently has Mutual Aid
Agreements with most of the Cities & Towns in Worcester County and this would be for the Towns such as Shirley which are in
Middlesex County.
This agreement gives the Police the authority to travel over the Town line and gives them the same powers and
indemnity that they would have if they were exercising their powers of arrest within in the confines of the Town of Lunenburg. We are
signed on to the State wide along with the Worcester County agreement and joining with Middlesex County would cover us completely.
Basically this would be used for when a major incident has occurred and would apply to all Towns in Middlesex County. The Chiefs of
Police Association is encouraging law enforcement agencies to go with Mutual Aid Agreements and Chief Marino believes this would be
a good idea in our efforts to become more organized as a Police Department.
Currently we appoint Officers from other communities to be able to work with our Police Department if asked to, and if this is signed,
that will no longer be needed. Officers will be able to operate within another Town when it's necessary and it will be self-initiated
Mr. Alonzo voiced his concern of giving Police authority within our Town to people who we don't know and he can see how this might
cause problems in jurisdictions.
Chief Marino assured him that this is only for extenuating circumstances and he cannot recall any incident where another agency did
not call to say that they were in this community. This is governed by Department Policy with rules and regulations and is generally only
used when another jurisdiction is calling you for help
After due and diligent discussion between the board and Chief Marino it was agreed to revisit this request again after further review is
done with comparing the Worcester County Mutual Aid Agreement with the Middlesex Mutual Aid Agreement.
