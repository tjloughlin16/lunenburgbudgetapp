LUNENBURG BOARD OF HEALTH 
MEETING MINUTES 
Monday, February 6, 2023 
 
The Board of Health met at 7:00 pm at the Ritter Memorial Building  
 
Members Present:  George Emond, Perry Jewell, and Evan Watters.    
 
MINUTES APPROVED 
 
December 22, 2022 
 
On motion by Jewell and second by Watters, the above minutes were approved.  All in favor. 
 
77 PENINSULA DRIVE 
 
Doug Smith met with the board on behalf of the owner.  This system is in failure.  This is a small lot 
with an irregular shape that borders Hickory Hills Lake .  Because of this, the available area for a 
system is limited.  The property is served by public water.  The wetlands (lake) are more than 100 feet 
away 
 
Variance requested:  groundwater offset from 5 feet to 4 feet.  This would reduce the surface grading. 
 
There are two wells on the property that need to be either decommissioned or approval granted for 
irrigation wells.  This property is part of an estate to be conveyed to the Conservation Commission.

requested variances. All in favor. 
 
493 WEST STREET 
 
Doug Smith met with the board of behalf of the owner.  This system is in failure.  The property is 
served by town water.  The lot has shallow depth of soil and very high groundwater.   
 
Variances requested:  Sieve analysis and reduction in 12”  on inlet and outlet tees.  This  is an existing 
house and the plumbing cannot be raised enough to allow the tank outlets to meet the offset. 
 
There is a shallow well on the site that needs to be decommission or approval granted for irrigation. 
 
After discussion and on motion by Watters and second by Jewell  the board voted to approve the 
variances.  All in favor. 
 
733 WEST STREET 
 
Dan Proctor met with the board on behalf of  the owner.  This system is in failure.  This lot has high 
seasonable groundwater at 18: and a perc rate of 29 MPI.

LUNENBURG BOARD OF HEALTH 
MEETING MINUTES 
Monday, March 6, 2023 
 
The Board of Health met at 7:00 pm at the Ritter Memorial Building  
 
Members Present:  George Emond, Perry Jewell, Paul Fortin, Evan Watters and Lisa Normandin 
 
MINUTES APPROVED 
 
February 6, 2023 
 
On motion by Normandin and second by Fortin, the above minutes were approved.  All in favor. 
 
1 VALLEY ROAD 
 
The Septic Permit was originally due to expire in April of 2021, during Covid.  In March of 2022, the 
then owner of the property requested a one year extension of the permit, which was approved until 
February 14, 2023.  The property was then sold.  The new owner would now like to begin installing the 
system and is asking for a one year extension to complete the work. 
 
After discussion, and on motion by Jewell and second by Fortin, the board voted to approve the request 
for a one year extension.  This is the last extension that will be granted.  All in favor. 
 
JEN DUFFY – RESIDENTIAL KITCHEN 
 
Jen Duffy met with the board.  Ms. Duffy had been in c ontact with Bridget Braley, Food Service, for 
Nashoba.  She was requesting a Residential Kitchen Permit to produce Jellies and Jam.  Ms. Duffy has 
a septic system and a private well.  These permits have been issued in the past.  Typically, if a Title 5 
has not be recently completed, the board would request one.  In addition, a well test can be requested. 
 
Ms. Braley advised that “because you have a private septic a business from your home is prohibited – 
under Title 5 Septic Regulations.  This would be cons idered a change in use of the property and could 
cause premature failure of your septic system”. 
 
Admin. Assistant Andrea Schnepf contacted DEP and spoke with James Laughlin.  There is nothing in 
DEP or Title 5 that disallows the issuance of a Residential Kitchen Permit if you have a septic system 
and there is no guidance from DEP on this issue.  DEP state that at one point they considered providing 
guidance but there is a reduction in requests and they never issued guidance.  This was then sent back 
to the local Board of Health to address. 
 
Ms. Duffy stated that she is now a member of the Lunenburg Ag Commission and would like to 
participate in the Lunenburg Farmers Market.  She has a small homestead on Goodrich Street.  The 
volume of jelly she would produc e is small.  She purchased the house less than 2 years ago and there 
was a Title 5 completed.  Her well has a double filtration system on it.  As Ms. Duffy currently has a 
full time job, she is not considering making her home kitchen her source of income. 
 
===PAGE 2===
After discussion and on motion by Watters and second by Norman din, the board voted to approve  a 
Home Kitchen License for Ms. Duffy.  All in favor. 
 
In addition, Chairman George Emond will pursue this with the Nashoba Board as this has an effect on 
all of the Nashoba towns and by extension, the farmer’s markets. 
 
STATE EARMARKS FOR SATE FY24 BUDGET 
 
Town Manager Heather Lemieux sent general information for possible requests for State Earmarks for 
the State Budget.  Member Evan Watters stated that as a member of the Finance Committee he is a part 
of this process should the Board have a request. 
 
After discussion, the Board did not have any request. 
 
Having no further matters before the board and on  motion Normandin and seconded by  Jewell, the 
meeting was adjourned at 7:45 pm, all in favor.

required.  An annual maintenance agreement is required for the Jet Treatment tank.

the condition of yearly maintenance agreement and deed notations.  All in favor.

Monday, April 3, 2023 
 
The Board of Health met at 7:00 pm at the Ritter Memorial Building

March 20, 2023

493 WEST STREET 
 
Dan Proctor met with the board on behalf of the owner.  This system is in failure and in the 
groundwater.  The house is connected to pubic water.  Soil testing  was completed and an area was 
found for the installation of a replacement system, however, the water table was so high a perc test 
could not be conducted.  The site is challenging in that it has very high seasonal ground water and 
limited areas of the required 4 feet of pervious soil. 
 
This matter has previously been before the board.  What is being requested now is a reduction in 
groundwater from 4 feet to 3 feet and inlet and outlet tees reduction.  This is due to the seasonal high 
ground water and to ma ke the installation of the system more affordable.  This property will require a 
three bedroom deed restriction and the destruction of the on-site existing well.

requested variances with the condition of a three bedroom deed restriction and destruction of the 
existing well.  All in favor. 
 
134 HOLLIS ROAD 
 
Dan Proctor met with the board on behalf of the owner.  This system would fail a Title 5 inspection 
due to high seasonal ground water. 
 
Mr. Proctor stated that they dug 11 test holes, all ledge, no workable materials.  A very shallow depth 
to bedrock was found throughout the site.  Without the minimum soil requirements, a sewage disposal 
system could not be designed.  The applicant is proposing to install a tight tank. 
 
Title 5 Regulations require the board to approve the use of a tight tank before they can move forward.  
In addition, the approval letter from the Board of Health must be recorded on the deed of the property. 
 
After discussion and on motion by Watters and second by Fortin, the board voted to approve the use of 
a tight tank on the above property.  All in favor. 
 
Once the design plan is approved by Agent Jim Garreffi, a maintenance plan needs to be i n place, and 
deed restriction for the tight tank, as well as a deed restriction for a three bedroom dwelling

forward with new construction on the lot.  There is also an old point well on the property.  The owner

The old point well would need to be totally disconnected from the house.  The point well can only be 
used for irrigat ion purposes.  The re will need to be a deed notation  that the point well is only for

LUNENBURG BOARD OF HEALTH 
MEETING MINUTES 
Monday, May 1, 2023 
 
The Board of Health met at 7:00 pm at the Ritter Memorial Building  
 
Members Present:  George Emond, Perry Jewell, Paul Fortin, Evan Watters and Lisa Normandin 
 
MINUTES APPROVED 
 
April 3, 2023 
 
On motion by Normandin and second by Fortin, the above minutes were approved.  All in favor. 
 
11 LONGWOOD ROAD 
 
Rick and Linda Hillson met with the board.  Mr. & Mrs. Hillson have had ongoing complaints of 
fumes coming from their neighbor’s oil furnace power vent.  The power vent was installed as a part the 
construction of an addition. 
 
Mr. Hillson called MA DEP and spoke with Michele Delmar re who in turn contact ed Agent Jim 
Garreffi.  In May of 2022, Garreffi spoke wi th Jim Ricci of the Lunenburg Fire Department.  Prior to 
Garreffi’s conversation with Ricci, a n inspection was done through the fire department and it was 
noted that there was an odor coming from the oil burner as well as a soot problem visible on the home.  
At that time, the power vent was replaced and  there was no longer a soot problem.   Mr. Hillson has 
sent emails to Jim Garreffi when he noted a problem.  Agent Garreffi  has responded to those emails by 
inspecting the property when he is able as well as sitting at t he property at times when the burner fires 
up.  He has noted an odor for approximately 3 – 4 minutes but then it disappears.  Agent Garreffi has 
done many inspections but does not believe that the intens ity and duration of the odor rises to the level 
of air pollution. 
 
Agent Garreffi sent a letter to the homeowner asking about the performance and maintenance of the 
burner and power vent.  The owner responded and advised that the both were working properly. 
 
There is approximately 100 feet between the power vent and the Hillson’s home. 
 
The Hillson’s stated that the owner of the adjoining property also has to close their windows w hen the 
burner is running.  Mr. Hillson stated that  he does not believe that the power vent is installed properly.  
The Hillson’s believe that weather, wind pattern and humidity have an affect  on the migration of the 
fumes. 
 
Mr. Hillson stated that they can’t enjoy their property, they can’t stay outside, and they can’t open their 
windows.  They suffer from physical symptoms such as coughing, burning throat and the fumes flare 
asthma. 
 
Chairman Emond asked why the air pollution definition cannot be used.  Agent Garreffi stated that the 
there is not a long enough duration to cite the regulation. 
 
===PAGE 2===
Mr. Hillson proposed installing a pipe up above the roof  line instead of the power vent to carry the 
fumes upward.  The estimated cost of a double wall chimney would be $2,000 for approximately 25 
feet.  He stated that communication with his neighbor is not an open communication right now. 
 
Member Normandin asking if a double walled pipe was installed  would that satisfy the Hillsons.  They 
agreed that it would. 
 
Jim Garreffi stated that he would like to  see more information as well as more inspections of fumes 
noted.  Chairman Emond asked that the Hillman’s keep a detailed log. 
 
After discussion, and on motion by No rmandin and second by Watters, this matter was continued until 
May 15th at 7:00 pm.  All in favor. 
 
Having no further matters before the board and on  motion by Normandin and seconded by Fortin, the 
meeting was adjourned at 7:50 pm, all in favor.
