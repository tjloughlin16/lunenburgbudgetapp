Lunenburg Finance Committee 
Meeting Minutes 
Thursday, May 19, 2022 
 
Location: In-person at Lunenburg Town Hall and broadcast on Zoom 
 
Present: Chairperson Peter Beardmore, Chris Menard, Dave Passios, attended in person. Jay Simeone, 
and Evan Watters attended remotely. 
 
Guests: Town Manager Heather Lemieux attended remotely. 
1. Call to Order 
Mr. Beardmore called the meeting to order at 7:00 p.m. and read a statement 
concerning the Open Meeting Law, and how all public meetings are legally being held 
remotely. 
2. Pledge of Allegiance 
 
3. Announcements: Mr. Beardmore announced the Memorial Day Ceremony to be held at 
Veterans Memorial Park on Memorial Day, May 30, 2022 at 10:00 a.m. 
 
4. Public Comment: Mr. Beardmore informed the board and the audience that he will seek 
reappointment to his seat on the Finance Committee.  
 
5. Chair Report: Mr. Beardmore asked members to think about topics for discussion and how the 
committee should convene over the summer. 
 
6. Town Manager/Finance Director Report: Ms. Lemieux reported that the Green Communities 
grant for $187,000 has been submitted, and that the town is accepting proposals for the LED 
streetlight project, which should be a turn-key operation. Ms. Lemieux also informed the 
committee that the town has received $68,000 from the state in reimbursement for Emergency 
Paid Sick Leave. Ms. Lemieux also reported the town has received a $18,000 grant for the high 
school score board, and a Winter Recovery Assistance Program grant in the amount of 
$262,678, which is to be spent on roadways and sidewalks by June 2023. Ms. Lemieux reported 
the chiller for the public safety building will not be delivered until August. The town will not 
receive federal funding toward the Flat Hill Rd Culvert project, according to Ms. Lemieux. 
 
7. Town Meeting Review: The committee discussed the way Article 6 (Capital Plan) and Article 19 
(30 School St) were decided, and most agreed the Salary Administration Plan article needed 
more time before it was presented at Town Meeting.  The committee agreed that they are in 
favor of the new electronic voting procedure. 
According to Mr. Beardmore the FY23 Capital Plan is the most expensive capital plan to date, 
and opined that the Finance Committee should play a larger role in the development of the 
capital plan, and that the article should be reworded in the future to allow for line item 
adjustments. Mr. Passios added the Town Charter, M.G.L.s, and the town’s fiscal policies are 
inconsistent in regard to the capital plan. Mr. Menard suggested the Finance Committee have 
access to the same documents as the Capital Planning Committee, and that their meetings 
===PAGE 2===
should be recorded. Mr. Menard also pointed out that projects on the plan were inadequately 
scoped, and that estimates were inaccurate. Mr. Simeone added that the new Facilities Director 
should be able to assist in getting accurate quotes for projects after he or she is hired. 
On Article 19, concerning 30 School St., Mr. Beardmore pointed out that with the article’s 
amendment, the charge of the direction of the project could be foreseen as unknown. Mr. 
Passios added that the original article as written should not have combined the demolition of 
the Old Primary School with the design plans for a park.  
 
8. Review of FY22 Goals and Procedures: The committee agreed that they’d like to see the actual 
budget of the departments that come before them. Mr. Beardmore proposed formulating a 
sample document, on which the department presentations should be based. On quarterly 
reports, Mr. Beardmore suggested to improve the facilitation of discussion. On Chapter 70, the 
committee agreed the discussion was helpful in terms of discovering how much more money 
Lunenburg spends on education in general, as well as Monty Tech,  in comparison to its 
municipal counterparts. The committee agreed to hold workshops over the summer to develop 
their goals and procedures for FY23. 
 
9. Topics and Goals for Summer:  
• Role of Finance Committee in Capital Planning process 
• Policies and Procedures 
• Budget Presentations 
• Debt and Debt Capacity  
 
10. Minutes: Mr. Menard motioned to approve the minutes from the March 31, April 14, and April 
28, 2022 meetings. Mr. Watters seconded. On vote, motion carried. (4-0-1) 
 
11. Committee Reports: Committee members updated each other on their respective liaison 
assignments. 
 
12. Public Comment: none 
 
13. Items for Future Meetings: Mr. Passios expressed his concern over the Primary School and 
Turkey Hill Elementary School, and suggested that if the town is neglecting these buildings 
because they are being replaced, they need to be honest about that.  
 
14. Next Meetings: June 9 –remote, June 23 (maybe) 
 
15. Adjourn At 8:44 p.m., Mr. Passios motioned to adjourn, Mr. Menard seconded. On vote, motion 
carried unanimously.

Lunenburg Finance Committee 
Meeting Minutes 
Thursday, June 9, 2022 
 
Location: This meeting was held remotely via Zoom 
 
Members present: Chairperson Peter Beardmore, Chris Menard, Dave Passios, Tom Balish, Evan Watters 
 
Guests: Town Manager Heather Lemieux, Karen Brochu 
1. Call to Order 
Mr. Beardmore called the meeting to order at 7:00 p.m. and read a statement 
concerning the Open Meeting Law, and how all public meetings are legally being held 
remotely. 
This meeting was recorded and is viewable on YouTube at  
2. Announcements: Mr. Beardmore announced a tentative Finance Committee Appointments 
Committee June 21. There is one vacancy on the Finance Committee. 
Candlelight vigil to “Stop the Violence” on Friday, June 10 at 6:30 p.m., after another mass 
shooting took place at Robb Elementary School in Uvalde, Texas. 
Volunteer Reception June 16, at 5:30 p.m. at the Eagle House. 
 
3. Public Comment: 
 
4. Chair Report: Mr. Beardmore said he would like to schedule topics of discussion by quarter after 
several conversations with Select Board Chairperson Michael Ray Jeffries.  
 
5. Town Manager/Finance Director Report: Ms. Lemieux reported that the state will be closing 
FY22 with a $7 billion surplus. The state senate proposals include a 5.4 percent increase in 
general government aid, which means $60 minimum per pupil in student aid. There is pending 
legislation to extend remote meetings into December 2023. There is a bill on the floor that seeks 
to add up to a 5 percent COLA increase annually to retirement funds. Ms. Lemieux will contact 
legislators to oppose the increase as the town would have no control over the annual unfunded 
liability assessment.  Another bill on the floor entails community host agreements with cannabis 
related businesses and the amount of control the state’s Cannabis Control Commission has over 
those agreements. The MBTA Housing Choice Act could face litigation regarding affordable 
housing.  
 
Ms. Lemieux also reported that the installation of the new IT network equipment has been 
installed, and all town email has migrated to Microsoft 360, except for the library. Ms. Lemieux 
also updated the committee on the FY21 capital plan. Monty Tech students were originally 
scheduled to work on an employee break room in the Ritter Building, but the work was 
postponed due to staffing vacancies at the school. The project is now slated to start in the fall of 
2022. Due to supply chain issues, the DPW has still not received its FY21 requested equipment 
(6-wheel dump truck, ¾ ton pickup with plow). The Police Department is still waiting on parts for 
consoles in the police cruisers. Mr. Beardmore inquired how current inflated prices will affect 
===PAGE 2===
funds appropriated for FY21.  The Architectural Preservation District Committee had to 
postpone the hearing for the Ritter Building and Town Hall envelope projects because there was 
no quorum. The hearing is now scheduled for June 26, 2022. The 925 Massachusetts Ave. 
property will face foreclosure if property owners do not respond to a previously sent notice. 
Asbestos abatement has been completed on the Town Beach building. End of year funds will 
contribute to the Town Beach building project. The construction portion of the project is out to 
bid.  
Mr. Passios asked for an update on the purchase of streetlights from Unitil. Ms. Lemieux 
responded that the attorneys are still discussing the terms of the purchase. Unitil has been 
informed of the non-working streetlights on Lancaster Ave. Mr. Passios also asked about the 
maintenance plan for the lights after they’re purchased. Ms. Lemieux said that plan is in the 
queue. Mr. Watters asked why the town would go against the COLA increase for retirees. Mr. 
Beardmore opined a $7 billion surplus in the state budget should be able to absorb the cost of 
the increase, noting the relation to the rise in inflation, and pondered whether the town or state 
should be financially responsible for the increase.  
 
6. Summer Calendar/Topics of discussion: The committee agreed they’d attempt to discuss seven 
topics over the summer. 
a. goals regarding procedures and budget presentation recommendations 
b. fiscal policies (being discussed by Select Board in August 2022) 
c. debt planning regarding the role of the Finance Committee weighing in on new building 
projects 
d.  senior tax relief 
e.  document management and migration, along with Open Meeting Law compliance in 
regard to document accessibility and transparency – Mr. Menard and Mr. Beardmore 
agreed that the migration to Microsoft 360 will make sharing documents easier than 
using the former town email system.  
f.  onboarding of new members –Mr. Balish volunteered to work on a “FinCom 101” deck 
g. Finance Committee role in the Capital Planning process 
Committee members also discussed inviting state delegation members to another meeting in 
the 2nd or 3rd quarter.  
Mr. Menard asked about Mr. Passios’s responsibilities, and how to take care of those moving 
forward as he will not seek reappointment to the Finance Committee, and his term is finished as 
of June 30, 2022. Mr. Passios is the vice chair and Finance Committee representative of the 
Capital Planning Committee, the Green Communities Committee, and the Parks Commission, all 
through 2023, and has acted as liaison for the Finance Committee. 
New committee liaisons will be assigned, and a new Finance Committee member will be 
appointed as of the next meeting July 14. Mr. Beardmore expects to discuss members’ new roles 
at that first meeting of FY23.  
The committee agreed not to meet June 23, which made this meeting the last Finance 
Committee meeting for Mr. Passios. Mr. Passios advised committee members to express their 
opinions, especially if those opinions oppose the general consensus.  
===PAGE 3===
 
7. Minutes Mr. Menard motioned to approve the minutes from the May 19, 2022 meeting, Mr. 
Watters seconded. On vote, motion carried. 4-0-1. 
 
8. Committee Reports Committee members updated each other on their respective liaison 
assignments. 
 
9. Public Comment Mr. Passios cited the shallow administrative level for the town’s facilities and 
grounds, as well as DPW, and urged committee members, and members of the public, to be 
aware of what’s supposed to be being done, and to report any issues or problems until the town 
can get the depth they need on that administrative level in those departments.  Mr. Balish 
thanked Mr. Passios for his service to the committee and to the town. Mr. Beardmore admitted 
that his first impression of Mr. Passios was wrong, and that he will miss him on the committee. 
The conversations, though they disagreed on many issues, were constructive and fair. Mr. 
Beardmore recognized Mr. Passios for putting in the most time, and work into the committee, 
and thanked him for serving. Mr. Passios reminded committee members that expressing a 
dissenting opinion on any board has a very important role in coming to a consensus, and 
bringing all the possible information to the surface, and to the public. Mr. Passios thanked 
committee members for their work on the committee.  
 
10. Items for Future Meetings refer to items a-g 
 
11. Next Meetings July 14 
 
12. Adjourn At 8:39 p.m. Mr. Passios motioned to adjourn. Mr. Menard seconded. On vote, motion 
carried unanimously.

Lunenburg Finance Committee 
Meeting Minutes 
Thursday, July 21, 2022 
 
Location: In-person and remote at Lunenburg Town Hall and broadcast on Zoom 
 
Present: Chairperson Peter Beardmore, Chris Menard, in person. Attending remotely: Tom Gray, Tom 
Balish, Evan Watters 
 
Guests: Town Manager Heather Lemieux, Finance Director Karen Brochu, Dave Passios 
1. Call to Order 
Mr. Beardmore called the meeting to order at 7:06 p.m. and read a statement 
concerning the Open Meeting Law, and how all public meetings are legally being held 
remotely. 
This meeting was recorded and is viewable on YouTube at https://youtu.be/8jvBlfar6ZI 
2. Pledge of Allegiance 
 
3. Announcements:  
 
4. Public Comment: Mr. Passios, commenting as a member of the public, informed the committee 
that though his term as a member of the Finance Committee was up at the end of June 2022, his 
term on the Capital Planning Committee as a member of the Finance Committee still has one 
year left, and he does not intend to resign from that position.  
 
5. Chair Report: Mr. Beardmore was recently appointed to the School Committee and will have to 
resign from the Finance Committee at the end of September. Mr. Beardmore expressed his 
hope for a smooth transition. 
 
6. Board Reorganization and Upcoming Calendar: Mr. Beardmore pointed out that with his 
resignation in September, the committee could be operating with only five members, and that 
choosing who will serve as liaison on other boards and committees could wait, but they should 
focus on who from the Finance Committee will serve on the Municipal Building Design 
Committee, and the Capital Planning Committee. 
 
7. Reorganization: Mr. Watters contacted Mr. Simeone via text during the meeting to inform him 
the committee was working on reorganization. Mr. Simeone informed Mr. Watters he had no 
intention to seek a higher position on the committee. 
 
a. Chair: Mr. Menard moved to nominate Mr. Watters as Chairperson, Mr. Balish 
seconded. On vote, motion carried unanimously.  
b. Vice Chair: Mr. Balish moved to nominate Mr. Menard as Vice Chair, Mr. Gray seconded. 
On vote, motion carried unanimously. 
c. Clerk: Mr. Menard motioned to nominate Mr. Balish as Clerk, Mr. Gray seconded. On 
vote, motion carried unanimously. 
Approved by Lunenburg Finance 
Committee at July 28, 2022 meeting. 
===PAGE 2===
d. Capital Planning Committee, finance committee appointee: Mr. Passios will continue to 
serve out his term as the finance committee appointee to the Capital Planning 
Committee. Mr. Menard and Mr. Watters agreed that the capital planning process 
needs to be more transparent, and that meetings should be recorded. Mr. Menard 
added that the committee will need to find a way to communicate with Mr. Passios as 
he won’t necessarily be in regular attendance at Finance Committee meetings.  
e. Municipal Building Design Committee, finance committee appointee: Mr. Watters 
motioned to appoint Mr. Gray to serve as finance committee appointee to the 
Municipal Building Design Committee, Mr. Menard seconded. On vote, motion carried 
unanimously.  
f. Liaisons: The committee did not formally decide on who would serve as liaison to the 
following committees, but did discuss who served in the past. Mr. Balish said he is 
willing to continue to serve as liaison to police and fire, and the library. Mr. Gray said he 
is willing to continue to serve as liaison to the land use departments. Mr. Watters, being 
a member of the Board of Health will continue to serve as finance committee liaison. 
Mr. Menard is willing to serve as liaison to the Parks Commission. The committee did 
realize that Mr. Simeone has served as liaison to the DPW, but he was not in attendance 
to accept the role. School Committee, Council on Aging, and Green Communities 
Committee currently have no liaison assigned, and no committee member expressed 
interest in serving.  
 
 
8. Town Manager/Finance Director Report: Ms. Lemieux reported that the EPA will fund the safe 
removal of underground gas tanks, and hazardous soil from 925 Massachusetts Ave. The 
previous property owners still have one year from July 6, 2022 to take ownership and pay back 
taxes.  
 
Ms. Lemieux also reported that remote meeting participation options have been extended to 
March 31, 2023.  
 
The town has been awarded a $500,000 small bridge grant to fund the Flat Hill Rd culvert 
project. The project will go out to bid in January 2023, with hopes the project will be completed 
December 2023.  
 
Ms. Lemieux also reported that the financial management letter comment on fraud risk 
assessment will be addressed in FY23 as there is funding available through a rewards program 
with MIIA to fund an assessment.  
 
The town has received a Notice of Intent from Loofah Farms to do agricultural business at 361 
Massachusetts Ave. The farm will seek tax incentives from the town. Mr. Menard commented 
that the parks department does own land adjacent to the farm property, and it would be good 
to consider granting access from Mass Ave. as opposed to Northfield Rd.  
 
===PAGE 3===
Potential warrant articles for fall Special Town Meeting (date TBD): adding Salary Administration 
Plan (SAP) positions to the compensation classification grid, funding for an outside consultant to 
perform a compensation study for SAP positions; additional funding for assessor’s budget for 
cyclical data inspections estimated at $35,000, and a prior year invoice from FY21.  
 
FY22 Q4 report will be presented to the Select Board on August 23, 2022. 
 
Ms. Lemieux has met with the DPW director to update the Pavement Management Plan as 
several roads have been addressed.  
 
A purchase and sales agreement on the street lights should be ready by the August 2, 2022 
Select Board meeting.  
 
The town has received one bid on the town beach building project, and it was much higher than 
expected. The plan for the town beach building may change. Ms. Lemieux will be meeting with 
DPW Director and Parks Commission for feedback.  
 
Road materials for FY23 has increased between 20-40 percent from the FY20 contract. 
 
Ms. Lemieux informed the committee that the state has passed their budget, and the town will 
receive $54,417 more in state aid than originally anticipated.   
 
9. FY23 Goals and Operating Principles: The committee agreed they’d request an overview of 
department heads’ FY24 budget presentations to include an overview of current fiscal year 
operations to date –successes, gaps, and priorities that influence budget requests. As well as  
budget requests, including details regarding line items included in the Town Manager’s budget 
recommendations, as well as a summary of spending for the first half of FY23, and expected 
transfers or lines that will be underspent. Mr. Menard added that when the committee meets 
with department heads before the budget presentations would be a good time highlight how 
each department operates, and during the actual budget presentations the committee can 
discuss the numbers.  
 
10. Discuss timing and plan of Fiscal Policy and Debt Planning: The Select Board will discuss fiscal 
policies on August 23, 2022. The committee agrees that there should be budgeting guidelines 
established when it comes municipal building design projects. Mr. Beardmore inquired whether 
there is a way to determine how much money the town could borrow while following the 
current fiscal policy in order to decide on a project based on cost. Ms. Brochu suggested the 
town would need to know how much money a project would cost in order to determine how 
much money could be borrowed.  
 
11. Discuss plan and timing for addressing Town Meeting amendments to capital plan articles: Mr. 
Beardmore would like to see the capital planning article written in a way that permits changes 
to be made within the town’s capital plan at Annual Town Meeting. Mr. Beardmore then 
pointed out that one elected Select Board may change the language in the article, but as the 
===PAGE 4===
board’s composition changes, so might the language. Mr. Beardmore then suggested writing a 
bylaw would permanently change the way the article is written. Mr. Beardmore will seek town 
counsel’s guidance, and hopes to get the item on the Select Board’s agenda in September 2022, 
in order to put the bylaw in effect at Special Town Meeting.  
 
12. Discuss new member on-boarding plan: Mr. Balish plans to meet with the Town Manager, and 
consult the Massachusetts Association of Finance Committees, and will be ready to report at the 
August 18, 2022 meeting.  
 
13. Minutes: Mr. Gray moved to approve the minutes from the May 19, and June 9, 2022 meetings. 
Mr. Menard seconded. On vote, motion carried. (4-0-1) 
 
14. Committee Reports no reports 
 
15. Public Comment 
 
16. Items for Future Meetings 
 
17. Next Meetings July 28, August 18, August 25

2. Special Town Meeting Bylaw Proposed on Capital Planning: Mr. Watters explained that Mr. 
Beardmore worked with Town Counsel to develop the language in the following proposed 
bylaw. Mr. Watters read the bylaw for the record. 
"§ 81-11 Amending capital appropriations. Warrant articles and floor motions shall not 
restrict Town Meeting voters’ ability to propose and approve amendments in totality, 
description, and/or scope to individual items or projects in capital appropriation articles and 
motions. Notwithstanding the above, amendments shall not limit the Town Manager’s 
authority to reapply capital funds among the individual items and projects in the capital 
plan, nor shall Town Meeting be authorized to increase the total capital plan appropriation 
printed in the warrant.” 
Mr. Watters explained that at the last Annual Town Meeting, some finance committee members 
felt they needed more information on some items on the capital plan before they could support 
the plan in its entirety. It was learned that because of the way the warrant article regarding the 
capital plan was written, residents that attend Town Meeting can only support or deny the plan 
as a whole, regardless of the amount of information available on each item. Mr. Beardmore 
explained that the proposed bylaw does not change the process of how the capital plan is 
developed, but it does seek to restore legislative authority to Town Meeting. Mr. Beardmore 
also pointed out that the capital plan has increased at about 15 percent per year since the 
current capital planning process began 20 years ago. Mr. Beardmore opined that the capital plan 
used to serve as a list of needs for the town, but has recently shifted toward a list of wants that 
lack proper vetting and oversight. Ultimately, Select Board members agreed that the proposed 
bylaw should be allowed to be heard by those who attend Town Meeting, but Mr. Jeffreys, Ms. 
Sroka, Mr. Dwyer, and Mr. Franco all opined they’d prefer the current capital plan warrant 
language remain the same, and fret that the approval of the proposed bylaw will cause a Town 
Meeting debate of the capital plan line by line, which will then lead to a longer Town Meeting, 
and therefore eventually, even less participation. Mr. Jeffreys opined that as it stands, those 
who regularly attend Annual Town Meeting do not fully represent the whole town. Mr. Jeffreys 
also pointed out that the school’s budget is accepted as a whole, and is not debated. Mr. 
Jeffreys expressed that he believes in the current process of how the capital plan is developed -

at November 10, 2022 meeting.

through proper vetting of department heads, the capital planning committee, and the Town 
Manager -and that Town Meeting participants should be able to trust the current process. Mr. 
Beardmore reminded the Select Board that if the finance committee thought the Select Board 
would not put the bylaw proposal on the warrant, that it would be put through via a citizen’s 
petition. Mr. Beardmore iterated that the bylaw does not propose to separate the capital plan 
into individual warrant articles. Mr. Beardmore pointed out that Town Meeting is allowed to

3. Debt Capacity: Mr. Watters explained that the finance committee has observed the tremendous 
effort put forth to develop concepts and ideas to remedy issues regarding municipal buildings, 
and all things related, only to find projects denied at Town Meeting because the cost is too 
expensive. The finance committee is satisfied with the town’s current debt capacity, but would 
like to see a financial forecast involving various scenarios to see how much money the town 
could feasibly borrow in order to form an affordable and obtainable vision. Mr. Jeffreys opined 
that the town is aware of what they can spend, but that the debt capacity is weighed down 
because of school building projects. Mr. Jeffries entertained the idea of relooking at the 11 
percent excludable debt policy, and suggested the possibility of putting limits on school projects 
in order to address municipal building issues, and other financial issues due to face the town. 
Ms. Lemieux pointed out that her annual five-year financial forecast does illustrate the town’s 
debt capacity. Mr. Beardmore suggested that the five-year forecast does not account for 
projects that need to be done, and the forecast they’re proposing could. Mr. Balish pointed out 
that a professional financial planner could form an analysis in a few weeks, and that the 
proposed forecast is not an exhaustive process. Ms. Sroka opined that she, and many people in 
town, would like to see any project get done, and understands the value in having a financial 
planner map out different scenarios in order to define and execute affordable projects. Mr. 
Jeffreys suggested a follow-up discussion on the finance committee’s idea.  
Mr. Jeffreys asked members of the finance committee what the Select Board could do to make 
them feel more supported. Committee members agreed that they’ve never felt not supported 
by the Select Board, and suggested that board members continue to attend, and watch finance 
committee meetings on pertinent issues. Select Board members agreed they’re favorable to 
more joint meetings with the finance committee, and are looking forward to collaborating more 
in the future.

7. Conversation with DPW Director Bill Bernard: Mr. Bernard informed the committee that the 
biggest issue the DPW currently faces is their fleet of trucks. There are four trucks used for snow 
removal currently out of service, and the department is still waiting on trucks ordered in FY22, 
and FY23, and doesn’t expect to see them for at least a year. Mr. Bernard will continue to seek 
funding from the capital plan to complete the fleet at about $525,000 per year to fund those 
trucks and other equipment.  
 
Mr. Bernard will continue on with the pavement management plan, which is currently being 
updated. The town’s roads appear to be in good shape, and the new plan should focus on crack 
sealing.  As far as sidewalks are concerned, Mr. Bernard reported that the town currently has 
ten miles of sidewalk, and he is working on a five-year plan to address sidewalk repairs to bring 
those up to date. Mr. Bernard estimates sidewalk repair will cost $400,000. 
 
Mr. Bernard also reported that the town’s sewer pump stations are nearing the end of their lives 
at 25 years. He estimates $300,000 to replace the stations and incorporate SCADA to monitor 
the new stations.  
 
Mr. Bernard also reported the DPW is keeping up with storm water regulations despite not 
having a full crew. The storm water task force is looking to form a storm water utility in order to 
address mandates.   
 
The DPW will be expanding the North Cemetery, which will need to be surveyed and plotted. 
 
Mr. Bernard also reported he’s received several calls from residents regarding tree trimming and 
removal. 
 
DPW ten-year planning includes addressing the 25 bridges and culverts in town regarding 
inspection and repairs. Mr. Bernard will seek grant funding for repairs. Drainage pipes will also 
need replacing.  
 
Mr. Balish asked about staffing at the DPW. Mr. Bernard said the heavy equipment operator 
position has been open since before he started working for the town.  Several applicants either 
don’t have the licensure required, or are seeking a higher wage than what the town currently 
offers.  
 
8. Conversation with School Department:  
a. FY23 Capital Projects Update: Dr. Burnham reported that the cost of the AC project at 
the primary school for which $230,000 was appropriated was largely underestimated. 
The school department is looking at alternative ways to cool the school in the warmer 
months.  
 
b. FY24 Capital Projects Requests:

2. Exterior Door Replacement at Turkey Hill Elementary School (THES) $160,000 
3. Access and Security upgrades at the primary school and THES $83,500, $131,000 
4. Upgrade Fire Alarm system at THES $287,000 
5. Athletic Complex feasibility study $50,000 
6. New pickup truck $68,000 
 
 
c. 10-Year Capital Projects Plan: Over the next ten years, the primary school seeks 
$618,500 in capital spending. Turkey Hill Elementary School needs include $15,056,822 
in capital requests, which assumes contributions from the Massachusetts School 
Building Authority (MSBA), and possibly $2,200,000 for the MS/HS to upgrade the turf 
field, walls, and concession stand.  
Over the next ten years district-wide, the schools plan to spend $343,923 on safety film 
for windows, as well as a replacement mower, 2 pickup trucks, plows, and a passenger 
van. 
d. 5-Year Staffing Plan: Dr. Burnham reported that the school department needs are 
constantly changing depending on the student body. Not all needs can be addressed 
due to lack of funding, but the school department prioritizes, and presents the staffing 
plan knowing there is some grant funding available to certain departments within the 
schools.  
e. ESSER Funded Positions: The school department is tracking data to see which positions 
will need to be eliminated as Elementary and Secondary School Emergency Relief 
(ESSER) funding runs out in 2024. Dr. Burnham assured the committee that the school 
department is well aware that municipal funding is not available to cover the positions 
made possible by ESSER funding.  
f. ARPA Funding: Dr. Burnham reported the school department requested funding for 
sidewalks leading toward the elementary and middle/high schools.  
g. Strategy for Continuous Improvement: Implemented in 2019, a living document that 
focuses on teaching and learning, climate and culture, equity, access, and achievement, 
and communications and operations.  
h. Budget Presentation Workshop: The school department presents their budget at the 
second January meeting of the School Committee. There is then a question and answer 
period on the budget at the first School Committee meeting in February. Dr. Burnham 
reported that oftentimes health insurance information becomes available in February, 
which tends to impact the budget plan, and another question and answer period is 
offered at the first March meeting of the School Committee. A public hearing on the 
final budget is slated for the second March meeting of the School Committee, where the 
School Committee usually approves the budget, which is then presented to the Finance 
Committee. Dr. Burnham plans to produce a recorded presentation of informational 
material for people to view on their own time. Mr. Beardmore added that he has 
discussed the Finance Committee’s preferences when it comes to the school 
department’s budget presentation, and that though focusing on the numbers is a good 
goal, there are challenges up ahead that will demand more qualitative discussion over 
quantitative.
