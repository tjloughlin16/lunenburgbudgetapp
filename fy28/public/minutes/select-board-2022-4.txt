Meeting with Fire Chief Sullivan on the Emergency Comprehensive Management Plan
Chief Sullivan stated the plan is a guidebook for a major incident or disaster in the community
that would affect the town for a long period of time, not the day to day operations. We are
updating it to a digital format that makes it easier to update and place on the state's website
emergency operations center. The plan delineates the steps to take in one of these extreme
events. It references other emergency plans such as the continuity of operations and the hazard
mitigation plans. It was used in 2008 and 2011 for extreme weather incidents.

Lions Club Update on Lunenburg Food Pantry
Anita Scheipers, 63 Chestnut Street and Corrine Scouten, Lunenburg Turkey Hill Lions Club
President, 830 Mass. Ave., presented an update on the Lunenburg Food Bank.
Ms. Scheipers introduced the Lions Club. They currently have 100 paying members, 50 non-
paying volunteers and high school volunteers.
Local Lions Club Chapter focus is on:
Community Service/Support
* Lunenburg Community Food Pantry
* North Star Meal Donations
* Vision & Hearing Exams/Aids
* Monthly Senior Pancake Breakfasts
* Easter Egg Hunt
* Trunk or Treat
* Gazebo Area Cleanup

* Boys & Girls Club support

* Gazebo Christmas tree
The International organization focuses on five key areas:
• Hunger

The cost of these projects is mostly covered by:

* February Calendar raffle
* Food sales at town events (band concert/boys & girls club)
Local Lions Club Chapter primary focus is on food insecurity in the community. Family need
has increased 100% since Feb/March of 2022.
The Lunenburg Community Food Pantry has a new location at the Thomas C. Passios Building.
The food pantry has recently established an Advisory Committee comprised of residents of all
ages, including students, to ensure the pantry meets the needs of the community.
Pantry is by appointment and serves approximately 16 families each week
Open: Sundays 10:00 am -1:00 pm, Tuesdays & Thursdays 6:00 pm - 7:00 pm
Walk-In Pantry - no appointment necessary-Serves up to 60 families each week
Open: Sundays 1:00 am-12:00 pm, Tuesdays & Thursdays 7:00 pm - 8:00 pm
Ms. Scouten noted the new location in the TCP Building; we also use the cafeteria as needed,
when it is available. It allows us to have a walk-in pantry. On behalf of the Lions Club I want to
extend our thanks for letting us have this space. We received the donation of a refrigerator and a
commercial freezer through the efforts of Mi. MacDonald, so we are now able to provide dairy,
fresh produce and frozen meats, which is a plus for these families.
Ms. Scheipers stated the advisory committee is to provide awareness of different dietary needs of
the families coming into the food pantry. She described the different levels of pantry service.
One requires pre-registration (by appointment) and the other walk-in service (no appointment
===PAGE 4===
required). Monetary donations fill in the items that are not donated. Volunteers are always
welcome. Anyone interested can call 978-660-8074 or they can go to our website:

1. Update on Creating a Senior Tax Relief Program through a Home Rule Petition
Ms. Lemieux reported they had a meeting about a week ago. Present was Mr. Alonzo,
Mi. Franco, Principal Assessor CJ Carroll and Finance Director Karen Brochu. We
reviewed some different communities that have adopted a senior tax relief program. The
criteria we used was the senior circuit breaker and those thresholds and different action
steps that we are going to take going forward. The Principal Assessor will be providing
data on what the impact is given different thresholds of different time that people have
lived in the community, 10 or 15 years and merge that with the data that he will receive
from the Town Clerk on age limits based on her residence list. This is basically the first
step where we can see how many properties we are talking about and what the impact
financially would be.
Mr. Alonzo noted the goal is to help seniors who meet certain criteria, residency and
age, to provide property tax relief, while making the burden minimal to the rest of the
residents. As soon as we hear back from the principal assessor we will have our next
meeting.

ARTICLD30, To see if the Town will vote to adopt the following "Resolution in Support of Changing the State
Flag & Seal of Massachusetts":
Whereas the history of Massachusetts is replete with instances of conflict between the European Colonists
and the Native Nations of the region, who first extended the hand of friendship to the Colonists on their
shores in 1620, and helped them to survive starvation during the settlers first winters on their land;
Whereas members of the Native Nation for who the State of Massachusetts is named were ambushed and
killed by Myles Standish, first commander of the Plymouth Colony, in April of 1623, barely two years
after the Pilgrims arrived on their shores;
Whereas the Colonial broadsword brandished above the head of the Native man on the Massachusetts
State Flag and Seal is modeled after Myles Standish's own broadsword, borrowed for that purpose from
the Pilgrim Hall in Plymouth by the illustrator Edmund Garrett in 1884;
===PAGE 16===
Whereas the belt binding the Native's cloak on the Flag and Seal is modeled after a belt worn by
Metacomet, known to the English as King Philip, who was among the Wampanoag leaders who
resorted to a mutually destructive war in 1675-76 in defense of Native lands against Colonial
encroachment;
Whereas the proportions of the body of the Native man in the Flag and Seal were modeled from a Native
skeleton kept in Winthrop, the bow modeled after a bow taken from a Native man shot and killed by a
colonist in Sudbury in 1665, and his facial features taken from a photograph of an Ojibwe chief from
Great Falls, Montana, considered by the illustrator to be a "fine specimen of an Indian," though not from
Massachusetts;
Whereas the history of relations between Massachusetts since Colonial times and the Native Nations
who continue to live within its borders includes the forced internment of thousands of so-called
"praying Indians" on Deer Island, in Boston Harbor, where they died by the hundreds of exposure in
1675, their subsequent enslavement in Boston, Bermuda, and the Caribbean Islands, the offering of 40
pounds sterling as bounty for the scalps of Native men, women and children in Massachusetts
beginning in 1686, increased to 100 pounds sterling for the scalps of Native adult males by 1722, half
that amount for Native women and children;
Whereas Native Americans were legally prohibited from even stepping foot into Boston from 1675 until
2004, when that law was finally repealed;
Whereas the 400# anniversary of the landing of the colonists at Plymouth Plantation in the year 2020,
afforded every citizen of the Commonwealth a chance to reflect upon this history and come to an
awareness of the possibility of a better relationship between the descendants of the Colonial immigrants
and the Native Nations of the Commonwealth;
Whereas Native Americans have long suffered the many abuses of racism, the appropriation of their
symbols for public schools and sports teams, the loss of their ancestral lands and the encroachment of their
cultural lifeways;
Whereas the area now known as the Town of Lunenburg shares a rich Native history with modern day tribal
Nations, who inhabited this area long before the first colonial settlers arrived circa 1718, such as the
Abenaki and the Nipmuc;
Now, therefore, BE IT RESOLVED that the Town of Lunenburg hereby adopts this resolution in support
of the work of the Special Commission Relative to the Seal and Motto of the Commonwealth, established
by a Resolve of the General Court in 2020 and appointed by the Governor to recommend changes to the
current flag and seal of Massachusetts; and in support of a new seal and motto for the Commonwealth that
may better reflect our aspirations for harmonious and respectful relations between all people who now call
Massachusetts home. The town clerk shall forward a copy of this resolution to Sen. Marc Pacheco and
Rep. Antonio Cabral, co-chairs of the Joint Committee on State Administration, who serve as members of
the Special Commission Relative to the Seal and Motto of the Commonwealth, and to Sen. John Cronin,
Rep. Michael Kushmerek and Rep. Danillo Sena, with the request they support the work of the
aforementioned Special Commission. (Submitted by Citizen Petition)

• Ratification of the Town Manager's Appointment of Police Sergeant- John Morreale
Chief Gammel read his letter for this appointment.
"As you are aware we have had a vacancy in the Sergeant's rank for some time now. As we were
able to backfill patrol positions, the vacancy was posted, and interviews were conducted on
Wednesday, April 6, 2022. At the conclusion of the process we were able to identify a candidate
to fill that vacancy. I am pleased to recommend Officer John Morreale for promotion to Patrol
Sergeant.
On February 2, 2022, the vacant Sergeant's position was posted in accordance with the
Collective Bargaining Agreement (CBA) between the Town of Lunenburg and I.B.P.O. Local
353, Article XXX, Promotions. The position was posted for 30 days, and I received three letters
of interest for the position.
On April 6, 2022, an interview panel was assembled, that included yourself, Chief
Michael Luth from the Groton Police Department and Chief Richard Burrows from the
Acton Police Department. Questions were asked of each candidate and their response
was rated on a scale of 1-5. At the conclusion of the interviews the panel was tasked
with ranking the candidates based on the interview.
Upon the conclusion of the panel interviews, Lt. Thibodeau and I met with the panel to
discuss the results. We were informed that all three candidates did well, and "we could not
go wrong with any of them." The strongest candidate was identified as Officer Morreale.
Officer Morreale was described as very well-spoken, knowledgeable and compassionate to
===PAGE 2===
give a few examples. It was also stated that he has a good working knowledge of the
Department's Policy and Procedures. Officer Morreale was a lifelong resident of Lunenburg until
recently moving to Ashby and is a graduate of Lunenburg High School. Officer Morreale holds a
Bachelor's Degree of Science in Criminal Justice, graduating from UMASS Lowell in 2014.
Officer Morreale completed the Reserve Intermittent Academy in 2014 and was hired as a
Lunenburg Reserve Police Officer in July of 2014. In June 2017, he was hired as a full time
officer and attended the Municipal Police Training Committee Basic Recruit Officer Course
(ROC) in Reading, graduating in 2018 with the 8th ROC.
Officer Morreale has completed several instructor level courses and is currently one of the
Department's Firearms, Less-Lethal, and Taser instructors. As well as being use of force
application instructor he is also currently one of Department's Field Training Officers, as
well as the Department's Criminal Justice Information System (CJIS) representative.
As the Department was working on Certification with Massachusetts Police Accreditation
Commission (MPAC), Officer Morreale was assigned as the Department's Accreditation
Manager. Officer Morreale was then certified as an Accreditation Manager through
MPAC and has played an integral role as we near completion. This has been a major
process to complete and without Officer Morreale's tireless work we would not be ready
for our certification.
Officer Morreale often volunteers his time with various community events, such as the
Boys and Girls Club "Are You Smarter than Our 6" Graders?" Another organization that he
is involved with is the Massachusetts Chiefs of Police Jimmy Fund Walk, annually assisting
with the fundraising efforts at Fenway Park. Officer Morreale is also responsible for setting
up and collection of the Jimmy Fund Collection cans at various stores throughout Town.
In his relatively short period time with the department Officer Morreale has made the most of the
opportunities given to him. Officer Morreale's commitment to the department and the community is
beyond reproach, which I can personally attest to after working with him for the last eight years.
Officer Morreale possesses the leadership skills that we look for in our supervisors. I believe Officer
Morreale recognizes the strengths of others, and knows how to address deficiencies in a positive
manner using teachable moments. Officer Morreale's compassion and understanding will help him
grow and navigate the inherent challenges that come with a Sergeant's role. It is also my belief that
he has the skills to develop younger officers into leadership roles throughout their careers.
I believe with his work experience, education, and leadership skills Officer Morreale is extremely
qualified for Sergeant's position. I highly recommend that Officer John Morreale be appointed as a
Sergeant. I thank you for your consideration of my request."
He presented Officer Morreale to the meeting. Officer Morreale thanked everyone for
having him in this evening and his family and friends for coming in this evening. He stated
he was grateful for this opportunity and was excited to get started on this next part of his
journey and continue working with the Town and its citizens.
The board congratulated Officer Morreale on his promotion and Chief Gammel for his hires
to the department.

Ms. Lemieux reported the notice was also provided to the Town Clerk and posted on the Town
website. The posting is a requirement according to Town Charter. The purpose of this
reorganization plan is to create a Director of Facilities and Grounds position that would oversee,
supervise and facilitate the comprehensive management of all town facilities and grounds. The
reason this is being recommended to town meeting is that it will improve the long and short term
management of our facilities and grounds with an individual whose background and skills relate
to property management, capital planning, building construction and grounds maintenance. This
position will help the Town strategize, prioritize and coordinate maintenance, repairs, safety and
efficiencies of municipal properties, as well as play a key role in future use and reuse of
properties. As there are no bylaws that would be affected by this reorganization plan, there are
no proposed bylaws with any amendments, revisions, repeals or otherwise as may be necessary
to accomplish this desired reorganization. Town meeting vote would approve or disapprove the
plan and cannot make any amendments.
The key benefits of creating a Director of Facilities and Grounds Position:
1. Hiring a professional level position with a background in facilities management.
Facilities management experience is not typical for Department of Public Works Directors.
An individual with this skill set will enhance and improve the town's current and future
facilities management plan.
2. A management level position whose sole focus is long term and short term planning and
maintenance of town facilities and grounds. Since the DPW Director is responsible for
===PAGE 5===
multiple different divisions, town facilities receive only a portion of that position's focus.
3. More preventative maintenance and less reactionary maintenance of our facilities and grounds.
4. Collaborate with the School's counterpart position on the overall maintenance plan for
facilities and grounds and work together with the Town Manager and Finance Director on capital
planning and financing alternatives.
S. Directed effort towards the health and safety of all staff and users of town facilities.
6. Create and maintain a property condition assessment of all town facilities and grounds
utilizing Facilities Management software.
This position will be appointed by the Town Manager, in accordance with Sections 4-2 (d) of the
Town Charter, with ratification of the appointment to be made by the Select Board.
The Director of Facilities and Grounds will have direct supervision for the following existing
positions: Facilities Superintendent, Facilities Custodian, and Recreation Director. Those
positions that are currently in the Laborers International Union of North America, Public
Employees, Local 39 will continue to assist with snow and ice removal operations, mostly
related to town facilities and grounds.
Essential functions of this position:
• Responsible for the planning, organizing, directing and control of all aspects of Town
facilities and grounds. This will include facility planning, renovation, construction,
energy conservation measures and efficiencies, preventative and regular maintenance,
and repair of all town-owned buildings and grounds.
• Responsible for a comprehensive approach to building maintenance and repair
coordination, oversight, inspection, oversight of maintenance logs, resolving any
warranty issues, establishing facility management schedules, and building security.
• Formulates procedures related to Facilities and Grounds operations, develops and
oversees employee schedules, and reviews and advises on daily maintenance issues.
• Ensures all facilities staff attend routine training development for essential functions and
the proper handling and application of all department equipment and tools; ensures staff
obtain required licenses and safety trainings.
• Focuses on the optimal facility use by staff, residents, and those who seek to conduct
business with or in the town to ensure an effective user-experience.
• Works with relevant town departments and elected Park Commission to advise and
recommend long term plan for all town facilities and grounds. Updates the ten-year
capital plan for all facilities and grounds projects that meet the Town's definition of a
capital project and are not funded in the operating budget. Presents and answers all related
questions for said plan and updates the Town Manager on the status of approved capital
projects as they progress.

facilities management and sustainability for all properties. Work with the Town Manager

3. Discussion on Draft Commemorative Policy
TOWN OF LUNENBURG
COMMEMORATIVE POLICY
I. General:
The Commemorative Policy provides a set of procedures to guide the location of commemorative items
and to assist with their management.
Commemorative items include requested signage within the town right of way by private parties,
plantings, plaques, benches or other types of donations to commemorate individuals, groups, or special
events.
This policy applies to any property under the care, custody, and control of the Select Board.
Il. Objectives:
The reasons for having a formalized procedure are to:
1. Provide a clear and consistent method for reviewing requests to place signs,
plantings, plaques, benches or other commemorative items on Town property;
2. Ensure that decisions involving such requests are well-considered and address the concerns
and needs of the Town relative to maintenance costs, durability of materials, aesthetics,
appropriateness of bench or tree location and type, and merit of the request; and
3. Allow for the placement of high-quality benches and plantings at an economical cost
to the donor and in the most efficient and cost-effective manner for the Town.
===PAGE 12===
Ill. Standards:
When considering a request for the placement of a commemorative item on Town-owned land, the
Select Board shall apply the following standards:
1. Acceptance and placement of a memorial bench, plaque, or planting on Town-owned
property shall be done sparingly, for good cause shown. At minimum, persons being
memorialized shall have, at some point in their lives, held residency or participated in the
community for over ten years.
2. Except as otherwise allowed by the Town Manager, all memorial benches shall
be deemed of acceptable material that fits the character of the landscape that it will
be placed.
3. All proposed memorial trees shall require review by the Tree Warden to ensure the
appropriateness of the species and its proposed location, as well as the sufficiency of the
selected tree's size and condition. The tree shall be planted by an experienced landscaper
under the supervision of the Tree Warden. The final location of the tree requires approval of
the Select Board
4. Donors will be required to pay for all costs associated with the donated item,
including materials and labor costs if installation requires hiring a contractor for
installation.
IV. Installation:
Donated commemorative items will become the property of the Town once the item is
accepted and installed. The Town will install the donation with town staff unless it requires
an outside contractor or equipment to perform the work.
V. Expected Life Cycle of Commemorative Item:
Donated commemorative items will be accepted by the Town with an expected life cycle.
• Benches and plaques will be considered at the end of their life cycle when the item
needs to be replaced. The donor on file will be contacted to notify them the item will
be removed due to the condition.
• Trees or other plantings that were commemorative in nature will be considered at the
end of their life cycle if the tree or planting has outgrown the original location due to
size and interfering with public safety or _years, whichever occurs first. The donor
on file will be contacted to notify them the tree/planting will be removed.
• Signage within the Right of Way will be assessed annually and the Select Board may
vote to remove commemorative signs upon recommendation by the DPW Director.
The donor on file will be contacted to notify them the signage will be removed.
• Other criteria for removal include if the item is vandalized in a manner that makes it
visually unacceptable, if the donated item needs to be removed to accommodate
changing landscapes, or infrastructure. The Town does not guarantee against the
effects of weather, vandalism, or theft.
VI. Notification:
It shall be the responsibility of the donor to provide the Select Board's Office with a current
address, phone number, and email address for purposes of notification regarding installation
or removal of their donation.
===PAGE 13===
VII. Procedures:
The procedure for requesting the placement of a memorial bench or tree on public property
shall be as follows:
1. Requests for permission to install new or replacement memorial
benches or plant memorial trees (or to place a memorial plaque on an
existing bench) on Town-owned real property shall be directed to the
Select Board's Office.
2. Requestors shall be provided with a copy of this policy.
3. Before submitting a formal request for commemorative item such as a
bench, plaque, or planting, the requestor is encouraged to discuss the
matter with the Director of the Department of Public Works.
4. All requests shall be made by completing a Commemorative
Request Form and submitting it the Select Board's Office.
Submitted request forms shall be forwarded by the Select Board's
Office to the Director of Public Works that is responsible for
maintaining the Town-owned land in question.
5. All comments and/or recommendations from the Department of
Public Works should be returned to the Select Board's Office
within 21 days of receipt.
6. The Select Board will then place on the next available meeting
agenda to take action on the request, including acceptance of the
gift per MGL ch. 44 § 53A ½ and subject to its recommended
conditions, ifany.
7. Once the request has been approved by the Select Board, it shall be
effectuated as follows:
• Donor shall submit payment to the Select Board's Office.
• The Town will coordinate with the donor on installation of
commemorative item, when conditions and circumstances permit.

2. Draft Tailings Policy
Ms. Lemieux reported this addresses uncashed checks issued by the Town. This has come up in
our audit management letter for the past two years. The auditors would like to see a written
policy.
I. Purpose:
To minimize the liability posed by uncashed checks and the associated negative impact on cash
position certainty, this policy sets guidelines for the timely resolution of tailings.
Il. Applicability:
This policy applies to the Treasurer/Collector, Finance Director/Town Accountant, or their
designees.
Ill. Policy:
The town's policy is to identify and research all uncashed checks (tailings) as a part of the timely
reconciliation of all bank accounts. All checks that are not cashed within 90 days of the date of
issuance will be investigated. Checks that are not cashed after 180 days will be reported to the
Finance Director/Town Accountant as uncashed so the General Ledger can be adjusted to
increase cash and to record a corresponding liability.
A listing of all uncashed checks shall be published once a year consistent with Massachusetts
General Law. Checks not claimed must be reported by the Treasurer to the Finance
Director/Town Accountant so that revenue can be increased and the liability can be eliminated.
IV. Procedures:
• All town bank accounts shall be reconciled each month; uncashed checks (tailings)
===PAGE 4===
vendor's check disbursement report.
• The Treasurer/Collector's Office shall send a notice of the uncashed check and the
amount to the recipient at their last known address and retain evidence of thismailing.
• If the check has not been cashed 180 days after the date of issuance, the
Treasurer/Collector, consistent with Massachusetts General Law, shall submit a List
of Uncashed Checks to the Finance Director/Town Accountant so that cash can be
adjusted and the liability of the tailings/abandoned property can be recorded on the
General Ledger.
• The Treasurer/Collector shall publish the List of Uncashed Checks consistent with
state law.
• The Assistant Treasurer shall prepare a List of Abandoned Property/Uncashed Checks
for all checks that are not claimed after the public advertisement and submit it to the
Treasurer/Collector for approval.
• After signing the document, the Treasurer shall submit the List of Abandoned
Property/Uncashed Checks to the Finance Director/Town Accountant. The Finance
Director/Town Accountant will increase the revenue of the Town on the General
Ledger and eliminate the liability.
V. References:
This policy is allowed through the town's acceptance of M.G.L. c. 200A § 9A, which occurred
at the Special Town Meeting on November 20, 2010.
The board will review the policy and vote on it at a later date.
