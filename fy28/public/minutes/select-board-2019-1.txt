Chair Brandon Kibbe reported they had called their meeting to order. Mr. Toale asked him

David Prokowiew, Planning Board; 1 am also a Member At Large; Bob Pease,

come before you tonight regarding the Right of First Refusal for 994 Northtield Road
which is removing part of the property from Chapter 61 status. Mr. Kibbe read a letter to
the Board aloud:
Dear Selectboard members,
On December 20, 2018, the Open Space ad hoc Committee met to to review the Chapter
61B notice of intent to convert to another use dated November 28, 2018, for 994 Northfield
===PAGE 4===
Unlike most Chapter 61 conversion notices that the Town receives, this notice was not
triggered by a sale and accompanied by a non-contingent bona fide offer to purchase.
Instead, this notice was triggered by the owner's intent to convert to non-qualified uses.
Our committee raises this question, isn't filing a permit application to convert the use of the
property an indication of intent? Therefore, was the timing of notice given improper?
As previously noted by your Board, the process and timeline as provided by statute is quite
different from this type of Chapter 61 notice. Namely, sale price is to determined by
appraisal and that appraisal is due only 30 days after receiving notice. Not only is this
process much more condensed than its counterpart that involves a bona fide offer, the
statute is quite ambiguous as to what action establishes "intent to convert" on the part of
the landowner and when the Town's right of first refusal is triggered in that proces.
Furthermore, because this type of withdrawal notice is quite rare, the courts have little
record to help clarify the ambiguity as since confirmed by Town Counsel.
Chapter 61 provides a very important open space protection role in town. First and
foremost, Chapter 61 provides property tax shelter for private landowners to maintain their
property as undeveloped for forest, farm and recreational open space. For its part, the
Town receives the Right of First Refusal in exchange for a significant reduction in its
property tax revenues. In other words, the Town indirectly pays a substantial amount of
money for these rights. The townspeople should demand that we are ardent stewards of this
resource.
There may only be a limited amount of these opportunities that the Town may be interested
in actually in pursuing and it would be a shame to lose one of those opportunities on
account of a procedural problem. We respectfully request your consideration of the
following two action items.
1) Allow the Chapter 61B Notice of Intent to Convert to other use at 994 Northfield
Road, dated November 28, 2018 to lapse without taking action. Since the statutory
timeline is expiring, this will have no impact on the landowner in terms of their
ability to proceed. This will however preserve precedence for any future notice of
an intent to convert not accompanied by a non contingent bona fide offer to
purchase.
2) Pursue establishment of guidelines or a policy to advise other Chapter 61
landowners that the Town should be consulted prior to seeking permits for any non-
qualified use to determine if notice is required. Likewise this procedure should
direct land use boards to consider a property's potential enrollment in Chapter 61
programs as part of their regular course of business to assure that no notice is
required. At a minimum this procedural change should be reviewed by your board,
the Board of Assessors and the Planning Board. As part of developing these
guidelines and procedures we would also recommend formally seeking guidance
from the Dept. of Revenue on the question on timing for landowners to file these
types of notices with the Town.
Thank you for your attention to these matters. Respectfully submitted, the ad hoe Open
Space Committee

The Architectural Preservation District Commission is charged with preserving historical structures in the
Architectural Preservation District established by bylaw in Lunenburg. To that end, it is my duty to inform you that the
town is in violation of the APD bylaw with respect to the Old Primary School. The Brooks House is also of
Section 2 of the APD bylaw explains the concept of Demolition by Neglect. This describes a situation where a
contributing property is in the gradual process of demolition without permission from the APDC through lack of
maintenance. Section 10 of the bylaw makes it explicit that demolition by neglect is illegal. Page 18 of the APD
Guidelines explains more about Demolition by Neglect and how the AP DC should handle the situation when it arises.
We believe that the Old Primary School is in a state of Demolition by Neglect because failure to perform basic
maintenance hasled to further damage which has compromised the integrity of the building as awhole. Ifleft
unchecked, this damage may be too costly to repair, and lead to the total demolition of the structure.
Viz:
• Roof leaks have allowed water into the walls, causing them to disintegrate.
• Vegetation has been allowed to set root around the base of the building, damaging the foundation.
• Plumbing has not been properly winterized, causing flooding and damage to HVAC systems.
The APDC requires the Town of Lunenburg to take action on these items, as well as amy other maintenance items that
might be required to prevent further deterioration.
It is furthermore our opinion that the disuse of historic buildings is and will remain a disincentive to their maintenance.
We feel it is consistent with our mission to recommend that the Board of Selectmen return them touse. The Building
Reuse Committee put considerable effort into finding away that the Town coulduse the Old Primary School, but in
the enddeclared it"surplus" along with the Brooks House. Since that time, the Town Manager and community
leaders have sought to create anoverall redevelopment plan for the downtown that wouldretain possession and
control ofmunicipal properties. While their intentions are laudable, they have failed to produce any acceptable plan
for how these buildings will be used and thus preserved in the future. Meanwhile the Old Primary School has continued
to deteriorate, and the Brooks House may soon suffer the same fate. The APDC therefore recommends that the Board
of Selectmen seek to lease or sell these two properties as soon as possible.

Richard McGrath, APDC Chair

(c) If the hearing authority deems a dog a dangerous dog, the hearing authority shall order 1 or more of the following:
(i that the dog be humanely restrained; provided, however, that no order shall provide that a dog deemed dangerous be
chained, tethered or otherwise tied to an inanimate object including, but not limited to, a tree, post or building;
(ii) that the dog be confined to the premises of the keeper of the dog; provided, however, that "confined" shall mean
securely confined indoors or confined outdoors in a securely enclosed and locked pen or dog run area upon the
premises of the owner or keeper; provided further, that such pen or dog run shall have a secure roof and, if such
enclosure has no floor secured to the sides thereof, the sides shall be embedded into the ground for not less than 2
feet; and provided further, that within the confines of such pen or dog run, a dog house or proper shelter from the
elements shall be provided to protect the dog;
(iii) that when removed from the premises of the owner or the premises of the person keeping the dog, the dog shall be securely
and humanely muzzled and restrained with a chain or other tethering device having a minimum tensile strength of 300 pounds
and not exceeding 3 feet in length;
(iv) that the owner or keeper of the dog provide proof of insurance in an amount not less than $100,000 insuring the owner or
keeper against any claim, loss, damage or injury to persons, domestic animals or property resulting from the acts, whether
intentional or unintentional, of the dog or proof that reasonable efforts were made to obtain such insurance if a policy has not
been issued; provided, however, that if a policy of insurance has been issued, the owner or keeper shall produce such policy
upon request of the hearing authority or a justice of the district court; and provided further, that if a policy has not been issued
the owner or keeper shall produce proof of efforts to obtain such insurance;
(v) that the owner or keeper of the dog provide to the licensing authority or animal control officer or other entity identified in the
order, information by which a dog may be identified, throughout its lifetime including, but not limited to, photographs, videos,
veterinary examination, tattooing or microchip implantations or a combination of any such methods of identification;
(vi) that unless an owner or keeper of the dog provides evidence that a veterinarian is of the opinion the dog is unfit for
alterations because of a medical condition, the owner or keeper of the dog shall cause the dog to be altered so that the dog shall
not be reproductively intact;
These recommendations are based on the incident as outlined in Sgt. Tocci's police report, as well as an incident involving the
same dog's on February 22, 2015, as well as Mis. Thibodeau's lack of cooperation and continued disinterest in complying with
town regulations regarding the vaccinations, licensing, and control of her animals.
