October 5, 2021
Bilotta Meeting Room, Town Hall and via Zoom
Members present: Tom Alonzo, Chair; Michael-Ray Jeffreys, Vice Chairman; Lou Franco,
Clerk; Todd Dwyer, Jim Marino
Also Present: Heather Lemieux, Town Manager; Karen Brochu, Finance Director; Dave Blatt,
Dave Passios, TCP Building Design Committee members; Peter Beardmore, Finance Committee
Chairman Alonzo called the meeting to order at 7:00 p.m. and asked everyone to stand for the
Pledge of Allegiance. He announced in accordance with the requirements of the Open Meeting
Law, please be advised that this meeting is being recorded and broadcast over the Lunenburg
public access channel and on Facebook Live on the Public Access Facebook page.
He noted Zoom is available for public comment only and gave the sign-in instructions.
The agenda lists all the topics which may be discussed at this meeting and are those reasonably
anticipated by the Chair. Votes may be taken as a result of these discussions. Not all items listed
may in fact be discussed and other items not listed may also be brought up for discussion to the
extent permitted by Open Meeting Law.
PUBLIC COMMENT:
Mi. Jeffreys encouraged all to reach out to veterans, in anticipation of Veterans Day on
November 11, and remind them that they are valued members of the community and that they
are cared about.
APPOINTMENTS:
Mr. Alonzo introduced the Lunenburg Little League District 3 Champions team in order to
present a certificate of appreciation to them from the Select Board. He read it aloud:
"In recognition of earning a Top 12 ranking in the state of Massachusetts and advancing
further in the tournament than any other Lunenburg team in their 69 year history! Playing
eight games, scoring 70 runs on 97 hits, with 14 doubles, 2 triples and 8 home runs, with a
418 batting average"
Present were Nate Ayles, Cayden Chow, Ethan Dionne, Mackinley Dumais, Jordan Sammet,
Conor Speck, Ryan Whitney, Gabriel Wright, Michael Wright-Manager, James McWalter-
Coach and Stephen Chow-Coach/League President.
Not present, but also recognized were Lugh Dumais, Simon Garcia, Jacob Melvin, and Cameron
McWalter. Applause filled the meeting room. Mr. Alonzo thanked the players and their parents,
coaches, and everyone who attended games and recognized their athletic achievement and all
their hard work put into playing. He congratulated them on behalf of the Board and the town and
their great run.
Coach Chow presented signs memorializing previous District 3 Champion teams from 1967,
1974 and 1995 as well as this year's team to be placed in Marshall Park. The team players
presented baseball caps to each of the Select Board and Ms. Lemieux. The Board presented the
certificate and took a photo with the entire team wearing their new baseball caps.

FY22 Budget Updates:
New Growth was certified at $430,254. We currently estimate a surplus of approximately $39,000. Due to
the impending retirement of the Principal Accounting Clerk in the Town Accountant office and after
discussions with the Finance Director that the position should be an Assistant Accountant to provide for
succession planning and someone that can step in for the Town Accountant if she were out for an
extended time, one of the FT22 budget amendments will be to ask for a transfer of funds to support this
position with additional hours. We are currently advertisingforan Assistant Accountantat24hoursaweek
and iftown meetingapproves fundingat the SpecialTown Meeting, thisposition would increaseto 32 hoursa
week.
The IT budget will be another budget that we will be looking for a 'FY22 budget amendment in order to
provide for sufficient funds for the identified network equipment and security needs. This transfer will
also address a backup plan in the event we do not get the Community Compact IT Grant.
The DPW Director informed me that the consortium bids for Road salt were received and increased
approximately 38%. This will impactour FY22 Snow and Icebudget, whichweare allowed to deficit
spend. The good news is that we didn't use much salt last year and have a good amount in reserves.

§ 250-4.9 Water Supply Protection District.
[Amended 5-5-2018 ATM by Art. 32]
A. Purpose. The purpose of the Water Supply Protection District is to promote the health,
safety and general welfare of the community; to protect, preserve and maintain present
and potential sources of water supply and watershed areas for the public health and
safety; to protect, preserve and maintain the existing and potential groundwater supply
and groundwater recharge areas within the Town for public health and safety; to
preserve and protect the lakes; ponds, streams, brooks, rills, marshes, swamps, bogs
and other water bodies and watercourses in Town; to protect the community from the
detrimental use and development of land and water within the district; to conserve the
natural resources of the Town; to preserve and maintain the groundwater table and
water recharge areas within the Town; and to prevent blight and the pollution of the
environment.
B. District delineation.
(1) The boundaries of the Water Supply Protection District shall be as follows: Zone I,
Zone II, and Zone III for the Town of Lunenburg, approved by the Department of
Environmental Protection (DEP) according to Map 1, entitled "Water Protection
District, revised October, 2009 LUNENBURG WATER DISTRICT AQUIFER
PROTECTION ZONES, dated July 2021."
(2) Where the bounds as delineated are in doubt or in dispute, the burden of proof that the
use is outside the Water Supply Protection District or does not impact the district shall
be upon the owners) of the land in question. However, the Building Commissioner
retains its authority to determine final location with regard to said Water Supply
Protection District.
(3) At the written request of the owners) of the property in question, the Town may
engage a professional geologist, a certified soil scientist or a registered professional
engineer trained in hydrology to determine the location and extent of a protection area
on the parcel in question and charge the owners) of said parcel for the cost of the
investigation.

Meet with PACC Chairman re: Letter to legislators re: Pending Bills on Streaming Video
over Public Rights of Way
Steve Walker, PAC Chairman, 227 Pleasant Street, stated there is a bill to establish a state-
wide policy concerning compensation for the use of digital infrastructure in the public right of
way. There are two versions sitting in committee: S 2200 (Senate) and H 130 (House). He
noted according to the Cable Act of 1984, a municipality can negotiate a franchise fee from
the cable company for using a public right of way; which is what we have been doing with
Comcast. The contract is negotiated every 10 years. We have three channels of broadcasting;
public, education and government. We received a capital infusion of $170,000 which we are
keeping in reserve for our future studio space. Video services are moving to the internet and
being streamed there. As people are doing that, the amount we receive should reduce. In
addition, people are doing cable cutting which is not using the cable service and instead
watching television via the internet. The bill would impose a statewide franchise fee on the
use of public rights of way for streaming services. Fees would be collected by the state rather
than the municipality and placed in a streaming entertainment fund and then be dispersed.
Twenty percent would go the Commonwealth, forty percent to municipalities based on
population and forty percent would go to the community media center such as PAC.
The impact of cable cutting will reduce fees to run public access cable, which is what we are
using right now. We presently receive about $200,000 per year in franchise fees and we have
seen a slight reduction in income at this point. This bill would help reduce the loss of that
revenue. The action being requested by PAC is for the Select Board to send a letter to the two
bill committees from the Select Board based on a MassAccess model letter on behalf of the
town. The House bill (H 130) is being sponsored by Representative Kushmerek. Mr. Walker

Tax Relief legislation for seniors, any possible legislation that would impact formula funding
changes related to Chapter 70 and Chapter 90, the State supplemental budget bill, the State's plan
for ARPA funding and the status of the Bond Bills that have funding earmarked for Lunenburg.

• The legislators confirmed that any bills on Host Community Agreements will not be acted
on this fiscal year, nor is there any impetus to move it forward.
• Senator Cronin was not aware of any bills that would adopt Tax Relief for seniors and did
not believe the Senate had any appetite to act on such bills. The House has two bills that
Representative Sena co-sponsored but they are not moving forward.
• The legislators did not foresee any changesto the funding formulas for Chapter 70 and
Chapter 90 this fiscal year but do expect a Chapter 90 funding formula bill in the future.

session.

The first presentation was from Wright Ostermier Landscape Architects (WOLA). Kathryn
Ostermier, Project Manager, was present via ZOOM, and Emily Wright and Nicholas Betts were
attending in person. They presented an overview of the firm, noting they use outside consultants,
such as Meridian Civil Engineering, when appropriate.
Ms. Wright stated they are optimists, spend a lot of time getting to know a site and understanding
its history and how people use it. They prioritize listening to their clients. Although the firm is
only three years old, they have over twenty years of combined experience. Ms. Ostermier stated
their approach is to hold a team kickoff and site analysis, then engage the community and the
town to seek input; work on conceptual designs and present them. After getting additional
feedback, they create the final design.
Mr. Alonzo asked based upon the RFP, what do you understand about the project; did you do a
site visit and if so, what is your impression of what the town is looking for?
Ms. Wright stated that she and Mr. Betts had stopped there earlier and there are some
complexities such as shared driveways/parking, and topography challenges, but what you are
proposing is really interesting and this would be a great opportunity to bring people to what is an
under-utilized site. Ms. Ostermier added you are looking to address a parking deficit in the area
and seeking a passive recreational space, which includes a farmers market. In the vocational
school rendering there were overhead structures as well as a playground and a place for
community to gather; not so much a formal park but a community gathering space that allows
flexible uses throughout the year. She thought it was important to incorporate parts of the
primary school whether it is materials such as the brick or footprints. It could be a really special
space.

1. Letters of Support re: House Bill 130 and Senate Bill 2200 on Streaming Video
over Public Rights of Way
This was discussed at the last meeting by PAC Chairman Steve Walker. The impact of this
bill would impose a statewide franchise fee on the use of public rights of way for streaming
services. Fees would be collected by the state rather than the municipality and placed in a
streaming entertainment fund and then be dispersed. Twenty percent would go the
Commonwealth, forty percent to municipalities based on population and forty percent would
go to community media centers such as PAC.
Mr. Jeffreys was not in favor of supporting this bill adding the impact of this will be a 5%
charge on everyone's cable bills. It was his belief that Comcast would not lose any money. He
did not believe Lunenburg would receive anything substantial back because of its smaller
population versus other larger towns and cities. Also, some cities have a public utility option
and or other providers of video broadcasting services. Lunenburg has one option. He believed
this is a creative way for the state to get more money.
===PAGE 9===
support for both legislative bills. Mr. Dwyer agreed with Mr. Alonzo, adding he could foresee
cable being phased out and streaming becoming the norm in the next 10 or 20 years. This is a
reasonable way to maintain the mechanism for funding some of the public access that is
valued in town.
Motion: T. Dwyer
2n: L. Franco
To sign the letters of support for House bill 130 and Senate bill 2200 in support of PAC
Vote: 4 in Favor, 1 Against (Jeffreys)
