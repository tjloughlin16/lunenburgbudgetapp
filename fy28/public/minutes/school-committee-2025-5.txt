8. Superintendents Report  Dr. Fortuna explains the theme of Ohana , a Hawaiian  word meaning Family.  
Schools are more than a place of learning they are a community where everyone belongs, unity, 
inclusivity, responsibility and love.  My commitment is to ensure that every student and family in 
Lunenburg feels, seen, supported, and valued.   I have sent out a survey to help with my entry plan .  
E arly survey feedback highlights strong pride in our teachers and in the sense of community that defines
our schools.  To date as of 6 pm we have received 184 responses, if  you  have not completed the survey, 
please do, I am really interested in hearing your voice.  Senior sunrise was well attended and is a 
tradition that marks the beginning of their senior year.   An example of the resilience  Lunenburg School 
District has,  was experienced on day 1 , when an unexpected fire occurred at Lunenburg Middle School, 
staff responded calmly and effectively ensuring that every student was safe.  We welcome Jennifer 
Traverso  as our new K -8 Literacy Coach Specialist, who is already partnering with teachers as they 
implement HMH and a new reading version.  Our team submitted entitlement and competitive grant 
application, we have launched a new website for staff and families to keep everyone informed more 
easily, thank you Dr. Bookis for your work on this.  Professional development this year will be building 
and department based, making it more relevant to day to day practice.  Our schools continue to grow and
evolve to meet student needs.  I would like to welcome Am y Monahan - HS Art, Anabel S auer - Primary
School Kindergarten, Beth Robinson  - Primary School MTSS Support K- 2, Jen Traverso  - district 
Literacy Coach, Amanda Lucy  -TLC Program, and Kayla Dwyer  –  Primary School Kindergarten.  We 
have 1,593 students enrolled across the schools including the ACE program.  Our new Business 
Administrator, Chris McNamara, has begun his work with energy and focus as you have seen tonight, 
through reviewing expenditures dating back to FY22, he is developing a multi-year projection through 
FY29.  Our maintenance and facilities teams worked tirelessly through the summer to get our school 
ready.  Turkey Hill received fire alarm upgrades, the security upgrades are nearly done, additional 
parking, office improvements at Central office moved to Turkey Hill.  New doors and hardware and  we 
replaced a freezer.  The Primary School finished HVAC upgrades, installation of new cameras, a grease 
trap, a dishwasher and a table at the primary school.  We created a kitchenette for the ACE program.  
Juliana Hanscom represented Lunenburg and federal level advocating in Washington DC ensuring our 
districts voice was included in national discussions about special education funding and staffing.  We 
have received DESE approval for our 240 262 federal grant as well as some Title 3 funding .

26.  My understanding is this is a FY 25 surplus, which is the override year, which the cuts that were 
made were ESSER cuts.  So we didn ’t cut beyond ESSER, which is a really important thing to 
remember, because if we made only ESSER cuts, and then we dug in with $500,000 into that budget, 
what does that mean?  Does that mean we didn ’t offer the services or did we actually make cuts that we 
as a town did not approve?  That is important, what I have learned after the override passed, is that the 
school is budgeted money and then the school can do what they want with the money.  When schools 
make decisions after the budget, we as citizens don ’t actually know how the funds were spent.  We had a
list of “ saves ”  and at the end of the day we don ’t know if those positions were actually saved.  The cuts 
that are listed don ’t actually have to be cut, other cuts could be made, other transfers could occur.  The 
budget is one thing, but now we are talking about the actuals that we do not have access to and we are 
not really informed of it after the fact.  I was criticized personally going into FY 26 –  did the schools 
actually spend all of the override money and it was never an outright easy answer.  The other question 
that I personally asked and probably Mandy too was, did all the teachers and paras actually get saved

put a lot on the line, two years in a row for overrides.   I went to bat for the school committee in front of

experts, and now I find out that we couldn ’t.  What could you even show us that makes us trust you 
now?  I think we need answers, because what was cut in FY25, I know what was cut in FY 26 , we need

Regular session 9/17/25 &amp; 10/1/25   Ms Brzozoski makes a motion to accept the minutes, Ms. Gilman seconds the motions, all approve

Line Item Transfers We want to transfer $13,500 from admin tech contracts to school committee dues, to cover superintendent search invoice. Transfer $2000 from school science supply line item at Primary School to the reading supply line item at the Primary school to cover cost.  Ms. Gilman makes a motion to approve transfers, Ms. Young seconds the motion all approve

Donations – Laura Barooshian is donating $100 for the high school field trips.  Music Gators has donated $500 to High School Music Program, $500 for the Middle High School Program and $500 for the Turkey Hill Music Program.   Ms. Brzozoski makes a motion to accept all donations with our gratitude, Ms. Gilman seconds the motion all approve

Superintendents Report Dr. Fortuna shares district highlights and celebrations, teaching and learning, professional development update.  Dr. Fortuna also announces the hiring of Director of Curriculum and Instruction position.  Spotlights are shared and recognizing a few teachers and staff members in the district. Finance and spending updates. Dr. Fortuna did a staff survey to engage feedback, communication and visibility, results are shared strengths and weaknesses that have been determined.

New Business
Action
James J. Larkin Scholarship Approval Committee has been supplied with the Larkin Scholarship Proposal and an outline of the process of how to go about creating a scholarship.  This will be classified as a gift and is the amount of $500 this Spring and then $250 every year after. Ms. Gilman makes a motion to accept this scholarship, Ms. Brzozoski seconds the motion all approve.
First Read Policies 1303.1 Advertising in the Schools, 3303.01 Cash Handling, 5201.03 Middle School Pathways, 5204.01 Competency Determination The committee takes turns reading through each policy.

Discussion
Trust Commission Mr. Sculimbrene met with the Trust Commissioner of Lunenburg this past Sunday.  They discussed what happens if someone makes the town a beneficiary as opposed to making a donation.  However, some of the trusts are very old, and the “purpose” of the trust is in a manner that is not feasible in modern needs.  It has been asked to gather all the trusts for the town, we could take this information to the attorney general and they can administratively modify them, which will then make the money usable for modern day needs.

Old Business
Action
Superintendent Draft Goals Mr. Sculimbrene makes a motion to approve, Ms. Gilman seconds the motion all approve
School Committee Goals Committee lists their goals as: the future of Turkey Hill, Primary School playground and parking lot, and budget process.  

Public Comment John Bowen, 162 Highland Street, Chairman of the Trust Fund Commission, Mr. Bowen takes this time to explain the responsibilities of the Trust Fund Commission and the basis of the inquiry.  Every declaration that you have for every trust needs to be in the treasurer’s office and they are currently not.  Part of our inquiry is to look at all trusts in town that are gifts, we want to see every gift that the school has and the declarations that are attached to that gift.  We have a duty to make sure the declarations are being met and the scholarships are being granted accordingly.

Subcommittee Reports Ms. Young reports the PACSAL committee met on October 2nd, this was the support version of the group and have another meeting on November 6th at 6 pm at the High School and also on Zoom.  The goal of the group at this time is to grow the group, to get more parents involved.  They were part of the Trunk or Treat event and it was a great success and were able to connect with new parents.   The Master Planning Steering Committee has been meeting and had a successful public information session and presentation on October 22nd at Smokestack Roasters in Lunenburg, there was good conversation and feedback received.  The next meeting will be November 12th at 6 pm, the place is to be determined.  
Ms. Gilman reports the PTO is working on finalizing details for a few events, they are having a movie event in December, which is very popular.  We have finalized our date for the Special Someone Dance which is February 7th.  We also have a night at Great Wolf Lodge, it is a day in March the rate is $125 per night and you get tickets to the water park as well.
Ms. Brzozoski reports the Middle School Advisory committee has met twice and talked about MCAS scores, very impressive the 8th grade civic scores were 20 points higher than the state numbers.  They have had their parent/teacher conferences.  The 8th grade trip is on, thank you to Jen Rios who stepped up, Amy Resnick has been doing it for years and is getting Jen up to speed on how to do it.  The cost is going to be about $1400.  LEAF has met, and they have gotten their nonprofit status so they are getting ready for a grant cycle.  The Municipal Building Design Committee has been meeting often, we have been talking about needs vs wants and which direction to go.  We also got a tour of the Brooks House, it is big and vacant, but does need a lot of work.
Mr. Sculimbrene reports he and Laura attended a policy subcommittee, we produced the cashless policy and the latest version of the advertising policy.  The High School Advisory Council met on October 30th, Mr. Santry presented the MCAS scores and school accountability numbers.  The slump in the scores for the 10th grade year has been a hot topic and there has been some research done to try determine the reason for the change.  We also discussed a little about the budget process and also changes to the PD day regarding ALICE training.

 Topics for Future Discussion
Future of Turkey Hill, Budget Process, Turf Field, Status of all non personnel facility items and projected/expected costs
