1. Meeting called to order at 7:00 pm. The meeting is televised and available through local cable and rebroadcast through
lunenburgaccess at youtube.
2. Police Department Budget Presentation
Chief Marino presented the recommended 2015 department budget, and discussed continued concerns due to staffing levels.
He also presented and discussed data regarding calls and assignments which he felt supported his sense that having 3
officers on duty per shift is necessary moving forward. While he recognizes money continues to be tight, he feels that the
department will begin to be perceived as ineffective because the work load is exceeding the staffing available to address it.
Additionally, he discussed community improvements which he felt would improve safety, demonstrate pride in ownership
and deter vandalism while providing improved access to community property such as the Marshall Park parking lot project
in the capital plan recommendation.
3.
Fire Department Budget Presentation
Chief Sullivan reviewed the department budget highlighting the need to have the safety building staffed 24/7 to improve
emergency response times for those shifts covered by call firefighters alone. He spoke about increasing rates charged for
ambulance calls to better recover associated costs.
4.
Capital Planning Committee
S. Raboin presented the Capital Planning Committee recommendations regarding the 23 capital requests and the thought
behind the prioritizations as presented to the Town Manager. He also discussed 'high ticket' items as opposed to 'very high
ticket' items which overwhelm the capital requests, as a $550,000 fire truck request did this year. Through discussions with
the Town Manager, the idea of financing the fire truck over 5 years was raised as an avenue to free up funds to address the
rest of the requests. This could potentially become a way to address 'very high ticket' items in the future.
5.
The Town Manager presented information on the debt service and retiring regular debt over the next several years. She also
discussed what would happen if the fire truck was financed, and as a future guide, using the combined current debt service
and capital budgets as a target for both debt and direct capital spending. As current debt is retired, the amount of excess
capacity could be used to either fund borrowing for the 'very high ticket' items needed in the future, or used to fund current
capital items or the pavement plan. This would create a means to fund capital needs without increasing the tax burden.
6. Budget Discussion
The Town Manager reported that, based on her recommended budget, there remains about $116,000 unallocated which we
will discuss against several items that were above target requests. She also discussed free cash, and plans to deposit
$74,729 to the stabilization fund to bring us to 5% of budget, and use the rest to offset the snow and ice deficit.
7. Meeting minutes
TB motioned to accept the minutes of 3/6 as written; KM seconded, unanimously approved.
8. Next meetings
Next Thursday we will hear from the School District and Monty Tech.
KM motioned to adjourn. SR seconded, meeting adjourned at 9:55 pm.
Minutes respectfully submitted by Terri Burchfield.

• There are 16 people (excluding reserve officers) in the Lunenburg
PD—1.6/1.000 population--below the recommended 2.5/1,000.
• About 15 experienced reserve officers augment PD staff, and are on
call and paid per diem from the Police Department overtime budget.
• In the five years from 2009 through 2013 calls for service, incidents,
and arrests increased significantly, taking more personnel time and
effort. Incidents have become more unpredictable and potentially
dangerous, partly from the increased chance that suspects have taken
designer drugs, formulated to induce specific effects.
• A goal is to have two officers on the road-at times not possible, like
when an extra officer is at the lock-for arrests or to monitor suspects.
• Added personnel time required to respond to "walk-ins" to the station
for help. In one 30 day period, there were 400 walk-in visits.
ii. Unfunded mandates for training and/or specific duty requirements (e. g.
School Resource Officer) take up time. The same is true for the Fire
Department (FD). An example is that the PD and FD must train responders in
the procedure to respond to potential Ebola cases.
ili. PD Capital:
• Cruisers run about 50,000 miles per year and need to be replaced
frequently. The chief keeps the old police cruisers as replacement
vehicles as they have low re-sale value.
• The PD is mandated to have body armor for policemen. This is
unfunded, though half the cost may be covered by a grant. There is
also an increased need for Tasers due to increasingly unpredictable
and dangerous behaviors confronted by Lunenburg police.
D. Fire Department, Chief Pat Sullivan
i. The FD has major capital requests in the next five years: Aging fire engine,
ladder truck, car, ambulance) and repairs over the next five years have an
estimated cost about $1.8 million.
ii. Another challenge is staffing. Currently:
• There are five career employees in the FD, three of whom are
firefighters. Two EMTs are on call and paid per diem.
• Call firefighters augment them, but it retention is a problem. The job
and requisite training take a lot of time: 3/5 of call firefighters quit
after a couple of years.
• Annual calls several years ago was several hundred. It's now
projected at 1,700 to 1,800 in FY2015--a 6% increase over FY2014.
ili. Chief Sullivan expects the number of calls to increase as the population grows,
especially the elderly population need two additional firefighters and perhaps
an additional EMT for coverage and to insure quick response time.
