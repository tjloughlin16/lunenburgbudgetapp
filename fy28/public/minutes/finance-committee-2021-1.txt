3. Parker Elmore, OPEB: 
Parker Elmore and Francis Fraine were present from Odyssey Advisors to 
address the GASB 75 report on Other Post Employment Benefits (non pension 
related) and answer any questions. 
Highlights:   
Total OPEB Liability as of 6/30/2020 is $56,141,225 (mostly health benefits 
earned by current and retired staff).  The OPEB Trust Fund as of that date had 
$375,269, leaving an unfunded liability of $55,765,956.  
Assumptions include the Municipal Bond Rates which drive the liability rate over 
time. Also includes termination and retirement rates, percent of employees who 
elect coverage through the town, and health care cost inflation.  
The Town currently has funded less than 1% of the liability, which is true of 33% 
of other Massachusetts municipalities.  
C. Menard asked questions related to the benchmark community comparisons. 
H. Lemieux reported that part of the Town’s actions to help the costs moving 
forward will be a move to Medicare for retirees currently covered on active 
insurance. This led to a 2% discount for active plan coverage. 
The service cost per eligible active employee is $6,330. 
Mr. Elmore also presented a 40 year forecast of the Total OPEB Liability. Costs 
appear stable over that time when considered using the present value of money 
against budgetary inflation.  
C. Menard asked if the Town was limited to investment options. We do have 
fiduciary responsibilities to invest responsibly. Long term we are currently 
assessed 6.72% return on investment.

was reviewed.  The basis used was $21 million dollars, upped to $27 million for

compared against debt policy ratios to see if such debt would comply. Spread over 
30 years, and going out over 40 years, it would be. Discussion ensued regarding 
longer vs. shorter term debt and why one is preferable over another. 
 
5. Department Budget Presentations

Majority of calls are Emergency Medical Response, with a large uptake starting 
with the jump in growth with the apartment complexes in town.  Plans are to 
transition to Paramedic level designation during FY22. Budget supports 1 new 
firefighter/paramedic in year 4 of the 5-year staffing plan. Paramedics are hard to 
recruit as they are high demand. Should have 24/7 Paramedic coverage during 
FY23. Above target requests for paramedic training and protective gear 
replacement are in the current budget numbers. Salary schedules are being 
considered as part of the issue around recruitment and retention. Desire is to

Police Department Chief Gammel 
Chief Gammel reviewed the Police Department organization chart and 
operations. The FY22 budget includes a new patrol officer position per the 10 
year staffing plan. There were over 20,000 calls for service over 2020. Motor 
vehicle stops were down due to Covid. The department is trying to assure 
supervision across shifts so differential is a new issue. The Chief reviewed 
departmental goals and what they mean over the next several years as the staffing 
plan continues to be implemented. Grant funded items were discussed, including 
body cameras which will be implemented soon. Questions regarding some of the 
forward-looking needs, such as increased admin support in 2023. Because there 
are many junior patrolmen, COLAs with steps will lead to larger annual salary 
line increases in the near term. Police reform requirements, especially around 
certification of reserve officers, will also have significant budget impact. The 
estimated cost to get the current reserve officers trained for certification over the 
next few years is around $90,000. C. Menard commented that these mandates 
really need to be funded at the state level and we should be following this up with 
our state legislators. D. Passios asked about whether there is enough information 
to rate the new hybrid vehicles. It’s too soon, but so far the Chief thinks they are 
good vehicles. He also commented on the need to track maintenance costs over 
the life of vehicles to have better data for planning.
