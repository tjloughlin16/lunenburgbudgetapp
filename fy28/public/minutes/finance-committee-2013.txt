The Treasurer/Collector, Eagle House Director, and Library Director were present for an informal discussion of
departmental operations and needs. This was scheduled to allow service level discussions and provide the newer
committee members the opportunity to hear from the department heads before the budget season begins and funding is
discussed. The department heads presented information regarding current operational status, challenges, initiatives,
and benchmarks against which they measured their department's activities.

No public comment.
• The Annual Town Report submissions are due February 25". We discussed whether FinCom would be
submitting anything. Mark will draft an outline and run it by the Committee.

• The current editions of the Municipal Financial Data booklet, Municipal Advocate, and the January Beacon
were circulated.

SG made a motion to accept the minutes of 12/13/2012. BL seconded, motion approved 7-0.

least once, some twice. One item under discussion is a police vehicle. There will be an opportunity to compare the
sedan vs. utility vehicle at the Safety Building on Wednesday morning.

on review of the building proposals.
6.
Town Manager Updates
Karen Brochu circulated copies of the Tax Rate Recap and Budget Report for review. The committee is asked to
forward any questions to Mark for inclusion as future agenda items.

since 2009. The development of 140 single family units will move forward.

Board meeting on 1/15, and health insurance on 1/22.

School Building Committee Update
L. Calmes will be presenting information regarding educational visioning as part of the High School study later this
evening. ME reviewed options that will be addressed and voted on regarding construction methods and building options.
Information will be presented March 27* with a vote on April 10".
5.
Department Budget Presentations
Chief of Police Jim Marino presented his budget recommendations and discussed areas he was seeking an increase. While
understanding that funds are not available now, he does expect to be back looking to increase the number of staff as there
has been an increase in calls and duties, but the department lags in number of officers available per thousand population as
compared to surrounding communities. Additionally, state mandates regarding training are driving changes to professional
development costs. He also wants to increase the Detective to Detective Sergeant to increase the number of shifts covered
by a supervisor.
Fire Chief Patrick Sullivan addressed his budget. The greatest need is getting the station staffed 24/7. The Chief's primary
goal in this is to improve on the time needed to get the first truck out responding to an alarm. While he is proud of the
response time the on call firetighters are averaging, the amount of time to get a fire from start to flashover has shortened
due to materials and chemicals in modern furnishings, and he feels improving on response time is a critical goal. Would
like to get the budget back to where it was several years ago.
Harald Scheid represented the Assessor's office. Budget has just one line that has more than a slight increase. The office is
required to inspect improved properties once every 9 years. The office seeks to fund a periodic inspection program that will
maintain this schedule. The Board of Assessors has supported having a regular line in the budget to allow about 4,200
properties to be cycled through the 9 years. The initial budget amount for contracted services is anticipated to be tapered as
the office brings inspections up to date. LM expressed his support for the line, as did ME.
6.
Public Comments
SR had questions regarding the regional dispatch and what savings were actually realized. JM reviewed the history of
regionalization, and state requirements regarding dispatch that would have doubled what was in there now. Dave Matthews
stated that the Town Manager retained the funds in the Fire and Police departments that had been in the budget which ME
remarked was part of the agreement to go forward with the dispatch. SR clarified that this then was a process to mitigate
anticipated costs with changes in requirements, and was told this was the case.
7.
Loxi Jo Calmes spoke on the history and timelines of the School Building Committee and process. April 10* will be the
date for a vote to be taken by the School Building Committee on what option will be presented to the MSBA. She also
reviewed the 3 days of Educational Visioning and Planning process with 40 students, staff, parents and administrators. A
report will follow summarizing the activities and conclusions from that process. Feasibility Study is expected to be
completed in June. ME encouraged committee members to visit the school website and forward any comments or
suggestions to him in advance of the 3/27 meeting.

2. Presentation of the 5 Year Financial Forecast
K. Speidel presented the Town Manager's forecast beginning with a review of assumptions made which lead to the forecast.
As the town did not see a drop off in local receipts, no bounce in receipts is anticipated. The outlook for state aid is also
absent any significant change. The revenue outlook anticipates a 2% annual increase, while expenditures are seen at 2.8%
annual increases, leaving deficits which will have to be addressed through the budget process, though more manageable
deficits than have been seen in the recent past. The town continues to look for opportunities to realize savings through
regionalizing services such as animal control and a regional lock up.
ME would have liked to have seen the assumptions showing debt service beginning in 2017 if the school project is
authorized to see what impact that might have. The interest rates are fixed for current debt. The Town Manager also
reviewed the improved bond rating for the Town, which will be helpful with obtaining favorable rates should the school
project go forward.
During public comment, Dave Matthews wanted to make the point that it is normal to see deficits projected, but that by the
time a budget is brought to the Town for approval, it will be balanced.
ME explained that we will have a balanced budget, and that the forecast is a tool to help guide budget decisions. He then
closed public comment and thanked Ms. Speidel for the work on the forecast.

3. Public Hearing on Special Town Meeting Warrant
ME opened the Public Hearing. The School Building Committee made a presentation explaining their recommendation as
presented in the 1 warrant article. Fiscal impacts discussed included the estimate that the cost to the town to do nothing
would be $25 million where the cost of the preferred option would be $37 million. The Board of Selectmen had already
voted to go with 30 year financing, which would mean debt service on the project would expire in 2048, and the cost of
tinancing was estimated at $32 million. Tax bills would reflect the cost of the debt in 2018, after permanent financing had
been obtained. The final actual cost of the project itself would not be determined until the RFP for construction had gone
out and been accepted. The School Building Committee will continue to take feedback and work on the design and cost
savings until the RFP is done. After questions from the public were addressed, ME closed the Public Hearing.
4. STM Warrant Articles
For the purpose of discussion, TB made a motion that the Finance Committee recommend approval of Article 1 as
presented in the Warrant. JH seconded. While the Finance Committee is unanimous in stating the need for a new high
school, questions remain regarding some design components and the concern that operational costs will not be neutral.
Vote to approve the motion to recommend approval was 5-1 in favor.
Article 2 was deemed to have no fiscal impact, so no action was taken.
