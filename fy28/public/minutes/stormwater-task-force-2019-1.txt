STORMWATER TASK FORCE (SWTF) ATTACHMENTS! 
MINUTES 
January 10, 2019 
 
The Stormwater Task Force (SWTF) met in the Ritter Building, first floor conference room. In attendance were 
Matt Allison, Phyllis Luck, and Jack Rabbitt. Adam Burney, Land Use Director, was also in attendance. The 
meeting opened at 6:30 pm.   
 
Minutes of the November 27, 2018 meeting were reviewed and approved. 
 
Task Force Chair, Phyllis Luck, began the discussion on the Minimum Control Measures (MCM’s) by 
addressing the proposals for year one of the Municipal Separate Storm Sewer System (MS4) Permit submitted 
by Design Consultants Inc. (DCI) and received by the SWTF yesterday.  Phyllis had spent the day reviewing the 
proposals, as well as the Cost Estimate sheets for the first 5 years of the MS4 Permit, which had been received 
by the SWTF on January 8. None of the others in attendance had had the opportunity to review the documents, 
due to their late arrival.  Therefore, all agreed that members needed additional time to actually review the 
documents prior to holding a meaningful discussion. Members agreed to review over the weekend and submit 
comments and questions to Adam Burney. There was some discussion on information in the proposals that was 
inconsistent; a possible cause mentioned was that these were simply typographical errors.  Further, a quick look 
at the Cost Estimate sheets seemed to put the 5 years costs beyond the Town’s budget capacity. The SWTF 
determined that it was timely to schedule a meeting with the Town Manager to discuss the proposals and Cost 
Estimate sheets. The meeting will define the scope of work required to fulfill the requirements of the MS4 
permit in more detail, specifically in the year one proposals, and review the Cost Estimate & Summary for the 
first Five Years of the permit. Phyllis Luck offered to reach out to the Town Manager to set up the meeting. She 
will advise possible times as soon as known.  Cost Estimate Sheets, as well as DCI proposals, are attached. 
 
The SWTF moved on to a review of the Open Space Committee’s draft update to the Town’s Open Space and 
Recreation Plan.  The document was praised by all in attendance and a vote was taken to write a letter of 
support, as requested by the Open Space Committee, to the Commonwealth of Massachusetts 
Executive Office of Energy & Environmental Affairs , Division of Conservation Services . A second letter, 
praising the efforts of the Open Space Committee and thanking them for their efforts, was also supported.  Both 
letters were signed by all SWTF members, and are attached to these minutes. 
 
Phyllis Luck, after hearing some discussion at a Central Mass Regional Stormwater Coalition (CMRSWC) 
meeting on the status of Water Districts, outside of Tow n control, such as the Lunenburg Water District, had 
previously inquired of consultants, Mike Clark of DCI and Ed Himlan of the Massachusetts Watershed 
Coalition (MWC), if they had any knowledge of any specific requirements.  Since they had no knowledge of  
such, Phyllis went back to CMRSWC members and learned that the Water Districts are considered in the 
Industrial category, and that there are no other special requirements that have to be met. 
 
Meeting Schedule:  Possible meeting with Town Manager, date uncertain, January 29 (Tuesday), February 28, 
and March 28.  Should additional meetings in the first quarter of 2019 be required, they will take place on the 
second Thursday of the month.  All meetings, unless otherwise noted, are held at 6:30 pm, in the Ritter 
Building, upper conference room; meeting dates are Thursdays unless otherwise noted. 
 
Motion: Jack Rabbitt        2nd: M. Allison 
To adjourn the meeting at 7:30 pm.                                                       Vote: All in Favor 
 
Respectfully submitted, 
Phyllis Luck, Chair SWTF 
Member, BOS

Proposal for YEAR ONE 2018-2019 Management (SWTF) MS4 Permit and Proposal for YEAR ONE 2018-
2019 Management MS4 Permit, both received by the SWTF on 1/1/19. Ms. Luck asked why the contractor
would have presented two proposals rather than one, incorporating all tasks. Further, why tasks under Minimum
Control Measure 6, "Good Housekeeping", were also split between the two proposals. DPW Director
Rodriquenz posed that this was a question for the contractor; Adam Burney offered several reasons why the
contractor may have separated the two seemingly intertwined proposals. It was clear that the "two proposal
approach" was not at the Town's request; therefore, clarification from the contractor was needed.
There was consensus among the group that the proposals were not cost effective for Lunenburg. Phyllis Luck
pointed out that the hourly rate for MWC representative Ed Himlan, had gone from $50 to $90; the rate for DCI
representative Mike Clark, from $100 to $150. Several specific items were discussed and it was determined
that the proposals needed to be negotiated and/or re-scoped as they exceeded the $40,000 - $50,000 that the
Town has available within the FY19 budget.
The group then looked at the latest version of DCI's "Cost Estimate For: Town of Lunenburg's General Permit
Small MS4 Permit, Years 1 - 5, and Summary, received via email earlier today. Again, the consensus was that
these estimates were beyond the Town's funding ability. It was hoped that the SWTF could take on the Public
Education and Outreach tasks, which would reduce those costs by about half. Phyllis Luck brought up that in
her work with the Central Mass Regional Stormwater Coalition (CMRSWC), about fifteen of the 30 member
communities complete all of these activities in-house, using the templates, workshops, and other resources
made available to them by the CMRSWC, and that perhaps the Town could take on some of these tasks in-
house. Town Manager Lemieux felt that the Town's employees did not have sufficient time to assist with any
of these tasks.
There was discussion about seeking other quotes for the work, but consensus was that there wasn't enough time
to do that and complete the Year One requirements of the MS4 Permit, so that the best course was to look more
closely at the proposals, determine what could be eliminated, what could be scaled back, and what could be
renegotiated for further cost savings.
The Town Manager suggested that DPW Director Rodriquenz, Land Use Director Adam Burney, and SWTF
Chair Phyllis Luck set up a meeting with Mike Clark, originator of the proposals, to discuss further. Phyllis
Luck offered to set up the meeting.
Phyllis thanked all for their inputs.

DESIGN CONSULTANTS, INC. (DCi) is pleased to submit this proposal for Professional
Engineering Services for YEAR ONE 2018-2019 Management of General Permit for Stormwater
Discharges from Small Municipal Separate Storm Sewer Systems (Permit) in Lunenburg,
Massachusetts (Town). DCi and our teaming partner, the Massachusetts Watershed Coalition (MWC)
will assist the Town in implementing and managing the Permit requirements.
This proposal provides Minimum Control Measures (MCM) 3 and 6 required in the first year of the
permit.
SCOPE OF WORK
TASK 1 - SMALL MS4 PERMIT COST ESTIMATE
The DCi Team will prepare an estimate of probable costs for the 5-year Permit Life. This estimate
will be in the form of an excel spreadsheet. The spread sheet will consist of five tabs with the detail
of the yearly permit costs. The costs will roll up to a summary page the details the permit costs through
the five year life of the Permit. Each year will be formatted so that it can be used as a stand-alone
document and included as back up for the permit roll up page. The DCi Team anticipates providing
this with thirty days of receiving the executed contract.
TASK 2 - ANNUAL REPORT
The DCi team will prepare the annual report for each of the permit years. As discussed, the permit
year closes on June 30 with the annual report due by September 30 of the same calendar year. The
first year's annual report period May 2018 to June 2019. The following years are a July to June period.
The DCi team will collect and summarize required information. It is expected that the US EPA will
provide a template for the annual report. The DCi Team will submit a Draft Annual Report to the
Town thirty days prior to the required submittal date. Following review with the Town, the DCi Team

TASK 4 -MCM 4: ILLICIT DISHCARGE DETECTION AND ELIMINATION (IDDE)
DCi will prepare a draft IDDE Program. This IDDE Program will meet the requirements of 12.3.4.6
of the Permit.
This Program will clearly identify the responsibilities of the lead Department
responsible for implementing the IDDE Program as well as other departments that may have
responsibilities of other aspects of the Program. The IDDE Program will include written procedures
to meet Permit requirements, including procedures for dry weather outfall screening and sampling,
and for catchment investigations.
DCI will assess and priority tank the Town's outfalls based on the potential to have illicit discharges
and Sanitary Sewer Overflows (SSO), and impact on public health. A priority order for screening
outfalls will be developed. Each outfall will be ranked as Problem Outfalls (known or suspected
discharges), High Priority Outfalls (near public beaches, recreational areas, drinking water supplies, or
shell fish beds), Low Priority Outfalls, and Excluded outfalls. Develop a schedule to inspect cach High
and Low Outfalls within the first three years of the Permit.
A catchment investigation procedure will be developed in accordance with 92.3.4.8 b. of the Permit.
This procedure will:
• Identify maps, historic plans and records, and other sources of data to identify system
vulnerability factors within each catchment,
• Include a manhole inspection methodology, and
• Establishes procedures to isolate and confirm sources of illicit discharges.
As part of this effort, DCi will need access to the Towns GIS system to obtain information on each
outfall.
DCi will provide the IDDE Program, the Priority Order Spreadsheet and Schedule, and Catchment
Investigation Procedures in electronic format to the Town.
TASK 5 - MCM 6: GOOD HOUSEKEEPING
The DCi Team will develop and inspection and maintenance procedures and frequencies for all
municipal stormwater treatment structures (not including catch basins). As required by the Permit,
DCi will inspect each structure. DCi has budgeted five man days for this work.

Regional Stormwater Coalition (CMRSWC) to save valuable dollars and get expert help. They realized
that most people don't attend meetings or forums, so used Public Access TV to help with marketing.
Folks in Ashland are familiar with government helping them keep Ashland clean. They have a "Green
Up Ashland" program where folks adopt roads. They get different colored trash bags and fill them
with trash found along their "adopted" road(s). There is an arrangement with the Town's trash hauler
to pick up these bags. Ashland also already had several utilities utilized by most Town residents,
including sewer, water, and trash, so another utility was less intimidating than it might be elsewhere.
Ashland "scrubbed" the Municipal Separate Storm Sewer System (MS4) permit requirement budget
from an initial cost of $2.4 million down to $1.4 million. They also looked carefully at
consultant/contractor quotes to reduce costs. In one instance, using MASS GIS, they were able to
reduce the cost of a contract from $120,000 to about $20,000. They wanted to get the most bang for
their buck.
Ashland's Stormwater Advisory committee looked at different options for assessing the Stormwater
Utility Fee. They felt the fee had to be fair and equitable for businesses, residents, seniors, and low
income. To keep it simple, they adopted a 2 tiered approach. Tier 1 included all single family home
owners, condominium owners, and owners of undeveloped parcels, who are charged $8.75 per quarter
($35/year). There is a reduced rate for qualified low income seniors, but no other reductions are
possible at this time. Tier 2, which includes all multiple family dwellings, businesses, and properties
exempted from property tax (schools, churches, government buildings), is based on a charge of $ 0.80,
per 100 square feet of impervious area. The SAC pledged to endeavor to keep the fee constant for the
first 3 years of the program. They expect to receive about $300,000 in revenue as a result of the fee
(Ashland issues 6,500 tax bills).

Adam Burney will add this document to the SWTF website by the end of this month
Town Manager Lemieux forwarded earlier today, as requested, a link to copies of the Public Education
and Outreach materials (attached) forwarded earlier by Comprehensive Environmental Inc. (CEI), the
Town's Municipal Separate Storm Sewer System (MS4) compliance consultant. SWTF members will
review these materials and will discuss usage of same at the next SWTF meeting.
Adam Burney reported that the MS4 compliance fall message has been posted on the SWTF website.
Phyllis asked that it also be placed in a more prominent setting, such as the Town's Facebook page.
Phyllis reported that this message has also been posted to the Lunenburg Bulletin Scroll on the local
cable access channel. Land Use Director Burney also reported that he'd updated the SWTF survey to
include a colored Town Logo stamp, and that he'd placed hard copies at the Senior Center, Town Hall,
and Library. Thus far, 19 persons have completed the survey on the SWTF's website. Phyllis asked
that this also be placed on the Town's Facebook page and on the Town's website, and feared that it will
remain "buried" if the only online access is via the SWTF's website. Phyllis will ask Town Manager
Lemieux to send out a Town Wide email to encourage recipients to fill out the survey online (or via
hard copy). The SWTF agreed that the survey should be available as a hand out at the November
Town Meeting. Phyllis will work with Adam Burney to insure this action is realized.
Adam Burney reported that one day of dry weather screening has been accomplished. Three days are
planned in Year Two, with six days planned in total for Years Two and Three combined, but the
amount of precipitation that has occurred this fall has made the Year Two goal difficult to achieve.
The SWTF discussed setting up a meeting with the Town Manager, Land Use Director, and DPW
Director to discuss projects planned and budget for Year Three. November 14 and November 21 from
5:30 to 7, will be targeted date options. Phyllis Luck will reach out to Town Personnel to try to make
this happen.
