Appointments/Hearings: 
LSIC year-end report: Wendy Gendron (Aquatic Restoration Consulting) and Joanna Bilotta. Wendy 
screen-shared the Lake Shirley Lake Management Annual Report for 2021-2022. She reviewed the Water 
Level Monitoring Chart of draw-down rates to control aquatic plants and allow residents to manage their 
shorelines. The target is 6’ but it was not achieved due to excessive precipitation during the year. The average 
was 3.7’ draw-down and the downstream flows were maintained. The herbicide pre-treatment was done on 
7/27 and 8/23, treating 81 acres with Diquat. The plant cover was consistent with the past year. Wendy screen-
shared the chart of plant species and plant diversity. They did not observe Bluegreen algae and coontail. They 
did see an overabundance of bladderwort and bushy pondweed, in relation to other species present. Volunteers 
did water quality checks, measuring water clarity. If the clarity goes below 5’, they take samples to look for 
algae. Wendy noted in July 2022 the nitrogen concentrations were extremely high but they did not know why 
and will compare it to July 2023 data. She stated they are not looking to propose any changes to the treatment 
plan for the coming year. The herbicide contractor will look to see if they need to do the clipper treatment plan 
for fanwort. Dominic will present the pre-treatment plan to the Commission next month. Bob P. asked if 
===PAGE 2===
Wendy’s report was available on the drive and Anna replied no. Wendy will forward another copy. Mary asked 
Wendy if there had been other historically high nitrogen levels in the past. Wendy replied she will look at past 
figures and send comments on past history. Bob asked if the nitrogen concentrations came from the north basin 
where the Catacoonamug comes in and Wendy replied correct. Rich B. asked if they took new samples and 
Wendy replied they just had the lab re-run it. She added that sometimes if the bottles get too hot, the 
preservative reacts to the plastic bottle, so they try to use fresh bottles only. Wendy pointed out that it was only 
the north basin that showed high levels. Anna will email the report, along with Wendy’s comments, to the 
Commissioners.

tree removal proposal plan for the property. They submitted a permit to split 68 Laurel Lane into 2 lots and

house on the existing foundation. They will leave the deck and sitework as is. Some trees will be removed to 
allow for the second story and the tree company will evaluate the health of other trees. Jaime screen-shared 
photos of the trees they want to take down and Mary asked if they had the plan on the shared drive. Ashley

wanted to start construction and they replied as soon as possible. He suggested holding their decision on the

water on treatment day and put out a tank acidifier if the ph is too high.  He added, based on past

      Tom M. said the proposed activity is to do a well treatment pilot study at the Hickory Hills Well 
Pumping Station. This will determine the treatment needed for iron, manganese and PFAS present in the 
well. Tom screen-shared an aerial photo of the site.  The equipment will be located inside the pump 
station and in a frac tank trailer parked adjacent to the station. They will pump from the station to the frac 
tank and the study will last 6-9 months, continuously monitoring the results. The discharge will be 10 
gallons/minute into an existing drain and drywell. After they receive approval from the Commission, they 
will file an application with the DEP to conduct a study and work would begin late summer, continuing to 
Winter 2024. Rick C. pointed out the wetlands limits and buffer zones at the project site. The frac tank 
will be located within the outer 50’ of buffer zone and the wetlands are south of the station. Mary asked if 
the frac tank would be automated and Tom confirmed it would, based on the level in the tank.  It will have 
a transducer to prevent overflow. Derek noted they will be there to monitor every day for the first week 
and then 2-3 times a week to check samples. Mary asked if the discharge would be drinking water quality 
and Derek replied yes, it would be treated for iron, manganese and PFAS. Mary asked if the non-
===PAGE 5===
optimized discharges would also be drinking water quality. Derek replied the discharges would exceed the 
quality of water in the ground and the drywell capacity would exceed the rate of 10 gal./ minute.  
   Rich B. asked if they have tested to see if the drywell will work and Derek said they did not design the 
drywell but will rely on empirical testing. If the water is not infiltrating properly, they would suspend the 
test until it has infiltrated, then resume the test. They will put erosion control around the drywell, in case 
of issues. Rich B. said they would need to resubmit their proposal with that information and Derek said he 
may have spoken out of turn on that. Rich B. noted that discharging water for up to 6-9 months may 
inundate the drywell and they want to make sure there is a backup plan. He asked how they will treat for 
PFAS. Derek replied they will use granular activated carbon and a surface modified clay. No chemicals 
are additionally used and the contaminated media will be hauled off site. There will be 4-6 media filtering 
columns (6” wide, 5’ tall) which will be used continuously for the duration of the pilot. At initial startup 
there will be back flushing for fine material and it may be done twice. Material will be captured, with 
PFAS waste tracked, hauled off-site to a landfill, or incinerated. Rich B. asked to be sent testing results 
and receipts for material hauled away.
