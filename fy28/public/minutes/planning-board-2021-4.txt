ANR PLAN:  159 & 165 Massachusetts Avenue:   The property contained two primary dwellings on one lot; a residence and a 
retail business.  Under Ch. 41, Section 81L, the Board was a llowed to divide the single lot into two nonconforming lots from a 
dimensional perspective.  Even though the single lot did not meet the lot requirement for the zoning district, the division would 
still make the two lots more conforming .  It would place a single building on each lot  versus the current two buildings on one lot.  
A division would also make it easier for the property owner to sell the two individual properties.  Both buildings have municipal 
sewer.  The applicant requested a waiver from the re quirement for a topographical plan.  P. Bertram made Motion to  approve 
the ANR for 159 Massachusetts Avenue, E. Torgersen Seconded, Vote passed 4-0. 
 
APPOINTMENT:  333 Massachusetts Avenue, Hannaford To Go:   The applicant was seeking a De Minimis change to  its 
2016 Site Plan Approval for the parking lot layout for Hannaford To Go (HTG).   The existing striping associated with the current 
drive-thru process would be removed and converted into parking spaces for HTG.  This would restore the site to its origina l 
layout.  P. Bertram made Motion to approve the De Minimis change for Hannaford To Go, E. Torgersen Seconded, Vote 
passed 4-0.

ANR PLANS:   
431 Chase Road-  Property owner Daniel Pierce presented an ANR Plan to create Parcel H consisting of 2.93 acres .  Parcel H 
would be conveyed from 490 Chase Road to 431 Chase Road , where the Pierce’s reside .  Parcel H is classified as 
agricultural/horticultural land under Chapter 61A and has been submitted to the Select Board for withdrawal.  A waiver was 
requested from submittal of a topographical plan.  P. Bertram made Motion to grant a request for waiver from the 
topographical plan requirement for the parcel at 431 Chase Road, M. Allison Seconded, passed unanimously.   P. 
Bertram made Motion to approve the ANR Plan as submitted for 431 Chase Road, M. Allison Seconded, passed 
unanimously.   
 
Below Agenda items were take n out of order.  Prior to Board endorsement of the ANR Plan for 101 Pleasant Street, the 
owner/applicant requested to withdraw the subdivision application.  See Motion on Page 2 under 101 Pleasant Street.

create Lot 1A which would retain #346.  The remaining land, Parcel A, would be combined with abutting land at 350 Electric 
Avenue.  P. Bertram made Motion to approve the ANR Plan for 34 6 Electric Avenue, M. Allison seconded, passed 
unanimously. 
 
101 Pleasant Street -  Trevor Fletcher, GRAZ Engineering, presented on behalf of property owner Danny Gardner d /b/a MA 
Pleasant LLC.  The property owner replaced the definitive subdivision applica tion with a 2-lot ANR Plan.  A common drive was 
proposed along the lot line between proposed Lot 1 and Lot 2.  P. Bertram made Motion to approve the ANR Plan for 101 
Pleasant Street, M. Allison seconded, passed unanimously. 
 
111 Mulpus Road -  Seth Donohoe, Dillis & Roy, presented on behalf of property owner James Warrick.  The Board previously 
endorsed a plan for the creation of Lots 1  and 2.  Lot 2 contained an existing barn with associated well.  At that time, it was 
thought the well was abandoned and a new one would need to be put in.  The well was found to be useable.  To allow the well to 
meet lot line setbacks, Parcel A was created which would be combined with the existing Lot 2 to create Lot 2A .  P. Bertram 
made Motion to approve the ANR Plan for 111 Mulpus Road, M. Allison Seconded, passed unanimously.

Ch. 250, Section 8.4. and Chapter 204 respectively of the Code of the Town of Lunenburg  for construction of a 372,000 square 
foot warehouse and distribution  building off Leominster -Shirley Road at 1263 Reservoir Road (Map 142, Parcel 0006) , owner 
Paul Keating (Heirs) c/o Mary Coburn, et. al., 998 Reservoir Road, Lunenburg, MA  01462 and 435 Leominster -Shirley Road  
(Map 143, Parcel 0007), Lunenburg, MA, owner J&A Realty Trust, Daniel L. McNiff Trustee, 280 Great Road, Shirley MA 01464.

In attendance , either in -person or via Zoom, were project engineer  Bill Hannigan, Han nigan Engineering  and representatives 
from The Brennan Group, Inc., John M. Corcoran & Co., Greenman-Pedersen, Inc., and MDM Transportation Consultants, Inc. 
 
Heather Monticup, Greenman-Pedersen, Inc. (hereinafter GPI) brought the Board up to date, noting an October 18, 2021 review 
letter f rom MDM Transportation Consultants, Inc.  As a result of requested clarification or supplemental analysis from MDM, 
updates were provided by GPI.  Based on those updates, a supplemental review letter was provided by MDM.   
 
Mitigation commitments from GPI included: 
 Prior to occupancy, pavement markers at the intersection of Leominster Shirley Road and Fort Pond Road.   
 Bicycle lanes along the site frontage. 
 Easement to the Town along a portion of the site frontage. 
 Prior to occupancy, a n intersection design report and 10% design plan with survey for the intersection  of Leominster 
Shirley Road at Fort Pond Road. 
 Within three months of occupancy, a traffic monitoring study.   
 Tree cutting and brush clearing along the south si de of Leominster Shirley Road at Reservoir Road to improve sight 
lines. 
 Working with the DPW to clean up existing sign clutter and additional signage along Leominster Shirley Road .

The Applicant also committed to a post -construction monitoring study to address potential unanticipated traffic impacts.  If the

engineered plan that could provide the basis for mitigation by other future developments in the area, state funding request o r

P. Bertram raised concerns that in the event the Town was unable to receive a grant, the cost of improvements would be 
Lunenburg’s responsibility.  GPI responded that the intersection needed improvements whether or not this project went in.  A 
final engineered plan would be their fair -share contribution.  A. Burney stated that the complete cost to renovate the intersection, 
from design to completion,  was beyond the commitment of a single development.  P. Bertram stressed that  if the facility 
exceeded 140 peak hour trips, it would be worsening the situation; she did not feel the Town should incur any financial burden.  
T. Cole asserted that the Applicant had done a good job mitigating the impacts of traffic associated with the project.  He went on 
to note current traffic increase due to the growth of industrial projects already located in that vicinity.  He did not feel it was this 
Applicant’s responsibility to mitigate, when a lot of the growth and safety concerns were already ongoing and not relative to this 
project.   
 
Dan Dumais, Senior Project Manager, MDM Transportation, stated that MDM did a sensitivity analysis to come up with a 
percentage increase of entering volume at the Leominster Shirley Road and Fort Pond Road inter section.  Normal warehouse 
use (e.g. dry goods warehouse)  equated to ~70 trips, a 5% increase to the intersection.  They also looked at a higher industrial 
use (e.g. warehouse that facilitated the movement of goods to a final destination) which equated to ~25% increase.  As the 
tenants were unknown, MDM came up with another threshold which would cause a significant change in the number of trips.  
Normal threshold would be ~10% increase in traffic, which for this project would be only 7 trips.  MDM doubled t he trips to 140 
which equated to 14 trips.   
 
Bill Hannigan, Project Engineer, Hannigan Engineering believed that a  lot of the traffic on the road came from Leominster, via 
Prospect Street (Leominster) to Route 2.  The traffic impact was being addressed wi thin the proposed mitigation.  If it went above 
140 peak hour trips, then some of the design features noted in the mitigation package would be implemented.   
 
P. Bertram opined there were too many unknowns as to tenants and cost of traffic improvements.  S he wanted the Decision to 
include a trigger that the cost be revisited if volume reached a 25% increase.   
 
T. Cole emphasized the beneficial tax revenue to the Town, which would help pay for the improvements.  He  reiterated the 
Applicant’s efforts to mitigate the impact of its project.   
 
P. Bertram explained she was in favor of the project, but not knowing the potential cost of traffic impacts, and if the facility 
exceeded the 140 trips, it would be financially devastating to the Town.  She did not belie ve the mitigation package went far 
enough.  She wanted to see an incremental fair share based on impact.   
 
A. Burney emphasized that the Board needed to put a finite timeline on the impacts being measured.  He proposed that a ny 
increase up to 446 site trips (as defined by GPI), would abide by current mitigation measures.  Anything over that number, within 
12 months of occupancy, would trigger additional mitigation.  GPI and MDM used the existing information to come up with the 
current numbers.  There would  not be a solid answer until vehicles were coming out of the site .  At this time, it would not be 
possible to determine the Applicant’s fair share.  A. Burney felt that the building would be at 100% occupancy within 12 months.  
The Applicant’s proportional share could be tied to the proportional increase in traffic.  It was determined that the initial timeline 
would be at 3 months, then at 100%.

APPOINTMENTS: 
101 Pleasant Street - Common Drive - Presentation was given  by Trevor Fletcher, GRAZ Engineering, LLC, on behalf of 
property owner Daniel Gardner dba MA Pleasant, LLC .  The zoning bylaw states that common driveways should be located 
along the common lot line.  The applicant requested the Board allow the use of an alternate location as suggested by the 
Conservation Commission.  The Commission  requested the applicant use  the location of the existing cart road, which would 
reduce the wetlands impact.  An easement would be provided on both side s of the driveway for utilities, drainage, access, etc.  
P. Bertram made Motion to approve the relocation of the common drive with t he condition that the easement be 
structured so as to include maintenance and utilities, M. Allison Seconded .  Discussion- T. Cole expressed an interest in 
seeing the easement language as the proposed drive would be almost fully located  on only one of the properties, with little 
impact to the other property that would be sharing the drive .  P. Bertram noted that the Board’s responsibility was to ensure the 
easement language note the inclusion of maintenance and utilities.  It would be up to the property own ers/buyers to ensure their 
individual protection.  The Motion passed unanimously .  It was noted that the applicant did not provide any  written 
correspondence from the Conservation Commission requesting relocation of the drive.  The Board stressed that in s imilar future 
situations it would like to see a hard copy of the Commission’s request, not just verbal.  
 
43 Summer Street - Uma Flowers - Representing the applicant was Attorney Valerio Romano.  Uma Flowers was awarded a 
Community Host Agreement from Lunenb urg.  The applicant  wanted to discuss the filing of a special permit application with the 
Board without having to obtain further licensing from the Cannabis Control Commission  (CCC).  Attorney Romano’s opinion was 
that a provisional license from the CCC, a s noted in the Zoning Bylaw Section 250 -4.15(F)(11), would not be required for 
submission of a special permit application.   The Board’s purpose in requesting a provisional license was to protect Lunenburg 
from applicants wasting the Board’s time when an applicant may not have the ability to obtain a license f rom the CCC.  The 
applicant had opened a facility in Pepperell, MA and would provide in the application packet, that final license from Pepperell in 
place of obtaining a provisional license.  T. Cole noted that Lunenburg was limited to two retail licenses.  If the Board allowed an 
applicant to move forward with an application prior to completing the process with the CCC, the town  was opening itself up to 
multiple applicants moving through the process at varying stages of the timeline.  A. Burney would submit Attorney Romano’s 
correspondence to Town Counsel for clarification on the ambiguity between Sections 250 -4.15(F)(11) and 250-4.15 (K)(1).  A. 
Burney recommended that for the record the Board take a vote on allowing Uma Flowers to utilize the Pepperell license for its 
provisional license with Lunenburg .  P. Bertram made Motion to allow Uma Flowers to move forward with their special 
permit application, M. Allison Seconded, passed unanimously.

The Hearing was to hear and review an application for a stormwater management permit under Chapter 204 of the Code of the 
Town of Lunenburg  for 97 Cross Street, 125 Cross Street, 430 Mulpus Road and 440 Mulpus Road.  (Map 014, Parcels 0005, 
0003, 0002, 0004 respectively), owner/applicant Harbor Classic Homes, LLC, 493 Lancaster Street, Leominster, MA  01453.

property consisted of ~14 acres previously subdivided into five (5) lots through the ANR Pla n process.  Lot 1 contained the

pea stone diaphragms along the length of the driveways  that would provide pre-treatment of the stormwater from the driveways.

listed in the letter.
