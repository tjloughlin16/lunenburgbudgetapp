September 11th Flash Floods Storm Event and MEMA Situational Awareness Update on Upcoming Rain
Although it is estimated we received over 7" of rain from last night's storm event, the damage in Town does not
compare to the devastation in our neighboring city of Leominster. Today Senator Cronin's office reached out to see
if we had any initial damage assessment to include in their request to the state for relief funding. DPW Director
Bill Bernard put together an estimate of approximately $24,000 in costs attributed to this storm and this was
forwarded to Senator Cronin's office at the end of the day. Representative Scarsdale and Representative
Kushmerek also reached out to see if we needed any assistance, as did our North Central Chamber of Commerce.
Today I shared a post from North Central Chamber of Commerce, as they have created a dedicated email hotline
for businesses affected by the flood event to contact them for assistance. That email is help@northcentralmass.com.
I would like to thank our public safety staff, including our Fire Department that responded with an engine, ladder
truck, dive truck and boat to assist Leominster, The engine and ladder truck responded to calls in the City and
assisted with evacuations on Exchange Street. The dive truck and boat assisted with evacuations at the
Meadowbrook Trailer Park. Also, thank you to the DPW crew who were out until midnight last night closing
certain roads that had flooding issues and clearing out drains until they were passable.
With the expected additional rainfall tomorrow and Hurricane Lee effects over the weekend, MEMA issued a
situational awareness update:
"The National Weather Service (NWS) in conjunction with the MEMA Agency Meteorologist are predicting
another round of showers and thunderstorms on Wednesday. High humidity coupled with a slow-moving cold
front will run the risk for heavy bouts of rainfall mainly between 12PM - 10PM. Rainfall rates of near 1 inch per
hour may occur across portions of western and central Massachusetts Wednesday afternoon and evening. While
these showers and thunderstorms are expected to be more progressive than they were Monday, any storm that
produces 1 inch per hour rain rates, especially over a previously hard-hit area, is likely to cause additional instances
of flash flooding."
Drier weather is expected to return Thursday afternoon into Friday.
"MEMA is actively monitoring Hurricane Lee through this week as it makes its way up the east coast. Daily
coordination calls with the National Weather Service, National Hurricane Center, and MEMA Command and
General Staff are underway. Future information on pre-event planning actions, activation, coordination calls, and
response posture will be updated in the coming days."
925 Mass Ave (TM Goal #1,ii) The EPA has approved funding for additional work to be done at 925 Mass Ave
that will include soil borings, soil samples, monitoring well installation, groundwater sampling, removal of the

Infrastructure, Facilities, and IT Updates
LED Streetlight Project: Pine Ridge Technologies began the LED Streetlight conversion project on September
Sth and ran into issues with the wiring on a majority of the streetlights they have attempted to convert. We
contacted Unitil and are coordinating a meeting between their Electric Operations Manager and the vendor
tomorrow to review what our contractor has found in the field. In the meantime, our contractor will proceed with
the conversion and will address any wiring issues as they are encountered at each light.
Senior Center ADA Patio Project: The Senior Center ADA Patio Project is expected to be complete by the end of
this month. The ribbon cutting ceremony will be October 11th at noon.
TC Passios Roof: There was yet again another leak at TC Passios that occurred over the weekend. After speaking
with the Facilities Director and discussing everything that has been done to try to find where the leaks are
originating, the patches that have been done, and that the roof is over is life expectancy, he is going to seek quotes
to repair that roof in phases. A warrant article has been submitted for the Special Town Meeting as a placeholder.
New Website: The question that was raised about accessing the town website with an iPad and having to double
tap in order to expand menus will not be an issue with the new website through CivicPlus. Our IT Administrative
Assistant contacted our current website host and they would have to remove the whole menu and rebuild it and
there would be a cost to that. Since we are moving to a new website in a month, we will not make this change on
the current website but it will be a feature in the new website.
Third Quarter Preliminary Tax Bills for FY24
third quarter preliminary tax installment cannot be more than the amount of the first quarter preliminary tax
installment (IGR 23-1, IV. B.)
This year, a "Pro-Forma" Recap Sheet, which does not contain valuation information, will need to be done to
issue a third quarter preliminary tax bill and actual tax bills cannot be issued for the third quarter. Once we
complete the certification process at the end of January/beginning of February, a tax classification hearing can be
held, a Tax Recap Sheet submitted to DOR to set the tax rate, and the 4th quarter actual tax bills issued once the
Tax Recap is approved.
Budget Updates
Health Insurance Estimate for FY25 and future years: The Assistant Town Manager/HR Director,
Payroll/Benefits Coordinator, our health insurance broker, and I met with our health insurance account
representative last week for our annual review. We were advised that due to the state of the healthcare industry
and medical trends, we should budget 10% for active plans for FY25 and expect 8-10% increases in health
insurance rates each year over the next five years. This was explained that due to low employment in the medical
fields and hospitals renegotiating their contracts, insurance rates are trending high. Our retiree renewal for
calendar year 2024 is estimated to be a 6% increase.

Meeting with Land Use Director re: Special Town Meeting Warrant Articles from the Planning
Board
Mr. Burney stated one of the articles presented for the STM warrant addresses off street parking.
Currently section 6.4c 5K of the zoning bylaw is common driveways. The Planning Board over the past
several years has heard more and more petitions for common driveways. The current language by right
allows two lots to share a common driveway as long as it splits the lot line between the two. In practice
it tends to be more complicated and there's language that allows the Planning Board and the
===PAGE 4===
Building Commissioner to independently make a determination that an alternative location is okay.
Essentially it's just a determination; people come to the Planning Board and sometimes it's easy and the
Planning Board says yes, this makes total sense, you're splitting a lot off your existing house and you
want to use the existing driveway; people know where it is, there's a curve in the road, why not? Other
times it's more complicated and we end up with some fairly ridiculously shaped lots.
Secondly the purpose behind common driveways is often to reduce pavement to reduce interactions
between driveways and roadways. The Planning Board doesn't have any real standards and wants to
formalize this and permit larger development on a common driveway. They've introduced the language
that you have in front of you on the warrant and the intent would be to separate it out from section 64 C5
and make it its own section 64 C7. It would allow for four common lots to share a singular driveway as
long as all of those lots have frontage on the road that the driveway intersects with. It creates a special
permit process for these common driveways, so it makes it a subjective process that the board will
review under their special permit standards, their rules and regulations for special permits and site plan
approval and it clarifies what the submission requirements would be, what the time frames are and it
gives them the ability to condition these common driveways. We spoke with the Fire Chief about
requirements he would look for; there are requirements in here for signs at the end of the driveway
showing the layout and number location as well as numerical signs at each of the branches for the house
that would be located there. It also codifies commercial common driveways MA DOT is highly in favor
of and has pushed other communities to introduce.
Motion: C. Nuzzolo
To accept Article N for the Special Town Meeting warrant
2"d: T. Alonzo
Vote: All in Favor

Community Compact Best Practices Grant Application
I applied for the Community Compact Best Practices Grant last week. Towns can apply for up to two "Best
Practices", which are outlined in the state's program guidelines. The two best practices I requested funding for
were:
1) Financial Management category to utilize the services of the Collins Center to create a comprehensive
package of financial management policies, financial procedures manual and a memorandum detailing any
additional findings and recommendations regarding financial structure and general administrative
practices. This request was for $30,000.
2) Resilient and Reliable Transportation category to create a multi-year asset management plan that
inventories and rates the condition of our bridges and culverts. This request was for $25,000.
Both of these projects are part of my FY24 goals, as they will be extremely valuable to the Town once completed.
As we are all aware, having written policies and procedures identify laws and regulations that need to be complied
with, provide guidelines for financial decision making, and outlines for internal processes that should be followed
by all stakeholders. The additional value of this best practice would be the inclusion of a high-level review of the
overall management structure and recommendations for improvements that will address prior year annual audit
comments, a Financial Management Review done by the Department of Revenue in 2008, and will address
questions about financial management structure in light of this past year's tax assessment issues.
The second best practice will be critical information we are lacking in our capital planning process right now and
overall long term planning. We have 26 bridges and large culverts and we do not have a corresponding
management plan of when they need to be repaired or replaced or the associated costs. To give an idea of the
financial impact, we have approximately $900,000 in local funds and the Small Bridge Grant funds to reconstruct
the Flat Hill Road culvert. The end product will be similar to our Pavement Management Plan with an inventory
assessment, priority rankings, and estimated costs.

===PAGE 3===
Departments Subsidized by Consultants/Interim Appointments: Assessing Department (Interim Principal 
Assessor); Finance Department (Interim Finance Director); Interim Planning Consultant; Interim 
Conservation Administrators – total hours short of being fully staffed is 200 hours a week.  
Causes: Significant increase in retirements vs. people entering into municipal careers, employee turnover 
and current pay rates. Pay rates are confined by collective bargaining agreements and the salary 
administration plan. Changes to pay scales requires town meeting votes or collective bargaining 
negotiations. Staying within the budget is crucial, and current employers are counter-offering wages that 
exceed the town’s pay scale. The strong labor market and inflation reduce the number of applicants for 
public services jobs. There is a need to maintain pay equity among existing staff.  
Impact: Staffing shortages lead to decreased productivity, backlog of work, increased possibility of 
missed deadlines, and decreased quality of work. Financial implications include town board, resident, and 
business dissatisfaction. Overworked employees experience burnout, reducing morale and the ability to 
take on new programs and services.  
Recruitment and Retention Strategies: Recruitment efforts include advertising on various platforms: 
MMA website, Mass Hite, professional association websites, Indeed, town website, town bulletin boards, 
Facebook, Instagram, the Ledger, North Central Chamber of Commerce website, and job fairs. 
Consultants are contacted to fill gaps when necessary. Networking and cold calls are used to attract 
candidates.  
Retention Efforts: Town administration conducts wage studies to ensure competitive pay. Employee 
engagement surveys are conducted to understand and improve morale. Training programs are provided to 
develop skills. Employee Assistance Program (EAP) and health and wellness programs are available. 
Efforts to improve work-life balance are prioritized.  
Future Steps: Address wages to offer competitive compensation. Define organizational mission and 
vision to enhance employee engagement. Create training and mentorship programs. Examine current 
staffing structure and budgeted positions. Build pathways for future employees by partnering with local 
schools and higher education institutions. Advocate for state programs and funding to support these 
initiatives.  
Board members expressed gratitude for the detailed report and acknowledged the significant efforts made 
to address staffing issues. Discussions included the importance of competitive pay, flexibility in training, 
and potential incentives for longevity and retention.
