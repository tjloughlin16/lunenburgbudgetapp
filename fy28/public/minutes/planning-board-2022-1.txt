ANR PLANS: 
 
44 Northfield Road -  The Plan was present ed by applicants John Harrison, Lunenburg, MA, and Tim Sullivan, Lyndeborough, 
NH.  A. Burney informed the applicants he had been contacted by the owner of the abutting property who informed him that the 
existing sewer tie in  for #44 was located where the applicant was proposing the driveway for the new lot.  That is something that 
would be taken up with the Sewer Commission, and was not relative to the Board’s endorsement of the ANR Plan.  P. Bertram 
made Motion to approve the ANR Plan for 44 Northfield Road, E. Torgersen Seconded, passed unanimously. 
 
41 & 47 Reservoir Road -  The applicant was represented by Brian Marchetti, McCarty Engineering, Inc.  The ANR Plan, which 
created Lots 9A and 10, was originally endorsed by the Board in January 2020.  The My lar was picked up by the applicant and 
then misplaced; therefore, the Plan did not get recorded at the Registry of Deeds.  The applicant was requesting the Board 
endorse the identical plan this evening for recording at the Registry of Deeds.  P. Bertram made Motion to re-sign the Plan for 
Reservoir Road, E. Torgersen Seconded, passed unanimously.   
 
APPOINTMENTS: Lunenburg Central- Presentation by William Hannigan, Hannigan Engineering, Inc. 
 
Request for De Minimis Change - The applicant submitted a request for a De Minimis change to the Site Plan approved by the 
Board.  During the title review it was determined that a portion of the property to the rear of the site was formerly owned by the 
MBTA.  There was a clause in the railroad’s deed that prohibited any  structures from being built on the old parcel.  The retaining 
wall proposed by the applicant extended into that parcel  and the applicant was concerned it could be considered a structure .  In 
addition, no work or grading could be done within 30 feet of the MBTA line without obtaining a permit from the MBTA.  It was 
expected this would be a lengthy process.  The applicant was requesting an adjustment to the grading at the rear of the site to 
provide for the 30 foot buffer.  To that end, they would be adding a retaining wall along the rear access drive.  With the Board’s 
approval of the De Minimis change, the applicant  would be able to redo the grade, stay out of the 30 foot offset, and build a 
retaining wall.  The Board  requested a formal review from Graves E ngineering, Inc. prior to taking a vote to approve the De 
Minimis changes.  As the applicant had an upcoming property closing date of January 18th, it was requested if the Board would

225 & 259 Leominster Shirley Road 
March 14, 2022

The Hearing was to hear and review applications for a stormwater management permit and site plan approval under Chapter 204 
and Chapter 250, Section 8.4. of the Code of the Town of Lunenburg 225 & 259 Leominster Shirley Road.  (Map 146, Parcels 15 
and 14 respectively), owner/applicant PSG Realty 225-259 LLC, 133 Leominster Shirley Road, Lunenburg, MA.  
 
Representing the owner/applicant PSG Realty ( Steven Powell ) was Brian Marchetti, McCarty Engineering, Inc.   PSG Realty 
received a special permit for dimensional variation from the Zoning Board of Appeals as the building did not conform to setback 
requirements, due to the parcel’s unusual shape.  PSG Realty planned to construct a 12,300 sf 7 bay garage and reshape the 
parking areas to provide for better trucking service, maneuverability, and enhanced stormwater management and landscaping.  
A septic system was located on the property and the building was served by a public well.  There was no stormwater 
infrastructure and all runoff was flowing onto Leominster Shirley Road  to existing infrastructure in the vicinity of Route 70.  The 
site consisted of approximately 2.36 acres with almost 85% of the site impervious.  The existing garage will remain operational 
until the new building is buil t.  Parking spaces would be located on the right side of the property for employees and visitors.  The 
main body of the building would be the garage, with the office located on the left hand side.  PSG Realty would maintain the 
setbacks approved by the Zon ing Board of Appeals. There was approximately 84,000 sf of impervious area; this would be 
reduced by approximately 4,000 sf.  The existing septic system would stay in operation during construction, but the existing well 
would be decommissioned.  PSG Realty  would be proposing, to the Water District, an extension of the water main from Keating 
on Reservoir Road all the way to Route 70.  PSG Realty was aware that the building could not receive an occupancy permit unti l 
the water main was extended.   
 
The proposed site would be graded for a stormwater collection system.  Catch basins would be located next to the office and on 
the eastern portion of the site.  The infiltration basin was proposed to be located where the current septic system is locate d.  Due 
to th is, PSG Realty asked for the stormwater soil testing to take place during construction.  Catch basins are located along 
Leominster Shirley Road and there is a pipe network for stormwater infrastructure along Leominster Shirley Road.  Once the ne w 
DPW Director is on board, PSG Realty will work with that individual to locate that infrastructure for tie -in.  The septic system 
would be reconstructed.

Due to cancellation of the February 28 th Board meeting, PSG Realty had the opportunity to revise and resubmit plans based on 
review from Graves Engineering, Inc. (GEI). Based on GEI review, remaining outstanding items were: 
 Non-compliance of the pavement setbacks; however, the proposed improvements  better organized the sit e, curb cuts, 
and improved landscaping along Leominster Shirley Road. 
 Soil testing had not been performed; however, B. Marchetti responded that was due to the current septic system being 
located under a paved area within the footpr int of the proposed infiltration system.  With Board approval, soil testing 
would be conducted once the replacement septic system was constructed.  
 
M. Allison had concerns with the recharge being proposed over the existing septic.  Given the footprint of the existing building 
and location of new building, little room was left for naturally existing soils.  B. Marchetti noted t he footprint of the septic system 
would be over -excavated and removed.  The soil was very sandy and depth to bedrock was not a conce rn; t here would be 
significant depth of natural soil.  In viewing Board of Health plans, the existing system was very shallow.  The existing system 
was located on the right side of the parcel; the proposed system would be located on the left side of the si te.   
 
P. Bertram inquired if soil testing had been done at the proposed location of the septic system.  It was done a while ago by PSG 
Realty and based on the soils excavated at adjacent properties, the sand was deep.  The system would be sized per vehicl e bay 
and office.  In reviewing department comments, per the Board of Health Agent, P. Bertram requested the Board’s Conditions 
specify dust mitigation.   
 
Per Stormwater Permit Standard Conditions, the stormwater infrastructure needed to be installed, and  functional, at the 
beginning of site work .  As PSG Realty would be placing the drainage system where the septic system was located, A. Burney 
made the Board aware that this would be an alteration to the Standard Conditions.   
 
Some Board members voiced co ncerns not knowing whether there would be a well or public water, the timeline on the Water 
District’s approval as to the extension of the line, soil testing not being performed in the location of the proposed septic system, 
and what would happen to the st ormwater during construction as the stormwater system would not go in until after construction.  
B. Marchetti responded that the building could be installed first as that would go where there is currently pavement.  He str essed 
that a SWPPP (Stormwater Pol lution Prevention Plan) would be submitted and sediment and stormwater would ha ve to be 
controlled during construction.  The application for extension of the water main was filed with the Water District in summer 2021 
and was currently under review.  B. Ma rchetti stressed that some of these issues were building permit issues and a building 
permit would not be issued until these items were finalized.  T. Cole suggested that the hearing be continued until PSG Realty 
had a decision from the Water District.  B.  Marchetti strongly felt that the Water District would agree to extension of the main.  A. 
Burney recommended the hearing be continued to allow time for resolution of some of the outstanding issues.   
 
M. Allison made Motion to continue the hearing to Marc h 28, 6:05 p.m., P. Bertram Seconded, Roll Call Vote- P. Bertram, 
aye; E. Torgersen, aye; M. Allison, aye; T. Cole, aye.  Passed unanimously.  
 
Hearing recessed 7:02 p.m.

The Hearing was to hear and review a request under M.G.L. Ch. 40, Section 15C, Scenic Road Designations and under M.G.L. Ch. 87, Section 
3, Cutting of Public Shade Trees to remove trees and a 50 foot section of stone wall for a new driveway at 780 Northfield Road (Map 038, 
Parcel 0017), Lunenburg, MA.  Owner/applicants were James and Allison Lattanzi, 331 Marshall Road, Fitchburg, MA  01420.    
 
J. Lattanzi noted he and his wife owned Hollis Hills Farms in Fitchburg, a short distance from the Lu nenburg line.  780 Northfield Road was the 
site of Pierce’s Christmas Tree Farm property , which J. Lattanzi had purchased .  He would be oper ating both businesses which required 
significant travel of farm equipment between the two locations.  He had concerns with the intersection of Northfield Road and Chase Road as it 
was historically dangerous.  He felt that dealing with that intersection posed high risk to his equipment, himself and his staff.  If he was allowed 
the new farm access road, he could avoid that intersection.   
 
He would remove four trees that were in the Town’s easement, and open up that section of stone wall to enter the property.   
 
The Conservation Administrator had noted to A. Burney that was considered agricultural land  under normal impro vement and therefore w as 
exempt from conservation  review.  J. Lattanzi noted he had also spoken with the Tree Warden, who noted he had no issues with the tree 
removal.  T. Cole opined that a 50 foot wide drive was rather excessive.  J. Lattanzi responded t hat the drive itself would not be 50 feet wide, 
only the road opening.  This would accommodate the turning of his farm equipment.    
 
Board members decided to schedule a site visit to get a better understanding as to location of proposed drive and trees be ing removed.  The 
visit was scheduled for Monday March 21st, 5:00 p.m.   
 
M. Allison made Motion to continue the hearing to March 28, 2022, 6:05 p.m., P Bertram Seconded, Roll Call Vote - P. Bertram, aye; E. 
Torgersen, aye; M. Allison, aye; T. Cole, aye.  Passed unanimously.

ARTICLE 27  To see if the Town will vote to amend the Code of Lunenburg, Section 250 -4.9, which is a 
Section of the Protective Bylaw entitled “Water Supply Protection District ,” §  250-4.9 Water Supply 
Protection District as shown below, or take any action relative thereto  
 
A. Purpose. The purpose of the Water Supply Protection District is to promote the health, safety and 
general welfare of the community; to protect, preserve and maintain present and potential 
sources of water supply and watershed areas for the public health and safety; to protect, 
preserve and maintain the existing and potential groundwater supply and groundwater recharge 
areas within the Town for public healt h and safety; to preserve and protect the lakes, ponds, 
streams, brooks, rills, marshes, swamps, bogs and other water bodies and watercourses in Town; 
to protect the community from the detrimental use and development of land and water within 
the district; to conserve the natural resources of the Town; to preserve and maintain the 
groundwater table and water recharge areas within the Town; and to prevent blight and the 
pollution of the environment.

B. District delineation. 
(1) The boundaries of the Water Supply Protection District shall be as follows: Zone I, Zone II, and 
Zone III for the Town of Lunenburg, approved by the Department of Environmental Protection 
(DEP) according to Map 1, entitled "Water Protection District, revised October, 2009 June 2020."  
(2) Where the bounds as delineated are in doubt or in dispute, the burden of proof that the use is 
outside the Water Supply Protection District or does not impact the district shall be upon the 
owner(s) of the land in question. However, the Building Commissioner retains its authority to 
determine final location with regard to said Water Supply Protection District.  
(3) At the written request of the owner(s) of the property in question, the Town may engage a 
professional geologist, a certified soil scientist or a registered professional engineer trained in 
hydrology to determine the location and extent of a protection area on the parcel in question and 
charge the owner(s) of said parcel for the cost of the investigation.

(l) The application of fertilizers for nondomestic or agricultural uses, provided that such application 
shall be made in such a manner as to minimize adverse impacts on surface water and  groundwater 
due to nutrient transport and deposition and sedimentation.  
(m) Planned residential development, subject to development plan approval and provided that no 
more than 15% of the required lot area is covered with impervious surfaces.  
(n) Those commercial and industrial activities permitted in the underlying district, subject to 
development plan approval to prevent adverse impact on the environment.  
(5) Uses prohibited in the Water Supply Protection District: 
(a) Storage of road salt or deicing chemicals;  
(b) Chemical and bacteriological laboratories;  
(c) Industrial uses which discharge process water, including any commercial or service uses 
discharging wastewater;  
(d) Storage of petroleum or other refined petroleum product except within build ings which will be 
heated by the petroleum or other refined petroleum product; however, nothing in this section 
shall prevent the owner of a tank or tanks existing at the time of adoption of this section to 
replace said tanks, provided there is no increase in capacity;  
(e) Any other use which involves the manufacture, use, storage, transportation or disposal of toxic or 
hazardous materials or generates, treats, stores or disposes of hazardous materials as defined in 
Article II in quantity exceeding 250 220 total gallons of liquid material(s) or 2,000 total pounds of 
solid material(s) at any one time per month.  
(6) Uses prohibited in Zone I and Zone II. The following uses are prohibited in Zone I and Zone II:  
(a) Public or private sanitary landfills, includ ing the deposition of any foreign or nonindigenous 
material upon the land except for normal and accepted agricultural;  
(b) Junkyards or recycling facilities;  
(c) Sewage treatment facilities or sludge and septage storage;  
(d) Commercial car washes;  
(e) Dry-cleaning establishments;  
(f) Metal plating or metal finishing;  
(g) Use of chemicals for deicing unless deemed necessary for public safety by the Board of 
Selectmen;  
(h) The use of septic system cleaners which contain toxic organic chemicals;  
(i) The rendering impervious of more than 15% of a required lot area;  
(j) The disposal of liquid or leachable wastes, except subsurface domestic waste disposal systems;  
(k) Dumping of snow brought in from outside the district;  
(l) The alteration of any natura l site features or topography, including but not limited to the cutting 
or removal of trees or other natural vegetation or the dumping, filling, excavation, grading, 
transferring or removing of any gravel, sand, loam or other soft material, rock or ledge p rior to 
obtaining all permits and approvals for final development plans required by this bylaw;

[4] General notes for sediment control that spell out the procedures for implementing the plan.

E. Limitations upon uses. In addition to the limitations set forth in Article V of this bylaw, all uses 
within the Water Supply Protection District, except for single -family dwelling lots on plans not 
requiring Planning Board approval, shall be subject to the following limitations: 
(1) Safeguards. Provision shall be made to protect against toxic or hazardous materials discharged or 
lost through corrosion, accidental damage, spillage or vandalism through such measures as 
provision for spill control in th e vicinity of chemical or fuel delivery points, secure storage areas 
for toxic or hazardous materials and indoor storage provisions for corrosive or dissolvable 
materials.

(2) Location. Where the premises are partially outside of the Water Supply Protection District, such 
potential pollution sources as on -site waste disposal systems shall, to the degree feasible, be 
located outside the district.  
(3) Disposal. For any toxic or hazardous waste to be  produced in quantities greater than those 
associated with normal household use, the applicant must demonstrate the availability and 
feasibility of disposal methods which are in conformance with Chapter 21C of the Massachusetts 
General Laws and all other applicable laws and regulations.  
(4) Drainage. All runoff from impervious surfaces shall be recharged on the site, diverted towards 
areas covered with vegetation for surface infiltration to the extent possible. Dry wells shall be 
used only where other meth ods are infeasible and shall be preceded by oil, grease and sediment 
traps to facilitate removal of contamination.  
(5) Monitor test wells. Where fertilizers, pesticides, herbicides or other potential contaminants are to 
be applied, utilized or stored and in the opinion of the Planning Board are a matter of concern, a 
groundwater monitoring program shall be established before the special permit is granted. Such a 
program shall adequately monitor the quality of the groundwater leaving the site through the us e 
of monitor wells and/or appropriate groundwater sample analyses.  
(6) Natural vegetation. No more than 30% of existing natural vegetation on any lot may be disturbed 
in an underlying residential district, nor more than 50% of existing natural vegetation on any lot 
may be disturbed in an underlying nonresidential district.  
(7) Technical reference. The technical reference to be used to prepare and review plans is 
"Guidelines for Soil and Water Conservation in Urbanizing Areas of Massachusetts," 1977, U.S.D .A. 
Soil Conservation Service, Amherst, Massachusetts. Specific guidelines to use include, but are not 
limited to: 
(a) Limit grading to only those areas actively undergoing current construction;  
(b) The smallest practical area of land should be exposed at one time during development;  
(c) Limit the length of time graded areas are exposed;  
(d) Provide temporary or permanent stabilization of disturbed areas at the earliest opportunity. Limit 
exposure to less than 60 days;  
(e) Retain and protect as much of the natural vegetation as possible;  
(f) Permanent improvements such as streets, utilities, storm sewer, vegetated waterways and other 
features of the development should be scheduled for installation to the greatest extent possible 
before removing the vegetative cover from an area scheduled for building construction;  
(g) Protect all fill slopes and cut slopes exceeding five feet in height from storm run -off through the 
use of diversion berms, drop chutes or other acceptable means;  
(h) Rough-graded rights-of-way awaiting installation of utilities and/or pavement should be protected 
by the installation of interceptor berms across the right-of-way so as to reduce the length of slope 
between berms to not more than 250 feet;  
(i) On sites where the above proced ures are impractical or not acceptable, where the topography 
permits, install sediment basins, desilting basins or silt traps to remove sediment from runoff 
waters.  
(8) Non-point source pesticide contamination. All pesticide use in Zone II shall comply wi th the 
provisions 333 CMR 12.00.
