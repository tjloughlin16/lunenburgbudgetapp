municipal buildings is televised. One of the first questions that I had regarding the potential reuse of the Passios as a
Town Hall was what is the reasoning behind the proposal, what is it we are trying to fix? And I learned that the reasons
are many:
1. There is significant work that needs to be done at the Town Hall and the Ritter
a. Town Hall Needs:
i. Mold Remediation
ii. HVAC Repairs
iii. Window Replacement

estimated best case for utility cost for this nearly vacant building is nearly $48,000. Using money from the

a. All departments under one roof
b. Savings on maintenance and utility costs
c. Possible consolidation of services between school/municipal

f. Fire Protection

Follow-up on the PAYT Extra Large Bag, Cost for Heavier grade large bag and Survey Results
===PAGE 5===
Ms. Lemieux reported at the August 8th meeting the Board had requested a residents' survey on potential
changes to the PAYT trash bags which included adding a 40 gallon extra large trash bag, increasing the
thickness of the large bag to 1.75 mil and changing from a twist tie to a drawstring.
She presented the survey results which was a 50-50 split between making no changes (37.47%), other changes
not listed (17.43%) and adding an extra large bag (15.63%), increasing the thickness of the large bag (27.45%)
or both (21.04%). 74% of the respondents would like the bags to be drawstring.
Other items the Board asked for additional information on was the amount of additional plastic these bag
changes would require and how many Waste Zero clients have a bag larger than 30 gallons. An extra large bag
would require 33% more plastic than the current large bag and increase the mil to 1.75 which would be 16.5%
more plastic. Waste Zero has three communities that offer bags larger than 30 gallons. Taunton sells a 35 gallon
bag only, Ayer sells a 15 gallon bag (20% of sales), a 33 gallon bag (54% of sales) and a 40 gallon bag (26% of
sales). Gloucester sells a 15 gallon (15% of sales) and 35 gallon (85% of sales).
Based on the results she recommended adding the extra large bag as a pilot, increasing the thickness of the bag
and making the bags drawstring style. Purchasing the minimum order of 50 cases of extra large bags will allow
us to see the demand for this size bag and the ability to discontinue it after all are sold based on usage. This
would not be included in the Waste Zero contract agreement and would be an additional 4-6 cents charge per
bag if included in the retail distribution program; bringing the cost per bag to $4.04/ $4.06 per bag or $20.20-
$20.30 per roll. Increasing the thickness of the large bags will increase the price by 1 cent per bag and change
the pricing for a roll of bags from $12.75 to $12.80/roll. There is no added cost for changing from twist ties to
drawstring. Ms. Luck asked when we pay trash hauling charges. Ms. Lemieux stated on a monthly basis.
Discussion ensued on the total cost of ordering extra large bags
Motion: R. Ebersole
2"d: P. Bertram
To accept Town Manager's recommendation for the PAYT bags
Vote: Mr. Toale-aye, Mr. Ebersole-aye, Ms. Bertram -aye, Ms. Luck- nay, Mr. McQuaid-aye

for communications lease agreement term lengths. Legal Counsel believed it was unlikely T-Mobile would sign

Mr. Toale opened with the following comment,
. "While not required by law and even discouraged in nearby
towns, it has been the tradition of the Board of Selectmen to encourage public input at our meetings through
Public Comment. Section 1.11 of the Board of Selectmen Policies and Procedures spells out the intentions of
the Board to:
• Strengthen communication with citizens
• Increase citizen participation
• And encourage input into board decisions
These guidelines call for a public comment section of the meeting of no more than 15 minutes with no more
than 3 minutes allowed per speaker, but gives considerable discretion to the Chairman. It has been my practice
to give considerable leeway to those wishing to speak, even allowing questions or brief statements on matters
under consideration during our meetings. When topics brought up during Public Comment appear to require a
lengthily response or detailed discussion, we usually put off this discussion to a future meeting for two reasons:
1. It gives us a chance to review the topic, the facts and gather the appropriate resources to have a
productive discussion, and
2. More importantly, it lets us put it on a posted agenda and publicize this upcoming discussion, so that all
residents that would like to participate in the discussion may attend and be heard.
I hope this makes the role of Public Comment during our meetings clear and I hope many of our residents take
advantage of this opportunity to be heard."

POWERPOINT PRESENTATION: Economic Development
Mr. Burney noted the presentation which is informational and conceptual is to open up the discussion.
Overview: 1) existing documents, 2) items in process, 3) community desires, 4) available resources, 5)
community needs, and 6) next steps. Slide 8, "Community Desires", will be renamed "Community Comments"
for clarification in the event the presentation is posted to the Town website. Mr. Burney noted that the vacant
commercial/industrial land (all privately owned) on Slide 9, encompasses 78.2 acres, of which 19 acres are two
parcels that are more than 80% wetlands. He noted that there are five parcels under earth removal permits
owned within the Industrial District by two landowners which comprise 85.6 acres.
Mr. Rodgers asked what economic development could be obtained in Lunenburg that would not be available in
both Leominster and Fitchburg? Ms. Bertram noted this will be a discussion item. She noted that she felt two
major items drawing individuals to Lunenburg are eco-tourism, e.g., trail system and land and it being a farming
community. Lunenburg also has a strong artist and craft community.
===PAGE 2===
Mr. Ebersole stated Lunenburg has gone back and forth wanting development and wanting to restrict it.
The town did not have zoning until 1960. The town worked against the extension of Interstate 190 through
Leominster into Lunenburg and New Hampshire. Going back to the 1800's there were people working against
the railroad coming through Lunenburg successfully and it only comes through the lower part of town. Route
2A used to be Route 2 and then it was moved south to its current location. When you go back to the 2000s when
Lunenburg was going to install sewer through the entire town, the town rose up and said no we don't want
sewer in the entire town for environmental reasons and this instigated a sewer use needs study. He noted the
Town Charter states the Sewer Commissioners shall in conjunction with other land use bodies, assist in
developing a long range strategic plan for guiding town growth and development. We also need to talk about
the high electric cost that Unitil is charging residents and that makes the difference why someone would move
here versus moving to Leominster, which doesn't have Unitil. He stated he personally supports local businesses
based on cost, service, quality and available services.
The lower right hand corner of town where P. J. Keating is located used to be strictly commercial. Now it is
office park/industrial park with the idea that we didn't want traditional commercial zoning. How far do we want
to have commercial to go? He liked that the Planning Board is reviewing their bylaws to make everything
consistent. The zoning map will be the hot button when it goes to town meeting. It takes a 2/3 majority vote to
change zoning bylaws. We need to address both the zoning and the non-zoning parts.
Mr. McQuaid added everything the Planning Board is doing with the Use Table and the Definitions is a great
step forward. He was afraid of trying to redraw the map; there will be tremendous pushback from the public
when you try to take a residential area and make it commercial. Everything we do as far as making decisions
regarding economic developmen is asking what does that econonic development add to the community. Does it
enhance the public realm or promote walkability? It needs to focus on contextually appropriate uses with
appropriate scale and design. A way to do that without redrawing the zoning map is to allow some things by
special permit in residential areas to get back to the organic neighborhood growth through mixed use that the
pause button has been pressed since the 50's post-war exclusionary zoning. We can identify uses that are
acceptable and appropriate that can go into residential areas; bakers, markets, craftspeople, artists, things of that
nature. Part of that can be done by embracing a progressive view and definition of what a home business is.
Greg Bittner suggested surveying residents to identify the wants and needs of the community. We have many
car dealerships and auto repair shops, but no auto parts store. We should look for development that supports
what we already have; the services that people now go to Leominster and Fitchburg to have. Then we can try to
find appropriate places for those things. He noted physically accessing some commercial properties can be
problematic.
Ms. Bertram agreed but we need to define that framework first. What is it that we are hoping to accomplish?
Personally, I want Lunenburg to maintain the open space that we have and improve and enhance it; then put
together businesses that will complement those opportunities.
Mr. Jeffreys noted if you live south of Massachusetts Avenue, you really have no reason to come to the center
of town unless you want to go to the post office. Depending on where you live determines how you use the
town. Who is coming through our town? We should focus more on services to our existing residents, such as
ecotourism. The first step is to poll the community and find out what they want. Maybe we should look into
solar as a form of revenue.
Mr. Prokowiew stated his background is in marketing and when he looks at the town he sees a bunch of issues.
We have a lack of infrastructure. We have to make it easier for people who are looking for someplace to land.
We lack vision and we need to find what we need to do to assist in this area to grow. There seems to be this
expectation that we are going to come up with the right thing for the right spot and make all these things

Ms. Bertram stated we can guide that vision by determining what type of things we want to encourage.
That is one of the discussions we have to have. I am pushing to support our existing businesses and farms and

market themselves and each other.

Cable Access Committee Request for Board of Selectmen to vote to issue Preliminary Assessment of Non-
Renewal
Chair Tom Alonzo provided an update on their progress. At the last meeting with Comcast on September 7'
we did not reach an agreement. Lunenburg is seeking 5% of Comcast's gross annual revenues as annual
operation support for Lunenburg's local access programming (up from 2.5% in the current license) and
$240,000 in capital grants over the 10 year license term (up from $115,000). Comcast responded with 5% and a
===PAGE 2===
$130,000 capital grant. The current license expired on September 30.
• The committee believes that its present
demand is reasonable, bringing only a modest increase in current subscribers rates; it also believes Comcast's
last offer was not sufficient. Because the license expired, the next step is to secure extra time from the Dept. of
Telecommunications and Cable to continue negotiations. Legal counsel submitted a request for an additional 90
days in which to conclude negotiations or deny Comcast's license application.
If during the 90 day period we reach agreement, the Town can grant a renewal license on the agreed terms. If
during the 90 day period we remain unable to reach agreement, the Town has the option of denying Comcast's
license application. This would trigger an administrative hearing by the Board of Selectmen with witnesses,
subpoenas and discovery. At the conclusion of the hearing, the Selectmen would have to issue a written
decision, granting or denying the license. If at that time their decision remains to deny the linese, Comcast can
appeal to the Dept. of Telecommunications and Cable or court.
Since that letter came, we have received a letter from Dept. of Telecommunications and Cable that the 90 day
extension has been granted. Comcast is being notified of that. Right now we have the gap between what we are
asking and what they are offering. We have been working with Public Access Cable Committee (PACC) on
their business plan which covers their future needs over the next ten year period. We have asked for an
educational channel as well which requires additional equipment.
Ms. Luck asked what the increase is to current Comcast subscriber rates. Mr. Alonzo stated it is currently $2.60
per month per subscriber. If agreed upon, it would go to $5.20 per month per subscriber. The second part is the
capital amount; that would raise the rate from 29 per subscriber per month to .62 per subscriber per month. This
is the first increase in ten years. Discussion ensued on what the increase in subscriber rates would provide: the
educational channel, a mobile unit for PACC, rent for the studio to name a few. Mr. Alonzo stated he will come
back to the board when he has more to report.
Lunenburg Water District Commissioners
Superintendent Fran McNamara noted Chairman Mark Bursch and Commisioner Mark Flagg were also present
tonight concerning questions about their agreement with the Town of Lancaster. He assured those present that
Lunenburg's access to water is their first priority. Everything they have done is to offset the costs to improve
infrastructure and update tanks and wells. Without the Lancaster revenue we are looking at a significant
increase in water rates. Ms. Bertram asked if businesses are exempt from water restrictions. Mr. McNamara
stated yes, we cannot impact the core function of the business, but we can restrict them from watering their
lawn, things like that. Ms. Bertram's concern is we are already seeing water restrictions. If our permit
withdrawal amount is lowered and we are supplying to Lancaster, the increased water usage will go up, isn't
that going to impact Lunenburg? He responded it is two seperate things. Our current permit has expired and we
are in the process of renewing with the state. The idea is we continue on the current permit which allows
540,000 gallons per day. That is an average day per year. Last year we exceeded it by 7000 gallons per day; this
was due to it being a dry year. People need to realize that with new environmental conditions coming down the
road, you are not going to have green grass like you have now. There are going to be significant restrictions on
water use. On the permit we use the residential usage number. We exceeded the 65 gallons limit per capita.
That set off a more restrictive usage on the permit. The new permit review gives Lunenburg an average of
505,000 gallons per day, based on historical use from 2010-2015. There will be a separate segment of the
permit for Lancaster. It is anticipated Lancaster will use 250,000 gallons per day but the actual figure is around
175,000.
Discussion about the rate charged to Lancaster ensued. Mr. Bursch stated Lancaster would be paying the same
rate as Lunenburg residents but they will need to cover their overhead expenses on top of that. There is a
Memorandum of Understanding between the Water District and Lancaster but there is nothing binding at this
time. Mr. Ebersole asked if the two towns would have two separate permits from DEP. McNamara stated it
depends on how it is written. Mr. Ebersole asked if the Water District could restrict selling pool water in order
to reduce usage. McNamara stated in the future, if needed due to more residential development, we could
eliminate it. Question was asked if more development came along, could the permit amount be increased.
McNamara stated yes, but it is not easy.

the School Committee does not intend to utilize the TC Passios, the Brooks House or the old Primary
School for future school building space. The vote by the School Committee to give these buildings to
the Town and the town meeting approval are evidence that the School does not intend to request these
buildings be used for future schools. The Tappe Study Space Needs did include the Superintendent's
Offices but did not include all the district central offices, IT offices, and special programs like School
Linked Services or the ACE program that are currently housed between the TC Passios and the Brooks
House.
• Capital Costs for Turkey Hill Elementary School: Facilities Director John Londa presented a capital
plan at the last two School Committee meetings that included replacement of the roof at Turkey Hill and
major renovations to extend the life of Turkey Hill including window replacement and HVAC upgrades
that Mr. Londa estimates will cost approximately $4M. Mr. Londa estimates the Turkey Hill roof was
put on around 1998 and, given a typical life expectancy of 25 years, is identified for replacement in
FY24 for $1M and if the Town receives reimbursement through the MSBA (approximately 50% of cost
of project), this would push the estimated $4M in major renovations to Turkey Hill out another 10 years
to FY34.
• Updated Space Needs Assessment: The Tappe Space Needs Assessment has been updated and now
includes all the current square footage for all the offices proposed to be in one municipal office building,
including the school offices that were not included in the original Tappe Study. If we do not consolidate
office space, the school administration offices, special program spaces, and IT offices will still require
space. The 2015 LPS Capital Plan designated $15,000 to update space at Turkey Hill for district central
offices. Depending on where district offices and programs are located, the estimated space needs is
between 23,355 SF- 28,560 SF. The original Tappe Study figure was 19,695 SF.
• Comprehensive Building Condition Assessment: The DPW Director and I have met with two firms
that will be providing proposals to do a comprehensive building condition assessment of the Town Hall,

TC Passios, the Brooks House, the Ritter Building, and the old Primary School. The final product
would be an objective look at the current condition of these buildings, what projects are considered
