3. Schedule for CPC review of each department's capital requests:
a. Depending on department availability and readiness of their request, the CPC will schedule
these reviews.
b. To make most efficient use of these meetings, reviews requests from larger departments
(e.g. DPW, Police/Fire, facilities) may be paired with reviews of requests from smaller
departments (e.g. library, senior center.)
c. Per Kerry S. review of the requests, there are no outstanding questions on the Police or
the Fire Department capital requests, so they are ready to be reviewed. Accordingly Jamie
is going to try to schedule the CPC review of the Police and Fire Departments requests at
the November 4 meeting.
4. Pavement Management Plan, presented by Jack Rodriquenz.
a. Jack noted that, at an estimated cost of $70,000,000 Town roads are the highest cost
asset the Town has. Maintenance of this asset is important.
b. He then reviewed a comprehensive list of roads requiring maintenance sorted by priority
from more important to less important.
c. It would require an expenditure of about $600,000 per year to maintain the overall quality
of the Town's roads at current levels. Note that in each year this refers to overall road
quality and not the quality of specific individual roads in town.
d. It would require annual expenditures of about $1,200,000 to significantly improve the
quality of the Town's roads.
e. The major constraint continues to be the amount of money the Town can afford to invest
in road maintenance.
f. Sources of funds are:
i. Chapter 90 funds from the state-which in FY2015 is about $400,000.
ii. Over the past three years Jack stated the Town has invested an average of roughly
$100,000 per year.

5.  Sewer Commission Computer Request as part of the Technology Capital Requests.  
a.  Background:  Technology capital projects include a $900 computer requested by the Sewer 
Commission. However, the commission has stated their intent to pay for all their capital 
requirements from the Sewer Enterprise Fund.  Discussion centered on whether the Town’s 
technology capital equipment request may include a request for a computer for the Sewer 
Commission.  If so, should the Town pay for the computer for the Sewer Commission?   
b.  Resolution:  After discussion, John moved that “The Sewer Commission could include any of their 
requests for technology equipment purchases in the Technology capital request plan.  However, the 
Sewer Commission will reimburse the Town for the cost of these purchases upon delivery to the 
commission.”  Dennis seconded the motion.  The Capital Planning Committee voted 4-0 in favor of 
this motion.  Note:  Heather S. had left the meeting by this time. 
c.  Jamie will write a letter to the Sewer Commission informing them of the decision taken by the CPC.  
  
6.  Discussion of Building re-use issues in advance of the December 2 meeting. 
a.  Jamie summarized the issues that will come before this committee at the next meeting regarding 
Town Hall.  These issues include painting Town Hall (at least the front of the building), and 
commissioning a study to determine the options, costs, and practicalities of renovation/re-use of 
Town Hall. 
 
7.  The agenda for the December 2 meeting was approved and John will send the approved agenda to the 
Town Clerk to post.

4-0 to adjourn at 6:46 pm.

1. Meeting called to order by Jamie Toale at 4:31 pm.
2. No public comment.
3. Discussion of what constitutes a capital project-main points:
a. The Mass. Department of Revenue Div. of Local Services defines capital assets as "all tangible
property used in the operation of government", and that definition defines "capital projects" in the
Town's financial policy document. The issue is whether items like a hazardous tree survey, a
building re-use study, or an intersection re-configuration plan should be included in the capital
improvement plan or as part of the operating budget.
b. They have attributes of either:
i. Each is one-time, non-recurring expense, dissimilar to other operating budget items.
il. None of the three is a tangible good, unlike other capital assets.
c. One idea is that expenses like these are non-recurring operating expenses.
4. The committee approved the minutes of the Tuesday, December 2 CPC meeting as submitted, 4-0.
5. Capital Improvement Project Priorities: The CPC reviewed all requested capital projects and assigned each

1. Meeting called to order by Jamie Toale at 4:31 pm.
2. No public comment.
3. Tour of TH Middle School to better understand the asbestos abatement situation there.
a. John Londa and Loxi Calmes showed the CPC the asbestos situation at THMS. Specifics include:
• Areas where ceiling tiles had become dislodged causing gaps between the tiles and presenting a
path for asbestos particulate to potentially drop down into hallways and/or classroom spaces.
• The asbestos over the ceilings is the coating of the steel beams supporting the roof.
• A few worn floor tiles in high traffic areas (for instance in corners where two hallways meet)
which exposed a layer of asbestos under the tiles' surface.
• Runs of some cable for communication that were to have been over the ceiling, but are now
running along the corner formed by the intersection of the ceiling and the hallway wall. The
cable runs could not be hidden over the ceiling tiles because of the presence of asbestos.
b. Loxi showed us the area where her office is proposed to be at THMS and John and Loxi showed us
the paging system they propose replacing as well as the area next to the computer lab they
propose making into a garage for vehicle maintenance.
c. Loxi also explained that as part of the renovation of THMS to become an upper-elementary school
the windows will have to be replaced. In response to a question Dennis asked about whether the
caulk around the windows contained asbestos, it turned out that the caulk does and that during the
renovation phase there would have to be another round of asbestos abatement to deal with that
issue.
