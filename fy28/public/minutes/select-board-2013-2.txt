3/5/13

MAR 1 3 2013
LUNENBURG TOWN
CLERK OFFICE
The Board of Selectmen met in the Joseph F. Pilotta meeting room at Town Hall as scheduled with Dave
Matthews, Tom Alonzo, Carl (Ernie)Sund, Paula Bertram and Steven M. deBettencourt
Absent was Town Manager Kerry Speidel.
Regular Meeting opened at 7:00 PM
7:00PM PUBLIC COMMENT
Mr. Matthews began the meeting by requesting from the public a picture of Lunenburg be submitted for the cover
of the town report. The photo should be sent to Linda Douglas at ldouglas@lunenburgonline.com.
Mr. Alonzo started the public discussion from the board by congratulating the Boys and Girls Club for their
successful fundraiser titled "Are You Smarter than a Sixth Grader" the previous Friday night.
Mr. Sund stated that Joanne McQuiad from the Green Communities Task Force has negotiated for paper and
cardboard recycling bins to be available to the public at each of the four Lunenburg Public School buildings
receiving areas. The Director of Facilities John Londa expects the schools to recycle about 15 tons of paper and
cardboard which will generate a small revenue of about $120.00 for the town. KLT Industries pays the town $8.00
per ton and the proceeds go into the Town's general fund.
Mr. Sund continued his public comments by announcing he will not be seeking another term as a member of the
BOS. Mr. Sund stated that he enjoyed his time serving the town since 1987 and that he will miss the people and
that the town of Lunenburg has some of the most dedicated employees.
Mr. Matthews thanked Mr. Sund for his term on the BOS. Those sentiments were echoed by Mr. Alonzo, Mrs.
Bertram and Mr. deBettencourt.
Mr: deBettencourt continued the public comments from the Board and also announced that he will not be seeking
another term on the BOS. Mr. deBettencourt thanked the people of Lunenburg for their support during his 22
years on the board.
Mr: Matthews thanked Mr. deBettencourt for his time and service on the BOS. Mrs. Bertram, Mr. Alonzo and
Mr. Sund echoed the sentiments of Mr. Matthews.
Mr. Matthews reminded the citizens of Lunenburg that there was a Town Caucus Monday, March 11th and the
election for the two empty seats on the BOS would be held on April 30th.
Rob Bowen spoke thanking Mr. deBettencourt and Mr. Sund for their time on the BOS. Mr. Bowen also
congratulated the Lunenburg/Leominster High School Girls combined ski team for winning the State
Championship.
APPOINTMENTS
7:00 p.m. - Common Victualer License for Bangkok Hill Restaurant- Mr. Rob Bowen spoke on behalf
of the Bangkok Hill Restaurant stating that although they are not in possession of a liquor license, Bangkok Hill
===PAGE 2===
still intends to go forward with their purchase and sale agreement so that they could still serve food.
Mr. Matthews asked Mr. Bowen if Bangkok Hill had obtained a Food Permit through the Board of Health.
Mr. Bowen responded by saying that he already had a food permit and provided a copy of it to the BOS.
Mr. Alonzo questioned the differences between the new owner and the old owner of Bangkok Hill.

Mr. Sund seconded. On vote motion carried, 5 in favor, 0 opposed.
7:15 PM- Request for Permit to Ride on Whalom Lake- Police Chief Marino spoke to say that there was
confusion about the Bylaw that anyone can apply for a permit to ride on Whalom Lake and that the BOS can issue
permits. The original intent of the Bylaw was to help control the ice races that were once held on the lake with
cars on Sundays. He also mentioned that they did not want you to be able to enter the lake unless it was from the
Leominster side.
The problem is that if you allow individual people to ride snowmobiles or any other vehicles on the lake the Town
has to be made aware if the ice is safe because since they issue the permits they are liable for any accidents.
Currently no one reports on if the ice is safe or not.
Mr. Matthews asked Chief Marino if the preference was to not issue permits to individuals because the permits
have never really been enforced.
Chief Marino responded by saying that they have enforced the permit rule in the past, including within the past
few weeks, but only if they have the manpower to enforce it on duty. Ultimately the concern is that people will
fall through the ice if the ice is not safe.
Ms. Bertram stated that as a member of a local snowmobile club she would question why the Bylaw exists. She
understood the point about groups of people on a lake requiring a permit but she questions why the Town of
Lunenburg regulates just the one body of water at Lake Whalom and not any of the other lakes in the town.
Chief Marino stated that the Town does have a Bylaw concerning Lake Shirley as well.
Mrs. Bertram reiterated her questioning of a Bylaw that regulates access to the lakes of Lunenburg by residents
who live on the lakes in question.
Chief Marino stated that it is difficult for the law to be enforced. What concerns him is the safety of the ice, there
is no good way to judge the safety of the ice or inform the citizens of the town of the safety of the ice, and that the
original intent of the Bylaw was to regulate the ice racing activity.
Mr. Alonzo stated that he would personally check with Town Counsel to see if they could either cease issuing
individual permits or add language to the permit stating that the Town is not liable for any accidents that happen
on the Town's lakes in regards to the safety of the ice.
Mrs. Bertam agreed with Mr. Alonzo that the liability of the Town when issuing a permit is a concern and that the
town of Lunenburg should not be regulating what the residents of the Town does on their lakes.
Mr. Sund stated that his concern was if an accident did happen and a life was lost or the Town had to do a rescue
and recovery mission who would be responsible for paying for that.
Mrs. Bertram stated that the responsibility fell on the owner in those situations.
Mr. Matthews stated that we don't have a history of major issues with the activities on the lakes and that the
Town does not employ any experts on ice so we have to defer to the residents of the Town participating in the
activities using common sense. Ultimately we need to update or eliminate the Bylaw so that we don't have
liability if any accidents happen.
===PAGE 3===
Mr. Alonzo stated that he isn't necessarily in favor of removing the Bylaw but that he would be in favor of
removing the special permit for use of the ice.
Mr. deBettencourt stated that the last time this was an issue was in 1991.
Ms. Bertram stated that it was time to revisit rewriting the Bylaw by consulting Town Counsel and addressing it
at Town Meeting in May but that the Bylaw should not be enforced until the issue was revisited.
CURRENT BUSINESS
1. FY2014 Budget Discussion - Ms. Bertram stated that she had many questions about specific line items
but would e-mail them directly to the Town Manager and the Finance Committee. Ms. Bertram asked Mr. Sund
why the purchase of a new street sweeper was left out of the Capital Plan request. She said that the DPW Director
has concerns about the current machine running for another year.
Mr. Sund replied that the Capital Planning Committee had worked out a figure of approximately $500k for
Capital Planning and that adding in a new street sweeper was not going to work out financially. If one was
purchased it could potentially drive the Capital Plan up to $700k. The Capital Planning Committee inspected the
current street sweeper and tound that it had low mileage, low usage and there was an opportunity to outsource
street sweeping opposed to purchasing a new street sweeper at the possible cost of $185,000.00.
Mrs. Bertram asked Mir. Sund if they had quotes or looked at other Towns with street sweepers.
Mir. Sund responded that he had looked at other Towns and the initial assessment was that outsourcing the
problem would be the best choice financially. He mentioned that getting quotes on expenses was the
responsibility of the DPW Director.
Mr. Matthews stated that the time for the citizens of the Town to come forth with issues and concerns about the
budget was at the upcoming Finance Committee meeting. Mr. Matthews also said that the budget was a balanced
budget as enforced by law.
Ms. Bertram stated that she wanted to recognize Fire Chief Sullivan's creative thinking on ways to offset costs at
the Public Safety Building and for his ideas on Fire Fighter Education.

Mr. Matthews began the meeting thanking everyone who turned out for the town caucus the previous
Monday. Mr. Matthews stated that some open positions did have candidates nominated to fill them while
others still needed candidates to step up and take them on. Nomination papers for interested candidates could
be picked up at town hall. Open positions include Board of Selectman, School Committee, Sewer

7:00 p.m. - State Senator Jennifer Flanagan, State Representative Jennifer Benson and State
Representative Stephen DiNatale - Senator Flanagan started by stating after redistricting went into
effect Representative DiNatale now represents portions of Lunenburg whereas before it was just herself and
Representative Benson. The Senator continued by saying she understood that the BOS wanted to talk about
the state budget but currently there is no Local Aid Resolution approved and there is a $2 billion tax package
attached to the Governor's budget which has not yet been voted on. The House of Representatives will be
voting on the budget the week after April vacation and the budget would move from the House to a Senate
vote sometime around Memorial Day.
Mr. Matthews stated that Town Manager has issued a preliminary town budget that mirrored the same budget
from the previous fiscal year opposed to one that mirrored Governor Patrick's proposed budget this year as a
safety precaution.
Representative DiNatale spoke thanking the BOS for the invitation to the meeting. Representative DiNatale
stated that he understood all the questions and issues the BOS had regarding Chapter 70 Funding, Chapter 90
and the Paper Form.
Representative Benson spoke and said that she currently sits on Health Care and Finance, Public Service,
Telecommunications, Public Utilities and Energy Committees. She also mentioned that she is chairing the
Special Education and Disabilities Caucus.
Representative DiNatale stated that he was sitting on the Higher Education, Telecommunications, Public
Utilities Public Service, and the Economic Development and Emerging Technologies Committees.
Mr. Alonzo asked both Representatives if the Municipal Electric Choice Bill had been re-filed for this year.
Representative DiNatale replied that he had filed the Municipal Electric Choice Bill.
Mr. Alonzo said that his support for the Municipal Electric Choice Bill has not waned at all despite the
difficulties in having it passed.
Representative Benson stated that she is co-sponsoring the Municipal Electric Choice Bill and that the recent
storms that have caused utility problems have been helpful in pushing that legislation.
Mr. Matthews stated that the Town Manager had put together a packet for the delegation to look at with
specific issues and causes the Town had specific interest in. He specifically outlined that the Town is
receiving lower State Aid for FY 12 and FY13 then they did in FY10 and this is an issue for the Town. Mr.
Matthews continued by stating if Chapter 70 was more in line with the municipalities needs then there
wouldn't be such a massive gap in funding between the Towns needs and what the State offers.
===PAGE 2===
Representative Benson spoke specifically to Chapter 70 and stated that Special Education was calculated into
the Chapter 70 funding formula. She said that the formula might be off today compared to when it was
originally drafted, but that there are assumptions in the Chapter 70 formula for in-house special education. In
the Governor's budget there is a push to get municipalities to provide their full local contribution number for
Chapter 70 in a faster manner and that 1/3 of Municipalities in the state are not providing their full
contribution and that 2/3 of Municipalities are paying too much.
Mr. Matthews stated that he does recognize there was additional Chapter 70 funding in the past year that
helped out the school district but that more funding is still needed.
Senator Flanagan said that we should keep in mind where the State was a few years ago and where it is
today. Massachusetts is one of only three states that has over $1 billion in its stabilization fund and that this
has helped lead the state to its highest ever bond rating. Reforms to State departments and budgets have been
ongoing to help streamline and minimize government and this has resulted in 6,000 less State jobs but has
also resulted in a higher demand for government services by citizens of the Commonwealth. Chapter 90
specifically addresses transportation needs of roads and bridges but the State has not received the proper
funding from the federal government for such projects and they are trying to avoid asking citizens for more
money in taxes, but that the current proposal put forth in the Governor's budget doubles the amount of
funding in Chapter 90 and is a viable option that is currently being discussed by the State Legislators and by
their constituents.
Senator Flanagan finished by saying they had to balance the needs of many constituencies and she
appreciated the input from the BOS on all issues but that no firm numbers could be provided as of yet
regarding the budget for the current fiscal year.

budget. Representative DiNatale personally views any tax increases as a pay cut to his constituents.
Mr. Sund requested that Representative Benson provide an update on the gasoline tax proposed in the
Governor's budget.
Representative Benson responded by saying there are proposals to change the gasoline tax but that nothing is
set in stone. The Governor's budget presents one solution to increase funds to transportation but it wouldn't
solve the entire problem..

that they needed to voice their support for particular tax increases if they wanted increased funding.
Mr. Matthews stated that budgetary reforms could be tighter in his opinion. Mr. Matthews continued by
saying that the Regional Dispatch is ready to go ahead but they needed Legislation in the State House to go
Representative Benson responded by saying that she put forth Legislation for the Regional Dispatch but that

and supporting it.

7:30 pm- Conservation Commission, Pleasant Street Request for Proposals - Representatives
from the Conservation Commission, Rob Verge, and Sharon Jordan attended the meeting to discuss with the
BOS a proposal from the Conservation Commission that involved accessing Conservation land on Pleasant
Street to remove some gravel and then sell the gravel to generate funds for future land purchases that the
commission and the town are interested in. Mr. Verge explained that the State has a lot of programs that can
help in such land acquisitions but that they require the municipalities to match funds in many of these
programs. Mr. Verge also noted that the project would include a few different aspects to it including some of
the gravel taken from the property being donated to the town to help sand the roads in the wintertime,
residents who live in the development at Tri-Town would be given a better access route into the conservation
land where the gravel would be removed from, and that the Commission would also like to add a handicap
access trail in the Conservation area. The land sits under Article 97 which labels the property as for "Passive
Use Only".
Four steps must be taken before the changes to the land can be made and they are as follows:
1. Conservation Commission must approve the project.
2. Coming in front of the BOS to see if they are interested in approving the project.
3. If BOS approve, the project must go to a town vote.
4. If the town approves the project, it has to be approved in a vote by the House of Representatives and the
State Senate in Boston.
Once the project is finished the land will go back to being labeled as for "Passive Use Only".
Ms. Jordan added that the property on Pleasant Street is under the care of the Conservation Commission but
that over the years the land and trails had been subject to a number of illegal and dangerous uses. The
property and the trails are heavily damaged and in need of major repairs. Ms. Jordan said that she would like
to see the trails restored, restoration of some of the wildlife habitats, as well as further access to the land for
people with disabilities as this is a requirement for Town properties.
Mr. Matthews stated that he believed it was a win-win situation all around.
Mr. Sund questioned if the current access to the Conservation land was locked off at the Tri-Town entrance
by a gate and which entrances to the land would the gravel and fill be removed from.
Mr. Verge stated that he did not believe that had been discussed by the Conservation Commission yet but
that Pleasant Street was an option as well as some residents who would be willing to allow access to the land
through their property.
Ms. Jordan stated that this decision would have to be included by any respondent to the RFP.
Mr. Sund repeated his question regarding to the access to the land at the Tri-Town side where the current
locked gate resides.
Ms. Jordan responded by saying that she was not expecting to maintain access to the land during the
renovations to the land.
Mr. Sund stated he was confused because he thought the project included access for the people at Tri-Town
and how it would be arranged.
Ms. Jordan stated that in the long term there would be access to for the Tri-Town residents.
Ms. Bertram echoed the chairman's comments that this is a "win-win" project. Ms. Bertram continued by
asking where the 30% re-grading of trails would fit into the plan to revive the Conservation land's trails.
Ms. Jordan stated that about 1/3 of the trails are impassable and it would be those trails that would be re-
graded.
Ms. Bertram pointed out that she did not see this specification in the RFP and she encouraged the
Conservation Commission to include it in the RFP.
Mr. Alonzo asked why the special legislation was needed for the Conservation land to be worked on.
Mr. Verge responded by saying that whenever land goes into Conservation and the Town receives any
money from the State for Conservation, the land goes under Article 97 so that it remains Conservation land
and isn't changed later on for soccer fields, schools, etc. Ultimately the purpose of Article 97 is for protection
of the land.
===PAGE 4===
Ms. Bertram asked how the Commission planned to place the land back under Article 97 for Conservation
land after the renovations were finished.
Mr. Verge said that when the Legislation was presented on Beacon Hill, it would be required in the
Legislation to revert back to Article 97 land.
Currently there is no time frame ready because they needed to get the Legislation through the State House
first.
Mr. deBettencourt said that the Town landfill would also have to be taken into consideration to get to this
piece of Conservation land.
Mr. Verge responded by saying that the Town landfill would not alter any plans and would not be touched in
any of the restorations to the Conservation land.
Mir. deBettencourt stated there needs to be a plan to recap the town landfill embankments after the gravel was
removed. Additionally Mr. deBettencourt requested that they have a Performance Bond for the contractor
that works the land in the end
Ms. Speidel asked at which point the Commission would request proposals from contractors.
Ms. Jordan responded by saying after all four steps of approval mentioned earlier were completed.
Mr. Matthews reiterated Mr. deBettencourt's concern about the town landfill and the traffic on Pleasant
Street along with the Performance Bond.
Ms. Bertram asked if the Conservation Commission wanted this project to go to vote at the 2013 town
meeting.
Ms. Jordan and Mr. Verge stated that they would like to see it go to vote at the 2013 town meeting.
Ms. Bertram motioned to support the Conservation Commissions proposal to go to town meeting. Mr.
Alonzo seconded the motion. On vote motion carried, 5 in favor, 0 opposed.

7:00 p.m. - Open Space Committee, Right-to-Farm Bylaw. Marion Benson, Planning Director for the
Town of Lunenburg, spoke to the BOS and stated that this was a part of the Town's Master Plan as an Open Space
Element. This has been discussed and developed by the Planning Board and the Conservation Commission and was
approved by the State. Ms. Benson and Ms. Jan Carrier from the Conservation Committee held a meeting, with

participants. Ms. Benson stated that she is in support of all of the farmer's requests.
Jim Lattanzi, Hollis Hills Farm, spoke on behalf of the farmers in Town. Mr. Lattanzi stated that the agricultural
community in Lunenburg was invited to a meeting to discuss their needs and desires a little over one year ago by
the Planning Board and the Conservation Commission. Mr. Lattanzi said that he has been fortunate enough as a
first generation farmer to work with enough community, State and non-profit groups to realize that Lunenburg is an
agricultural gem. Part of what farmers in the town want is some form of official representation to help promote
agriculture and agricultural business in the town. Mr. Lattanzi stated that many towns have an Agricultural
Commission made up of a majority of members who are involved with the agricultural industry but also containing
members who may have an interest in agriculture but are not directly involved in the industry. Mr. Lattanzi stated
that Lunenburg is the largest and most agriculturally diverse community in Worcester County. Mr. Lattanzi told the
BOS that what the farmers have done is submitted to Article 4 of the Town Warrant to officially form an
Agricultural Commission and gathered the necessary signatures to do so. Mr. Lattanzi stated that the commission
has the backing of the Planning Board and that the farmers were seeking official support from the BOS.
Mr. Alonzo asked Mir. Lattanzi to describe whant an Agricultural Commission would do.
Mr. Lattanzi said the mission of an Agricultural Commission revolves around education, protecting and preserving
the land, promoting agricultural business and to work with the community. Mr. Lattanzi outlined some of the
Commissions responsibilities as educating the community about what is available to them as well as what the
mechanics are behind running and operating an agricultural business, ácting as an avenue for mediation and
reconciliation for issues or concerns that arise in the agricultural community, promoting farmers markets and
advocating to protect the agricultural land that the Town has. Mr. Lattanzi stated that there were a number of APR
Farms in town, farms that the State has purchased the development rights on to permanently restrict the land to
agriculture use alone. There are other properties in the town that wish to be APR properties and the Agricultural
Commission would advocate on behalf of these properties. Furthermore, Mr. Lattanzi stated that if the Town were
• looking for funding or special grants to help with the agricultural industry in town, the State would recognize
Lunenburg above other communities if it were to have an Agricultural Commission.
Mr. Alonzo mentioned that a prior member of the BOS had advocated on behalf of an Agricultural Commission but
that support for the idea never caught on. Mr. Alonzo thanked Mr. Lattanzi and the farmers for pushing for an
Agricultural Commission and stated his support for the commission.
Mr. Lattanzi stated he would be speaking to this Article at Town Meeting.
Ms. Speidel stated that she talked to Mr. Lattanzi earlier about fixing some of the language in the Warrant to make
the Commission part of the Town ByLaws. Ms. Speidel stated that the issue was a technicality but since it was a
Petition Article she could not personally change the language without the permission of the Farmer's.

citizen or one of the Unitil workers reports that a light is out. Ms. Speidel stated that Unitil told her that they don't

into 5 separate groups and every year 1/5 of the lights they own are up for maintenance. The bulbs Unitil uses have
a life expectancy is between 5-7 years. Ms. Speidel noted that Unitil's maintenance plan seems to be inadequate.
The town pays $56,000.00 per year for street lights. There are 75 communities in the Commonwealth that have
purchased the street lights from the utility company and they hire an independent contractor to maintenance the
lights. The average savings for a Municipality purchasing their own lights and hiring a contractor to maintain the
lights equals a savings between 40-75%. Ms. Speidel spoke to a consultant who said he would provide a free
preliminary analysis of whether or not the Town should purchase their own street lights.

7:40 p.m. - School Building Committee, CM at Risk: Project Time Table, Related Matters: Jeff
Luxenberg and Greg Cohen representing Joslin, Lesser and Assoc., stated that they were attending the
meeting tonight to give the BOS an update on the school building project and the CM at Risk method of
construction in regards to the new school building.
Mr. Luxenberg began by stating the CM at Risk delivery method was part of the 2004 Construction
Reform. CM at Risk is an approach where the Town submits an application to the Office of the Inspector
General, with the assistance of Joslin, Lesser and Assoc, if the Town requests it.
If the town requests the assistance of the firm there is a certification attached to the application that the
Town's Legal Counsel must approve on behalf of the BOS or School Building Committee before the firm
can submit the CM at Risk application to the Inspector General. Mr. Luxenberg stated that the traditional
approach is called Design Bid Build which is a linear process where a building is designed, put out for
bid, and then the building is built. Under Design Bid Build the owner, with the help of the owner's
Project Manager, Architect and School Building Committee reviews the design, advertises the design
publicly, and then people can come in to review and bid on them.
Two weeks before general bids are due, filed sub-bids that take up about half of the project and include 16
various trades including electric, plumbing, etc. will be tabulated and sent out to those who have bid on
the design.
Two weeks after that the general contractor takes those bids, includes the ones that they want in their bid,
and submits those bids to the Town. Whoever the lowest bidder is the Town is required to select that
general contractor so long as they have a certification from the Division of Capital Asset Management
and Maintenance and are pre-qualified.
Mr. Luxenberg stated that this process works well if you get a good general contractor and does not go
well if you do not get a good general contractor. There are often scope changes from unforeseen events
that will increase the final cost of the building, however under the Design Bid Build process the general
contractor will often look for holes in the contract to increase their funds, regardless of the initial bid put
The concept of CM at Risk came out of the 2004 Construction Reform Law and the idea is to bring in a
Construction Manager carly during the design process of the job. This position will be similar to a
general contractor, but will help review the drawings and cost estimating so that when the review is
finally done they cannot tack on additional costs if unforeseen circumstances increase the cost of the
project after building begins.
First a Construction Manager will be selected based upon qualifications before the building is fully
designed and they provide a fee for what they would charge for overhead and profit. Then they give you
an estimate on what they would charge for general conditions or general requirements.
There is a Selection Committee which consists of a representative(s) of the Building Committee, the
Architect and the Owners Project Manager. The Committee will oversee two phases, the request for
===PAGE 3===
qualifications and the request for proposals. In the qualifications the Committee must make sure the
building firm is qualified for the job, and those that are qualified are able to send a proposal.
In the proposal there is a section for a fee and a non-fee section. The Committee reviews the non-fee
proposal first and then the fee proposal, combines the two proposals together, and selects a proposal that
best suits the job, but isn't necessarily the least expensive proposal.
The CM at Risk method then uses an "Open Book Process"
", which means the Construction Manager will
review with the Town all the costs of building and negotiate a Guaranteed Maximum Price (GMP). The
GMP then becomes the limit, where if the final cost of the project comes in less than the GMP, the money
goes back to the Town.
If the price of the project exceeds the GMP, the over costs are the responsibility of the firm. All of the
construction numbers are audited and submitted to the MSBA. There will be Change Orders added onto
the GMP to cover changes in site conditions, etc., but room for a Construction Manager to tack on extra
costs at the end is severely diminished.
The advantages of going the route of the CM at Risk method include: the MSBA will provide a 1%
additional reimbursement it you use the CM approach, contractor is able to be selected based on
qualifications and the overall fee, the Town is able to proceed with early phases of the building while still
finishing the design of the building, minimize disruption to students by giving advanced warning on how
the construction of the building will proceed, the Construction Manager will have advanced knowledge of
who the trade contractors will be and will be able to develop a better relationship with them.
The Construction Manager sits with the town and de-scopes non-trade contracts, and if a GMP can't be
agreed upon the Town can go back and change to the General Contracting approach.
Some of the down sides to the CM at Risk method include: less competition from non-trade contractors,
up front pre-construction costs that could cost the Town .5%, coordination issues with the early release
packages, the GMP is not executed until after initial construction begins, which limits the Towns options,
and first day hiring cost would be slightly higher.
Ultimately, Joslin, Lesser and Assoc. recommend the CM at Risk process to build the new school
building and is looking for the BOS concurrence.
Mr. deBettencourt stated that past construction projects that used the General Contractor method has
produced many problems because the Town was unable to pre-qualify contractors. He stated that he likes
the pre-qualification method used in CM at Risk and prefers the CM at Risk method.
Mr. Alonzo also stated that he believes the CM at Risk method is better than the General Contractor
method but asked if the CM at Risk bids still require the town to choose the lowest bidder.
Mr. Luxenberg stated that that the Town would have to choose the lowest bidder but the Town would be
able to prequalify them. This only affects the trade contractor and not the non-trade contracts.
Ms. Bertram agreed that the overall concept of CM at Risk makes sense but questioned the funding
component of starting to build the building before agreeing on a GMP.
Mr. Luxenberg stated that the firm was trying to get someone on board with the project early so that they
can review the cost. The 1% that the MSBA will return to the Town for choosing the CM at Risk project
should cover all the costs of any early building projects.
Ms. Bertram asked from a timeline perspective when the project could start.
Mr. Luxenberg stated that on August 8th the firm would be submitting the schematic design submission
to the MSBA and a cost estimate would be needed between 2-4 weeks before the schematic design
submission. The MSBA will then approve the project in a "Scope and Budget Agreement" on October
2nd. Local approval would be needed after MSBA approval and once local approval was had then the
===PAGE 4===
project would be put into a design process that would take one year. By October 2014 the building would
be fully designed with the possibility of some construction having started in the summer of 2014 but with

Ms. Speidel stated that invitations were sent to all of the abutting properties to the project explaining what was happening

Ms. Domigan presented the BOS and the public with a brief history of the Summer Street Project and explained the
relationship between the project and the communities of Leominster, Fitchburg and Lunenburg. She stated that she was at
the meeting tonight asking for consensus on one of the concepts. Ms. Domigan continued by explain that there were 3
Concepts which the communities could choose from for the project.
Concept A involved reducing the size of the intersection on Summer Street in Lunenburg for better geometry, introduce a
turn lane, and potentially remove pavement to replace with a grass strip down the center of the road.
Concept B would include a center turn lane which would start in Fitchburg on John Fitch Highway and would change the
roadway section to 47 feet.
Concept C would be just for Fitchburg and it would look at putting in a center median, but just in the city of Fitchburg.
This concept would not really work due to the surrounding neighborhood. The city acknowledges this and will not move
ahead with Concept C.
Ms. Domigan stated that Concept B, with the center turn lane, was the best idea and she was seeking consensus from the
BOS in support of Concept B.
Ms. Bertram questioned how large the shoulders of the road would be.
Ms. Domigan responded that the shoulders would be five feet, as per MassDOT regulations.
