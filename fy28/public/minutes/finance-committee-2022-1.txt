Lunenburg Finance Committee 
Meeting Minutes 
Thursday, January 13, 2022 
 
Location: In-person at Lunenburg Town Hall and broadcast on Zoom 
 
Present: Chairperson Peter Beardmore, Chris Menard, Even Watters, Thomas Balish, Jay Simeone via 
Zoom, Dave Passios via Zoom (for a portion of the meeting) 
 
Guests: Town Manager Heather Lemieux, State Senator John Cronin, State Representatives Mike 
Kushmerek, and Dan Sena, School Committee Chairperson Carol Archambault. 
1. Call to Order 
Peter Beardmore called the meeting to order at 7:00 p.m. and read a statement 
concerning the Open Meeting Law, and how all public meetings are legally being held 
remotely. 
This meeting was recorded and is viewable on YouTube at https://youtu.be/TMmBa6ueRGI 
2. Pledge of Allegiance 
 
3. Announcements: Mr. Beardmore informed the committee that Massachusetts Municipal 
Association of Finance Committees would be held remotely, and the fee to attend has been 
waived.  
 
4. Public Comment: Ms. Archambault thanked Senator Cronin, and State Representatives 
Kushmerek and Sena for attending the meeting, and highlighted that the Student Opportunity 
Act has created funding challenges for towns like Lunenburg.  
Mr. Beardmore expressed his concern about the town’s capital plan, and pointed out that last 
year the town spent more available funds on capital than ever before, and none of the capital 
plan funded any school projects, and that this year’s plan intends to spend even more, but lists 
school projects as numbers 12, 14, and 27 on the list of priorities. Mr. Beardmore also drew 
attention to the fact that meetings of the Capital Planning Committee have not been recorded, 
and minutes have not been made available.  
 
5. Town Manager Legislative Budget Priorities Requests: Ms. Lemieux explained that she and the 
Select Board discussed last year’s legislative budget requests on January 4, 2022. The town 
requested $127,000 for a fire alarm upgrade at the Lunenburg Primary School, and received 
$25,000, and requested $40,000 for LED lighting at the library, and received $40,000 from the 
state. $180,000 for firefighter turnout gear was funded by the FY22 capital plan and the fire 
department’s annual budget, and $355,000 was requested for hazardous material abatement at 
the Old Primary School.  Ms. Lemieux requested the Select Board go for projects not funded by 
bond bills, such as $330,000 for the Flat Hill Rd culvert, $371,000 (updated cost) for hazardous 
material abatement at the Old Primary School, $300,000 for 955 Massachusetts Ave (former gas 
station), $100,000 for improvements at the senior center, $250,000 for a commercial kitchen, 
and $450,000 for street scape projects.  Ms. Lemieux said the Select Board asked the Parks 
Department if they have upcoming costs to include in the FY23 capital plan, which could also be 
===PAGE 2===
covered by ARPA funds. Ms. Lemieux has hired a consultant to wade through the possible 
stipulations concerning local ARPA funding.  
 
6. Discussion with Legislative Delegation: Mr. Beardmore read a letter he wrote to members of 
the legislative delegation listing the finance committee’s major concerns including: Chapter 70 
funding, ARPA funds, the cost of the Police Reform Act, reporting requirements, records 
retention, and municipal building projects. Senator Cronin drew attention to neighboring 
communities that do benefit from Chapter 70, but also acknowledged that Lunenburg faces its 
own challenges with the program. Senator Cronin suggested Lunenburg find a way to grow its 
base budget. Senator Cronin said he could advocate for the town when it comes to per pupil 
reimbursement. On ARPA funding, Senator Cronin said the town would have a lot of flexibility in 
spending the $3 million, and suggested the town appoint a committee to recommend how those 
funds should be spent.  
Senator Cronin also commented on the Police Reform Act, and said he’d be happy to advocate 
that the 800 hour training mandate be funded by the state.  
Representative Kushmerek agreed with Mr. Beardmore’s letter that the Student Opportunity 
Act did fall short for Lunenburg. Rep. Kushmerek informed the committee that there is a caucus 
of rural districts that are facing the same types of challenges, and questioned whether there 
would be enough of those districts to make some noise. 
Representative Kushmerek pointed out that ARPA funds do need to be appropriated by 2024 
but don’t have to be spent until 2026, and said that it would be tough to find projects that aren’t 
eligible for the funds.  
On the Police Reform Act, Rep. Kushmerek pointed out that the police force would be phasing in 
their 800 hours of training over three years, but also acknowledged the challenge the 
department faces when it comes to retaining their officers after the training is complete.  
On municipal buildings, Rep. Kushmerek addressed projects happening in surrounding 
communities, and informed the committee that those projects are either tax-payer funded, or 
have been funded by private investors, which was made possible via incentives such as tax 
credits to those investors. Rep. Kushmerek pointed out that relying on tax credit programs can 
take decades before a shovel hits the ground.  
Representative Sena suggested that Chapter 70 has helped the state, as a whole, and that the 
formula for how funds are distributed needed to be updated, but that it could also be modified 
to assist towns like Lunenburg. Rep. Sena also said the town has to get creative when it comes 
to affording education, and mentioned the discussion to consider an increase in property taxes. 
Rep Sena said that he realizes the Police Reform Act is expensive. Rep. Sena also asked the 
committee what the town’s priorities are. 
 
Mr. Menard asked for a push toward electronic storage of records, and pointed out not only the 
amount of room storing physical documents take, but the energy expended to control the 
climate of the storage room as well. Mr. Menard also reminded those in attendance of 
Montachusett Regional Technical School and pointed out that Lunenburg pays substantially 
more per student than any other municipality in the region. Mr. Menard mentioned the town’s 
senior citizen population, and opined that where there is a discussion about education, there 
should also be a discussion about senior citizens, who have made it possible for the town to be 
where it is. Mr. Menard also talked about the challenge the town faces with 40B, and the impact 
it has on the town’s infrastructure and resources.  
 
===PAGE 3===
Mr. Beardmore added that the utility companies the town has to deal with are unsatisfactory, 
and there are no alternate services.  
 
Rep. Kushmerek brought up the recent re-filing of the Muni-choice bill, which has been filed 
several times over several years, and has yet to be passed. Mr. Beardmore commented that the 
town needs feasibility just as much as legality when it comes to dealing with utilities.  
 
Mr. Beardmore suggested the committee make the town’s financial priorities an agenda topic to 
discuss at the next meeting.  
 
7. Town Manager/Finance Director Reports: Ms. Lemieux reported that the Ritter Building and 
Town Hall envelope projects will be going before the Architecture Preservation District 
Committee, that bids for work on the Public Safety Building have come in below projected 
project costs, and that the electrical work that’s taken place at Town Hall brought out 200 
pounds of old wiring. Ms. Lemieux also reported that the town has received 36 responses to the 
survey concerning 30 School St, and that two conceptual designs for the lot will be released 
January 18, 2022. A contract for cleaning the town’s municipal buildings has been put out for bid 
since the current cleaning company cannot keep up with the current scope of work. 
Ms. Lemieux also reported that she’s hired consulting firm Capital Strategic Solutions to navigate 
ARPA spending. The consultant will give a presentation at the February 1, 2022 meeting of the 
Select Board. 
 
8. Annual Town Meeting Warrant and related updates:  Ms. Lemieux informed the board that 
Town Warrant closes March 21, 2022. 
 
9. New Municipal Building Design Committee charge: Mr. Beardmore suggested formerly 
revisiting who should serve as the finance committee representative as the charge of the 
former T.C. Passios Design Committee has changed.  
 
10. Minutes: Mr. Watters motioned to approve the minutes of the October 28, 2021, November 4, 
2021, November 16, 2021, and December 9, 2021 meetings. Mr. Menard seconded. Vote passes 
4, 0, 1.  
 
11. Committee Reports Committee members updated each other on their respective liaison 
assignments. 
 
12. Public Comment 
Lunenburg resident David Rodgers phoned in to say that the issue of busing students needs to 
be addressed, and the town should be prepared to do something about it, and in regard to 
members of the state legislature, Mr. Rodgers opined that there is no way the town can afford 
to address all the issues it faces, and advised to prioritize what reasonably can be tackled.  
 
13. Items for Future Meetings: Priority list for Rep Sena, Green Communities Committee. 
 
14. Next Meetings: Jan. 27, Feb. 17 
 
===PAGE 4===
15. Adjourn: At 9:26 p.m. Mr. Watters motioned to adjourn, Mr. Menard seconded. On vote, 
motion carried unanimously.

Lunenburg Finance Committee 
Meeting Minutes 
Thursday, January 27, 2022 
 
Location: In-person at Lunenburg Town Hall and broadcast on Zoom 
 
Present: Chairperson Peter Beardmore, Jay Simeone, Evan Watters, Tom Balish, Tom Gray, Dave Passios 
via Zoom (for first hour of meeting) 
 
Guests: Town Manager Heather Lemieux, Town Accountant Karen Brochu, School Committee 
Chairperson Carol Archambault 
1. Call to Order 
Mr. Beardmore called the meeting to order at 7:00 p.m. and read a statement 
concerning the Open Meeting Law, and how all public meetings are legally being held 
remotely. 
This meeting was recorded and is viewable on YouTube at https://youtu.be/6E3z38z9SBI 
2. Pledge of Allegiance 
 
3. Announcements: A consultant from Capital Strategic Solutions will give a special presentation 
concerning ARPA funds at the February 1, 2022 Select Board meeting, State Legislative delegates 
will be in attendance.  
 
4. Public Comment: Ms. Archambault expressed the school committee’s disappointment in the 
Capital Planning Committee’s capital plan for FY23, as it doesn’t list the air conditioning project 
for the Primary School as a high priority, and inquired whether the project could be eligible for 
ARPA funds.  
 
Mr. Beardmore expressed his continuing reservations about the capital plan, and his 
disappointment that the meetings of the Capital Planning Committee were not recorded. Mr. 
Beardmore pointed out that the Finance Committee and the Select Board have a right to weigh 
in on the plan, and should do so moving forward.  
 
5. Town Manager/Finance Director Report: Ms. Lemieux also informed the committee on 
Governor Baker’s proposal on the cherry sheet, which came in at $983,000 more from state aid, 
including an 11 percent increase in Chapter 70 funds.  
Ms. Lemieux also had an update on the gas station at 925 Mass Ave. She has been in contact 
with the EPA, and anticipates funds from a federal infrastructure bill in the summer. Though the 
town does not own the property, the EPA will assist in navigating through the process of 
cleaning up the property.  
Ms. Lemieux informed the committee that Townsend is withdrawing from the regional dispatch 
program, and has been assured that the town will not see a financial impact for FY23 or FY24. 
Ms. Lemieux said she spoke with the Lunenburg Central project manager, and the project should 
be finished in 2023, and is expected to bring at least 200 new jobs to town.  
The Tax Lien Auction has been rescheduled to March 17, 2022 at 10:00 a.m.  
===PAGE 2===
6. Annual Town Meeting Updates: none 
 
7. Update on streetlights project: Ms. Lemieux informed the committee of her last discussion with 
the Select Board concerning the conversion of street lights to LED. Town Counsel has contacted 
the Department of Public Utilities, and were advised to correspond with Unitil. 
 
8. Legislative priorities, follow up on Legislators visit: The committee agreed to include 40B on 
their list of priorities as well as possible state aid or grant opportunities for 30 School St. Mr. 
Gray suggested there should be some kind of resource on the state level to help municipalities 
find options when it comes to aging municipal buildings as the issue is not unique to Lunenburg. 
Mr. Beardmore also brought up legislation that’s been filed on fees paid by Comcast which 
support the town’s public access channel. With more households disconnecting their cable 
subscriptions, the fees being paid by Comcast will decrease, which would have a financial impact 
on Lunenburg’s public access cable channel.  
 
9. Capital Plan Presentation and Discussion: Ms. Lemieux talked about how the Capital Plan is 
created, and what is on the plan. There were 31 capital requests. One project was removed due 
to not receiving a cost estimate. The remaining 30 capital requests total $4,043,049 for FY 2023 
 
 
 
Mr. Watters pointed out that four of the twenty recommendations are vehicles and asked 
whether there’s any concern about the availability of those vehicles. Ms. Lemieux said she 

===PAGE 3===
realizes there are challenges, but those challenges don’t mean the need goes away, and the 
funds need to be appropriated to order the vehicles. Mr. Watters also questioned why records 
retention was a higher priority than air conditioning at the Primary School.  Mr. Watters also 
asked how ARPA funds fit into the capital plan. Ms. Lemieux responded that there will be a 
prioritization process to fund projects with those funds, and added that the spending is under 
the discretion of the Select Board, and therefore, won’t have to go before Town Meeting.  
 
Mr. Beardmore commented that the Finance Committee can request a joint meeting with the 
Capital Planning Committee to discuss their decision, and also pointed out that Ms. Lemieux was 
presenting her recommendations. Ms. Lemieux added that the Capital Planning Committee does 
put a lot of time and effort into developing their list of recommendations.  
 
Mr. Beardmore recognized the significant amount of money being put toward facilities 
maintenance, and pointed out that there have been many discussions about the town’s lack of 
maintaining their buildings, and suggested designating capital funds each year to keep up with 
the maintenance of town facilities. Ms. Lemieux suggested the committee put the conversation 
into context, and pointed out that there was $4 million in requests, 66 percent of which had to 
do with town facilities as a result of the past Vertex studies. Mr. Beardmore questioned the 
prioritization of replacing the HVAC at the senior center, or fixing the furnaces at Town Hall over 
installing air conditioning at the Primary School, which was designed with windows that cannot 
be opened.  
Mr. Simeone asked why buildings with HVAC or furnace issues have not considered the Green 
Communities Committee for possibilities in funding.  
 
10. Five-Year Financial Forecast: Ms. Lemieux described the required five-year financial forecast, 
which is a living document, subject to change as more information becomes available. 
Mr. Watters inquired about the increase in health insurance costs for FY23, and whether the 
Health Insurance Stabilization Fund would account for that increase. Ms. Lemieux explained that 
the fund would serve as revenue in her preliminary budget, and would be used to supplement 
the cost.  
Mr. Balish inquired about the school’s five year staffing plan expenditures. Ms. Lemieux 
responded that a long-term staffing plan was developed in the past, similar to the police and fire 
departments. Ms. Lemieux explained that some expenses are covered via grant money, but the 
plan has been put in place to fund those expenses when grant funding dries up. 
Mr. Balish suggested using percentages to describe increases and decreases on the forecast as 
the information could be easier to digest when presented.  
 
Both the Five -Year Financial Forecast and the FY2023 Capital Plan Recommendation 
presentations can be found at Lunenburg Town Hall in the Town Manager’s Office as well 
as online at https://www.lunenburgma.gov/news-announcements/fy-2023-fy-2027-
financial-forecast-and-fy-2023-capital-plan-presentations. 
 
 
===PAGE 4===
11. Municipal Building Design Committee update: The committee discussed the role of the 
representative on the Municipal Design Committee, and decided to bring a more proactive 
financial approach with the new charge. 
Mr. Balish moved to appoint Mr. Beardmore to represent the Finance Committee on the 
Municipal Building Design Committee, Mr. Gray seconded. On vote, motion passes 4-1.   
 
12. Minutes none 
 
13. Committee Reports: Committee members updated each other on their respective liaison 
assignments. 
 
14. Public Comment Lunenburg resident David Rogers phoned in to express his concern over the 
town projecting deficits, and the possibility of an override of Prop 2 ½.  
 
15. Items for Future Meetings Department heads will present their budgets 
 
16. Next Meetings Feb 17 
 
17. Adjourn At 9:54 p.m. Mr. Watters motioned to adjourn, Mr. Balish seconded. On vote, motion 
carried unanimously.

Lunenburg Finance Committee 
Meeting Minutes 
Thursday, February 17, 2022 
 
Location: In-person at Lunenburg Town Hall and broadcast on Zoom 
 
Present: Chairperson Peter Beardmore, Jay Simeone, Evan Watters, Tom Balish, Tom Gray, Chris Menard 
via Zoom, Dave Passios via Zoom (for last part of meeting) 
 
Guests: Town Manager Heather Lemieux, School Committee Chair Carol Archambault. 
1. Call to Order 
Mr. Beardmore called the meeting to order at 7:00 p.m. and read a statement 
concerning the Open Meeting Law, and how all public meetings are legally being held 
remotely. 
This meeting was recorded and is viewable on YouTube at https://youtu.be/Qx7EgjovsNE 
2. Pledge of Allegiance 
 
3. Announcements: none 
 
4. Public Comment: none 
 
5. Town Manager/Finance Director Report: Ms. Lemieux gave the committee an update on what 
she discussed with the Select Board at their meeting earlier in the week. Electricity and natural 
gas contracts are to expire on November 30, 2022. The town will solicit bids in spring. Ms. 
Lemieux also informed the board that the Meadow Woods trailer park, which has been in 
receivership for many years, will begin paying back taxes at $100,000 per year until all back 
taxes are paid off. Close to $1 million is owed. The annual $100,000 will go to free cash.  
 
6. Discussion on ARPA presentation at Select Board meeting on Feb 1, 2022: Ms. Lemieux 
explained that Lunenburg’s $3 million share of ARPA funds falls under the “lost revenue” 
category, and the Select Board will decide how the funds are allocated. Consultants from Capital 
Strategic Solutions will hold public outreach meetings to get the community’s input on how 
funds should be spent. Ms. Lemieux highlighted 30 School St., Marshall Park, and costs related 
to the latest Vertex study on town buildings as possible projects to be funded with ARPA funds. 
Ms. Lemieux also informed the committee that several suggested projects may also be funded 
by alternative sources, and that much research will be done before the decision is made to 
spend the money. All possible projects will be evaluated and prioritized.  
Mr. Beardmore expressed his concern about the T.C. Passios building project, Town Hall and 
Ritter building, as well as the Turkey Hill Elementary school, and opined the town must be very

Ms. Crockett informed the committee that Preliminary FY23 Chapter 70 funds have 
increased to $5,988,520,366, which is a $485.3 million (8.8 percent) increase from FY22. 
Based on Student Opportunity Act figures regarding Chapter 70, Monty Tech will spend 
$19,043 per pupil, not including transportation, capital expenses, or debt service. 
Lunenburg’s foundation enrollment has increased from 83 to 94, which increases the 
town’s required minimum contribution to $1,012,282 (a $140,597 increase) for Monty 
Tech, which is 8.2 percent of all Lunenburg’s Chapter 70 funds. Ms. Crockett reminded 
the committee that 96 percent of the Monty Tech assessment is governed by the state, 
which determines contribution by average income and property values in town. After 
adding in transportation at $24,131 and capital at $17,964, Lunenburg will end up 
paying $11,217 per pupil. Monty Tech’s total budget is $30,796,896, which comes in

there is already a waiting list for the school’s regional communities. She explained that the 
grade 10-12 spots are saved for students who enrolled as freshmen, then moved out of the

7. Proposed Fiscal 2023 Wage Schedule and Proposed Salary Administration Bylaw Change: Mr. 
Arnold informed the committee that the Personnel Committee has met with town counsel in 
regard to the Salary Administration Plan (SAP), and upon recommendation from the attorney, 
the plan does need to be updated. Those updates include: removing the layoff procedure from 
the SAP as it is already covered in the Town Charter; adding Human Resources to the grievance 
procedure; changing language regarding what constitutes a full-time employee; changing 
personal time from hours to days; removal of Family Medical Leave Act language as it was 
conflicting with what’s stated in the Charter; upgrading the salary grid to reflect minimum wage, 
which means a 28 percent increase for Grade 1, a 22 percent increase for Grade 2, and an 18 
percent increase for Grade 3. The remaining 17 grades will be increased by 2 percent. The 
increase on the grid will cost the town $44,250.77 annually. There are approximately 25 
employees on the SAP, and about 15 are on-call personnel.  Ms. Bilotta-Simeone added that 
those on the upper portion of the grid would only be getting cost of living increases after they 
===PAGE 2===
reached the highest grade and step, and upgrading the grid addresses that issue. Mr. Passios 
commented that increasing pay on the lower end will help bring in new talent, and keeping 
increases, and longevity pay will help with retention. Mr. Simeone asked if there were plans for 
a professional town-wide wage study, and also suggested the Personnel Committee consider 
separating wage schedules for hourly, salaried, and seasonal employees. Mr. Gray agreed that 
keeping track of wages for comparable positions is important. The Finance Committee agreed 
that wages will be a major topic of discussion in FY23. Mr. Passios thanked the current and past 
members of the Personnel Committee for bringing the SAP to its current status.  
 
8. Public Hearing on FY23 Budget, Capital Plan, and all financially relevant Annual Town Meeting 
Warrant Articles: Mr. Beardmore opened the public hearing. Ms. Lemieux presented the Town 
Manager’s FY2023 Recommended Budget. The town is projected to receive $30,854,794 in 
property taxes, $10,390,105 in State Aid, $3,110,153 in local receipts, and $2,350,230 in other 
available funds, totaling $5,789,417. Financial articles include: funding the first year of the 
collective bargaining agreement with the Clerical union, which involves transferring $15,303 
from the FY22 Salary Reserve Account; $100,000 from free cash to cover expenses regarding 
consultant fees, removal and disposal of solid waste materials from the Woodruff property; 
$450,000 from free cash to demolish 30 School Street, and create a green space design; An 
amount to be determined to establish a committee to commemorate the town’s 300th 
anniversary; $44,250 from the FY23 Salary Reserve Account to fund Salary Administration Plan 
wage increases; and $2,162,849 for the Capital Plan funded by free cash, appropriated funds, 
and unexpended capital. Mr. Beardmore recollected a past meeting where Mr. Passios 
expressed concern over whether the cost of installing HVAC at the Primary School was accurate, 
as it was made aware that the electricity at the school may not be appropriate for the originally 
intended HVAC. Mr. Passios said he asked for more information but could not get an answer. 
Mr. Balish asked where the School St project would be in terms of progress after spending the 
requested $371,000. Ms. Lemieux answered that the building will be razed, grading will be 
complete, and design details will go to another Town Meeting for funds for new construction of 
the property. Mr. Simeone asked for justification of a part time IT employee. Ms. Lemieux 
answered that the new employee would be responsible for administrative duties and website 
content.  
Public Hearing public comment: Lunenburg resident Dave Rodgers phoned in to share his 
opinion on items in the capital plan, and the amount of money proposed to be spent on 
equipment, vehicles, and projects.  Committee members went on to discuss the issues Mr. 
Rodgers raised: DPW request for utility tractor/mower to handle roadside brush, which is a top 
resident concern, a Ford F-150 pickup truck for the police department to handle towing, 
community outreach, and transporting personnel, and the Flat Hill Road drainage design project. 
Chief Gammel commented that the police department’s current fleet of vehicles does not have 
the proper braking system for the trailers currently in use, and that the police department needs 
a four-wheel drive utility vehicle. Mr. Passios added that the Capital Planning Committee had 
several meetings and determined which items were affordable. Mr. Gray added that he will 
always look to the department heads to best determine their needs. Mr. Menard expressed his 
concern over finding the source of the water problem on Flat Hill Rd, and wants to be sure the 
problem is fixed so as not to cause another drainage problem on surrounding roads. Mr. 
===PAGE 3===
Beardmore suggested outsourcing some of the brush clearing from roadsides. Ms. Lemieux 
pointed out there is no appropriation for outsourcing. Mr. Beardmore suggested the committee 
has the right to make amendments to articles, and raised a scenario where the committee could 
vote to recommend disapproval of the capital plan article and then make an amendment that 
would garner the recommendation to approve. Mr. Passios commented that the committee 
could recommend to reduce the amount of the capital plan, but could not remove specific items 
from it. Committee members went on to discuss how the town handled the capital plan in 2020 
in the throes of the Covid-19 pandemic. Mr. Passios moved to close the public hearing, Mr. 
Menard seconded. On vote, motion carried unanimously.
