The hearing opened at 7:32 PM with the reading of the legal notice published in the Sentinel & Enterprise December 26, 2016
and January 2, 2017. The Public Hearing was to hear and discuss an application for Site Plan Approval under Section 8.4. of the
Protective Bylaw of the Town of Lunenburg for construction of a parking lot at 9 Electric Avenue (Map 074, Parcel 0009) and a
building addition at Asian Imperial Restaurant, 5 Electric Avenue (Map 074, Parcel 0008).
Senior Project Manager Robert Oliva, David E. Ross Associates, inc. represented the Applicant- Min Xu, d/b/a Asian Imperial.
Also in attendance was Pete Pelletier who will be the general contractor for the project. The owner has purchased the property
at 9 Electric Avenue for the purpose of constructing additional parking for the restaurant. The two lots combined will be
approximately 41,000 - 42,000 sq. ft. There are currently thirty-three (33) parking spaces. The restaurant has thirty (30) tables
and sixteen (16) places at the bar. The existing driveways at 5 Electric Avenue will be maintained. The house at 9 Electric
Avenue has been demolished. The proposed new parking lot will have a driveway connecting to the existing driveway at 5
Electric at both the rear and the front of the lot. After reconfiguration there will be a total of fifty (50) parking spaces. On
November 22, 2016, the Zoning Board of Appeals granted a Special Permit for a dimensional variation for parking setbacks.
(The plan presented to the Zoning Board of Appeas was for fifty-two (52) spaces.) As a result of the proposed expansion to the
front of the restaurant, the parking in front of the restaurant will be regraded for accessibility. The handicap parking will be
moved from in front of the restaurant to across the parking lot. Currently there is an underground detention system which
connects to the system in the street. An additional underground system is proposed under the new parking lot which will connect
to the street. Lights are proposed at the two islands leading into the new parking lot. There is an existing wall between 5 and 9
Electric Avenue which will be modified and a wood guard rail placed on top. A wall is proposed at the back corner of the new
parking lot starting at approximately seven (7') feet dropping off with grade towards the front of the lot. Landscaping is shown on
the plan. There is no walkway from the upper to the lower parking.
K. Chenis is concerned with vehicles entering the site and pedestrians walking from the upper parking lot down to the restaurant..
Applicant to look at options. There is no information available yet on the floor plan of the building addition; possibly add six (6)
tables and ten (10) seats at the bar. With thirty-six (36) tables and twenty-six (26) counter seats, forty-nine (49) parking spaces
are required. Currently proposed are fifty (50), but possibly one (1) may be lost for pedestrian access. At this time, no spaces
have been designated pick-up only. G. Bittner would like to see a complete proposal to include parking and seating. He is
concerned with individuals parking where it is not allowed and would like to see something done to make those areas
unattractive for parking. Applicant to look at. Applicant to file a Notice of Intent with the Conservation Commission. Trees will
have to be provided in the parking lot per Protective Bylaw, Section 6.4.3.e)9.a. D. Prokowiew would like an elevation drawing of

The hearing opened at 6:37 PM with the reading of the legal notice published in the Sentinel & Enterprise December 26, 2016
and January 2, 2017. The Public Hearing was to hear and discuss an application for Site Plan Approval under Section 8.4. of the
Protective Bylaw of the Town of Lunenburg for construction and operation of two street hockey rinks at 691 Chase Road (Map
021, Parcel 0003).
Applicant, Richard Breau made his presentation. His desire is to install two street hockey rinks in two phases. The first
immediately with a three-sided building approved by the Board of Health and a parking lot. In the fall he would install lights and
then install the second rink either next spring or summer. He is asking for the following waivers:
Drainage Calculations
Impact Reports
Traffic
Stormwater

Many of the above waivers are being requested for financial reasons as he and his wife are financing this project personally. By
doing so, he can keep fees down. He plans on doing clinics with children, getting involved with the ice hockey team, and holding
leagues. Leagues will range from children age four (4) to age sixteen years plus (16+). He would also do adult, co-ed, and
women's leagues.
G. Bittner noted the design submitted to the Planning Board is slightly different than that presented to the Zoning Board of
Appeals. R. Breau responded it was redesigned so there is nothing in the wetlands or buffer zone. The boards are four (4) feet
high, with a twelve (12') foot fence around the back. The three (3) handicap parking spaces (near the three-sided building) will
be paved and painted. A. Burney noted there will have to be accessible pathways through the site. R. Breau will do 3-4 foot wide
pathways. D. Prokowiew inquired if everything will flow towards Chase Road. The rinks are slightly pitched toward both sides.
Rinks are processed gravel, then 3-4 inch tar asphalt base, then a plastic deck mat. Thirty-six (36) parking spaces are proposed.
D. Prokowiew inquired if the parking could be expanded. R. Breau noted that teams consist of ten (10) children so there would
be fifteen-twenty (15-20) vehicles per rink. He went on to say that many parents drop their children off and then come back later
to pick them up.
Games would start at approximately 6:00 PM during the week. Weekends would be approximately 9:00 AM - 5:00 PM. The
Zoning Board of Appeals approved the Modification of a Special Permit for operating hours of Monday - Friday, 3:00 PM - 10:00
PM and Saturday and Sunday, 7:30 AM - 9:00 PM. Light shutoff time would be by 10:15 PM after the 9:00-10:00 PM game. (G.
Bittner thought that the Zoning Board of Appeals hours of operation for Saturday and Sunday were 7:30 AM - 8:00 PM.) D.
Prokowiew was concerned with lighting being a nuisance to residents across the street on Windermere Drive. R. Breau
responded that once he is ready to install lights a representative from the light company will come out to the site to advise him.
The lights will be directed towards the rinks with shielding. He has been quoted 400 watt lights at 20-22 candlelight. The light
company will test the lights at the Leominster rink, and if that is determined to be sufficient, R. Breau will purchase the same
wattage. As the Leominster rink is older and darker, he may decide to go up to 1,000 watt lights. A. Burney noted the proposed
light located on the southwest side of the first rink lines up with Windermere Drive. That will need to be taken into account when
the lights are designed so there will be no interference with the intersection with Chase Road.K. Chenis asked if any trees on
the road side were being taken down. No.
A. Burney noted that if the Board was concerned that the rink excavation would have the potential to cause silt flow, it could
condition a hay bale line along the edge of excavation to ensure there is no siltation.
D. Prokowiew referred to the Fire Chief's email which notes that ambulance access is tight. R. Breau will put in a spot for
turnaround near the handicap parking and will probably eliminate three (3) spots near the first rink. The parking plan indicates 36
spaces. K. Chenis suggested the parking be redesigned with spaces located in the center, face to face, with the road circling the
parking lot. This would provide ambulance access and eliminate the necessity for 3-point turns for drop offs. D. Prokowiew
inquired as to how the boundary of the parking lot will be marked. R. Breau responded that he would probably have pillars in the
middie and roped off.
K. Chenis asked what will prevent gravel silt from running into Chase Road during a rainstorm. R. Breau responded that there
will be a 30 ft. paved entrance (note: the plan indicates 40 ft.) with a crushed stone swale on the side to act as a French drain.
The plan does not show any lights in the parking area. R. Breau noted there would probably be one spotlight on top of the three-
sided building. Board responded that will be pretty far away from the parking lot. R. Breau responded that he tentatively plans
on closing by 7 PM in the spring and everyone should be off the property before it gets dark.
The Board would like to see this application altered to be a single phase one-rink project with a modified parking lot and that R.
Breau return to the Board for a modification at the time of construction of the second rink and also any other design element
changes. The current requested waivers remain.
M. Allison asked about the dispersion of rainwater on the rinks. The rinks pitch two (2") inches side to side; water will flow into
the grass and processed gravel. M. Allison suggested R. Breau ask Whitman & Bingham about gravel recharge along the sides.
Board requested a landscape plan.

G. Bittner inquired if R. Breau will want to erect a sign at the site entrance. A. Burney recommended R. Breau include that as

A. Burney noted that when the parking lot is redesigned R. Breau needs to comply with the Protective Bylaw, Section 6.4.3.e)9.a.

this iteration of the site plan, if approved by the Board it would be for the parking lot, rink, porta potty and pro shop, minus
lighting. An entrance gate for security purposes will be annotated on the revised plan. He will contact the Fire Department
regarding the type of lock so they will have access.
The lighting and photometric plan would be part of the modification for the second rink.
Per the Agent to the Board of Health "the existing well shown under the parking lot shall be discontinued - a permit from the

plan approval under Section 8. 4. of the Protective Bylaw of the Town of Lunenburg  for a 22-unit townhouse development at 274 
Prospect Street, Map 98, Parcel 10.   
 
The Applicant was  not represented.  A Request to Withdraw Without Prejudice was presented to the Board at the February 27,  
2017 Hearing continuation.   
 
A brief summarization of the project’s history was given.  The Building Commissioner had previously submitted a letter to the 
Board noting that the Board had purview to approve the appropriate density for the site.  At the request of the Applicant, the 
Building Commissioner issued a subsequent letter stating eight (8) units per acre were applicable to 274 Prospect Street.  As  a 
result of that letter, Board consensus was to petition August 2016 Special Town Meeting to remove th e Lake Whalom Overlay 
District; the Article did not pass.  Following STM, Town Counsel submitted an opinion letter to the Planning Board refuting the 
opinion of the Building Commissioner and stated he did not feel the proposed site qualified for application under the LWOD.     
 
Before approving the Applicant’s request for Withdrawal Without Prejudice, the Board was seeking Town Counsel’s opinion that 
the Applicant is not eligible under the LWOD.  Town Counsel opined there are no ten (10) acres parcels left in the LWOD; the 
Applicant is not eligible for a waiver as they don’t have ten (10) acres.  He went on to note that ultimately it is up the Bo ard to 
interpret the LWOD Bylaw.  The Board inquired as to what action should be taken if the Applicant reapplies.   Town Counsel 
responded that the Board should accept and act on the application  to avoid the risk of a constructive grant .  During the hearing 
process, the Board could take a position and at that time deny the application as not qualifying under a special permit.     
 
Town Counsel noted that if the Board remains silent, it is deemed to be With Prejudice; then the Applicant will be under a two -
year ban from bringing the same project back.  If an application is submitted within those two years, it has to be materially 
different.  If the Board accepts Withdrawal Without Prejudice, the Applicant can reapply at any time.   
 
Motion, D. McQuaid, to accept the Withdrawal Without Prejudice, Second, K. Chenis, all aye.

4.18
4.18.1
Purpose:
Temporary Moratorium on the Sale and Distribution of Recreational Marijuana.
By vote at the State election on November 8, 2016, the voters of the Commonwealth approved a law regulating
the cultivation, distribution, possession and use of marijuana for recreational purposes. The law provides that it
is eftective on December 15, 2016 and the Cannabis Control Commission is currently required to issue
regulations regarding implementation by March 15, 2018 (see Section 11 of Chapter 351 of the Acts of 2016).
Currently under the Zoning Bylaw, Recreational Marijuana Establishments and Marijuana Retailers are not a
permitted
use in the Town and any regulations promulgated by the State Cannabis Control Commission are
expected to provide guidance to the Town in regulating Recreational Marijuana Establishments and Marijuana
Retailers.
The regulation of Recreational Marijuana Establishments and Marijuana Retailers raise novel and complex legal,
safety issues and the Town needs time to
Recreational Marijuana Establishments and Marijuana Retailers and address such novel and complex issues, as
well as to address the potential impact of the State regulations on local zoning and to undertake a planning
process to consider amending the Zoning Bylaw regarding regulation of Recreational Marijuana Establishments
and Marijuana Retailers and other uses related to the regulation of recreational marijuana. The Town intends
to adopt a temporary moratorium on the use of land and structures in the Town for Recreational Marijuana
Establishments and Marijuana Retailers so as to allow the Town sufficient time to engage in a planning process
to address effects of such structures and uses in the Town and to adopt provisions of the Zoning Bylaw in a
manner consistent with sound land use planning goals and objectives.

For the reasons set forth above and notwithstanding any other provision of the Zoning Bylaw to the contrary,
the Town hereby adopts a temporary moratorium
on the use of land or structures for Recreational Marijuana
Establishments and Marijuana Retailers.
The moratorium shall be in effect through November 30, 2018.
During the moratorium period, the Town shall undertake a planning process to address the potential
impacts of recreational marijuana in the
Town, consider the Cannabis Control Commission regulations
regarding Recreational Marijuana Establishments and Marijuana Retailers and related uses,, determine whether
the town will prohibit on-site consumption at Recreational Marijuana Establishments and Marijuana Retallers and
shall consider adopting new provisions of the Zoning Bylaw to address the impact and operation of Recreational
Marijuana Establishments and Marijuana Retailers and related uses.
4.18.4 Severability.
The provisions of this Bylaw are severable, If any provision, paragraph, sentence, or clause of this Bylaw or the
establishment, or circumstances shall be held invalid the remaining
portions shall remain in full force and effect.
A. Burney noted minor modifications were made as a result of Town counsel review. Two changes, noted above in italicized
bold, will be made on Town Meeting floor.
The Warrant Article prohibits the location of recreational marijuana related uses from now until December 1, 2018 or until the
Town has added language to the Zoning Bylaw that would permit them in appropriate areas of Town.

The Hearing was to hear and discuss an application for Site Plan Approval under Section 8.4. of the Protective
Bylaw of the Town of Lunenburg for construction of an office and garage for a municipal water supplier at 496 Massachusetts
Avenue (Map 073, Parcel 0004).
The Applicant, Lunenburg Water District, was represented by Anthony Cleaves, Whitman & Bingham Associates. The Water
District currently has an office located off Rt. 13, but wishes to construct a new office and garage. The office will be 2,400 sq. ft.
and the garage 6,000 sq. ft. The building will be oriented on the site such as to hide its size from Massachusetts Avenue.
Individuals passing by on Massachusetts Avenue will see only the front of the office. The site is approximately 7 acres. A utility
easement crosses the property on the west side and along the rear of the site. The site will be accessed with a new driveway
from Massachusetts Avenue just west of the existing curb cut. There will be a total of 14 parking spaces located at the front and
sides of the building. The building will be surrounded by pavement. The dumpster area is proposed on the west side. Fuel
source will be propane; there will also be an emergency generator. Water will be municipal. Sewer service will be from an on-
site sewage disposal system. To limit the use of catch basins and manholes, sheet flow on a majority of the site will be to grass
swales, as well as catch basins, and a stormwater basin. Flow that is not captured by the infiltration basin due to elevations will
go into a catch basin at the bottom of the driveway into a water quality unit piping into an area on the side of Massachusetts
Avenue. Permits have been filed with MA DOT for tying in utilities and a curb cut. The Conservation Commission has issued an
Order of Conditions as a portion of the project is within the 100-foot wetlands buffer. Septic plans have been submitted to
Nashoba Board of Health.
G. Bittner inquired if the existing culverts on the road are adequate, meanwhile noting that he is aware the Applicant is only
responsible for what is on their site. A. Cleaves responded that with the stormwater components they will be putting in place, it
will decrease the rate of runoff leaving the site and will not make the situation any worse.
K. Chenis asked about the holding tank for the garage and where the water is going. As there is no municipal system to tie into
and this is a garage with overhead doors, the plumbing code requires floor drains. The floor drains will go into a 1,500 gallon
tight tank with floats and a high water alarm system.
Peer review was done by Jeff Walsh, Graves Engineering Inc. A. Cleaves went through the review letter of April 14, 2017 (on file
in the Planning Office) and verbally provided his responses.
