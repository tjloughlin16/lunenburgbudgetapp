7:00PM Public Hearing on General on Premises All Alcohol License for MLMR, Inc. d/b/a Primetime Pub, 5
Summer Street, Unit 10: Mr. Alonzo read into record the Notice of Public Hearing and invited the applicant to speak to
the board about the application and business.
Mark LaPrade, representing MLMR spoke to the board saying that he would like to be open by the beginning of the new
year and explained the type of establishment he would be running along with the hours of operation. Primetime Pub
would be open during weekdays from 12 Noon - 1AM and on weekends from 12 Noon - 2AM. Entertainment will be kept

employee TIP certified staff for this enterprise after all required approvals are received

establishments for many years. Mr. LaPrade would be acting as a working Owner/Manager at this business.

Ms. Speidel replied that they are not required to be on duty all of the time.

7:15PM FY2014 Tax Classification Hearing, Board of Assessors: Harald Scheid, Regional Assessor reported to the
board concerning the FY2014 Tax Classification. He explained that this hearing is for the BOS to determine if they will
levy a single tax rate across all classes of real estate and personal property or move to a split rate. Mr. Scheid stated that
Lunenburg has a small commercial and industrial tax base and the Board of Assessors recommends to the BOS the
adoption of a uniform or single tax rate.
Mr. Scheid went on to say that it would take a significant increase in the industrial/commercial tax base to yield a modest
reduction to residential property taxes. (See attached Tax Rate Options spread sheet)
Mr. Scheid commented on valuations saying that they believe that they have reached the bottom of the market and
evidence points to a stabilized real estate market. They are making some minor valuation changes that overall will result
in very small value reductions at similar levels that were seen in FY2013. Earlier in the year there was some strong
activity in the real estate market and if this continues we may see some price appreciation.

1. Review of Tax Exemption & Deferral Programs with Regional Assessor (Taken out of order before the 7:15PM
Appointment): Harald Scheid, Regional Assessor, explained to the board the available Statutory Tax Exemptions and
Deferrals which are available to qualifying Lunenburg residents for FY2014. These included exemptions and deferrals for
the elderly, Veterans, and the blind. He mentioned that approximately $80,000 is granted in tax relief in a given year but
reiterated the fact that you must meet certain qualifications in order to receive benefits, which sometimes can be difficult to
do. If a person is qualified for a tax deferral, their taxes would be deferred with simple interest as a lien on the property
until the property owner passes or the home is sold
Complete information can be received at the Lunenburg Assessors office, the Eagle House Senior Center or they can be
downloaded at the Assessors section on the Town web site. (See Attached Property Tax Exemptions and Deferrals sheet)
Also mentioned was the State Circuit Breaker Bill which is a reduction off of qualified individual's income tax, related to
information on this particular tax refund.
Mr. Ebersole added that the Circuit Breaker refund is in the amount of up to $1000, there is an income limit along with an
asset limit which is based on assessed value.
This also looks at taxes that are paid in a person's water and sewer bill.
Even if a person does not pay taxes, if qualified, they can still receive money back from the State.

3. Accept/ Approve Consultant Agreement, Colonial Power Group: Brian Murphy, President of Colonial Power
Group, came before the board to ask for an extension of the existing contract, which if it hasn't already, will expire shortly.
Mr. Murphy gave a brief history of the Colonial Power Groups affiliation/purpose with the Town, which has been in
existence for approximately 3 years now. He explained that from 1/2012 - 12/2012 the participating consumer's power
supplier was ConEdison Solutions, who was the low bidder, and they did two 6 month contracts with the Town.
At the end of 12/2012 the power markets were such that Unitil's default service was the best price available and at that
time a recommendation was made to suspend the program until the market became favorable again. The suspension
was lifted in June when the Town selected Dominion Resources for a 1 year contract that would be installed in a two 6
month installment process.
The first installment was from 6/2013 to 12/2013 and under the terms of the contract the supplier had the right to serve the
participating consumers from 12/2013 to 6/2014 providing it can be beat Unitil's basic service offering. They feel they can
an accomplish this and also are offering an extension through 11/2014, which the Town Manager has accepted. Colonial
Power Group is asking for the opportunity to continue to manage this process for the Town, as long as it sees fit.
Mr. Murphy continued on to say that they would like to try to get another power company into the mix. A particular
company that they are looking at is Hampshire Power. The Town of Lancaster is currently using them and if it is
successful there, Mr. Murphy hopes that they will become one of the bidders.
To date the current program in Lunenburg has saved residents and businesses $183,301.72, which is a cumulative total
since the programs inception in 1/2012. The bulk of the savings came in 1/2012 but since then the market has tightened
and the margin of savings now is much slimmer than it was which comes at no cost to consumers. Consumers can opt
out of this program at anytime they choose.
===PAGE 3===
Commission payments for Colonial Power Group since 11/2010 have amounted to $54,031.13. These payments do not
come from any Town funding, but come directly from the energy providers that Colonial Power Group works with. In
essence, the rate payer in town pays the commission through a "per Kwh fee" (1cent per Kwh) that's embedded in the
rate that the supplier has to include.
Ms.Speidel mentioned that she had researched other consultant company's commissions and the rate that Colonial
Power Group receives is a standard rate. Ms. Speidel is very pleased with the amount of savings that have been seen to
Mr. Murphy mentioned that it was also important for people to know that prices are going up. The current rate that the
Town's participating consumers have is 7.75 cents per Kwh and will be increasing to 9.2 cents per Kwh. This is reflective
of a broad rate increase that all rate payers will be absorbing across the State as well as across New England. This is
due to market rules that are put in place by the Federal Energy Regulatory Commission (FERC) to address fuel shortages
that occurred last January.
We did not feel this because the independent system operator that manages the grid in New England was able to keep
power flowing, but because of the shortage of natural gas coming into New England on a very thin pipeline, some power
generators were unable to obtain the gas to turn on their generating units. This happened during a cold snap and created
situations that were close to a "brown out". During cold snaps gas becomes more difficult to transport through pipelines
and when it's in limited availability, certain facilities have first rights to it such as homes, hospitals etc...and power plants
need to wait.
To address this, the International Standards Organization (ISO) and FERC have offered incentive payments to generators
who can run on both natural gas and oil and have directed these generators to stock pile oil for the winter. Companies
have gone out and spent $78M on oil to have on hand in case this happens again. That cost, by FERC's order, is
immediately put onto consumers. These prices should moderate after the winter is over.
Although there are improvements being made to the size of the natural gas pipeline, they are not slated to be initiated until
Ms. Bertram moved to approve the Consultant Agreement with Colonial Power Group as contained in the packet. Mr.

4. Open Warrant for STM; set closing date; review draft Ballot Question: Ms. Speidel informed the board that there is
a Special Town Meeting (STM) and Special Town Election (STE) schedule included in their packets and reviewed it with
them. (See Attached)
She mentioned that the dates were set at last weeks BOS meeting but were not on the agenda and asked the BOS to
officially open them this evening. She explained that in order to meet the requirements of the Charter and/or Bylaw the
warrant needs to remain open until 4PM n 11/25/2013.
The Massachusetts School Building Association (MSBA) is very particular concerning language for the warrant article and
the ballot question. Copies of the draft article and draft ballot question have been sent to the School Superintendent for
further information to be added to them. When the drafts are completed they will be sent to Town Counsel and the MSBA
for final approval. The Town's Bond Counsel will also receive copies to ensure that all their requirements are met.
Discussion: Mr. Matthews stated that if the MSBA is not giving their final commitment until 11/20/2013, he was not sure
how the BOS will be able to review and submit opinions by 11/19/2013.
Ms. Speidel replied that she feels that neither the BOS nor Finance Committee should plan to have a recommendation
included in the warrant. If they choose to do so, then they should look at holding a special meeting.
Mr. Alonzo spoke to say that he felt that there would be no major changes between the time that the MSBA votes on this
matter and when STM and the STE are held.
Ms. Bertram voiced concern over wording in the MSBA Bulletin which states that "A city or town must vote to appropriate
and authorize the full amount of a Project's cost, including both the local share and the MSBA's share, if any."
Mr. Ebersole replied that the actual article says that "the amount of borrowing authorized shall be reduced by any grant
set forth in the project funding agreement." You still have to appropriate the entire amount but you are only going to
borrow what's left over after MISBA gives to you, so technically you're buying the whole project.
Ms. Bertram stated that typically when you we go to town meeting we say that we will "go up to" a certain amount. We are
asking for full authorization for the total project and this concerns her. She realizes that the expectation is that the grant
will be received from the MSBA but what we are asking people to do at the STM and the STE is to fund the whole project.
Mr. Matthews replied that we are only going to borrow our share, not the whole thing, and at any time we can stop the
whole project.
===PAGE 4===
Mr. Ebersole added that the reality is that we will not go forward with out the MSBA. What's good is that the School
Building Committee has been putting out what the Town's share and the MSBA's share will be and we just need to
educate people that we have to appropriate the entire amount, but the only thing we will be responsible for is our share.
Ms. Bertram stated that she wished this could be added to the article, we are going to have to do everything we can to
make that clear, and this will be difficult.
Ms. Speidel said that we can ask about including that information in the article.

BOARD OF SELECTMEN
MEETING MINUTES
11/19/2013
The Board of Selectmen met in the Joseph F. Bilotta meeting room at Town Hall as scheduled with Chair Person Tom
Alonzo, Vice Chairperson Paula Bertram, Jamie Toale, Robert Ebersole and Town Manager Kerry Speidel.
Not present was David Matthews.
Regular Meeting opened at 7:00 PM
7:00PM PUBLIC COMMENT
Mr. Alonzo commented on the recent act of racial graffiti along with other questionable acts which were reported that
participated in the vigil which was held in support of the student and to stand up against racial intolerance. The
allegations from previous games is also under way. Mr. Alonzo said that he hopes that the person who committed this act
can be brought to justice hopes that we can recommit ourselves to the principles that he believes are the core values of
the school and the community at large, which are the principles of inclusion, of mutual respect, of tolerance and diversity.
He mentioned that the school has made a commitment to reassert themselves in programs such as anti bullying and
diversity and we as town's people should do this on our own. Hopefully we can begin the healing process with the student
and the family to who this was directed against. We ask that if anyone has any information they contact the Lunenburg
Police Dept.
Mr. Ebersole spoke to say that he supports the Chairs statement and he looks forward to a speedy resolution to this so
that the town can move ahead to heal and move forward
Ms. Bertram spoke to say that she echo's the Chairs sentiments and added that it was heartening to see everyone at the
vigil. We all need to come together as a community, which she thinks that we are. We are an inclusive community and
this is what we need to be. She also encouraged anyone with information on this crime to come forward.
Mr. Toale spoke to support the Chair's comments. He went on to say that he is very proud of the town and community,
continues to be so, and expects to be so in the future.
Les Szabady, 920 Mass Ave, spoke to the BOS and said that it was a terrible thing that happened last week. He feels
that the healing process for all would be to reconsider and let the team play the Thanksgiving Game. He feels that the
actions of one person can not be blamed on the entire team and you need to take into consideration the players, the
band, the cheerleaders and all of their families. This is a tradition in the town, we would like the support of the town and
would like for the BOS to reconsider to let them play.
Mr. Alonzo responded to clarify the separation of the powers and authorities within the town. The decision to cancel the
game lies with the School Committee and the School Administration. They have an elected board which determines that
He advised that this group present this case to them.
Mr. Alonzo went on to say that as a resident/official of this town who has no authority in that decision, he would like to
publicly say that he supports their decision.
He understands Mr. Szabady's point's, he believes that it's not an
assessment against the team or any of the other groups. This comes down to a safety issue and we do not want another
incident happening, when the ones that are before us are no where near a resolution. It is unfortunate that the people
who had nothing to do with this will pay a price, and he does not want this to be looked at as an assessment of blame and
suggested again that this be taken up with the School Committee and School Administration.
Mary Ulf, Autumn Rd. Spoke to the BOS to say that she understood what the Chair had just said and that she will speak
to the School Committee. She has a daughter who is a senior at Lunenburg High School and she herself is a substitute
teacher there. She does not believe that the seniors or anyone else on the Football team should be penalized for à crime
that we do not know has occurred. It's not fair to assume that it was a student or athlete of our High School.
Steve Powell, Winterhill Rd. spoke to the BOS to say that the media spins information. The team truly stands behind the
victim, they are friends with him, and everyone on the team want him back on the team. We hope that whoever did this
===PAGE 2===
comes forward to get help so you don't affect the rest of the kids. Lunenburg is a great town and to see this stuff written in
the paper and to watch the news you wouldn't think that. Contrary to what the media says they were all at the vigil. This
stuff goes on everywhere, no one is exempt from that, they fully support Isaac and we all want him back.
Mr. Alonzo spoke to say that he's sorry that the broadcasting media is not here tonight because it was a glaring omission
on their part to report that the entire football team along with the coaching staff, were not present. A lot of what the media
is reporting are inaccuracies at best along with hear say and third party speculation.
Greg Berthiaume, Chair of the Lunenburg School Committee, read a statement from the School Committee which stated
their support of the victim and his family and that they will continue to work to show that hate has no place in our schools.
It reminded people that there are two ongoing investigations. The first is being investigated by the School Department
which concerns an incident at both the Lunenburg vs. Worcester South football varsity and junior varsity games that
alleged that racist statements were used against the Worcester South players by Lunenburg athletes.
The second is being investigated by the Lunenburg Police and it concerns a racial slur which was scrawled on the Phillips
home. This investigation is focused broadly and is not limited to just the football players. They expect a thorough and
swift investigation into both of these matters.
The letter stated support for the students, football team, faculty, coaches and administration for their efforts over the last
tew days along with their support for each other and for Mr. Phillips and his family. The recommendation of the coaches,
school administration, and Superintendent was to postpone and then cancel the Oakmont game. Although the coaches
were hopeful the Thanksgiving game could be played they support the decision of the administration and the
Superintendent to suspend that game. This recommendation was based on the safety and well being of our students,
players and fans in the emotionally charged environment that has been generated by these most recent acts. They also
believe it would be highly inappropriate to play the remaining games while there is an ongoing investigation.
They asked for the community to continue to support the students, athletes, coaches, faculty, administration and each
other through these challenging times
Mr. Berthiaume said that this was unanimously approved by the 5 members of the School Committee.
Lisa Connors, Lunenburg, spoke to say that she was confused because they are saying it might not be the football team
and then they are taking it out on them by not letting them play.
Tammy Reynolds, 101 Stonefence Rd. spoke to say that by this decision we have just empowered the person who
committed this crime.
We have gentlemen here who have worked very hard and we have seniors who will leave this
school tarnished because we make a decision to allow that person, likely an immature teenager, to dictate to us the
program that we as a town should be supporting. Ms. Reynolds is confused as well.
Ms. Bertram spoke and reiterated that this not a decision of the BOS and is a decision of the School Committee. She
encouraged the people in attendance to establish a dialog with them. This board does not have that authority.
Ms. Ulf spoke again to say that there is a poll going around via Facebook and the Sentinel & Enterprise on whether or not
you agree with this decision. She asked what would happen if there were enough votes, would this be reversible.
Mr. Alonzo replied that he does not want to speak for the School Committee, a poll is certainly popular opinion and does
not carry the weight of a Town election and is not binding. It may guide or influence their decision but he did not want to
speak for them.
Rick Hill, Oak Ave., stated that he believes that the School Committee made a hasty decision by canceling the
Thanksgiving game.
What is happening right now is not only effecting Lunenburg but St. Bernards as well. Mr. Hill
believes that this is not a safety issue. He said that the Worcester issue was not an issue that day. We understand how
bad this issue is but if the Worcester issue was such a major problem then why wasn't it an issue then.
Mr. Hill said that the Worcester team were the ones using the language and maybe one or two of our kids retaliated. It's
High School kids, kids cleats get thrown into the trash can and tires being flat happens across America.
You're going to punish all these kids because of hear say. The problem that happened at the Phillips house is very sad
but keep in mind if we find out it was any of our kids that did that, no matter if they play sports or not, we need to protect
those kids, let them come forward without the vigil, without the media and the lynch mob to say I'm sorry | screwed up. At
the end of the day they are teenage kids and all of these kids here deserve to be playing football on Thanksgiving.
Mr. Hill asked if the BOS could supercede the School Committee.
Mr. Alonzo replied no.
Dave Ulf, Autumn Rd. spoke to say that he thinks the BOS and the Town Manager are giving everyone lip service. All
these people here are looking for support and you say that it is up to the School Committee. You are elected officials of
this town and we expect you to run this town. He does not see this as being viable and the School Committee needs to
be up here to listen to this. He asked why are we here and why do we elect you people.
===PAGE 3===
Mir. Alonzo replied that it would be helpful for everyone if people understood who has what authority in town. To know that
the School Committee as a 5 member elected body has the authority over the School Department by State Law, not by
my decision, and not by anyone in this rooms decision, our State Law gives them that authority. You elect them to run the
school. I understand your anger and frustration but it should be directed at the places where you can say make that
change. I'm trying to be honest with you and I'm sorry I'm getting agitated. I don't want to be accused that I'm not doing
something I could do to help you when I don't have that authority, which I made clear at the beginning of this meeting.
don't have that authority not because I choose not to exercise it, but because the law does not give it to me. I you're
asking why you're here tonight I assume it's because you want to voice your opinions and they are important to me. I'm
sorry that we can't do all the things you want us to do, and we may not even agree with the things that you want to do, but
you need to respect the fact that we are hear to listen and | understand both sides. It's not something that I take lightly
and I'm sure that it's not something that the School Committee takes lightly. It is a very difficult decision but the decision
rests with them.
Robin Corbett Sunset Ave. spoke to say that she went to the School Committee meeting tonight and she feels that the
decision was made before she got into the room and she doesn't feel like she had a say. She has been the team mom for
the Juniors and Seniors for years, it breaks her heart, and she's not going to stop until Thanksgiving is over. They
deserve to play and she will remain behind them until she can't anymore.
Anne Nash, 1790 Mass Ave spoke and asked why the School Committee was here with a prepared statement, it this is a
town meeting.
Mr. Alonzo replied that this is a regularly scheduled Selectmen meeting.
Mr. Berthiaume spoke to say that this was a public opportunity on TV for us to explain our justification behind what we did
We do have a meeting tomorrow and I hope that people will come and we invite you to share your concerns at the public
speaking section of our meeting. Given the short time available we wanted to get the information out as early as we
possibly could.
Ms. Nash spoke to say that when you have children who have been on the team for all these years, it's heartbreaking and
it's unfortunate that this one incident can ruin it and it's unfair to these kids. My heart goes out to this boy and his family
but to blame all these kids, we don't know who did this. She went on to say that hopefully the School Committee will hear
the people out and change their minds because it means a lot to a lot of people.
Mr. Szabady spoke and asked the School Committee Chair if they went to the meeting tomorrow is there a chance that
they would change their minds
Mr. Berthiaume replied that the decision is made by a 5 member board and we cannot talk about that outside of a
meeting. We just held an emergency meeting that was duly posted and we will be able to talk about those things in an
open meeting tomorrow night. Due to the Open Meeting Law, we cannot talk about that tonight.
Laura McGrath, Cushing Lane, spoke to say that in addition to it not being fair to punish the whole team there is
something bigger here and that's that we live in America and we are innocent until proven guilty. She feels that the entire
team is being held in a cell until the investigation is completed and you don't even know if it was somebody from
Lunenburg. I'm not saying that the act wasn't disgraceful but we salute the flag every day, we have sons and daughters in
the military, and in America you are innocent until proven guilty, or at least I thought you were.
Stuart Leinson, 494 West Townsend Rd. spoke to say that he knew the vigil was supposed to be about peace but all it
was about was persecuting the coaches and asking for resignations. It was actually disgusting. Why make the decision
to cancel the game until all interviews are done. All the kids have been interviewed by FBI and State Police and they are
not cracking these kids down. Mr. Leinson went on to say that this should be reconsidered, they have worked too hard,

3. Review transition to Regional Dispatch with Fire Chief & Police Chief: Fire Chief Patrick Sullivan and Police Chief
James Marino came before the BOS to give an update on the transition to the Regional Dispatch Center in Devens from
the Public Safety building in Lunenburg.
Chief Sullivan spoke to say that there had been some technology issues such as internet connections, difficulty sending
EMS reports to the hospitals, and the station alerting system. All issues, if they haven't been resolved already, are being
corrected as they move forward. The rapport between the staff at the Dispatch Center and the Fire Department is good
and when there are problems they are addressed by the Director at the Dispatch Center in a timely manner.
Mr. Alonzo asked if the transfer of the servers over to the Regional Dispatch had freed up anything in the Town's
Information Technology (IT) budget.
Ms. Speidel replied no because the Towns IT staff is still very involved with the IT at the Regional Dispatch. This may
lessen but this transition has required a lot of their time and when they are finished they will take on other tasks.
Mr. Alonzo clarified his question by asking who was responsible for maintenance to the equipment and if that frees up the
Towns IT time.
Ms. Speidel replied that we are still months away from that time.
Mr. Ebersole added that the Lunenburg Public Safety building is the back up in the event that Regional Dispatch should
happen to go down.
Chief Marino said that they still maintain 4-5 servers at the Public Safety building, which has nothing to do with Devens
and it was just the records management software that was transferred to the Regional Dispatch servers.
Mr. Alonzo asked about the incoming mandate for Emergency Medical Dispatch (EMD) and if it has been used yet
Chief Sullivan replied that Lunenburg was using this for 1 year prior to the when the Regional Dispatch was, which was
challenging due to the fact that they were using only 1 dispatcher. The advantage to having this software is that the
second dispatcher can get the call processed quicker which meets the mandate. Now that they are getting into their work
flow, they are seeing information coming in quicker to emergency personnel over the radio.
Mr. Ebersole asked if Lunenburg's Town building data shows up on the software also.
Chief Sullivan replied yes. The computer data base from Lunenburg Police & Fire formed the core of the new system
which was created at Devens. All of the other community's data was merged into that and nothing was lost.
Mr. Ebersole mentioned that 70% of calls are now coming through mobile phones and asked if there was anything that
can be done as far as triangulating caller ID
Chief Sullivan replied that there is a system in place and the Director at Regional Dispatch is looking into getting set up to
receive those calls directly. Currently they go through the State Police then they are forwarded out to the local dispatch
center. It would be a function of time and money to be able to do that.
Chief Marino spoke to say that there have been a few bumps in the road, they are making a lot of progress, and most of it
is technology issues. Initially there was an issue with the phone system which was resolved. Lunenburg does not have a
Voiceover IP and Regional Dispatch does. It would work better if Lunenburg had this but it is very expensive and is not
being asked for in this year's Capital Plan.
There were issues with technology concerning a vendor who was slow in installing/moving equipment which was badly
needed and most of that has been resolved. They are still working on a few issues such as if an outside agency is trying
to communicate to someone inside the lock up after hours and there is no desk person, they do not have a transponder to
get into the building. The vendor is working on a system at Regional Dispatch to deal with opening some doors at the
Public Safety building by pushing a button from that location.
Another issue is that if there is a problem in the lock up there is a button that is pushed which enables a camera to swing
around in that area which will notify the dispatcher with a tone. None of this equipment has worked since June 28 and
Chief Marino was not aware of this problem until 2-3 weeks ago. The vendor is also currently working on that problem.
===PAGE 6===
The Citrix connection is still not 100% stable. There was a large issue with the booking camera which was that it wouldn't
work because the software resides in a different location.
The Town's IT along with Regional Dispatch and the
manufacturer have managed to make it work which only amounts to about 60% of the time but they continue to work on
that problem.
Chief Marino would like to have the dispatchers from Regional Dispatch ride along with the patrol people so they can
familiarize themselves with Lunenburg's procedures and policies. His hope is that this will accomplish a team concept.
Chief Marino went on to say that it this is a growing pains issue which they are starting to get over but this takes time, and
his staff understands that.
He remains concerned that there is still 12 hours per day along with weekends that the Public Safety Building is not
manned. He would like to see this changed and has discussed it with the Town Manager. The Desk Officer maintains a
phone log and there is a sign in log in the lobby also, but this cannot be done after hours. It's a very busy place from
7AM-7PM and he's not sure how they can maintain this without staff.
Chief Sullivan agreed with Chief Marino's staffing concerns and said that there was at least one episode where an
unknown person was walking around the building at night knocking on doors after hours. There was one member of the
Fire Dept. on duty but when he looked, he could not find the person and this is a concern.
Ms. Speidel reminded everyone that with the savings that were realized by going to the district were left in Public Safety.
This is being used to fill the position of Desk Officer who also is monitoring the lock up. As we work on the budget we will
have to justify the need for additional funding within Public Safety. We have a very small Police & Fire department for the
number of calls and activity that happens there and this issue is not going away.
There are a number of other communities who are interested in joining the Regional Dispatch District which would further
reduce Lunenburg's assessment which could be used to supplement our Police & Fire Dept. With the addition of more
communities comes other challenges and Ms. Speidel believes that more time is needed to become familiar with existing
issues before this can be done.
She believes that all the staff at the Regional Dispatch has handled the change very well, which has been challenging at
times, and complimented both the Police and Fire Chiefs for the work they have done.
Mr. Alonzo stated that the issue of the Public Safety building being unmanned should be a priority. He asked if there were
any other "red flags" that have not been addressed yet.
Chief Marino replied no, other than the issues that the vendor is still working on they are on the road to getting things
taken care of.
Mr. Alonzo asked about when a call comes in after hours and it rolls over to the dispatch is the call recorded separately on
their side of what comes in to the Lunenburg Police Dept. number as opposed to the 911 calls.
Chief Marino replied yes it is.
Mr. Alonzo said that this could be a good way to track the calls when the station is unmanned.
Chief Marino added that they are having a radio issue which is worsening. There is a 118' radio antenna in Devens and
there is a city north of Boston that is on the same frequency as us which interferes with us. They have been cooperative
with turning their power down. Chief Marino applied 1 year ago for a new frequency and they are difficult to get. He did
obtain one but there were a lot of restrictions on it and when they did go to program they were told it would cost
approximately $15,000 - $20,000 to do so. They continue to work to resolve this issue.
There is a repeater tower on Leominster Shirley Rd. and they installed an antenna on it for the Police & Fire Dept! Chief
Marino asked for Capital Plan funds to purchase a receiver for that which he believes should be a priority. He also asked
for a stand by generator for the Sunny Hill Rd. repeater site.
Ms. Bertram asked if they were requesting additional funding for the Voiceover IP.
Chief Marino replied that the phone system is antiquated but stable.
Chief Sullivan added that it works but it is a challenge for it to come into our station then go back down to dispatch then
back again to us.
The BOS thanked Chief Marino and Chief Sullivan for their updates this evening.
