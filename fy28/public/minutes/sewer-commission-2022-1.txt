Minutes of 01/11/22 Sewer Commission meeting- via Zoom (Video Conference)
Attendees: Vice-Chair- John Reynolds, Clerk- Brett Ramsden, Members: Mike Deveikis and Mike Nault, DPW
Director- Rob Oliva, Business Manager-Barb Lefebvre, Minutes Clerk-Jane Ranley
Absent: Chair- Dave MacDonald
Guests: Richard Blanchflower (5 Chestnut St.) and Greg Roy (Dillis & Roy)
John opened the meeting at 6:01 pm. and did a roll call of attendees. The Commission began the meeting by saying
the Pledge of Allegiance.
PUBLIC COMMENT- none
APPOINTMENTS -
Rich Blanchflower, 5 Chestnut St. and Greg Roy (Dillis & Roy)- Greg stated Rich had gone before the
Commission a couple years ago with a request to add the property to the Sewer Service Area (SSA) for a large
residential development. Rich later withdrew the request but has now modified his plans for the property. Greg
shared the GIS map for the property which shows the small section of frontage on Chestnut St. that is currently the
only portion of his property in the SSA. Originally, Rich proposed a 40-unit development on the site, but now wants
to use the majority of the back of the property for a horse rescue facility, for which he is seeking grant money. The
equestrian facility would be staffed and need water & sewer. Since the soil has a very shallow depth to ledge, there is
no place to put in a septic system and it would need to have a sewer connection. The applicant would also like to
add a much smaller residential development in the front of the property, and it would be advantageous to connect to
sewer. Barb noted that the property in the front of where the equestrian facility would be is 733 Mass. Ave. Brett said
he is open to the residential development and an equestrian facility. Greg estimated there would be about 20
residential, in line with current zoning and wetlands regulations. John inquired about test holes and Rick replied he
went out with Whitman & Bingham for test holes. They hit hard pan ledge 2'-2.5' everywhere and in front it is only 8"-
they would have to blast. John suggested providing the Board of Health with the results of the test holes. Mike D.
asked about the size of the Equestrian Barn and Rick replied it could be 2,000 to 5,000 sq. ft. Barb confirmed the
property on Mass Ave. has been bettered and Greg confirmed there is a manhole there. Mike D. suggested
discussing sewer expansion on Chestnut St. as a future agenda item. Mike N. stated he is open to the idea but would
want more details on the size of the farm & residential development. Brett asked about hitting ledge when getting a
line in and Greg replied they will hit ledge right away. They may want to look at a low-pressure system and extend
the system with a force main. Rob said the need has been demonstrated for sewer and he would be in favor of
adding it to the SSA. John noted the next step is for the town to vote to add the property to the SSA. Greg said it
would be helpful to know if the Commission is in favor of the plan. Barb clarified the first step is the SSA expansion
approval at Town Meeting and the Commission can recommend or not recommend approval. Once approved, they
would still need to present the plan to the other committees in Town. John noted they can recommend going ahead
with adding it to the SSA, but that it does not automatically approve the connection - they are 2 separate processes.
The Commission agreed that they are open to the idea but not ready to take a vote on a motion. Greg said it was
good feedback and their next step is to get it on the Town Meeting warrant. They will also work on developing some
concept plans.
CURRENT BUSINESS -
American Rescue Plan Act (ARPA) funds (Coronavirus Local Fiscal Recovery Fund Policy) - Rob noted Barb
reached out to Fitchburg and Leominster for a meeting. Fitchburg will be hiring a new person, as the Interim
commissioner is also leaving, but a meeting with Leominster has been set up. Rob reported he went through the
document for the use of funds, which shows money is available for water/sewer projects. He is not certain if they can
do the pump station projects but may be eligible under a SRF (Clean Waters State Revolving Funds) project. At
present, they would need more clarification on what they can do. John asked Barb to add the discussion to future
agendas and asked if there is a deadline for applying. Barb replied the Town Manager has asked for a list of
suggested projects to be submitted to the Select Board. Rob added that the document notes costs are obligated by
12/31/24 and to be expended by 12/31/26.

requested changes and she will put it on the next agenda.
Upland/Eastern Ave/White Street extensions- Barb noted she also just received the approved contract from Town
Counsel and will be sending it to Wright Pierce. If they are ok with the contract, we can move ahead. If Wright Pierce
makes any changes, it will need to go back to Town Counsel. Wright Pierce is preparing to start the borings in 2
weeks -around the 24th or 25'h. John confirmed if Wright Pierce agrees with counsel changes, they are good to go
and he asked Barb to keep Dave updated.
Search for Assistant Business Manager- Rob said they interviewed a few candidates last week and have another
interview coming up with a candidate with Munis experience. The position has been readvertised on the state
municipal website.
FY21 Annual Report request- Barb reported she received the notice from the Select Board office for the Annual
Report for FY21 (ending 6/30/21) The Commission discussed, and Jane agreed to put a draft together for Barb.
Minutes - 11/9/21 meeting
Motion: Brett R.
To approve the minutes for 11/9/21.
Mike N. abstained as he was not present for the 11/9 meeting.

received a few and will be reminding the others. FY23 Budget will be on the 1/25 agenda. Barb will be starting the
Q3 billing about the same time. There were no Invoices to review.

The Commission discussed the in-person meeting on 1/25. Barb noted masks are required in all Town buildings. The

Attendees: Chair- Dave MacDonald, Vice-Chair- John Reynolds, Clerk- Brett Ramsden, Member: Mike Nault, DPW

Pledge of Allegiance.
PUBLIC COMMENT- Brett thanked Jane for the nice job on the annual report.
ANNOUNCEMENTS - Barb noted that work on the Q3 billing starts soon.
CURRENT BUSINESS -
FY23 Draft Sewer Budget- Barb reviewed the highlights since the Commission had received the draft shortly before the
start of the meeting. She notes that FY23 Revenues are projected as $1,094,617, which is an increase of $26K over FY22.
Purchase of Service numbers haven't been adjusted from FY22 for the Commission to review and discuss at the next
meeting. Payroll reflects updates but will need a new salary grid from the Personnel Committee for vote at Town Meeting to
be final. Since Barb is at the top of her grade, they will be going to town meeting to ask for a cost-of-living (COLA) increase
and the Accountant had recommended putting an estimated COLA in the 'reserve line until Town Meeting. The Assistant
position is in the budget for $25,310 and she is hopeful the position will be filled before 7/1 so the amount is for a full year.
The Utilities are about the same as last year. As for Other Expenses, Barb put in $50K for the Capital plan for now but will
be a topic of discussion. Leominster usage was estimated at $500K for FY22 and right now it is projected to be about
$484K. Barb reported the FY22 budget is looking good right now. Dave asked if they will be putting any money back into
Retained Earnings for FY22 and Barb replied it is hard to tell at this point. We are 'in the red' (expenses over revenues) right
now but we're about to do the Q3 billing so revenues only reflect 50% for year, while some yearly expenses have already
been posted. In regard to the Maintenance spending for FY22, Rob said he still has money to spend in the spring for
projects such as jetting and routine camera-ing. Dave asked Rob to look at what he would need in FY23. John said the
pump stations are a priority - especially the Dana St. station. Brett said the maintenance program is incredibly important,
and Mike N. agreed that the upkeep of the pump stations is at the heart of it all, plus preventive maintenance which leads to
less callouts. In regard to the Empey (CTE) contract, Barb noted they are going into year 3 and there will be an increase,
which was not noted on the FY23 draft yet. Rob reported the maintenance was done on the generators. Barb noted they had
a 1-year contract for the generator maintenance and AI (CTE) is working on that. John said since CTE is heading into year
3, we should plan to start work on the new contract well ahead of time. Dave suggested looking at extending the contract.
Rob stated that we would need to discuss this with the Town Manager.
ARPA funding- Rob reported that the ARPA funding for a sewer project would need to be a SRF-type project to get high
priority. He added that Heather (Town Manager) has scheduled a Zoom meeting on 1/25 with a consultant regarding the
ARPA funding that he will be attending and suggested having a Commissioner sit in on the meeting.
Upland/Eastern Ave/White Street Engineering Design - Barb received the Wright Pierce contract, approved by Town
Counsel. She added that the Commission needs to vote approve the contract tonight, and requested that at least 3
Commissioners come into the office to sign off on the document in person so she can send it to Wright Pierce.

Street/ Maple Parkway extension.

that there were 2 sections of our regulations that Town Counsel is looking for the Commission's input. Barb read Counsel's
comments on Section 6-3 (Criminal Penalties), Fitchburg(Jeff Murawski) comment #31. The Commission also needs to
accept Counsel's changes to 4-3 (Discharge Documents), Fitchburg's comments 18&19, where he suggests reducing it to

Minutes of 02-08-22 Sewer Commission meeting- via Zoom (Video Conference)
Attendees: Chair- Dave MacDonald, Vice-Chair- John Reynolds, Clerk- Members: Mike Deveikis and Mike Nault,
DPW Director- Rob Oliva, Business Manager-Barb Lefebvre, Minutes Clerk- Jane Ranley
Absent: Brett Ramsden (Clerk)
Dave opened the meeting at 6:01pm. and Barb did a roll call of attendees. The Commission began the meeting by
saying the Pledge of Allegiance.
PUBLIC COMMENT and ANNOUNCEMENTS - none
CURRENT BUSINESS -
FY23 Draft Sewer Budget- Dave suggested tabling the discussion and have a special meeting on 2/15 to discuss
the budget. The Commission discussed the special meeting and agreed to meet 2/15.
Motion: John R.
Second: Mike D.
To have a budget meeting on 2/15 to discuss the budget.
Roll Call Vote: Mike D-aye, Mike N.-aye, Dave-aye and John-aye.
American Rescue Plan Act (ARPA) fund- (Coronavirus Local Fiscal Recovery Fund -Rob attended the
consultant's presentation on ARPA funds. Originally, he thought the money would be just for wastewater projects, but
actually the town will be getting a certain percentage of money for general use. The funds would be easier to use and
also a pump stations project could be funded as an SRF project. Dave suggested prioritizing projects and look at the
1/1 (Inflow/Infiltration) in the Whalom area. Rob noted he and Barb met with Jake Fleming in Leominster regarding
their ARPA fund projects, which will include sewer flow studies and metering. They discussed working together on I/l
in the Whalom area, with flows going to Leominster. Rob agreed the Commission could prioritize projects, including
the Whalom area. John asked Rob if he had any thoughts on whether a good part of the I/l problem is coming from
Whalom. Rob replied there are old services in that area and some additional flow study could help. He added the
Lunenburg system is young compared to others, but there may be spots with leaky joints that haven't been identified.
The amount of I/l does not raise eyebrows with the DEP and a good percentage of what they see is most likely from
the Whalom area. John asked Commissioners to notify Rob and Barb with any potential projects. Barb noted Paul
Casey (Flow Assessment) confirmed that the equipment in the meter pits has all been updated. Rob added Flow
Assessment has committed to another lease year, at the same price. Rob will talk to Kevin Olson (Wright Pierce) for
guidance on possible projects. Dave asked about the deadlines and Rob replied 2024 to come up with projects and
2026 to spend the money.
Policy/By-law updates- Dave stated this is an opportunity to update policies for the town meeting. Barb noted she
will be talking to Town Counsel on 2/24. The Warrant Articles are due by 3/21 and they would need to name any
policies they are updating. They will also need 2 public hearings on the policy changes before town meeting. Barb
screen shared an excel sheet of Policies/By-laws for the Commission to review for the Spring Town Meeting.
1. DEP requirement- Barb will confirm if it is still required on projects over 1,000 feet.
2. Drain Layers- Barb will ask Counsel if necessary to still require a notary stamp.
3. Prorating of bills- Barb noted the minimum is $107. Commission discussed and Barb will check with the
Water District regarding their prorating policy.
4. Wells- Barb stated there are 9 users on wells and currently there is a set quarterly charge of approximately
$200 for each. The Commission discussed options for charging based on number of bedrooms, number of
residents or square footage of the house. The Commission will discuss the policy again at a future meeting.
5. Connection Policy-Rob stated they currently charge $1,000 to re-connect to an existing service or laying
a new service to a property that was previously sewered. Since it involves administrative work and an
inspection by him, he suggested charging $50 to $100 as a fairer amount. The Commission discussed and
agreed to change the fee from $1,000 to $100.
Motion: John R.
Second: Mike D.
To change the re-connection fee from $1,000 to $100.
Roll Call Vote: Mike N.-aye, John-aye, Mike D.-aye and Dave-aye.
===PAGE 2===
6. Change to Bonds process - Rob noted they currently only require performance bonds with road openings.
Barb added she has had drain layers ask about requiring bonds and other towns do require them. She
noted in Fitchburg, drain layers give the City $5,000 on account. Dave said the bond is to cover damage to
the infrastructure. The Commission discussed and Rob said he will get more information for future
discussion.
7. Change to Specifications - Rob stated they do not have details spelled out for specifications for a new
service. He would like to offer more detailed information for engineers, noting 6" pipes and slope of 2%.
Barb also asked to discuss the use of the terms service vs extension. Commission will discuss this later.
8. Waiver - Rob said Town Counsel suggested adding a paragraph to the by-law about granting waivers. Barb
said the Commission had discussed the waiver in 2018 and decided not to do it then. She suggested the
Commission look at it again and discuss it.
9. Easements - Rob suggested an easement would be a minor compromise to connect a property to sewer, if
a property could not do a septic system and there was no other way to connect. In the past the Commission
did not allow easements because they did not want to encourage development through an easement. He
suggested wording it carefully or do them on a case-by-case basis. He asked to consider an easement to
sewer in cases of Title 5 failure and the only option was a tight tank. The Commission discussed and agreed
it should be on a case-by-case hardship basis. They will also get an opinion from Town Counsel.
10. Deduct Meter policy - Barb said the policy needs to state that the meters should read in cubic feet.
11. Grease Trap policy - Discussion on number of establishments that are in compliance. Barb reported that
most are compliant. Dave noted when the policy first started there was a lot of establishments not inspecting
their grease traps and they were paying a company to do inspections. Now establishments are paying a fee
even when they are compliant. The Commission discussed non-compliance fees, charging for inspections

Kevin Olson, Wright Pierce-Upland/Eastern/White/Maple Parkway extension discussion- Barb screen-shared
the memo provided by Wright Pierce. Kevin explained they were hired by the Sewer Commission to look at the
potential for sewer extensions at Upland/Eastern and at White/Maple Parkway. They have done soil borings to get a
preliminary understanding of the ledge in those streets and developed conceptual plans for a gravity sewer layout. In
addition, they looked at a low-pressure system, similar to what the Town did on Pratt Street and Lakeview Ave.
several years ago. The preliminary costs were estimated, which was converted into a betterment fee estimate. Brett
asked if it was necessary to go 5'to 6' deep. Kevin replied that for a gravity system it would be deeper than that but
for a low-pressure system they typically go 5' or a little more. He added that it could be riskier in going shallower and
the difference between 4' & 5' would not make a big difference in cost. Kevin noted that they found most of the ledge
on Eastern and Upland. If the town moved forward with a low-pressure system in either area, the ledge quantities
would be on the lower side. Barb noted there was a big difference in digging for the gravity vs. the ledge. Kevin said
they could revisit it again, but the 20% contingency could cover it if they are a little short on the ledge quantity. The
project costs come down significantly with the low-pressure system but all private property costs such as the low-
pressure grinder pumps are on the resident. The gravity sewer would be a $57K betterment and the low-pressure
system would be a $40K betterment- both paid out over 20 years. Mike noted the cost and maintenance for the E1
pump and that the E-1 is what you go with if you have no other options. Kevin estimated the average septic system
costs about $30K and there are some tight tanks on Upland. He added the gravity seemed like the better option, but
costs are high, so they wanted to give another option. Dave noted if they go with the low- pressure system, the
customers will get charged up front for a privilege fee and the E1 pump, but the privilege fee can be spread over 20
years. Brett asked for an estimated installation cost on an E-1, without needing to blast. John replied there are
internal or external pumps. Where there is ledge, the internal pump is the better option and costs $7K plus $500 for
the electrical hookup. Dave stated for few thousand more, they can go with a gravity system and pay over 20 years
as a betterment. John pointed out both systems need to go below the frost line from the house to the main. The cost
to handle the ledge would be about the same. Kevin said they used GIS for making estimates and he suspects that
there may be no homes who have pipes that go straight out due to the ledge. The gravity option may be more
challenging and they may need a pump to pump up to a gravity line. Dave asked Matt for recommendations. Matt
replied that they just bid on a project with cost of $13K for a grinder pump, with installation. Kevin added the low
pressure system shows a lower betterment cost, but they need to install the pump and you have another piece of
equipment that could break. Gravity systems do not generally break. Brett asked if the gravity system needs to be 8'
deep and John replied the external pump needs to be 7' deep. Kevin said they created profiles based on borings for
the gravity system and they have options to get the service above the ledge but under the frost line. John noted the
E-1 pumps require Unitil or a generator, in case of power outage. Mike said he likes the gravity option, with
significantly lower upfront costs. Barb noted the residents have not yet been surveyed and it would be easier to sell
as a combined project. The Commission discussed and agreed to present it as a total project for both areas. Matt
