Capital Planning Committee Minutes 
October 26, 2021 
The Capital Planning Committee met in the Ritter Building as scheduled with the following members present:  Tom Alonzo, 
Dave Passios, George Martin, Paula Bertram and Brian Lehtinen.  Town Manager Heather Lemieux was also in attendance. 
1. Call to Order – Chairman called meeting to order at 5:36 p.m. 
 
2. Public Comment: Comment from the Committee, Comment from the Public – No public or Committee comment.  
 
3. Approval of Minutes – No minutes available, Paula will send out minutes of last meeting and this meeting before the 
11/2/2021 meeting. 
 
4. Review Capital Requests Submitted by Fire Department and Land Use Director – 
 
Land Use Director – Adam Burney, Land Use Director, explained that the Planning Board is requesting funding to complete 
a Master Plan.  Previously the Boards strategy was to submit two $50,000 articles in two fiscal years.  The first of which 
was approved.  During the process it was determined that we had very inaccurate population projections.  A portion of 
the $50,000 funding ($7000) was used for a population study.   Following the population study, a bid was put out for 
completion of 2 chapters of the plan: zero bids were received, and all potential bidders indicated that the Town was asking 
too much for the small amount of funding available.    Adam conducted research and found that other communities were 
spending between $135,000-$225,000.  Mr. Burney noted that the r amp up and mobilization by the successful bidder is 
the bulk of cost, once that is completed, during the writing  of the various elements all the same general sets of data , 
information, public input, and participation is utilized.  The cost of ramp up/mobilization is in the 65,000 range.  Therefore, 
the Planning Board has requested $110,000 in this year’s Capital Plan.  Coupled with the $43,000 remaining from the initial 
article, there is $153,000 available to complete the entire Master Plan as one project.  The master plan sets a foundation 
for policy and for decision making and creates one unified document that helps guide municipal decisions that are made 
on many different levels. 
 
George Martin asked for further information about what a Master Plan is.  Mr. Burne y explained that a Master Plan is 
required by Mass General Law.  It is  a blueprint or map to where the Town will go over the next 20 years .  It covers land 
use and zoning, cultural and natural resources, housing,  and economic development.  It is designed t o guide decision 
making through consensus building.  There are 8 elements within the plan, each element has goals and objectives, and 
the plan outlines an implementation plan that helps the Town achieve those goals.  An example would be if the goal were 
to encourage more industrial development the plan would identify areas that would be appropriate, identify possible uses, 
and ways to help facilitate that goal.  Tom Alonzo asked for clarification regarding the required funding; the original plan 
was to eventually obtain $100,00 0 and now the Planning Board is seeking a total of $153,000?  Mr. Burney replied 
affirmatively.  Tom Alonzo question if the Board would make a commitment to keep the Master Plan up to date?  Paula 
Bertram, (Planning Board member) stated that she thought the Board is committed to updating the plan as needed to 
keep it current.  Adam Burney noted that the current plan is 19 years old.  Mr. Burney has dealt with communities that 
have much older plans.  Paula Bertram noted that the community has changed significantly since it was originally written 
(advent of sewer and dense development).  Adam Burney told the Committee he expects that it will take 18 months to 2 
years to complete update.    Brian Lehtinen asked if any consideration was given to doing the plan inhouse?  Mr. Burney 
responded that there are certain  elements that really can’t be done inhouse.  For example, c ompleting the facilities  
element in house could be a recipe for disaster and it may be colored by political views.  Mr. Burney added that it would 
be an incredibly big lift to do inhouse and having a consultant would be advantageous.  Tom Alonzo noted that doing the 
plan inhouse would take much longer as the Planning Board is occupied with conducting current business and could also 
negatively affect volunteerism.

The Capital Planning Committee met in the Ritter Building as scheduled with the following members present:  Tom Alonzo,

and Superintendent Burnham presented the capital plan that was approved by the School Committee. 
Primary School Air Conditioning Project – Superintendent Burnham explained that air conditioning  will improve 
comfort to the building’s occupants.  The air conditioning at the middle high school provides a positive impact and 
keeps humidity down.  The school is required to keep the environment within s pecific temperature range for some 
students that have heat limitations  and IEP’s, because high heat can trigger seizures.  The school t ypically tries to 
install air conditioner s of some type in any classroom that houses students with heat limitations.   This presents a 
challenge because students are not in one room all day.   
Dave Passios asked about the electrical capability of the building to accommodate the system that is needed, he noted 
that when it w as designed back in 2005  it was designed to accommodate air conditioning.  Mr. Murphy responded 
that there is space in the nearest panel .    The current engineer is advocating for a separate p anel because of surge 
concerns and equipment damage.  However, before John Londa left, they drilled down and determined that there is 
enough room in the main panel to support the large equipment, and that the secondary panel can accommodate the 
smaller equipment. Mr. Passios question if this new equipment would max out these panels.  Mr. Passios noted that 
over the last four- or five-decades corners have been cut in upgrades and then we have issues later down the road.  
Tom Alonzo questioned if the pricing proposed is with a separate panel? Mr. Murphy responded that it is not, this is 
a paired down number.  Mr. Murphy added after completing the drill down and it was shown that there was enough 
capacity the engineer indicated he will sign off.   Dave Passios cautioned that this be vetted 100% with the engineer. 
Brian Lehtinen questioned why the air conditioning wasn’t installed in 2005 when the building was constructed?  John 
Murphy responded that it was to bring down the overall cost of the school.    
Tom Alonzo noted that the description of the project is for air conditioning in classrooms and questioned if any of the 
other schools have air conditioning?  Superintendent Burnham responded that there are smaller units in server rooms 
and the office.  Mr. Alonzo questioned what areas of the school this proposed project will cover.  Mr. Murphy 
responded that it would serve the classrooms and larger areas such as the library and café.  Mr. Alonzo questioned 
how many students have special needs due to heat sensitivity?  Superintendent Burnham did not have that number 
however there are currently 7 large units installed in classrooms.  She added that there is a student at TCP in the ACE 
program that falls into that category. 
Tom Alonzo questioned h ow many days on average do we exceed th e heat threshold?  Superintendent Burnham 
responded that medical providers identify the temperature range based on the individual student .  She added that 
the temperature is different every year, second week in June this past year we had a very hot spell for several days in 
a row, and there were instances of students and staff showing symptoms of heat exhaustion and having to go home.  
Superintendent was not sure if anyone from the primary had to go home during the same time period.

RITTER BUILDING 
 
1. Call to order -at 5:38pm. In attendance was D Passios, Brian Lehtinen, Town Manager 
Heather Lemieux, Tom Alonzo, and Paula Bertram via Zoom 
2. Public comment: comment from the Committee, Comment from the Public-B Lehtinen 
stated that he sent some questions to the Town Manager regarding the Priority list for the FY23 
Capital Plan. 
 Asked: Is there a score for the $25K for Marshall Park, he couldn’t find a number and assumed 
it the Town Beach not Marshall Park. H Lemieux answered that she would check will R. Oliver to 
see if he has a figure and fill out the evaluation for that project. She found that the Marshall 
Park Survey was not included in the DPW Director’s presentation or worksheet.  
Asked: What is the life or expected life left on the Brooks House roof? Answered: According to 
John Londa, that roof is 25-30 years old and it is at its life expectancy but there are no active 
leaks. 
Asked: What is the estimated cost just for the cost of a pump generator the senior center?  
Answered: She will check with R. Oliver and their Facilities Director. 
Asked: Looking at the full list, the police had 4 items but on the priority list there were only 3. Is 
the $67,805 replacement vehicle removed from the list, or a duplicate entry? Answered: She 
only has 3 on the list, doesn’t see a 4th. She sent an attachment of the Excel document with 
each of the individual worksheets for the departments and the Chief’s PowerPoint 
presentation. She followed up with a confirmation that the Cruiser has been pushed out. 
D Passios asked H Lemieux to go over the list. She responded with DPW Director recommended 
taking the Public Safety Building HVAC and Windows off and if the drainage around the building 
is addressed it would relieve moisture in the training room. She’d like a professional window 
assessment and possibly get funded through Green Communities.   
D Passios spoke about getting a generator from the hardware store to run the sump pump 
rather than have it on the Capital Plan. He also voiced his opinion that the senior center is going 
to need a full size generator for future use as an emergency shelter.  H Lemieux added that she 
spoke with the COA director, and the generator was requested for the center to be used as an 
===PAGE 2===
emergency shelter for ice storms, the running of the sump pump during power outages, and for 
a cooling center. She added that the Fire Chief supported it.
