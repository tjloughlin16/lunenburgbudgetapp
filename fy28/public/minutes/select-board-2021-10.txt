Meeting with Lisa Leblanc re: Civics Curriculum and Partnership with Town Government
Ms. LeBlanc thanked the board for allowing her to come to speak to them about the connections
between the civics curricular changes in the history and social science department and how that
impacts our connection to local government and some of the connections we are hoping to foster.
She noted the Dept. of Secondary Education emphasizes that students should be educated in the
history of the Commonwealth, the United States and the World. And that we are educating
students to make civic choices and assume their responsibility for strengthening equality, justice,
and liberty in and beyond the United States.
Standards for History and Social Science Practice, Pre-K-12*
1. Demonstrate civic knowledge, skills, and dispositions.
2. Develop focused questions or problem statements and conduct inquiries.
3. Organize information and data from multiple primary and secondary sources.
4. Analyze the purpose and point of view of each source; distinguish opinion from fact.
5. Evaluate the credibility, accuracy, and relevance of each source.
6. Argue or explain conclusions, using valid reasoning and evidence.
7. Determine next steps and take informed action, as appropriate.
Civics Knowledge and Disposition
• Civic knowledge includes the core knowledge in the Content Standards relating to civics
and government, economics, geography, and history.
• Civic intellectual skills encompass knowing how to identify, assess, interpret, describe,
analyze and explain matters of concern in civic life.
• Civic participatory skills encompass knowing how to make and support arguments, use
the political process to communicate with elected officials and representatives of
government, and plan strategically for civic change.
• Civic dispositions encompass values, virtues, and behaviors, such as respect for others,
commitment to equality, capacity for listening, and capacity for communicating in ways
accessible to others.
The three grades that have the local significant tie-in are:
Kindergarten/Grade 1: Community/members and their roles including government, voting,
elections and being a citizen
Grades 3 & 5: MA cities and towns today and in history, responsibilities of governments at the
federal, state and local levels
Grade 8: Rights and responsibilities of citizens, structure of MA state and local government
(Governor Baker has mandated that students in grade 8 have a full year of civics.)

Discussion and Presentation on Special Town Meeting Articles on the TC Passios Debt
Exclusion Article and Design Funding Article
Mr. Jeffreys stated tonight's intent is to provide the presentation for the warrant articles
submitted on behalf of the TCP Building Design Committee.
One article is for the renovation of the TCP building and the other is for funding the design
development for a new build at the same site. The renovation figure was originally $23.5
million, but has increased by 30% to almost $29 million. The article for design development
requests $350,000 from free cash.
The cost of the new build is $39,000,637. The reasons for the price increase are because the
costs of construction, steel, drywall and concrete have increased dramatically since the spring
as well as an increase in supply and demand.
===PAGE 6===
Mr. Jeffreys presented slides detailing the scope and work of the committee and the architect,
including an overview of the current building condition, interviewing town and school
departments and other groups interested in using the building, holding meetings to get
feedback from stakeholders, reviewing floor plans to best utilize the building, reviewing
interior/exterior plans to address traffic flow and parking access and reviewing costs analysis
to find savings.
$54,000 was appropriated at the 2021 Annual Town Meeting to research what a new build
would cost. DiGiorgio Associates were hired to provide a preliminary design and rough cost
estimate for comparison. Design consideration included separate entrances for town, school
and community use of spaces, parking, green space for various programs and open space to
accommodate concerts, community events, etc.
He presented a building usage breakdown of new (33,170 sq. ft.) versus renovated (33,170 sq.
ft.). He moved on to the wording for Article 8 which requests the $350,000 for the design
development,
To see if the Town will vote to raise and appropriate or transfer from available funds,
$350,000 for the design and engineering required to construct a new building, Lunenburg
Town Hall and Community Center, that consolidates town offices, school uses, and
community space at the location of the building and land known as the TC Passios,
located at 1025 Massachusetts Avenue, shown on Assessor's Map 060, Lot 055, and any
and all incidental and related costs, including but not limited to, site evaluation,
engineering, and final design services; or take any other action relative thereto.
A "yes" vote would:
* Identify that the Town wants to move forward with a new Town Hall and Community
Center at the location currently occupied by the TC Passios Building.
* Commit funds, from free cash, to complete the building design process in anticipation
of a vote at the 2022 Annual Town Meeting, to construct a new Town Hall &
Community Center.
Mr. Jeffreys reviewed the terminology used, the process of designing public projects, how the
various town buildings are currently used by town, school and community.
Terminology:
Program: what the space is used for; a set of activities with a particular long-term aim.
Adjacencies: what is next to what; close or near
Schematic Design Phase: Conducting program assessments, surveys, and field tests while
engaging stakeholders in order to identify the feasibility of a project, prepare a
diagram/depiction, and its rough estimated cost.
Design Development Phase: Advancing schematic design by conducting analysis of major
building components including foundations; structures; electrical systems; plumbing; and
heating, ventilating, and air conditioning (HVAC) systems in order to prepare detailed floor
plans, architectural renderings, and its estimated cost.

Article 8 Funding Source is Certified Free Cash
What is Free Cash?
As per the Division of Local Services (DLS), MA Department of Revenue, "Free Cash is a
revenue source that results from the calculation, as of July 1, of a community's remaining,
unrestricted funds from its operations of the previous fiscal year based on the balance sheet as of
===PAGE 8===
June 30. It typically includes actual receipts in excess of revenue estimates and unspent amount
for the year just ending." It is unspent taxes that have already been collected.
As per DLS "free cash should be restricted to paying one-time expenditures, funding capital
projects, or replenishing other reserves."
He presented a history of past available Free Cash ranging from $484,117 in FY15 to $2,666,962
in FY22.

Single or Split Tax Rate
Municipalities must decide whether (1) to tax all classes of property at their full and fair cash valuation share
of the tax levy, which results in a single tax rate, or (2) to reduce the share of the tax levy paid by the
residential and shift those taxes to commercial, industrial and personal property taxpayers, which results in a
split tax rate. Either way, the total tax Levy remains the same.
Levy Allocation
The Select Board must decide the percentages of the tax levy that each class of real property and personal
property will bear. To do so, a residential factor is adopted. The residential factor governs the percentage of
the tax levy to be paid by Class One, Residential properties. The difference is shifted to Class Three--
Commercial, Class Four-Industrial and Personal Property (or "CIP").
The adopted factor cannot be less than the minimum residential factor (MRF) calculated by DOR. The MRF represents the maximum
shift allowed in the tax levy for the year and establishes the parameters for local decision-making.
Single Tax Rate
A residential factor of "1" results in the taxation of all property at the same rate. Each property class pays its
full and fair cash valuation share of the tax levy (ex: if the value of all residential properties make up 80% of
the total assessed valuation, residential taxpayers will pay 80% of the tax levy).
Split Tax Rate
A residential factor of less than "1" reduces the share of the tax levy paid by the residential class and increases
the share paid by the CIP classes. The result is two tax rates: one for residential properties and a second,
higher rate for CIP properties. A factor greater than "1" may be adopted, which would have the opposite
effect. The maximum shift in the tax levy allowed for the year is determined as follows:
