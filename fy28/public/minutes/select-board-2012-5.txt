10/23/12
The Board of Selectmen met in the Joseph F. Bilotta Meeting Room, Town Hall as scheduled with David Matthews, Paula
Bertram, Steven M. deBettencourt, Tom Alonzo, Carl "Ernie" Sund and Town Manager Kerry Speidel present.
Regular Meeting opened at 7:00 PM with the Pledge of Allegiance.
7:00PM PUBLIC COMMENT
Mr. Alonzo thanked the Fire Department for holding their Annual Open House the previous weekend. He commented
that it was very well attended and how great it is that the community comes out to support this event. Mr. Sund echoed
Mr. Alonzo's comments.
ANNOUNCEMENTS
Mr. Matthews announced that the "Yard Waste Days" at the landfill will be open on Saturdays from October 20" through
November 17" from 8AM - 4PM. Additional information is available at www.lunenburgma.gov

8PM.

October 25" from 5:30-7PM for people who reside in Precinct B.
Mr. Sund announced that the Lunenburg Fire Department will be holding a "Meat Raffle" on November 9"' at the Public
Safety Building.
APPOINTMENTS
1. Chief Jim Marino to present Joshua Matthieu, Patrol Officer - Lunenburg Police Chief introduced Joshua
Matthieu, Patrol Officer, to the BOS. He gave a brief overview of Officer Matthieu's education and employment history
with the Town of Lunenburg and requested that Officer Matthieu be given a full time position on the force to become
effective on November 28, 2012.
2. Mike Padula, Right of First Refusal, for a portion of 131 Leominster-Shirley Road - Mr. Padula spoke to
the BOS to request removing this portion of land out of Chapter 61A. Ms. Speidel read the terms of the sale to the BOS,
which is the sale from Padula Lunenburg LLC to PSG Realty 393-435L.I.C at a purchase price of $900,000. This land is a
5 acre parcel that is contained within a larger parcel.
Mr. Matthews stated that the normal process for this procedure would be to have the Conservation Commission and
Planning Board evaluate the request before the BOS can act upon it.
Ms. Bertram asked Mr. Padula if the sub-division of the property has been done yet to which Mr. Padula replied no.
Ms. Speidel will relay this information to the Conservation Commission and Planning Board for their input.
3. Video & CD Rentals, Automatic Amusement Device License - A representative from El Dorado Amusement
came before the BOS to request an Automatic Amusement Device License to operate a CD/DVD rental kiosk within their
establishment located at 5 Summer St.
Ms. Bertram moved to approve the license requested for El Dorado Amusement. Mr. Alonzo seconded. On vote, motion
carried, 5 in favor, 0 opposed.

4. North Leominster Rod & Gun Club, One Day Liquor License - Mike Sauvageau, Building Inspector/Zoning
Officer spoke to the BOS regarding the recent complaints from a resident of Lancaster Ave regarding excessive noise
coming from the North Leominster Rod & Gun Club. Mr. Sauvageau gave a history of the complaints from this resident
which date back to 2006. He stated that he visited the Lancaster Ave residence along with the Rod & Gun Club to
investigate the noise decibels and reports of illegal expansion of gun ranges at that club.
Mr. Sund voiced his concern about the size of the weapons being shot at the club and said that while researching the club
via Google Earth, it appeared that expansion has occurred on that property. Mr. Sauvageau replied that Mr. Sund is
probably seeing the concrete "bunkers" which they shoot from and that when he visited the club and asked about
expansion they told him that there has been none. His opinion is that the "bunkers" are approximately 30 years old and
the weapons being fired are within the legal limits of the noise ordinance and unless you can get them to voluntarily move
the range there is nothing that can be done to help this situation. By law the club can operate during the hours of 8AM
and 10PM.
Mr. Sauvageau stated that he is sympathetic towards the complainants issue but after due and diligent inspection it was his
opinion that the North Leominster Rod & Gun Club are within their rights legally to operate their shooting range and are
not in violation of any zoning law regarding expansion of firing ranges. Mr. Sauvageau has submitted a chronology of his
findings to the Town Manager.
Mr. Matthews suggested that the size of the gun range be documented incase any further complaints of expansion should
arise in the future. Ms. Bertram spoke in agreement but cautioned the use of Google Earth for documentation due to the
fact that there are other situations that can change the topography that may not have anything to do with expansion.
Mr. Alonzo moved to approve the one day wine & malt license for the North Leominster Rod & Gun Club on November
16, 2012 from 6PM-11PM. Ms. Bertram seconded. On vote, motion carried, 5 in favor, O opposed.

3. Review Letter from RCAP Solutions regarding potential in-kind contributions for Eagle Heights
Housing Project - Mr. Matthews explained to the public who RCAP Solutions was and what its purpose is.
They are currently trying to procure HUD funding to develop the old Primary School into senior housing. A
follow-up letter was sent by RCAP Solutions to the BOS detailing "In- Kind" donations which the Town of
Townsend had given to them for the Townsend Woods project, which HUD considers important in order to
maximize a future application submittal. RCAP stated in this letter that the donation of the building and land as
well an In-Lieu-of-Tax Agreement (PILOT) would have a significant impact on the viability of the Eagle Heights
development in HUD's eyes and in the application in a number of scoring categories, particularly those related to
leveraging resources.
Mr. Matthews stated that this has been an ongoing process for a couple of years and that a large contingency of
Lunenburg senior citizens have voiced their desire to have this project built in the center of town, to which the BOS gives
their full support.
Ms. Speidel stated that there is no deadline date set for the next round of grant funding through HUD and this matter does
not need to be decided on this evening.
Ms. Bertram said that the BOS and community have fully supported this project but there is a cost associated with this
type of development in regards to infrastructure and she would like more information from RCAP on what they are
looking for in terms of a PILOT.
Ms. Speidel stated that the Board of Assessor's has stated that they do no believe that there is any legal basis for providing
a PILOT for this type of development even though this company has negotiated PILOT agreements with other
communities for similar projects.
The BOS discussed that there was no mention in the RCAP's letter of a PILOT agreement with Townsend and agreed that
they could provide the same concessions that Townsend did which are as follows:
• Donation of nearly 6 acres of prime building land valued conservatively at $250K
• Unitil waived hookup fees as well as installation of electric and gas lines a not cost to project.
• Townsend Zoning Board wavier of 40B Filing Fees.
• Townsend Fire Dept. and other town entities wavier of various inspection fees.
• Extension of the Land Disposition Agreement to provide HUD/Lenders underlining Town's support of project.
Mr. Alonzo stated that the BOS could also ask the Sewer and Water Departments to waive fees also.
Ms. Speidel will update all information pertaining to the Eagle Heights project to be discussed again at a future BOS
meeting and will contact the Sewer and Water Departments so that discussions can begin with them also.

Mark Cappadona, representing Colonial Power Group Inc., came before the BOS to provide an update on the Municipal
Aggregation Program. Due to current market conditions the group is unable to attain a rate that is lower than Unitil's
Basic Service rate, so the program will be suspended until market conditions improve and they can deliver a lower rate to
the community. In the 12 months that the program has been running the participants in Lunenburg have saved
approximately between $120,000 and $150,000. The final numbers from Unitil have not been received yet but it is
estimated to be about $30.00 per household. Each houschold participating in the program will be notified through the
mail of this situation. When the program begins again, it will be automatically done and participants will not need to sign
up again.
The current rate which the residents are paying is $0.0585 which will be increasing to Unitil's rate of $0.07023. Currently
the best Colonial Power Group could do was approximately $0.0760. It could possibly be within a month that the rates go
down again but at this time there are no suppliers that can supply a rate lower than Unitil. The change to Unitil's Basic
Service will take effect with the December meter reading for all participating customers. Colonial Power Group will
continue to monitor rates over the course of the winter and the Town will resume the program once it is able to obtain
rates that offer residents and businesses cost savings.

3. Notice of Commonwealth's Proposed Acquisition of Agricultural Preservation Restriction (APR) on
Property in Lunenburg
a. 294 Chase Road, Lanni Orchards
b. 490 Chase Road, Pierce Farm
Ms. Speidel explained that Mario Lanni from Lanni Orchards and Daniel & Kathleen Pierce from Pierce Farm have filed
applications for APR with the State on their respective properties. They are looking to sell their development rights on the
properties to the State in exchange for a sum of money which would help them maintain their farms.
The State will come out to look at their properties and determine the value of the development rights. If the development
rights are purchased a lien is placed on the property which is filed at the Registrar of Deeds and it cannot be removed. If
the properties go into this program they are required to continue to farm the property in the manner that it is currently
being farmed.
Ms. Speidel explained that there are many steps to this process but two in particular are of concern this evening. The
Board first needs to read into record that there is an application for APR on the Lanni Orchards property which will begin
a 120 day clock. The Pierce Farm property application was already read into a record at a prior BOS meeting.
===PAGE 3===
The second action is that the State would like for the Town to be a partner in the purchase of the APR. The way that the
State determines the amount that they would seek from the community is based on an evaluation of how "farm friendly"
the community is. Generally they are looking for a 20% commitment from the Town but there are a series of evaluations
that are made and if the Town is undertaking activities that make the community "farm friendly" there would be a
reduction in the contribution. Ms. Speidel was advised that the reduction would be to 10%.
If the Town chooses not to be a partner it would be called a "bargained sale" and the property owner would receive 10%
less in the sale. If the Town chooses to partner with the State, this matter would need to go to Town Meeting for. a vote.
Currently the State is looking to see if the Town is interested in beginning this process.
Ms. Speidel stated that this is difficult time to be considering making any type of contribution to purchase development
rights. She feels that for a reasonable amount of money the Town can ensure the protection of these two properties as
farmland and that it deserves some serious consideration.
Mr. Matthews read into record the Notice of the APR on Property in the Town of Lunenburg owned by Lanni Orchards,
292 Chase Rd. and then signed it.

3. Review Policies & Procedures- CORI Policy - Mr. Matthews explained what the CORI (Criminal Offender
Record Information) procedure was and stated that the BOS has never had a CORI Policy in place to guide decisions and
discussions.
Mark Terry, Labor Counsel for the Town of Lunenburg, was present to assist and answer questions regarding the draft
CORt Policy which had been distributed to the BOS. He explained that there have been changes regarding the CORI
statute, one being that all employers, not just municipalities, are required to adopt a CORI Policy if they fall into a certain
category which is defined as running five CORI checks a year. This policy will apply to individuals who are seeking
employment with the Town, volunteers, internships, and to those who are applying for licenses.
Mr. Terry said that there is a "model policy" which the State has provided, to which Labor Counsel has suggested some
modifications. The change occurred in Paragraph 3B which made reference to a conditional offer of employment, which
has been removed. The change in the law was designed to preclude employers of all kinds from trying to make decisions
based on conviction information.
Discussion ensued regarding the issue of Executive Session to discuss licensee CORI findings and if the licensee would
be allowed into Executive Session for discussions. Mr. Terry stated that you could go into Executive Session under
Reason 7 of the Law to discuss things that are considered confidential by State Law because the Statute itself precludes
===PAGE 2===
the general dissemination of the information. Under this provision of the law the individual does not have a right to be
present in that discussion. Although it is not necessary, Mr. Terry suggested that it might be wise to invite the individual
into Executive Session so that the BOS are sure that they have the correct information regarding the individual.
Also discussed was when the time is appropriate to run a CORI check. The design of the policy is first to adopt a policy
that complies with the state requirements, secondly is the implementation of the policy which will include some of the
procedures that are adopted and the statutory consideration of when it is required and when it is not, and third is a general
policy judgment as to who is making the decision, what the decision is and why the decision is being made. Mr. Terry
also recommended that consistency is necessary when running CORI checks on employees. He also called to the board's
attention that the policy calls for the Town Manager to be the person adopting the administrative procedures.
Board members were in agreement that they need to adopt procedures fairly quickly and that they need to specify what
circumstances determine what their procedures will be regarding CORI checks.
