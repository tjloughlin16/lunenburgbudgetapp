STATUS REPORT FROM PRINCIPAL ASSESSOR.
 Status of 2025 Abatement Applications. 8 of 58 update. Other items as needed.
There have been 2 applications for 8 of 58 processes for remediation for 2024 
taxes. Once the abatements for this year are completed the Board will then review
the abatements, including conducting inspections. Once those are completed the 
Board will make any needed adjudications about what changes should then be 
provided to Commissioner of Revenue.
John Rabbitt states everyone has received their tax bills. Residents have 30 days 
to apply for an abatement. Rena Sweezey confirms there have been 3 abatements 
filed.
Rena Sweezey suggests straightening out the site indexes/neighborhood maps and
starting with the lakes.
Public comment- Resident/Applicant has submitted questions in writing to the 
Board. She inquired about the length of days until she received an update or 
answer from the Board. Brian Laffond states there is a plan to decide and hold a 
vote at the next meeting on January 22nd.  It is unclear how long the state will take
to make a decision.

 Review and Approve Minutes of December 18, 2024, November 20, 2024, November 6,

CALL TO ORDER
 The meeting opened at 6:30 PM.
OPENING COMMENTS
 Board
 There were no comments from the Board.
 Public
 There were no comments from the public.
STATUS REPORT FROM PRINCIPAL ASSESSOR.
 Status of FY2025 Abatement Applications, CAI/Vision Property Record Cards Update. 
Other items as needed.
Lynda McQuade provided the following updates:
- 6 abutters list requests were received.
- 109 real estate exemptions were processed. There are an additional 6 real 
estate exemptions from the first of the year.
- A total of 16 real estate and personal property abatements applications have 
been received. The deadline is February 3rd.
- 16 motor vehicle excise tax abatements have been processed, and the office is 
currently up to date.
- 490 forms of list have been mailed out for FY 26.
Lynda McQuade confirms that the property cards online have been updated, and 
the CAI property card has been removed. Property record cards are updated once 
a year after the actual tax bill goes out.
The goals for next month include:
-      Growth for FY 26
-      Schedule inspections for FY 25 real estate and personal property    
    abatements
-      Review fiscal 26-chapter lien applications
The Interim Town Manager is working on the budget now.  Lynda McQuade submitted 
the following documents on January 21:
- Salaries, contracted services, maps, postage, office supplies, mileage 
reimbursement, dues and memberships, schooling
o $17,000 for cyclical inspections to be completed by Vision. This is a 3-
year contract.
===PAGE 2===
- $25,000 is budgeted for the neighborhood revision. Per Department of 
Revenue there are 500 lake properties that have site indexes to be reviewed.
- $7,500 to update maps.
- $22,000 for personal property appraisals by Real Estate Research Consultants 
(RRC).
CURRENT BUSINESS
  Review and Approve Minutes of January 8, 2025, December 10, 2024, November 20, 
2024, November 6, 2024, October 16, 2024, October 2, 2024, Executive Session of 
December 10, 2024.
 The Board plans to review and approve all minutes at the next meeting on 
February 5, 2025.
 Review and Approve Invoices, Expense V ouchers, Billing Errors, etc.
 This topic was skipped for this meeting.
 Assessor Conference at the Senior Center, February 3, 2025.
 The Board reviewed the slides provided by John Rabbitt and discussed their 
edits. The Board thanked John Rabbitt for all his hard work preparing for the 
conference.
 Senior Means Tested Exemption.
There was discussion about creating a subgroup tasked at working on these 
exemptions.
 Neighborhood/Site Index Review/Plan in response to DOR Directives.
 Lynda McQuade suggests using Rena Swezey and Denise McCue to 
complete the review in-house. Lynda McQuade suggests starting with the 
lake properties.
Sarah Cammer asks if there is a document with a format or template that is 
submitted when a bid is put out. Additionally, she states that policy needs to be 
written down somewhere with characteristics that belong in a neighborhood.
 Policy and Homeowner Letter for Issues with Deeds.
Lynda McQuade confirms there have been some issues regarding clarity with 
deeds. Lynda McQuade suggests creating a policy stating if the deed is unable to 
be understood then the homeowners need to reach out to the attorney who 
prepared it. The Assessor’s Office has spoken to 2 real estate attorneys, and they 
suggest contacting the BAR association.
Sarah Cammer and Brian Laffond suggest reaching out to the registrar of deeds 
and find the policy regarding multiple properties and multiple parcels on a single 
deed.
 Assessor Website.
===PAGE 3===
Brian Laffond sent over the old versus the new spreadsheet with a pivot table 
attached. Lynda McQuade added land use codes. If all members are in agreement, 
Brian Laffond would like to add this document to the website. Approved.
POSTPONED TOPICS
  Clause 41 and 17 Assets/Income Level Adjustments for Exemptions (Brian)
  BOA Policies
UPCOMING MEETING/EVENTS SCHEDULE
 Upcoming Meetings: February 5, 2025, February 19, 2025, March 5, 2025, March 19, 
2025

 Public
There were no comments from the public.
 Board
There were no comments from the Board.
ADJOURN
 There was a motion to adjourn and enter into executive session by Sarah Cammer,
seconded by John Rabbitt.
The meeting closed at 8:42 PM.

STATUS REPORT FROM PRINCIPAL ASSESSOR.
• FY2026 Interim adjustments, Addition of tax billing responsibilities in office, CAI
map update. Other items as needed.
In the last month there have been 12 abutters lists requests. A total of 70
motor vehicle excise tax abatements have been processed in MUNIS and are
awaiting payment processing by the tax collector.
While reorganizing the office a few months ago there were a few issues identified
regarding Chapter land properties. In town there are 91-chapter land properties
including: 29 forestry, 35 agriculture, and 27 recreational. 9 of these properties
didn't have liens and there were 21 applications missing. Letters have been sent
and are in the process of being filed with the registry of deeds. Due to no
response, there were 7 properties that have been removed from chapter land, 4
Chapter 61A and 3 were 61B. Additionally, there are 4 properties were that were
in chapter land that had no liens and were sold. Thus roll back tax bills were
issued. One property that was sold in Nov. 2021, has no application and no lien,
but wants to "continue in Chapter 61B". There was an apparent communication
breakdown during the transfer of property. The Board wishes to postpone a
decision on this property until our next meeting. At that time, if we have a valid
application and the lien is in progress, we could decide whether to issue roll-back
order or not. We will revisit this in August.
The Assessor's Office is getting ready to start FY26 cyclical inspections program
phase 2. 500 blue postcards were mailed. If a blue postcard is received, residents
should expect a visit from the Vision appraiser. If you receive a blue postcard and
===PAGE 2===
do not want anyone to visit your home, please call the office and make them
aware. There is no exact date that the Vision inspector will start.
Robin Nano has started preparing the FY27 Chapter Land applications that will be
mailed August 1s.
Lynda Cajka has begun developing a draft of the scope of work RFP to address
the Neighborhood review and revision directive from the DOR.
Lynda Cajka has been in contact with the Vision appraiser, and they indicated that
the office will be receiving the interim adjustment on time this year. They have
started the interim adjustments. Current due date is September 30, 2025.
Current assessed value of growth for FY26 is $27,758,770. The dollar amount
that does not include personal property is $398, 615.93. Personal property
(currently being done by RRC) will increase that Growth.
Goals for next month include:
- Finalizing FY26 growth
- Continue Chapter 61B inspections
- Reviewing registry of deeds to find any parcels that were split. Due to
vacancy in the planning department the office is not receiving any map
updates. Sarah Cammer requested a list of those properties in question.
A resident called with questions regarding their property value. Through research
it was determined that the property has a 20% economic obsolescence. We
discovered there were 131 properties that had that obsolescence code. 34 of them
were functional, meaning they would be addressed with the building as a
functional issue and range from 5% to 50%. Typically, the DOR doesn't support
adjustments over 15%. Denise McCue and Lynda Cajka will be completing
inspections of all of those properties.

• 41A Income Limitation
There is a maximum income of $20,000 to partake in the deferral program. The
Board anticipates modifying the income number at the next town meeting as it

Brian Laffond will write up a warrant article for the next town meeting to be

• Senior Means Tested Exemption Presentation at Senior Center.
Lynda Cajka created a brochure with an application that is available at the
adult activity center and the Board of Assessors Office.
Requirements for exemption include:
- 65 years of age or older
- Owned or occupied your primary residence in Lunenburg for the
past 15 years.
- Must have been granted the circuit breaker income tax credit on
your 2024 tax return.
- Annual income level for 2024 can't exceed $72,000 for single
individuals, $91,000 for head of households, or $109,000 for
married couples.
- Asset limit is $275,000 including checking, savings, second home,
trust fund, securities, and bonds.
- Must submit an application to the assessing department every
year.
- The max exemption for 2026 is $2,730. Additionally, applicants may
also receive an elderly exemption in the same fiscal year if they qualify.
