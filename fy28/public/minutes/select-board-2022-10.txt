Resignation of Full-time Police Officer Nicholas Marino effective October 12, 2022
Chief Gammel thanked Officer Marino for his time in Lunenburg and noted he served the
Town well and was moving on to the Acton Police Department. He was sorry to see him go
but was confident wherever he goes he will be a great addition.
Ratification of Town Manager's Appointment of Paul Theodoulou to Full-time Police
Officer effective October 12, 2022
Chief Gammel stated we interviewed seven candidates and they were ranked based on the
interview results. Officer Theodoulou has been a Lunenburg Reserve Officer since May 2022.
He completed his Field Training Officer (FTO), and has worked numerous hours covering
cruiser shifts, prisoner watches, desk shifts and details. Recently he has covered the open desk
position, made a seamless transition and has taken over the initial intake process for License to
Carry Firearms. He grew up in Fitchburg, and served in the Army National Guard until 2018.
He obtained the rank of Sergeant and received several commendations and awards. His unit
was activated and deployed to Afghanistan during Operation Enduring Freedom. Officer
Theodoulou was hired full-time by the Fitchburg Police Department in 2015. He attended the

Ratification of Town Manager's Appointment of George Tibbetts to Reserve Officer
effective October 12, 2022
Chief Gammel continued on:
We have been actively recruiting to fill vacant reserve officer positions. We identified a
Fitchburg State University Police Program candidate who we believe will be a great fit for our
department and community. I am pleased to recommend George E. Tibbetts ("Trey") for
appointment as a Reserve Police officer. Trey is a lifelong resident of Lunenburg, graduating in
2018. Trey was an accomplished athlete and earned a scholarship to Assumption College. Trey
left Assumption College after his freshman year to take advantage of the Police Program at
Fitchburg State University. He successfully completed the program, earned a Bachelor of
Science degree in Criminal Justice in May of 2022, magna cum laude. Upon graduation Trey was
enrolled in the 5th Recruit Officer course at Fitchburg State University, graduating in September
of 2022. Trey is currently enrolled in the Master of Science program at Fitchburg State
University as part of the Police Program, with an anticipated graduation date of May 2023. He
has successfully completed his background investigation, pre-employment physical, and pre-
employment psychological test. He has met all requirements of Peace Officer Standard and
Training Commission (POSTC), and is a certified police officer. Trey comes to us with an
impressive resume. Trey has constantly demonstrated strong leadership skills. As well as being a
leader, Trey has volunteered for several community programs, including Working for Worcester,
Lunenburg Youth Football, and the Lunenburg Gift Mart.
Trey was on our radar since he was a step intern with our department through LHS. We
recognized his potential and maintained contact with him. I am impressed with his work ethic, as
he continued to hold seasonal jobs throughout high school and college, while maintaining a high
GPA.
Upon completion of his background investigation, I was not surprised to hear all positive
statements. Trey was found to be a very level headed person, hard worker with a strong work
ethic. We believe Trey will be a valuable asset and we look forward to seeing his future
accomplishments. I request that George E. Tibbetts be appointed to the position of Reserve
Police Officer to be effective October 12, 2022.
Officer Tibbetts thanked Chief Gammel for all his comments and he thanked the Town for their
support. The board members welcomed him and thanked him for serving the town in his new
position.

Reasons to consider this change: The month of May has many activities happening around town

Town Property Site Visits
Members present: Michael-Ray Jeffieys, Chairman; Tom Alonzo, Vice Chairman; Lou Franco,
Clerk; Todd Dwyer
Member not present: Heather Sroka
Also Present: Connor Fisher
The Select Board scheduled a meeting for Saturday, October 15, 2022 at 10:00 a.m. for the express
and limited purpose of conducting site walks on certain Town-owned properties currently under
the jurisdiction of the Select Board. The properties in connection with which these site walks were
posted were:
104 Fairview Road
247 Highland Street
1249 Massachusetts Avenue
Also present was Connor Fisher, a senior at Lunenburg Public High School who joined the walk-
throughs as part of a school project on which he is working.
The meeting was called to order at 104 Fairview Road at 10:04 a.m.
104 Fairview
The walk started at 10:09 a.m. The frontage along Fairview Road through which the Town has
access is approximately 108 feet long. Lou Franco noted that much of the land appears to be
maintained and hayed. Michael-Ray Jeffreys noted that various people are doing this and that we
like other towns might want to have some formal agreements in place acknowledging and allowing
this.
As the walk-through proceeded the question was raised, with reference to maps provided to each
member of the Board, whether the Town has an easement in the vicinity of parcels 1 and 45, which
are mutually adjacent.
It was noted that more than half the parcel is heavily vegetated, perhaps 1/3 is open fields of
presently-shortened grass that were recently hayed/mowed. Also noted and remarked upon by
several present was the presence of substantial milkweed and goldenrod.
In addition to open fields, the Board and Mr. Fisher walked through some wooded trails back to
the starting point at the frontage on Fairview Road.
This site visit ended at 10:44 a.m. and members along with Connor Fisher proceeded to 1249
Massachusetts Avenue.
===PAGE 2===
1249 Massachusetts Avenue
This site walk began at 10:49 a.m. This parcel is landlocked and lacks frontage along Mass.
Avenue. It was noted however, that the parcel could be appealing for development because of its
proximity to Massachusetts Avenue. Limited dumping was noted, although there appeared to be
no hazardous materials present.
This site visit ended at 11:03 a.m. The Board decided not to visit 247 Highland Street and the
meeting was therefore adjourned at 11:03 a.m.
Respectfully submitted,
Lou Franco
Clerk

Presentation from Police Department on Certification Process and Status
Police  Chief Gammel  and Sgt. Morreale  were present . Ms. Lemieux  noted the  certification 
process was started by former Police Chief Marino in 2015.  Chief Gammel  confirmed it has
been a long process but finally came to fruition on October 26 th . This voluntar y program 
opens our department up for  review to assure the highest professionalism is met ; the 
certification process includes review of our policies and procedures, our rules and 
regulations , our facilities and also what our operations are within the police department .
Sgt. Morreale  reported he was assigned  as  accre ditation manager of the process last 
September. We started out reformatting all our policies to make sure we met the standards. 
We went t hrough all our training records and  how we retained them and made sur e we met 
the standards. We reviewed our facility, cellblock evidence room, even the lobby , to make 
sure all met certain standards. We had a mock assessment and they provided us with a list of 
recommendations in advance of the actual assessment in July. That went well and in October 
we met with th e board and they awarded us certification through the Massachusetts Police 
Accreditation Commission  (MPAC) . Now that we are certified we are aiming to become 
accredited through MPAC.  Chief Gammel  noted w e would not have been able to meet these 
standards if it w e re  not for th e  cooperation of the entire d epartment.  I would be remise if I 
did not also re cognize the Union President Officer  Bradley McNamara. Officer McNamara 
was an integral part of the process as we had to introduce new polici es, and re-write old ones.
Officer McNamara worked diligently with us, and addressed any issues that arose in a 
cooperative manner . He also thanked Public Safety Coordinator Amanda Moore  for all her 
assistance. The board praised the Police Department for accomplishing the certification.

Christopher (C.J.) Carroll
Mr. Carroll read from his presentation:
The Select Board must hold a public hearing to consider the available tax rate options under property
tax classification. The hearing is held after the Assessors have determined final values, classified all
properties and reported this information to Department of Revenue.
Municipalities must decide whether (1) to tax all classes of property at their full and fair cash
valuation share of the tax levy, which results in a single tax rate, or (2) to reduce the share of
the tax levy paid by the residential and shift those taxes to commercial, industrial and personal
property taxpayers, which results in a split tax rate. Either way, the total tax Levy remains the
The Select Board must decide the percentages of the tax levy that each class of real property
and personal property will bear. To do so, a residential factor is adopted. The residential factor
governs the percentage of the tax levy to be paid by Class One, Residential properties. The
difference is shifted to Class Three-- Commercial, Class Four-Industrial and Personal
Property (or "CIP").

septic system. The failed system is actually on Town property, where a leach pit is over the
line. So we need to remove that. Dillis and Roy did the survey work to determine the property
lines. The existing septic tank will have to be pumped out, removed and filled in with clean
sand. As far as construction to get the (new) septic tanks into the property, the best access
would be to go through the adjacent town property; there's no trees on the town property, it's
just light vegetation, all sand and gravel. There's an existing drain line that goes from the
drain in the street and comes down by Shirley Lake. We have to remove two sections of
fence on Mr. Martin's property and what we would do is bring our equipment around the
town property to the front of his existing carport, dig all that material out, put it on trucks and
truck it off site. Discussion ensued about an existing brown fence. Mr. Martin stated the
fence was there when they purchased the property five years ago. It was determined the
brown fence was on town property and should be removed. Mr. Proctor reported they go
before the Conservation Commission next week for the Notice of Intent for the septic
installation; this plan was approved by the Board of Health.
We will need to install erosion control and remove the old leach bed and septic tank. We
would remove material for the new septic and install the plumbing/electrical, backtill
everything and then everything would be done on town property. It would take three days
maximum for the install and then we have loam, seed and mulch.
