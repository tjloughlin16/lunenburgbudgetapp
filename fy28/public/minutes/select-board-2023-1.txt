Ms. Lemieux read aloud a press release:  IMPORTANT UPDATES RELATED TO FY 2023 
REAL ESTATE TAXES 
 
On January 17, 2023, the Lunenburg Select Board voted to initiate an investigation as authorized by Section 3-2(e) of 
the Lunenburg Town Charter into the process that contributed to the FY23 property valuation and tax assessments. 
During this process the date the tax bills were mailed was investigated due to reports it may have been mailed after 
December 31, 2022. Due to documentation from the Postal Service that contradicts the Affidavit of Mailing from 
company that handles the mailing of the Town’s tax bills and their associated postage statements, it has been 
determined there is enough doubt for town officials to determine that tax bills may have been mailed after December 31, 
2022. Based on this information it has been determined the Town will rely on the Postal Service date of processing, 
January 6, 2023, as the date of mailing for the third and fourth quarter tax bills. Following this determination, and per 
M.G.L. c. 59, Section 57C, the due date for the third and fourth quarter tax bills will be May 1, 2023 and no interest is 
due on the third quarter taxes between the period of February 1, 2023 and May 1, 2023. The Tax Collector will be 
adjusting any interest amounts or interest paid on tax payments that were made after the original February 1, 2023 
deadline. Any interest on 3rd quarter tax bills paid after February 1, 2023 will be applied as a partial payment towards 
the fourth quarter tax payments. If both the third and fourth quarters have been paid and the adjustment of interest 
results in an overpayment based on the total amount due for the fiscal year, the Collector’s Office will initiate refunds 
after June 30, 2023. If you have questions related to your FY 2023 property tax bills, please contact the Tax Collector’s 
Office at (978) 582-4130. 
Under M.G.L. c. 59, Section 59, the Lunenburg Board of Assessors has voted that based on the reliance of a tax bill 
mailing that is later than December 31, 2022, the deadline for anyone who has not already submitted a tax abatement 
application is now May 1, 2023. Tax abatement applications can be found on the town website at: 
https://www.lunenburgma.gov/departments/assessors-office/real-estatepersonal-property-abatement-forms-procedure 
For anyone who has previously submitted a tax abatement application to the Board of Assessors and is approaching 
the 90-day deadline, the Assessor’s Office has mailed out extension form letters. The Board of Assessors, and the 
consultant the Town has hired to assist the Town in conducting all the necessary inspections and valuation 
recommendations to the Board of Assessors, is making all efforts to meet the 90-day deadline from the date the 
applicant filed the tax abatement request to the Assessors, but due to the sheer volume of applications, applications 
may require an extension in order not to be deemed denied. Written consent from property owners is required for 
extensions and the length of the extension is at the discretion of the property owner. The property owner also has the 
right to request more than one extension if the length of the first extension is not sufficient. Extension forms can be 
found on the town website at: https://www.lunenburgma.gov/departments/assessors-office/real-estatepersonal- 
property-abatement-forms-procedure 
Questions related to tax bill amounts due or interest amount adjustments should be directed to the Tax Collector’s

Ms. Lemieux stated the goal of my public comment tonight is that I hope it will clear up some misinformation 
that has been mentioned both publicly and what I have been told. Comments have been made that I put a gag 
order on employees and that this was construed as I am either not being helpful or not allowing employees to do 
their job or that I was covering up something. I believe this stems from an email that I sent back on Thursday 
January 19th after the January 17th meeting held on the tax assessment issue at the middle/high school.  I am 
aware that email that I sent to town employees was circulated to people who are not Town employees. I will 
read from that email for the rest of the public who has not seen it.  “Good afternoon, at the Select Board’s 
meeting on January17th, the board addressed concerns related to the tax assessments at length and ultimately 
voted to proceed with the inquiry into an investigation of the matter pursuant to section 3-2, section e 
of the Town Charter. This email was to notify employees that the Town is not presently in a position to 
comment substantially due to the pending investigation.  Employees are to direct all questions to 
the Chairman of the Select Board, Michael Ray Jeffreys and I and are not to comment on the matter. The Town 
through the Select Board will make every effort to share new information with the public as it becomes 
available.  Sincerely, Heather”  
This was not a gag order for employees not to continue to do their jobs, but it was intended to notify employees 
that there was an investigation into this matter and communications about the investigation would be channeled 
through the principal investigator Chairman Jeffreys and myself. 
Just to add some more context to the situation at that time in the days following the January 17th meeting, we 
had the media calling our offices, approaching employees outside of Town Hall, including employees that didn't 
even work in the Assessor's Office. This was in no way intended that employees could not continue to do their 
jobs. I also want to clear up the misinformation that I directed the Board of Assessors that they could not handle 
the abatements. I had no such conversations with the Assessors individually or collectively. It has been 
known early on that due to the number of abatement applications expected that we would need to hire an 
outside consultant to conduct inspections and provide recommendations on abatement applications. This was 
discussed publicly at Select Board meetings. The board voted publicly to allocate funding from ARPA towards 
===PAGE 11===
the investigation which included the cost to hire this consultant. It has also been stated publicly that requests for 
proposals were sent out once with no response and it was re-advertised. This was all done to support the Board 
of Assessors and getting through the over 400 abatement applications. I never stated nor insinuated or had a 
conversation with any of the assessors or staff that the assessors could not handle abatements. I've also said 
publicly that I have no jurisdiction over the Board of Assessors nor does the Principal Assessor nor does the 
Select Board; they are an elected body accountable to the voters. 
As I stated at the January 10th meeting I am sympathetic to the affected taxpayers and that I would help guide 
taxpayers through the process by relaying important information and seeking answers to your questions. To say 
this has been a difficult last three months is an understatement; a constant flow of foot traffic, emails, phone 
calls and follow-up was required of the Assessor's office, especially during those first few weeks. Our office 
was inundated with emails, various public records requests, countless hours have been spent assisting with the 
investigation, working with the relevant staff that is constantly being asked to provide answers to questions. All 
of this in addition to the normal daily/weekly/ annual responsibilities. My staff and I understand the urgency of 
the situation in individual situations. I ask the public to understand that we want to provide accurate, good 
information to questions and any important updates that may need to be addressed. This may not meet the 
expectations that information should be immediately available, it takes staff time and other competing priorities.
