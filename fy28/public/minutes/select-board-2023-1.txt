Town Manager Report on FY24 Budget Priorities
Ms. Lemieux read from her memo to the Board:
Each year, prior to my presentation of the preliminary budget proposal to the Town, I review with the
Board foreseeable budget priorities for the next fiscal year. In line with prior years, there are always
more needs than resources and the fiscal approach is to balance the needs of the schools, town
departments, and capital needs while adhering to our financial policies and maintaining strong
To date, departments have been sent target budgets and I had meetings with various department
heads to discuss their needs for next fiscal year prior to sending out the target budget. After
receiving each department's budget request in mid-January, the Finance Director and I will meet
with every department and have more thorough discussions about their budget requests.
On the projected revenue side, currently the target budget includes a 2½ percent increase from the
prior year's levy limit, an estimated new growth figure of $200,000, the debt exclusion amount for
FY 24, level funds state aid, and includes a $50,000 increase to local receipts. By the end of
January, we will receive the Governor's budget proposal, which will amend our state aid estimate
until the state budget is finalized.
At this point in the budget process, I foresee staffing needs to be addressed conservatively in Fiscal
Year 2024. In prior years, the ten-year staffing plan for the Police Department and the five-year
staffing plan Fire/EMS were part of the preliminary budget presentations. The five-year Fire/EMS
plan was realized this fiscal year and the Fire Department obtained their Paramedic License on
January 2, 2023.
===PAGE 4===
and oversight will be a need that we will look to outsource. Trash and recycling are one of the top
resident complaints we receive and the goal of having a coordinator is to address these complaints
and resolve ongoing pick-up issues. Once the Board approves the pending ARPA funding requests,
federal reporting requirements, procurement, and oversight of projects will be added
responsibilities that we currently do not have the staff resources for. Outsourcing grant writing

Another area I will be recommending resources be allocated is training for staff. We currently have a

contractual, non-union employees. The impact of both will need to be borne through the FY 2024
Salary Reserve appropriation.
It is still relatively early in the budget process and there are many moving parts that become known
later in the fiscal year. As budget developments come about or needs are identified, I will keep the
Board updated.

Ms. Lemieux read the notice aloud:
"The Assessor's Office, according to guidelines set forth by the MA Department of Revenue,
must use market data to value each property in town at its full and fair cash value. One of these
guidelines is to stay within a certain assessment to sales ratio. This means that properties
similar to yours that sold in 2021 (the year analyzed to reach 2023's values) are the basis for
property valuations.
The past couple of years has seen a significant increase in purchase prices, not only in
Lunenburg, but statewide and nationally. This is the reason for the base increase in value
across the board, but it is also the reason for the corresponding decrease in the tax rate from
$17.19 per thousand in 2022 to $14.62 per thousand in 2023. State law prevents towns from
taking advantage of this market shift by collecting more taxes more than the annual increase.
This means that an unusually high increase in taxes on your property is tied to sales of
properties similar to yours. Again, according to DOR guidelines, we must make these changes
to assessed values according to actual sales.
This year we saw an unprecedented increase in sale prices on waterfront property in
Lunenburg. In order for us to assess waterfront properties correctly, we must use actual sales
to determine assessments. Otherwise, we would not be able to achieve the required
assessment to sales ratio that the Department of Revenue requires.
Any taxpayer who feels their assessment does not reflect the value of their home as of the
assessment date of 1/1/2022 is encouraged to file a real estate abatement no later than
February 1, 2023 along with any supporting evidence (i.e. sales of homes similar to yours,
incorrect public record data, recently completed appraisals). The Board of Assessors will then
determine if your value is fair.
Please keep in mind that your January tax bill does not necessarily reflect the dollar amount of
your tax bills moving forward. Any fiscal year that an assessed value increase occurs, that
increase is reflected only on the January and April bills (also referred to as Actual Bills). The
July and October bills were preliminary and calculated based on the prior year's information.
So your annual tax increase is condensed into two tax bills rather than spread across four bills.
The best way to calculate what your annual taxes are is to multiply the assessed value by the
$14.62 tax rate/ 1000 (.01462)."

What qualifies as a Capital Project?
===PAGE 7===
• any planning, feasibility, engineering or design study related to a capital project or to a
capital improvement program consisting of individual projects.
• equipment for public improvements when they are first constructed such as
furniture, office equipment, or playground equipment;
• major equipment which is expensive and has a relatively long life such as a fire
apparatus, garbage trucks, and construction equipment.
Capital Planning Process
> The Capital Planning Committee (CPC) creates a Capital Plan that consists of all
capital requests submitted by each town department for the next ten fiscal years.
› The CPC's first meeting to begin the FY24 Capital Planning process was on August
3rd, the Capital Planning documents were sent to departments in September and the
Capital Planning Committee met with departments from late October until the end of
November.
• This year the Town Manager transitioned the forms to the same software used for
budgeting, ClearGov, and all the relevant information was submitted through this
platform.
> The data collected is based on the UMass Collins Center Capital Planning documents
that include various categories such as effectiveness of government, the effect on
operating costs, effect on revenues, whether the project is mandated by the State or
Federal government, whether grant funds are available, and how the project contributes
to such things as economic growth, public safety, public health and education. Each
category receives a rating and an overall score based on the aggregate score of all the
categories (the higher the rating, the higher the priority).
> The Capital Planning Committee evaluates capital requests based on the responses to
these categories and as a group, the CPC reviews the merits of all the projects. Each
member submits their ranking of each project and these are averaged to achieve a
composite ranking.
> The CPC prioritized 29 capital project requests for FY24 and provided the prioritized
list to the Town Manager on December 28th.
> The Town Manager may modify this list of capital projects as part of the proposed
budget for the next fiscal year. This capital budget is presented at the Annual Town
Meeting, amended as necessary to reflect the will of Town Meeting, and voted on.
> This plan reflects the same projects that the CPC provided the Town Manager with
differences in the prioritization of some of the projects.

Review American Rescue Plan Act Funding Requests, Evaluation Criteria and Process
Mr. Jeffreys noted the board had planned to have this discussion on the 17' but circumstances
did not allow time. The board members provided feedback and he wanted to sort through some
things. When it came to pandemic mitigation noted in the draft there was agreement that it could
be removed as it is already mentioned in the CARES Act. Also in the draft there was funding
allocated to the Town Manager and Finance Director in the general administration budget and
there was agreement to remove that as well. There was also agreement to remove the water and
sewer departments out of the infrastructure allocation and combine that into the general fund.
The board was split on burial and cremation reimbursement, staff bonuses and support for non-
profits. After doing some more research in the 63 page document that states what funds can and
cannot be spent on, there would be a split in staff, not everyone would be eligible. We would
have to pinpoint critical roles. Mr. Jeffieys did not want to put ourselves in the position of pitting
some staff against others.
He wanted to revisit the reimbursement of cremations and burials. Ms. Sroka asked how many
people are on that list and how long would we be paying out. Mr. Jeffreys stated right now we
have confirmation of two deaths in Lunenburg but it could be more. If we provide it I would like
it to be retroactive to the beginning of the pandemic through 2026. Most burials vary from five to
seven thousand dollars and cremations are usually two to three thousand dollars. Discussion
ensued and the general consensus was not to reimburse for burials and cremations.
Discussion turned to providing support for non-profits as the board was split on this as well.
Suggestion was made to only support non-profits based in Lunenburg and those that are for the
benefit of the community, such as the food pantry (Lion's Club) and the Boys & Girls Club. It
was suggested that they provide evidence of how the pandemic changed their needs.

Discuss Draft Bylaw to explicitly prohibit AirBnB like services in residential zones
Mr. Jeffreys stated the board has been informed that the property at 15 Cliffview Terrace has
been identified as being used as an AirBnB. Ms. Lemieux and he met with Town Counsel

specifically to prohibit rentals shorter than 31 days and prohibit them in residential zones. This
would be a modification to the use table. Anyone wanting to do this would need to go the Zoning
Board of Appeals for variance. The amendment would need to go in front of the Planning Board.
