Town of Lunenburg 
Historical Commission 
August 7, 2018 
Rebecca Lantry, Chair 
Richard A. McGrath, Member 
Cullen Dwyer, Member 
Jennifer L. Sanderson, Member 
C. William Lakso, Member 
 
 
Minutes 
 
Following appropriate public notice, a special meeting of the commission was held August 7, 
2018 at the Ritter Memorial Building, 960 Massachusetts Avenue, Lunenburg.  Chairperson 
Rebecca Lantry called the meeting to order at 7:00 o’clock, p.m. with a quorum present (Lantry, 
Sanderson & Lakso.)  Present and representing the cemetery commission were William E. Tyler 
and Lorraine Mendoza. 
 
The purpose of the meeting was to act on the application of the Lunenburg Cemetery 
Commission to demolish a former office building administered by the cemetery commission, 
having an age in excess of 75 years, but no longer utilized.  
 
Mr. Tyler explained details of the now-unused building, and the need to replace it with a garage 
for storage of the commission’s equipment.  Following additional discussion, and on motion 
regularly made and seconded, it was unanimously voted:  That no demolition delay be imposed, 
but that the cemetery commission exert reasonable efforts to advertise the availability of the 
building for removal, prior to razing. 
 
(It is noted that subsequent to the meeting, the availability of the building for removal was 
published in the Lunenburg Ledger.) 
 
Attest: 
Jennifer L. Sanderson & 
C. William Lakso

Town of Lunenburg 
Historical Commission 
September 4, 2018 
Rebecca Lantry, Chair 
Richard A. McGrath, Member 
Cullen Dwyer, Member 
Jennifer L. Sanderson, Member 
C. William Lakso, Member 
 
 
 
Minutes 
 
The regular monthly meeting of the Lunenburg Historical Commission was held this date at the 
Riter Memorial Building, 960 Massachusetts Avenue, Lunenburg.Chairperson Rebecca Lantry 
called the meeting to order at 7:35 o’clock, p.m with a quorum present (Lantry, Sanderson & 
Lakso.) Present and representing the Cemetery Commission was William E. Tyler and citizen 
guest, James J. Larkin. 
 
A representative of Troop 1728 of the Boy Scouts presented a donation of $90.00 to Ms. Lantry, 
for the commission, representing the excess amount of a fund raising event related to the survey 
and installation of National Historic plaques to qualifying homes in the Historic District. 
 
Minutes of the special meeting of August 7, 2018 were reviewed and accepted by unanimous 
vote. 
 
Mr. Lakso presented copies of a report on progress being made on the inventory of homes of the 
community, referred to as the Form B Project.  The report was accepted by unanimous vote. 
 
On the subject of organizing a plan of action relative to the Primary School, a number of 
possibilities accomplished by cities and towns was presented by Mr. Lakso.  He is also 
contacting Mr. Skelly of MHS, inquiring if information is available of potential developers of  
school buildings for re-use. 
 
 
 
 
===PAGE 2===
 
 
Mr. Tylere and Mr. Larkin joined in the discussion of how to identify what is known as the 
Arnold – Litch burial ground.  It is understood that a blank headstone is available to the 
Cemetery Commission, and a suitable memorial plaque design has been drafted.  On motion 
regularly made and seconded, it was unanimously voted:  To arrange for the acquisition of the 
stone, create a bronze plaque to be secured to it, secure approval from the town planning board, 
and determine from the department of public works, the best location within the right of way of 
West Street Terrace to position it. 
 
There being no further business to come before the commission, the meeting was adjourned at 
7:25 o’clock, p.m. 
 
Attest: 
 
 
 
C. William Lakso

Town of Lunenburg 
Historical Commission 
 Rebecca Lantry, Chair Richard McGrath, member    
Cullen Dwyer, Clerk    
Jennifer Sanderson, member 
Bill Lakso, member 
Meeting Minutes 
December 4th, 2018, 6:30PM, Ritter Building 960 Massachusetts Avenue 
Meeting Opened by Chair Lantry at 6:30 
Demolition Delay Hearing: 
Keith Michon Esq. appeared to represent his client and owner of 4 Townsend Harbor Rd regarding the demolition 
application for the barn on the property.  The barn in question had been inspected by members of the Commission 
and deemed in too poor condition to be worth saving, and the Commission voted unanimously to permit demolition 
without the imposition of delay. 
Review of minutes: 
Minutes for November 6th were not available for this meeting, and so review of them was continued to the next 
meeting. 
Correspondence: 
Mina Corpuz of the Fitchburg Sentinel and Enterprise contacted Lantry regarding the demolition of the old cemetery 
building, and the damaged monuments that had been found around the site.  Bill Tyler of the Cemetery Commission 
was present at the meeting, and commented that he had received this inquiry and responded.  Some old monuments 
that had broken had been removed from the cemetery and moved to the site of the building, and upon demolition, 
more damaged monuments were discovered partly buried.    Other gravestones of concern were duplicates or mis-
printed stones that had been sent to the cemetery accidentally. 
Present business: 
Lantry presented an invoice to the clerk for the bronze plaque she had ordered to mark the location of the Arnold-
Litch cemetery. 
Lantry also announced the voting results from the fall town meeting, and the town’s decision to extend the demoli-
tion delay period to eighteen months.  Discussion followed as to when the law would take effect.  Tanner Cole of the 
Planning Board, posited that the law would be enforceable from the date that the warrant was submitted, which 
would mean that the Battles House on 68 Elm Street would have an extra year of protection. 
Lakso reported continued progress on the K-House project. 
Unfinished/New Business: 
Lantry announced that the Battles House and its barn might possibly be saved by relocation.  Tanner Cole was 
present to explain the condition of the house and barn, and the benefits and difficulties involved in moving them to 
his planned Christmas tree farm on 100 Howard Street.  He said that the barn would be easier to move and of greater 
benefit to him.  He was not sure about the house.  Lakso motioned to release the barn at 68 Elm Street from demoli-
tion delay for the purpose of disassembly, relocation and restoration, as discussed.  The motion carried unanimously. 
Next Metting: Scheduled for January 8th, 2019 
Meeting adjourned: at 8:17
