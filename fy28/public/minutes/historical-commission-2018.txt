Meeting Minutes
1-2-18, 6:30PM, Ritter Building, 960 Massachusetts Ave.
Meeting called to order by Chair Lantry at 6:35. McGrath, and Dwyer present.
Review of Minutes: Minutes from December 5th, 2017 were read and approved unanimously.
Correspondence:
Massachusetts Historical Commission sent notices.
They are accepting applications for preservation
project applications, Round 24. They are also accepting nominations for the 40th annual preservation
awards.
The Lunenburg planning board sent notices of two hearings that were not of concern.
Demolition delay bylaw: Review of the model bylaw suggested by Mass. Historic led to discussion of
whether or not Lunenburg's bylaw should be updated. All agreed that the model included procedural
details found wanting in Lunenburg's current bylaw. Lantry suggested extending the delay to 18 months
and/or stipulating that the delay extend six months from when the applicant attend a scheduled hearing.
Dwyer questioned the current bylaw's authority to impose such conditions, though McGrath felt that they
were reasonable and normal. The commission agreed to study and compare the current bylaw and the
model bylaw.
Historic Marker Project: Lantry has sent Purchase Order to the foundry as proof of spending approval
so that they will go forward with the work.
New Member search: Ideas discussed: to post bills at Town Hall, the public Library, and to get Amory
Phelps to find a new member for us.
New Business:
Dwyer presented a model Letter of Recognition and Appreciation to be sent to town residents that took it
upon themselves to preserve or restore historic properties. The letter was written particularly with the
recent work to the Old Methodist Church in mind. Chairperson Lantry agreed to sign such a letter.
Next meeting: Feburary 6th, 2018
Adjournment: 7:23PM

had sent to Elaine Peterson for inclusion in the 2017 Town Report.
McGrath spoke with Land Use Director Adam Burney, who said that the town planning workshops had
generated ideas for future use of downtown properties that included demolition of the Brooks House and

April.

did not know what matter this was in regards to, and intended to find out.

sought applications in recent years for the Heritage Landscape Inventory Program. This program would
inventory and recognize places of historical and cultural importance, including cemeteries. Lantry sug-
gested that this might be a good fist step for preserving and restoring the Anrold Litch Cemetery.

Meeting Minutes
4-3-18, 6:30PM, Ritter Building, 960 Massachusetts Ave.
Meeting called to order by Chair Lantry at 6:55. All members present.
Review of Minutes: Minutes from March 12th, 2018 were read and approved unanimously.
Historic Marker Project: Lantry reported that the markers had arrived from the foundry.
Demolition Requests:
John and Brian Herrick appeared to defend their application for demolition of a house at 99 Boucher Rd.
McGrath had inspected the property from the outside. It appeared to be a very simply built house from
the 1940's on a rubble foundation. After several questions and some discussion, the Commission decided
that the house was of little historical value and voted unanimously to permit demolition. McGrath was to
inform the building inspector of the decision the following day.
The applicants complained that they found it difficult to find contact information for the Historical Com-
mission. Lakso had noticed that the information on the town website was in need of revision, and provid-
ed a list of improvements for Dwyer to complete. Dwyer will contact Steve Malandrinos to update the
web page, and Lantry will ensure that the Demolition Delay Determination form used by the Building
Department is revised to include contact information as well.
Form B project:
Chair Lantry requested that the commission proceed in taking inventory of all historic properties in the
town, especially the "K-houses" noted on the 1934 map of Lunenburg by Cyrus Kilburn. Sanderson and
Lakso, also of the Historical Society, had much material prepared for the project. The Commission dis-
cussed the Form B format used by Mass Historic to inventory significant historical resources. Former
commissioner Damon McQuaid had made some progress on the project; Dwyer and Lantry agreed to lo-
cate his electronic files on the project.
Stone Walls:
Richard McGrath contacted the building department regarding the demolition of a protected stone wall on
Northfield and Holman roads. The building department explained that the demolition was done for for-
estry purposes, and referred the matter to the Conservation Commission. McGrath contacted Matt Mar-
row who agreed to require the logger to restore the wall. McGrath noted that the wall was casually built
and that restoration could also be casual.
Sanderson noted that another wall had been demolished on Northfield across from the golf course.
Mcgrath agreed to investigate the matter.
===PAGE 2===
Brooks House and Primary School:
McGrath had not contacted Mr. Hoats of Mass Historic yet, but requested that Lakso and Sanderson write
articles for the Lunenburg Ledger on these historic buildings to make people aware of their historical im-
portance.
Franklin S. Francis house:
McGrath inspected the work in progress at 944 Mass Ave. He noted a grand fireplace with a granite man-
tel and Lyre-shaped brickwork. He also reported that, contrary to what is written in the Heritage of the
Turkey Hills, the house is not balloon-framed.
Historic Milestones:
McGrath reported that he had undertaken painting in the letters of the historical milestones in town with
archival lithographic paint. The markers at Flat Hill, Reservoir, and Fish streets had been painted, but the
one on Page street still needed attention. Also, the one on Fish Street has a fissure on the top that threat-
ened to break the stone, which he intended to fill with waterproof epoxy.
He also offered to replace two milestones that currently sit out in back of the Historical Society building.
Sanderson and Lakso expressed enthusiasm for this endeavor, and agreed to vote on the measure at the
Historical Society meeting the following day. Lantry motioned to encumber the Commission's budget to
the limit of current available funds for the project. The motion carried unanimously.
Arnold Litch cemetery:
McGrath intended to speak to Jack Rodiquenz regarding the Commission's rights and responsibilities in
protecting and restoring the cemetery. Lakso quoted a passage from Mass General Law explaining that
construction and use of such sites must stop until the state archeologist was able to inspect and report on
the site. He said the state archeologist was willing to visit and help the cause.
Next meeting: May Ist, 2018
Adjournment: 8:25PM

Minutes 
 
Following appropriate public notice, a special meeting of the commission was held August 7, 
2018 at the Ritter Memorial Building, 960 Massachusetts Avenue, Lunenburg.  Chairperson 
Rebecca Lantry called the meeting to order at 7:00 o’clock, p.m. with a quorum present (Lantry, 
Sanderson & Lakso.)  Present and representing the cemetery commission were William E. Tyler 
and Lorraine Mendoza. 
 
The purpose of the meeting was to act on the application of the Lunenburg Cemetery 
Commission to demolish a former office building administered by the cemetery commission, 
having an age in excess of 75 years, but no longer utilized.  
 
Mr. Tyler explained details of the now-unused building, and the need to replace it with a garage 
for storage of the commission’s equipment.  Following additional discussion, and on motion 
regularly made and seconded, it was unanimously voted:  That no demolition delay be imposed, 
but that the cemetery commission exert reasonable efforts to advertise the availability of the 
building for removal, prior to razing. 
 
(It is noted that subsequent to the meeting, the availability of the building for removal was 
published in the Lunenburg Ledger.) 
 
Attest: 
Jennifer L. Sanderson & 
C. William Lakso

Minutes 
 
The regular monthly meeting of the Lunenburg Historical Commission was held this date at the 
Riter Memorial Building, 960 Massachusetts Avenue, Lunenburg.Chairperson Rebecca Lantry 
called the meeting to order at 7:35 o’clock, p.m with a quorum present (Lantry, Sanderson & 
Lakso.) Present and representing the Cemetery Commission was William E. Tyler and citizen 
guest, James J. Larkin. 
 
A representative of Troop 1728 of the Boy Scouts presented a donation of $90.00 to Ms. Lantry, 
for the commission, representing the excess amount of a fund raising event related to the survey 
and installation of National Historic plaques to qualifying homes in the Historic District.

vote.

community, referred to as the Form B Project.  The report was accepted by unanimous vote.

possibilities accomplished by cities and towns was presented by Mr. Lakso.  He is also 
contacting Mr. Skelly of MHS, inquiring if information is available of potential developers of  
school buildings for re-use.

Meeting Minutes 
December 4th, 2018, 6:30PM, Ritter Building 960 Massachusetts Avenue 
Meeting Opened by Chair Lantry at 6:30 
Demolition Delay Hearing: 
Keith Michon Esq. appeared to represent his client and owner of 4 Townsend Harbor Rd regarding the demolition 
application for the barn on the property.  The barn in question had been inspected by members of the Commission 
and deemed in too poor condition to be worth saving, and the Commission voted unanimously to permit demolition 
without the imposition of delay. 
Review of minutes: 
Minutes for November 6th were not available for this meeting, and so review of them was continued to the next 
meeting. 
Correspondence: 
Mina Corpuz of the Fitchburg Sentinel and Enterprise contacted Lantry regarding the demolition of the old cemetery 
building, and the damaged monuments that had been found around the site.  Bill Tyler of the Cemetery Commission 
was present at the meeting, and commented that he had received this inquiry and responded.  Some old monuments 
that had broken had been removed from the cemetery and moved to the site of the building, and upon demolition, 
more damaged monuments were discovered partly buried.    Other gravestones of concern were duplicates or mis-
printed stones that had been sent to the cemetery accidentally. 
Present business: 
Lantry presented an invoice to the clerk for the bronze plaque she had ordered to mark the location of the Arnold-
Litch cemetery. 
Lantry also announced the voting results from the fall town meeting, and the town’s decision to extend the demoli-
tion delay period to eighteen months.  Discussion followed as to when the law would take effect.  Tanner Cole of the 
Planning Board, posited that the law would be enforceable from the date that the warrant was submitted, which 
would mean that the Battles House on 68 Elm Street would have an extra year of protection. 
Lakso reported continued progress on the K-House project. 
Unfinished/New Business: 
Lantry announced that the Battles House and its barn might possibly be saved by relocation.  Tanner Cole was 
present to explain the condition of the house and barn, and the benefits and difficulties involved in moving them to 
his planned Christmas tree farm on 100 Howard Street.  He said that the barn would be easier to move and of greater 
benefit to him.  He was not sure about the house.  Lakso motioned to release the barn at 68 Elm Street from demoli-
tion delay for the purpose of disassembly, relocation and restoration, as discussed.  The motion carried unanimously. 
Next Metting: Scheduled for January 8th, 2019 
Meeting adjourned: at 8:17
