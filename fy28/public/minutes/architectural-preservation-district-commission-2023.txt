2) Vision of beginning of APDC & Village District 
a. Why does APDC get questioned more than the Village District 
i. Village District keeps “new” single family home in the district, but does not limit 
renovations and work on existing structure.  Allows a denser footprint on the 
individual parcels.  Allows more flexibility
 
ii. APDC is seen as a more restrictive, saving the historical aspect of the town and 
being the other half of the pairing of the Village District.
 
iii. Negative comments from the 2015 creation 
1. The usual concern that property owners object to any regulation to private 
property
 
b. What was vision of the  
i. Historical Committee, Building Use Committee, and Planning Board all trying to get 
on board with the creation of both the APDC and VD.
 
ii. APDC was built with strong anti-demolition protections and addressed demo by 
neglect which gave the town some muscle to protect its historical buildings and 
village appearance. 
iii. Wording on bylaws and guidelines needs to be simplified, could include examples. 
1. The “how to” preserve and restore

Meeting Minutes 
Tuesday April 11th, 2023 
Ritter Building 
Call to Order: At 7:44 with McGrath, Dwyer, and Lockwood present. 
Public Comment: None 
Review of minutes: 
The minutes for the March 7th meeting were read aloud and approved with the following alterations: McGrath was present, the 
name of Amory Phelps was misspelled, and it should be included in the minutes that the APDC agreed to take no action 
regarding the window replacement at the Phelps house without a CTA. 
Correspondence: 
The Massachusetts historic commission contacted McGrath and communicated its seven-year plan, which was read aloud.  
Lockwood mentioned the benefits of a community preservation act to achieve some of the goals therein.  Discussion followed 
and it was agreed that a CPA would be beneficial to the goals of the APDC as well. 
Old Business: 
Ritter Building Renovations: Dwyer explained that he had communicated with the Select Board and explained that the APDC 
awaits a new application for a CTA, but there has been no reply so far. 
Building Inspector: Per previous meeting discussion, Dwyer reached out to the Building Inspector to discuss a revised application 
process involving closer coordination with the building department, and also to ask about enforcement measures.  No reply so 
far. 
Bylaw Review: building on previous discussions regarding the intent, purpose, and workings of the APD bylaw, Lockwood offered 
to draft a revised bylaw for review at the July meeting. 
New Business: 
Lockwood contacted Rob Bowen, one of the owners of the Old Methodist Church at 50 Main Street.  Deterioration of the steeple 
remains a concern.  Mr. Bowen never received our previous letter expressing the APDC’s concern, and asked when it was sent 
and to what address?  He also inquired if grants were available to historic preservation work, and if the APDC could recommend 
any contractors for the work?  Dwyer agreed to contact him and answer his questions. Bowen explained that he recently had the 
building painted, and did some other structural repairs.  He had plans to repair the slate roof, and had gotten a bid from a 
contractor to repair the steeple. 

===PAGE 2===
Report from Historical Commission: 
The Lunenburg Historical Commission is seeking a grant to update the town’s inventory of “K-houses”, or buildings noted on the 
1838 Kilburn map.  They intend to use the grant to hire a professional to do the work. 
The Commission is currently researching the history of black persons residing in Lunenburg. 
The Commission had no meeting in March due to an outbreak of COVID-19, but intends to present its military veterans’ banner 
project at the next regular meeting of the APDC. 
Other business:  
Dwyer will pursue setting up the next meeting in the Ritter basement where there is equipment that will enable the meeting to be 
on ZOOM. 
A CTA application from Ted Gaudet was received at the meeting, regarding re-roofing the house at 876 Massachusetts Ave.  
Lockwood and Dwyer were prepared to approve the application, but McGrath was concerned about the lack of detail in the 
description of the work.  Members present agreed to approve it provisionally, pending the outcome of McGrath’s further 
investigation. 
Next Meeting: May 2nd, Ritter Building basement, 7:30PM 
Close of meeting: 9:06PM

The meeting was called to order at 7:43 pm, 8 August 23. It was to be recorded in accordance with the Open 
Meetings Law, with the chair (Anthony Sculimbrene) addressing the attendees and reminding them that not all items 
listed on the agenda may be discussed, and other topics not listed may also be brought up for discussion. 
The chair welcomed Chris Ruth and Damon McQuaid to the meeting. As there were no members of the general 
public present at this time there were no comments. 
 
Approval of 11 July 23 Meeting Minutes was requested by the chair. They had been prepared and distributed by 
William Lloyd prior to his departure for business abroad. Richard McGrath moved and Nathan Lockwood provided 
a second. There was no discussion about the minutes and they were accepted. 
 
The chair advised the members of the commission that he had corresponded with the architect by phone to discuss 
the Ritter project and to get an idea as to when the project would be ready for review and what was being proposed 
in the way of changes from what the APDC had seen and disapproved in 2022.  
 
Certificate to Alter, 960 Massachusetts Ave (Ritter Building) discussion included comments from the chair 
regarding the completeness of the CTA application. There were emails sent between the chair and Chris Ruth and 
the chair and Adam Burney as well as emails from Damon McQuaid. The chair had questioned whether or not all of 
the materials indicated on the CTA had been delivered to the APDC for their review. Ultimately, all of the 
information was received by email except for a description of the window frame color. And as such the CTA was 
thought not to be complete. However, as colors are exempt from review by the APDC the CTA was deemed to be 
complete.  
A debate ensued regarding the window color. The chair believed the windows should be white to maintain the 
historic character of the building and to match the other buildings in the district. Nathan, another committee 
member, mentioned that the windows in the district were mostly white due to being vinyl replacements. Damon 
defended the choice of black windows, stating that it would create a contrast with the brickwork and that it was a 
common choice for historic preservation projects. The committee discussed the importance of distinguishing the 
windows of the addition from the original building and the need to maintain the character of the historic structures. 
Thhe committee debated whether a public hearing was necessary. Nathan Lockwood argued that the application was 
complete and that the committee should not delay the project. The chair on the other hand, believed that a public 
hearing was necessary in order to comply with the Architectural Preservation District Bylaw. The committee 
eventually agreed to declare the application complete pending the submission of minor updates discussed during the 
meeting. They also agreed to schedule a public hearing within 45 days of today’s meeting to provide an opportunity 
for public input. 
 
The APDC meeting concluded with discussions on property owner notification, public outreach, and modifications 
to the guidelines and certificate to alter. The committee also briefly mentioned the work plan for the upcoming fiscal 
year. The next meeting, scheduled for September 12th, will be a public hearing to further discuss the Ritter 
renovation project.
