The Board of Selectmen met in the Lunenburg Town Hall, Joseph F. Bilotta Meeting Room, as scheduled with
Jamie Toale, Chairman; Robert Ebersole, Vice Chairman; Tom Alonzo, Clerk; Paula Bertram, Member; Phyllis
Luck, Member and Interim Town Manager David Marciello

PUBLIC COMMENT:
Mr. Alonzo reminded all of the Homecoming Parade and new school dedication on Saturday, October 22°
Open House at the school is from 11:00 a.m. to 12:00 p.m. The parade begins at 12:00 and the ribbon cutting
commences at 12:45 p.m. outside by the main entrance, weather permitting and inside the auditorium if weather
doesn't cooperate. A reception with refreshments will follow. The football game begins at 2:00 p.m. with
parents and player recognition at 1:45 p.m.
Mr. Toale reminded all that October 19' is the last day to register to vote in the November 8th Presidential
election. The Town Clerk office will be open from 9:00 a.m. to 8:00 p.m. Their hours on Thursday will be from
9:00 a.m. to 3:00 p.m. They are closed on Friday.
Kathy Murray presented on behalf of the Lunenburg Family Giftmart to request use of the T.C. Passios
Building for their charity event in December. Lunenburg Family Giftmart was created because of an identified
need in the community. Through the generosity of the community they have been able to offer a way for
Lunenburg families in need to shop for free. They work with St. Boniface Church, the Lions Club, the
Lunenburg Food Bank and the Lunenburg School District. This is a one day, invitation only event. For the past
seven years the event had been held at St. Boniface Church, which is unavailable this year. They are coming to
the Board with this time sensitive, emergency request. Their fundraising and gift collections must begin
immediately to ensure the success of the event; however without a guaranteed space they cannot do so. She
stated she had met with Facilities Director John Londa yesterday to work out the details. Mr. Toale stated the
tenants were contacted and there is no issue with the event.
Motion: P. Bertram
2"d: T. Alonzo
To approve use of the T.C. Passion Building for the Lunenburg Family Giftmart as outlined

Town. Mr. Londa presented risks of each the contracts that the Board should be aware of. He stated he has been
working with the Interim Town Manager, legal counsel and Solect Energy's legal counsel to work out the

discuss these further at their next meeting. They requested copies of contracts of other solar projects for

Trish Domigan - VHB- Summer Street Project Update
Ms. Domigan reported the 75% plan, which means it is 90% completed, was submitted to Mass DOT in May of
this year. She added they are moving the project along to final design which is very exciting. The
advertisement date is May 6, 2017. The entire project construction cost is $9.9 million right now. Mass DOT
===PAGE 2===
asks for updates on the construction cost regularly. All of the environmental permitting is completed. They will
now be going to the property owners concerning easements and are in the process of scheduling a neighborhood
meeting to talk to the Lunenburg property and business owners along Summer Street for the beginning of
November. There are 26 temporary and 6 permanent easements that are required for the project. Most of the
permanent easements are on MBTA property. She will be scheduling a meeting with them next week.
Appraisals are scheduled for January 2017. VHB will be coming to your meeting next April to present the

CURRENT BUSINESS
1. Election Ballot Questions Forum
Mr. Toale polled the room for interest on each of the four ballot questions. Mike Mackin, 26 Cortland Circle,
School Committee and School Building Committee Chairman, presented on Question 2. Question 2 would give
the state Board of Elementary and Secondary Education the authority to approve 12 new charter schools or to
expand existing charter schools as a result of increased enrollment each year beginning on January 1, 2017.
Priority would be given to those charter school applicants who seek to open a charter school in public school
districts that are in the bottom 25 percent in the two years before application. Further, the Board would establish
standards by which annual performance reviews would be judged. Mr. Mackin was asking voters to vote "no"
on Question 2. Mr. Mackin stated he was speaking as a resident. He stated there is nothing in Question 2 that
addresses quality, financing or the current problems in financing our public schools. Public schools serve
children in thirteen categories of disabilities, charter schools do not. This has a budget impact. There are 180
school districts that have passed resolutions opposed to Question 2. He named several organizations opposed to
Question 2. He concluded by saying he is not against charter schools just the impact on the school system. He
stated it should be up for public forum discussion. He thanked the Board for their time and hoped that they
would discuss it.
Dave Rodgers, 82 Highland Street, spoke of his grandson who lives in Fitchburg, who attended parochial school
and a public school in Fitchburg. He was accepted into The Sizer School, a charter school. He just received his
report card and got five Bs and one A. The issue that is most important to Mr. Rodgers is the direct result of a
charter school education for his grandson. He also wanted to disclose that he is on the Foundation Board of the
Sizer School. In terms of fiscal responsibility and management, they are very well run. He personally is in tavor
of funding charter schools. He thanked the Board and stated he agreed with Mr. Mackin that the subject needed
more debate and discussion.
Mr. Alonzo thanked both for coming forward and thanked Chairman Toale for putting this on the agenda. He
believed having a forum on this item and others that impact this town are important. Mr. Alonzo stated he was
opposed to voting yes for Question 2. You are taking a single funding stream and splitting it. Those charters are
not overseen by publicly elected bodies that have direct accountability to taxpayers. The idea that there is a
lottery to choose students creates a poorly competitive system that leaves the current school system at a loss for
funds. The public school system has no choice on where people want to go. For those reasons, he is opposed to
voting in favor of Question 2.
Mr. Ebersole stated he is concerned with the privatization of the school system. He is appreciative of the good
charter schools we have in the area and the success rates for some of those students, but is concerned with the
economics. There is no cap on these additional charters and that is why he opposed to Question 2.
Mr. Toale thanked those who spoke tonight and encouraged everyone to vote.

receptive to this due to seeing the need and overall benefit. The outcome was successful. Recently we attended a
state conference at which our IT Director was a panelist and Barre was noted for being a model for this. It was a
proud moment for her and staff. Mr. Alonzo asked if there were any obstacles that she had to overcome. Ms.
Lemieux replied working out the details of the position, performing a salary survey, making sure it fit into the
current compensation plan and creating an intermunicipal agreement with the other town.
===PAGE 2===
Ms. Bertram asked if she considered her management style as a commander or a coach and to provide an
example. Ms. Lemieux replied more like a coach; my style is more hands off. The department heads are the
professionals and they know how to run their departments. If they need some guidance or help with issues, I am
more than happy to support them and help, if needed.
Ms. Luck stated many communities are faced with implementing the new MS4 permits dealing with
stormwater. Barre is not one of them. Can you share with us anything you know about it? Ms. Lemieux stated
she did not know a lot, but in years past we were asked if we wanted to participate in a regional grant program.
Ms. Luck asked how Barre deals with storm water. Ms. Lemieux replied we have a Water Commission that is
part of the Dept. of Public Works. The water employees handle all the issues related to drainage and repair of
those structures.
Mr. Toale asked what has been the most difficult management problem that you dealt with in the last five years.
Ms. Lemieux stated there was a decision made by the Barre Selectboard stemming from a situation where there
were problems in a certain department for many years. The Board tried to assist with solving those problems
with the department head and the union. Ms. Lemieux stated she was the liaison for the Board in this matter.
The issue was not able to be resolved and in the end there was a vote of no confidence. The Board decided not
to renew that person's contract. That whole process and the outcome was the most difficult as it wasn't very
popular in some respects.
Mr. Ebersole asked how she would deal with reduced available revenue in a budget year. Ms. Lemieux stated
she would look for savings in other areas, going out to bid for electricity, plan changes; another source could be
selling town land. Mr. Alonzo asked her to explain something complicated but that you know well. Ms.
Lemieux replied many people are not familiar with the municipal budget process. Breaking it down into steps
and having visual aids is helpful. Another complicated process is setting the tax rates for a town. Mr. Alonzo
asked her to explain that further. Ms. Lemieux stated you start with a budget worksheet and you look at your
starting tax levy, add your new growth and your 2½ percent limit, hit your maximum allowable levy, add in
various revenue sources, add in each department's total budgets and add in your bottom line. That's like a
balance sheet to update numbers.
Ms. Bertram stated she wanted to understand Ms. Lemieux's role in the oversight of Barre employees and her
responsibility for day to day operations. Ms. Lemieux stated it is a little different than Town Manager. In
Lunenburg's charter, you have direct appointments. With a Town Administrator the Board of Selectmen does
the various appointments, but I am the liaison for them. My role is to oversee the day to day management of
departments, make sure things run smoothly and to deal with any issues. I report to the Board and keep them
informed. I am the personnel administrator.
Ms. Luck asked if she ever had to hold a subordinate accountable for their actions. Ms. Lemieux answered yes,
there was a situation where an appointment was made to a position and it was within the probationary period.
We did end up letting that person go and I was involved in that process. It was not easy to make or do. It had to
be done in a delicate manner because of their relationship to the finances. It was the Board's decision but I was
aware of the knowledge that led to her release and I related that to the Board and they asked me to carry out it
out. That person did not take it well, but you have to remain calm and not try to get into a "back and forth" with
them about the situation.
Mr. Toale asked if he were to poll people Ms. Lemieux works with, what percentage of them would not be fans
of hers. Ms. Lemieux stated she couldn't say she was everybody's best friend. She believed she got along with
the majority of people she works with but obviously being in her position you're not going to make friends with
everyone. Mr. Toale asked if he were to talk with those people who are not fans, what kind of words he would
hear. Ms. Lemieux stated even those who may not be my biggest fans have said positive things. I can't think of
what they would say; that I am an honest person; that I try to do my job well. I am not entirely black and white,
but I hold integrity to a high level for all the department heads.
Mr. Ebersole asked her something she'd be happy doing every single day for the rest of her career. Ms.
Lemieux replied the budget part of the job. The budget is the framework on what you provide your services
from. She stated she really enjoys that. Policy is important as well.
Mr. Alonzo stated before this town was under a charter, the job position was called chief administrative and
financial officer. What side is your strongest; administrative or financial and how would you go about
strengthening the weaker of the two? Ms. Lemieux stated she really enjoys the financial part but the
===PAGE 3===
administrative portion is very rewarding as well. Finance is probably her strength. To strengthen the
administrative side, I don't have a good answer for that, maybe spending more time on that part of the job,
seeing processes through, receiving constructive criticism and feedback on how to improve those skills from the
Ms. Bertram asked how you would go about producing a working environment that supports maximum
productivity. Ms. Lemieux stated you have to know what motivates people besides income. If people's needs
are met, the really rewarding aspect is that they are part of a solution; helping department heads achieve their
goals.
Ms. Luck stated communication among town boards and committees as well as department heads can be
difficult to achieve. What specific actions have you taken as a town administrator to improve communication
among these groups? Ms. Lemieux stated she has learned to over communicate most of the time; making sure
you are relaying the message to everyone and not just a select few. Also attending meetings so you can listen to
what's going on; email is important. Ms. Luck asked what you would do as a first step to improve
communication here in Lunenburg. Ms. Lemieux stated call different department meetings, so everyone can
come together and discuss what each department is currently going through and see if there are mutual needs
that they may not have been aware of that could be addressed at those meetings. With the department heads
here, it seems like there is already a cooperative nature. They were all very welcoming and knowledgeable in
their fields so to facilitate that would appear to be an easy process.
Mr. Toale asked if it was better to be perfect and late, or good and on time. Ms. Lemieux replied perfect.
Obviously everyone wants employees that arrive on time at work, but I think striving for perfection in your field
is definitely an asset to have as department heads and staff. You want to motivate them to the next level not to
just come to work to the daily grind. Mr. Toale asked if you were working on an important project for the town,
is it more important to get it absolutely perfect but miss the deadline. Ms. Lemieux stated no, not to the point
where you miss the deadline. I put a lot of effort into everything I do, I try to make it as best as possible.
Instead of reinventing the wheel on different things, I look for best practices from other communities. I reach
out to other communities and take the best.
Mr. Ebersole asked her to describe a time when she screwed up. Ms. Lemieux stated going back to making sure
that all parties are notified; this was a lesson that I learned. We entered the Community Compact program and
one of the best practices areas we chose was regionalization and I brought it to the Board of Selectmen and they
endorsed it. But we hadn't informed all of the Planning Board members because over time it had evolved into a
regional planner position that we created for the town. That kind of had some pushback where they were
reluctant at first but in the end they came on board after they were comfortable and there was success in the
program. We are now in year two of the program. I went to them immediately because I wanted to reapply and
make sure everyone was aware.
Mr. Alonzo asked who is the smartest person that you know personally and why? Ms. Lemieux replied probably
my mother. She stated she has been fortunate to have many mentors in her life, especially her predecessor who
taught her a lot about her field and about being a people person, vital for this role. He taught me a lot of life
skills, approaching the day with a positive attitude.
Ms. Bertram stated you talked about wanting to work in a bigger community. What challenges are you looking
for in this position? Ms. Lemieux stated Lunenburg is going through some of the same challenges as Barre such
as looking at net metering agreements and Complete Streets Program. Economic development would probably
be my biggest hurdle. My experience has been around applying for Massworks grants to improve infrastructure
and the Capital Planning portion of that. We are also involved with our regional planning agency to apply for
the Massachusetts Downtown Initiative grant which we did not receive when we applied, but ended up
receiving technical assistance funding through the development committee. I find new projects exciting and I
am a lifelong student.
Ms. Luck asked what your strategies are for getting things done during the day while still managing to react to
situations that arise. Ms. Lemieux replied being organized helps me immensely. I have my daily planner that
holds details of the different projects and goals. These tools help me focus and keep me refocusing. I have an
excellent assistant that helps me throughout the day and assists me on projects if needed.
Mr. Toale stated you did your research and met with the town manager screening committee and various
department heads. What surprised you the most about Lunenburg? Ms. Lemieux stated what surprised me the
===PAGE 4===
most coming from a small town was that some people said there is not a lot of business here. From my
perspective it looks like you have a lot of businesses. When I did the tour with Chief Marino he took me around
town, I got to see the various areas. I did not know the extent of the farms in town, so that was pleasant to see;
the beautiful farmland and the lakes.
Mr. Alonzo asked what opportunities in your present job have eluded you. Ms. Lemieux stated she always
wanted to explore economic development more in Barre. My first few years were spent on housekeeping things,
making sure all the policies and procedures were updated. We've been slowly working on position descriptions.
Police Chief Marino asked if there was anything in Lunenburg that jumped out at her. Ms. Lemieux stated the
recreation aspect. To further explore that as a natural resource and that is something that would bring people
into Lunenburg. You have your beautiful lakes and recreation land. Chief Marino asked if she had any desire to
come and live in the community. Ms. Lemieux stated right now she is pretty grounded with her family where
she is.
Mr. Toale asked Ms. Lemieux if she had any questions. She asked the Board what they think are the major
issues right now facing the town and what are the opportunities?
Ms. Bertram responded that she would like to see the economic development go forward. She believed
Lunenburg had an opportunity to excel in ecotourism because of the natural resources and the numerous trails.
She stated as a Board they have done a good job on policies and procedures. She thought there is some more
work to do. She added she would like to see some standardization for personnel evaluations. It is a tool for the
employee as well as for the person responsible for overseeing that department.
Mr. Alonzo stated storm water is a huge challenge; it is going to be tough to stay on top of that because of the
cost involved. Internally a challenge is that we run the town on a skeleton crew basically in every department;
that leaves us with little flexibility. Another challenge is how to deal with all the municipal buildings. What we
do with that is both a challenge and an opportunity.
Mr. Ebersole added economic sustainability of the community. How do we do what we do with the limited
resources that are available? Mr. Ebersole stated we have a strong commitment of residents who participate on
various committees. Ms. Luck stated she sees the issues with storm water but also sees the lakes and streams as
an opportunity to improve the conditions.
Mr. Toale stated he is confident this town can do a lot of things when they focus on it, set a goal and try to
achieve it. His only concern is that they get enough people in town to participate. We just need to zero in on
whatever that vision is.
Ms. Lemieux asked what key issue makes Lunenburg unique and a good place to work? Mr. Ebersole stated we
have committed staff here who want to do the right thing. They may not always get the funding or the tools, but
after talking to the Finance Committee and saying what they want, then with whatever they get, they make it
happen. Mr. Alonzo stated from a natural resource point of view, this is a beautiful town and it makes it a great
place to want to live in. What makes it a great community is that we act as a community and even though we
may not always agree, we've developed a sense of constructive open dialogue that you don't see often in many
other communities. This Board has tried to be as transparent as possible; we share every bit of information.
Ms. Bertram stated she agreed with Mr. Alonzo and Mr. Ebersole. She added the miles of trails that we have
are phenomenal. We also have a lot of focused community groups; the Lions Club, for example. There truly is a
sense of community. Our school system is phenomenal. Everyone in town does everything they can to make
sure residents know what is going on, because our department heads and volunteers go the extra mile. Because
of that we have a strong sense of community. Ms. Luck agreed adding it's a very welcoming place. When you
need help people are there for you. Mr. Toale stated time was up and that the interview would continue next
week. Ms. Lemieux thanked the Board. She wanted to thank the department heads for being so helpful and
welcoming and she appreciated that.
After a brief recess the Board reconvened at 7:02 p.m. to interview David Marciello.
Mr. Toale asked Mr. Marciello to tell them briefly about him and why he is interested in the Town Manager
position. Mr. Marciello stated he was an attorney and has a Bachelor's degree in Political Science and a
Master's degree in Public Administration. He was the Town Administrator in Rehoboth for ten years.
He stated he has twenty years of experience working as a municipal attorney. He stated he is the Interim Town
Manager for Lunenburg and is interested in making it permanent.
===PAGE 5===
Mr. Ebersole asked what you have done that you succeeded at, but wouldn't want to repeat. Mr. Marciello
stated he does not want to work for a divided Board. He has done it numerous times and it is not a pleasant
existence. I did well at it, we coexisted and we did monumentally good things.
Mr. Alonzo asked have you ever been involved with introducing new ideas or programs into a community in
which you have worked and tell us about it and describe the steps you used to improve its acceptance. Mr.
Marciello stated he was hired in Rehoboth for one main purpose. Personnel issues were a complete shambles at
the time. They did not have any working policies or any tracking of people that were paid hourly. I had to start
from scratch, had to write the policy, all of the procedures and install tracking. It took a lot of collective
bargaining and about a year and a half. Mr. Alonzo asked what it made it successful. Mr. Marciello replied
there was no way to track how many hours people worked. It was successful economically and liability wise.
Ms. Bertram asked do you consider your management style as a commander or a coach and provide an example.
Mr. Marciello replied there has to be hierarchy; an operational plan. You have to know the chain of command
but you have to bring all the stakeholders in. You have to delegate authority but ultimately the chain of
command has to rule. So I prefer both and implement both.
Ms. Luck stated Massachusetts towns are faced with implementing the new MS4 permit. Can you share what
initiatives you have undertaken to tackle the requirements of this permit in your last job and how the community
you serve in has funded the requirements of the permit to date. Mr. Marciello stated I wrote the MS4.
I was instrumental in designing our program; it was a lot smaller than it was today. I went to a lot of seminars, I
had consultants. I helped create educational videos on public access, showing how to do things correctly and
incorrectly. We made pamphlets. In the second phase we had to come up with timetables and in the third phase
we had to implement it. Ms. Luck asked how you would implement it in Lunenburg, He stated I do not know
the town well enough yet; I need to learn what issues we have as well as resources.
Mr. Toale asked what has been the most difficult management situation that you had to handle in the last few
years. Mr. Marciello stated Rehoboth; it was during the recession. We had to face potential layoffs. We had
under-performing departments. We did not layoff or cut hours and instead took cuts in pay. I gave back almost
10% of my pay to keep the budget balanced. Eventually we came out of the recession.
Mr. Ebersole asked how he would deal with reduced available revenue in a budget year. Mr. Marciello replied
there is no one right answer. It depends on the situation you have and using the right tools.
Mr. Alonzo asked him to explain something that is complicated but that you know well. Mr. Marciello stated
Proposition 2 1/2. People want to know why their taxes went up 4% when there is Prop. 2 ½. The reason is the
town as a whole can only raise taxes 22% on the town plus any new growth that it had, plus any exemptions,
debt exclusions and overrides. That is your starting point. "John Smith" could have more because of
assessments.
Ms. Bertram asked if he could tell them about a goal he set and walk us through your thinking and planning
process. Mr. Marciello stated in 2011 my contract as Town Administrator was not extended. I thought I could
get another job easily with my experience. I was frequently told I placed second as I did not have my Master's
degree. So I went back to school. I became a graduate assistant and worked fifty hours per week and I graduated
in just over two semesters. That was a goal I set, I had to do it quickly. I had a family to support. Those were
my goals, I needed to recredential myself and to excel and I achieved it all.
Ms. Luck asked him to tell them about a time as Town Administrator he had to hold a subordinate accountable
for their actions. Mr. Marciello stated that happens frequently; everything from the minor trivial thing to
termination. He stated he couldn't be specific because they were personnel issues. Mr. Toale asked if I were to
poll everyone you worked with, what percent would not be a fan of yours. Mr. Marciello replied he could think
of a few, but you are never going to please everyone. He stated when he left Rehoboth, there were people who
were initially critical of him who told him he would be missed
Mr. Ebersole asked him what something is that you'd be happy doing every single day for the rest of your
career. Mr. Marciello stated if there was one positive change at the end of each day, and that isn't possible, that
would make me happy.
Mr. Alonzo stated before this town was under a charter, the job position was called chief administrative and
financial officer. What side is your strongest; administrative or financial and how would you go about
strengthening the weaker of the two? Mr. Marciello stated actually the charter states that the Town Manager is
the Chief Financial Officer, unless that has been changed. I don't see myself as the CFO, I am administrative.
===PAGE 6===
I understand budgets in the whole; the omnibus and the big picture. I need the financial geniuses to make sure
they walk me through the minutia. Mr. Alonzo asked him how he would strengthen his financial side. Mr.
Marciello stated he relies on the experts and every time you do that you learn a little more, you have more
expertise.
Ms. Bertram asked how you would produce a working environment that supports maximum productivity. Mr.
Marciello stated it's lateral, it's all about relationships. You bring everyone into the mix, the stakeholders.
Ms. Luck stated communication among town boards and committees as well as department heads can be
difficult to achieve. What specific actions have you taken as a town administrator to improve communication
among these groups? Mr. Marciello stated he committed a faux pas when he first started here. I sent out an
email to the School Committee but neglected to send the email to the School Superintendent. I had put in my
email please share this but it did not reach her. I faced my mistake and apologized. Communication is key but in

Ms. Luck asked how you would improve communication among all the Boards. Mr. Marciello stated he would
make sure all the information got to all the Boards by acting as an intermediary.
Mr. Toale asked is it better to be perfect and late or good and on time? Mr. Marciello stated good and on time
because steady wins the race. You want consistency and accountability. You want to improve but being late
doesn't cut it.
Mr. Ebersole asked what your relationship was in the past with legislative delegations and other people outside
of the community that you have worked in. Mr. Marciello stated he was a liaison on all different levels, state,
federal, all the agencies. Some were friendly informal relationships and some were "go through the channels."
Mr. Alonzo asked who is the smartest person you know personally and why? Mr. Marciello replied his old
accountant; I think his IQ is 185. Numbers just fell out of his head
Ms. Bertram asked what challenges are you looking forward to should you get the position. Mr. Marciello stated
I've already started with some policies. Every town this size works on a skeleton crew. Everyone tends to focus

Town Manager Candidates - Economic Development Presentations and Follow Up Questions
1) David Marciello
Mr. Marciello began his presentation by stating you should have short and long term goals. He stated viable
economic development involves the use of a comprehensive package of strategies and tools.
• Successful development in small towns is always multifaceted. Community development equals
economic development.
• Successful economic development strategies are guided by a broadly accepted vision. Case studies of 45
towns who undertook successful economic development initiatives found that in small towns, people are
the most valuable resource.
• Be proactive. Utilize future oriented approaches but also address the town's current needs.
• Use regionalization strategies to identify common opportunities and partnerships beyond municipal
boundaries.
• Short term success can build long term momentum.
• Address abandoned/blighted property via municipal fines, liens and receiverships.
• Long term goals include creating and developing smart growth; Mr. Marciello noted the Lunenburg
Village District and having a Master Plan in place.
===PAGE 2===
• Things we can start doing right now include having Mixed Land Uses, taking advantage of Compact
Designs, creating a Range of Housing Opportunities and Choices, creating Walkable Communities,
providing a Variety of Transportation Opportunities
Ms. Bertram asked Mr. Marciello if he knew of any smaller communities using the receivership option for
abandoned/blighted property. Mr. Marciello replied that Dracut is using this option. Further discussion ensued
on this option. The Board proceeded with other questions for Mr. Marciello.
Mr. Alonzo asked if there was any better answer to any question from last week that he would like to modify.
Mr. Marciello stated that he could not recall any of the questions from last week but would want to expand on
this presentation if given the opportunity.
Mr. Alonzo asked what the longest commute is that you ever had for a job and how long did you keep that
commute up? Mr. Marciello replied when he was in the National Guard for six years and it was fourteen hours
every few weekends.
Mr. Alonzo asked what in your mind determines when you will delegate something and when you will
personally do it yourself? Mr. Marciello stated he would always delegate to an expert or whoever is best based
on the scenario.
Ms. Luck asked how you would handle a disagreement between two subordinates or two boards. Mr. Marciello
stated elected boards are autonomous and you try to mediate and get consensus. If they are appointed, it
depends on how contentious it is. You let everyone have their say and again try to get consensus. You find out
what the issues are and where there is agreement and try to build on that.
Ms. Luck stated in the written questions we asked you how you view your role of the Board versus how you
view the role of the Town Manager and you mentioned that your view is not relevant. She stated she actually
thought it was. Please explain how you view the role of the Board versus the Town Manager. Mr. Marciello
stated the role of the Board is to set policy. The Town Manager effectuates the policy.
Mr. Ebersole asked regarding management style, how you would do evaluations? Mr. Marciello stated you use

person to likewise evaluate themselves. It is a formalized way on the anniversary date rather than doing
everyone on July 1st
'. Ms. Luck asked how much in advance the employee would get the evaluation sheet. Mr.
Marciello stated it is a public record, they could get it at any time.

Mr. Alonzo stated we are in a key location and are blessed with the three lakes and trail system. It is important

2) Heather Lemieux
Ms. Lemieux thanked the Board for inviting her back. She stated her first task was to identify the major
components in developing an economic development plan for Lunenburg and the steps she would take to
develop that plan.
• Vision (comes from the stakeholders and what they want for their community)
• Quality of Life (ex. Affordability, Sense of Community, Transportation Availability, Public Services)
===PAGE 3===
• Support for More Business (Water/Sewer Infrastructure, Freight Rail, Accessibility)
• Smart Growth ( Update the Master Plan, Review Priority Development Area Study by MRPC, Zoning
Bylaw Review)
Step 1 Perform an Assessment by Meeting with the Stakeholders (town officials, civic organizations, business
owners, residents) to identify the vision. You would need to review town and zoning bylaws, and any MRPC
reports. Next would be to perform a SWOT (strengths, weaknesses, opportunities and threats) analysis.
Strengths would be Route 2A and Route 13, the freight line, the five year pavement management plan and
natural resources. Weaknesses/threats would be a lack of vision, zoning bylaws, perception of not being
"business friendly," lack of water and sewer in certain town areas.
Step 2 Work with the Stakeholders to Improve on Weaknesses and Threats:
• Enter into a Community Compact with Commonwealth of MA to fund Economic Development Self
Assessment Report or related development plan;
• Work with the business community to identify their needs and support growth
• Identify Priority Development Areas and ensure necessary infrastructure is present and roads are
accessible. Possible areas- Leominster Shirley Road, Summer Street, Chase Road, Whalom District
• Infrastructure Improvements: Apply for Mass Works Grant to fund projects outside the Five Year
Pavement Mgmt. Plan; Apply for Complete Streets Program Funding that will improve accessibility and
provide up to $400,000 for construction
• Zoning Bylaw Review; Work with Land Use Director and Planning Board to ensure the endorsed
"vision" is implemented through necessary zoning bylaw updates.
Step 3 Promote the Strengths and Opportunities in Town:
• Village District; Focus on developing the Village District to create a vibrant downtown
• Freight Rail: look at adjacent available industrial/commercial parcels that wouldn't require significant
costs to develop. Work with Mass Development to educate business owners about loan/training
programs.
• Maintain the high Quality of Life; fair tax rate, high level of public services, affordable housing
• Natural Resources- preserve Open Space and expand use of recreation land. Promote trail use through
access to trail maps on the town website.
• Market the "Brand" of the Town; whether it be a vibrant downtown with shops or recreational areas to
visit, or unique community events, market it through social media and website that could be tied to MA
Office of Travel and Tourism
Step 4 Adhere to the Vision
• Follow the Economic Development Plan as agreed by stakeholders and adapt over time by consensus
• Every plan needs a champion to see it through and move it along and supporters that will assist in
carrying it out.
Mr. Alonzo asked in your mind what would be the goal why 1 as a resident would want economic development
in town. Ms. Lemieux replied most likely to lower your tax rate; bringing in commercial business could
potentially lessen the resident's tax burden. He asked what the biggest obstacle(s) is to implementing this plan.
Ms. Lemieux replied getting the consensus from all the parties. You could hold forums, have monkey surveys
on the website, survey through the tax bill mailing or meeting with public officials.
Mr. Alonzo asked it there was any better answer to a question from last week that she would like to modify.
Ms. Lemieux stated there were a few. She thought her mother was still the smartest person she knew but
another was retired Senator Stephen Brewer.
Mr. Alonzo asked what the longest commute is that she ever had for a job and how long did she keep that
commute up? Ms. Lemieux replied she worked in Boston but lived in Warren. She commuted from Warren to
===PAGE 4===
Worcester to the train station and went in to South Station and then she walked to Ashburton Place which is the
building behind the State House. She did this for a year and a half. She left due to layoffs. It took at least a three
hours round trip.
Mr. Alonzo asked what in your mind determines when you will delegate something and when you will
personally do it yourself? She replied she has a great assistant so I delegate a lot to her. Department heads are
knowledgeable in their fields. I trust them to carry out their work and get information from them.
Mr. Ebersole asked regarding management style, how you would do evaluations? Ms. Lemieux replied they
haven't done evaluations in Barre since 2008, but just this year she created two forms, one for management and
the other for non-management positions.
Ms. Luck asked her to talk about a grant that she was proud to receive. Ms. Lemieux stated she has applied for
many and has been pretty successful. Just this week they were awarded a generator grant through the FEMA
hazard mitigation program for the DPW for the fuel pumps and the DPW Building.
Mr. Toale stated we talked about the difference between a Town Manager and a Town Administrator. He asked
if you were to get the job as Town Manager, what you see as the exciting part to that difference. She replied
with a Town Administrator there are more layers versus Town Manager having that direct relationship with
department heads. I would feel very comfortable and honestly don't have any real concerns. I feel like I'm
ready for that next step in my career.
Ms. Lemieux asked the Board members what they see as the role of the Town Manager versus the Board of
Selectmen. Mr. Toale stated hopefully both are pursuing the same vision and goals. Once that task is
accomplished the Board role is to set vision and policy, deliver that to the Town and listen to the Town's needs.
Obviously the Town Manager role is more operational and managing all the department heads.
Ms. Bertram agreed with Mr. Toale but added the Town Manager should bring forward initiative and innovative
ideas. Someone who thinks outside the box and looks for creative opportunities. Not someone who is looking to
the Board for guidance, but a strong leader. Our job is to create policy and procedures and make important
decisions. The Town Manager would keep a pulse on the community and what's going on around the state.
Mr. Ebersole stated this Board has turned over throughout the last several years. This allows us to focus on
projects such as storm water, roads, economic development, governance, etc. Clearly we are at a point now that
we have a Board recognizing what the Town Manager can and should be doing.
Mr. Alonzo added because we are employed other places we don't see what's going on in the town. It's
important to bring up the opportunities or challenges that you are presented with day to day; not only
recommendations, but options. I want to know about all the options before we need to make the decisions. Any
task that is not understood well should be communicated to us that you need more clarity.
Ms. Lemieux asked if the town has looked at becoming a Community Compact town. Mr. Toale stated we
discussed it early in the process and wanted to wait and see if it was worth it for the town.
The Board thanked Ms. Lemieux and took another recess before resuming the meeting at 7:44 p.m.
Mr. Toale stated the Board should deliberate about the two candidates and discuss about how to proceed.
Ms. Bertram stated she believed they had a viable candidate. Ms. Lemieux is better suited for this community
due to a number of reasons. The fact that she is currently working in her field is to her credit. Her written
responses were excellent and she has a strong financial background. Ms. Bertram was very interested in some of
the items that she brought forward regarding economic development, such as leveraging our existing traffic
corridors and how we can benefit from that. She has exhibited a lot of time to get to know our community,
meeting with department heads and finding out their concerns and gaining some of their knowledge.
Mr. Ebersole supported Ms. Lemieux as the candidate. Both candidates were technically competent. Her
presentation skills were something that he felt more comfortable with; it was more interactive with the Board.
Ms. Lemieux feels more approachable when I think of her interacting with the public and town employees.
Mr. Alonzo stated we had two good candidates with varying skills, strengths and weaknesses. He is looking
more for a financial person and that more critical to him. Ms. Lemieux is more of a financial person and Mr.
Marciello is more of an administrative person. Ms. Lemieux's presentation skills were better, they were more
engaging and that is going to be more important for future meetings. Overall the better fit is Ms. Lemieux.
Ms. Luck stated she had opportunity to speak to seven Department Heads and the Executive Assistant to the
Town Manager. Since these are the boots on the ground people who will be working with the Town Manager
===PAGE 5===
every day, she thought their input was valuable. Although their input was varied, some themes emerged. I heard
that Ms. Lemieux appeared to be a good listener and was very personable. They liked that she said she was a
coach and they could see that and appreciated that type of interaction. It was mentioned that she worked in a
smaller town with less staff and she has probably done a little bit of everything, so she'll understand what's
going on here. Budgeting seemed to be a strength of hers. They majority felt she would be easy to work with.
They felt Mr. Marciello was diligent, a hard worker and personable. His temperament was intense and a little
