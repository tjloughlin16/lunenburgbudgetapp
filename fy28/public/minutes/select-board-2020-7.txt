Request to Rename a Tributary off of Mulpus Brook:
The U.S. Board on Geographic Names (BGN) has received a proposal from a resident of
Lunenburg to apply the new name Balish Brook to an officially unnamed stream that flows into
Mulpus Brook in Cowdrey Nature Center.
The Board of Geographic Names isresponsible by law for standardizing geographic names for
use by the Federal Government and accepts proposalsto name unnamed geographic features or
changethe names of already named geographic features. Because the Board of Geographic
Names places importanceon localopinion when it votes on these proposals, local governments
whoare electedto represent its citizens who might have an interest in the namingare asked for
their input. Thisrequest was received by both the Board of Selectmen and the Conservation
Commission.

Request to Purchase Town Owned Land at 671 West Street:
An abutter to the town owned land at 671 West Street has inquired about purchasing a 100 × 150

taken for school purposes and transferred it to the Board of Selectmen for general municipal
purposes and authorized them to sell all or portions to various abutting landowners. Our records
indicate that 5 portions of the property were sold in 2010.
I also notified Conservation and Open Space about this inquiry and if either committee had interest

topic on their agenda. The Conservation Commission has expressed an interest in this parcel, as
well as five other town owned parcels under the care of the Board of Selectmen that they may be
requesting be transferred for conservationpurposes.

Baker-Polito Administration Releases Enhanced Community Level Data on Spread of COVID-19
On Tuesday, August 11",
", the Baker-Polito Administration released additional data on community level
spread of COVID-19 in order to target additional resources and implement community specific strategies
to stop the spread of the virus. While Massachusetts has seen an overall decrease in COVID-19 cases and
hospitalizations since May, there has been a slight uptick in certain communities recently.
The Administration released data on the average daily cases per 100,000 residents, average percent
positivity, and total case counts, for all 351 Massachusetts cities and towns over the last two weeks.
Based on the average daily cases per 100,000 residents, each city or town has been designated as a higher
risk, moderate risk, or lower risk community. Going forward, this information will be updated and
included in the Department of Public Health's weekly public health dashboard, which is published each
Wednesday. Any city or town designated higher risk is considered to have a high level of COVID
infection, and will receive additional support from the Commonwealth to address the spread of the virus.
Stop the Spread Initiative:
As part of the Stop the Spread Initiative launched by the Baker-Polito Administration, there are 17 free
testing sites in higher risk communities. All residents in these 17 communities, including asymptomatic
residents, are urged to get tested and these free testing sites are also open to all Massachusetts residents.
For a list of the free testing sites visit: https://www.mass.gov/info-details/stop-the-spread.

not exempt.
