Architectural Preservation District Commission  
 Richard McGrath, Chairman     
Jim LaVeck, Vice-Chair    
Cullen Dwyer, Clerk.    
Brian Corcoran, Member.     
                           
Meeting Minutes 
Tuesday March 14th, 2019 
Ritter Building 
Call to Order: At 7:00PM 
Present:  Chairman McGrath, Corcoran, LaVeck and Dwyer 
Review of Minutes:  
Minutes from the meeting on March 12th were read and approved. 
New Business: 
Charles and Sally Dyer appeared to support their application for a CTA at 876 Massachusetts Avenue.  The application was to 
reroof their barn with either colored steel roofing or asphalt shingle.  The barn is of peg and beam construction.  It age is un -
known, but the barn was moved to its current location sometime after the civil war.  The commission approved the application as 
written, with the option to use either roofing material. 
Mike Brule appeared to support an application for the house at 92 Leominster Road.  The alteration would be a like-for-like re-
placement of the architectural shingle roof.  He brought photographs and sample materials to support the application.  The appli-
cation was lately submitted, since the posting of the agenda.  As a procedural matter, LaVeck motioned that the APDC deliberate 
on the matter.  Corcoran seconded and the motion passed.  Approval of the application then likewise passed.  Chairman Mc -
Grath agreed to put “Review of CTA’s” as a regular feature on future agendas, so that the Commission could deliberate on any 
applications that come before it. 
Old Business: 
McGrath reported that he had been coordinating with D & G graphics on the design of the signs for the APD.  He intended to 
confer with Rodriquenz regarding selection of materials. 
  
Old Primary School:  The Commission conferred on its observations from the site visit on  4-4-19.  Kit Walker of the DPW had 
shown the commission around the building and discussed his efforts to preserve it as well as ongoing problems he faced.  The 
BoS and town Manager had taken no action to comply with the APD bylaw regarding Demolition by Neglect.  The Commission 
unanimously agreed to draft a letter to the BoS and Town Manager, and to send a copy to the DPW as well.  The letter shall in-
form them that action on this matter is overdue, and include a list of items that specifically require immediate attention. 
Next Meeting: May 14th, 7PM 
Adjourned: With no further business before it, and APDC adjourned at 7:50PM

Architectural Preservation District Commission  
 Richard McGrath, Chairman     
Jim LaVeck, Vice-Chair    
Cullen Dwyer, Clerk.    
Brian Corcoran, Member.     
                           
Meeting Minutes 
Tuesday June 11th, 2019 
Ritter Building 
Call to Order: At 7:03PM 
Present:  Chairman McGrath, Corcoran, LaVeck and Dwyer 
Review of Minutes:  
Minutes from the meeting on May 14th were read and approved. 
Correspondence: 
McGrath had stopped at the town manager’s office to inquire if there was any plan of action to bring the town into compliance 
with the APD bylaw regarding Demolition by Neglect of the Old Primary School.  LeMieux sent a courteous reply, but gave no 
indication that any decision had been made, or course of action intended on the matter.  The APDC concluded that it had better 
explore other options to preserve historic properties owned by the town. 
McGrath also spoke with Mr. Rodriquenz of the DPW about the Old Primary School, who informed him that he had done a walk-
through with and architect some years ago, and found the basic structure sound.  But even at that time he felt that the interior 
would need to be gutted.  He had no funds or resources allocated for the preservation of the building. 
New Business: 
Officers were elected for the year.  By unanimous vote, Richard McGrath will remain Chair, James LaVeck Vice-Chair, and Cullen 
Dwyer recording clerk. 
LaVeck noted that the Ritter Building sign was missing. 
Old Business: 
McGrath presented an updated design for the APD road signs.  Some did not care for the font used.  Dwyer proposed to present 
various fonts to choose from for the next meeting. 
  
Old Primary School and Brooks House:  With still no action from the Town Manager’s office or the Select Board, the APDC dis-
cussed strategies for preserving disused town properties.  LaVeck reported that he would be joining the Capitol Planning com-
mission this year.  He believed that all available funds were spoken for this year, but that funds for the maintenance of historical 
town properties could possibly be allocated from the general fund in 2020.  He will learn more about the process in the coming 
months. 
Corcoran believed it might be helpful to get a contractor’s estimate for the work needed to preserve the roof, parapet and down-
spouts of the Old Primary School.  McGrath agreed, and will get permission from the DPW for the contractor to inspect the prop-
erty, and also for volunteers to clear vegetation. 

===PAGE 2===
As care of town properties is officially the duty of the town manager, LaVeck intended to speak with Town Counsel.  He wanted to 
review the APDC charter, and get advice on the extent of its charge and whether or not it would be overreaching its authority by 
getting a contractor to estimate the costs of repair and upkeep of the Old Primary School. 
As the town had voted to authorize the Select Board to sell the Brooks House building, town manager LeMieux said she intended 
to draw up a bid package to sell the house.  Dwyer opined that the house was of greatest historical value left in its present loca-
tion.  He agreed to review the video from the joint workshop with the Select Board, and record the details that were discussed 
regarding selling the house for use as a private residence in its original/current location.  McGrath agreed to talk to Mr. Burney 
regarding the same.  Regarding the bid package, the APDC agreed to inform LeMieux that removal of the building would not be 
allowed without stipulations; namely: that the house would remain under the jurisdiction of the APDC’s CTA application process, 
regardless of where it was moved to, that the house would remain in the town, and that possession of the house would revert 
back to the town if the conditions imposed were not fulfilled within a certain period of time. 
Next Meeting: Tuesday, July 9th, 7PM 
Adjourned: With no further business before it, and APDC adjourned at 8:27PM

Tuesday July 9th, 2019 
Ritter Building 
Call to Order: At 6:58PM 
Present:  Chairman McGrath, Corcoran, LaVeck and Dwyer 
Review of Minutes:  
Minutes from the meeting on June 11th were read and approved with certain clarifications. 
Old Business: 
Town Properties- Old Primary School 
LaVeck reported on discussion and public comment from recent Select Board meetings.  One town resident had presented plans 
and a concept drawing of how the property might be used, which involved removal of the building.  In LaVeck’s opinion, the inac-
tion of the Select Board regarding maintenance of the Old Primary School stemmed from a general feeling on their part that they 
needed a complete plan for all the town properties, including the possible move of town offices to the T.C. Passios building and 
re-use plans for the Town Hall and Ritter Building.  They seemed reluctant to devote resources to efforts not part of that plan. 
Partly for the benefit of Lunenburg Ledger reporter Matthew Stewart (who attended the meeting), McGrath and Dwyer reviewed 
the APD bylaw and guidelines regarding demolition by neglect and enforcement measures. 
  
Brooks House: Dwyer reviewed notes taken from the APDC’s joint workshop with the BoS on January 15th.  At that time, Land 
Use Director Burney said that since the Brooks house had been disused as a single family home for more than two years, the 
village district bylaw would require a variance to reinstate such a use.  The house shares the same lot as T.C. Passios, and 
would need to be subdivided to create a separate lot.  Access from Massachusetts Avenue would require a curb-cut from the 
Mass DOT, which might be denied due to safety and space issues between the library and high-school.  Access to the property 
via the library parking lot might be possible, though that would remain a practical and political hurdle. In sum, he felt that there 
were significant obstacles to re-use as a single family home, and that the historic character of the building would make it unat-
tractive to home-buyers anyway.  Adams mentioned that the Tappe study estimated the cost of demolition would be $30-40K, and 
moving the house would be about $30K. McQuaid noted that the Tappe study was for restoring buildings to “current use” and did 
not really apply to this situation.  He also noted the town’s past record as a negligent land-lord, and thus cautioned against rent-
ing the building. Finally, McQuaid mentioned that off-site parking for residences was permitted in the village district, and might be 
an option for the Brooks House. 
After some discussion, Corcoran concluded that despite the difficulties, selling the Brooks House as a single family residence 
would still be best way to preserve the property and its historical value.  The clerk will contact the planning board to pursue the 
next steps. 

===PAGE 2===
LaVeck suggested approaching the town manager to allocate funds for future maintenance of historic town properties.  McGrath 
thought that a letter should be drafted for this purpose.  After some discussion, it was agreed that the APDC would first seek 
advise from the Town Manager. 
Next Meeting: Tuesday, August 13th, 7PM 
Adjourned: With no further business before it, and APDC adjourned at 8:07PM

Tuesday August 13th, 2019 
Ritter Building 
Call to Order: At 7:05PM 
Present:  Chairman McGrath, Corcoran, LaVeck and Dwyer 
Review of Minutes:  
Minutes from the meeting on July 9th were read approved. 
CTA’s: An application for a CTA at 909 Massachusetts Avenue (the Harley House).  The application was not entirely clear to the 
board, and some questions lingered as to what was being approved.  Chairman McGrath attempted to contact the applicant dur-
ing the meeting, but was unable to make contact.  Decision on the application was tabled until the next meeting. 
Old Business: 
Town Properties- Old Primary School 
LaVeck reported on his discussion with the town counsel, which advised him that the APD bylaw is written so that enforcement 
requires resort to a court.  This legal action would need to be authorized by Select Board.  As it seems unlikely that the Select 
Board would authorize legal action against itself or the town manager, town council had little to offer in the way of options to force 
action on maintenance of the Old Primary School. 
One remedy discussed would be to change bylaw by vote at town meeting, so that it would authorize APDC to asses fines for 
non-compliance. 
Another option discussed would be to ask the building commissioner to enforce an order to act.  However, as Lunenburg current-
ly has no Building Commissioner, this did not seem like a very promising route. 
LaVeck motioned that the clerk draw up a cease and desist order to the Town Manager and send a copy to the Select Board 
regarding the demo by neglect of Brooks House. 
  
Brooks House:  
Dwyer announced that the the APDC was on the Planning Board’s agenda for September 9th.  He planned to attend and explain 
APDC’s position on the Brooks House: namely that the best option would be to sell the house for use as a private residence.  He 
hoped to discuss the process and steps required to subdivide lot, provide parking, easement, and to re-zone the property to this 
end. 
APD signs: McGrath is still working of getting correct proof for the sign layout. 
Next Meeting: Tuesday, September 10th, 7PM

Meeting Minutes 
Tuesday September 10th, 2019 
Ritter Building 
Call to Order: At 7:00PM with all members present. 
Review of Minutes:  
Minutes from the meeting on August 13th were read approved with certain clarifications. 
CTA’s: An application for a CTA at 909 Massachusetts Avenue (the Harley House) was reviewed with the applicant (John Kypri-
anos) present.  The sliding door in question is on the west side of the house toward the back.  The material he selected was 
vinyl.  LaVeck motioned to accept the application, Corcoran seconded and all voted in favor. 
Old Business: 
Town Properties- Old Primary School 
In a recent meeting of the Select Board, the future of the Old Primary School was discussed.  LaVeck reviewed the video record-
ing of the meeting and expressed dismay at M. Jefferies apparently disrespectful comments toward the APD bylaw.  Another 
revelation from the Select Board meeting was that, due to typesetting errors, certain portions of the APD bylaw were never sub-
mitted to the State Attorney General’s office, and so were never ratified by the state of Massachusetts.  The omission included 
the definition of Demolition by Neglect.  LaVeck had noticed the omission and notified the Town Manager, Land Use Planner, and 
Cerk by email back on November 15th, 2017; but the problem was never rectified. 
LaVeck also noted that the town’s current capital 5-year plan includes funds for demolition of the Old Primary School in 
2023-2024. 
At 7:40 the APDC went to recess, and reconvened at 7:50 in the Bilotta Room of Town Hall to meet with the Select Board. 
Facilities Manager Kit Walker had submitted an estimate to the Select Board amounting to a one-time expenditure of almost 
$11,000 for the items requested by the APDC to arrest the deterioration of the Old Primary School. 
M. Jeffries noted the discrepancies between the bylaw passed at town meeting and the version submitted to the State.  He 
agreed with T. Alonzo that the evening’s discussion should proceed with the assumption that the problem would be resolved and 
that the version passed at town meeting was the one in operation. 
LaVeck expressed his displeasure at M. Jeffries’ previous comments, and Jeffries apologized, explaining that his comments were 
not intended as LaVeck had taken them. 
T. Alonzo noted that the troubles with the Old Primary School stemmed from the town’s lack of consensus as to what to do with it.  
He asked if the APD wished to preserve the building, and McGrath replied in the affirmative, noting its architectural and historical 
value. 
D. McQuaid saw little value in the building, citing the cost to renovate it to modern standards.  But P. Luck had spoken to Ron 
Dion who was optimistic about its potential. 
K. Adams noted that attempts to convey the property had been three times rejected at town meeting, strongly indicating that the 
town wanted to keep the property.  She also noted that the property had been neglected even before its disuse. 
It was also noted that the Old Primary School building was still covered under the town’s liability insurance. 

===PAGE 2===
With the discussion concluded, the APDC retired to allow the Select Board to deliberate on the matter. 
Next Meeting: Tuesday, October 8th, 7PM 
Adjourned: With no further business before it, and APDC adjourned at 8:30PM

Meeting Minutes 
Tuesday October 8th, 2019 
Ritter Building 
Call to Order: At 7:00PM with all members present. 
Review of Minutes:  
Minutes from the meeting on September 10th were read and approved.  Ron Dion was present to clarify that he was not a pro-
fessional builder. 
Correspondence: 
The Town Manager sent an electronic letter to the APDC to clear up the confusion over the APD bylaw.  She explained that the 
town clerk made a formatting error when the bylaw was sent to the Attorney General’s office, obscuring parts of the document 
including the definition of Demolition by Neglect.  She also acknowledged that LaVeck had noticed the omission and notified her 
at that time.  She is currently taking measures to correct the issue.  She had discussed the matter with Town Council, who was of 
the opinion that the error would not affect the validity of the law passed at Town Meeting. 
McGrath sent letter to Paul Holtz at Mass Historic asking for advice on economic feasibility of renovating a building like the Old  
Primary School and bringing it up to modern building codes. 
New Business 
Ron Dion was present to discuss the Primary School.  He had followed Select Board meetings, carefully reviewed the Vertex 
study, and personally inspected building.  He had found few real problems with the building in the report or in his inspection.  
Dion said that claims of mold contamination and the dilapidated state of building are greatly exaggerated by certain members of 
the select board.  He also discovered the building permit for last roofing job 20 years ago.  He expressed annoyance at lack of 
basic maintenance on the building.  Dion also said it was very well-built and has unique historical character for the town center. 
LaVeck noted that K Adams was preparing a warrant article for the Special Town Meeting on November 12th 2019 for a non-
binding resolution to demolish the Old Primary School. 
The Special Town Meeting conflicted with the APDC regular meeting time, so the board voted to reschedule the meeting for No-
vember. 
The planning board included discussion of the Brooks House with the APDC on their 9-23-2019 agenda.  The APDC was notified 
via the Lunenburg email system, but for various reasons no members of the APDC attended the meeting.  Chairman McGrath 
will contact them to express interest in meeting at later date. 
Old Business: 
APDC signs: the board reviewed a map of the APD and agreed where signs should be located: NW of 71 Highland; 15/27 Oak; 
NE of 125 Chestnut; East of 1044 Mass Ave, (none on Whiting); North corner of Rolling Acres and Lancaster; South of 123

Leominster; W of 860 Mass ave; McGrath will indicate positions on a map and send the map and addresses to the DPW and 
Town Manager. 
Next Meeting: Tuesday, November 5th, 7:30PM 
Adjourned: With no further business before it, and APDC adjourned at 8:07PM
