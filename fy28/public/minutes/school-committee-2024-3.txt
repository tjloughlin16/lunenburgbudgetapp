Sarah Comer, 304 Lancaster Ave I have watched a lot of meetings, and it compelled me to come out and say thank you.  
For the first time in 6 or 7 years I have the felt value I place in education has been recognized here amongst the committee 
and represented to our town government.  I am very grateful to every single person on this board and this room who 
addressed the gross inadequacies in the funding for the schools.  This year was particularly brutal and the things that were 
said, some people have commented on those and some people took them personally, and I have to say as a member of the 
public when my voice is not represented, I feel the same way, so I wanted to say thank you. I think it's ridiculous that every 
single year you have to justify why the schools need more than a 2 ½% raise when they employ so many more people in the 
town than the rest of the town. Thank you for the rainbow budgets, I think there are now nine versions of a school budgets.  
Our Board of Selectmen was lamenting that the town manager would have to redo one, that's not fair.  I truly thank all of you 
and also the parents who stood up for the budget.

Mary Foyle, 985 Flat Hill Road I am actually speaking more as myself and a parent on this one, myself includes an 
employee in the Lunenburg Public Schools, as an English teacher.  Words matter. I do appreciate that from a fiscal system 
standpoint why we've called it a “needs-based” budget, sitting as a high school teacher and a high school parent sitting here 
as a person who moved into this town because, I worked here and fully believed that the education system was some of the 
best there was to offer. I’ve said this before I'm getting a little more dubious of that as time has rolled on and you're not the 
first school committee to have dealt with budgets like this. In my 20 plus year career here we've been through some tough 
years.  Covid unfortunately fortunately I don't know which one it is, was a Band-Aid for what was some pretty significant 
writing on the wall so I do appreciate that you're being a school committee, who's saying, well hey this isn't good enough.  
With the words matter I think maybe as part of your people talking about the budget we also have a different word than needs 
based because, when I get what I need I feel like that's enough to what I'm supposed to do. As a high school teacher and as a 
parent of a high school child, at best it's a limping budget. It's a budget that is actually not enough to be considered 
sustainable. So I get fiscally how it has to be called “need-based”, then that makes sense to people who are in the know of 
money management, but if you're not understanding how municipal systems work and what those words mean -  “needs 
based” actually says something very different than what that budget actually is. 
 
Ms. Brzozoski I wanted to talk about the silver linings of this process that has been absolutely wonderful.  It has opened up a 
conversations towards a better cooperation.  This entire process has brought attention to a very detailed breakdown of the 
budget in a way that I am not sure the public has ever been so engaged in.  We have she a lot of light on just how very little 
we have been working with over the years and how thin the margins are.  I have watched a lot parents come in and want to 
look at the budget, with intentions to cut the waste, only to find out that that has already been eliminated.  The most 
surprising things I have learned since joining the school committee is to learn at just how lean we have been running.  I can’t 
believe we have been getting by on so little.  We need to do a better job at conveying how rough things are and how it 
directly affects student achievement.  We need to hammer home the message that making kids wait a year for these vital 
services affect their development.  There are only 17 districts in the entire state that spend less per pupil then we do.  The kids 
of Lunenburg deserve a good education.  I also wanted to correct something…a phrase I have been hearing is that 30% of 
Lunenburg are below the poverty line, it is actually 7.2% of Lunenburg residents are below that poverty line as of the 2022 
Census.  You know what lifts children out of poverty. A quality education.  Every kid in Lunenburg deserves and needs a 
quality education.  The override isn’t perfect, and the timing is no ideal, but perfection doesn’t happen in government, we 
need to move forward and do what we can to make sure this next year for students matter, and get the job done we know we 
can.
