Jamie Toale, Chair; Robert Ebersole, Vice Chair; Tom Alonzo, Clerk; Paula Bertram, Member; Phyllis Luck,

Committee Reports
Ms. Luck attended the School Building Committee meeting. There was a report presented on contingency
spending. To date, $162,912 has been spent. There was a separate sheet with possible expenses adding up to
another $70,000. A final sheet titled discretionary items totaled $4,251,000. This list includes artificial turf for

Current Business
1. Review/Comment on Marketing Plan for Community Solar Project
Ms. Lafleur reported Joe Fiori and Eric Misbach of Nexamp were present this evening regarding the community
solar project marketing. Mr. Misbah presented a brief overview of Nexamp. The community solar project that
could provide a 15% savings on residents' electric bill is a 650kw array and is under development in fitchburg.
Nexamp's advantages are that net metering project credits are available to homeowners, renters, condominium
owners and non-protits. A 15% discount applied to your electric bill is guaranteed for up to twenty years. There
are no sign up costs and no service or cancellation fees. Changes can be made every six months. There is no
maintenance for the home owner and there is no installation of equipment on the homeowner's property. The
===PAGE 2===
signup process is simple; a person would send Nexamp their electric bill and Nexamp sends you a contract after
analyzing whether you are eligible. Nexamp would administer the signup process for Lunenburg residents. In
order to spread awareness of this opportunity, Nexamp would like to send out emails via various distribution
lists, post information on the town website, use word of mouth, social media and have a Nexamp presentation
and sign up event. Nexamp has already signed up 20 customers so far via their website. There are 50 spots
remaining plus or minus ten depending on the electrical load of each residence.
Mr. Alonzo stated he was in favor of this project and requested reserving a time period where only Lunenburg
residents can sign up for the program. Mr. Fiori stated Nexamp executives have expressed that they can't
provide exclusivity to Lunenburg residents. They can't turn away other people because they are not from
Lunenburg, but they can stop marketing it. Further discussion about the sign up process ensued. Mr. Alonzo
stated that residents can go to www.solarizemybill.com or call 800-945-5124 to sign up. He suggested a sign up
event for residents. A link will be posted on the town website for ease of sign up. The estimated "go live" date
for the project is June 2016. Resident Greg Bittner of 129 Pleasant Street asked how he should refer people.
Mr. Alonzo suggested directing them to the town website link. The Board thanked Mr. Fiori and Mr. Misbach.

Conservation Commission members Tom Bertram, Jack Rabbitt, Brad Kennedy and Carl Luck presented
regarding their disclosures. Ms. Luck and Ms. Bertram recused themselves due to being related to Carl Luck
(husband) and Tom Bertram (son) respectively.
Jack Rabbitt, Vice Chair of the Conservation Commission, spoke first. He stated the commission members were
filing these in an effort to be open and honest. He sincerely wants to protect the environment. Over the past year
it has become apparent the wetland regulations need updating, specifically the buffer zone definitions and what
constitutes being a structure. Four of the seven commission members live on lakes and would be affected by the
changes. They contacted the State Ethics Commission and were informed there was a conflict and to file the
disclosures. If four out of seven are in conflict, the commission could not discuss changing regulations. They
are looking for guidance from the Board. Mr. Ebersole stated he was okay with the request with the
understanding that if an issue affects the members' properties they would need to recuse themselves versus
rulings that affect all residents. He advised the discussions be televised and proposed regulations be published
in the newspaper, and a reporter is invited to the meeting.
===PAGE 2===

updating and Massachusetts law has been updated this year so new revisions are needed. They would like to
clean up the discrepancies between both.

involved. As it stands now, they have a bylaw that was enacted in 1997 and regulations that were updated in
2014 whose general terms and procedures need to be aligned. Ethics has deemed that there are financial impacts
to these members that merit exemption. He did not believe the Board should act on a broad exemption. If they
do, theoretically four members who live on lakes could abolish the buffer zone. He did not believe they would
do so but that would be the reality. Speaking as the Chairman, he would like to see a discussion only.
Carl Luck, 50 Sunset Lane, echoed the same request as Mr. Bertram and Mr. Rabbitt. One reason he came on
the commission was to review and implement policies. He stated we certainly would recuse ourselves if our
properties were affected. He hoped the Board would support the request for determination so they could
continue on and do their jobs.
Brad Kennedy, 17 Crocker Ave, wanted to make clear the reason that he joined the commission was to make
lakes healthier.

This meeting was not televised over the public access channel.
Mr. Toale called the meeting to order at 7:00 p.m.
Pavement Management
Ms. Lafleur stated this workshop was put together to discuss one of the Board's goals. Ms. Lafleur announced
there were two consulting engineers present, Rich Benevento and Mike Pompili of WorldTech Engineering,
Woburn, MA. She added they were here to discuss the different items the Board identified as important under
this goal. Mr. Benevento and Mr. Pompili created a presentation.
Mr. Benevento stated there was a reevaluation of the Lunenburg roads over the summer and fall and that is now
in the program and available for use to determine the pavement condition index. Lunenburg is a "62" out of
"100." In order to determine where the Town wants to be is the level of investment the Town wants to make;
pavement management in the Northeast is very dynamic. Roads and bridges worsen with age. Establishing
priorities is a town decision, where and how much to invest. Main roadways have the highest priority as they
are used the most and cost more to maintain.
If you take funding to work on the local roadways, then you are taking away from the prioritized roadways too.
What do you want to invest in your program, keep the status quo or something else?
You want to be maintaining; preventative maintenance is better than redoing an entire road; this buys you some
time.
Mr. Pompili presented a summary of the present condition of the various town roads and the costs to maintain
them via preventative actions such as crack sealing and overlay. An overlay costs about a quarter of the cost of
road construction. He added runoff surface water and overload of structural capacity are the two main reasons
roads start to break down.
Discussion ensued on various types of roads and costs for restoration. "Excellent" rated roads change to "good"
rated roads in an average five years and that is when crack sealing would be warranted. If you do nothing, the
roads continue to deteriorate and costs to restore grow significantly.
Mr. Rodriquenz stated that success with maintaining the present pavement management plan is to increase
funding annually. Ms. Bertram agreed but asked where the funding is coming from? Do we take part of the
funding for the main roads to keep up the collector, local and secondary roads?
Discussion ensued on how to inform and encourage taxpayers to pay for these roads, as well as how to fund
future new improvements while still maintaining current roads. Mr. Pompili stated they could supply scenarios
based on certain amounts of funding. Mr. Toale stated the Board needed to know what the actual cost was of
getting a road done.
Traffic Rules & Orders
The Board reviewed current traffic rules and orders as well as draft traffic rules and orders along with Police
Chief Marino. One item discussed was increasing parking fines. The discussion will continue at the January 19,
2016 meeting.
Animal Control Policy & Procedures

animal inspector qualifications and duties. The group also reviewed the MRPC feasibility study on regional
animal control.
Review of Special Town Meeting Articles

The Board signed an accounts payable warrant in the amount of $474, 419.08.
The Board adjourned

FERC Filing as Intervener (Pipeline Project)
Ms. Lafleur reported she received notice from Congresswoman Tsongas' office recently relative to the
forthcoming application filing by Kinder Morgan with FERC (Federal Energy Regulatory Commission). The
application was filed on November 20" and started the 21 day clock on filing as intervener status. The deadline
to file would be December 7". Interveners have the right to participate in hearings before FERC's
administrative law judges, file briefs, file for rehearing of a Commission decision, have legal standing in a
Court of Appeals if they challenge the Commission's final decision and be placed on a service list to receive
copies of case-related Commission documents and filings by other interveners. Interveners have the
responsibility to send any documents or communication to other interveners, FERC and Kinder Morgan.
There are a number of agencies that are active in this process. The Attorney General commissioned a study on
the need for increased gas capacity and the study found that under existing market conditions, there is no
electric sector reliability deficiency through 2030 and that no additional gas pipeline gas capacity is needed to
meet electric reliability needs. Ms. Lafleur encouraged the Board and others to go to the Attorney General's
website to read the entire findings report.
Ms. Lafleur reported a resident recommended the Town join the Northeast Municipal Coalition Against the
Pipeline. Their mission statement reads as follows: Gathering knowledge and working collaboratively, this
coalition of municipalities shall provide representation and information to relevant government bodies and the
public concerning the Tennessee Gas Pipeline project proposed by Kinder-Morgan and its effect on our
communities. The group meets on a periodic basis and the next meeting is on Monday in Lynnfield, She
recommended the Town joining the group. She would be happy to attend the meetings if there is no board
interest. Ms. Lafleur stated the Board would need to decide soon if they want to file as an intervener. Ms. Luck
asked it there is a down side of being an intervener. Ms. Lafleur stated the cost of using legal counsel to prepare
and file the petition is minimal but the expense of legal counsel following the process could add up.
The Board discussed filing as an intervener and joining the coalition. The Board concurred that legal counsel
could file for the Town but does not want them to review the docket on an on-going basis. The Board members
will do this task themselves.
Motion: R. Ebersole
2'd:T. Alonzo
To file as an intervener to FERC and to join the Northeast Municipal Gas Pipeline Coalition

Review of the Net Metering Agreement with Nexamp
Ms. Lafleur reported the Board had authorized her to negotiate the net metering agreement for the project that
was proposed to them. She presented an agreement for consideration and announced Kopelman and Paige
Attorney Rick Holland was here to review the highlights of the agreement with the Board and address any
questions.
Mr. Holland stated this agreement is for twenty years and has an option to renew for two five-year terms based
on both parties' agreement. The Town would be purchasing fifty percent of the net metering credits generated
by the system. There is a minimum floor price of .08 cents. The overall value of each credit changes over time.
There is a basic service charge that fluctuates with the market and there is a transmission/distribution charge.
After reviewing the Unitil website regarding net metering credits, the anticipated rate that Unitil would charge
the Town would be the small commercial rate. One good thing in the agreement is the Town is not required to
pay for credits unless they show up on the invoices. The Town can elect where the credits should be applied.
Mr. Alonzo asked what the Town's methods of termination are in the agreement. Mr. Holland stated in section
2.2 there are early termination provisions. Either party may terminate on the default of the other party, such as
failure to pay for the credits, or (section 4.2) a change in applicable law or a governmental authority order that
materially restricts Nexamp's ability to deliver Net Metering Credits to the Town; that materially restricts the
Town's ability to receive or use Net Metering Credits; that disallows the Nexamp's qualification as a Private
Facility under the Private Cap, or that occurs before Nexamp secures a Private Cap allocation.
Mis. Bertram asked if the timelines have been specified. Attorney Holland stated it would be determined by
section 2.2(c); the construction commencement deadline date is within 180 days from the date the agreement is
signed; the mechanical completion date is within 270 days of the effective date. This does not mean it's
commercially operable until the utility gives permission to commence operation.
Ms. Bertram asked for confirmation that the Town could use the percentage that they are obligated to purchase.
Ms. Lafluer stated the amount they are talking about would be equivalent to what the public safety building
would use. The annual value of this is around $20,000 to $25,000. The annual expense just for municipal
buildings outside of the school is around $140,000.

Ms. Lafleur reported she attended the Northeast Municipal Pipeline Coalition meeting yesterday. There were
about fifteen communities attending. Lunenburg will be hosting the next meeting at the Public Library on
January 11** at 10:00 AM. The Attorney General issued a report on the pipeline project which basically says
under any scenario increased gas capacity is not needed to meet the state's electric reliability needs. Kinder
Morgan did file its official application. There are more detailed pipeline maps available but she is not sure who
created them. There are 701 newly impacted property owners on the entire length of the pipeline that are not yet
notified. For any change in the pipeline routes after the FERC approval, eminent domain taking of property is
not an option for Kinder Morgan. The official docket number is CP16-21. A helpful website is
notrackedgasinmass.org. She was impressed by the level of knowledge present at the meeting. DEP is taking
the position that the permitting process goes through impacted local conservation commissions. There was a
strong sense that municipalities should not cede that right to the state even due to the lengthy undertaking.
