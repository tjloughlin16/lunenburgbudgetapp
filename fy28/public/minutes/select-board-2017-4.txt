Public Hearing to Discuss the Unifier Festival at 300 Holman Street: Weekday Entertainment and
Sunday Entertainment License for June 22- 25, 2017.
He reminded those present that the Board of Selectmen is here to review the Weekday Entertainment and
Sunday Entertainment Licenses. The Board of Selectmen does not have jurisdiction over zoning.
Morgan Leger, Lunenburg Ledger reporter, stated he has been covering the Unifier event and the background of
the former Lane property, part of which is now owned by Daron Massey.
Attorney Thomas Lesser, of Lesser, Newman, Aleo & Nassar, LEP, stated he was here representing the Unifier
Festival. Attorney Lesser noted the Board previously issued an entertainment license after consultation with the
Building Department, Board of Health, Police Department and the Fire Department. The Unifier Festival has
been vigilant about working with those departments and finding out what their concerns are. Those concerns
were addressed as conditions of the entertainment license that was issued. The festival occurred for the last two
years in Tolland, MA and had no incidents. This festival is family oriented for ages eight to eighty and there is
no alcohol or drugs allowed at the festival. This event is professionally produced by Greg Jones who
coordinates the Green River Festival with 15,000 attendees. This festival is limited to 500 tickets. It is issued
appropriately under section 181 of M.G.L. Chapter 140: No application shall be denied if the anticipated harm is not
significant or if the likelihood of its occurrence is remote. The mayor or selectmen may impose conditions upon a license
but said conditions may only relate to compliance with applicable laws or ordinances, or to public safety, health or order,
or to steps required to be taken to guard against creation of a nuisance or to insure adequate safety and security for patrons

Rain Barrel & Compost Bin Proposal from Storm Water Task Force (SWTF)
Ms. Luck reported currently rain barrels ($56) & compost bins ($43) are individually sold at cost.
The 2017 Annual Town Meeting established a revolving fund so that sales revenue from the rain barrels and
compost bins will be placed into this fund (formerly they went into the Town's general fund). The grant
through which these are purchased, currently $3400 annually, allows funds to be saved and expended over time
on a variety of named items/tasks. There is no cost to the Town to purchase the rain barrels/compost bins. It
benefits the Town is to get more of them out into the community to mitigate stormwater runoff issues (rain
barrels) and reduce the amount of waste (compost bins) that needs to be hauled out of town.
===PAGE 5===
In an attempt to get more rain barrels and compost bins out into the community, the SWTF has suggested that if
purchased together, the pair be sold for $80 (a $19 savings). Town departments/boards may also request to
receive barrels/bins at no charge. There is a Conservation Commission request outstanding at this time.
It seems appropriate to establish a policy, approved by the Board of Selectmen through which these goals may
be accomplished.
The Storm Water Task Force, in an effort to promote the use of rain barrels and compost bins in our
community, proposes the following policy:
• Rain barrels/compost bins, when sold as a pair, will be sold at a reduced price. Using the current cost of
$99 per pair, the proposed reduced rate would be $80 for the pair (a $19 savings, about 20%).
• Rain barrels/compost bins may be awarded when requested, to Town departments/boards at no charge.
• Any other such reduced price/give away offer will be at the discretion of the SWTF, as approved by the
Board of Selectmen.
Ms. Bertram applauded the effort in order to get them out into the community. She asked how long is the grant
good for. Ms. Luck stated we apply annually. Ms. Bertram asked if the Town is paying for the compost bins and
the rain barrels with the grant funding. Ms. Luck stated yes. Ms. Bertram stated she did not want to leave the
discount amount open- ended and proposed establishing a limit. Mr. Ebersole recommended this be done with a
limit of $1000 annually, to include what we give away at no charge. Further discussion about use of the grant
funding ensued
Motion: R. Ebersole
2nd. P. Bertram
To approve the policy as submitted with a limit of $1000 per year for the amount discounted or given
away
Vote: All in Favor

Mr. Jeffreys responded the Planning Board is responsible for working in the community to develop and revise a
Master Plan and M.G.L. c.41, § 81D requires the Master Plan to have an "economic development element
which identifies policies and strategies for the expansion or stabilization of the local economic base and the
promotion of employment opportunities." Our town charter states in section 3-10 (b) that the Planning Board
shall make careful studies of the resources, possibilities and needs of the town and shall make plans for the
development of the town. He believed the Planning Board's role in economic development is defined; its
members should consistently offer viable short-range and long-range solutions to the legislative body, in the
form of proposed and revised bylaws that advance the strategic planning for the town. The board is expected to
routinely assess whether current zoning and regulatory processes promote the desired economic effect. The

land. I think that when contractors and developers go before a Planning Board to get approval for a project, they

who review proposals in advance; come to board meetings with prepared questions, concerns and
recommendations; and who treat all people with professionalism and respect, help promote the economic
development of town.
