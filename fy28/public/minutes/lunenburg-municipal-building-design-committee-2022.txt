Meeting Minutes 
May 16, 2022 6:00pm 
Members Present: Kristina Masaitis, Brian Lehtinen, Michael-Ray Jeffreys, Matthew Allison 
Members absent: David Blatt, Greg Roy 
Town Employees: Superintendent Burnham, Steve Walker 
Community Guests: Anthony Sculimbrene, APDC representative, Carol Archambault, Laura Kelly, Sophie 
Shapiro 
Meeting called to order at 6:02pm. 
1. Public Comment:  None 
2. Discuss Extended Day Programming Needs with School Committee and Superintendent of Schools: 
Michael-Ray Jeffreys summarized the change from TCP Building Design Committee to the Municipal 
Design Committee and the need to involve stakeholders in the process. Brian Lehtinen summarized the 
contingency plan for school programs at TCP to initiate a discussion with the superintendent and school 
committee members. Kristina Masaitis asked about enrollment in programs and what the reduction 
would be. The Superintendent stated the cap for extended day is 75 but could not say what the exact 
enrollment would be if programming would move to the cafeteria of the schools.  The enrollment would 
be no greater than 30 in each school but would depend on staffing. The gym space is not available due 
to basketball programming.  The superintendent discussed the ACE program and the importance of life 
and job skills in the community in which these students live in, rather than outsourcing. Peter 
Beardmore commented that this needs more discussion and that TCP is not a long term space for the 
programming and we need to find another space for them. He stated he would like to see feasible 
alternatives, because we know the programs are in jeopardy but what is the path forward. The 
superintendent responded the response was just in regards to bringing programs back to the school 
buildings. There is a possibility of using available municipal buildings for programming. Community Links 
Programs also need a dedicated phone line per the grant.  The superintendent stated it was premature 
to reach out to Fitchburg and Leominster Public Schools about taking on the students from our ACE 
program for work experiences which would be more cost effective than renting space. Matthew Allison 
stated that many adults do not have children in the schools and all four programs are like day care. The 

===PAGE 2===
superintendent stated that all these students are entitled to a public education until age 22. Matthew 
Allison stated that we heard from the Superintendent stated we could relocate programs to the schools. 
Brian Lehtinen stated that at prior meetings we had discussed that school programs could be moved 
back to the schools if space was needed for expanding town hall and offices but it was not ideal, as we 
see in the letter from the superintendent. Kristina Masaitis asked about shared space for some of the 
programs like at the Boys and Girls Club during the day.  Brian Lehtinen also stated he has concerns that 
the TCP building and space may not be available for school programming and could Ritter or Town Hall 
be a possible location and what would need to be done to the buildings for the schools to use the space 
or a Turkey Hill renovation or new build have space. Michael-Ray Jeffreys stated that the town will not 
support a $40 million dollar project and we have to focus on what we can pass and whatever solution is, 
we cannot have a net loss to the community in order this to pass. There is likely a time that TCP will not 
be in use and the most cost effective way is to remove the occupants. A long term solution may be 
Turkey Hill add on for permanent homes of these programs.  Michael-Ray Jeffreys inquired of the school 
committee on a position on the land of TCP and should it be used for a future school. Additionally, can 
the schools do a records review to save square footage space on record keeping similar to how the town 
just approved. Michael-Ray Jeffreys stated we need to get buy in from both seniors and parents. 
Anthony Sculimbrene asked about the four programs, how many are required to be provided a public 
education, and are they here to stay. The superintendent stated 3 out of 4: ACE, Community Links, and 
the Pre School program have an educational component and the programming is here to stay because of 
the community needs. The ACE program is a school program that is required by the state. Other 
programs could fall under the recreational department or a boys and girls club. The boys and girls club 
had stated prior that they could not accommodate for more grades. The superintendent is not sure if 
she will be able to staff a safe program that is self-funded. Staff are paid by the fees and if we need to
