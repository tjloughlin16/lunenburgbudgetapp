LUNENBURG PUBLIC LIBRARY DIRECTOR’S REPORT 
   FY25 Budget Update 
 
   Staff Update 
I will meet with Deb Goodsell, the new Director for the COA, on Thursday afternoon. We plan to 
discuss coordinating and collaborating on programming and a potential joint grant opportunity. She 
and I have already discussed regular collaborative meetings.  
I trained Kaitee on book common book repairs, and she will help us moving forward to keep the 
volume of books trapped in “Damaged” status to a minimum. 
We have been working on coordinating the renovation project with staff to minimize disruption. 
Construction should start January 24th with the two new offices, then progress to the demolition and 
changes to the Teen Room space. At this point, we are estimating two months total for the work. 
Although there will not be dedicated room space for teens, the majority of the collection should still 
be accessible once it is setup in the Main Hall, and the rest will be in storage. Teen visitors will have a 
designated computer bank and area during afterschool hours, where Kaitee will be on duty to assist. 
We have asked the schools, and Boys and Girls Club to include informational details in their 
newsletters. We will send out a special newsletter message with details of the renovation plan, too. 
Announcements will also be made on social media, and requested to be made by the Select Board. 
Our Budget requests are due to the Town Manager by January 21, and I have prepared our list of 
requests. 
I will have School Resource Officer Munroe offer an informal staff training at our February staff 
meeting on de-escalating interactions and approaching patron interactions with respect. I am also

January 2025 
Summary of Last Month’s Programs & Events 
➢ We restarted the Saturday morning music clinics on December 7th with Lunenburg music teacher 
Jason Graham. And it was a great success for the first session in the series with 12 attendees. 
➢ The winter artisan showcase and December concert took place on Sunday December 8th throughout 
the library. We had over 30 vendors selling their crafts inside from 9 am to 1 pm, Santa took pictures 
from 9 am to noon, and Chris and Katie Laford entertained an audience of 62 people with their Celtic 
Christmas concert from 2 – 3:15 pm. It was a smashing success for the library and despite the 
amount of effort it took to coordinate, I think this is something we will definitely consider doing as a 
new annual tradition! 
➢ 24 puzzle enthusiasts joined us for a special holiday puzzle race on December 17th from 6 – 7 pm. It 
was a very challenging 500-piece puzzle, but the teams had a wonderful time working together to 
finish what they could in the allotted time. We will host another puzzle race during the February 
school vacation week. 
➢ Craft and Chill took place on a snowy evening on December 20th after the library closed at 6 pm. 
There were 13 people who braved the elements for cookie decorating, recipe sharing, and hot cocoa 
sipping. Of note were four women who each brought a teen son with them for a group mother-son 
outing. They all had a blast and appreciated that the library offers programs that support a multi-
generational experience. 
➢ I ran a totally new in-house created program for the first time: an all-ages holiday music bingo 
program the day before Christmas Eve. We had huge registration numbers, with over 40 people 
expressing interest in attending, so we prepared accordingly. However, only 23 people came for 
holiday music bingo that day, which ended up working out for the best as the rounds went by much 
quicker than anticipated. We gave out mini trophies to winners and were able to do a few extra 
rounds. Ages of attendees varied from toddlers to seniors, but all enjoyed the experience. We may 
attempt another music bingo program in the future.  
➢ On December 30th the library held the introductory “Sew Simple” meeting for anyone interested in 
learning to sew to meet, discuss potential projects, materials that may be needed, and other details 
for our monthly meetings going forward. Only three people were in attendance, but I am optimistic we 
will have better success at the Sew Simple sessions this year. 
 
This Month’s Programs & Events

HIGHLIGHTS FOR MARCH: 
SENSORY STORY TIME WITH MISS PAM: 
Pam Wockenfuss presented Light Up the Night sensory story time for an eager group of 10 children.   She 
shared this wonderful story with the kids and their families and then allowed time to experience the story with 
all of their senses through the use of individual sensory boxes.   Parents/caregivers are encouraged to 
participate along with the children as Pam explains the many benefits of sensory play. Lots of thought and 
sensory development awareness goes into developing the bins and I love that the kids get to keep them.
  
ART CLASS WITH MISS JULIE 
We held our first art class with Julie Harrold and the children loved it! Julie has a BFA in Studio Arts, is a former 
Elementary art teacher/Art director and currently a homeschool mom who also teaches art classes in her spare 
time.  She has a wonderful rapport with the kids, and they all benefit from her supportive, encouraging manner of 
teaching. 

===PAGE 8===
                
 
 
S T E M WITH CHRIS:  
Two great S T E M classes with Chris this month-  
Simple Circuits: This workshop focused on creative thinking, and problem solving through circuit wiring concepts 
such as parallel and series circuits.  Experimentation was key as the kids built their projects and got to see what 
didn’t work and they worked to find a solution that would. 
Hydraulic Critters: In this class, the kids learned about how hydraulics work while creating a hydraulic bug.   
                    
MOTHER’S DAY SEED PLANTING: 
On a cold, drizzly Saturday day we gathered in the Story Craft Room for a two-part plant event.  At first, we talked 
about gardening – how the process of gardening starts and what plants need to grow.  Then we planted some 
marigold seeds.  
As the seeds sprout and begin to develop, library visitors can watch the seedlings grow until they are ready to 
become a gift for someone special. The librarians here at LPL will take good care of the growing plants until the kids 
come back in May to transplant the baby plants into bigger pots and decorate them for Mother’s Day gifting.

LUNENBURG PUBLIC LIBRARY DIRECTOR’S REPORT 
Annual Town Meeting on May 3 was the big event in the last three weeks since we met. It was a long 
meeting, running until about %:30, but a positive one for the library (See preceeding Budget update), 
and approval of a capital plan that include HVAC design and planning for the library. The two large 
debt exclusions failed to achieve 2/3 majorities on the Town Meeting floor, but are on the ballot this 
Saturday, while the Citizen’s Petition Override of Prop 2 ½ to raise funds to give the schools $2.1M 
passed and is not on the ballot. 
The Friends approved money for the Conroy Memorial Garden, and I met with the artist I would like 
to work with to discuss the project. Kevin Boussom, son of Laura, is excited to work on the project 
and anticipates completing his work before the fall. Robin was able to source the remaining plants 
she needs from Central Mass Gardens, and we are likely to have a bed prep and planting day in the 
next few weeks (likely May 24). 
I met with Deb Bookis on 4/16 and hoping to meet with her and Tara (STEM club leader) to continue 
that conversation. Kaitee has returned from FMLA and is working on developing a partnership with 
Pam Vallee.  
Our SRP is finalized (save one day where we will have a program but have had one presenter unable 
to come through, leaving us scrambling). We will be serving meals again, M-Th at 12 noon from June 
23 to July 31. We will be working the same vendor as last year. 
We have started gathering data for our Staffing Workflow Study. This week all staff will log their 
tasks throughout the day and Lydia and I will compile and analyze afterward. Likely we will repeat 
the log during the SRP to deepen our data. Lydia and I have identified a few areas already that 
require some attention in our staff hierarchy to help clarify roles and assignments. The goal for this 
study is to document data that will support our requests for funding additional staff positions or 
adjustments to our current organizational hierarchy. All staff are working on their individual projects 
according to our staffing plan. I have put copies of the staff evaluation in our meeting folder for your 
review. I plan to complete individual evaluations and reviews during the summer for all staff. I am 
considering creating an rolling schedule for evaluations that would happen around hiring 
anniversaries to spread out the rather large task of reviews. 
Next week is the annual MLA conference on the Cape. Melanie and Joslyn are slated to go. I will 
attend a Mental Health First Aid training on May 20. I am also planning to get together with Library 
Directors from Leominster and Fitchburg to share ideas and progress. Ongoing Zoom calls between 
Directors on the topic of our current funding situations continue.

LUNENBURG PUBLIC LIBRARY DIRECTOR’S REPORT 
Our HVAC system needed attention early in May. THE board that had been repaired two years ago, 
needed some tweaks to programming and an electrical repair. The system seems to be operating 
efficiently again.  
The new cleaning crew seems to have learned how to enter the building without setting off the 
alarm. They are tasked with cleaning everything. So, we expect them to do so. Most departments in 
town reported having repeated issues at our last department head meeting. 
Still waiting for Lakeview to have time in their schedule to get the Proctor Memorial Garden water 
feature running again. Test pits for the rainwater garden happened last month, and Robin and I met 
with the design firm and Bill Bernard to discuss specifics of the latest draft design. The test pit results 
dictate that the garden function more as filter than as an aid to infiltration, and we have tempered 
the maintenance needs of the plantings with a focal point, so that our volunteer labor can effectively 
help establish the native plants that will be going in. The landscaping crew will be able to mow 
around the filter beds without disturbing the native plants. 
▪ Technology 
Another of the new batch of Dell public access computers broke mid-April. It has been sent to the 
manufacturer for repairs. 
Mason helped to install a security monitor in the Director’s Office mid-April. Melanie and I have 
begun to gather data for our ongoing digital resource assessment.

Summary of Last Month’s Programs & Events 
➢ On Saturday April 5th we had 20 people join us for another session of the music clinic series. 
➢ Janet Winer, Abigal Perrine, and I have launched community information sessions about the 
upcoming 20th anniversary of the library building. We held one on April 8th that was not well attended 
but the morning session on April 28th had two volunteers attend. 
➢ Our craft & chill program for April was an LCC funded paint night on April 11th. We had 20 people join 
us for a fun family night out that was a huge hit! 
➢ Monthly Music Series: Irish Poetry & Song with Kate Chadbourne was on Sunday April 13th and drew 
35 attendees for another stellar performance by Kate! 
➢ A homeschooling advocacy group gave an introductory information session on Tuesday April 15th to 
14 people with varying interests in homeschooling.  
➢ On Wednesday April 16th we hosted Jay DiPrima for a return program with Henry David Thoreau. 
This time he read Thoreau’s work “Economy” in period costume to 16 people. 
➢ We had a return visit from Cathy Karp for another Visible Mending Workshop on Friday April 18th. 
There were 8 people at this workshop. 
➢ During the school vacation week, we had five teams with a total of 18 participants join us for another 
Puzzle Race on Wednesday April 23rd.

anyone to come and share their favorite poem with the community. However, only one brave local 
poet came to recite his favorite work. Instead of holding the program, he and I had an in-depth 
conversation on poetry for a few minutes during which he shared some insights to help us next time.
