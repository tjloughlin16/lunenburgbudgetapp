LUNENBURG PUBLIC LIBRARY DIRECTOR’S REPORT 
   Staff Update 
May 19th, Melanie attended the annual MLA conference. Some valuable sessions included discussion 
of book challenges and legal frameworks, civic literacy post-election, and updates on the current 
challenges around IMLS funding and cuts. I was certified as a Mental Health First Aid responded at a 
workshop on May 20, and the skills learned should prove valuable in referring those in need to 
resources that will help them and in reducing stigma around mental health challenges. 
I met with Deb Bookis and Tara Bastien of the schools to discuss ongoing parterships and 
collaborations. It seems as if these efforts may result in resumption of 5th grade visits! We plan to 
share resources and support around STEM topics as well.  
Town Administration will be initiating “Tri-Board” (Select Board, FinComm, and School Board) 
meetings starting this month. Their charge will be to advise on a comprehensive fiscal plan for the 
town’s future. This group will ask for more information, earlier about FY27 needs. I will be presenting 
in August about the library’s anticipated needs. This puts a little more pressure on Lydia and I to 
complete our staffing study earlier than we thought we might. 
The Conroy Memorial Garden is about half planted. Around 20 people gathered on May 24, including 
Robin, Lydia, Susan Conroy, and family and friends. Kevin Boussom is working on the sculpture and 
anticipates delivery before the fall. 
North End Subaru has expressed their commitment to another year of support for the Month Music 
Series, and their intention to support us long-term. Both Library and Jason from North End are 
appreciative of the partnership. 
   Programs 
Mother’s Day Planting 2.0 had 38 attendees! Our Enchanted Plants program brought 16, despite 
competition with the rescheduled Cruise Night. Niki’s Weight Strengthening sessions has seen a dip 
in attendance. A few program cancellations happened this past month due to low registration 
numbers, like the Women in Jazz performance, Sew Simple Sessions, movie night, and video game 
night. 
You may not have realized it but our magnificent Children’s Librarian Debbie Laffond held her final 
storytime in May. Her retirement will come in the Fall, and she will not deliver the Fall storytime 
sessions.  
Coming up: Summer Reading! Registration is ongoing, events start 6/23, including free summer 
lunches! 
   Outreach 
We have been reaching out to potential partners for outreach services, including Arrive Lunenburg 
and Tri-Town Apartments, and Smokestack Coffee Roasters. We are assembling a Welcome to 
Lunenburg and the Library packet of resources.

 STORY TIME WITH LOCAL AUTHOR STEPHANIE LUCIER 
Local, beloved elementary teacher Stephanie Lucier came by to share her story based on her 
cat Binx: Lucky believes that he is the unluckiest cat in the world. He has tried every good luck 
charm he can think of and still he finds himself involved in silly mishap after mishap.  But with 
the help of his mom and positive thinking, Lucky perseveres and learns that luck is something 
you get to make for yourself! 
Ms. Lucier teaches at the Lunenburg Primary School and has a passion for writing. She 
currently resides in Massachusetts, with her family and her own little "Lucky" named Binx. The 
children were very excited to have their teacher as a guest reader and really enjoyed the story 
and accompanying craft.

MOTHER’S DAY PLANTING 
The kids who had planted seeds in April came back to the library to repot them into larger 
containers, after decorating them for someone special, to give away for Mother’s Day.  They 
were quite impressed to see how the seeds had grown into small plants!  We all worked 
diligently to make sure the seedlings survived and were happy to send them on to their new 
homes!

Summer Reading Program. I also assisted at the Scout table where we offered the kids a 
chance to make a wind powered balloon car- a very popular craft! All of this was accompanied 
by millions of bubbles produced by the library's bubble machine!

Summary of Last Month’s Programs & Events 
➢ On Saturday May 1st we had 12 people join us for the final session of the music clinic series. Due to 
their success, we will negotiate continuing the series in the fall of this year.  
➢ We hosted a fabulous Floral Design Workshop on Tuesday, May 13th. There were 15 people in 
attendance, and it was a very engaging group that made the workshop a huge success! 
➢ The annual Spring Puzzle Swap that took place on Saturday, May 17th was another successful swap 
event. We had 52 people visit to exchange 472 tickets for selections from the 656 donated puzzles. 
➢ Wednesday May 21st we postponed our Monthly Music Series program with jazz flutist Galen Abdur-
Razzaq on Zoom for a tribute to women in jazz performance. We will most likely reschedule in late 
winter or early spring when virtual programs may be more appealing. 
➢ On Friday May 23rd, we hosted an opening reception for the art exhibit: Lunenburg High School AP & 
Honors Art Thesis Show. That was very well attended, with an estimated 58 people visiting the 
reception. The exhibit ran May 17th-31st and is something we will continue to do with the school in the 
coming years. 
➢ The library sponsored author Varla Ventura for a unique talk on botanical folklore based on 
information from her book Enchanted Plants on Wednesday May 28th. There were 16 people at this 
informative and engaging talk, despite the draw of Cruise Night happening concurrently outside.  
➢ On Thursday May 29th the folk-rock duo Creeque Alley returned to perform. One of our most popular, 
well-received, and enthusiastic performers, we were very excited to welcome them back! There were 
25 people enjoying the music and singing along that evening. 
This Month’s Programs & Events 
➢  Local Lunenburg resident and personal crisis coach Pamela Dussault visited on June 4th for a talk on 
managing caregiver burnout. There were 11 people here for the program and it was very well 
received, with calls for more support groups and workshops to be held at the library in the future. 
➢ On Thursday June 12th we will host a return visit with Mark & Raianne for a folk duo concert as part of 
the Monthly Music Series. 
➢ There will be a summer puzzle race on June 17th. 
➢ For a creative workshop, the library will be holding a fused glass pendant making class on June 18th. 
➢ And as part of summer reading, on June 25th there will be a “bad art” night for patrons of all ages to 
come to the library and make creative artwork using odds and ends pieces from the craft supply. It 
should be a fun event to mark the beginning of summer!

LUNENBURG PUBLIC LIBRARY DIRECTOR’S REPORT 
   Staff Update 
We are coming off the best statistical Summer ever for the library! Our SRP was a wild success, with 
more than 400 participants. Starting registrations at the beginning of June also supported the 
readers who were interested in starting as soon as possible. The majority of registrants signed up 
during the month of June. We also served more than 1600 lunches during SRP, thanks to Main Street 
Bank and the Friends! The summer was very busy and staff have been putting in the extra effort to 
make it a spectacular experience for all who visit the library! 
FY25 concluded with very positive statistics on the whole. Submitting ARIS in August, we counted 
68,459 visitors between July 1 and June 30. 77,543 physical items circulated (for a value of $1.148M), 
with another 34,363 digital circulations. These are all increases, with the digital circulations jumping 
almost 30% over FY24. 
The next two months promise to be quite busy for our staff. Debbie Lafond’s retirement will be in 
early October. The day for the public to visit her and express their appreciation will be October 6 
between 10 and 5. Our celebrations of the 20th Anniversary of opening this facility take place during 
October. 
We are engaged in two job searches, Children’s Librarian and a Library Page. We are about to hire for 
the Library Page position, and we will begin to invite applicants to interview for the Children’s 
Librarian position late this week or early next week. The Town Manager organized a town employee 
wide event in August, which continued to support a friendly, collaborative atmosphere for town 
employees. The Library’s own Marabeth Balboni won recognition for her excellence. 
I presented to the Tri-Board (Selectboard, School Board, Finance Committee) meeting about Library 
budget anticipated needs for FY27 and beyond on August 26. The presentation was much more 
detailed than my usual presentations and much earlier in the year. I also participated in the 
Massachusetts Library Aid Association scholarship selection process, although I was not able to 
attend in person this year. 
We are supporting significant effort to engage in professional development for both Joslyn and 
Kaitee. They have signed up for a number of courses and workshops over the summer and into the 
fall. Joslyn has worked really hard to develop a story-time calendar for September-December and will 
be delivering those story-times. This endeavor will be a hand-on learning experience to help her 
develop her Children’s Librarian muscles and to help the organization bridge a transition to a new 
Children’s Librarian. Kaitee is focusing on customer service and reader’s advisory skills. We are also 
attempting to rebuild our 5th grade visit program, and establish a partnership with the school 
librarian at the Middle-High School. So far, after school crowds are even bigger than last year! 
I will be targeting two grants this fall. One will be from Emerson Hospital and the other will be a 
Community Foundation grant. We will also be introducing at-home delivery for Meals on Wheels 
participants. If those home-bound individuals would like personally selected library materials, they 
can join our program and the books will be delivered and returned by the Meals on Wheels drivers.

LUNENBURG PUBLIC LIBRARY DIRECTOR’S REPORT 
FY25 library programs brought in nearly 7,000 attendees at 410 programs. We hope that this upward 
trend will continue as we focus this year on balancing staff time with engagement at events. 
Especially with the Children’s Room transition and other anticipated commitments for our wonderful 
staff, we will have fewer resources to direct toward programming, so this balance is very important. 
After a brief lull in activity during August, the school year has really blasted off to a quick start. Book 
groups have grown (11 for both Bookworms and In-Betweeners groups), and our first story time had 
solid turn out (30) for a musical theme.  We are endeavoring to plan and schedule programs a bit 
further out to help with our workflow and conscientiously selecting events to offer. Our first Paint 
Night was a booming success: with only 20 available spots, more than 30 people were wait listed. 
In our Teen Room, we are also offering an activity (less structured and more “come and go” 
atmosphere than a “program”) three days each week. We are also offering approximately one teen 
program per week with an immediate goal of increasing program attendance. Our focus here is to 
develop an expectation around activity and engagement being regularly available for teens, an 
expectation that has been missing for a few years. 
Coming up: Around the World Storytime (9/17), Teen Coding Club (starts 9/16), LBA Lunch and Learn 
with Jay’s Cryptic Creatures (snakes, 9/20), a rebranded “After Hours” video game night (9/26), 
Monthly Music Series: History and Legends through Song with Sultana (9/24), “Stamped” Discussion 
and Author Q&A (9/25), Flu Clinic (9/20), and Weight Strengthening with Niki (returns 9/30). 
Anniversary Celebrations will be an Opening Ceremony on 10/3 and Closing Event with concert on 
10/24. Scavenger Hunts for three different age groups will be offered as well. 
   Outreach 
Outreach has been moderately successful over the summer. Having the Friends participate makes a 
lot of sense. Moving into the Fall, we are targeting events like LBA’s Trunk or Treat and visits to 
Arrive and Tri-Town. These are data gathering engagements for us, and could be very useful as a 
chance to advertise Friends benefits. Smokestack Coffee Roasters is our next target for partnership. 
We will also be starting at-home delivery partnering with LAAC’s Meals on Wheels as mentioned 
earlier in the report. 
▪ Building & Maintenance

this Fall. This project will add a significant new demo space to the property and support education 
around dealing with storm water runoff for homeowners and for businesses. 
▪ Technology 
As part of our preparation for the Tri-Board presentation, I met with IT department (Casey, Mason,

schedule should be a part of that department’s presentations moving forward. 
It does seem that our ongoing hard drive issues in our public access computers are a result of 
improper selection of hardware. Deep Freeze is likely frying the hard drives, and we may need to 
upgrade to Enterprise quality SSDs to avoid the issue. We upgraded to cloud-based management of 
Deep Freeze to save significant time for Melanie.

HIGHLIGHTS FOR JUNE, JULY & AUGUST: 
• Our Summer Reading Program this year was a great success!  As seen above, 
we increased our numbers in all areas of registration and program attendance.  
We had 17 reader reward prizes- 90% of the items culled from donations!  It's so 
rewarding to have kids and families embrace and engage in the many activities 
that we have worked hard to put together! 
• Starting summer reading registration on June 1st proved to be a good decision.  
We were able to capture and inform more young families during a time when it 
was not as busy here in the library. 
• Joslyn did an excellent job planning and executing the weekly craft sessions for 
the kids. There was lots of enthusiastic participation in these events! 
• Weekly lunch and story time started off slowly but definitely gained momentum 
as we went along.  Offering a storytime event in the summer was a request that 
we have had many times, and it felt appropriate to offer this service. Sitting in the 
air-conditioned Story Craft Room, eating your delicious, free lunch while listening 
to a story or two definitely worked for many families. 
• Our Ice Cream Social was a big hit!  We provided the ice cream and invited 
families to bring their favorite toppings.  We had a well-organized set-up and lots 
of volunteer help which certainly contributed to the success of this event- and 
who doesn’t love a bowl of yummy ice cream shared with friends on a steamy, 
hot day! 
• We hosted a return favorite event this summer- Water Day. Using a couple of 
hoses, some buckets and sponges, a jar of marbles, and, of course, our lovely 
water feature- the kids were able to play with water in so many fun ways!  This 
fun event cost $0, required lots of sweat equity and help from an army of 
volunteers!   Kids and water- we couldn’t miss! 
• A visit with the Whale Mobile was also a star event of the summer.  Sitting inside 
a 40’ whale while someone educates and entertains you about these amazing 
creatures is an experience that the kids will remember for a long time! 
===PAGE 11===
• Another big hit of the summer was an alpaca story time with Kristi Mahoney.  
Ms Mahoney shared her book Alpacas Make Terrible Librarians (it was agreed 
that they do) and then we all got to visit with and learn about Jackie Chabot’s 
alpacas! 
• Kids and families got to meet Dr Fortuna, our new superintendent, here at the 
library.  Dr. Fortuna hosted a lively story time where she shared a couple of 
books about starting school and answered questions from the kids and families.  
She was wonderful- calm, relaxed and friendly! 
• On a warm, sunny Friday afternoon, kids and families gathered at the DPW 
building to “touch a truck” So many participants for this great event!  We were a 
presence at this and shared, along with information about our fall activities at the 
library, temporary truck tattoos, lollipops and lots of goodwill.  We are hoping this 
becomes an annual event! 
• And, lastly, a huge thank you to Main Street Bank for providing the opportunity to 
serve free lunches every Monday-Thursday. Many families came by every day 
to take advantage of this, and we know that for many of these it was fulfilling a 
need.  It felt great to be able to do this for our patrons.

LUNENBURG PUBLIC LIBRARY DIRECTOR’S REPORT 
   Staff Update 
We are currently interviewing candidates for our open Library Assistant position in the Children’s 
Room. Our Teen Librarian Kaitee has resigned, and the job has been posted last Thursday. Although 
we are now at a critically low staffing level, with two parental leaves coming up, we can move to fill 
our open positions.   
Lydia and I have met and discussed with the Town Manager and the Assistant Town Manager/HR 
Director the duties Lydia will perform while I am on leave. They are in negotiations with the Union 
about a stipend to compensate Lydia for this additional responsibility. 
I submitted two grant applications in early November. One for $1000 from the Bemis Community 
Fund for five monthly sessions of music and movement storytime with Monument Square 
Community Music School. The other grant was from the Emerson Hospital Catherine Gallery 
Community Grant for materials to support the LAAC’s Memory Café program. There will be 10 
memory kits that can circulated to Alzheimer sufferers and their caregivers. These will be housed at 
the LAAC and catalogued by the library. 
The Town’s Financial Department continues its transition. The Finance Director who was hired has 
decided not to stay, and payments will continue to be slow. Two consultants have been tasked to 
help, one with payroll and one with Accounts Payable. An Assistant Accountant has been hired. 
STM has a significant update to the SAP. 
   Programs 
The biggest update in Programming is our Teen calendar. We have had to cancel a number of 
programs, though we are continuing what we can. One of the two book clubs will likely not continue, 
but we have serendipitously had a volunteer paraprofessional from the High School who will try to 
get a board game club off the ground on Thursday afternoons! She is learning about DM’img D&D 
campaigns and may be able to take over leading that series. Our Teen CPR program has a number of 
registrations despite being scheduled the weekend after Thanksgiving.  
Our Anniversary celebration was a big success and the Friends seemed very happy with the result. 
We have continued to offer the Library of Services fundraiser. Our Small Works 2025 exhibit in 
hanging and we had almost 30 attendees at the opening. The Wildcrafted Apothecary Event was also 
remarkably successful with 25 attendees. This year’s Fall Puzzle Swap had 56 attendees, but more 
than 760 puzzles were dropped off (a record high). Our bluegrass duo drew 51 attendees, and our 
weekly Stitchcraft group has create more than 50 hats to donate to the local Meaghan’s Hats charity! 
Holiday programs are about to hit. The Winter Artisan showcase will be December 7th. 40 vendors 
signed up within minutes of the registration being opened. 10 vendors will provide an indoor 
“Farmer’s Market” in the Teen Room during the event. “The Dirty Martinis” will perform afterward. 
They have inquired about continuing a regular market during the winter season, too. The First 
Thursday of December will be our annual craft to follow the tree lighting in the Town center. And 
December 13th will be the family gingerbread house event that has been popular the last few years.

Coming up: 11/20 Board Game Club, 11/29 CPR for Teens, 11/22 The Dangerous Art of Chainsaw 
Sculpture, 12/4 DIY Ugly Sweaters, 12/7 Winter Showcase, 12/13 Gingerbread Houses. 
   Outreach 
Our presentation at Baker Creek received very positive review and Melanie was invited back to talk 
to them again. She had 17 interested and curious residents in attendance on 10/28. Trunk or Treat 
was AMAZING. We saw several hundred children and families, and this event with the LBA is 
definitely something we should do every year. Similarly, Literacy Night was a great event to be a part 
of with about 50 interactions. Most were library users we know very well, we did sign up 8 people for 
library cards. 
▪ Building & Maintenance 
The furniture purchased by the Friends for our Café has arrived and looks fantastic. On the whole, it 
brightens and opens up our café area. We are still tuning in the HVAC settings to get the temps in the 
range we want. 
▪ Technology 
Our Staff Color printer was replaced and now resides in the Children’s Room Office to make labels 
for cataloging. The new printer is fantastic, so far. 
CWMARS has suggested that all libraries with a fiber line work towards CIPA compliance. With the 
developments over the years with filtering software, this seems like a reasonable course to enable 
some significant discounts.

Summary of Last Month 
➢ The main highlight of October was a fantastic showing of support and engagement 
for our Library Building’s 20th Anniversary celebrations. Additionally, we had some 
great single programs to round out a successful month of events at the library! 
o On October 3rd we had a welcoming event for the month-long celebration of 
the library building being 20 years old. This event included remarks from 
committee members, Friends, and the Director, as well as a presentation by 
Bill Tyler of the historical society on the history of libraries in town. Donuts 
was donated by Hollis Hills Farm and cider was donated by Lanni Orchards. 
There were an estimated 50 people at this opening event. 
o Our monthly music concert was on October 9th by bluegrass duo Flynn 
Cohen and Laura Orshaw. This stop was part of their local tour for bluegrass 
concerts, and we drew an impressive 51 people for their show here. 
o Two authors of a true crime book based in Rockport came on October 22nd to 
discuss their book and sell copies. We had 8 very engaged attendees come 
for a cozy book talk that evening. 
o At the end of the month, we closed the anniversary celebrations with two 
events: a family-oriented “birthday party” for the library with a balloon 
twister, face painting and temporary tattoos; and a jazzukulele performance 
sponsored by the local community council. Both were major successes with 
a combined estimated 75 people at the library for the evening.
