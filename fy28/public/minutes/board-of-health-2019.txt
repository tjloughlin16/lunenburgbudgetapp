395 ELMWOOD ROAD 
 
Kim Kriesner, owner of the property met with the board.  Jack Maloney was also present.   
 
This is new construction. A septic system was designe d for this lot and the n the plan was amended, 
raising the system one foot.  The septic permit was issued in accordance with the revised plan. 
 
When the system was installed in was installed one foot lower than the approved design plan.  The 
approved septic permit was never picked up prior to the system being stalled.  The septic permit is still 
at the board of health office. 
 
Because this is new construction, the septic system must be fully compliant.  Chairman George Emond 
advised that board cannot give a variance to ground water offset on new construction.   
 
After a lengthy discussion, the board advised that this is an issue between the owner of the property 
and the installer of the septic system.  A variance cannot be given for the one foot difference in  
groundwater offset due to this being new construction.  The plan was designed and submitted in 
accordance with the rules and regulations.

stated that the second floor, at present, was unoccupied.  The remainder of the interior work was in 
process and she and her husband were working with the insurance company. 
 
Mrs. Paradis requested an extension in time to complete the repairs. 
 
===PAGE 2===

until February 28th to complete the required repairs.  All in favor. 
 
SEPTIC LOAN PROGRAM 
 
Discussion continued until February 18th.

120 Highland Street 
  
120 HIGHLAND STREET 
 
Jack Maloney met with the board on behalf of the owner.  This system is in failure.  There are no wells 
in the area and the property is served by public water. 
 
Although testing showed adequate pervious soil, the water table is high.  A Presby system is being 
proposed and allows for a gravity syst em.  This would further allow for the site to be graded in an 
manner to minimize potential runoff onto the abutting property. 
 
A groundwater variance from four feet to three feet is being requested. 
 
After discussion and on motion by Jewell and second by Mayhew, the board approved the variance.   
Roll call vote, all in favor. 
 
748 PAGE STREET 
 
Chris Knuth met with the board on behalf of the owner.  This matter was previously before the board.  
A Title 5 was completed in November of 2018.  Both the system and the well tested fine but the well 
was less than 50 feet from the septic system.  At that time, the well was tested and sanitized as part of 
the Title 5 inspection.  The board advised that e ither the well or the septic system would need to be 
replaced and the decision as to which was at the discretion of the owner. 
 
===PAGE 2===
Mr. Knuth is before the board with a design for a replacement septic system.  As part of the 
replacement, Mr. Knuth is asking th at the existing 1,500 gallon septic tank remain.  There is no 
information on file for a 1,500 gallon septic tank on the site although it appears to be fairly new.   
 
Mr. Knuth stated that it is reasonable to allow the tank to remain.  The existing tank is 42 feet from the 
exiting well. 
 
After discussion and on motion by Mayhew and second by Fortin, the board voted to allow the existing 
tank to remain on the following conditions: 
 
1- The installer of the replacement system certify that the existing tank is wate r tight and in good 
condition; 
2- The well be tested by Agent Jim Garreffi 
3- Well tested to ensure a sanitary seal by a certified well driller 
4- The property be deed restricted as a two (2) bedroom house. 
 
Roll call Vote:  Emond – yes, Jewell – yes, Passios – No, Fortin – yes, Mayhew – yes.  Variances 
approved. 
 
2020 BUDGET 
 
The 2020 Budget was available for review including an increase of 5% to the Nashoba Associated 
Boards of Health as well as in increase of 4 hours per week to the salary line item for the 
Administrative Assistant. 
 
On motion by Fortin and second by Jewell, the budget was approved, as Amended.  Roll call vote, all 
in favor. 
 
SEPTIC LOAN 
 
Several months ago, Chairman George Emond drafted a letter to Town Counsel with regard to the 
expanded use of the existing Septic Loan Repair Program money. 
 
After discussion, it was determined that no further action was necessary on this matter at this time. 
 
PROPOSED CANNABIS REGUALTIONS 
 
The members of the board where provided with the proposed Cannabis Regulations as draft ed by the 
Planning Board.  Administrative Assistant advised that the Planning Board is interested in hearing all 
comments and opinions with regard to these regulation from all residents of the town. 
 
As far as the Board is concerned, The Board of Health does not take any position or offer any opinions 
regarding the discussions surrounding Marijuana. 
 
Meeting adjourned at 8:30 pm

210 CHASE ROAD 
 
Summit Engineering met with the board of behalf of the owner.  This system has been in failure for a 
considerable amount of time.  This matter came before the previously, with a permit being issued.  The 
system was never installed.  This site has very high ground water, a small loping lot, high perc rate and 
onsite well. 
 
The previously designed system was very expe nsive to install and the applicant has re -designed the 
proposed system.  This newly designed system, a FAST system, includes a treatment septic tank to 
produce a cleaner effluent prior to discharge.  This will also reduce the footprint of the leaching area by 
50%. 
 
Variances being requested are less than 100 feet to a well and the use of a sieve analysis.  After 
discussion, and on motion by Jewell and second by Fortin, the board voted to approved the variances 
provided that a service contract for the FAST s ystem is in place prior to the COC being issued.  All in 
favor. 
  
211/219 W. TOWNSEND ROAD 
 
Mark Horrigan met with the board.  Mr. Horrigan owns two unimproved building lots with expired 
septic permits.  There has been no work begun on these lots.  Mr. Hor rigan is asking the board to 
extend these septic permits for an additional. 
 
After discussion, the board advised Mr. Horrigan that an extension is given when work has begun but 
cannot be finished in the required time.  Because work has not begun, extensions would not be given. 
===PAGE 2===
 
113 SANDY COVE ROAD 
 
The owner of the property, Mike Tully, met with the board.  A replacement system was designed for 
this property.  The house is connected to town water.  As part of the new system design, it was 
determined that there was also a well on the property.  The holding tank for the well in the basement of 
the house.  The owner would like to keep the well as an agricultural well. 
 
After discussion, the board advised that the well can remain provided it be disconnected from  the 
residence (proof provided to the board) and a deed restriction notification be placed on the property. 
 
9 WINTER Hill ROAD 
 
The owners of this property would like to finish space in the basement.  The house currently has a total 
of nine rooms (includi ng 4 bedrooms) and the septic system is designed for four bedrooms.  The 
proposed basement work would cause the house to exceed the capacity of the septic system, based on 
the total room count.  The homeowner can increase the size of the septic system, or place a deed 
restriction on the house. 
 
After discussion and on motion by Jewell and second by Mayhew  the board voted to allow a deed 
restriction of 4 bedrooms and a total of 10 rooms. 
 
NEW EVENTS APPLICATION 
 
The Select Board is in the process of creating  a new permitting process.  The proposed language has 
been provided to the board for comment. 
 
After discussion, the board is requiring that anyone applying for an Event Permit meet all Board of 
Health Regulations as all events are different and provide th eir proposal directly to the Board of 
Health. 
 
DARREN MASSEY EVENT APPLICATOINS 
 
Darren Massey has applied, through the Select Boards office, for permits to hold events at his property 
on Holman Street.  All events held in Town have to meeting Board of Health regulations. 
 
25 PEARL STREET 
 
The system at this property is in failure.  The homeowner has not responded to requests from the 
Board.  This matter will be marked up for hearing in Housing Court. 
 
Meeting adjourned at 8:30 pm

32 PEARL STREET 
 
Scott Kirouac, owner, met with the Board.  Mr. Kirouac is proposing an addition to his house at the 
above address.  Since there are no Board of Health records, it is the Board’s practice to establish the 
septic system capacity (number of bedrooms) based on the Assessor’s records.  The Assessor’s records 
indicate the house has six rooms and two bedrooms.  Based on the proposal submitted, Mr. Kirouac

69 KILBURN STREET 
 
Dawn Proctor met with the board on behalf of the owner.  This system is in failure.  The lot contains 
significant wetlands, is served by a private well as is the abutter.  Variances requested are offset to  
wetlands and offset to groundwater .  Granting these variances allows the proposed system to be 100 
feet from all wells.  George Emond stated that because the sewer line is 187 feet, he would like to see a 
cleanout added. 
 
After discussion and on Motion by  Emond and second by Mayhew, the variances were approved with 
the addition of a cleanout.  All in favor. 
 
134 YOUNGS ROAD 
 
Dawn Proctor met with the board of behalf of the owner.  This system is in failure.  The lots in the area 
are small.  Testing was completed and there is no area that would allow a 100 foot offset from the 
===PAGE 2===
existing wells (both abutter and owner).  Variances requested are distance to well, 100 feet to 82 feet 
and distance to slab, 10 feet to 3.8 feet. 
 
After discussion and on Motion by Em ond and second by Fortin, the variances were approved.  All in 
favor. 
 
147 COVE ROAD 
 
Doug Smith met with the board on behalf of the owner.  This system is in failure.  The site is served by 
Town water.  The lot is small and has high ground water.  Varianc es requested are reduction in 
groundwater from four feet to three feet and distance to cellar wall from 20 feet to 7 feet. 
 
After discussion and on Motion by Mayhew and second by Fortin, the variances were approved. 
 
90 HEMLOCK DRIVE 
 
Doug Smith met with the board on behalf of the owners.  This system is in failure, the house is served 
by Town water. The lot is on a peninsula and the shape of the lot makes is difficult to design a fully 
compliant system.  Variances requested are offset to wetlands from 100 feet to 70 feet and offset from 
tank to cellar wall 10 feet to 5 feet. 
 
After discussion and on motion by Fortin and second by Mayhew, the variances were approved. 
 
322 FLAT HILL ROAD 
 
John Boardman, Engineer, met with the board on behalf of the owner.  Th e owner is requesting a well 
variance from 50 feet to 14 feet.  The owner has a large parcel of land and is proposing dividing it into 
three lots.  One will contain the existing house and the two remaining lots will be to the rear of the 
property off of a common driveway.  The lot which has the original house on it is served by a private 
well.  Once subdivided, this well does not meet the offset requirement from the newly created common 
drive. 
 
George Emond stated that he does not believe that this is a var iance that the board can grant.  The 
owner has created this situation by subdividing the property and believes that a new well will have to 
be drilled. 
 
After discussion, it was decided that this matter would be continued until January 6, 2020 at 7 pm. 
 
64 ARBOR STREET 
 
Matthew Woodrow (Woody) was present at the meeting together with Ryan Stober.   
 
Chairman Perry Jewell stated that the Court had dismissed the case without prejudice meaning that the 
board could refile and seek relief.  Chairman Jewell state d however, that was not the intention of the 
board, but rather , the board is requesting that the septic tank be replaced in accordance with the 
conditional Title 5 report issued in October of 2018.

hour.  He also feels that the board has not treated him fairly and used faulty information to bring him to 
Court.  He feels that he has been persecuted and demeaned by the Board. 
 
Chairman Jewell stated that the Board may have made errors and, at this point, would just like the tank 
replaced. 
 
After discussion, it was agreed that Mr. Woodrow would replace the tank.  It was agreed that he could 
have one year, or until January of 2021 for the tank replacement.   Replacement must be by a licensed 
installer certified by that installer. 
 
Motion by Fortin, second by Mayhew.  All in favor.
