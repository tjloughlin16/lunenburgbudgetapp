b.
855 Massachusetts Avenue: NOI-26-5
Notice of Intent by the Town of Lunenburg for the management of vegetation at Marshall Pond
Matt Ladewig (TRC) and Christopher Ruth (Town) represented the project. The Town asked TRC to map the
pond and the vegetation overgrowth. Last October, they found little open water in the pond, mostly bio volume.
Photos showed fan wort and whitewater lilies. The overabundance of plant growth is impacting fish and wildlife
habitats.
TRC recommends addressing the fan wort as it is a non-native species and then thinning the white-water lily.
They recommend a management program that includes physical and chemical controls. Benthic barriers are
negatively buoyant and are placed on the bottom of the pond. They are good for seasonal use areas where users
enter the pond (for example, launching kayaks). Hand harvesting is included in the long-term plan but is not
practical right now. It could also be used if new non-native species were introduced. The plan lists hydra raking,
but they are not proposing to dig into the bottom of the pond. Consider it mechanized removal of biomass.
Two chemical control measures are included: systemic and contact herbicides to manage the plants (mostly fan
wort) and algaecides to control algae blooms in the future.
The request is for a 5-year Order of Conditions with annual monitoring.
M. Ladewig confirmed that a public notice would be placed, and the few abutters would be notified prior to an
application. While there are no label restrictions, it is better if the water is not moving around. The fluoridone
level is so low that any downstream impact is minimal and would have no impact on aquatic life. To maintain the
required level for the entire period, both a liquid and granular version are used, as are booster treatments. The
plants will die off slowly and as they do, the fish population should increase. Once dispersed on the water, you
can swim safely without irritation.
T. Doucette asked if the plastic benthic barriers contain any PFAS. M. Ludwig did not know for certain but
mentioned that many of the barriers use geotextile fabric and noted they are only installed for a few months. Like
===PAGE 3===
a weed barrier, they kill all the plants underneath them. They are then removed and stored for reuse. M. Wilson
noted that there would need to be a condition that they are not stored in the 30-foot or 50-foot zones.
