ANR PLAN – 1325 Massachusetts Avenue – No changes to previously endorsed Site Plan Approval. Permitted Contractor 
storage facility and two office uses . Intention of ANR Plan is for creating separate lot for one of the office uses . Both lots have 
adequate frontage, access and drainage easements. Historical Commission waived demolition delay for existing building intending 
to be demolished. M. Allison made a Motion to approve ANR Plan. Seconded by A. Reid, all aye.  
 
PUBLIC HEARING – 525 Chase Road  - See Separate Minutes. M. Allison made motion to approve modifications to Site 
Plan Approval and Stormwater Permit, A. Reid Seconded, passed unanimously.  
 
RIGHT OF FIRST REFUSAL - Parcel 4, Leominster Rd. – Continued  
Classified r ecreational land (M.G.L. 61B) intended to convert to Resi dential use. Discussed value to Town is minimal from 
perspective of Planning Department. Awaiting Open Space Committee’s recommendation though time for Decision has become 
limited. M. Allison made Motion to recommend not pursuing Right of First Refusal for Parcel 4 . Seconded by A. Reid, all 
aye. 
 
DECISIONS – Lot A 524 Northfield Road 
No discussion, decision approved and signed by Board. 
 
BOARD DISCUSSIONS: 
Right of First Refusal: Discussed process driven by the state and local Towns/Cities capabilities to have control over the process 
in order to meet State requirements.  
Master Plan – Consultants are in process of finalizing Public Engageme nt Plan. Website to direct the public for information that 
will be accessible as well as plan to engage with the public in -person. Consultants will build a p hysical modal of the Town . M. 
Brenner will be involved in bi-weekly Master Plan meetings. Document for the Board will be at next meeting to look over Plan.  
Solar Bylaw- Discussed parking lot solar and is not something that is doable in Lunenburg right now. Inflation Reduction Act has 
funding for Green Technology per EDC Meeting and is something to start looking into to prepare for Fall Town Meeting. Possibility 
of making parking lot solar an accessory use within commercial and industrial zones. Suggested to be a Special Permit within 
those zones, which would allow businesses in those zones to build large scale solar.  
Section 250 -6.4., Driveway & Entrances - A. Burney sent draft to Board members , Fire Chief  and Building commissioner. 
Discussed drafts that were distributed. Discussed editing max grade  and max length  to be the same between residential and 
commercial driveways. Discussed common driveways with reduced frontage lots as well as turnouts on/adjacent to shared 
driveways.

BOARD DISCUSSIONS: 
Master Plan Steering Committee : Discussed the upcoming meeting scheduled for Monday September 18, 2023 at 6:00 PM. It 
aims to set expectations, organize the committee, and introduce the lead consultant. It was noted that the Select Board has not 
yet chosen a representative for the committee. 
 
Solar Bylaw: Discussed a draft of the Solar Bylaw, specifically addressing large-scale ground-mounted solar installations. An 
amendment was proposed to exclude parking lot canopies from being counted as large-scale ground-mounted solar installations. 
This change aims to accommodate federal funding requirements for parking lot canopies. Concerns were raised about the 
impact of the bylaw on different types of solar installations. It was noted that state and federal incentives have shifted towards 
brownfield redevelopment sites, rooftops, and away from deforestation-based installations. Rooftop installations are encouraged, 
and many buildings will be required to be "solar ready" under the stretch energy code. Board will consider revising the Solar 
Bylaw to base regulations on square footage rather than kilowatts, addressing the changing technology landscape.  
T. Willsmer made Motion to prepare a warrant article for the proposed changes to the Solar Bylaw, includ ing the 
addition of regulations for solar canopies. Seconded by A. Reid, all aye. 
 
Section 250-6.4., Driveways & Entrances: There was a discussion about the maximum number of lots allowed for residential and 
commercial properties in common driveways. The question was raised about whether it should be four or six for residential  
properties and whether increasing the limit would lead to more dense development. The consensus was to maintain a maximum 
of four for residential lots to limit the intensity of development while allowing for some flexibility.  It was mentioned that the 
common driveway must provide access to multiple lots or structures. The following discussions were noted: 
 
Number of Lots Allowed: There was a debate about how many lots should be allowed to share a common driveway. 
Some argued for four lots, while others considered three or five lots. Noted that Director had a proposal for six lots in a 
commercial context.

Length of the Common Driveway: Board raised the issue of specifying different lengths for common driveways based 
on whether there's town water available. It was suggested that six lots with town water would have a longer driveway 
(1800 feet) than four lots without town water (1200 feet). 
 
Safety and Road Standards: Safety concerns were brought up, especially regarding visibility, blind corners, and the 
width of common driveways. There were discussions about whether shared driveways should meet c ertain road 
standards to ensure safety. 
 
Access and Frontage Requirements: Clarified that each lot sharing a common driveway should have the required 
amount of frontage on the road to which the common driveway connects. This helps ensure that il lusory frontage isn't 
used to bypass the regulations. 
 
Retroactive Application: It was confirmed that these regulations wouldn't be retroactively applied to existing properties 
but would only affect new developments. 
 
Commercial and Residential Use: It was clarified that regulations applies to both commercial and residential, and any 
commercial development would still require additional permits. 
 
Adjacent Driveway Use: The discussion touched on the possibility of adjacent properties using an exist ing driveway as 
a shared driveway. It was noted that this could be addressed through the special permit process.  
 
Aquifer Protection Bylaw: M. Brenner expressed concerns about the lack of controls regarding design features of projects. A. 
Burney clarified that this bylaw amendment is mostly driven by the Department of Environmental Protection (DEP) and that 
additional controls can be added later if needed. M. Allison raised concerns about the enforcement of the bylaw, and A. Burney 
pointed out that enforcement is covered under section 8.2 of the zoning bylaw.
