Minutes, 2/26/24 
Greg Roy – Public comment – asked about ROFR process. M. Allison responded in starting the process with the town 
and Open Space Committee.   
317-321 Sunnyhill – stormwater permit- public hearing 6:10pm
Greg Roy of Dillis and Roy representing Tim Battles. At the last public hearing we received peer review from GEI. Continued to 
tonight’s meeting to give time for ZBA to respond. Did give variance for driveway grade. Feb 22 nd issued letter from GEI. 
Response to comments – revised plans to include the name and phone numbers of applicants. Soil testing has been performed 
and included in plans. Site zoning information is included in the revised plans. Revised calculations for stormwater void trench. 
No additional revisions were required or noted from peer review. Recharge trench was modeled at 100% - recalculated to be 
modeled at 40%. M. Allison asked about maintenance requirements for stormwater rain gardens. It is included in the plans. 
Greg Roy is requesting a temporary conditional certificate of occupancy. The building commissioner is looking for Planning 
Board input.
2022 building permit was granted for work to begin on the property of 321 Sunnyhill. Last fall the applicant requested a certificate 
of occupancy. Property was flagged due to more than an acre of disturbance. Both lots were then cleared – greater than an acre 
of disturbance with no stormwater permit issued.
Tim Battles explained he has a contractor – for both lots it is to be $49,000. Per Richard Harris, to issue Performance Guarantee 
to ensure the work is completed and acknowledgement of the recipient of the property to precludes any issues once property is 
transferred.
Greg Roy supplemented request with the following language “Prior to issuance of the final Certificate of Occupancy, the work 
stormwater system shall be constructed, and an as-built plan prepared by a professional engineer be submitted to the planning 
department and building commissioner documenting that the work was constructed in accordance with the approved plans and 
conditions of approval. Until this work is completed, the planning board is amenable to allowing for a temporary certificate of 
occupancy provided that the work is completed within 120 days of this order being issued. “
Hearing closed 6:47PM
M. Brenner mentioned letter from Graves is met to satisfaction. Conditions considered accordance with approved plans, 
completed plans within timeframe, stabilization of structures. Discussed standard residential construction hours.

255 Sunnyhill - Continued public hearing   
Opened 6:56PM  
McCarty Engineering – John Plefka. Scheduled site walk with conservation commission this Wednesday. Requested 
continuation upon completion of site walk with conservation commission.   
Dave Rogers – 82 Highland Street – public comment – this is the 3rd or 4th continuation for this project. Are there any other 
hurdles to move project along other than waiting for this project.   
Response was engineers were waiting on issued DEP number for NOI and awaiting conservation site walk.   
Mr. Rogers continued – “the area has a lot of wetlands, and it would be a travesty to have a house built in an area of wetlands. I 
do not think the building of this site makes sense and hope the Board hears and acknowledges comments.”   
Mr. Harris made a note to have Mr. Rogers express concerns about Conservation regarding concerns for wetlands and 
conservation of land.   
Ken Matson – public comment – Leominster Rd. Driveway is at an elevation 2ft below his driveway. A lot of water comes off the 
hill and runs into his yard and is concerned about water flowing onto his property. Ken is asking if he can “take it to the bank” of 
that McCarty engineering that driveway will in fact be built 2ft below. M. Allison said plans need to be in conformation and 
compliance with plans submitted and approved to the planning board. Plan shows a 2ft grade at least.   
Continuance is being requested until the next Planning Board meeting March 11th. No major changes and just waiting on 
conservation site walk.   
M Allison made motion to continue to March 11th. A. Reid seconded. 7:25PM  
  
Certificate of completion for 147 & 159 Holman – GEI reviewed property and plantings in basin need to be planted and stone 
trench drain has minor sedimentation that needs to be removed. M. Brenner asked for clarification and why plans are not 
complete. Per Mr. Harris plantings for stormwater drains and sedimentation in drainage trench that need to be removed means 
requirements are not met yet and will need additional reviews. A. Reid did not feel comfortable issuing certificate of completion 
based off GEI review. Per M. Allison if sedimentation exists in stone trench it basically has failed. M. Allison moved to not yet 
release certificate of completion until comments from GEI review are resolved. T. Willsmer seconded.   
M. Allison made Motion to not release performance guarantee until comments are resolved. T. Willsmer Seconded.   
  
Board Discussions:   
Solar Energy Systems: M. Brenner is not comfortable putting language together to present as a warrant. Green Communities is 
interested in giving input, but not anticipating being able to put anything together to present at the Annual Town Meeting. Add-on 
of Battery Energy Storage systems is important in including in language of bylaw.    
By next Monday Richard Harris will provide a draft of the battery energy systems bylaw. It will help regulate storage systems; 
solar operating systems have a battery storage system in place as accessory use. The fire chief and police chief have helped 
implement a site safety plan to include as a precursor to include prior to approval. A preliminary level of detailed review will be 
included in the draft bylaw.   
Current bylaw includes threshold of 10kw between small and large and most other towns have that as a threshold of 10kw 
between small and medium. It is in question to include in water supply protection district due to possible risk of leeching into the 
ground or contamination due to batteries in ground. Discussed Site Safety Plan requirement which addresses precautionary 
measures to be taken by developers and homeowners in compliance with bylaw developed by fire department in handling safety 
measures in the case of disaster to reduce risk of fire, contamination or other disaster. A bylaw would address any battery 
storage unit based on size and/or storage capacity.   
M. Allison recommended using language from Marijuana bylaw.   
Scenic Roads – M. Allison is almost ready to submit draft to Board  
Water Supply Protection District - Mr. Harris submitted a line-by-line comparison of the water supply protection district for the 
board to review.   
M. Allison has concerns about current bylaw does not match M.G.L. and that special permits are required, and the planning 
board has not issued special permits for the water supply protection district.   
DEP map has Zone 1 and zone 2 overlapping – if map has zone 1 and zone 2 in same area, it counts as zone 1 and is controlled 
by the state not by the municipality.   
The Planning Board will have discussion about drafts at the next planning meeting March 11th prior to public hearing for warrant 
articles.   
The Green Committee will have a possible joint meeting on March 6th with the Planning Meeting to discuss solar bylaw.

intent to sell. M. Brenner summarized the purpose of ANRs for the benefit of the public and new 
board members: endorsement that re-drawn lot lines meet a small list of specific requirements, i.e. 
frontage and accessibility from the road. He specifically noted that there are certain questions they 
ask applicants (which the Planning Board doesn’t directly consider) to help applicants avoid backing 
themselves into undesirable situations (i.e. being ineligible for connection to septic).

Outdoor Dining Policy 
The board took several minutes to review the context and content of their documents. T. Willsmer 
summarized that the state was putting the Alcoholic Beverages Control Commision’s Covid-era 
practices into official long-term practice. R. Harris explained that the Select Board asked for 
departmental comments while making the corresponding policies. He surmised that the Planning 
Board's involvement would be limited to applications involving outdoor dining making sure there aren't 
elements which preclude outdoor dining. 
 
M. Allison noted that there would be some specific impacts with outdoor dining as opposed to brick-
and-mortar (i.e. pedestrian safety, parking, etc.) R. Harris brought up an example of a brick-and-
mortar dining establishment which later sought to add an outdoor patio, exposing them to the 
additional considerations that that entailed. 
 
M. Brenner asked whether the Planning Board wants to be included in the list of departments that 
would be reviewing outdoor dining with alcohol, such as in OpenGov. The board briefly compared 
different orderings of the hypothetical process an applicant for a liquor license looking for outdoor 
dining would take. M. Brenner approved of the idea of the board being a part of the review list, and 
emphasized the value of including explicit language about whether Site Plan Review or Special 
Permit may be required. 
 
R. Harris said the Select Board should build these considerations into the Outdoor Dining Policy so 
that applicants would know which boards they would need approval or communication with beyond 
the Select Board. He briefly specified the language to be used in the Planning Board’s feedback to 
the Select Board, emphasizing the importance of directness of requests and clarity of instructions to 
applicants. 
 
M. Allison asked if the opaqueness of the application process was a factor in businesses leaving the 
town, and disapproved of ambiguous language (i.e. “may require” as opposed to “requires”). M. 
Brenner asked about how Change of Use is defined and whether its scope is entirely contained to the 
“Use Table” (Town Code § 250-4.1G). R. Harris replied alteration of an approved Site Plan or Special 
Permit requires modification of the Site Plan or Special Permit. 
 
M. Allison clarified that since the Zoning Enforcement Officer (who is the Building Commissioner in 
Lunenburg) ultimately decides on Change of Use, the fact that he is also the temporary Land Use 
Director mildly simplifies the number of different places an applicant needs to go for approval. M. 
Brenner then explained the consolidation benefits of virtual applications submitted through OpenGov, 
which is currently being built out. R. Harris and L. O’Connell agreed. 
 
M. Brenner said that although he was comfortable relying on the current Building Commissioner for

Water Supply Protection District 
M. Brenner noted that the draft document of the proposed WSPD amendments included recent 
changes. R. Harris said that the document was a revised draft of the one examined at the 2024-04-22 
Public Hearing. He described the timeline of its revisions, mentioning back-and-forth communication 
with Joan Pease and Bill Hannigan and their affirmation of the changes as-written. 
 
R. Harris reiterated a previously suggested timeline for the Board: Discussion on the July 8th or 
August 26th meeting where all members could attend and comment, followed by a Public Hearing in 
September if the board was comfortable. He also noted that the Water District wants to be involved in 
future discussions about the Water Supply Protection District. 
 
M. Allison informed M. Brenner that a member of the Water District was present, and M. Brenner 
invited him to speak. Mark Bursch (Water Commissioner) said that the Water District hadn’t seen 
any of the proposed changes and requested a copy for review. He also claimed that the changes 
needed to go back to DEP for approval. He mentioned that the District isn’t worried about the 
document’s current wording in Zone 1, but expected issues with the changes of treatment in Zone 1 
and treatment by Special Permit in Zone 2 since the District is already engaged in this. 
 
R. Harris clarified that ”existing is allowed to continue”. M. Bursch asked Mr. Harris to clarify the 
statement, interpreting it as the DEP allowing for treatments in Zone 1 and 2 but the town restricting 
further treatment in those Zones. R. Harris offered to send M. Bursch a copy of the document to make 
his own judgment. He then noted that communities have the right to impose restrictions beyond those 
of the DEP model. M. Bursch acknowledged this, but reiterated that further restrictions would have 
significant financial impact to the Water District and its water takers. R. Harris replied that it would be 
up to the Board and the Town Meeting. 
 
M. Brenner noted upcoming discussions of the Water District Amendments on July 8th and August 
26th, and said that more voices are better. M. Bursch pointed out that the Water District hadn’t been 
invited to these meetings and wanted more proactive invitation from the Town, which M. Brenner 
affirmed while acknowledging staffing issues with the Planning Department. M. Bursch signaled the 
Water District’s intention to get a review of the bylaw from the DEP. M. Brenner acknowledged the 
request and reiterated the non-technical background of the Planning Board’s members. 
 
M. Brenner asked the Board to review the material in-depth prior to the July 8th meeting.
