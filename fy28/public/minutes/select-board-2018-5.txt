Section 1.05 Duties & Responsibilities - Standards of Conduct
A member of the Board, in relation to his/her responsibility to the community, shall recognize that his/her primary role is
to set policy, with responsibility for administration delegated to the Town Manager;
• recognize that he/she is a member of a team, and shall abide by all Board decisions once they are made;
• be well informed concerning the duties of a Board member on both state and local levels;
• remember that he/she represents the entire community at all times;
• accept the Office of the Selectmen as a means of unselfish public service, not to benefit personally or
professionally from his/her Board activities;
• in all appointments, avoid political patronage by judging all candidates on merit, experience and qualifications
only; and
• abide by the provisions established by the Commonwealth in M.G.L.,c. 28A as they apply to municipal officials.
As a member of the Board, a Selectman shall:
• endeavor to establish sound, clearly defined policies which will direct and support the administration for the
benefit of the people of the Town;
• recognize and support the administrative chain of command and refuse to act on complaints as an individual
outside the administrations;
• give the lown Manager full responsibility for discharging his/her decisions and solutions;
• not make statements or promises of how he/she will vote on matters that will come before the Board until he/she
has had an opportunity to hear the pros and cons of the issue at a Board meeting;
• make decisions only after all facts on a question have been presented or discussed;
• uphold the intent of executive session and respect the privileged communication that exists therein and;
• treat with respect the rights of all members of the Board despite differences of opinion.

While employees maintain and use personal web pages and websites, blogs, microblogs, social networking sites
and other forms of social media while off-duty, their status as employees of the Town requires that the content
of any postings on those social media sites or other web pages not be in violation of existing Town by-laws,
policies, directives, rules or regulations. The Town's image as a professional organization comprised of
professional employees is critical to maintaining the respect of its constituents. Although the Town recognizes
that employees may choose to express themselves by posting personal information upon electronic media sites
through personal websites, social networking sites, blogs, microblogs, chat rooms, or other electronic means or
by making comments upon electronic sites hosted by other persons, groups or organizations, this right of
expression is not free from limitation. That is, while the Town acknowledges its employees have the First
Amendment right to free speech, that right is not absolute and extends only to matters of public concern. The
term "public concern" is a legal one, but generally refers to matters of concern to the community rather than
the individual. Employees must exercise caution with respect to comments they post in general, and in
particular those concerning the Town, a particular department of the Town, and/or the Town's employees.

We will wait to see if these amendments make it into the filed Economic Development Bond Bill. We

our behalf.

the disposal of the property at 17 & 23 West Street, which are two adjacent lots on West Street that have

out to bid for disposal.
Green Communities Competitive Grant:
As announced at last week's meeting, the Green Communities Competitive Grant application
that the Town submitted this past spring was awarded in the amount of $198,867 for a variety of
energy efficiency projects at the Public Safety Building, the Town Hall, the Ritter and Turkey
Hill Elementary School. These include LED Lighting upgrades, plug load controls, condensing
boilers, building envelope tightening, pipe and fitting insulation and HVAC retro
commissioning. The total estimated annual electricity savings is 186,000 kWh, 2,241 therms for
annual natural gas savings and 703 gallons of oil per year which translates into approximately
$39,000 in energy savings. Once we receive the fully executed contract documents back from
the Department of Energy Resources, which we mailed today, and meet our utility
representatives regarding the energy incentives, we can proceed with having the approved
energy consultant Energy Source move forward with projects. The goal is complete the projects
by this fall.

positions within the existing classification schedule for the ensuing three years, which is one of the
responsibilities set out in the Salary Administration Bylaw. The last review provided an updated wage
and classification schedule through Fiscal Year 2019 and the goal was that the impending review would
be presented at the May 2019 Annual Town Meeting for acceptance. Due to the comprehensive nature
of performing a compensation and classification review, the Personnel Committee is recommending to
hire a third party consultant to perform the review. We will be soliciting quotes and would need to
request an appropriation at the Special Town Meeting to fund this study. This will include performing a
market analysis of towns with similar demographics with positions with the same level of
responsibilities.

PUBLIC COMMENT:
Ms. Adams reported she attended a Montachusett Regional Planning Commission (MRPC) Americans
Disability Act (ADA) Transitions Plan meeting and they are encouraging towns to look at three criteria
on how to be more ADA compliant in open and recreation spaces, public rights of way and buildings.
Speaking as a citizen and Selectman, she has inquired about holding these meetings in a facility that is
more ADA compliant like the collaborative classrooms at the Middle/High school. She encouraged the
Board to not only look at long-term solutions but to use what we currently have at no additional cost.
Ms. Adams referred to Article 22 of the May 5, 2018 Town Meeting warrant, regarding the conveyance
of real property. She requested to discuss that topic going forward in open session as she saw no
detrimental effect doing so.
Ms. Luck announced the Lion's Club Pancake breakfast is tomorrow morning at the Eagle House Senior
Center from 8:00 - 10:30 a.m. The cost is $2.00 per person.
Ms. Luck attended the grand opening of the Lunenburg Pollinator Habitat located at 123 Hollis Road on
Sunday morning. There are labeled plants that attract pollinators along the path. There is an attractive
butterfly sign on the site and she encouraged everyone to take a walk there.
Judit Ernst, 568 Flat Hill Road, spoke about banning plastic shopping bags. She submitted a citizen's
petition for a Special Town Meeting warrant article addressing the ban.
Marilyn Talley, 172 Peninsula Drive and Carolyn Finch, School Nurse, spoke about the Stillman
Country Fair on September 8 and offered a table to the Selectmen for volunteer solicitation for town
committees. This year funds from the fair will partially fund a new initiative, William James College
Interface Referral Services, through the Wellness Advisory Committee for the schools. It is a referral
service for mental health services. This is a service we can purchase for the entire town not just the
school district. Based on information provided by the caller, it matches them to a mental health service
provider from their database, usually within two weeks.
