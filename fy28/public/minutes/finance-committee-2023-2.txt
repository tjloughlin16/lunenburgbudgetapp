 Ms. Lemieux said that since the June 13 they have had some invitations for bids that came in. 
Guardrail repair services were awarded to Premier Fence LLC in the amount of $80,886 and that will be 
paid out of the Mass DOT Winter Recovery Assistance program funds. Invitation for bids for Page 
Street Culvert and the town received a bid from Powell Construction in the amount of $86,913 to be

week and the Assistant Town Manager is evaluating that as well as working on the IFB for the generator 
project at the Adult Activity Center and the two dump trucks which are being put out to BID since the 
town hasn’t had any luck with the State bid contract.

Update on the tax assessment  - Municipal Finance Services has fulfilled their contract and the total was 
$82,400 and that was all paid out of ARPS funding that the select board allocated. The total abatements 
issued from the assessor side is $1,351,147 and that does not include the tax collector issuing interest.

October/November time Fram  but she believes the town has a really competitive application. 
  
6. New Business  
Investment options for better returns - Mr. Watters spoke with Ms. Brochu via email  briefly 
and she was going to send over a report of all the funds they currently have and that this may be a 
conversation to be had with the treasurer. Mr. Watters said he would invite the treasurer to a future 
meeting. 
Mr. Gray said he didn’t now if there were regulations on how much risk we can take but would like to 
know their limitations. Mr. Gray also said interest rates mater and looking at cash flow and what cash 
flow looks like for next year needs to be known. 
Mr. Balish said to build on Mr. Gray’s point that in a federal money market fund today the yields are

July 27, 2023 
Members Present: School Committee Member Brian Lehtinen, Town Manager Heather Lemieux, 
Vice Chair Chris Menard, Board Member Jean Russell, Board Member Jay Simeone, Chair Evan

1. Call to order 
The meeting opened at 7:00 PM. 
2. Pledge of Allegiance 
3. Announcements 
The Finance Committee has a new member, Jean Russell. Evan Watters reiterates 
that there are still open positions available to join the committee. 
4. Public comment 
No public comment. 
5. Town manager/Finance report 
Heather Lemieux discusses current bid openings. The need for guardrail repair 
services bid of 80,886 dollars was awarded to Premier Fence. Funding from the 
Mass DOT Winter Recovery Assistance Program was used for this project. A bid 
from Powell Construction in the amount of 86,913 dollars was received for the 
Page Street culvert. A proposal from Vision regarding the 5-year reassessing 
evaluation was received with an estimated cost of 112,000 dollars. The Roadway 
Maintenance Fog Ceiling bid is due this coming week. The bids for snowplow, 
sewer operations, and maintenance bids are currently out and due by August 7. 
The road materials for DPW are being put out to bid soon. 
The EPA for administrative paperwork and removal of materials and tanks are 
currently being worked on for the 925 Mass Ave project. An application for a 
grant through Mass Development for Real Estate Technical Assistance Program 
has been submitted and a response is expected by the fall. 
The Senior Center Patio Project is still on schedule with an anticipated 
completion date in September. 
The ADA Ramp drawing has been completed by the architect. This will be sent 
out to the APD for review. 
Fiber is being installed at the Town Hall by Comcast. There is an order for new 
phone systems for the Town Hall, Ritter building, Library, Lunenburg Adult 
Activity Center, and DPW. This transition will take place over the coming months. 
The CivicPlus website's anticipated live date is in September and training will be 
completed prior to the launch.  
6. New business 
a. Committee reorganization

The committee is currently operating without a clerk and there have been no 
applicants for administrative positions. Evan Watters wants to wait until the next 
meeting when additional members will be available to discuss reorganization.  
b. Liaison reorganization 
Committee members act as a liaison between the Finance Committee and other 
committees. Jean Russell agrees to take on the Facilities Committee. The group 
agrees to discuss this at the next meeting so Evan Watters can bring a list of 
liaisons and additional members will be present. 
c. Past meeting minutes plan 5/25, 6/8, 6/22 
There is no clerk for this committee, so the minutes have not been recorded for 
the above-referenced dates. Karen Menard from the Parks Commission agrees to 
help and complete the meeting minutes. 
7. Old business 
a. Tax assessment/abatement update 
The town manager was not currently on the Zoom meeting so there are 
no updates.  
The last update Evan Watters is aware of regarding the tax bills is that 
they were going to be sent out.  Chris Menard believes that checks were 
being mailed out. 
8. Committee Reports 
Chris Menard states that the Parks Commission submitted a 500,000 dollar grant 
for Marshall Park.  
Brian Lehtinen from the School Committee has no update as they have not had a 
meeting since the prior meeting. 
Evan Watters states that none of the committees he oversees has met since the 
initial kick-off meeting.  
9. Topics for Future Meetings 
For future meetings, there will be a look back at the committee policies. 
10. Public comment 
No public comment.

Passios roof repair. Chris Menard questions if there are any fiscal policies related

and thinks that the Select Board and School Committee should meet. Jay 
Simeone believes the scope of work falls under Chapter 30B. Heather Lemieux

Lunenburg Finance Committee Meeting Minutes 
November 13, 2023 
 
Members Present: Board Member Tom Gray, Board Member Dave Passios, Board Member Jean 
Russell, Board Member Jay Simeone, Chair Evan Watters. 
 
1. Call to Order 
The meeting opened at 7:03 PM.   
 
2. Announcements 
Evan Watters announces Dave Passios has joined the Finance Committee. The board now 
consists of 6 members.  
 
3. Public Comment 
No public Comment. 
 
4. Town Manager Report 
Heather Lemieux does not have any additional reports but wants to review some of the 
articles with the Committee. 
 
5. Old Business 
a. Deferred STM Warrant Articles Updates 
Article 1 
Article 1 addresses budget adjustments for FY 24. Heather Lemieux refers to a 
previously sent out chart that reflects the changes in revenue projection.  
- There is an additional $200,000 difference from the annual town meeting 
estimate.  
- The estimated state aid presented at the annual town meeting was $119,000 
lower than projected since the annual town meeting.  
- The balance levy capacity of $9,772.70 will go towards the overall additional 
allowable appropriation.  
-  A salary survey was done at the request of the select board chair. The board 
all agreed to add an additional $20,000. This is based on the area comparable 
to other towns. 
Motion to approve Article 1 by Tom Gray, seconded by Jay Simeone. Roll call 
vote. Unanimous vote.   
      Article 2 
A total of $1,413.52 was meant to be carried over at the end of the fiscal year, 
but ultimately it wasn’t. These wages were supposed to be paid out FY 23. This 
money came from excess revenue. 
Motion to approve Article 2 by Tom Gray, seconded by Dave Passios. Roll call 
vote. All in favor. Unanimous vote. 
      Article 3 
===PAGE 2===
This article addresses collective bargaining that was finalized by the sick board 
and the bargaining unit. Comp analysis was completed by every bargaining unit 
to make sure employees were being paid fairly and by agreement it was found 
based on the 8 communities that were surveyed that the positions within the 
bargaining unit were severely underpaid compared to comparable positions and 
comparable towns between 8 and 20 percent. The bargaining agreement has a 
requirement to fund the first year. 
The board inquired about what employees this would cover. Heather Lemieux 
states it would cover Assistant Town Clerk, Town Accountant, Assistant Treasurer, 
Assistance Collector, DPW Executive Assistant, public Safety Coordinator, 
Conservation Administrator, Administrative Assistance. This would be an 
unknown cost but would be within budget. 
Motion to recommend approval for Article 3 by Tom Grey, seconded by Jean Russell. 
Roll call vote. Unanimous vote. 
b. Deferred STM Warrant Article Votes 
There are no additional deferments to address.

their voting clickers.

No public comment.

Motion to adjourn by Jay Simeone, seconded by Tom Gray. Roll call vote.

Lunenburg Finance Committee Meeting Minutes
November 30, 2023 

Members Present: Member Tom Gray, Vice Chair Chris Menard, Board Member Dave Passios, Board Member Jay Simeone, Chair Evan Watters.

1. Call to Order
The meeting opened at 7:05 PM.

2. Pledge of Allegiance

3. Announcements
a. Plan Lunenburg Survey
The Master Plan Steering Committee survey is available, and residents are encouraged to participate.

4. Public Comment
There was no public or committee comment. 

5. Town Manager Report
There are currently 2 projects out to bid.
Lunenburg activity center generator project bids are due December 13.
Public Safety Building carpet project bids are due December 20.
There is an update regarding the third quarter preliminary building and pro forma tax recap. Heather Lemieux confirms that Karen Brochu has completed the pro forma tax recap. This step must be completed since the town won’t be setting a tax rate until after the certification process. It is essentially the same as the tax rate recap except there is no valuation information just tax levy information. 

Heather Lemieux has worked with multiple departments and the DOR to create a tax bill insert. This insert will give information about why they are receiving a third quarter preliminary bill and confirms that this quarter bill will be the same amount as their first and second quarter preliminary bills. Once evaluations are completed for the certification process and tax rates are set residents will only get one actual tax bill this year for the 4th quarter. 

Dave Passios questions if there is anything that can be done for people who are having trouble paying the third quarter and then the fourth quarter by the deadline. Heather Lemieux states that the deadline cannot be extended. The third quarter estimated bills will go out at the end of December and they are due February 1st. Any abatement applications deadlines will be extended to May 1st.

Evan Watters asks when the budget calendar will be provided. Heather Lemieux confirms that one is available on the drive. 

6. Old Business
a. STM wrap-up
Dave Passios shares that he felt the votes went as expected. Evan Watters states he was surprised by the personal attacks but also stated everything seems to be open and transparent. 

7. New Business
a. Capital Plan Committee update – Chris Menard
Chris Menard states that all department presentations for the fiscal year 2025 have been completed. Additionally, each department’s 10-year capital requests were provided.  The upcoming meetings will be focused on the FY 25 requests and ranking priorities. The 10-year capital requests will be looked at in the first part of the next calendar year.
Below are the 2 changes to the FY 25 request:
There was a change regarding the request for Marshall Pond treatment. The updated request will be significantly less money as the work being done will only be on a small section of the pond. It will still include raking and chemical treatment.  
The parks department has escalated the basketball and tennis court repair at Wallace Park to a higher priority request. This is the most utilized facility in town and is showing wear. 
Below is a list provided by Chris Menard with a brief description of requests by departments:
Police Department: radios, police vehicles, training simulator
IT: upgrades at the library and other town buildings
Schools: entire window repair and an estimated 17.6 million dollars’ worth of capital projects identified.
DPW: multiple stormwater items/repairs
Facilities and Parks: discussion about senior center parking lot, fixing building, keying of all buildings with cards and master keys.

Chris Menard confirms that all presentations and spreadsheets were added to the SharePoint drive and to come to next meetings prepared to discuss concerns regarding priority levels.
	
Chris Menard inquires what the current free cash estimate is. Heather Lemieux shares that the free cash certified value is currently 1.87 million.

Dave Passios comments about the school department presentation and concerns with Turkey Hill. The issues with Turkey Hill need to be dealt with when they occur.  There are a few things there that are simple fixes. Dave Passios questions why they aren’t being dealt with now until the building gets so bad a major project is needed. He also believes that the all-inclusive study of Turkey Hill Elementary will be money well spent. Chris Menard states that the Turkey Hill items listed in the capital plan are half the total value with an assumption there will be matching funding associated.

Dave Passios has a comment regarding next year’s capital projects for police. There are items such as bullet proof vests and cars that are a known item to occur on specific years. Those types of items need to be pulled out of the capital fund and somehow be incorporated into the operating budgets. Dave Passios intends to go visit other cities and towns and see if they have a fleet managements department or someone that manages the entire fleet in town.

b. Treasurer Year End Cash Report for DOR
Evan Watters is requesting the treasurer to attend the next meeting to discuss her report.  The committee members have not had a chance to review the year-end report so this will be reviewed at the next meeting.

c. Municipal Investing
There is a slide on the treasurer’s year end cash report that shows where money can be put into and how it can be spread out. This will be reviewed when Myleen attends the upcoming meeting. 
Evan Watters questions why interest rates on the majority of accounts have very low interest rates.

8. Minutes
a. 11/13/23
The minutes were tabled to next week so members can review.

9. Committee Reports
Chris Menard posted to the drive multiple departments worth of projects anticipated for the next 10 years. Dave Passios agrees to discuss further with green communities regarding grants. 

10. Topics for Future Meetings
Meeting with the Treasurer.

11. Upcoming Meetings
a. Dec 7th (off-cycle) Evan Watters will send out an email to check availability to see if a quorum will be met to hold this meeting. 
b. Dec 14th (no quorum)- Canceled.
c. Dec 28th
d. Jan 11th
e. Jan 19th-20th (MMA Annual Meeting) Dave Passios will be attending and encourages other members to attend.

12. Public Comment
There was no public or committee comment. 

13. Adjourn
Motion to adjourn the meeting by Tom Gray, seconded by Chris Menard. All in favor.
The meeting ended at 8:32 PM.

Lunenburg Finance Committee Meeting Minutes
December 7, 2023 

Members Present: Vice Chair Chris Menard, Board Member Dave Passios, Board Member Jean Russell, Board Member Jay Simeone, Chair Evan Watters.
1. Call to Order
The meeting opened at 7:10 PM.

2. Pledge of Allegiance

3. Announcements
a. Plan Lunenburg Survey:  PlanLunenburg.org
Evan Watters encourages residents to take the survey to provide their input.

4. Public Comment
Anna Lockwood - 91 Main Street- watched prior meetings and feels there are some disadvantages now that recreation and facilities fall under the same group. Facilities requests tend to become a higher priority. Anna Lockwood hopes the committee will see that the parks is different from facilities and there are different needs. The board agrees it may be difficult to ask for funding when looped under facilities. Heather Lemieux confirms that parks is in the division under facilities. Dave Passios thinks it may be worth separating when the departments submit their capital plan. 
Evan Watters suggests having 2 separate capital planning requests. A list for 20k to 500k projects and a list for over 500k projects.

5. Town Manager Report
Heather Lemieux has nothing new to report.  

6. Old Business
a. Treasurer Year End Cash Report for DOR 
The treasurer, Myleen Mallari, provided 2 documents to the committee to review. This includes a report of all accounts that the town has and what was provided to the DOR. 

Dave Passios questions how often the accounts are reviewed and if money could be moved from one type of investment to another to get a better interest rate. Myleen Mallari confirms they review cash books every month. Myleen Mallari has spoken to a vice president representative of each institution if there are change in interest rates. Most accounts are fixed investments. 

Dave Passios asked a question regarding liquid bearing accounts. Some accounts have less than half percent interest and there is the question of whether this has to do with the accounts being fluid. Evan Watters asks why the maximum percentage rate doesn’t increases. Myleen Mallari states a new account was created because there was no interest rate to open a new account. Evan Watters sums up by saying this rate will increase and it’s just because it’s a new account.

b. Municipal Investing
Evan Watters questions if the banks on the documents just banks for general accounts or is there anything specific for municipalities within the banks themselves. Myleen Mallari states they all fall under the master law and under chapter 44 section 55 operating funds.

Chris Menard questions if there is any ability to invest like we would our own retirements funds where they are doing better than the rate of inflation. The rates seem to be mediocre around the board. Myleen Mallari states the stabilization funds fall under chapter 44 section 54. They are in a safety type of investment. The institution Bartholomew specializes in and has an expertise on investments and how to invest those funds without losing anything. 

Evan Watters asked if they could go into a money market account. Myleen Mallari confirms they can, but they must be secured for insurance. They must fall under certain guidelines and the legal list of the Commonwealth of Massachusetts General Laws. Some of the banks have lower interest but they don’t charge fees to make this available to the taxpayers. 

Dave Passios questions if there are a lot of fees on these accounts. Myleen Mallari states they do charge fees for wire. They offer free service to have investments there with a good interest rate. 

Chris Menard questions if there is a policy related to how to invest money. Heather Lemieux confirms there is, and it can be found on the select board policy and procedure page 47.

c. Capital Plan Committee Input 
Dave Passios is putting together a list of future projects that may qualify for Green Communities. Dave Passios Will provide spreadsheet to chair of green communities and contact those departments to get into the look early.

Below are 2 changes to Capital Plan request:
The study and schematic design for windows at Turkey Hill Elementary School was pushed back another year so it will now fall under FY 26.
The Marshall Pond request decreased to $50,000. Instead of treating the entire pond they will only start with a small portion.

Evan Watters shares that at the School Committee meeting they shared frustration that the MSBA was not getting their recommendations out and what projects to go forward with and fund. The school department will be writing a letter to send to the commissioner of MSBA to advocate for smaller communities that work on a smaller budget and don’t have reactionary funds available. Heather Lemieux states that back in 2021 Attorney Costa researched the MSBA funding laws and the MSBA rule is that they can recover any payments made to the town and stop any future payments to the town if it was outside of that period of time. 

7. New Business
a. Budget Season Calendar
Members can find a copy of the budget season calendar in the Finance Committee folder.  The budget season kicks off January 18th when the town manger presents the Capital Plan recommendations.

8. Minutes
a. 11/13/23
The approval of the minutes will be discussed at a further date as some members weren’t able to find them under the committee group drive.

9. Committee Reports
Dave Passios states that the Green Communities Committee has a meeting scheduled for the week of the 18th.
Evan Watters discusses the Plan Lunenburg survey.

10. Topics for Future Meetings
An additional member will be added to the committee and appointed on December 13th. The committee will now consist of a full board. At an upcoming meeting there will be a full committee reorganization and committee liaison assignments.

13. Public Comment

Motion to adjourn the meeting by Chris Menard, seconded by Dave Passios. All in favor
