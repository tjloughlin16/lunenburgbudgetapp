The meeting commenced at 9:52 p.m.
Attorney Costa reported this is the second round of litigation that pertains to the subdivision applications that
were made by O'Brien Homes to the Planning Board. The first application was made for a single subdivision of
over 100 homes and the Planning Board denied it. O Brien Homes appealed to the Land Court. Judge Long
heard the case and went to trial and decided post-trial in favor of the Town. During those proceedings there was
discussion back and forth between the Judge and Attorney Bard (who represented the Town at the time) and the
Judge asked Attorney Bard if O'Brien Homes could just create multiple subdivisions instead of the one. Bard's
answer wasn't expressly yes. He noted the idea behind Article V, Section 5.6 (Cluster Development) was the
Town was advocating for smaller self-contained subdivisions operating as smaller communities as opposed to
this much larger development of 100 plus homes on the same swath of land. The case proceeded and Judge
Long ruled in favor of the Town.
O' Brien Homes then proceeded to divide the one large subdivision into ten smaller subdivisions and came again
before the Planning Board with fewer homes than proposed previously, but still having a substantial number.
Only a couple of these subdivisions could function separately with actual frontage and access from the main
road. The others were built onto the other subdivisions and O'Brien said this is what Attorney Bard said we
could do and now we are doing it. The Planning Board said no, this is still not the purpose and intent of Section
5.6 and denied O'Brien Homes again. O'Brien Homes appealed and it went back to Land Court. The case was
again assigned to Judge Long as is often the case when you get repeat related cases in the Land Court. Judge
Long went through the discovery process and there were some issues with documentation and information that
was requested from the Town. Not all that information was produced, searches that were undertaken were not
extensive enough. Outside experts and consultants got involved, internet servers and private computers were
analyzed and additional documentation was produced. That was a source of great frustration for O'Brien Homes
and their legal counsel. It caused some frustration in Land Court with Judge Long.
One Planning Board member who is not named in the lawsuit because he was not present at the vote, circulated
an email to at least one member of the Board of Selectmen and the Planning Board stating we should really just
sock it to O'Brien. That was the baseline that became a part of the litigation. Judge Long decided in 2016 that
he would bifurcate the case. This is a case that involves multiple counts and some can proceed more quickly
than others.
Judge Long stated we've gone through this lengthy discovery period which is the Town's fault due to failure to
produce the requested information. He decided to go to trial as to whether the Town acted properly and if we
need to get to the bad faith claim. It will be up to O'Brien Homes on whether they want to pursue the bad faith
claims, if needed.
When we last met in early June, we had just received the decision from the court which took two years, that
ruled in favor of O'Brien Homes; stating the Board was wrong to deny these ten subdivision approvals. The
question that was put to O'Brien Homes in that decision was "What do you want to do with the bad faith
claims?" As we had suspected O'Brien Homes said they were going to pursue the bad faith claim. We had a
status conference with Judge Long on July 24"; he established a schedule for additional discovery to occur.
Atter some back and forth between the parties, we were required to identify our potential witnesses several
weeks back. Don Borenstein, the attorney representing O'Brien Homes, is requiring we identify those witnesses
by tomorrow. He had a deadline of a couple of weeks ago to submit a memo to the court that summarized his
===PAGE 2===
think by any means you have a slam dunk case, he had some general concerns with the Town's litigating
position. If you appeal to the Appeals Court and you win, not only do you overturn the Land Court and not
have a 100+ unit subdivision, you also move the bad faith claim, because how can there be bad faith if the
decision was properly rendered to deny the subdivision?
The strongest argument the Town has is that O'Brien Homes had ten subdivisions and just sliced up what
existed before and only two of these subdivisions have the adequate frontage and can sort of exist
independently. The others were built off those subdivisions and would not have frontage or access other than
through the other subdivisions and would require those other subdivisions to be built. If you believe Judge
Long's interpretation of Section 5.6 and the context of these ten subdivisions, you are essentially nullifying
Section 5.6. If anyone can come in and slice up subdivisions, then what does Section 5.6 stand for?
The sequence in terms of pursuing an appeal is we have 30 days from the date of final judgment, which is this
Friday, to file a very simple notice of appeal. That gets filed with the Trial Court of the Land Court. The Land
Court then assembles the record which it sends up to the Appeals Court. They send Attorney Costa notification
that the record has been assembled and transmitted and that puts him on a ten-day clock to officially docket the
appeal. Then he has 40 days to draft and file his brief. Usually assembling the record takes months, but Judge
Long is retiring early next year and he wants to wrap all open cases up before he leaves. Discussion ensued on
the bad faith claims as well as what would happen if the appeal was won or lost.

Board of Selectmen present: Jamie Toale, Chairman; Robert Ebersole, Vice Chairman; Phyllis Luck,

Water Commissioners present: Mark Bursch, Mark Flagg, John Esposito

Mr. Toale called the Board meeting to order at 7:00 p.m. The water commissioners called their meeting
to order and conducted some regular business before discussing the draft intermunicipal agreement
(IMA) between the Water District and the Town of Lancaster with the Board of Selectmen.
Mr. McNamara reported MassDOT is replacing the bridge deck on Route 2A by Maki's. While under
construction the road will be reduced to one lane for traffic with a traffic light. They agreed they will
replace a water pipe while working there if the water district pays for the pipe.
He also reported he is in the process of applying for an earth removal permit through the Board of
Selectmen office for the Massachusetts Avenue construction site of the new water district offices.
McNamara reported under a USDA loan, any structure over 1500 square feet calls for an elevator to be
installed. They may have to reduce the size of the basement in order to avoid that.
Mark Bursch reported the water district is discussing the possibility of selling water to the Town of
Lancaster. The IMA presented for tonight's discussion is a first draft from Lancaster. The water
district's legal counsel has not reviewed it yet.
The Lunenburg water district is considering selling water to Lancaster because they need to fund their
capital improvement plan and the only other option to fund it would be to raise rates for Lunenburg
water users. Their original plan was to expand the water district but met resistance due to the amount of
residential construction coming into the area and having voters from another community in the water
district. Based on the resistance, the commissioners researched an IMA. Support of the Board of
Selectmen would make it an easier process. It is anticipated Lancaster would use 185,000 gallons per
day according to Title 5 estimates, which are usually double what actually is used
Mr. Toale stated initial concern was that Lunenburg has enough water for its current and future needs of
the residents and the current district before supplying anyone else. Another consideration is the Dept. of
Environmental Protection (DEP) permit.
Bursch stated the initial idea was in the short term we would use the resource from Lunenburg to take
care of Lancaster's needs and for the long term develop another water source within that area that would
take care of their needs and give Lunenburg an additional resource. However, we cannot own a water
source outside of the district for the district, so the IMA was the best option. Mr. Ebersole disputed that
===PAGE 2===
His concern with expanding the district is that you have a water district meeting where 10-30 people run
the annual meeting. Thus 50 people from Lancaster could take over the water district.
Mr. Esposito stated we can't control whether the voters are going to come in and vote or not. All I care
about is getting the money from Lancaster to fix our infrastructure so we don't have to raise rates for
Lunenburg users. Based on the chart provided by Lancaster of 185,510 gallons per day of usage by
2023, potential income for the water district from this agreement could eventually be $350,000 per year.
McNamara stated Lunenburg's current use averages 540,000 gallons per day for Lunenburg but the new
permit will be reduced to 505,000 gallons per day. We are currently capable of pumping 1.5 million
gallons per day. If Lancaster happens, he would like to increase his permit by 250,000 gallons per day.
So it would increase to 755,000 gallons total per day. He is working on modifying the old permit
to 185,000 gallons per day in a separate section, so we would never take any of Lunenburg's allowed
amount for Lancaster. It would be two separate permits. Mr. Toale asked how are you going to deal with
35,000 less gallons per day with the new permit? McNamara stated we would be more restrictive on
usage. We will need to educate the public about conservation. Every five years the permit increases by a
certain percentage. If there is more development, we can apply for a modification to the permit. 70% of
Lunenburg receives water from the water district via 2430 connections.
Some of the commercial businesses in Lancaster would like to expand in size but can't because they
need sprinkler installations for fire protection. One concern previously expressed was when water use is
restricted it only affects residential customers. Businesses are not restricted on their usage.
Discussion ensued about possibly extending the water district into Lancaster by an act of legislature
rather than have a well in Lancaster that the water district may not own or have any control over.
The water commissioners asked the Board's opinion on this proposal, although they don't need their
approval, it would be easier to present to water users if the Board endorsed it.
Mr. Toale was supportive of an IMA as was Mr. Ebersole but the wording in the agreement needs to be
strong. Mr. Ebersole stated the issues for him in an IMA are making sure Lunenburg water district
doesn't pay any money to construct or maintain in Lancaster; water rates should be at a premium in
addition to the maintenance costs and the concept of water restriction. Ms. Luck stated she agreed with
the concept of an IMA but needed more specifics and public support. She thought the district voters
need opportunity to weigh in on this proposal. She would not support it if the water users did not.
The water commissioners will continue their discussions with Lancaster and have applied to the DEP to
increase the amount allowed under the permit. They will check back with the Board in early 2019 on
their progress.

Attorney Costa stated any bylaw that addresses things like lighting and noise is a challenge.
There are two ways to regulate these, qualitatively or quantitatively or both. When you regulate
qualitatively it is a more subjective standard which gives you some discretion. When you
regulate quantitatively, it is more objective. The challenge is do you have the resources or the
mechanism to complete the measurement and to then determine if there has been a violation and
then to enforce the violation in a manner that will upheld in appeal? What Costa sees more often
is a more subjective qualitative standard where it states lighting shall be sufficient to ensure the
public safety at all times on the premises.

classrooms/space, before demolition, please check in with me first.
3) Inclusion of a town representative to participate in union negotiations
Ms. Luck stated when she looked at the last union contract and the effect it would have, she felt
the contract did not look at what were the goals of the school system. Could the school system
afford this? Adding a million dollars over three years is a lot. She did not think this would
benefit the school system. About 70% of the budget goes to the school, so the Town has a huge
stake in this. The School Committee would benefit from having someone from the Town; Town
Manager, the Finance Director or a Finance Committee member... someone with a more global
look at the budget, participate in negotiations.
Mr. Toale asked who currently participates in the negotiations. Ms. Sroka stated the School
Superintendent, two members of the School Committee and our Business Manager too, but he
was not with us last time we negotiated. Dr. Burnham stated she foresaw working with the Town
Manager in this process as well. Both boards agreed that the Town Manager would be involved
in the negotiations.
