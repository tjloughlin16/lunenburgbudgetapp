LUNENBURG PUBLIC LIBRARY DIRECTOR’S REPORT 
I have begun work on our grant application with Main Street Bank. This is a two step process with an 
initial expression of interest and then a more detailed application. 
   Programs 
Craft and chill, the collaboration between Lydia and Kaitee, was successful again this month with 13 
attendees! We hosted a flu clinic in early October. 35 attended the blacksmith demo, which was 
entertaining and informative. 33 attended last month’s concert.  
Storytime attendance has been high to kick off the school year, and there have been many new faces 
so far. 
Teen video game night started back up, and a half dozen attended. There was some competition this 
month, with a football game and a soccer practice that went late. The teen advisory board will 
continue to assist Kaitee with sponsoring and organizing events each month, but this group has been 
very constructive and enthusiastic. 
Coming up: The weight strengthening series returns for a fall session. The next concert is the 
Baseball Show with Howie Newman Oct 16. Kaitee and Lydia are offering an adult D&D series that 
has garnered a number of registrations. We will have a cookie decorating program for Halloween for 
teens on Oct 17. This Tuesday we also offered an intro to sewing class for ages 6-12, highlighting our 
Library of Things sewing machine. 
   Outreach 
We are currently working to evolve the 5th grade visits into outreach at the schools. The Artisan 
Market has concluded for the season. We visited three times, with two drop in craft offerings and 
about a dozen interactions in addition each time. We are planning to participate in the vendor fair 
organized by the Agricultural Commission in January. We are also working on a community meal, and 
finding events/gatherings to attend at Arrive Lunenburg and Tri-Town Apartments. 
   Building & Maintenance 
Our teen room renovation has gone out to bid. Our painting/exterior maintenance has been 
awarded, with work to start soon. Lakeview is working on repairing our Proctor Memorial Garden 
water feature as I write the report, and they should be done with that by Thursday. 
Building inspection occurred this week, with no major issues. We need to continue to keep an eye on 
the utility closet entrance and the boxes we store nearby.  
   Technology 
We have revised the security camera quote several times, and I hope that the order will go in now, so 
that we can install this new equipment funded by the Capital Plan. This project is my top priority for 
IT. We are still waiting for Deep Freeze licenses for our newest public access computers. Microsoft 
Office licenses have been received for those devices. Suzor IT ordered the Deep Freeze licenses, 
library staff ordered the Microsoft licenses. Ryan and Kyle (techs) have been responsive in helping us 
with minor issues around the library, but procurement continues to be a challenge for Suzor IT.

Summer Summary 
- Summer Reading: 57 teen readers who logged a combined 81,968 minutes of 
reading. (2023 saw 41 teen readers with 71664 minutes logged)

a great time doing crafts, using the computers and games in the teen room, 
discovering new books, and generally socializing in the library. The Shapiro Group 
director and I have agreed to continue this partnership next summer as well.  
Fall Summary 
- Back to school: Influx of new and old faces in the afternoons with the teens 
returning to hang out after school. So far, no serious issues with behavior  
- Teen Video Game nights returned in October. Will continue for the rest of the school 
year.  
- Partnering with Hannaford in Lunenburg to offer virtual cooking classes again in 
October and November. Currently have 6 registrations.  
- October reading challenge currently ongoing with 2 readers participating

o The In-Betweeners Book Club: for ages 10-13, switches off between middle 
grade and YA reads month to month.  
o Teen “BYOB” book club: for ages 13 – 18, Bring Your Own Book, gives teens a 
chance to meet with other readers and discuss what they are currently 
reading without the pressure of assigned reading.

including the successful Sushi making program. Continuing to grow this 
group.

incarcerated youths.

Trustee Report 
Adult Services Department 
October 2024 
 
Fall is in full swing, and we are busy here at the library! 
 
A few outstanding programs we hosted this September include: 
➢ The Alex Minasian Jazz Trio performed in our main hall on Monday September 9th as part of our 
monthly music series. We had 33 people join us for a beautiful evening of jazz in the library. 
➢ On Monday September 16th we held a puzzle race where three teams of four were in competition 
to complete a 500-piece puzzle the fastest. Everyone enjoyed it and we will be hosting another 
puzzle race in December. 
➢ One of our most exciting events for the fall, was an outdoor onsite visit from Greg Burns of Oak 
Tree Forge for a forging demonstration on Wednesday September 18th. It was fantastic to see 35 
people of all ages here to watch and ask questions of our local blacksmith.  
➢ 16 patrons learned about the history and music of the street organ with Tony Gangi on 
Wednesday September 25th. Some even got to try the street organ themselves and practiced 
playing songs for the crowd! 
➢ The September meeting for the Friday night craft & chill group on September 27th was a big hit 
with 13 people making and sharing different salsa recipes and sipping on virgin margarita 
mocktails. We continue to hit our target demographic of 18–40-year-olds in this program and got 
some interesting feedback from patrons that they prefer our Lunenburg events to other local 
libraries, so it’s encouraging to hear! 
Looking ahead, here are the October events at the library: 
➢ The autumn session of our weight strengthening series with Niki Holtzman starts Tuesday October 
15th, skipping a week in October, but running six weeks to end on November 26th.  
➢ Starting the same day, a new Adult Dungeons and Dragon campaign will kick-off on Tuesday 
October 15th as well. The campaign will run 10 weeks to end on December 17th.  
➢ Our monthly music series concert will be held on Wednesday October 16th with a visit from Howie 
Newman for a baseball themed music/comedy show that is sure to entertain. 
➢ On Saturday October 19th, Portsmouth NH native Jeremy D’Entremont will visit to give a presentation 
on haunted lighthouses of New England – just in time for spooky season to start! 
➢ Carrying forward the Halloween feelings, the library will also host the curator of the Edward Gorey 
House in Yarmouth on Wednesday October 23rd for a talk about Edward Gorey’s life and his 
influence as “Mr. Sinister Cozy” in the literary world. 
➢ Wrapping up October will be our Craft & Chill night on Friday October 25th where we will make mug 
cozies and sip on warm mulled apple cider. 
➢ We are also hosting our usual book clubs on the first Tuesday morning and second Wednesday 
evening, the baking book club on the first Wednesday evening, Community Supported Meditation 
groups every other Tuesday evening, Community Chat & Color every other Thursday morning, and 
our stitchcraft knitting/crochet group every Tuesday afternoon.  
 
Respectfully submitted, 
Lydia Paolilli

   Staff Update 
User’s Council was this week. COA Director interviews conflicted, so Stacy Schuttler, Director of 
Townsend PL acted as my proxy. We voted on the new privacy policy that the Bylaws Committee 
worked on, and the Fy25 CWMARS budget amendment and FY26 CWMARS budgets and 
assessments. I actually anticipate our assessment to decrease for FY26, even with our increase in 
membership fee as full member. This is due to our adding speed to our internet line and a 
corresponding increase in discount applied to our line cost.

adding details to our staffing plan. Lydia and I have been developing some efficiency changes with

for our Children’s Room staff.

also be partnering with the Food Pantry to put out a dropbox through the holiday season. 
Interim Town Manager did thank me for helping on the changes to Capital Plan Committee 
presentations this fall. 
The LSTA grant program this year has been delayed with changes. I will be attending a virtual 
information session on these changes Thursday morning, and will plan to apply again for a civics hub 
grant this year.

LUNENBURG PUBLIC LIBRARY DIRECTOR’S REPORT 
I reached out to one individual, who I have spoken to in the past, about joining the Board. He is 
available, interested and has completed the talent bank form. 
   Programs 
Our Small Works @ LPL 2024 exhibit is installed and features really impressive local artists. Our 
opening reception drew almost 30 people on Saturday afternoon. Generously priced artwork will 
hopefully lead to many sales. 
Our puzzle swap continues to be popular, drawing 45 people this month. That is a slight drop off, but 
I have no doubt, we have established a strong tradition that will continue to be beneficial. 
D&D and our weight strengthening series have been successful, with quality experiences even 
though attendance numbers are relatively small. Curated experiences also continue to be successful, 
like our book in a bag series for kids, book discussion groups, our tween cooking series with 
Hannaford. Our weekly stitchcraft group continues to draw about 10 people, and we were surprised 
with the response to our intro to sewing for adults program, which seems like it will turn into a 
regular sewing and socializing session.  
Coming up: 30 vendors are signed up for the holiday fair on Dec 8, and our holiday concert promises 
a lovely experience later that day.  
   Outreach 
Our current focus is to initiate a partnership with the Primary school with a holiday book exchange 
and library card signup program. Debbie and I are working on finalizing the details. Melanie and I  are 
also working ondeveloping a community meal, and finding events/gatherings to attend at Arrive 
Lunenburg and Tri-Town Apartments.  
   Building & Maintenance 
Our teen room renovation bids have come in under budget. The winning bid is $107,000, which give 
us room for contingencies.  Our painting project finished well under budget as well, around $35,000. 
The Proctor Memorial Garden ARPA funds repaired the water feature and we ran it for several weeks 
before packing up the pump for the winter so that it would not be damaged. 
   Technology 
We have updated our licensing for wireless access points (Through CWMARS). We will need to 
replace those devices next year. Security camera upgrades look to be about to happen, though 
Suzor IT has requested a meeting Thursday afternoon to discuss.

November 2024 
 
A few outstanding programs we hosted this October include: 
➢ The autumn session of our weight strengthening series with Niki Holtzman started Tuesday 
October 15th with 10 people in attendance. This year’s fall session has lower attendance than 
anticipated, but the participants are grateful for the classes and getting a lot from them. 
➢ A new Adult Dungeons and Dragon campaign kicked off that day as well. So far, we have 6 
people playing through a campaign that will run for 10 weeks to end on December 17th.  
➢ Our October monthly music series concert was held on Wednesday October 16th with a visit from 
Howie Newman for a baseball themed music/comedy show that 13 people attended. 
➢ On Saturday October 19th, historian Jeremy D’Entremont gave a presentation on haunted 
lighthouses to 17 people who braved the library despite the homecoming parade! 
➢ Carrying forward the Halloween feelings, the library hosted the curator of the Edward Gorey 
House on Wednesday October 23rd for a talk about Edward Gorey’s life and his influence as “Mr. 
Sinister Cozy” in the literary world. There were 17 people in attendance for this program as well. 
➢ Wrapping up October was our Craft & Chill night on Friday October 25th where 14 patrons sewed 
their own mug cozies and sipped on warm mulled apple cider. 
➢ Our book clubs maintain popularity with continued strong attendance, but the most rewarding 
aspect of our meetings are the discussions we have each month. Not always related to the book 
we read; the discussions carry deep meaningful themes that connect our members to each other 
and lend support when needed. This is one area where we can confidently state we have built a 
community in our library. 
 
Looking ahead, here are the November events at the library: 
➢ On Thursday November 7th we are running a Sewing 101: Sewing Machines Demystified workshop 
for attendees to bring their own machines and learn how to use them. 
➢ Our fall puzzle swap will be held on Saturday November 9th. 
➢ Leominster native Ronny LeBlanc will return for a paranormal hotspots program on Tuesday 
November 12th. 
➢ Editing and writing experts are hosting a memoir writing workshop on Saturday November 16th

   FY25 Budget Update 
 
   Staff Update 
We submitted our Letter of Intent for LSTA grants this year. We plan to apply for a civics hub grant, 
with a revised and distilled version of last year’s grant. 
Building on her long experience with cataloging and technical services, we will be making Marabeth 
a “Records Manager” for our Evergreen catalog. This status will allow her to create original 
cataloging records for items not already in our network. 
Our Angel Tree was very successful this year. Several dozen children will receive gifts because of the 
connections made at the library. Our Food Pantry dropbox has been busy, too. Many donations to 
the pantry were made by vendors at the Artisan Fair, too. 
Most of the staff and their partners gather for our first holiday party in a number of years. It was a 
festive night and nice to see our professional and dedicated staff in a more relaxed environment. I 
am planning to bring a staff-wide training session to the library in the next month or so to refresh 
and support developing our performance in high stress situations and when dealing with unhoused 
populations.  
Lydia and I continue to strategize staff development and cross-training.

Trustee Report 
Adult Services Department 
December 2024 
 
A few outstanding programs we hosted this November include: 
➢ On Thursday November 7th we ran a successful Sewing 101: Sewing Machines Demystified 
workshop for 8 attendees. Each person brought their own machine and learned about how to use it. 
Because of the interest in this program, we will be hosting a monthly open sewing program for novice 
sewers to gain confidence in a community setting. These will start in January 2025. 
➢ Our fall puzzle swap was another success on Saturday November 9th. We had close to 600 puzzles 
and 45 people visit the library for new to them puzzles. 
➢ Leominster native Ronny LeBlanc returned for a paranormal hotspots program on Tuesday 
November 12th to a smaller than normal crowd of 12 people, but they were engaged in the material 
and made some good connections. 
➢ Editing and writing experts led a memoir writing workshop on Saturday November 16th where 5 
people spent two hours learning about the best ways to start writing down their stories.  
➢ The November installment of the monthly music series was held on Wednesday November 20th with 
a visit from Misty Blues frontwoman Gina Coleman and a keyboard accompanist. We had 26 people 
join us for the performance which was of a stellar caliber.  
➢ We ended the month with our afterhours Craft & Chill program, run with Kaitee, where we taught 9 
people how to make veggie sushi rolls on Friday November 22nd 
 
Looking ahead, here are the December events at the library: 
➢ We are restarting the Saturday morning music clinics on December 7th with Lunenburg music teacher 
Jason Graham. These will meet once a month through May. 
➢ The winter artisan showcase and December concert will both be taking place on Sunday December 
8th throughout the library. We will have over 30 vendors selling their crafts inside from 9 am to 1 pm, 
Santa will be taking pictures from 9 am to noon, and Chris and Katie Laford will entertain us with a 
Celtic Christmas concert from 2 – 3:15 pm. It should be a busy day at the library! 
➢ A special holiday puzzle race will be taking place on December 17th from 6 – 7 pm.  
➢ Craft and Chill for December will be cookie decorating and recipe sharing on December 20th after the 
library closes at 6 pm. 
➢ I will be running an all-ages holiday music bingo program the day before Christmas Eve in the 
afternoon. It should be a fun break from holiday craziness for families. 
➢ On December 30th we will have an introductory “Sew Simple” meeting for anyone interested in 
learning to sew to meet, discuss potential projects, materials that may be needed, and other details 
for our monthly meetings going forward.
