Summary of Requests (total $760,000)
•
HVAC Redesign and Construction ($300,000) To address increasing inefficiency and
maintenance costs and to correct performance issues that have been present since its inception, this
project would redesign the HVAC system for the library facility with a commitment to increased
efficiency, environmental awareness, and sustainability.
* Address increasing maintenance, respond to difficulty sourcing replacement parts
* High priority for Library Trustees and Director
Target increased efficiency, resilience, and sustainability
Benefit to entire community, especially library users
* Capital Plan project for 2030, at tail end of 15-25 year expected lifespan for HVAC.
•
Teen Room Renovation ($150,000) To address the increasing need for dedicated space to support
the young adult (tween and teen) demographic, this project would design and construct a renovation of
the library facility to expand the teen room by approximately 40%. The project would include the
construction of two additional offices for staff use to address increasing staffing numbers and the
displaced work area resulting from the teen room expansion.
* Address increased teen traffic that has impacted access to library services for all demographics, and
increased staffing volumes
* Inadequate 450 sq. ft. Teen Room
* Renovation to increase that space by 40%, add two staff offices, sacrificing only a small amount of shelf
space
* Benefit all library users, improving access for adults, teens and children/families
* Increased opportunity to partner with Boys and Girls Club?
• Restoration and Expansion of Proctor Memorial Garden ($25,000) To address the utility and
appeal of library grounds, this project would restore the Proctor Memorial Garden and expand the
ecologically designed gardens on the library grounds to create an appealing, supportive, and nourishing
outdoor space. The project would expand the seating available for library programs, restore plantings,
replacing the water feature pump, re-seat stones on the patio as necessary, add a shade canvas, and install
===PAGE 3===
ecosystems.
• Exterior Painting and Maintenance of Library's Building Envelope ($60,000) To address the
deteriorating exterior of the facility, this project would paint and maintain the building envelope.
** Protects the investment the town has made in the facility
* Proper maintenance ensures no disruption of library services
Safeguards the assets stored in the facility and access to those assets for the taxpayers and residents
• Installation of Backup Power Generator ($100,000) To address needs for warming, cooling, and
shelter, this project would install a backup power generator at the library facility to enable the library to
operate independently from reliance on the power grid.
* Increase resilience in response to weather events
* Trustees have volunteered the facility as both warming and cooling facility for town in emergency
situations
* Commitment by trustees and director to expand this role for the facility
* Expanded "community center" role for library benefits all residents, and at-risk demographics, in
particular, during weather emergencies

Hall of the Library, this project would install solar blocking shades to mitigate seasonal interference with
the use of the Main Hall.
** Address solar glare in Main Hall
* Potential impact on solar gain: installation of 27 similar shades in Children's Room in 2019 and 2022
have had significant impact on cooling
* Benefit to visitors, in particular, but also potential improvement in efficiency of cooling operation, could
lessen the burden of running the facility
Purchase of Outreach Vehicle for Library Outreach Services ($100,000) To address

services more accessible to all community members, this project would purchase, renovate and

to increase the library's outreach services.
Support initiatives that are part of LL's Strategic Plan for FY22-27
Enable staff to more effectively deliver outreach services
*
Assist in engaging with underserved population and non-library users (priority in strategic plan)
* Cellular hotspot would enable delivery of internet access to underserved areas
* Not funding this request would require staff use of personal vehicles to deliver outreach and

Discussion and Vote on Chapter 269 of the Acts of 2022 re: 5% Local COLA Option for Worcester
County Regional Retirees
Ms. Lemieux reported in November of 2022 we were notified by PERAC which is the Public Employee
Retirement Administration Commission that the Governor signed into law Chapter 269 of the Acts of 2022
which provided for an additional cost of living adjustment for retirement boards to act on. Our local retirement
board, Worcester County Retirement System, had previously voted under the sectional law that allows them to
vote every year on the cost of living adjustment which was a 3% adjustment. This change in law allowed
for an additional 2% added to that 3%, so up to 5% which they did vote on, to ask all the member communities
but it requires a 2/3 approval vote of those communities. The board discussed and decided to wait one week to
vote.

Small Bridge Program grant and we have capital funds. We have a consultant who has designed the new bridge
and put the contract specifications together. We are going to be seeking temporary easements to move some
utility poles and we are working with four residents for easements. Out of the four, one wants to be paid and the

obstacles to overcome. Looking back over the last two years we average between seven to twelve openings at

Age Friendly Community
COA Director Sue Doherty filled out and submitted our application to AARP to become an Age Friendly Community
and I provided a letter of commitment that accompanied the application. Today we received our approval letter that we
are now the 793rd community to enroll in the AARPNetwork of Age Friendly States and Communities. Age
Friendly States and Communities is anorganizational affiliate of the World Health Organization Global Network for
Age-Friendly Cities and Communities which started in 2006. Through the age-friendly program, AARP helps
participating communitiesto becomemore livableand age-friendly by creating safer and more walkable streets,
addressingsuch thingsas housing and transportation, better accessto key services, and opportunities forresidentsto
participate in communityactivities.
A core group of public officials started this process last year, working with MRPC on putting together a survey and
getting the results, and we will now re-group in the coming months to put together an action plan to make Lunenburg
more "Age Friendly". There are a lot of things already in the pipeline, such as the Marshall Park project and
implementing a Means Tested Senior Citizen Tax Exemption Program that will aid in this effort.
Thank you to the COA Director Sue Doherty for being the steward and moving this forward.

Ms. Lemieux commented on the recent resignation of two members of the Board of Assessors. Last Wednesday
the Chairman of the Board of Assessors (BOA) Richard Letarte and member Kevin Fish resigned. Recognizing
that this may be cause for alarm, this meeting is intended to address the short and long-term implications of
being down to one member of the BOA. The board cannot hold meetings or vote on any required votes until at
least one of the two vacancies is filled, as a quorum of two members would be required to hold a meeting and
the single remaining member cannot act on his own. Before Mr. Letarte and Mr. Fish resigned, the board voted
on all the pending abatement applications and at their last meeting on August 1st addressed a handful of
abatement applications that had additional information that impacted their assessed values. However, anyone
that wished to meet with the board on their abatement application to provide supplemental information that has
not already done so, and received a signed off abatement form will not be able to do so until there is a quorum
of that board. Those that filed abatement applications on time and received notification of the decision of the
BOA and wish to appeal to the Appellate Tax Board (ATB), must do so within three months of the date of the
decision per Mass General Law Chapter 59, section 65. Outside of any persons that still wish to meet with the
assessors to provide supplementary information on their abatement application that are still within the three
months of the date of the decision on their abatement application, there are no required votes of the board or
signatures needed that are necessary in order for business to proceed as normal until the tax classification
hearing and the relevant tax rate forms need to be submitted this fall. The forms that are submitted must be
signed by assessors that have become certified by taking Module 0 and 4 under the DOR-101 course.
I have been meeting with office staff and our consultant to check on day-to-day operations and discuss the
progress of the required actions, such as making sure data entry on new construction inspections, lot splits and
sketches are entered, sending sales verification forms etc., to confirm we are on schedule to keep on track for
the tax classification hearing and submittal of the required tax rate forms and what we need to do for our five-
year certification of values process. I will continue to meet weekly with the interim assessor and the consultant
to monitor the progress.
To summarize, in the short term operations can commence without a major impact. The long-term implications
are more dire. There exists no alternative other than having at least two members of the BOA that have passed
Module 0 and 4 under the DOR course 101 to sign off on the tax rate forms and ultimately certification of
values. This will require at least one registered voter, preferably two, to step up and be willing to take these
self-paced online courses and be able to pass them as soon as the end of September.
