Meeting Minutes 
October 16, 2023 6:30pm 
Members Present: Kristina Masaitis, Michael-Ray Jeffreys, Greg Roy, Tom Gray, David Blatt, Matthew 
Allison 
Members absent: Brian Lehtinen 
Town Employees: Town Manager Heather Lemieux  
Community Guests: School Committee Chair Carol Archambault, School Committee members Laura 
Brzozoski, Peter Beardmore, APDC representative Anthony Sculimbrene 
Meeting called to order at 6:31pm. 
 
The School Committee met with the Lunenburg Municipal Building Design Committee to talk about the 
plan for municipal building design. The current thought process is for phase one to be the renovation of 
Ritter building that would become the new permanent home for the school offices. 
Town Hall will be renovated into a meeting space in phase two. School administration will leave the TC 
Passios building, and Town Hall will be consolidated into the Ritter Town Hall complex.  
The current plan is to turn TC Passios into a community center. 
Long-term disposition of TC Passios would be food pantry, community kitchen area, storage for the 
town, PACC studio, and some remaining offices. After school, before school, programs would need to 
come back into the schools, and there would not be expansion potential for the Boys and Girls Club. 
We haven't shored up phase three, but Turkey Hill is a much more attractive building for many reasons, 
including the ability to accommodate the Boys and Girls Club expansion, the before school after school 
program, and the community kitchen. The school committee will discuss future needs for Turkey Hill 
School. Limitations of that building include the furnace. As far as keeping this building up, there are 
some things that can be done before the ADA stuff gets triggered, but the reimbursement from the state 
can only happen if the building remains a school for 10 years. 
The School Committee hasn’t gone into major discussion about a new school yet, because they want to 
see what this committee comes up with. If a major system goes down at Turkey Hill and we can't run the 
building, which would be a bigger problem than any other problem that any other board in Lunenburg 
has tried to solve in the last year. 
The school committee has been working on a plan to maintain Turkey Hill as a school for the next 10 to 
15 years. This plan involves spending $14 million inside the next several years to cover everything from 

===PAGE 2===
roof to windows to HVAC to electric to plumbing to ADA. If you are going to spend half of what you 
would spend on the replacement value of the building, it should live past its 15-year time horizon. Laura 
asked if there is plan to abandoning TC Passios down the line. The plan is investing at Turkey Hill and 
keeping TC Passios largely the same, so they could make it 10 more years to get where they needed to 
be.  If we can rehabilitate Turkey Hill and make it a future community center, we can reduce the cost of 
TC Passios and in 10 years we will be able to step into Turkey Hill as this community center and use TC 
Passios land for something else. If we embark on this phased plan and it takes five, six, or seven years to 
get to phase three, we can still adapt it to fit our needs. If we are in the four to five year window with 
phases one and two, and we will finish phase one before we do go on to the next phase so we can 
stagger the cost implications to the town. By the time we get around to phase three, we're talking about 
something in the four to five year time frame. This could be optimistic. The school committee hasn't 
made a decision yet, but most people feel that we need a new building. The current building was made 
to be a middle school, and it's not a vibrant place for young learners, but the school committee 
understands that we need to work with the town. We did the asbest mitigation without needing to close 
the school, and we built an elevator without needing to close the school. It's harder to do when you're 
doing a full scale change to the HVAC distribution system. The school committee isn't sure if a 
renovation to Turkey Hill will make it a school conducive for the purpose of the age group it's intended 
for.  
Mr. Gray: It seems pretty clear that the school committee does not see Turkey Hill as a long-term viable 
school. I think the money is better spent building a new school rather than putting the money into 
Turkey Hill. 
After phase two, the town needs to figure out what happens to Turkey Hill. The school committee is 
here to help, but the town doesn't have the money to turn Turkey Hill into a community center. 
The biggest buildings in town are the school buildings, which drive the major portions of what we 
borrow in this town. We need to think critically about how we move forward and get together as a town 
to figure out how we are going to get these projects done. 
We need to start thinking about phasing as a town and put together a financial road map and a plan that 
gets us there. The town will look different in 2038 if we renovate Town Hall, build a new Town Hall, and 
build a Turkey Hill. We will also have some form of a community center. When we left the primary 
school it needed a new roof, new electrical work, new plumbing work, and it needed all those things 
when the new school got built. So we needed to spend a lot of money to turn those buildings into 
something else. If Turkey Hill is turned over to the town, the building needs to be in a better position to 
be converted to a future use. The town is considering spending $15 million on renovations. 
If we get 30-40 years out of that building currently with it sitting with the schools, we can get a certain 
percentage of that reimbursed or paid for once it leaves school hands.  In 10 years, we will no longer 
need to resolve any more building issues and we can tear down TC Passios, take over Turkey Hill and 
build a new school, and we fully resolve. And when we take over Turkey Hill, it won't need immediate 
renovation. 
The prioritization process that was done a year ago included replacing Turkey Hill Elementary School, as 
well as managing longstanding issues around town offices and municipal use, as well as dealing with a 
school department issue around Municipal Administrative Office Space. The school committee has 
committed themselves to working with the town and with this committee and that they are doing what 
they can to shore up the building so that we don't have another Primary School on our hands. 
David Blatt mentioned the green committee and nobody shows up to our meetings since John Londa 
left. There is free money available for everything you talked about, you just need to use it. 
The school committee has its own budget, but the maintenance costs for the buildings get turned over 
to the town, so they switch from being on the school budget to being on the town budget. 
===PAGE 3===
Carol said she had never been contacted by Green Communities, and Peter said he would go to Green 
Communities. The schools have a new facilities manager. Matthew Allison said a long time ago that if we 
had a master plan, these discussions would be in that master plan. We haven't added the cost of 
bringing a building to future use into the cost model when we built new schools, which is why we have a 
municipal building design committee. Replacing any one of these major systems is very likely going to 
trip an ADA requirement. We don't actually know what the ADA implications are, and they may require 
some substantial work. Turkey Hill needs to be considered in the financial equation, and a reserve 
should be set up in case of a critical failure. But I’m not sure that keeping Turkey Hill going is as feasible 
as you may have conveyed. Turkey Hill may be a higher priority than finding a place for the town 
manager and Ritter, but temporary boiler facilities is a possibility. Turkey Hill was not included in the 
Tappe study, and the current use of the building is not in accordance with the requirements of Zoning 
for building use. The building is still a 55 acre school campus, and that's important to people. 
When we look at working conditions in the Town of Lunenburg, we are down, including the finance 
director, the assistant Finance director, the principal assessor, the land use director, and the 
conservation agent. These are critical roles that the town needs to perform its basic functions. 
When we talk to all of our staff, we find out that the TC Passios has 40 holes in the roof and 40 active 
leaks. If we want to provide an attractive work environment for people to work in, we have to look at 
our buildings. 
The current approach is to do phase one and phase two, and then phase three is tied into both phase 
one and phase two. The plan is to take over Turkey Hill and provide a home for the boys and girls club. 
Anna Lockwood said that she appreciated everyone's points and where everyone stood, but that the 
committee needed to get going and get an idea of how much Turkey Hill would cost. 
Nathan served on the building committee for the new high school, and he and Mr. Roy were the first 
ones to talk about upgrading Turkey Hill. The school committee needs to make it a priority to get a good 
handle on Turkey Hill's needs. 
Phase one and phase two make sense, but we need to get the ball rolling on Turkey Hill so that we can 
make a decision sooner rather than later. A capital request for engineering studies should be done to 
have the data we need to decide what to do with Turkey Hill. 
The school committee is generally in agreement with the current kind of outline that we have, and they 
would like to proceed with advancing phase one and phase two, and then phase three be involved with 
how Turkey Hill falls in with the phase three of this plan. 
We have $225,000 set up for architectural engineering costs. We need to get Turkey Hill numbers nailed 
down for renovation. We need to think about other buildings that might need to be renovated in the 
next 10 or 15 years.  
Mr. Allison thinks phase one and two would complete the charter of the committee, and that phase 
three should be looked at by a different group. He also thinks that phase one and two might take a little 
bit longer than four or five years. 
I would be surprised if phase one and phase two got completed within six years, but we can look 
forward to having our voices heard and having our input added to the master plan.  
The next meeting is on November 6th. 
 
Tom Gray, made a motion to adjourn,  seconded by David Blatt at 8:38pm. Roll call vote: Tom Gray, aye, 
Greg Roy, aye, David Blatt, aye, Matthew Allison aye, Kristina Masaitis aye, Michael-Ray Jeffreys, aye.

Meeting Minutes 
November 8, 2023 6:30pm 
Members Present: Brian Lehtinen, Michael-Ray Jeffreys, Greg Roy, Tom Gray, David Blatt, Matthew 
Allison 
Members absent: Kristina Masaitis,  
Town Employees: Town Manager Heather Lemieux  
Community Guests: NA 
Meeting called to order at 6:31pm. 
The Town Manager and the Facilities Director met with the Municipal Building Design committee to 
discuss a proposal to repair the roof of the Municipal Building. The roof is leaking in the middle portion 
of the building where the school has offices and the PACC has their studio space. 
The roof is beyond its life expectancy, there have been several leaks, and the Public Access area is fully 
utilized. The town has funding left over from the 30 School Street project, so it seemed like a logical 
place to move the funding from. 
The town manager asked for approval of $300,000 for the replacement of the roof over the central 
portion of the TC Passios. The council members agreed and Heather added that the roof is getting worse 
every time they go up on it to patch it. 
Heather asked a question about repairing the roof at this time when we have other plans for that space 
and possibly demolition. She said that we still need to use that space and can't jeopardize public health. 
The town received a hard quote of $300,000. I don't think that this represents a waste of funds and we 
wouldn't necessarily have to do major structural changes to the roof system. 
We need to have that building in workable condition for us to do phase one and phase two, and if we 
don't do it we'll have to spend a lot of money on other things to house the people in the Ritter and Town 
Hall. 
The water damage underneath the central portion of the roof has compromised the asbestos, and the 
tile with asbestos containing material is the number one thing that gets airborne. 
The Municipal Building Design Committee unanimously recommends approval of Article 11 at the 
special Town Meeting in 2023. They thank the Town Manager for their time and attention, and say they 
will have a presentation prepared for the meeting. 

===PAGE 2===
We want to break this process into three phases: renovate the Ritter building, build an addition, and 
move all town offices into the new town hall. Then renovate the current Town Hall to become the 
historic Town Hall. 
The town would consolidate town and school offices into one location, and dedicated community space 
for meetings. The third phase is to create a community center in TC Passios. 
We will decrease some of the code upgrades, but we also want to maintain locations for voting, food 
pantry, PACC, and town clerk and similar offices. We are partnering with the school committee to work 
on what is needed to shore up Turkey Hill. 
TC Passios would be a better building to do all those things we want it to do, and would also be able to 
address critical needs. The school committee has asked for a capital planning study of the Turkey Hill, 
and Mr. Allison is procuring a computer with CAD, and the intention is to come to the town with a 
funding article in the spring. 
Mr Blatt would work with Mr Jeffreys on the presentation.  
The moderator will accept questions after the update, but the presentation will be brief and the 
audience will be the same as the past for the most part. 
We should put in the presentation what we are working on between now and the spring to identify cost 
and timeline so that we can kind of preempt some of the questions that are going to come up. 
The size of the annex to the main building is roughly the same as a 35,000 square foot building, so the 
cost should be substantially lower at least.  
We have some good language and will use some of the ARPPA funds to get a cost estimation going. We 
will make award at our first meeting in January and have all the numbers in the early part of the spring 
and ready for town meeting. 
We are meeting again on the 21st, the week of Thanksgiving to talk about RFP language in detail so we 
can get that out the door. Mr. Lehtinen provided some minutes to us, and we will do a roll call vote on 
them. Mr. Gray, Mr. Roy, Mr. Allison, Mr. Blatt, Mr. Lehtinen unanimously approved. 
The board will meet again on Monday, November 20, and will then meet again on the 4th and 18th of 
December.
