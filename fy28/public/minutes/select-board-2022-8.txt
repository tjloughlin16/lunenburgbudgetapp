FY 2022 4 Quarter Financial Report from Finance Director
Ms. Brochu began with Revenues:
Revenues
Total collections for FY22 are $46,695, 138.62. Actuals exceeded estimates by
3.54%, or $1,596,086.69. Below is a summary of the individual categories:
• Local Receipts: We exceeded our FY22 Local Receipt estimate by
33.68%, or $1,049,137.83. The only category of local receipts below
estimates was investment income - 2.24%, or $559.99 below the estimate
of $25,000.00. All other categories exceeded estimates by large
percentages.
Other excise (meals tax) exceeded estimates by $32,048.26, or 32%;
Departmental revenue - school exceeded estimates by $138,443.10 - this is
primarily due to an increase in the municipal Medicaid reimbursement for
eligible students; Licenses & permits exceeded estimates by $126,106.75, due to
the building permit issued to Lunenburg Central, 475 Leominster-Shirley Road;
Departmental revenue other (ambulance revenue) exceeded estimates by
$24,467.50, or 10%; Motor vehicle excise exceeded estimates by $118,280.15, or
7%; Penalties & interest exceeded estimates by $306,518.44, or 153.25%, due to
increases in penalties & interest on tax liens due to the payoff of the tax lien for
===PAGE 2===
Meadow Woods trailer park and other payments received prior to the tax lien
sale; and finally Miscellaneous non-recurring revenue exceeded estimates by
$232,945.39, or 110%, due to payments received for emergency paid sick leave
in the amount of $71,453.56, payments for prior year Meadow Woods water
betterment revenue in the amount of $121,249.13, and also unclaimed tailings
revenue in the amount of $21,848.81.
• Taxes; The total tax levy for FY22, net of the overlay, is $29,833,387.33.
We received 99.6% or $116,992.51 less than the levy estimate. We also
received $585,466.77 in tax lien revenue.
• State Aid: We received 1.31%, oi $120,946.87 more in state aid than the
FY22 estimate, This was primarily due to receiving $83,961 more than
estimated for reimbursements on tax abatements for veterans and surviving

Expenditures
The total budgeted expenditures for FY22 are $45,099,051.93. We have
expended/encumbered $44,384,375.85, or 98.4% of budgeted expenditures. The
balance of unexpended/unencumbered funds is $714,676.08.

Ms. Lemieux read from her memo the eligible categories that ARPA funding can be used on:
1. Supporting the Public Health Response: This includes COVID-19 mitigation efforts,
medical expenses, behavioral health, and certain public health and safety staff.
2. Addressing the negative economic impacts caused by the public health emergency:
Respond to the far-reaching public health and negative economic impacts of the pandemic,
by supporting the health of communities, and helping households, small businesses, impacted
industries, nonprofits, and the public sector recover from economic impacts
3. Premium Pay for Essential Workers: Provide premium pay for essential workers, offering
additional support to those who have and will bear the greatest health risks because of their
service in critical sectors
Water, Sewer, and Broadband Infrastructure: Invest in water, sewer, and broadband
infrastructure, making necessary investments to improve access to clean drinking water, to
support vital wastewater and stormwater infrastructure, and to expand affordable access to
broadband internet
5. Replacement of Lost Revenue: Replace lost public sector revenue, using this funding to
provide government services up to the amount of revenue lost due to the pandemic. The Final
Rule by the US Treasury allows recipients to elect a standard allowance of up to $10 million
to spend on government services through the period of performance. Electing the standard
allowance does not increase or decrease a recipient's total allocation. Therefore, Lunenburg,
since our allotment is less than $10 million, could choose to use all our funds under this
category.

30 School Street Hazardous Materials Abatement and Demolition
Ray Dunetz has begun the initial work for the 30 School Street project. His survey crew was at
the building last week to survey the existing building, including doors, walls, stairs, roof,
windows, basement equipment and building elevations. The deliverable for this portion of
work will be an existing conditions survey.

and soil samples. The final report is expected to be complete no later than December.
The remaining tasks include preparation of 100% bid documents, advertising around January
2023, and construction (demolition) occurring in the spring of 2023.

Ratification of the Town Manager's Appointment of Facilities Director Paul Visconti
Ms. Lemieux stated she was excited about tonight's appointment of the Facilities Director.
This is a brand new position that was created as part of a reorganization plan through the
Town Charter process approved at Town Meeting. We received 55 applications; out of those
===PAGE 2===
town facilities as well as the school buildings and facilities. Previous to that I worked for
Department of Corrections, Executive Office of Public Safety. I worked in conjunction with
DRM; Division of Capital Asset Management and Maintenance (DCAMM) on several large
projects. I sat on the CommBuys board for the prime grocery contract from 2014 to 2017.
One thing I've always taken from every position is that I treat what I'm doing as if it's my
own, so when I'm spending money or looking for resources, it's not always the quickest
resource or the least expensive, but it's going to be best for the town, for the building and
sustainability of it going forward.
Mr. Dwyer and Mr. Franco had no questions but welcomed him and looked forward to
working with him. Mr. Alonzo asked what challenges he faced while working in Medway.
Mr. Visconti stated some of the most challenging things are pulling other assets or getting a
group colaboration between the town entities. So the state entities and working with private
sector vendors and pulling everything all together; DPW responsibilities, Parks and Rec's
responsibilities, buildings responsibilities; how all that gets allocated. I found that usually to
tackle these problems that come up they're usually not very complex problems but what I
found is the lack of reporting and having sort of a key person to be the reporting structure.
Then the facilitation of the work, how to follow up and make sure it gets allocated to the
proper vendor and then getting them in there in a timely fashion. I want to be the point person
and have the ownership. I'll make sure that it gets to the proper person; we'll come up with a
plan and then working the financing and the allocations of funds and whose budget it comes
out of. Then getting the proper vendor in to do it in a timely fashion. That was one of the
biggest challenges anytime you start a new job, getting to know who the key people are.
Mr. Alonzo thanked him and stated he looked forward working with him as well. Mr.
Jeffieys stated he was happy to have this position created after identifying the need, noting
Mr. Visconti was clearly qualified to take on this responsibility. He looked forward to the
work he will do for Lunenburg as a community.

Ginny Albertson Presentation on Pollinator Habitats
Ms. Albertson, 50 Upland Ave. appreciated coming in to talk about pollinators. She has been
researching this for about 10 years. She started the Lunenburg Community Pollinator Habitat
which others have since taken over. She is a member of the Xerces Society for Invertebrates
Conservation, a member of the MA Pollinator Network, Native Plant Trust, Wild Seed
Project (ME.), Grow Native Massachusetts and the National Butterfly Association.
We are in the greatest global species die off in recorded history and time is critical as we can
still change it around. She explained briefly about ecosystems, biodiversity and the need for
plants to sustain a healthy planet. She referred to Bringing Nature Home written by
entomologist Douglas Tallemy and how his book talks about how we can sustain wildlife
with native plantings. We have already started in Lunenburg but we can do a lot more and
we need to get more people involved.
Ms. Albertson spoke about plant/pollinator relationships; how plants and pollinators depend
upon each other. Native plants are ones that were here before European settlers arrived.
Author Tallemy recommends 70% of your garden be planted with native plants. The town
can plant native plants, trees and shrubs on public lands with appropriate guidance.
Herbicides, pesticides and fertilizers should not be used on native plantings. Once established
you do not have to water them. She recommended homeowners start out small by leaving a
part of their lawn unmowed. We have to teach our children about this. She recommended
going to www.xerces.org for more information and encouraged everyone to try to do
something, even planting in a pot if you don't have a yard. Monarch butterflies have been
added to the red list of endangered species of the International Union for Conservation of
Nature due to the fact that the eastern population has decreased by 90% over the last 40
years. They only lay their eggs on milkweed (botanical name Asclepias spp.). Lunenburg has
eight species of milkweed present. Monarchs overwinter in Mexico. Since each generation
typically lives only a month, they lay eggs in different states along their migration route in

PFAS BENCH TESTING
• Rapid Small Scale Column Testing (RSSCT)
• Alternative approach for pilot testing of different filter media
• Useful for PFAS media comparisons
• Provides limited performance data for use in full-scale facility design
• Approach is not feasible for evaluating manganese treatment
ON-SITE PILOT SCALE STUDY
• Can be used for pilot testing treatment for iron, manganese and PFAS
• Provides practical data for full-scale sizing of permanent treatment facility
• Pilot study duration can last 1 week - 1 year
• Equipment is housed in small trailer or available space at well facility
• Typical pilot study requires:
• 6-inch diameter filters

PILOT STUDY - OPTION 1
• Biological Filtration Pilot Study Only
• This option includes conducting a pilot study that utilizes biological filtration to
remove iron and manganese. It appears that the iron and manganese levels present in
the well are at sufficient concentrations at low flow rates of approximately 100 to 200
gpm to be able to conduct the pilot study without having to pump the well at a higher
rate. A PFAS pilot study would not be included under this option if the Water District
decides to use granular activated carbon (GAC) media for treatment.The media would
need to be included on MassDEP's approved technology. list. MassDEP will require
full scale sampling to confirm that adequate PFAS6 treatment is being provided once
the permanent facility is constructed. This approach will not provide the Water
District with any specific operation and maintenance (i.e. media replacement) costs
for the PFAS treatment system, and it commits the Water District to using GAC
media.
• Duration of this Pilot Study is approximately 2 to 3 months
• Total budget for this option is approximately $160,000
PILOT STUDY - OPTION 2
• Biological Filtration Pilot and PFAS Bench Scale Testing
• This option includes conducting a biological filtration pilot study as described in
Option 1 along with a Rapid Small Scale Column Test (RSSCT) for PFAS removal.
The RSSCT is used to test and compare multiple filter medias for PFAS removal and
obtain theoretical breakthrough curves. The well would also be pumped at the lower
flow rates (100 to 200 gpm). As PFAS concentrations appear to be directly
proportional to the flow rate of the well, the RSSCI would allow the sample water to
be spiked to the desired level of the various PFAS compounds without operating the
well at full capacity for an extended period. This option will be more expensive than
Option 1, however it will provide the Water District with specific information on the
cost difference between the multiple filter media tested. Option 2 will not predict
actual breakthrough times, so conservative estimates will be used to calculate the
required media replacement frequency.
• Duration of this pilot study option is approximately 2 to 4 months
• Total estimated budget for this option is approximately $210,000
