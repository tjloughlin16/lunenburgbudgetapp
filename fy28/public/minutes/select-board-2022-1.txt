1. Encroachment on Town Property at Rear of 993 Massachusetts Avenue
===PAGE 4===
Ms. Lemieux reported this is a property where there are encroachments of boulders on town
property within the right of way. A trespass/encroachment letter was mailed to the property
owner in June 2021 with a survey showing the right of way and no response was received back.
The DPW Director and I met with the property owner to see if the boulders could be moved
voluntarily and there was resistance to that request. She followed up again by phone with the
owner who said she would move them, but they have not been moved. We then requested an
opinion from Town Counsel which he gave at our last meeting and has provided in writing.
The owner was notified of tonight's meeting.
Mr. Alonzo read aloud part of the legal opinion:
Encroachments onto,
into or upon
property owned by the Town e.g. the aforementioned right-of
way, constitute continuing trespasses; and are actionable in a court of law. These actions are commonly
brought in the Superior Courts.
It is our right to petition the court to issue a preliminary injunction to get this expedited.
Mr. Oliva stated snow plowing is an issue if the boulders are in the right of way. It can damage
our equipment or our drivers could possibly get hurt if they strike one hard enough. If we have to
work in the right of way, we'd have to move them. Town manager and I have both been
informed that has been damage to vehicles who are parking along Memorial Drive.
Mr. Alonzo noted this has been brought to the property owner's attention and we have tried to
work with them. They have to date refused to move them and you have seen Town Counsel's
opinion and his recommendations. We can go to court to get a preliminary injunction to force the
property owner to move them or we can move them ourselves, but that has issues that he would
not recommend. But we can't have boulders that interfere with the right of way. Discussion
ensued about how to proceed and the different scenarios if what could happen if one of the two
options was taken.
Motion: T. Dwyer
2"': L. Franco
To follow Town counsel's recommendation to go to court to get an injunction to remove the
boulders
Mr. Jeffreys stated he would support this motion but still preferred the town moving them
because going to court could be a lengthy process and it is now January and snow-plowing
season. In addition, the owner is fully aware of the survey and has done nothing even though
they are encroaching on the right of way and people parking near the senior center have hit the
boulders.
Vote: All in Favor

3. ARPA Funding for COVID-19 Rapid Tests for Seniors
Ms. Lemieux reported last Wednesday afternoon the State administration announced
that municipalities would be able to purchase COVID-19 Rapid Test Kits using the state
contract bid. Since then I was put in contact with a representative from Operational
Services Division, who procures and oversees all the state bids/contracts, who provided
me additional contact information for different manufacturers and distributors under
two different state contracts. It was relayed to me that the supply for Rapid Test Kits is
very limited right now, especially through these state contracts, since all towns/cities are
===PAGE 5===
trying to purchase them simultaneously, statewide school systems were provided kits,
and 2.1M kits were issued to 102 cities and towns in MA with the highest percentage of
families below the poverty level. I contacted seven different companies and heard back
from two. One said there was a 4-8 week lead time and the other was able to send me a
case immediately. We will be distributing 100 kits to the Senior Center to distribute and
some will be reserved to distribute to employees. Weknow there has been requests by
seniors for at home test kits so this will assist with that need.
We have seen an uptick in employees testing positive or close contacts and in order to keep
our other employees safe and those that test positive back to work, these test kits will be
needed.
4. Request from legislators for fiscal priorities for FY 2023
Ms. Lemieux read from her memorandum to the board:
Each fiscal year, as the state begins their budget process for the next fiscal year, our legislators ask for a
prioritized list of our budget priorities. These priorities would be any projects that we would like
them to advocate for funding as earmarks to be included in the state budget. Last year the
following projects were submitted as priorities (listed according to priority level):
1. $127,000 for fire alarm upgrade at the Lunenburg Primary School.
2. $355,000 for hazardous materials abatement and demolition of the old Primary School
building and feasibility and design for future use of the space.
3. $180,000 for Firefighting Turnout Gear that would provide each firefighter two sets of
gear so that after each fire, gear is cleaned of hazardous materials.
4. $40,000 for the installation of new LED lighting fixtures at the Library.
As you are aware, the fire alarm upgrade at the Lunenburg Primary School (Item #1) was funded
through a $25,000 earmark from the State and the balance was funded through the school budget. The
Town also received a $40,000 earmark from the State for the installation of new LED lighting at the
Library (Item #4) as part of the FY 2022 state budget. The FY 2022 Capital Plan funded $100,000 for
Firefighting Turnout Gear and the annual funding in the Fire Department's operational budget alleviates
this project request (Item #3). I would like to submit the following list of projects for consideration for
FY 2023:
1. Projects part of Chapter 228 of the Acts of 2018: $300,000 for the redevelopment of the
property at 925 Mass Avenue, $250,000 for upstart costs of a community commercial kitchen
for small business owners that need commercial kitchen space, and $450,000 for façade and
streetscape improvements in the downtown area of Main Street, Leominster Avenue, and
Lancaster Avenue.
2. $371,000 for hazardous materials abatement and demolition of the old Primary School
2021 was $750,000.
4. $100,000 for Senior Center building improvements (Combines different FY 2023 capital plan
requests)
Ms. Lemieux advised the board that only four requests can be submitted. The delegation is being
invited to the first February meeting and that is when they would need the final requests.

What Qualifies as a Capital Project?
The Town's Policy on Capital Expenditures (Financial Policy Section 3.02) defines
capital projects as items that cost $20,000 or more and have a useful life of three years or
longer.
•
The Massachusetts Association of Town Finance Committees defines capital projects as
"major, non- recurring expenditures, for one of the following purposes:
• acquisition of land for a public purpose;
• construction of a new facility or external expansion or major rehabilitation of an
existing one. Examples of such town facilities include public buildings, water and
sewer lines, roads and playing fields;
• any planning, feasibility, engineering or design study related to a capital project or
to a capital improvement program consisting of individual projects.
• equipment for public improvements when they are first constructed such as
furniture, office equipment, or playground equipment;
• major equipment which is expensive and has a relatively long life such as a fire
apparatus, garbage trucks, and construction equipment.
Capital Planning Process
• The Capital Planning Committee (CPC) creates a Capital Plan that consists of all capital
requests submitted by each town department for the next five fiscal years.
