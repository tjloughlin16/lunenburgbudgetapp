Lunenburg Council on Aging                                             
Meeting Minutes  
January 8, 2019 
 
CALL TO ORDER: 9:30 a.m. 
 
Present:  Director Sue Doherty, Chairperson Pete Lincoln, Cheryl Moisan, Betty DiGiacomo, Diane Nowd, Jane 
Rabbitt, Deb Lincoln, Judy Tarbell, Sarah Grant, Barbara Brown. 
Absent:  Jacquelyn Dwyer, Kevin McNally 
 
GUESTS:  Town Manager Heather Lemieux, Selectman Jamie Toale, Eagle House Supporters Liaison Marie 
Burchfield, DPW Director Jack Rodriquenz, Skateboard park representatives Anthony Caiozzo, Griffin Caiozzo, 
Aidan Moore, Aiden Swift. 
 
ANNOUNCEMENTS:  Mr. Lincoln announced that anytime there is a delay at school, the COA meeting will be 
postponed to the third Tuesday of the month. 
 
MINUTES APPROVAL:  Ms. DiGiacomo motioned to approve the minutes from the December 11, 2018 
meeting, Ms. Grant seconded.  On vote, motion carried unanimously. 
 
CURRENT BUSINESS:  
1. Director’s Report:  
 Skateboard Park discussion: Ms. Doherty informed the board that at the last Board of 
Selectmen meeting the location of the proposed skateboard park was discussed among the 
Selectmen, the DPW, and Parks Commission. Mr. G. Caiozzo, Mr. Swift, and Mr. Moore gave 
a brief presentation of a topographical plan of the park, which addressed parking by 
relocating a section of the Kids Kingdom playground. The presenters suggested that most 
skateboarders would be dropped off by parents, or would come straight from the schools 
nearby. The presenters also claimed that most of the activity at the park would occur from 
3:00 p.m. to dusk, implying their actions wouldn’t affect Eagle House patrons. Mr. Lincoln 
asked why the location between the T.C. Passios and Kids Kingdom was chosen. Presenters 
replied that Lunenburg Police Chief Jim Marino accepted that location in terms of visibility 
over a section of Marshall Park, which is more susceptible to graffiti. Mr. Swift added that 
other locations are too secluded, and the old skate park at Marshall Park is deteriorated, 
and that behind the school next to the turf field isn’t accessible to people who aren’t 
Lunenburg students. Mr. Moore stated that the Board of Selectmen decided the location 
next to the T.C. Passios is best and also suggested that more parking would be made 
available to the Eagle House when the park is finished.  Mr. Lincoln informed the presenters 
that when the addition was put on the Eagle House, a septic mound was required, and 
when sewer came, and the mound was removed, the COA at the time presented a plan for a 
parking lot that never went through because they couldn’t raise the $300,000 the parking 
lot would cost, and there were no grants available at the time. Mr. A. Caiozzo opined that 
the park wouldn’t acerbate the parking situation at the Eagle House, and asked if patrons of 
the Eagle House ever park at the T.C. Passios, and pointed out that the park will be small, 
and is not designed to be a destination, but a resource for local skateboarders. Mr. Lincoln 
then asked if the presenters, who stated they are currently in the eighth grade, if they plan 
to continue to use the park throughout high school. Mr. Swift replied that there are high 
school students who drive to other parks outside of Lunenburg. Mr. A. Caiozzo said that he 
knew of people in their twenties and thirties who have expressed interest because they

skated in the past, and now their children are learning to skate. Mr. Lincoln asked if there 
would be classes, and commented that a skate park in Leominster was built years ago, and 
no one is using it now. Mr. A. Caiozzo replied that skateboarding will be recognized as an 
Olympic sport in 2020, and also said that kindergartners are being recruited now, and that 
there is collaboration with other parks and the YMCA to offer classes. Ms. Nowd asked if the 
park would be illuminated at night. Mr. Moore responded that the park would close at dusk, 
so no one should be there at night. Mr. Lincoln asked if the town would be held liable for 
any injuries incurred at the park. Ms. Lemieux responded that incidents at the park would 
be covered under the town’s current insurance policy. Mr. Lincoln asked if ownership of the 
park would be turned over to the town. Mr. Rodriquenz replied that the Park Commission 
would be responsible for the park upon its completion. Mr. Lincoln asked if the town would 
fund repairs to the park. Mr. A. Caiozzo responded that a “Friends” group will be 
established to cover the upkeep of the park. Ms. Doherty expressed her concerns about the 
use of the park when school is out for summer vacation, and the safety of its users who may 
be riding their skateboards to the park via Memorial Dr. Ms. Doherty also questioned 
whether the Eagle House bathrooms will be expected to be available to those using the 
skate park. Ms. Doherty said she supports the idea of relocating part of the playground in 
order to fit in more parking, and also asked about potential sidewalks down Memorial Dr. 
and whether the gate on Memorial Dr. that prevents access between the T.C. Passios and 
Memorial Dr. was due to come down. Ms. Lemieux responded that the Montachusett 
Regional Planning Commission has been tasked with doing a study on the area, and that 
sidewalks on Memorial Dr. will fall under the Commonwealth sponsored Complete Streets 
project.  Mr. Toale pointed out that there are several projects happening simultaneously, 
and many meetings among abutters, including town employees,  will be coming up to 
discuss what could work best for everyone. “Whoever puts the first shovel in the ground 
will need to know how other projects will be affected,” he said.  
 Bridges Together: Ms. Doherty reported that the conference call with Turkey Hill Middle 
School principal Heidi Champagne, fifth grade teacher Rhonda Connery, outreach 
coordinator Pauline Roy, and Ms. Doherty addressed the issues of the last session, and it 
was decided to host both the teachers and the volunteers to the same training session to 
assure each person understands their roles for the next session to begin in February. 
 FY20 Budget: Ms. Doherty reported she’ll be asking for $100 more this year to cover the 
rise in the Montachusett Opportunity Council donation amount.  Ms. Rabbit moved to 
accept the proposed budget, Ms. DiGiacomo seconded. On vote, motion carried 
unanimously.  
 Capital Plan: Ms. Doherty asked Mr. Rodriquenz if her requests have been included in his 
proposal to the Capital Plan Committee. Mr. Rodriquenz replied that Ms. Doherty’s requests 
weren’t eligible for the capital plan because they weren’t over the $10,000 minimum. Ms. 
Doherty then asked about the reconfiguration of the roof above the main entrance to the 
Eagle House, and Mr. Rodriquenz replied that the request is being discussed. 
 Dementia Friendly Community: Ms. Rabbitt informed the board that the “Forget-Me-Not” 
is the designated symbol for dementia, and that Lunenburg students are working on 
designing logos for the Dementia Friendly Community concept. Ms. DiGiacomo commented 
that the ID cards, which let people know in a subtle way that the person they are dealing 
with has a memory problem, are very helpful. Ms. Rabbitt also reported that Paula Bertram 
will be representing the Lunenburg Planning Board on the Dementia Friendly Community 
focus group.

 Memory Café: Ms. Doherty reported the December café was well attended, despite the bad 
weather.  
 Building Report: Ms. Doherty reported that the DPW is working on getting quotes to paint 
the interior of the building.  Ms. Lincoln asked about the Veterans Affairs Agent’s office 
moving out of the Eagle House. Ms. Doherty explained that since the office is not 
handicapped accessible, it will be used for additional storage.  
 Upcoming Events: Ms. Doherty reported an Eagle House Supporter fund raiser involving the 
2018 World Series Trophy is in the works. The trophy might be at the Eagle House in early 
April. 
 
EAGLE HOUSE SUPPORTERS REPORT: Ms. Lincoln reported the Supporters are working on donation envelopes 
to supply area funeral homes. The finance report was tabled. The ping pong ball 50/50 raffle brought in $235. 
The Supporters are working on developing a Facebook page. Ms. Burchfield commented that holding a 50/50 
leading up to special events works well. 
 
NOTICES AND COMMUNICATIONS:  Ms. Doherty informed the board that AARP will not be coming to do taxes 
at the Eagle House this year, because they don’t have enough volunteers. 
 
BOARD COMMENT: Mr. Lincoln said he attended the Eagle House New Year’s Eve party and he enjoyed it.   
 
PUBLIC COMMENT Ms. Lemieux asked the board if they had a time preference concerning upcoming forums 
in regard to town business. The board agreed they would prefer meetings during the day. Mr. Toale 
announced that the Board of Selectmen would be discussing Town Meeting warrants, and marijuana 
prohibition. 
 
ADJOURNMENT At 10:55 a.m. Ms. DiGiacomo motioned to adjourn. Ms. Grant seconded. On vote, motion 
carried unanimously. 
 
UPCOMING MEETING SCHEDULE: Tuesday, February 12, 2019 at 9:30 a.m. at the Eagle House.

Lunenburg Council on Aging                                             
Meeting Minutes  
February 12, 2019 
 
CALL TO ORDER: 9:30 a.m. 
 
Present:  Chairperson Pete Lincoln, Director Sue Doherty, Cheryl Moisan, Diane Nowd, Jane Rabbitt, Jacquelyn Dwyer, 
Deb Lincoln, Judy Tarbell, Barbara Brown 
 
Absent:  Betty DiGiacomo, Kevin McNally, Sarah Grant 
 
GUESTS:  Selectman Jamie Toale, Eagle House Supporters Liaison Marie Burchfield, Gaynor Grieci 
 
ANNOUNCEMENTS:  Mr. Lincoln asked the board for a moment of silence in remembrance of Arnie Rill who passed 
away recently. He was a Meals on Wheels driver for many years. Ms. Doherty announced that the Eagle House would be 
closing all programs in the afternoon due to the weather. 
 
MINUTES APPROVAL:  Ms. Nowd motioned to approve the minutes from the January 8, 2019 meeting. Ms. Rabbitt 
seconded. On vote, motion carried unanimously.  
The minutes of the January 8 meeting were discussed among board members. Mr. Lincoln said he felt as if the 
board was “blindsided” by the attendance of the students organizing the park, Town Manager Heather Lemieux, and 
DPW Director Jack Rodriquenz at the last meeting, and repeated his concerns about the location of the park regarding 
traffic, the potential of Town Hall offices moving to the T.C. Passios, and the reasoning behind the importance of the 
park’s visibility. Ms. Dwyer commented that she wished she could have been at the last meeting, and opined that given 
the visibility of the proposed location, people using the park would be better behaved than they would if the park were 
less visible.  Ms. Nowd urged those against the idea of the park to look for the positive aspects. Ms. Brown asked what 
happened to the skateboard park at Marshall Park, and Ms. Tarbell asked what was wrong with Marshall Park for a 
location. Ms. Doherty commented on the way people drive up and down Memorial Dr., and the parking on both sides of 
the road, and stated that if town offices are to relocate to the T.C. Passios, traffic on Memorial Dr. will surely increase. 
Mr. Toale commented that the Building Reuse Committee has proposed to use the T.C. Passios as a municipal building, 
and to keep the gymnasium and cafeteria open for community use.  Further steps in engineering the possibility will be a 
topic at the next Annual Town Meeting. Meanwhile, meetings involving various town boards and commissions, town 
employees, and abutters are in the works. Mr. Toale referred to the skateboard park as “a dream the Board of 
Selectmen are supporting,” but also pointed out that the funds are not there yet. Mr. Toale also opined that the Board 
of Selectmen are trying to look for ways to make the park work, as opposed to looking for ways that it won’t work. Ms. 
Lincoln expressed her fear, as a senior who doesn’t see very well, is that she won’t see pedestrians as she’s pulling onto 
the road.  Mr. Toale commented that Memorial Drive is on the top of the list when it comes to the state sponsored 
“Complete Streets” program, but also reminded the board that the town was not awarded the grant last year. Ms. 
Rabbitt commented that the kids who want the park are so positive, and she doesn’t want to dash their hopes. Ms. 
Brown added that as plans for the center of town progress, the board should have a better look at the big picture in due 
time. Mr. Lincoln reminded the board that the Council on Aging’s task is to focus on the safety and well-being of the 
senior citizens of Lunenburg, and not on its youth.  
 
CURRENT BUSINESS:  
1. Director’s Report:  
 Bridges Together: Ms. Doherty reported the training meeting may be disrupted given the weather 
forecast, and informed the board that eight more volunteers are needed. Ms. Nowd pointed out 
that winter is a tough time of year for volunteers to commit, and that she hopes the program 
doesn’t die because some people have badmouthed the program. Ms. Doherty commented that 
maybe three times a year is too many sessions, and maybe next year it should just be held in fall and 
spring. Ms. Nowd added that changing the day from Monday to Tuesday was challenging for her 
because she had to change a lot of appointments around to make things work, but also pointed out 
that students should be better prepared before class starts, as opposed to coming off the weekend.  
Unanimously approved by the COA at 
March 12, 2019 meeting.

 Finance Committee meeting/FY20 Budget 2/21/2019 @ 7p.m.:  Ms. Doherty requested Mr. 
Lincoln’s presence at the meeting, and informed the board she would only be asking for $100 more 
than last year to cover the donation to the Montachusett Opportunity Council for meal services.  
 Capital Plan: Ms. Doherty reported she found out that Eagle House requests were included in the 
capital plan after it was released online. An HVAC quoted at $45,000 is on the list for FY22, 
reconfiguring the main entrance is quoted for $88,000 in FY22, a generator for the entire building is 
quoted for $75,000 in FY24, building automation is quoted for $25,000 in FY26, and a new boiler is 
quoted for $22,000 in FY22.  
 Dementia Friendly Community: Ms. Rabbitt reported that she went to the Marlboro Senior Center 
with Keith Lanzilotti, the Memory Café facilitator, and that she got some ideas, such as distributing a 
questionnaire among the faith-based community. Ms. Rabbitt explained that that’s how Marlboro 
got started with the dementia/age friendly concept. Ms. Doherty added that she asked the pastor 
from St. Boniface to attend the next task force meeting to collaborate with Ernie Farrar, who is 
currently on the task force. Ms. Rabbitt also brought up the dementia friendly logo, and was told 
that there is a nationwide logo of a forget-me-not flower design, but opined she’d like to find a way 
to incorporate the designs Lunenburg students have created. Ms. Dwyer added that the medical 
community uses purple angels, originated by the Alzheimer’s Association, to identify hospital 
patients who have dementia. Ms. Doherty commented that any entity that hangs a dementia 
friendly sign on their door should be properly trained as “dementia friendly.” 
 Memory Café/Grant Award: Ms. Doherty reported she received a $5,000 grant from the Greater 
Lowell Community Foundation and the Community Foundation of North Central Massachusetts. Ms. 
Doherty and Keith Lanzilotti will be attending a meeting of the Ayer Rotary, as well as a Jewish 
Family Services percolator meeting. They will be giving a power point presentation on how the 
Memory Café has been developing in Lunenburg.  According to Ms. Doherty, people will be 
attending the Jewish Family Services meeting from various locations via Skype.  
Ms. Doherty reported that the January café didn’t go as planned because the guests were 
actually soliciting their wares to those attending. It was made aware to Joy Memory and her partner 
that the Memory Café is not the right atmosphere for selling items, and that Ms. Doherty was under 
the impression the women were only going to teach and talk about essential oils. 
 The Central Massachusetts Agency on Aging was also in attendance to present their $500 grant 
check to Ms. Doherty. Ms. Doherty thanked everyone who volunteers at the café to make it such a 
success. Ms. Rabbitt commented that Ms. Doherty does a great job with setting up, and 
transforming the Eagle House into an appropriate venue for guests.  
 Shuffleboard Court: Mr. Lincoln said he can’t think of a reason why someone can’t be found to 
finish the project, and that he’d like to have things that younger, more able-bodied seniors can do. 
Ms. Doherty commented that she’s invited the Lunenburg Friendly Seniors to have their annual 
summer BBQ at the Eagle House this year, and using the shuffleboard court could be a nice feature. 
 Building Report: Ms. Doherty reported she is still waiting to hear from the DPW on the backs of the 
signs that direct people to the Eagle House.  The senior property tax work-off person is back to 
supplement the cleaning of the interior of the building.  
 Upcoming Events: Ms. Doherty announced the Eagle House will be hosting a basic American Sign 
Language tutorial for town employees, based on a suggestion from Eagle House patron Wendy 
Newcomb. 
 
EAGLE HOUSE SUPPORTERS REPORT: Ms. Lincoln reported that Marie Burchfield chaired the last meeting as the 
president was out of town. Ms. Doherty requested funds for a 60-inch television for the many upcoming programs that 
need a big screen display at the Supporters meeting. Ms. Lincoln also reported that the donation envelopes to be 
distributed to funeral homes were not proofread, and need to be reprinted after corrections are made. Ms. Lincoln also 
gave an update on the World Series Trophy. The trophy was originally slated to arrive sometime in April, but the father 
of the man who is in charge of the trophy is very ill, so a date has yet to be determined. Ms. Lincoln informed the board 
to let Ms. Burchfield know of any “in memory of” donations made so she can list them on a page for the summer 
newsletter. Ms. Lincoln also reported that the horticultural students are willing to help with the raised garden beds, but 
===PAGE 3===
Ms. Doherty reminded the board that the garden grows over summer vacation and the students won’t be available. Ms. 
Burchfield commented that only those who weed the garden should be allowed to reap its bounty.  
 
NOTICES AND COMMUNICATIONS:  Ms. Doherty informed the board that Meal Site Manager Sandy Ricci, and Outreach 
Coordinator Pauline Roy have received their “Serve-Safe” certificates. 
 
BOARD COMMENT: Ms. Rabbitt asked if there was anything that could be done about the acoustics in the room where 
the Memory Café is held, and suggested a request for baffles in the capital plan. 
 
PUBLIC COMMENT: Mr. Toale announced that Annual Town Meeting will include the Charter Review, and many other 
topics, which will extend the meeting from Saturday, May 4, and continue to Monday, May 6, 2019.  
 
ADJOURNMENT: At 11:09 a.m., Ms. Nowd motioned to adjourn the meeting, Ms. Dwyer seconded. On vote, motion 
carried unanimously. 
 
UPCOMING MEETING SCHEDULE 
Tuesday, March 12, 2019. 9:30 a.m.

Present:  Chairperson Deb Lincoln, Director Susan Doherty, Cheryl Moisan, Betty DiGiacomo, Diane Nowd, Jane Rabbitt, 
Pete Lincoln, Judy Tarbell, Barbara Brown, John Pesenti, Susan O’Sullivan 
 
Absent:  Sarah Grant 
 
GUESTS:  Council on Aging liaison to the Eagle House Supporters Marie Burchfield 
 
ANNOUNCEMENTS:  Ms. Lincoln wished a happy birthday to all board members who have November birthdays.  
 
PUBLIC COMMENT: none 
 
MINUTES APPROVAL:  Mr. Lincoln commented that the board took a vote at the October meeting that wasn’t included 
in the minutes. Ms. DiGiacomo motioned to approve the October 8, 2019 meeting minutes as amended. Mr. Pesenti 
seconded. On vote, motion carried unanimously. 
 
CURRENT BUSINESS:  
1. New Council on Aging Board Member Orientation: Ms. Doherty supplied the board with two handouts 
describing the breakdown of the Massachusetts Elder Network, as well as a chart distinguishing the different 
roles of the COA board members, chairperson, and the director. Board members are to review the handouts 
and discuss what information should go into the new orientation packet at the December meeting.  
 
2. COA Bylaws vs. Polices and Procedures: Ms. Lincoln commented that the Town Charter is minimalist when 
it comes to describing the role of the Council on Aging, and added that COA policies and procedures should 
include meeting times and how to address possible issues, such as absenteeism. 
 
3. Director’s Report:  
 Building Report: Ms. Doherty reported that capital planning time and that the following projects will 
be submitted: reconfiguration of the foyer to avoid ice dams, a powerful generator for possible 
power outages, an additional parking lot behind the Eagle House, painting the entire interior of the 
building, remodeling both kitchens, and replacing the rugs. Ms. Doherty added that outside of the 
capital plan, the flooring in both restrooms in the older section of the building need replacing. Mr. 
Pesenti commented on the reconfiguration of the roof above the foyer and highly recommended 
installing a Hunter Cool Vent to help with ice dams. 
 
 Update on Director Salary Grade/Step Increase: Mr. Lincoln reported that a letter supporting the 
director’s grade/step increase was submitted to the Personnel Committee in June of 2019, and that 
the increase was approved and referred to the Board of Selectmen who approved it, as did the 
Finance Committee. The increase was put on the Town Warrant for the town to decide at the 
Special Town Meeting scheduled for November 12, 2019. Mr. Lincoln informed the board that in late 
October a member of the Personnel Committee requested a job description from Ms. Doherty 
claiming they didn’t have one.  According to Mr. Lincoln, Town Manager Heather Lemieux called Ms. 
Doherty to inform her that the portion of the warrant article concerning the director’s step increase 
would be removed because the Personnel Committee didn’t have enough information to conclude 
that the scope of the director’s position has increased. Ms. Lincoln reported that she spoke with a 
member of the Personnel Committee, who was not at the Personnel Committee meeting when they 
decided to remove the director’s step increase from the warrant article. That member then 
consulted the chairperson of the Personnel Committee who called an emergency meeting, which

Mr. and Mrs. Lincoln attended, and supplied the committee with two pages listing the work put in to 
additional hours to what the director is paid for, as well as additional responsibilities the position 
has taken on.  According to Ms. Lincoln, three out of five members of the Personnel Committee 
attended the emergency meeting, and the Town Manager was also in attendance. Ms. Lemieux 
opined she thought the position should be increased from 11 to 13, as opposed to 14 for the

been posted since April 2019.  Ms. Lincoln also said she’d continue to pursue the matter after the 
Special Town Meeting if needed, and is also looking into changing the current title of COA Director 
to Director of Elder Affairs to better reflect the job description. Ms. Doherty thanked the board for 
their support.  
 
 Sand for Seniors and other Fire Dept. Programming: Ms. Doherty informed the board that the Sand 
for Seniors program had begun for the season, and that Captain Jim Ricci also has combination lock 
boxes for emergency entry available for those who need it.  
 
 Director Medical Leave: Ms. Doherty informed the board she will be out of work from December 13 
to the 31st due to surgery.  
 
 Town of Lunenburg Property Tax Work Off: Ms. Doherty reported that workers have handed in 
their time sheets, which are being processed up at Town Hall. Applications for 2020 are available to 
be filled out, and feature new positions. The Property Tax Work-Off Committee will meet again in 
January to assign positions to workers. 
 
 Upcoming Events: Ms. Doherty described upcoming programs and events at the Eagle House.  
 
 
EAGLE HOUSE SUPPORTERS REPORT:  Ms. Lincoln informed the board that the Ham and Bean Supper fundraiser 
brought in $1,200, and that the Supporters’ board is down two members.  Ms. Lincoln also recognized Pat Kennedy for 
sending out cards to people who are bereaved or ill. Ms. Burchfield added that she wants people to know that the Eagle 
House Supporters care about them.  
Ms. Lincoln said a few people have talked about whether there will be another Uptown Stroll, and has suggested that 
those interested in organizing the event to form a committee soon, and possibly schedule the Ham and Bean Supper to 
end the event.  
 
NOTICES AND COMMUNICATIONS:  none 
 
BOARD COMMENT:  Mr. Lincoln said he’s been in contact with Lunenburg Police Chief Jim Marino who has been trying 
to contact the person responsible for removing the boulders  from Memorial Drive. 
 
ADJOURNMENT At 10:53 a.m., Ms. DiGiacomo motioned to adjourn the meeting, Mr. Lincoln seconded. On vote, 
motion carried unanimously. 
 
UPCOMING MEETING SCHEDULE 
Tuesday, December 10,  2019 at 9:30 a.m.

Lunenburg Council on Aging                                           
Meeting Minutes  
December 10, 2019 
 
CALL TO ORDER: 9:30 a.m. 
 
Present:  Director Sue Doherty, Vice chairperson Jane Rabbitt, Cheryl Moisan, Betty DiGiacomo, Judy 
Tarbell, Sarah Grant, Susan O’Sullivan  
 
Absent:  Chairperson Deb Lincoln, Pete Lincoln, Diane Nowd, Barbara Brown, John Pesenti 
 
GUESTS:  Council on Aging Liaison to the Eagle House Supporters Marie Burchfield 
 
ANNOUNCEMENTS:  Ms. Doherty informed the board that kitchen aide Judy Alario passed away on 
Thanksgiving. Ms. Rabbitt requested a moment of silence. Ms. Doherty also announced that COA van 
driver Helen Godin resigned from her position. Ms. Nowd informed the board that Mr. Pesenti is in the 
hospital after a fall. 
 
MINUTES APPROVAL:  Ms. O’Sullivan motioned to approve the minutes of the November 12, 2019 
meeting. Ms. DiGiacomo seconded. On vote, motion carried unanimously.  
 
CURRENT BUSINESS:  
1. New Council on Aging Board Member Orientation: The board decided to forward this topic to 
the next meeting when hopefully more members could be present. 
 
2. COA Bylaws vs. Policies and Procedures: The board decided to forward this topic to the next 
meeting when hopefully more members could be present. 
 
3. Director Report:  
 Building Report: Ms. Doherty reported the outdoor shuffleboard court has been 
covered with a tarp to protect the court from the winter weather. Ms. Doherty also 
reported that DPW Director Jack Rodriquenz has been by the Eagle House taking 
measurements for the proposed parking lot near the playground.  
 Town of Lunenburg Senior Tax Work-Off Program: Ms. Doherty reported the 
committee has received six applications, two of which are new people to the 
program. The committee will meet in January to discuss the applications. New 
positions for the program include monitoring in the schools, and a minute taker for 
town boards and commissions.  
 Director Grade and Step Increase: Ms. Doherty informed the board that at Special 
Town Meeting, Selectman Michael Ray Jeffrey proposed to amend the warrant that 
was read at Town Meeting to reflect what was written in the warrant that was 
distributed to households before the meeting, which put the Council on Aging’s 
director position at a Step 14. The amendment was approved by the Town. 
 Memory Café Lending Library: Ms. Doherty reported that she purchased books 
written specifically for elders with dementia with grant money from the Greater 
Lowell Community Foundation and the Community Foundation of North Central 
Massachusetts. The concept of the library is to make it available at the Memory

the following month. Ms. O’Sullivan inquired whether there would be books 
available to the caregivers as well.  
 Lunenburg Care and Kinship Group - Grandparents Raising Grandchildren: Ms. 
Doherty reported the first meeting of the support group will take place at the Eagle 
House on January 27, 2020 at 10:00 a.m. 
 Upcoming Events: Ms. Doherty briefly went over upcoming events scheduled at the 
Eagle House in January. The January Newsletter will highlight the differences 
between the Council on Aging, the Eagle House Supporters, and the Lunenburg 
Friendly Seniors. Also included in the upcoming newsletter is a handout with facts 
concerning real estate tax deferment information for seniors. 
 
EAGLE HOUSE SUPPORTERS REPORT: In Ms.Lincoln’s absence, Ms. Burchfield reported that the Eagle 
House Supporters are currently running their ping pong ball fifty/fifty raffle, which will be drawn at the

that have been labeled as belonging to the Supporters. Ms. Burchfield also mentioned that the Eagle 
House Supporters’ Board has two vacancies.

new member of their board since former COA member Jacquelyn Dwyer resigned. Ms. Doherty replied

On vote, motion carried unanimously.
