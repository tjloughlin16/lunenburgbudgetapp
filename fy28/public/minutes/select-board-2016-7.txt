1. Change Orders for Middle/High School Project
Supt. Calmes reviewed the school budget revision requests based on change orders and some line item transfers.
She reported the original amount before the construction contingency changes was $4,170,774. The remaining
amount after change orders is $2,982,048. There will be money remaining after the project ends.
Change Order #14 includes adding cabinet unit heaters on the second floor in Area B, totaling $15,834. Also
added was an outlet for a digital screen in the weight room in the amount of $888. Convectors in locker rooms
were relocated due to accessibility issues ($3,680) Ploor finish changes around the control booth in the main
mechanical and electrical rooms were done ($1401) and a boulder wall around the concession stand retaining
wall was installed ($10,039) Totaling $31,842.00
Change Order #15 reworks the wood wall cap around the auditorium wing walls for added railing ($12,244);
furnish and install alternate fin tube radiator covers around A-B and B-D connectors ($3,835); provide elevator
operator for owner furniture delivery due to delays caused by the Verizon strike ($6,632) and over
excavation, crushed stone import and added foundation drainage ($2021) Totaling $24,732.
Change Order #16 addresses post occupancy changes; athletic field resizing ($25,619), owner requested MB
and TB changes ($13,535), Stair #2 open riser infill panels ($3,574), added glass around Athletic Director's
office ($2,227), casework and flag holder additions ($15,419) added wall clocks ($4,240), added signage in
various locations ($5,526) Totaling $70,140.00
Change Order #17 Added a Radio Repeater System for Police Department ($34,437) and Added Auditorium
Wheelchair Seating Rails ($3,632) Totaling $38,069
Change Order #18 added plantings around Oak Avenue property line ($6,415) and added roof walk-off mats
($15,366) Totaling $21,781
Change Order #20 is an Other Project Transfer Budget Revision Request in the amount of $6000. for additional
commissioning of HRU units (air cooling)
Change Order #21 was for additional cabinets in the amount of $1530.00
Change Order #22 was due to architect's amendment #11 for resizing the athletic field in the amount of $1794.

The meeting commenced at 6:30 p.m.
Mr. Tale presented a base contract provided by Labor Attorney Mare Terry for the Town Manazateurs
contract. He had met with Ms. Lemieux earlier and had an idea of what she was interested in. Mr. Toale read
aloud some points Attorney Terry had made about the contract.
Ms. Lemieux is interested in a $60.00 monthly cell phone allowance, at least four weeks of vacation and
payment for her professional development (dues, subscriptions, meetings etc.). The Board determined a limit of
$5000 annually for the professional development.
Mr. Toale noted a "cooperation" clause in the contract that states she would provide information or testify as to
her actions as Town Manager subsequent to her working as Town Manager and to be reimbursed per diem for
expenses associated with her testimony and/or cooperation.
Discussion centered on Ms. Lemieux's request to have four, preferably five weeks' vacation. Consensus was to
settle for 20 days of vacation for the first year, 22 days the second year and 25 days the third year.
Mr. Toale stated Ms. Lemieux preferred 180 days' notice from the Board if her contract is not renewed.
The Board moved on to salary. Ms. Lemieux is seeking $120,000 annually. Discussion ensued over the amount.
The Board decided to offer $118,000. Mr. Toale will send an offer letter with the above discussed details.
Discussion turned to performance evaluations and incentives. It was decided to place wording in the contract to
amend the base salary annually depending on performance evaluations and other criteria.

Discussion of Hiring a Stormwater Expert
Ms. Luck stated she has attended many stormwater workshops and one recommendation is to look at what you
are planning to pave and determine what stormwater issues can be economically addressed to further the
mission of preserving the integrity of the roadways, reduce road flooding and eliminate some of pollutants from
entering the waterways by redirecting storm water. She stated she had reason to believe by setting aside less
than 10% of the funds that have been targeted for storm water for an engineer would enable them to develop a
plan to address this issue. She believed a plan such as this would allow the town to apply these efforts to fulfill
the requirements of the new Ms4 permit, demonstrate to the townspeople that we are serious about stormwater
management and provide a model of how successful stormwater management improves our roadways. This
would provide a real-time assessment of stormwater management that we can use at budget time. She
introduced Mike Clark from DCI Engineering and stated he would like to address the Board.
Mr. Clark stated he started working with the town on the new Ms4 permit. You have funding set aside for
repaving and you want to dedicate some of that toward stormwater management so you don't have to return
later and tear up new roads. There is design and permitting but there is also implementation and procurement.
Traditionally you would rely on the engineer for a standard design. There may be other alternatives to doing
that, but at this point he didn't know enough about the watershed and paving plans to determine that. He also
hasn't talked to the DPW Director so he didn't know where he would stand on this. Conservation Commission
is also involved in this process.
===PAGE 7===
Ms. Bertram stated this Board will be holding a workshop meeting to discuss pavement management, identify
stormwater issues on the planned roads and discuss the Complete Streets Program. She noted he is researching
what has been done up to the present regarding the Ms4 permit process. Is this information going to be helpful
in determining future stormwater improvements? Mr. Clark stated he just signed a contract with the town last
week. As we go through the permit process, there is definitely information there. Another key issue is to look at
the pavement management plan.
Mr. Ebersole stated he thought procurement at this point was too early. While he supports the concept, he
wanted to see the information we already have and participate in the pavement management plan/streets
workshop first with the DPW Director. Ms. Bertram agreed and thought a review of the data we already have
was the first step.
Mr. Clark stated from a planning view you look at all the existing state and local GIS mapping and identify
where water is being discharged. Then the Town can prioritize locations and strategize.
Mr. Toale stated there is a stormwater task force that is addressing that, a pavement management plan and there
is the Conservation Commission. All of them intersect at various points. Who do we want coming to the
workshop and what is the desired outcome of it?
Ms. Bertram stated there are the pavement management plan and the Complete Streets program. We need to
know which roads are coming up for paving and what the drainage issues are and what the proposal is to
address them. That is the stormwater piece. The second piece is the Complete Streets Program and a discussion
about whether it fits Lunenburg.
Mr. Ebersole stated he saw this as expanding and enhancing the pavement management plan. Further discussion
ensued
DPW Director Rodriquenz stated when this plan was formed we estimated the pavement and stormwater
improvement costs, which was difficult to do. $500,000 was dedicated to stormwater. We consider the
subsurface utilities; we always considered these types of things, especially drainage. This is something
we've been doing all along. In terms of soil conditions, how the roads work, the condition of the structures, he
believed he was the best person to make that decision. Where we really need help with is to review existing
conditions which is time consuming and labor intensive. We combine the structure analysis with the cleaning of
the catch basins so we know what we are doing before we pave.
Another component of stormwater is where the water goes and many times it affects private property. We need
to work with residents and get neighborhoods involved. We don't have easements. He has been working with an
experienced certified horticulturist and other companies on rainwater gardens and the local micro-climate.
There are a lot of things in process.
Mr. Ebersole stated when we get to paving we should advertise it so people are aware. We can talk to property
owners about granting an easement for placing a rain garden. Ms. Bertram stated this is the kind of dialogue we
want to have at the workshop. Mr. Toale suggested scheduling this as soon as possible after the new Town
Manager starts, probably February. Ms. Luck thanked Mr. Clark for coming in this evening.
There was no further public comment.

Tom Bertram, 312 Townsend Harbor Road, presented a report on the Lunenburg Snow Riders Club trail work.
He began with the 2013 Recreational Trails Program (RTP) grant amounting to $24,259. The RTP grant is
issued through the Department of Recreation and Conservation (DCR) and is collected through a federal gas
tax. The club completed erosion mitigation on the Cowdry Lot parcel, specifically Old Tote Road and Muskrat
Swamp Trail areas. They also built a handicapped viewing station and a kiosk at the request of the Conservation
Commission at the Harold Harley Site. They purchased trail grooming equipment. The club created a connector
trail between the Cowdrey lot and the small town forest. One remaining task on the 2013 grant is to install
gravel for trail maintenance and install drainage on the large town forest trails.
The 2015 RTP grant in the amount of $34,799 is almost entirely dedicated to the Lane Property off Holman
Street. The club constructed a parking lot at the Lane Property, built five footbridges along Mulpus Brook and
Proctor and Ramsden have all been instrumental in donating equipment and time.
A multi-purpose bridge over the Mulpus was constructed. This bridge is big enough to get machines across for
maintenance and access out to the town forest.

seeking a proclamation issued from this Board.
