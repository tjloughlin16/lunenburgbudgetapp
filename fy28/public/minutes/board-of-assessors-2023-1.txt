Minutes from the BOA (Board of Assessors) meeting held: January 31, 2023
Location: In person at Lunenburg Town Hall and broadcast over the Lunenburg Public Access channel
Members present: Interim Chairperson, Christine Higdon, Board Member, Kevin Fish. Also present
Principal Assessor CJ Carroll and Rena Swezey.
Rich Letarte in attendance by cell phone.
Christine opened the meeting at 2:42 pm and stated before addressing items on the agenda, she will
open for public comment.
Open Meeting:
• Christine had members introduce themselves.
• Kevin Fish made a statement in regards to abatement procedures and communication with the public.
• Lou Franco introduced himself and explained in advance that his statement to follow is entirely his own
words and he is not speaking on behalf of the Select Board. Lou spoke of the abatement process and
addressed recent incorrect information and innuendo that had appeared on various private town related
social media pages. In closing, Lou asked that the BOA make liberal use of their ability to extend the
deadline of filing abatements.
• Several residents spoke stating their dissatisfaction, questions and concerns regarding tax bills and the
abatement process.
• Rich Letarte stated he does feel the assessments were done in accordance with the rules and
regulations of the Department of Revenue.
• Kevin made a motion to approve the use of the Massachusetts Appellate Tax Board Three-Year
Average Worksheet. Rich seconded. All in favor.
• The BOA reviewed motor vehicle abatement applications.
Adjournment:
• Meeting adjourned at 3:53 pm.

Location: In person at Lunenburg Town Hall and broadcast over the Lunenburg Public Access channel
Members present: Chairman, Richard H. Letarte, Board Members, Christine Higdon and Kevin Fish.
Also present Principal Assessor CJ Carroll, Rena Swezey and Administrative Assessing Assistant, Sheila
Craigen
Also present: Member of Lunenburg Select Board, Lou Franco
Lou Franco opened the meeting at 3:02 pm. For clarification, Lou explained it was determined and
announced at previous SB (Select Board) meetings that he will be serving as a facilitator of meetings of
the Lunenburg BOA, relative to the lakefront tax situation. Lou explained his limited functions working with
the BOA Chairman. Functions to include moving meetings through the agendas and moderating public
comment. With permission from the BOA Chairman, Lou may respond to questions or give opinions if
appropriate. Lou stated he does not have the power to make or second motions or to vote. Lou stated he
will not be involved in any executive sessions.
Christine spoke extensively with statements, questions and concerns regarding the decision of a
moderator to the BOA. Ultimately, Kevin made a motion to appoint Lou Franco as a temporary moderator,
to the BOA through the lakefront abatement process. Rich seconded the motion. Christine continued
conversation with concerns regarding the agreement. A new vote ensued with the outcome of 2 (two) in
favor and 1 (one) opposed to appoint Lou Franco as a temporary moderator with an amendment to the
agreement.
Open Meeting:
• Christine made a motion to approve the February 22, 2023 minutes. All in favor.
Public Comment
• Phyllis Luck had questions, stated concerns and gave opinions regarding the abatement process. Carl
Luck made comments to the BOA regarding the handling of the investigation. Carl also expressed
concerns regarding the agenda and the abatement process. John Rabbit had questions concerning
procedures and made suggestions for future handling.
Adjournment:
• Christine made a motion at 4:09 pm to enter into executive session and not to return into open session
after exiting executive session. All in favor.
Executive Session:
• Executive session minutes will not be released.

owners by the  BOA regarding extensions of their  abatements and  the  time  it  would take  for the  
BOA  to  review  those  abatement applications. Question  was asked  about public access  to  the  
BOA meetings. Mr. Letarte stated they  would have  to be limited to  20-30 minutes before the 
meeting due to  the  volume of applications . Our hands  are  tied  currently   because we  are 
awaiting for site visit reports from the inspectors.
Ms. Lemieux reported when she  spoke  with the  owner  of Municipal Finance Services  today 
they  said they've gone through 207  inspections so thus  far;  they're expected   to  do  another 60 
this  Friday and Saturday. He's working  on tables  for  recommendations  and they're  also  working

inspections. I think the  expectation is the  ones that have been done by April  23 rd .  I want to  
make  sure  to  clarify that  versus the  400  applications; they  haven't done  400  inspections  so  I 
wouldn't anticipate that  they  would have  that  done  by  April 23 rd .  That's  why  extension letters  
are  being  sent  out. So I think you're right they're halfway there.
Question  was  asked in  what  order the  inspections  are  being  done.  Ms.  Lemieux stated they  are  
going  out  in  both order  of receipt and mutual neighborhood areas. Mr.  Alonzo asked for  
clarification on  the  question regarding property owners being  able  to  attend the  abatement 
hearings. Mr. Letarte  stated it  could be  under a 30  minute public  comment  period at  the 
beginning  of  their  meeting.  Mr.  Alonzo  stated he  believed these  owners  wanted to attend  
the  executive  session  portion  that  addresses their  abatement application  versus  making  a  
public  comment. Mr. Letarte  stated we  had  entertained doing that, but  did not think it was 
possible due to time  constraints.
The  BOA  discussed the  possibility  of  allowing  property  owners  into  executive  session  
individually  and  noted even  two or three minutes per  owner,  which is unrealistic, would 
amount to  an additional 15 hours, thus extending time  out needed to review all  400 
applications. Their  plan was  to review  60-100 applications per week.  Town  Manager  
Lemieux advised whatever  they decided to  be  consistent through the   entire  process.
The  Select  Board  concurred with  her  statement.  Several  scenarios  were  discussed on the  
abatement process according to the now assumed mailing date  of the tax bills.
The  board moved  on to  the  general  framework  of the  investigation.  Mr.  Jeffreys  read  
excerpts  from  a  document detailing the investigation.
