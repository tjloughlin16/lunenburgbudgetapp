occurred since the last meeting. Jack stated that i/l and metering has been fairly well-covered by the discussions with Mr. Pederson of Weston & Sampson.
in the workshop, he handed out a disk for the members on a program called FOG, for Fats, Oils and Grease control, put out by the New England Interstate
Water Pollution Control Commission. After review of the information ourselves, we could share it with the residents in a televised meeting. It is a 4-step
program. First, determining where we are. We have a list of 23 non-residential kitchens in Town. Jack and Corey Dowd (sewer technician) will then work
with the plumbing inspector and the Board of Health to visit each location, ensure grease traps installed and up-to-code. 3d step is to inspect and verify there
is a maintenance program in place, and provide those locations with a checklist to self-monitor. Last step is public education - the CD has great slides,
brochures available for the public. Jack pointed out that just 1 tsp a month in 10,000 homes equates to 70 barrels of grease. It only takes a little bit from
each house and grease is the killer of any sewer system. After investing $13 million in a sewer system, we have to be diligent in keeping it working- both the
Town and its residents share responsibility. Bob reviewed his conversation with Loxi Jo Calmes, Superintendent of Schools, as to the maintenance of the
school grease traps, and that currently they are cleaned only once a year and that Bob pointed out it needs to be done more frequently than that. We will be
getting back to the schools regarding the maintenance of the traps now that school is back in session. Bob stressed that with a good maintenance program,
we may avoid the expense of an external grease trap system installation for $5-25,000 per unit per school. He noted that Sean Patricks had installed a new
grease trap with their recent expansion. And that he had attended the last Board of Health meeting and explained our process to them. He provided a
sample grease trap regulation, provided by Dave MaDonald. The Board of Health is looking into this as well and wants to work with the Sewer
Commission. As Jack had mentioned, we need to coordinate with the Plumbing Inspector and Board of Health and also the Building Inspector for changes
of use. Bob noted we are still in the investigation stage and learning what each of the Town departments are currently doing. Dave thanked Jack and offered

Fitchburg IMA Inputs to BOS by Sewer Commission
December 12, 2010
Background:
1. Current IMA remains in effect till March 11, 2019
2. Lunenburg does not have sufficient capacity to support areas already bettered that
have right to connect now and no ability to sewer new areas as defined in CWMP
that would go to Fitchburg in the future
a. With Highteild commitment and I/I uncertainty, we are out of capacity but
~1/3 of relevant bettered properties are not yet connected
3. There have been several fact find discussions between Fitchburg's Joe Jordon and
Sewer Commission contractor Weston and Sampson. No agreements were made
nor were tough issues addressed to any meaningful extent. (see memos from W/S
dated Feb. 25, 2010, March, 11, 2010 and April 13, 2010)
Goal:
1. Secure additional capacity to cover future needs of areas already bettered plus
future needs per CWMP of ~ 250,000 gpd. Spit between Summer and 2A TBD.
2. Improve rate paid (Fitchburg's "fee" of $1.65 per 100 cf is biggest contributor to
rate difference re:Léominster at $3.60)
3. Provide protection against funding major capital that is not driven by, nor benefit
Lunenburg (such as separation of sewer and storm water drainage which is big
issue for Fitchurg)
4. Improve the document in a number of contractual and legal areas.
Major Negotiation Points/Recommendations
1. Contractual details: there are many areas that the base contract has been updated
and improved as can be seen in the change history. These are too numberous to be
• addressed here but should be reviewed and understood by negotiating team.In this
briefing document we only focus on the key operational/business issues.
2. Capacity: Secure agreement that accounts and secures future capacity required per
CWMP at build out through 2026 of ~250,000 gpd. Spit between Summer and 2A
TBD (see para. 12.1)
3. Funding of John Fitch Highway line upgrade and capacity increase: Fitchburg
wants Lunenburg to define it's future capacity needs and is willing to set this
aside, without a sunset date, as long as Lunenburg agrees to pay their proportional
share up front. This is not ari unreasonable approach as it exchanges a guarantee
of future capacity, for an up front payment. We have left this in our draft response
but we recommend it be removed quickly if other discussions on rate basis do not
go well. This issue is at the heart of why we need a riew IMA, securing capacity.
(see para. 12.1)
4.
Elimination of $1.65 rate fee adder: We have removed this from current rate as
starting point of negotiations. This is same as in Westminster's IMA with
Fitchburg. They will argue history and uniformity. We would argue that it needs
to be justified and is not in other IMA's we have, notably Leominster without
saying it. (see para, 17.1)

5. Base rate equal to Fitchburg resident rate: need to break down cost elements to
validaté we are only paying fair share and not paying for improvements to
Fitchburg system that ate not required or benefit Lunenburg. Big issue is need for
Fitchburg to separate sewerage from drainage. This major capital cost should not
find it's way into Lunenburg's rate. (see para. 6.1, 6.2, 17.3 and 17.4)
6. Automatic rate increase equal to Fitchburg residents: same issue and ref as point 4
above, should only include costs that address applicable infrastructure or benefits
Lunenburg in some way. Need to make sure we get chance to review and dispute
before implemented (see para. 6.1, 6.2, 17.3 and 17.4)
7. Metering: Current and proposed IMA uses meter at town line to determine
volume. Currently we use water usage and propose a five year period to get
cotrect/accurate metering in place. At end of period, we propose to revisit method
and even relevant rate before changing method. Currently Fitchburg residents pay
based on water usage, not output. In this way current billing method for
Lunenburg is consistent with Fitchburg billing: If Lunenburg output is measure
for billing, then we are paying separately for any I/I whereas it is included in
resident rate. This would double bill Lunenburg and therefore the base rate would
need to have I/I portion removed. Should be easy enough to determine ration of
water in verses volume processed, i.e. portion of rate associated with VI. (see
para. 10.1 and 12.2)
8. Transparency in cost accounting: to assure above items are visible and Lunenburg
has the ability to validate rate increases, Fitchburg must collect costs and share
them with Lunenburg in a regular and timely manor to allow time for review and
appeal. This theme is in several places in revised draft. Key is to separate
operational and capital based cost impacts. (see para. 6.1 and 6.2)
9.. Tri Town: Fitchburg seems motivated to put this back under Lunenburg control.
One advantage to them is they can not lien the property in case of payment issues.
This is one of our few areas to provide incentive to Fitchburg, hold in reserve as
long as possible. What can we get for taking on this burden/cost

d. Full/partial offset for John Fitch upgrade
e. Future concession, waive connection fees equal to fees collected from
development.

Fitchburg proposal. Fitchburg will need time to review and digest before
meaningful discussions can happen.
11. Negotiations support: the Sewer Commission has invested in engineering inputs
to date but feel the next discussions should be between Fitchburg and
representatives of the BOS. We also propose that a member of the Sewer
Commission be an integral part of these negotiations. Unless, and until Fitchburg
involves technical representatives into this meeting we do not propose the our

Proposed Negotiation Strategy Comments/Recommendations
1. We have time, there is no immediate issue driving a new IMA and we should not
appear: to be under pressure to do so
2. Offer to take over Tri Town only after all other issues are put on the table and
agreement sought. This is one of our few negotiation advantages, should be used
carefully
3. Seek a fair agreement in which Lunenburg pays its fair share of costs plus a
reasonable fee to compensate Fitchburg for the commitment of future capacity
and access to their infrastructure.
4. Take bottoms up, clean sheet approach. All elements are open for discussion
including but not limited to; rates, capital improvements, capacity, metering..etc

even if only one party benefits

and including declaring an impasse. In parallel, do detail homework on
implementation time/cost and only when fully understood, re-open negotiations
with Fitchburg. Other than vaguely alluding to this at this point of negotiations is
not recommended
