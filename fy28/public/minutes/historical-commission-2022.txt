LUNENBURG HISTORICAL COMMISSION MINUTES 
FEBRUARY 15, 2022 
 
Meeting called to order at 7pm with a quorum present, no public present. Secretary Jennifer Sanderson 
read previous minutes with a unanimous vote to accept. 
No new correspondence.  No public comment. 
NEW BUSINESS:  Chairman Richard McGrath has submitted our annual report, and will be forwarding 
copies to the other members. 
OLD BUSINESS: The Attorney General has received our by-law revisions, and we are waiting for approval. 
In March the commission will review the town by-laws and will determine whether any changes in 
wording or any updates are required. 
Megan will reach out to the Fitchburg Historical Society regarding African American history in 
Lunenburg. Jennifer Sanderson has only been able to find vague references to the Savage Family in the 
Lunenburg archives. 
Once the ground thaws in (hopefully) April/ curbing will be placed at the Litch Memorial.  Richard 
Mcgrath has retooled the lettering. The bonnets need to be set, and lead flashing will be placed to help 
preserve the monument.  
Regarding 30 School St., plans remain in effect to demolish said structure. A public hearing is scheduled 
for 3/10 at the Ritter. The APDC will vote to either accept or reject the proposal. 
The Facebook page of the APDC is going well. A list of endangered properties in the district will be made 
public, including 30 School St., the Brooks House, 53 Lancaster, the Old Methodist Steeple, and the 
Town Pound. 
In regards to the K House survey, a ten page attachment on every building needs to be professionally 
done. 24 need to be surveyed, but are outside the expertise of any boardmember. Bill Lakso will make 
inquiries to the Mass Historical Society in hopes of getting an estimate. 
Next meeting March 8th. Motion to adjourn.

LUNENBURG HISTORICAL COMMISSION 
MINUTES OF MEETING 
APRIL 5, 2022 
Meeting called to order at 7:10 by Chairman Richard McGrath with a quorum present. No public 
present. Minutes of February 15th meeting were read by Secretary Jennifer Sanderson and 
unanimously accepted. 
No new correspondence, no public present. 
NEW BUSINESS:  Richard McGrath has received a talent bank form from William Loyd of 
Highland St. Mr. Loyd is a member of the US Airforce, with a background in construction and 
civil engineering in addition to human resources and project management. A copy of the agenda 
was sent to him by Richard, but he is not present. 
AFRICAN AMERICANS IN LUNENBURG:  Richard has been in contact with the architect 
who has worked with both the Medford and Townsend Historical Societies who informed him 
that the census of 1740-1760  has all slaves listed in Mass.Mass was the first colony to outlaw 
slavery in 1783. Negroes are identified by the letter C in these censuses. 
LITCH CEMETARY: Richard will be finishing up over the course of the next few weeks, at 
which point we will take photos to accompany a press release.  
Not much public attendance for the APDC CTA meeting about 30 School St. Strong support in 
favor of tearing it down. The general consensus is that it is beyond repair, and only going to 
attract more vandalism. The town never properly mothballed the property, which would have 
improved its current condition. The vote has been postponed for several weeks. The Selectboard 
has complained that the APDC is violating the rules by continuing to advocate for saving the 
building. Will go to town meeting in November. 
K HOUSE SURVEY:  Jennifer Dougherty has suggested that we check the Mass Historical 
Preservation website for names of consultants, and send out an inquiry detailing our needs.  
Richard spoke with Ryan Hayward of the Medford Society and learned that several planning 
grants are coming up. 
Still nothing happening with 68 Elm St.  
Motion to adjourn. Next meeting May 3,2022

LUNENBURG HISTORICAL COMMISSION 
SEPTEMBER 8, 2022 
MINUTES 
 
Chairman Richard McGrath called the meeting to order at 7:06 P.M. with a quorum present. 
Minutes of the June meeting were read and unanimously voted approved. 
There was no correspondence or public comment. 
Looking to the future activities of the Commission, Mr. McGrath plans to draft notes to present 
to other chairpersons of commissions and committees, explaining the Community Preservation 
Act, in hopes that the town will favorably consider adopting it. 
The Commission’s budget of $3,000.00 was discussed, including a suggestion that part or all of 
it might be designated toward the town’s support of the survey of K-houses, where $12,500.00 of 
the $25,000.00 cost would be reimbursed upon completion. 
On the same subject, Mr. McGrath introduced the question if Freedom’s Way might be 
approached for a grant. 
The director of the DPW has taken an interest in the Arnold-Litch burial ground, and arranged a 
ground penetrating radar procedure, which disclosed the exact location of burial sites.  As a 
result, the partially completed memorial will need to be relocated.  In addition, the landowner has 
offered to donate the land occupied by the site to the town. 
The Goodrich burial site was also surveyed by GPR, locating six unmarked graves. A suitable 
memorial will be a possibility in the future. 
The meeting was adjourned at 7:20 P.M. 
 
Jennifer Sanderson
