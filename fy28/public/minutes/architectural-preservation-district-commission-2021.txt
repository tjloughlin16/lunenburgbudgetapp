Meeting Minutes 
Tuesday January 14th, 2020 
Ritter Building 
Call to Order: At 7:06PM with all members present. 
Review of Minutes:  
Minutes from the meeting on December 17th were read and approved. 
Correspondence: 
Elaine Peterson called for report for the 2019 town Report.  She set a firm Deadline Feb 15th 
Old Business: 
Old Primary School: 
LaVeck reported that the Select Board approved funds to stop the deterioration of the Old Primary School.  Dwyer will contact the 
Town Manager and ask what the plan is to utilize those funds. 
McGrath Joined Mass Historic’s Online Forum, and received many responses regarding the reuse of similar buildings.  Maynard 
reused a similar building for elderly housing.  Beverly also converted a similar building for elderly housing and other community 
spaces.  They put out to bid with deed restrictions on preservation of exterior and interior features.  The use was not stipulated in 
the call for bids, but preference stated.  The town sold the property to the new developer.  McGrath will assemble the responses 
and the committee will prepare an educational presentation on the possible re-use of buildings like the Old Primary School to 
educate and inspire the public of Lunenburg. 
Monday Jan 27th, 6:30 at town hall the APDC is on the Planning Board’s agenda to discuss the future of the Brooks House.   The 
APDC will advise the planning board to subdivide the property and sell the house in place, without deed restrictions, for use as a 
dwelling or mixed-use building.  McGrath will approach the board of library trustees to see if they would be willing to cede a park-
ing space from their lot for use at the Brooks House. 
LaVeck reported that there were some capitol planning requests for the interior of Ritter Building. 
APDC Signs:  
McGrath got the design of the new signs to Jack Rodriquenz, who will order them and install them in the spring.  A map and de-
scription of sign locations was delivered to the DPW and Town manager. 
New Business: 
LaVeck announced that his term of service would expire this year, and that he did not intend to re-apply. 
Inventory of contributing properties: 
Dwyer will take a look in his old files and try to find a list of contributing properties, which would serve as a check-list for the bi-
annual inspection of the APD 
Next Meeting: Tuesday, February 11th, 7:00PM 

===PAGE 2===
Adjourned: With no further business before it, and APDC adjourned at 7:47PM

Meeting Minutes 
Wednesday April 28th, 2021 
Meeting held Remotely 
Call to Order: At 7:04PM with McGrath, Dwyer and Nicastro in attendance. 
Review of Minutes:  
Minutes from the March 24th meeting were read, Dwyer motioned to accept them as read and Nicastro seconded the motion, 
which carried. 
CTA Applications: 
None 
Correspondence: 
McGrath wrote to Matt Alison of the planning board regarding the next steps to be taken with regard to the Brooks house, and 
also to solicit the planning board’s opinion on warrant article 24 of the upcoming ATM. (Article 24 proposes using town meeting to 
appeal decisions made by the APDC.)  So far there has been no response. 
The APDC received two letters from Rhonda Lisio which were read: one objecting to warrant article 24, and another criticizing 
the Select Board for its uncompromising stance regarding the demolition of the Old Primary School. 
Selectmen Jeffries submitted a long, clear and thoughtful letter offering perspective on the situation with the Old Primary School, 
and the circumstances surrounding Article 24. 
James LaVeck submitted a letter objecting to Article 24. 
Secretary Brian Corcoran submitted a letter notifying the APDC that his term will end in June this year, and that due to his busy 
schedule he will not seek reinstatement.  McGrath wrote back to him acknowledging his resignation and thanking him for his 
service. 

===PAGE 2===
Old Business: 
APD signs: 
As requested, the DPW moved the APD sign on Oak avenue to the correct location, and replaced the missing sign on the east 
side of Massachusetts Avenue.  However, now another sign has gone missing on the west end of Massachusetts Avenue. Mc-
Grath has notified that DPW that it needs to be replaced. 
Old Primary School: 
Chairman McGrath took a moment to review his extensive credentials as a stone mason and brick layer, and concluded with his 
opinion that the masonry structure of the Old Primary School is in fair condition, and that its demolition is not necessary. 
Despite its uncertain future, Dwyer has been completing a Form B to submit to the Mass Historical Commission for recognition of 
the Old Primary School as a registered contributing structure to the Lunenburg Historical District.  He requested any additional 
resources that might give background to the buildings historical significance, as well as advise on what architectural style it 
should be listed as. 
New Business: 
Article 24: 
Discussion followed regarding Article 24, and it was agreed that the APDC would oppose it.  There was some confusion, be -
cause Elaine Peterson had forwarded an original and amended version of the article.  It appears that the original version was 
printed on the warrant.  The original version creates a special appeals process just for town-owned buildings, and the revised 
version opened that process to the owner of any building in the APD.  Dwyer agreed to review the recorded meeting and prepare 
a statement that included the salient points that had been discussed in opposition the warrant article. 
Public Comment: 
At the end of the meeting, Rhonda Lisio joined to verbally express her objection to article 24.  She believed that the Select Board 
intended to change the article to the revised version on the day of Town Meeting. 
Next Meeting: May 26th, 2021, 7PM 
Adjourned: With no further business before it, and APDC adjourned at 8:32PM

Tuesday July 13th, 2021 
Joint Meeting with Select Board, Town Hall 
Call to Order: At 7:00PM with McGrath, Dwyer and Nicastro in attendance.  Town manager Lemieux and all members of the 
Selelct Board were also present: Alonzo, Jeffries, Franco, Dwyer and Marino. 
Discussion of the disposition of the Old Primary School Building: 
Alonzo began by explaining that the Select board was about to draft a Request for Proposal for a landscape architect to design a 
town park on the Old Primary School property, as per resolution at Town Meeting.  He said that the wishes of the town expressed 
at official meetings made it clear that the people wished to keep the land as town property, and that a park was the most popular 
way to use the land.  Alonzo personally opined that with the new town hall project approaching, the people would not be willing to 
fund the full renovation of the Old Primary School, and that if outside funds were available for such a project, the Select Board 
would need to know about that now. 
Jeffries said that the building had served its purpose, and only had sentimental value to certain people who had attended school 
there. It should be demolished and the town supports that view. 
Franco agreed that the building had no real historical value. 
McGrath reviewed his credentials as an operative mason, and also reviewed the extensive history of school building re-use in 
Lunenburg and neighboring towns.  He then reviewed the history of Demolition by Neglect and the APDC’s efforts to persuade 
the Select Board to maintain the building.  He advocated maintenance and reuse. 
Alonzo defended the Select Board’s neglect, saying the funds were not readily available, and that he did not see much will from 
the town to spend funds on its preservation.  He reviewed the history of attempts by Mr. Jewel to convert the building to elderly 
housing before the economic downturn dried up the HUD funding. 
Jeffries approached state delegation and inquired about available funds for rehabilitation of the building.  He found that most of 
the state funds were for conversion of buildings to housing for towns with larger populations.  Other funds may be available from 
smaller institutions for smaller amounts. 
McGrath defended the historical value of the Old Primary, saying that ordinary people were just as important to the town’s history 
as the notables. 
Cullen Dwyer argued that the building had irreplaceable historical architectural value beyond its purely functional value.  He con-
fessed that support for its reuse had little voice in the public forum. 
General discussion followed supporting compromise, cooperation, and the desire to find a path forward that is agreeable to all 
parties. The possibility of converting it to an open-air pavilion was discussed, and Alonzo invited the APDC to be a part of the 
RFP process. However, Jeffries dissented saying that he would only agree with the cheapest option, and selectman Dwyer con-
curred.  McGrath cautioned that the APDC would be constrained by the language of the bylaw, and Alonzo encouraged the 
APDC to review that bylaw before contributing to the park design process. 
Adjourned: The joint workshop adjourned at 8:15PM
