Town of Lunenburg 
Bylaw Review Committee 
Ritter Hall 
Meeting Minutes 
Thursday May 12, 2016, 6pm 
1.  Call to Order: Meeting called to order 6:15pm 
2.  Attending: Rob Bowen, Nancy Gray, Cathy Hennessy, Michelle Smith, Kathy Herrick 
3.  Public Comment: None 
4.  Minutes from April 28 th- -one typo correction. Chair entertained motion to approve 
minutes with typo corrected. Second by C. Hennessy. All attending voted to approve 
minutes. 
5.  K. Herrick stated no comments received from outreach letter requesting feedback from 
the various departments on the ByLaws. 
6.  Chair spoke with building inspector on Art. 5 Sec. 8 provision on erecting signs. No 
action taken .  
a.  Both the Chair and the Land Use Director feel Art. 5 Sec. 13  should be covered 
under Zoning Bylaw. This statement was followed by a motion. 
b.  Motion to repeal Art 5 Sec 13 . Second by N. Gray. All attending voted 
unanimously to repeal . 
7.  Chair spoke with Director of Department of Public Works (Jack). Article 5 Section 2  
question whether it addressed town right of way to extend beyond paved ways. Director 
recommends expanding this to include ‘right of way’  as well. Chair recommended the 
committee ask Town Counsel for review. 
8.  Chair spoke with Land Use Director (Adam) on Article 7 . This has been revamped by 
other towns to regulate: Leave permitting to Board of Selectmen (B.O.S.). Under review 
by Land Use Director.  
9.  Chair spoke with the Chief of Police who will review and revise fines within Article 9 . 
10.  Art. 9 Sec. 9 and 10 . Earth Removal ordinance. The Land Use Director opined that the 
Planning Board may be a more appropriate permitting authority than the B.O.S. 
11.  Next task is to summarize all recommendations for submission to B.O.S. from meeting 
minutes held to date by Secretary. 
12.  Codification will be returned soon. Plan to share recommended changes via Interim 
Report to Board of Selectmen. Next meeting will be June 9. 
13.  Adjourn. Motion to adjourn by Chair at 6:55pm. Second by C. Hennessy. All attending 
voted to adjourn. Meeting adjourned.

Town of Lunenburg 
Bylaw Review Committee 
Recommendations June 9, 2016 
1.  General recommendation to compare Charter to ByLaws-this will be done via 
Codification. 
2.  Through Town Charter Section 7-7 (b), the Bylaw committee was impaneled and did 
a thorough By-Law review of all Lunenburg Town By-Law Articles. 
3.  Articles 1,3,4-No recommended changes at this time . 
4.  Articles 2 
a.  Bylaw committee recommendation  in Section 2  to a timeline for town 
committee minutes review and posting. Transmission of approved minutes 
should follow state law.  
b.  Article 2, Section 1 -recommend to add  “minutes must be approved in 30 
days and periodic review of executive session quarterly”.  
c.  Article 2, Section 6 -move to repeal  and reference Town Charter section 3-2 
part (c). 
d.  Article 2 Section 8 -move to repeal  and refer to conflict of interest 
Massachusetts General Law. 
e.  Article 2 Section 12 -move to repeal  collection of fees and licenses and refer 
to Massachusetts General Law 
5.  Article 5 
a.  Recommend amending  all language references to “man” or “men” to gende r 
neutral language.  
b.  Article 5 Section 2  Director recommends expanding this to include ‘right of 
way’. Committee requests Town Counsel review . 
c.  Article 5 Section 13 . Move to repeal . The Committee and the Land Use 
Director see this section is covered under Zoning Bylaw.  
6.  Article 6  
a.  The Town Manager has recommended to the committee to repeal Section 3 . 
7.  Article 7  
a.  Land Use Director opined that the Planning Board may be a more appropriate 
permitting authority than the B.O.S. No other recommendations for changes. 
8.  Article 8-Repealed previously and not applicable 
9.  Article 9: 
a.  Section 8 – Boating Laws – no recommended changes. 
b.  Section 9 and 10. Earth Removal ordinance. The Land Use Director opined 
that the Planning Board may be a more appropriate permitting authority than 
the B.O.S. 
===PAGE 2===
c.  Section 11 – Committee recommends reference change  from Superintendent 
of Highways to D.P.W. Director. 
d.  Section 12 and Section 15-move to repeal  and refer to Town Charter section 
3-2 (d) Appointments. 
e.  Committee recommends numbering change  “Section 23A” to “Section 24”. 
10.  Article 10 Section 3 fine amounts are under review  by the Chief of Police.  
11.  Article 11-Repealed previously and not applicable 
12.  Article 12  
a.  Sections 1 and Section 2 recommend repeal  as both sections are covered by 
the Town Charter sections 2-3 (b), (c) and 5-3.  
b.  Section 3 recommend repeal  as it conflicts with Town Charter. 
c.  Section 4 recommend review  by Town Counsel as section is referenced 
Town Charter Article 3-1 (f). 
13.  Article 16-Zoning enforcement under review  by Land Use Director. 
14.  Article 17-18-No recommended changes at this time . 
15.  Article 19-The Article that was voted in November 2015 Special Town Meeting will 
be inserted. 
16.  Article 20- No recommended changes at this time . 
17.  Article 21-Wetlands addressed at May 2016 Town Meeting. No further recommended 
changes. 
18.  Article 22-Committee recommends repeal   
a.  Section 1 Composition is contained in Town Charter section 3 – 6 (a)  
b.  Section 2 Powers and Duties is contained in Town Charter section 3 – 6 (b).  
19.  Articles 23-25- No recommended changes at this time  
20.  Article 26- Committee recommends repeal .  
a.  Sections 1 and 2 conflicts with Town Charter sections 3-14.  
b.  Section 3 conflicts with Town Charter Section 3-1 (a). 
21.  Article 27-Committee recommends change reference  from Town Charter section  
6-6 to 6-7. 
22.  Article 28-32- No recommended changes at this time . 
23.  ByLaw Committee understands Codification changes are in process and will be made 
to Board of Selectmen separately and were not under review by ByLaw committee.

Lunenburg Capital Planning Committee 
Meeting Minutes 
October 26, 2022 
 
Location: Ritter Building  
 
Present: Chairperson Tom Alonzo, Dave Passios, George Martin, Peter Beardmore, and Matthew 
Brenner. 
Guests: Town Manager Heather Lemieux, Lunenburg Schools Superintendent Dr. Kate Burnham, School 
Facilities and Grounds Steve Pusatari, School Committee Chairperson Carol Archambault, School 
Resource Officer Brad McNamara, Lunenburg Police Chief Thomas Gammell, Lunenburg Resident 
Rhonda Lissio 
Call to Order: Chairperson Tom Alonzo called meeting to order at 6:01 p.m., and read a statement 
regarding the Open Meeting Law and how all public meetings are legally being held remotely, and how 
to attend and participate in those meetings. 
Public Comment: none 
Presentation of Capital Planning Requests: School Department 
Dr. Burnham explained that last year’s capital request for the air conditioning at the primary 
school is not financially feasible.  
Dr. Burnham presented the schools’ ten-year plan for capital planning projects. Over the next 
ten years, the primary school seeks $618,500 in capital spending. Turkey Hill Elementary School 
will seek $15,056,822, which assumes contributions from the Massachusetts School Building 
Authority (MSBA), and possibly $2,200,000 for the MS/HS to upgrade the turf field, walls, and 
concession stand.  
Over the next ten years district-wide, the schools plan to spend $343,923 on safety film for 
windows, as well as a replacement mower, 2 pickup trucks, plows, and a passenger van.  
For FY24 Dr. Burnham prioritized the school department requests: 
1. Safety Window Film Districtwide $160,000 
2. Exterior Door Replacement at THES $160,000 
3. Access and Security upgrades at the primary school and THES $83,500, $131,000 
4. Upgrade Fire Alarm system at THES $287,000 
5. Athletic Complex feasibility study $50,000 
6. New pickup truck $68,000 
 
Safety and Security requests: 
 Safety Window Film: The school department hopes to prevent, or slow down an 
intruder with the safety film, also the film is tinted, which decreases solar glare 
and decreases cooling costs. 
 
Mr. Brenner asked whether schools are still considered the safest place for 
children to be, and about the cost benefit analysis of the protective film, as well 
Unanimously approved by Capital 
Planning Committee at 11/21/2022 
meeting.

as how well the tinting works as far as cooling is concerned. Mr. Brenner also

Mr. Beardmore opined that it’s worth the investment if there’s any chance of 
preventing an attack.  
Chief Gammell added that the protective film is only a part of a comprehensive 
plan, and pointed out that if one aspect can even slightly change the plan of an 
attacker, it gives first responders an opportunity for prevention.   
 
 Exterior Door Replacement: Dr. Burnham explained that THES is the only school 
in the district with just one door to go through in order to enter the building. All 
other schools have a double door with a vestibule area. Dr. Burnham explained 
that the current doors have antiquated locking mechanisms, which cause staff 
members the need to be vigilant and pull on the doors to make sure they are 
latched and locked. 
 
 Access and Security Upgrade THES and Primary School: New vestibule at THES, 
upgraded door access controls and alarm keypad (to match LMHS), change from 
numeric codes to access cards, additional and upgraded security cameras, 
additional motion detectors. Having all the same security system makes 
entering schools easier for faculty, and also first responders.  
 
Mr. Alonzo asked if there is an itemized list to describe the cost of each item 
when it comes to the amount of cameras, card readers, etc. Mr. Pusatari replied 
that a lot of the cost has to do with wiring for the project.  
 
Mr. Pusatari explained that a new system will make things like entering the 
building by first responders, and not needing to know separate codes for 
separate buildings much simpler. 
 
 THES Fire Alarm System upgrade: New, and additional smoke detectors, 
upgrade and add fire alarm pull stations, new fire alarm panel and system with a 
panel interface screen. Upgrading of the system will bring it into compliance 
with current code, and reduce risk of system failure due to age.  
 
Planning Request: 
 MS/HS Athletic Complex feasibility study: current conditions show sunken 
pavers, leaning fencing, leaning walls, and drainage issues on the track and turf 
field. The study will determine whether fixing the drainage issues is even 
possible, as well as adding restroom and storage capacity, and prioritizing what 
site remediation is needed.

maintenance plan.

Beardmore opined that it should be the Capital Planning Committee’s purview to look at alternative funding 
sources, and that the Town Manager should explain his or her intentions on how funds in the Special Purpose 
Stabilization account are to be spent.

Meeting Law and how all public meetings are legally being held remotely, and how to attend and participate in those 
meetings. Link to recorded meeting: https://youtu.be/3DtWeBgX5tE 
Public Comment:  
CPC Members: Mr. Beardmore made clear that he sees a new ambulance as a top priority, and also acknowledged 
that there is a request to fund that ambulance with some of the $3 million available in ARPA funds. Mr. Beardmore 
added that there is also $2 million dollars in the Special Purpose Stabilization fund, which he believes is for such 
purchases.  Mr. Beardmore pointed out that there is also a request to fund the radio equipment for the fire 
department with ARPA funding. Mr. Beardmore added that ARPA funds have been available for over a year, and the 
requests for the ambulance and radio equipment were made in late summer of 2022. Mr. Beardmore also 
commented on the DPW requests that were approved on Capital Plans from FY22 and FY23 for trucks that are on 
backorder, and suggested using funds currently available to order the other two trucks requested by the DPW now, 
instead of waiting for FY24, and to also purchase the ambulance with available funds, and remove those items from 
the FY24 Capital Plan requests. 
 
Public: Ms. Archambault thanked the committee for their discussion on the safety item requests for the school 
department, and asked that they carefully consider all the items, including the protective film for the windows on the 
schools, and cautioned the committee to make preparations now, and avoid finding the town in a position where they 
are wishing there could’ve been something done to prevent a tragedy. 
 
Discussion of Capital Planning Requests and Preliminary Prioritizations:  
Flat Hill Drainage Construction $240,000: Committee members agreed that this is an important project, but that more 
information is needed in order to justify the amount requested to spend. The main concern is where the water will be drained 
as it’s removed from the current site. 
Senior Center Parking Lot $150,000: Committee members acknowledged that the senior center needs more parking, but 
would like to see more information regarding the ADA Patio project, as well as a more definitive plan for the playground next 
to the proposed parking lot, which accounts for only eight parking spaces.  
Mr. Beardmore rated the project as one of the last items on the list, and pointed out that the amount of money 
requested for the project was submitted before the plans were drawn, and questioned whether $150,000 is a reliable 
estimate for the project. Mr. Alonzo, and Mr. Martin did not give the project a rating. Mr. Passios rated the project as 
number 16 out of 29 items, and clarified that he thinks the project has merit, but also agreed that more information 
would be reassuring, and would support a motion to remove the item from the plan. 
Ms. Lemieux commented that she thinks the parking lot is important as the location will provide access to the new 
patio, and help alleviate parking on Memorial Drive, which has been a problem for several years. Ms. Lemieux added 
that a plan for the lot has been provided to the committee, and she will seek answers on the question of how 
drainage will tie in with the patio.  
Approved by Capital Planning Committee 
at Dec. 28, 2022 meeting.

Brooks House $130,000: Committee members agreed that if the town can’t decide what to do with the Brooks House, it’s 
challenging to justify putting money into the proposed projects. Mr. Beardmore added that given the recent change in charge 
of the Municipal Building Design Committee, putting money into the Brooks House now, before knowing what that committee 
will determine regarding the Brooks House does pose a challenge. Ms. Lemieux reminded committee members that Town 
Meeting did support selling the Brooks House, but no advertisement has been published.  
Present members current rankings: 
 
After some discussion on whether and how to rank items on the plan regarding pending information as well as additional 
funding sources, committee members agreed some would change their current rankings. Mr. Alonzo anticipates receiving Mr.

Bylaw Review Committee
September  4 , 2025  Minutes
Members present: Rob Bowen (Chair), Jen Duffy (Vice Chair), Caesar Nuzzolo (Clerk), 
Nancy Gray, Kathy Herrick
Call to Order
Mr. Bowen called the Bylaw Review Committee to order at 6:47PM.
Elect Officers
Mr. Nuzzolo nominated Mr. Bowen for the position of Chair. Mrs. Gray seconded. The 
Committee unanimously voted for Mr. Bowen for the position of Chair.
Mr. Nuzzolo nominated M s. Duffy for the position of Vice Chair. Mrs. Gray seconded. 
The Committee unanimously voted for Mrs . Duffy  for the position of Vice Chair.
Mr s. Gray nominated Mr. Nuzzolo  for the position of Clerk . Ms. Duffy  seconded. The 
Committee unanimously voted for Mr. Nuzzolo  for the position of Clerk .
General Discussion
Mr. Bowen brought up the Draft Financial Policies currently under review by the Town 
and that the draft policies should be reviewed to ensure alignment with the Charter and 
Bylaws. Mr. Bowen further stated he has done an initial review of these draft policies 
and that there were some inconsistencies. Mr. Bowen will raise these inconsistencies 
with the Town Manager.
The Committee reviewed the draft email to be sent out to 
Boards/Committees/Commissions to solicit feedback from them regarding their 
respective Bylaws. The Committee discussed a deadline to include  for this feedback 
and decided on December 1, 2025  and that the Committee can still begin a preliminary 
review of Bylaws until feedback is collected. Mr. Bowen will further review the letter  and 
Ms. Herrick will send it out to Boards/Committees/Commissions.
The Committee discussed the process by which they will follow to review Bylaws. The 
Committee decided it will begin with “Part 1: Administrative Legislation ” at their next 
meeting.
Ms. Herrick will print out  copies of the Bylaws and members can pick them up at her 
office.
===PAGE 2===
The Committee discussed how recommended Bylaw changes will be presented at Town
Meeting and that it depends on the nature of the recommended changes. Changes may 
be presented as an “omnibus ” package or be separated in separate recommendations 
to be voted on separately if determined that would be appropriate.
Set next meeting(s) date, time, place
The Committee decided to meet once a month for the remainder of the year and 
potentially more frequently next spring to prepare for Annual Town Meeting.
The Committee agreed that 6:45pm is a good time to hold meetings of the Committee.
Adjourn
Mr. Nuzzolo motioned to adjourn at 7: 26pm . Mrs. Gray seconded. The Committee voted 
unanimously to adjourn.

Bylaw Review Committee
October 2, 2025  Minutes
Members present: Rob Bowen (Chair), Jen Duffy (Vice Chair), Caesar Nuzzolo  (Clerk), 
Nancy Gray, Kathy Herrick
Call to Order
Mr. Bowen called the Bylaw Review Committee  to order at 6:47PM .
Public Comment
Mr. Bowen asked if there were any public comments. There were no public comments.
Update on Request to Town Committees and Stakeholders
The Committee reviewed  the email that will be sent out to 
Boards/Committees/ Commissions to solicit feedback on their respective bylaws.
Mr. Nuzzolo move d  to approve the language of the email and authorize  the  Town Clerk 
to send it on behalf of Committee . Ms. Duffy seconded the motion.
The motion passed unanimously.
Preliminary Review of Bylaws
The Committee began reviewing “Part  1 : Administrative Legislation ” of the Town ’s 
Bylaws.
The Committee discussed the Select Board ’s  recent vote to delegate enforcement 
authority of Chapter 232  Unregistered Vehicles to the Building Commissioner and 
whether or not  the Bylaw should be modified to reflect this . The Committee determined  
the Select Board should remain the enforcing authority since a vote delegating this 
authority could be undone.
The C ommittee discussed updating the header  in   the table under § 1-3 Noncriminal 
Disposition entitled “Enforcing Person ” to  “Enforcing Authority ” since some of the 
enforcing authorities are committees, not individuals.
===PAGE 2===
The Committee discussed the term “DPW Director or designated agent ” and whether or 
not  all enforcing authorities are able to  designate  authority. If this is the case  then the 
Committee agreed the term “or designated agent ” should be removed.
The Committee discussed the current fines and whether or not  they should be modified. 
The Committee agreed to reach out to enforcing authorities to ask.
The Committee discussed  the need to ensure  Chapter 12 Boards, Commissions and 
Committees  aligns with the contents of the Charter. The Committee discussed Chapter 
12 Article II Building Committee , specifically at which point in the building project does a 
Building Committee need to be created. The Committee also discussed  the Municipal 
Building Design Committee  and how it technically does not qualify as a “Building 
Committee ” defined in this bylaw, but rather a separate committee reviewing the overall 
building needs of the Town and agreed that no update to this section needed to be 
made to reflect this .
The Committee discussed Chapter 5 § 5-1  Records and minutes : noting the timelines 
defined for Boards/Committees/Commissions to submit their minutes to the Town Clerk.
The Committee discussed the need for Boards /Committees/Commissions to r eview  their
respective B ylaw s  and  recommend changes, however that  The Bylaw Review 
Committee should standardize language between the sections for all 
Boards/Committees/Commissions, particularly language around membership .
The Committee discussed whether or not  living in T own  was required for membership to
serve as a volunteer and whether or not  a B ylaw that sets minimum requirements of 
membershi p across all Boards/Committees/Commissions was needed. Ms. Duffy will 
review these sections and draft standardized language  to review next meeting .
The Committee discussed “o fficial minutes ” defined in Article I Town Clerk  §  60-6  and 
what exactly qualified a set of minutes as official.  The Committee discussed the need to 
ensure the Town maintains a backup of all agendas and minutes other than the Town 
website, as the data stored there could be lost someday.
The Committee discussed § 70-10  and replacing the word “bump ” with a more 
professional word. The Committee also discussed the use of terms such as “calendar 
day ” and “working day ” and the need to  go  through the Bylaw and ensure the correct 
terminology is used in each instance. The Committee discussed using terms such as 
“days excluding weekends and holidays ” to add specificity.
General Discussion of Progress, Timeline
===PAGE 3===
The Committee decide d  to review approximately the first 20 pages of Part II: General 
Legislation during the next meeting.
Set next meeting(s) Date, Time, Place
The Committee decided to meet on October 30 th  at  6:45 pm.
Public Comment
Mr. Bowen asked if there were any public comments. There were none.
Adjourn
Mr. Nuzzolo  motion ed to  adjourn at 7:57pm. Mrs. Gray  second ed. The Committee voted 
unanimously to adjourn.
