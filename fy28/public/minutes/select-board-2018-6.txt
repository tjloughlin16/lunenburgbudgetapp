Summer Street Road Reconstruction Project Update from MassDOT:
The contractor, Baltazar, is continuing with drainage installation in Fitchburg. MassDOT met with the reclaiming
subcontractor last week to discuss their schedule for beginning work on the site. They will be taking cores and
samples this week to establish their job mix formula for the project. They will submit this to MassDOT, who has
28 days to review it. Upon approval, it progresses according to their schedule and the weather. Ideally, the road
would be reclaimed from the Subway plaza in Fitchburg to the bridge over Baker's Brook. However this project
calls for an emulsion injection into the reclaimed material. The emulsion injection is finicky, it can't be done the
day after or the day of any rain, and the temperature needs to be 50 degrees or higher. It will also need to be paved
over within a week of injection. Weather conditions will factor into the remainder of the 2018 construction season.
Baltazar is expected to have a second crew on site in mid-September. This crew will most likely begin work on the
Baker's Brook Bridge.

As enacted, the purpose of the current Earth Removal Ordinance "is to protect the health, safety and welfare of the public by
regulating the removal of earth so as not to create dust, washouts, noise, ponds, and other hazardous conditions." As you
acknowledged, Keating has been operating under permits issued by the Board of Selectmen continuously for over a half
century. Keating believes that its performance has continually fulfilled the purpose of the ordinance.
Keating was encouraged by Land Use Director Adam Burney's workshop presentation of the draft new bylaw that he
circulated to you the previous day. As Mr. Younkin stated, we look forward to the opportunity to review and to contribute to
the fine-tuning of the bylaw at the appropriate time, noting in particular the necessity to distinguish requirements for sand and
gravel operation from those for rock quarries.
In addition, please note that, in 2006, the Massachusetts Department of Fish and Game, Division of Fisheries and Wildlife,
Natural Heritage and Endangered Species Program (NHESP) mapped nearly 40 percent of contiguous Keating property in
Lunenburg and neighboring towns as a Priority Habitat of Rare Species and as an Estimated Habitat of Rare Wildlife. In
2008, Keating initiated with the NHESP a comprehensive review of the mapping and its implications. NHESP Regulatory
Review Manager Jon Regosin, Ph.D. explained that gravel pits that are not redeveloped or that are not allowed to reforest
provide good habitat for certain animal species, such as the Blanding's Turtle that inhabits Keating land. The review
culminated in a NHESP determination that Keating may continue its long-standing operations. Given the threatened species
habitat on Keating land, Keating encourages the town to accommodate such circumstances when establishing earth removal
closure reclamation provisions.
Given Keating's reliable ongoing performance, the new bylaw efforts, and the species habitat, Keating is troubled by Mr.
Rhodes's recommendation that you not renew two permits and that you restrict renewal duration to three months for six other
permits. Keating is particularly concerned with Mr. Rhodes's suggestion that certain engineering, including provisions for
reclamation of habitat, be undertaken for the latter six permits as a prerequisite for further permit renewal, since such
engineering may prove to be unusable going forward if it does not meet the criteria that the new bylaw and its implementing
regulations ultimately establish.
Keating was heartened by Mr. Burney's advice to you to renew all permits now, in August 2018, and again in August 2019,
in anticipation of the development by Keating and other permittees of comprehensive engineering data for applications to be
made under the new bylaw in 2020. Accordingly, Keating respectfully asks that you renew for one year in the customary

Section 5.05 Disturbances of Newly Paved Roads (Adopted 9/5/17)
Added to this section:
VI. EMERGENCY REPAIRS:
A. DEFINITION: An immediate cut into the newly paved roadway that is required to address a public safety issue.
Examples of this may include, but are not limited to, a ruptured water main or water service, a faulty gas pipe, or
sewer break.
B. Immediate excavation of the area is allowed in cases of emergency repairs outside of the permitting process in
this policy
C. The utility or subcontractor that performs the temporary emergency repair will be required to notify the DPW
Director by the next business day. The temporary emergency repair will include backfilling the hole with material

2. Update from the Land Use Director on the status of the MS4 Phase Il Permit
Ms. Luck gave a brief description of storm water management enacted by the Environmental Protection
Agency (EPA). Municipalities must file a MS4 Phase II permit, a general permit for storm water
discharges from small municipal separate storm sewer systems, to request permission to discharge under
the National Pollutant Discharge Elimination System (NDES). We are in the second phase of the
permit and we need to file a Notice of Intent (NOI) on how we are going to manage storm water over the
next 5 - 10 years.
Land Use Director Adam Burney stated we are currently in year one, month three, of the 2018 MS4
Phase II permit issued by the EPA. The EPA essentially gives the Town a series of storm water
management goals but they don't advise how to achieve them. The current permit is issued in a ten-year
time frame and the NOI tells the EPA how we are going to meet the storm water management plan
goals. A comprehensive storm water management plan will help determine the cost of doing so. The
NOI lists the tasks and who is going to implement them. Our first annual report will be due on
September 29, 2019.
Ms. Luck stated some education outreach associated with the NOI will be done this weekend at the
Stillman Country Fair. We will have an enviroscape showing pollution runoff. Mr. Ebersole asked if
there is any movement to have the state take over the permitting process. Mr. Burney stated yes there is;
the MA Dept. of Environmental Protection has been in discussion with the EPA through the writing of
this permit. They were a cosigner of the issuance of the permit. By signing on to it they have taken some
of the responsibility of it. There is a push to do it, but there is a need for significant staff increase as well
as budgeting and specialization. He believed Massachusetts will take over the permitting.
Mr. Ebersole wondered if there would be additional funding from the state because there's not going to
be any from the federal government. Mr. Burney did not think so.
Mr. Ebersole recommended the management plan costs be incorporated into the capital plan budget in
case opportunities for funding any shovel ready projects arise. Mi. Burney stated that would part of the
storm water management plan; which says here is what we are doing and what it costs. He noted the
next time to check in with this Board will be around early January 2019.
