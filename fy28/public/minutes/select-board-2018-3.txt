to bear with them. He opened the meeting with a comment on the events at the close of the meeting last
Tuesday. "As part of our public comment process, we ask speakers to identify themselves and give their
addresses. This is to ensure that the public record is correct and it reflects accurately who was speaking. Last
Tuesday a citizen made an attempt at humor when asked to follow this procedure and the response was
considered offensive. It is my intent as the Chair of this Board to make sure that all citizens feel comfortable
during our meetings and that the process of town government is inclusive and open to all. The newspaper
reported that the comment drew laughs from the Board and for this I am sorry. I take my responsibility as Chair
seriously and will work to improve the perception of the Board through my future actions. I ask my colleagues
to do the same.

1. Social Media Policy Discussion
Mr. Toale noted this Board adopted a social media policy on July 18, 2017. It was asked that we review that this
evening. Mr. Ebersole read the Statement of Policy and noted it is posted on the Town Website. He wondered if
it covered only paid town employees or does it include town volunteers as well. Mr. Toale stated the way he
understood it covers anyone who speaks on behalf of the Town on social media.
Mr. Ebersole read the following aloud from the policy, "Employees representing the Town on town social
media sites shall conduct themselves at all times as a professional representative of the Town and in accordance
with all Town Policies..... Any employee authorized to post items on any of the town's social media sites shall
not express his or her own personal views or concerns through such postings. Posting shall only reflect the
views of the Town. " "Employees shall refrain from using social media sites and tools for personal use while on
work time or when using Town provided equipment, unless it is work-related as authorized by the Town
Manager or his or her personal designee. Employees shall have no expectation of privacy when using social
media sites during work hours, or when using Town computers, systems or technology. "
It also addresses employee's personal use of social media sites outside of work. "The Town is aware that
employees may maintain or contribute to a form of social media outside of their job functions. Employees that
post on social media sites outside of work should not discuss work-related information unless the matter about
which that are communicating is a matter of public concern protected by the First Amendment. Employees
shall not disclose any Town- related information that is not already considered public information. As
representatives of the Town, employees should exercise caution with respect to comments they post concerning
he Town, is statelyars or the discussion at one of the Massachusets Municipal Association Annual Meetiars
social media workshops was any official, appointed, employed or elected, should be very careful; we have an
added responsibility. If more than a quorum of a Board is making comments on a social media website, it could
constitute a violation of the Open Meeting Law if everyone is deliberating, so some towns don't post anything.
He believed this policy balances the employees' and other official's rights to make comments on social media
with the balance of the Town's needs.
Ms. Bertram believed the second paragraph is very murky, which reads,
"If employees personally access and/or use external social media platforms, they shall not reference the Town

representative of the Town. It, however, an employee makes or expresses any comment about Town

2. Review of Skateboard Park Location Proposal at TC Passios
Mr. Toale reported he was at the presentation at the Senior Center and met with Town Manager Lemieux
afterwards. She has created a map as a result of that meeting. This proposal was based on good visibility and
location near the schools and Teen Center. The group needs to have a location in order to proceed forward to
fundraise. Exact dimensions are not finalized.
Option #1 places the skate park between TC Passios and Eagle House Senior Center on Memorial Drive and
Option #2 places it to the right side of the Public Library on Massachusetts Ave.
Ms. Bertram preferred Option 1. She also wanted to present to this Board for discussion the feasibility of
creating more green space where the Brooks House is currently located and removing the turnaround area in
front of TC Passios and making that a public park. That entire green space would become a recreation area
adjacent to the skate park as well as a potential community center at TC Passios. She was not in favor of
Option 2. Mr. McQuaid preferred Option 1.. He suggested reserving some green space along Memorial Drive
for future outdoor events.

Mr. Toale thanked everyone for coming. Jeff Mendoza stated the community wants agriculture to be at the
forefront of the economic development in Town. Dragonfly Garden Cafe is providing retail/counter space to
local farmers on Saturday mornings at the restaurant. Ms. Bowen reported the "Poopapalooza Farm Tour" sold
out by Noon on the day of their event.
Ms. Bertram personally supported turning Town Hall into a future Farmer/Artist Collaborative space. She
believed there was opportunity for utilization of the cafeteria at TC Passios. Is there strong interest in
developing a Farmer's Collaborative? There are grants available that could help with building restoration and
program implementation. There has to be a core group that is going to implement this and work with the
farmers to get this off the ground.

of money if you advertise properly. Snow mentioned if you "Google" Lunenburg farms, only three come up.

quarterly Farmer to Table dinner at the TC Passions cafeteria.

smaller farm and a day job would welcome having a Farmer's Market venue to sell at. Stillman's Dairy has

Agricultural Energy Grant Program (Ag-Energy) -
provides reimbursement of up to $20,000 and funds
projects pertaining to energy efficiency and
renewable initiatives.
Agricultural Environmental Enhancement Program
(AEEP) - supports agricultural operations looking to
install conservation practices that prevent direct
impacts on water quality, ensure efficient use of
water, and address impacts on air quality.
Participants are reimbursed up to $25,000 for the
cost of materials and labor necessary for the
installation of the approved practice.
Agricultural Food Safety Improvement Program
(AFSIP) - provides funding for agricultural operations
looking to upgrade their food safety measures,
thereby allowing them to maintain or increase their.
competitive market açcess while reducing food safety
risks. Participants are reimbursed up to $20,000 or
75% of the total project costs.
Agricultural Preservation Program (APR) - preserves
and protects agricultural land, including state
designated prime farmland soils, from being
developed into non-agricultural operations.
Participants are paid the agricultural value of the land
in exchange for a permanent covenant.
Commonwealth Quality Program (CQP) - assists
members in improving their farm operations in
order to maintain and improve market access. The
CQP seal helps consumers, buyers, and business
partners identify products produced and harvested
in Massachusetts. Members must incorporate food
safety practices and Best Management Practices
(BMPs) that promote environmental sustainability
and stewardship. Currently, CQP supports the
produce, forestry, lobster, aquaculture, dairy, and
maple sectors.
Massachusetts Farmers' Market Nutrition Program
(FMNP) - provides elders, women, and children in
the Federal Supplemental Food Program for
Women, Infants, and Children (WIC), with coupons
redeemable at farmers' markets. Participation
benefits farmers with additional sales opportunities.
Farm Viability Program - improves the economic
viability of participating farms through the
development and implementation of farm viability
plans. Participants receive environmental; technical;
and business planning assistance to expand and
upgrade their existing operations.
Massachusetts Grown & Fresher - a logo program
for consumers to identify and purchase local
Massachusetts grown farm products, specialty
foods, and agri-tourism as well as culinary tourism
activates.
Matching Enterprise Grants for Agriculture Program
(MEGA) - offers individual business planning and
technical assistance to support the special needs of
beginning farmers. Financial assistance is available
for capital needed to implement strategies
recommended through the planning process.
Urban Agriculture - provides funding to address
challenges facing urban farmers and to support
municipalities with technical assistance with the
development of zoning ordinances, public
education, and land assessments.

LED Streetlight Conversion:
At the March 6th meeting I reported that I had requested the accumulated depreciation value of our 335 streetlights
and the estimated cost if Unitil were to replace these lights with LED fixtures. We received the formal quote from
Unitil that the accumulated depreciation value is $81,808.79. In response to our request for a proposal for Unitil to
provide and install new LED fixtures for the 335 streetlights that they would continue to own and maintain and we
received two options. The first option includes an estimated cost for Police Details in the amount of $45,000, the
undepreciated costs of the existing High Pressure Sodium lights in the amount of $81,808.79, and an estimated cost
for removing and installing new LED lights for $33,500 and an energy efficiency incentive credit for $33,500 for a
grand total of $126,808.79. Option 2 includes the same costs and credit minus the $45,000 for Police Details
(assumes that the Town would agree to waive the Police Detail cost) for a grand total of $81,808.79. Until estimates
the Town would save approximately $29,760 a year by converting to LED. The other option the Town would have is
to purchase the existing street lights from Unitil and go out to bid to have a third party install new LED streetlights
and then the Town would be responsible for replacing and maintaining the lights. The Green Communities Task
Force has a meeting on May 10th to review the quotes from Unitil.

The Selectmen signed an updated Public Employee Committee Agreement on April 10th and all the unions that make
up the Public Employee Committee have voted to approve the agreement, which will go into effect on July 1st. A
Public Employee Committee Agreement is mandatory since a vote of the Board of Selectmen in May of 2008 to

introduces a deductible type plan and reduces the monthly premium for all plans, and will reduce the town's OPEB
