THURSDAY JANUARY 6, 2022 
MINUTES 
1. Open Meeting-6:31 In attendance was Karin Menard, Dave Passios, Chris Sullivan, Ana 
Lockwood. Also in attendance was DPW Director Rob Oliva. 
2. Public and Board Comment- D Passios commented on the misinformation about the Marshall 
Park project and what the Master Plan means moving forward. He added that he’s been involved with 
getting the project off the ground and believes the Master plan will provide a road map for the future 
of Marshall Park, and that doesn’t mean there will be wholesale bulldozing and changing of a 
particular area. This process will include documentation of what's there now at the park and what 
condition it’s in. Next will be what other possible uses or changes could be made or not will be made. 
3. Budget presentation and discussions-Rob Oliva presented the Park’s budget and went through 
the line items for targeted FY23. Items were a park supervisor salary of $24,581, Town Beach 
salaries of $20,000 and discussed that the requested amount was $26,000 reflecting last year’s 
budget going over and discussed that any overages be taken from the Park’s revolving account. 
Personnel totaled $44,581.44. 
Purchase of Service was $46,498.00 and Department Expenses were $6,400 for a total of 
$52,898.00.  
Total Park Budget is 97,479.44. He opened it up for discussion. 
A Lockwood asked if the purchase of services is for third Party maintenance of the parks. R Oliva 
answered that it pays for landscaping, fence work, and other contractor type services. She also asked 
if the FY22 amount was the actual monies paid out. He confirmed it was the actual budget, and went 
on to give figures and reasons of money remaining. A Lockwood asked what the basis of the 
Department expenses were, if it were equipment. R Oliva answered that he will look into that, some 
went to Truck and Treat, possibly some bills paid from there. 
Karin stated that she’s looking to use the money from the revolving account to pay for some of the 
Town Beach salaries. Her question is at the end of the fiscal year the Commission has money to go 
back to free cash and wondered if he had a project or maintenance issue that would use up that 
money. He answered that he doesn’t have a plan right now but have ongoing issues at Marshall Park, 
or if gear or programs could be needed by Parks and Rec. A Lockwood commented that she’d like to 
see a plan for using any overages. 

===PAGE 2===
D Passios stated that he agreed with Karin’s comments about paying excess expenses from the 
revolving account and that this has been done for years and asked if the grounds contract is invoiced 
monthly, quarterly or a lump sum so they’d be seeing more invoices before the end of the fiscal year. 
R Oliva answered its monthly and will start picking up with the spring cleanup and mowing up until 
July 1.D Passios added that there are projects that they’d like to see done but not necessarily be 
done right away. If there are projects in May that are contracted but held over beyond July 1 it would 
help reduce how much money goes back to the general fund. He told of the monies that have gone 
back to general fund over the years that could have been used to maintain the parks. 
Motion made by D Passios to support the DPW Director’s FY23 Budget seconded by C Sullivan.  
Vote was unanimous. 
Motion made by C Sullivan to temporarily adjourn the meeting until 7:00pm, seconded by D Passios. 
Vote was unanimous 
4. 7 PM Public forum with WOLA to Present Site Analysis and Collect Community Input-K 
Menard opened the meeting at 7:00pm.  
C Sullivan stated that this is a Master Plan, the intent is to make the park better by improving on its 
features and utilize the 28 acres there.  He spoke of the misinformation going around about 
bulldozing to level the park and stressed that is not the intention. 
Kathryn Ostermier from WOLA welcomed everyone and gave an agenda for the meeting.  Also there 
from WOLA was Emily Wright, Allison Fairweather, and Nick Betts from Meridian Associates.  
The first steps in their plan process was a public meeting with the Park Commission on December 2nd 
followed by a site visit to Marshall Park with Commissioners, Parks and Rec, and DPW. They were 
able to get some of the town’s perspective on maintenance, future programming and recreation. They 
undertook a Mass GIS analysis of the park and their goal is to share their understanding of the park 
so far, and to get the public’s understanding.  Then in the weeks that follow, work with Meridian to put 
together two design alternatives in which the public can identify what way to proceed with the Park’s 
vision. 
Site Analysis: Marshall Park sits in the middle of Lunenburg in between two watersheds and is near 
some critical natural landscape areas that the Commonwealth have identified as having unique 
wildlife. She described the context of the Park within the cultural center of the town with civic 
buildings, school buildings, businesses and houses of worship. She added the recreational facilities in 
Lunenburg. 
Zoning is in the residential district and the limited business and residential district. She discussed the 
access way to the Park and the parking availability. She noted that there are no ADA parking or 
pathways and no formal connection between the park itself and the pond. She mentioned that there is 
electrical and water access but no restroom facilities. 
She identified what is in the Park currently, such as the 90 foot baseball diamond, softball field, little 
league field and a t-ball field and other kinds of recreation, basketball court, a fitness circuit, the 
former skate park, a small playground and a track. The unique features mentioned were fieldstone 
historic walls, cinder track rolling equipment, a stone table and a Pontiac in the woods. There’s 
monuments and memorials and should be highlighted in the park. 
She then showed the Land Cover, both manmade and natural, of the park and the regulated areas of 
the park and surrounding land. She then opened the meeting up for questions. 
===PAGE 3===
K Menard asked in the buffered areas of wetlands is where the basketball court is and if no work can 
be done there.  K Ostermier answered it would come under jurisdiction of the ConCom if work is done 
there and gave instances of what could be done. 
Dan Proctor of 1529 Mass Ave asked about the status of Marshall Park septic system. K Menard 
answered that Marshall Park is now part of the sewer District and D Passios mentioned the system 
has a draft plan in place. 
Kristin Tuttle of 45 Chestnut St asked if WOLA looked into how pedestrians are going to access the 
park. K Ostermier answered that it will come into play as part of the design process. 
Anthony Caiozzo asked with regards to the buffer zone that goes around and understands that it can’t 
be leveled but is there a multi-use potential like bike trails or other uses. K Ostermier answered that it 
depends on which buffer it was, the 30 foot buffer would be a difficult sell to the ConCom but it might 
be easier in the 50 or 100 foot zone. D Passios added that it wouldn’t be impossible just be more 
difficult to permit. 
Cliff Worthley 53 Woodland Ave asked what BPW on the map was to which K Ostermier answered it 
is a bordering vegetated wetlands. She went to the map with no mike so it was inaudible. C Worthley 
asked which areas are currently unused. K Ostermier pointed out an area that is steep and not great 
for development and isn’t in the buffer. D Passios filled in the audio that was lost. The gray area of the 
map has not had a definitive survey done and that it is on the Capital Project List to be brought 
forward for the FY23.A complete survey will be done after July. 
Corrine Scouten 830 Mass Ave asked if the talk of putting parking where the old bath house was has 
been scrapped. K Menard mentioned that right before she joined the Commission when the parking 
was going in, trees were cleared from a neighbor’s property and everything was paused and never 
gained any momentum after that. It’s not on the table to do now and part of the design will have 
parking not necessarily in that place. 
Marianne Mascari of 54 Chestnut St mentioned that 3 to 4 months of the year when there’s baseball 
games it becomes a one way street and there’s cars backed up from one end of the park to halfway 
through Chestnut St and it becomes hazardous for two way traffic. She asked if parking is under the 
jurisdiction of the Commission. K Menard answered that the street itself is under the Town and any 
land of the parks was under the Commission so some of the parking that happens along Chestnut St 
is on Park’s property. The Commission can advocate for safety on the street. K Menard voiced 
concerns about getting Fire Trucks down there. Discussion followed on problem with parking and how 
to solve it. M Mascari recommended signage. D Passios mentioned that this parking issue has been 
going on for decades and that a combination of additional parking availability and enforcement on 
Chestnut Street would make a huge difference. 
M Mascari wanted to know what happens with garbage pickup because she finds it on a daily basis. 
Menard answered that falls under the DPW. A Caiozzo commented that there should be trash 
facilities within the park facility and recommended it be part of the design through this process. C 
Sullivan mentioned that the parks have a carry in/carry out policy. Discussion followed on the pros 
and cons of trash facilities within the park. 
Chris Martin of 981 Northwood Rd clarified that there are trash receptacles and a dumpster in the 
park during baseball season. K Menard stated that they are brought in by Little League and are not 
publicly maintained.  
K Ostermier went over the survey results with 431 responses, the bulk being ages 18 to 50, and the 
ways the park is used is for walking, baseball, softball and t-ball. When asked what people wanted in 
the park the highest responses were nature or walking trails, public restrooms, a new playground, 
===PAGE 4===
access to the pond and a walking track. Next was a pavilion picnic area, improved parking, a 
rectangular multi-purpose field and hard surface courts. Some other uses included restored fitness 
circuit, softball field, baseball field, maintenance building and Skate Park.  Asked what would cause 
people to come to the park more frequently, the number one response was a dog park along with ice 
skating, concerts, concession stand and a picnic and grill area. 
A Community Workshop was next and a link was sent out for Zoom participants. 
1. Describe you most recent visit to Marshall Park. 
 Some responses were watching my grandchildren play, baseball, walking my dogs, walking, 
dog exercising, watching kids play ball, baseball, and walking with dogs, play soccer. 
2. What would you like to change the most at Marshall Park? 
      Kirsten Tuttle commented that accessing it is important from pedestrians to cars and possibly 
a walking bridge over Marshall Pond. Other comments were rejuvenation and bathrooms, access 
from both streets, designated parking, football courts, pickle ball courts, access from the center of 
town. Chris Martin added access to the pond. Allison Melvin commented that there are features in 
need of repair. 
3. What concerns you about the future of Marshall Park? 
      Comments were over development. Chris Martin commented that the baseball fields would 
go away. Dave King was concerned with neglect. 
4. What would you like to see in the future of Marshall Park? 
 Comments were, more love from the town in maintaining the field, a place where all ages can 
go to enjoy, upgrade to baseball field concession stands and scorekeeper huts, public restrooms and 
Chestnut St parking, walking trails and multi-use fields, the ease of use ability, a website for info, 
mountain bike trails, the pond needs lights for skating. 
John Fortune 50 Hemlock Dr. mentioned that he walked through the park recently and feels it’s in 
tough shape. He’d like to see pickle ball and tennis but can’t do that (on school grounds) when school 
is in session, so he and seniors would like to go to Marshall Park. He mentioned that the park should 
be maintained and bathrooms, parking and easy access are something that seniors would like to see.  
Greg Roy 105 Chestnut St would like to see the park to be vital again and be a hub for folks to use 
with good walking access.  
Tim Murphy 97 Island Rd asked what will be done with the pond, if there would be an area where 
people could fish and ice skate and if it would be cleaned out. K Menard answered that it is in the 
Parks jurisdiction and it is part of this process. C Sullivan mentioned the benches and hockey nets will 
be back there this winter. D Passios added that as they go through the process he’s learned so much 
about the park and asked the public to bring in any photos and history of the park. Send to the Parks 
Commission or the Historical Society. A Lockwood offered that she has pictures of the pond from the 
early 2000s that her neighbor documented and the health of the pond had degraded and believes 
from a mix of reasons. The town has sewers so less water flowing to the pond, the more use of 
fertilizers that run off into waterways to the pond which creates overgrowth. 
K Ostermier proceeded to the next part of presentation stating that now that there is an understanding 
of the kids of activities people are interested in at Marshall Park, it’s helpful to identify how people 
would like it to look. A slide was shown of three different aesthetic styles:

She then asked for a vote in person and zoom. 
Anthony Caiozzo commented that he like C if it could be made more durable. K Ostermier answered

K Ostermier opened the meeting up for questions.   
D Passios agreed with the maintenance concerns with any one of the styles that they will need proper 
surface coatings to maintain them. 
K Menard answered the question by a resident of where the money was coming from for this project 
that there are grants and that it’s a project to be done in stages.  
Chris Menard 672 Page St, also on the Finance Committee, stated that they are tracking a lot of 
federal grants to be earmarked towards improvements of parks and having a Master Plan helps 
placing of the grants. Discussion followed on securing grants. 
Chris Martin asked if other properties were looked at instead of Marshall Park which is taken up by 
baseball fields. K Menard answered yes but that it has to be aesthetically a fit.  
Tim Murphy reiterated on maintenance and repair and make sure the design requires less care and 
maintenance. 
K Ostermier talked of the next steps which is WOLA and Meridian will compile and analyze the 
feedback from that night and meetings with the Park Commission, DPW and Recreation Dept., and 
the survey that the Commission did. This will guide their design work, doing two concept design 
alternatives. In early February they will present the designs and cost estimates to the public and 
getting feedback. Once a preferred alternative is selected they will move forward and develop that 
and finish the process. 
Dan Proctor asked if he could get an existing conditions plan so the town’s people could discuss 
using some of the resources that they have such as civil engineers, construction and excavating 
people, plumbers and electrician etc.  He’d like a roundtable discussion with the commission. D 
Passios responded that he’s aware of the volunteerism of the public and it was before the town had 
an active Park Commission. The town has liability for projects that are done and projects have to be 
properly vetted, not like in the past. 
A resident asked if the when they come back with the two designs if certain things can be 
incorporated from one design to another. K Ostermier answered that if it all fits that can be done, that 
they’ll work to bring the two preferred portions of the alternatives together. 
Anthony Caiozzo asked to have the map brought up again to highlight where the skate park is 
proposed. Discussion followed on the proposed area. Dawn Johnson 357 Lancaster Rd voiced her 
concerns about just hearing where the skate park is going and it’s already decided before the rest of 
the plan is in place. K Menard explained how the skate park evolved into that space. A Caiozzo

MINUTES
1. Open Meeting K Menard opened the meeting via zoom at 7:00pm. Present: Karin Menard, Chris Sullivan, 
Dave Passios, Ana Lockwood. Also in attendance, Rob Thompson Recreation Director 
2. Public and Board Comment-D Passios made the statement that with the review that he has done on the two 
proposals to be presented that evening, he feels that neither one of them represents his vision of what they have 
said they want for this project and what the public has expressed their concerns over, he intends to move that 
they pass over that agenda item. He believes it’s not ready for public review. 
C Sullivan stated that the hockey nets and benches are back down on Marshall Pond. 
3. Approval of Minutes: 7/14/21, 7/28/21, 8/25/21, 9/27/21, 10/27/21, 12/2/21, 12/20/21. 1/4/22 
A Lockwood mentioned that the 1/4/22 (actually date 1/6/22) minutes need to be edited before approved, D 
Passios agreed as he had some concerns with them. 
Motion by C Sullivan, seconded by A Lockwood to accept the listed minutes submitted excluding 
1/4/22(1/6/22) Roll call, C Sullivan- Aye, A Lockwood-AYE, D Passios-Aye, K Menard- Aye. 
4. 7:15 WOLA-2 preliminary design presentations, discussion and feedback by commission for Marshall Park- 
D Passios proposed a motion to be voted on that the two proposals that were distributed to the board for review 
previous to the meeting do not get disseminated in the meeting pending further review and discussion by the 
board and their architects and engineers in a public setting. K Menard stated that the meeting was there for 
public discussion. D Passios moved that the designs not be released that evening. K Menard stated that the 
designs are already out there for the public. A resident mentioned that they are public documents. K Menard 
mentioned that the meeting was for WOLA to be gather information from the meeting’s discussion about the 
project. Discussion followed on going ahead with WOLA and discuss what is liked and what is not of the two 
designs. D Passios withdrew his motion 
K Menard gave the link for the viewers at home of a google folder with the preliminary designs and stressed 
they are preliminary and that WOLA is there to get feedback from the commission so they can make 
adjustments as needed. 
Two Concept designs (Option 1 & Option 2) were screen shared by K Ostermier. She went through both 
Options describing the overall concept. 
Option 1-Entering from Rt 2A drive straight into the park. 100 Parking spaces, central to what they are calling 
the heart of the park. There is a turnaround that is sufﬁcient for a ﬁre truck to safely turn around and exit. In the 

===PAGE 2===
heart of the park is the skate park, pickelball courts, a larger lawn, a playground, restroom facilities , snack 
stand and picnic area. The little league/softball ﬁeld was left where it was, adjusted the t-ball ﬁeld. There is a 
small and large loop for dog walking/walking which could continue and connect with a path to the pond for an 
overlook. There is parking by the 90 foot diamond taking pressure off of the street parking. The low lying areas 
are for storm water collection and drainage. They would reuse the existing paved courts as a dog park. They still 
have to locate a ﬁtness track. They wanted to keep this all in the central portion of the park, have younger kids 
at the playground while the others are in school and also have times when the seniors can use the pickelball 
courts, etc. Aesthetically, from the survey, people tended to prefer the rustic approach. 
A Lockwood asked if the 90 foot baseball diamond is in the same location or a different one to which K 
Ostermier answered that it would be reoriented. It could be used for a softball ﬁeld. 
Alternative 2- It would be using shared outﬁelds, the sum of both is greater than the two separate ﬁelds. The 90 
foot baseball diamond has been realigned and reconﬁgured. The entrance is off 2A with similar parking with a 
larger parking area to right, enter into one large parking lot with a turnaround at the end. There is the skatepark 
with benches around it for people to sit, on the other side of the pathway is the playground and the memorial 
lawn. The existing memorials would be moved and set next to the benches themselves. Then a smaller lawn 
with an overhead structure that connects the snack shack and the restrooms. There is an active zone with the 
pickelball court and basketball court. There are U shaped benches for the courts. Some grading needs to be done 
at the existing little league ﬁeld and they thought it would be interesting to inset granite benches into the 
hillside. The path splits off to a proposed overlook of the pond and include the dog park and picnic benches. By 
combining the outﬁelds of the diamond and the softball ﬁeld it gives an area for other types of ﬁeld play. 
There’s another parking area set off Chestnut St and a cinder track/asphalt track that continues all around the 
park for walking. There’s 110 parking spots. They had some ideas for signage for the park. 
K Ostermier opened the meeting up for questions and comments. 
Nick Betts from Meridian Associates stated he was available to answer any of the civil questions, A Lockwood 
asked if he could speak on that. He went on to state that the soils at the park are difﬁcult because of a high ledge 
and a shallow cover of soil. He mentioned that with the parking, from a civil perspective, you have to meet 
stormwater regulations with the conservation commission. Parking should be kept away from wetlands while 
still using the existing entrance drive. The project can be classiﬁed as a redevelopment which has less strict 
standards than an all new development. Locating the building adjacent to the parking allows for easier access 
for maintenance vehicles and deliveries. If the septic system design located at the end of the road is pursued it’s 
still within reach of the approved leachﬁeld. It gives ﬂexibility to either have the septic pumped or to connect to 
the town sewer in the future. The paths would run parallel with the grade in order to minimize the amount of 
grading that’s need to meet the ADA requirements. 
K Menard asked in Option #2 the grade seems to be multi-level grades and thinks the 90-foot diamond is lower 
than the current softball ﬁeld and how would they level it. N Betts answered that it will never be a dead ﬂat ﬁeld 
but with grading and spreading they could get a ﬁeld perceived as ﬂat and drainage would be taken in account. 
C Sullivan stated that Option 2 is closer but still has concerns. One tricky things is the school use and the 
overlap of games at the softball and 90-foot diamond ﬁelds. He likes the use of the unutilized areas and wants a 
discussion on the loss of the t-ball ﬁeld. He likes the idea of the dog park. He appreciated the addressing of the 
ﬁre engine being able to turn out and exit. He liked the memorial park, the overhang, small playground ideas 
and thinks pickelball and the basketball court are important. He likes the parking plans. He questioned the 
orientation of the two ball ﬁelds coming together and parking access. He liked the walking areas. 
A Lockwood spoke about the overlap of the baseball games and how they worked. She liked the combined 
outﬁelds and possibly have a soccer league in the fall. Liked the dog park being a small area fenced in but
