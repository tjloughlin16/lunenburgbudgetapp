Under Lunenburg Protective By Law chapter 335, the Lunenburg Conservation Commission will hold a public hearing for a Notice of 
Intent by Joann Magurn for work at 238 Fire Road 16 for the construction of a single family home with a drive way crossing.  
 
Brian Butler, representing the applicant, reviewed the prior hearing history.  He also reviewed the 
appeal of the denied Order of Conditions to Mass DEP. He reviewed the history of the appeal site 
walk and noted he is anticipation the issuance of a superseding order of conditions. He noted this 
notice of Intent was filed under the local by law only. He outlined the features of the site including 
the existing cart road that is 900 feet in length and that the site was formerly used for camping. He 
===PAGE 4===
noted that there is plumbing, and concrete pad features formerly used for camping.  Mr Butler 
outlined the comments from the fire chief recommending a 12 feet wide driveway but not required 
it. DPW had no comment. Mr. Butler outlined the resource areas, their location and its relation to 
the proposal. He noted the Bordering land subject to flooding and its extent. He noted the reason 
they did not relocate the access as it would involve work regrading and cutting trees and would 
still be within the buffer zone area of the isolated wetland. He noted that the 900 square foot 
crossing of the flood plain was not consequential. He outlined the function of the culvert that would 
provide for the wildlife crossing and a secondary culvert that would function in a similar manner. 
He noted the retaining walls were not considered a barrier as the culvert would allow for migration. 
He stated that the use of the exiting roadway would minimize any work required. He noted that 
there would be 28 white pine trees that would be altered and outlined the mitigation/planting areas 
on the plan as well as the compensatory flood storage areas. He outlined the erosion controls. He 
noted that there would be removal of concrete on site with re naturalization of the areas. He also 
reviewed the location of the house proposed. He noted the septic system was approved. He noted 
that the proposed house is not typical of a lake front lot being 15 acres in size. He also noted that 
this design does not inhibit wildlife habitat activities. He noted that DEP advocated the use of a 
stable and paved surface. 
  
JR noted that his prior opinion was that the proposed activities would cause degradation of the 
site. He asked if the fire chief asked for a radius turn and noted that there was a recommendation 
for a 12 foot wide driveway. Butch Magurn noted that his conversation with the fire chief noted that 
the fire department had no jurisdiction on the driveway and he and Joann Magurn were open to a 
12 foot driveway. JR asked about the status of the appeal to DEP. Brian Butler noted that Dep 
representative Gary Dulmaine indicated he was told a superseding order would be issued. JR 
asked if the trees to be removed included the driveway. Brian Butler noted that there was no trees 
to be removed in the driveway be limbs removed only. He reviewed the replacement standards 
and reviewed the national definition of trees. JR asked about the septic system. It was noted that 
it was approved. CL asked what minimum definition of tree counts were used. Cl asked if the 
agent approved the septic system or was it the Board of Health. Butch Magurn noted it was a 
Board of health permit. CL noted that the narrative clearly states the resubmittal was forwarded to 
ask for a revote of the full board. RB questioned if the application was an back-door appeal of an 
application that had not been approved by the commission and if the application should be 
allowed. BP noted that the application had a paved driveway and the application that had not 
been approved was for a gravel driveway so the application was different than the one that had 
failed to gain approval. The commission agreed the chair should seek a opinion from town 
counsel. 
 
The meeting was continued to March 17 by a motion of CL and a second by JV and a unanimous 
vote to approve the motion.

Pursuant to MGL ch 131, s 40 as amended and Under Lunenburg Protective By Law chapter 335, the Lunenburg Conservation 
Commission will hold a public hearing for a Notice of Intent by Empire Management Corp for work at 757 Chase Road for the 
demolition and reconstruction of a Commercial building with grading and parking.  
 
James Alamonte represented the applicant. Brian Lafferty and Brandon Ducharme were also 
present. Mr. Alamonte represented the applicant. He outlined the properties owned by the 
applicant as shown on the submitted plans. He outlined the details of each proposed construction 
areas on the plans. He outlined the septic utility and the water utilities. He outlined the wetland 
resource areas and the relation to the resource areas. The proposal is to construct a new 
===PAGE 5===
dealership generally in the same location. There would be a new dealership building proposed 
that will make the use of the facility more efficient and will be within branding specifications by 
Subaru. He noted the new curb cuts and new landscaping as well as the new storm water 
infrastructure and a three percent decrease in impervious surface meant to satisfy DEP storm 
water requirements. He outlined the upgrades and expansions to the storm water system. There 
is no work within the 30-foot buffer zones and minimal work within the 50-foot buffer zone. He 
outlined the work proposed within the entirety of the 100-foot buffer zones and the mechanics of 
the storm water system He also outlined the proposed erosion control barriers.  
He outlined the subsurface infiltration system designed to handle the storm water infiltration 
system. He noted the other board’s approvals that have been granted. BP asked about the 
demolition work of the former Mazda Dealership. Mr. Alamonte noted the building will be 
remaining. JR asked if there was any fuel storage. The applicant answered no. Brian Lafferty 
confirmed no fuel storage. JR asked about oil storage. He noted there will be some bulk oil 
storage in the building and a waste oil burner. The oil storage is contained by a double walled 
tank. JR asked if there is a holding tank designed and a response plan. It was noted that the 
design will be finalized by a plumber. JR asked what monitoring and containment was called for. 
The applicant noted that a plumbing engineer would be retained for a final design. 
 
RB asked about guardrails on the north side of the parking lot. The applicant noted it is planned 
for and there was snow storage proposed out of the buffer zone. RB asked to continue to allow for 
the other boards to finish their work. RB asked what size storm the detention basin was designed 
for. The applicant noted that he would firm out the details.  JR asked it the 100-year storm water 
was sufficient MM noted that the regulations had the 100 year as a maximum. KJ asked if the well 
exiting would be abandoned and the answer was yes. KJ noted that three was no DEP number 
yet. BP asked about the meeting dates of the other boards considering the application.  
 
He suggested that perhaps we should wait to the April 7, 2021, meeting. The applicant agreed. 
RB asked for a schedule of construction to be developed. The applicant agreed.

Old Business   Continued Workshop for continued discussion of changes of the land use regulations  and the 
creation of a document to inform the public about the land use regulations.  
 
RB read a statement outlining the reasons why he had placed the hunting with firearms initiative 
on the regulation review. He had noted that hunters in Lunenburg are both tax paying and license 
payers. He also noted the educational and safety course requirements that hunters go through. 
He cited that overpopulation of deer in Massachusetts and cited mortalities of deer by auto 
accidents and starvation due to overpopulation and that hunting is an effective way of population 
control. He noted that in 40 years in the state there have only been three incidents of fatal 
===PAGE 6===
accidents recorded.  He noted the two parcels that are the only ones now under consideration and 
why they are the ones selected. He suggested different ways to spread information on safety and 
hunting laws in the town of Lunenburg. 
 
CC excused herself from the meeting at 9 PM. 
 
CL felt a workshop extra meeting could be very productive and can help to address concerns and 
felt that perhaps we should adjourn this session in favor of reconvening for the workshop. BP 
expressed that with all of the amendment requests placed in by members that the session could 
continue this evening in addition to the extra session. He noted section 3.1.4 adding language for 
the reporting of missing signs.  Both CL and RB stated that adding the language may not be 
necessary. BP suggested adding appendix A and B for permitting forms and fishing and hunting 
guide excerpts. BP noted that he had sent the appendix for hunting to Pat Huckery for review who 
suggested the wording of the law be added and that a comment that there is no pheasant hunting 
in Lunenburg as it is not stocked.  
 
ML had a question regarding an organized group charging a fee noting a situation that if a local 
school has a hiking course for which they charge a student fee for , would it be require a permit 
application from Lunenburg. MM noted the college would be a not for profit activity and the 
permitting would be required for a for-profit activity. CL noted that the different between profit and 
nonprofit was not spelled out in the proposed regulation. KJ noted that with the current wording it 
should apply to all organizations profit or nonprofit.  
 
Cl asked about Pheasant hunting and noted that he had spotted a family of pheasant at the 
northwest town forest. BP noted that Ms. Huckery’s comment stated that there was no pheasant 
stocked in Lunenburg. CL felt the note is not relevant. JV stated that he did not see Pheasant 
hunting in Lunenburg as there is not the population not the appropriate field space to make 
Pheasant hunting desirable.  CL noted that in the land grant management program had provisions 
in the document for procedures for changes to it that he was concerned could conflict with the 
procedures for changes in the draft document the commission is working on. BP noted he 
reached out to the appropriate state official to answer the questions if the commission need the 
commonwealth’s permission to make changes in the land uses noted in the land management 
plan. The response was deemed vague in language by BP. He replied to the communication and 
reiterated the original question. Cl noted his concern was the land management plan that was 
accepted and that if local land use policy was changes would change the land use management 
plan outside of a written procedure. He noted an example that the current plans on Table rock and 
The Lane property only allow bow hunting and a change to that may have to follow a different 
procedure. RB asked if a simple change to the land use plans referring to the local land use policy 
would suffice. CL noted that the process outlined in the land management plan be added to the 
local regulations to be consistent with process. BP noted that Brandon Kibbe had written the 
document to be consistent with the current local regulations. RB suggested simply updating each 
land management plan once the regulation was completed. BP noted that he wanted to defer 
action until such time as the commonwealth responded to the follow-up questions. The langue of 
requiring a 2/3 majority was discussed. RB noted that the commission has no other requirements 
on any other business it does requiring a 2/3 vote and suggested that not be adopted. KJ JV and 
BP agreed. It was noted that the police department would aid in enforcement. CL made a motion 
that the notification language be changed to reporting to the agent and the police as appropriate. 
CL noted that the ultimate responsibility for enforcement falls on the commission. After 
examination RB noted that the agent is not a 24 hour position and keeping the word or gives the 
residents the option of who to report it to if the agent is off duty.  
 
The issue of the definition of passive recreation was examined. BP noted it was verbatim from the 
state standards. It was decided that the language of the CMR be left as is. JR read language for a 
preamble for the terms “quiet enjoyment” and the addition of language adding community 
gardens. He asked what are the goals of this endeavor.  Cl reviewed 1.4 regarding the prohibition 
of certain types of guns to eliminate the terms “ the use of” and to change “is” to “are”. RB noted 
===PAGE 7===
that per the second amendment people should be allowed  
to carry. JV asked CL if concealed carry was an issue. CL noted he was not in favor of any 
firearms possession on conservation lands. JV noted he disagreed with CL on this issue. CL 
referred to a ruling from legal that the commission has a right to ban them . KL noted he was not 
in favor of a firearms ban. JR noted that he felt the open carry has a different effect than 
concealed carry. He felt that concealed weapons only should be allowed with the exception of 
hunting weapons during the times of the year and areas that it would be appropriate. RB and BP 
noted they were against banning firearms.  Cl also proposed adding language from the current 
hunting regulations mentioned in the appendix, being moved from the appendix section. KJ noted 
that he felt it was not necessary and redundant. JV noted that there was a prior conversation 
where it was decided not to move the section. 
 
Cl suggested that 1.6 reflect the language of the current regulations in section 2.20 regarding 
dogs. BP noted it was discussed prior however, it would be reconsidered. JR noted that based on 
the WMA regulations there was requirement that dogs be kept on a lease, however he felt that 
Dogs could be kept under direct vocal control. KJ ,JV and RB felt it was sufficient as currently 
read. Cl clarified the town by law and would send it to the commission for further review. 
 
CL suggested inserting that in addition to the smoking restriction, that language for grills and 
portable stoves be added. JR noted he agreed with CL. KJ, RB, JV and RB felt it would be 
sufficient as is. 
 
CL asked for a motion to adjourn, JR seconded. During discussion KJ asked what the chairman 
wanted to do. BP noted that he wanted to go through CL’s suggestions as JR also has some 
lengthy presentation and wanted to give JR sufficient time for his presentation at the next meeting. 
The motion was declined by a vote of BP, KJ, JV, RB to decline.  
 
1.9 CL suggested language adding “in accordance with change process as outlined” RB had no 
opinion on this issue, KJ had no issue with it, JV was also in agreement with the suggestion. 
 
CL asked that sections 2.5 and 2.7 be reinserted into the new document. Cl wanted to know why 
we should delete those items. RB noted that there was existing state law that dealt with public 
behavior and containers. JR asked about further language concerning quite enjoyment. BP noted 
that it during previous discussions it was decided it would be eliminated due to varying 
perspectives on what constituted quiet enjoyment citing KJ’s example of how snow mobiles are 
allowed , however could be perceived as  nuisance by some. CL felt that it was not sufficient to 
delete the language in order to rely on other mention of general laws. RB noted that there were 
other laws in effect that cover the items from the original language. KJ noted that examples of 
others “quite enjoyment” activities and that there were a variety of spaces on conservation lands 
where activities that were perceived as a disturbance such as snowmobiles, Hunting, mountain 
bikes and meditating. KJ echoed RB’s concern as to who would be the arbiter of such a regulation 
item. 
 
CL asked the alcoholic and glass containers being prohibited be reinserted. KJ asked CL what the 
issue was with glass. CL noted that he was concerned about safety. KJ noted that he has seen 
plastic and glass containers on land however, felt adding in the regulation was necessary. CL felt 
that glass was still an issue and he also felt alcoholic beverages should not be on the property. 
RB reviewed section 205-10 of the local code for consumption of alcoholic beverages. Cl noted 
that the issue was possession not consumption. RB re-read the section of local code noting 
possession prohibition of open containers. KJ noted it can be left as is. JV agreed with KJ. RB 
also agreed with KJ and JV. JR agreed with CL. BP noted he would like to ask CC about this 
issue at the next meeting.  
 
Section 2.1 and 2.11 CL suggested that 2.1 be deleted and 2.11 be made 2.1 and add language 
that refined the line. He felt the sections were redundant. All members agreed with the suggested 
change A review of section 2.4 requiring tree stands be removed by the end of the applicable 
===PAGE 8===
hunting season. KJ noted that he was in agreement with CL and asked JV and RB if state law 
would require them to be removed at the end of the season. BP suggested that it could be 
inserted into the beginning of section 2.4 and read the proposed language. Members were in 
agreement with BP’s suggestion. 
 
Cl noted Town Counsel recommended that Hunting Hill be added to the list in section 2.5.  BP 
noted that he had extensive conversations by email with Elizabeth Newell of the Commonwealth 
and was forwarding that correspondence to the commission for review. ML asked a question if 
lead free ammunition was present in Massachusetts. RB noted that lead shot is not allowed for 
the use of shot for water fowl. BP recounted his conversation with the commonwealth and that it 
was there interpretation that when the CR was signed for hunting hills, there would be further 
restrictions on hunting in all forms on the hunting hills conservation land. Town Counsel would be 
shown that conversation so he could render an opinion on it.  
 
4.2 CL suggested that the language be added that the LCC Administrator be the point of contact 
for reporting violations to make it clear to the public who drives the enforcement. It was noted by 
BP that a meeting with the police chief resulted in a commitment from the chief that they would

would report to the commission. MM noted that he had no issue and that there was a need for one
