1. Borrego Solar Landfill Project - Ms. Speidel stated that she would like to update the board on the three solar
projects together.
Ms. Speidel said that the Borrego Project has been going on close to two years and that it is a Town initiated project at the
landfill. In the beginning, the landfill was identified as being a good location to put a solar farm but they have found that
although this location is good for aesthetic purposes it is not a good location for economic purposes. Borrego has worked
diligently with Unitil and at this point in time they are awaiting the results of an Anti Islanding study, which was due on
December 7, 2012, and Unitil has not delivered it to them as of this meeting.
Lunenburg's Town Attorney, Rick Holland, has been in contact with Borrego and has reported that Borrego has lost
interest in this project. They have put out a tremendous amount of time and money with this project and do not want to
walk away from the project but would not object if the Town decided they did not want to move forward with this project
The original project was hoped to be approximately 2MW and has been changed to . 75MW which is not economically
feasible for anybody.
The problem is that the Borrrego project needs to be cleared up before the Town can move forward with the Mass PV1
project.
Ms. Speidel reported that back in October the Town had signed a PPA with EPG Solar to purchase credits from this
project which is approximately IMW. The credits would be purchased at approximately 11.32 cents per KW which is a
savings of about 33% over what is currently paid. The PILOT document has been drafted and the attorney's are in
agreement with the document. The PILOT is currently with EPG and the property owner. This property is currently part
of a bankruptcy filing and the bankruptcy court has to approve any land lease or purchase of this property which has
slowed down this project. Back in December EPG informed Ms. Seidel that they had just completed an appraisal on the
property which was required by the bankruptcy court, and the appraisal came back significantly less than what the
property owner had expected. Due to the holidays, there has not been much progress made on this project. EPG cannot
move forward until the property owner signs off on the PILOT but they believe that they can still meet the May 31, 2013
build out deadline.
Ms. Speidel reported on the Mass PV1 project which would be a 2MW facility. On November 13" the BOS met with
representatives from Mass PV1 and at that time agreed to purchase the credits from .5MW with the option to purchase up
to the full 2MW's. The board has 90 days to act on that action which will expire on February 13, 2013. Ms. Speidel feels
that from an economic point of view it would be more advantageous to purchase credits from this project because they are
looking to sell at a better rate of .9 cents per KW hour which would save approximately 47%.
===PAGE 2===
Ms. Speidel has had conversations with the Town of Townsend about purchasing credits from this project and they seem
to be interested. She also met with the Water District about purchasing credits but they are not interested because they
currently have a better rate. Mass PV1 has also been out talking to other communities about the purchase of credits from
this project which could be to our benefit as well. Lunenburg has agreed to purchase SMW's but Mass PV1 would like to
sell all of the credits to Lunenburg. If Lunenburg does not purchase the remaining 1.5MW's then Mass PV1 will be
responsible for selling them elsewhere.
Attorney's for the Town and for Mass PV1 have been reviewing the language in both the PPA and the PILOT and are in
agreement that it is satisfactory.
Board members were in agreement that the Town needs to walk away from the Borrego Project although Mr. Sund felt
that he would like Borrego to initiate the conclusion of the project. He would also like to investigate further the cost
savings for purchasing more credits from Mass PV1.
After lengthy discussion among the Board members Mr. Matthews asked Ms. Speidel to contact Borrego concerning the
termination of that project. In regards to the EPG Solar Project, for the time being nothing will be done at this point in
time but EPG will be reminded that the PPA is dependent on the PILOT. As for Mass PV1 they still have a month to act
on this project and will continue to investigate the purchase and sales of net metering credits.

Forecasting.y. Budgeting

Goals of Forecasting

1. Consider Exercising Right of First Refusal, Aro Property - Ms. Speidel informed the Board that the Conservation
Commission and Planning Board have both submitted their recommendations on the Aro property as follows:
The Planning Board recommends that the town acquire the property, due to the financial impact on the town for
developing in that part of town, and the impact on town services and infrastructure.
The Conservation Commission stated that this property is at the head waters of Mulpus Brook and any disturbance in the
flow of or pollution of this water would have a devastating affect on anything downstream. This would include but not
limited to vegetation, wild life, and areas that feed off of the brook that includes Hickory Hills Lake. Any disturbance in
the flow could result in stagnant waters in the lake and would also reflect in the condition of the area and the values of
homes that are there. Acquisition of this land would give the Conservation Commission access to a now land locked
parcel and would allow the land to stay in its natural state to be used by the residents of Lunenburg for passive recreation.
Mr. Matthews reminded everyone that this property had been before the BOS several years ago to exercise First Right of
Refusal and it was passed on due to the cost of the property. The developer has negotiated a new price with the property
owner which is $2.1m for 147 acres of land.

1. Review and/or Execute Power Purchase Agreement and PILOT Agreement for Solar for Mass PV1 and/or
PILOT for EPG Solar - Ms. Speidel explained that since the last discussion with the BOS the States Net Metering
Queue has gone into effect. As of January 24#* any of these projects that would be producing solar credits are now
required to submit their applications to the DPU queue for review.
===PAGE 2===
Ms. Speidel updated the BOS on the state of the current solar projects which the Town is currently involved with as
follows:
EPG Solar - EPG has submitted their project to DPU and it is in the queue. EPG and the land owner have come to an
agreement on the land lease, which has been approved by the Bankruptcy Court. The written agreement is being prepared
and both parties will be able to sign the PILOT this month. EPG anticipates beginning construction in March and
concluding by May 31, 2013. Because the project is just under 1 MW, it will be classified as a Class 2 rather than a Class
3 facility and therefore Unitil does not have the ability to issue a check, and can only issue credits. The Town would need
to file a Schedule Z and Unitil will decide whether they will be issuing a check or credits.
Ms. Bertram mentioned that MART is now a Net Metering facility. They received a grant and have installed a small solar
array on top of their building which only produces 7k KW. She said that they were having problems with the snow load
on the panels and that they were also approved by Unitil to be Net Metering.
Borrego Solar - Borrego Solar has been tied up with studies and have been waiting on results of an Anti-Islanding Study
from Unitil, which they have not received yet. Ms. Speidel contacted Borrego on their status, explained the Town's
dilemma and asked if they wished to continue with the project. The Project Manager from Borrego emailed Ms. Speidel
saying that they are committed to the project and would like to wait to see what Unitil tells them. They also understand
that the Town has other projects going and may miss out on other opportunities if they continue with the Borrego project.
Although Borrego did not agree to end this project with the Town, they do understand if the Town wishes to do so as to
not miss out on other opportunities.
Mass PV1- The Attorney's have agreed to conclude the negotiations on the PPA by the end of this week. This project will
be a Class 3 facility and as such, Unitil will have the ability to issue credits or a check at their discretion. They will not
make this determination until the Town files a Schedule Z, which cannot be done until it is sure that project will be
moving forward. Mass PV1 has yet to file an application to get into the DPU Queue, which Ms. Speidel has asked them
to do.
Ms. Speidel has contacted other communities, Ashby, Fitchburg, and Townsend, about purchasing credits from the Town.
To date, she has received a response from Townsend indicating interest. Ms. Speidel has also been contacted by an agent
for an eligible buyer, but does not know who this buyer is. The Lunenburg Water District is not interested because they
already have a better deal. It does not appear that the Town will have any difficulty in selling any excess credits.
2. Vote to include Debt Exclusion Question on Special Election Ballot, 03/16/13- Ms. Speidel explained to
the board that at the last BOS meeting the board voted to have the Special Town meeting and Special Town Election.
The Board must now vote the specific question for the ballot. Because the question is a Debt Exclusion, MGL requires
that the board must vote by a 2/3rds majority to include the question on the ballot (MGL Chapter 59, Section 21C, 1 ½, J
and K). This would mean that at least 4 members must vote in favor of placing the question on the ballot. The Board
must also vote the question exactly as it will appear on the ballot as follows:
Question No. 1. Shall the Town of Lunenburg be allowed to exempt from provisions of Proposition Two and One-Half,
so-called, the amounts required to pay for the bond issued in order to purchase a 147 acre property, known as the Aro
Farm, located at 400 Howard Street, Lunenburg, MA, Map 6, Lot 21, for conservation purposes?
Ms. Speidel also noted that the Town Clerk has also asked that the BOS vote to authorize the use of paper ballots, as
opposed to the printed/programmed ballots, in order to save time and money.
Mr. Matthews spoke and said that at last weeks BOS meeting, the vote to hold a Special Town Meeting was a 3-2 vote
and opened it up this evening for discussion to the board. Although the board agreed that the Town could not afford to
make this purchase, they remained at a standstill.
Mr. Alonzo said that he was in favor of bringing this question forward because it is a decision that should be made by the
whole Town. Ms. Bertram spoke and said that it is a decision for the Town to make and not by us as five individuals.

on this, he voted against this because he does not think it is a good use of our funds, the Town can't afford it, and he has
not changed his mind on that.

Mr. Alonzo started the meeting with a public comment from the board inregards to the Boys and Girls Club of Lunenburg
having a game show fundraiser in the high school auditorium called "Are You Smarter Than Our Sixth Graders?"
Admission is by donation at the door.
Mr. Sund continued the public comments from the board by referencing the previous week's discussion regarding the Aro
Farm property and why he chose to vote no to buying the property. Mr. Sund stated that he did not have a compiled list of
reasons why he voted against the purchase of the farm prepared and ready to show the public during the previous week
but he had compiled a list since the last meeting. Mr. Sund continued by reading his reasons for voting against acquiring
the property which included the property being very complex and cannot be commented on without a recent review; the
property is assessed by the Town as being valued at $1.1 million dollars and we would not want to spend $2.1 million to
purchase it. There are 13 sheds, a barn, 2 commercial structures and one residence that the Town would be responsible to
remove. The property may have contaminated substances or soil that the Town would be responsible to remove and all
additional costs of cleanup would fall on the Town of Lunenburg.
Additionally Mr. Sund cited the Jones House that the Town had owned which despite being appraised at $250,000.00, the
Town sold it at only $10,000.00.
Mr. Matthews responded to Mr. Sund by thanking him for the information but stating the board's vote was whether or not
the issue should go to the townspeople at Town Meeting to vote on and that the information he presented would have been
good information for the townspeople to know if the issue went to Town Meeting.
Ms. Bertram responded by echoing Mr. Matthews statement. Ms. Bertram spoke specifically to the issue of contamination
on the property stating she had attended all the Planning Board meetings regarding the property and had met with the
Board of Health that day to discuss it. Ms. Bertram stated that there had been extensive testing of the soil on the property
including perk rates and ground water and no contamination has been found. This is the first time any concern of
contamination had been heard. Ms. Bertram said that Board of Health agent Jim Gariffy has visited the property several
times over the past couple of years and has not brought forth any concern of contamination on the property to the Planning
Board.
Carl Luck, 50 Sunset Lane, spoke to the BOS concerning last week's vote. Mr. Luck respectfully requested that the two
members of the board who voted against allowing the issue to go to Town Meeting reverse their vote and allow the board
to again vote to send the issue to Town Meeting. Mr. Luck requested that each Selectmen explain why they decided to
vote against sending the issue to Town Meeting and not the reasons they had for not wanting to acquire the property. Mr.
Luck stated that the information provided by Mr. deBettencourt and Mr. Sund in regards to why they did not want to
purchase the land was good information, but that it was not relevant to why they voted against letting the issue go tọ Town
Meeting to allow the people to make a decision on the matter themselves. Mr. Luck finished his public comments by
stating the Town's Conservation Committee and Planning Board support purchasing the property and that the two
Selectmen who prevented this issue from going in front of the townspeople had abused their powers.

for Summer Street matched what each community was trying to accomplish.

3. Update on the School Building Committee Activities - Mike Mackin 26 Cortland Circle, updated the BOS on
where the School Building Committee was with the Feasibility Study on new schools. Mr. Mackin stated that last fall after
review of almost a dozen firms the committee chose Joslin Lesser as the Project Manager. Working with the Project
Manager, a request was sent out for design services in December and 14 proposals were returned. The Massachusetts
School Building Authority Design Selection Panel and the Lunenburg School Building Committee then met to
recommend an architect for hiring. Mr. Mackin stated that the Lunenburg School Building Committee did not have final
say on the architect but was one of 15 votes on a panel to choose the final architect. The two groups reviewed 10 groups
and 3 finalists were identified. The two groups met again on Feb. 2nd to interview the three finalists. Tappe Associates
was designated as the designer. The LSBC met with Tappe last week to discuss the groups approach and design. Mr.
Mackin stated that they were in the very beginning stages of the Feasibility Study. The study will look at options that will
provide the most educationally sound and cost effective solutions which included renovating the High School, renovating
and expanding the High School into a Middle/High School, construct a new High School, construct a new Middle/High
School and also what grade combinations would be best for the schools. The whole process must be finished with
preliminary designs by September 2013 to go to the MSBA for their approval and then to the voters of Lunenburg in a
Special Town Meeting for their approval. Mr. Mackin said that a series of meetings would be set up with the firm's
Educational Consultant for educational vision and planning. These meetings would include interviews with current
students, parents, teachers, administrators, local officials, & townspeople, on March 6th, 7th and 13th 9-3 at the Thomas
C. Passios School. Mr. Mackin stated that the new school should not be considered just school buildings but rather
community buildings.
