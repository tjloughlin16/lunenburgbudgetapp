4 Quarter Financial Report for FY2017
Finance Director Brochu reported FY17 was a good year.
Revenue
On the revenue side, the total collections for FY17 were $37,110,671.82 or 101.07% of FY17 estimates through
June 30, 2017. Under Local Receipts, we collected 116.36% of the FY17 Local Receipt estimate. Amounts
received that were above target were Departmental Revenue- Schools (192.59% or $64,810.30 more); Licenses
and Permits (124.36% or $60,631.31 more); Motor Vehicle Excise (116.43% or $253,604.29 more); Penalties
and Interest (113.02% or $23,367.87 more); and Miscellaneous Non-Recurring revenue (150.84% or
$156,417.75 more).
Some categories that we received below what was estimated are Fines and Forfeits (63.01% or $13,205.89
below); Local Option Meals Tax (85.25% or $17,413.99 below); Departmental Revenue-Other (87.98% or
$33,900.51 below) and Investment Income (83.17% or $9,098.25 below).
Under Taxes - we received 99.02% or $241,667.06 less than the levy estimate. We also received $96,616.58 in
tax lien revenue which wasn't included in the estimate tax levy. Under State Aid, we received 100.65% or
$50,285.99 more than the state aid estimate due to an increase in our charter tuition reimbursement.
Expenditures
We expended/encumbered $36,811,166.27 (99.49%) of budgeted expenditures as of June 30, 2017. We had
appropriation closeouts of $358,084.74. State assessments were $170,787 higher than the cherry sheet
estimates. This was largely due to school choice/charter tuition for students going out of district. A large portion
of the appropriation closeout is in the school department and represents the net increase in choice/charter
assessments and charter reimbursement revenue.

Architectural Preservation District Commission (APDC) /Continuation of Guidelines Review
Chairman Richard McGrath asked if there were any questions or comments.
Mr. Ebersole noted the property owners in the APD were notified via letter that the APD guidelines discussion
was tonight. APDC Member Cullen Dwyer stated one issue that caused some confusion before was shutters;
they were included in the design standards along with a description of what was historically valuable for
shutters. Previous comments were that it was inappropriate, so it was moved to the glossary or definitions
section. There is a description on how to hang shutters, but it is no longer part of the design standards.
Previously under Reconsideration/Reapplication, it had read the commission had seven days to reconsider any
decision that it made. That was in case any new information came to light or in the case they wanted to
reconsider. After consideration the APDC decided it wasn't necessary as it caused confusion; so it was
removed. Now the decision will be final, but the applicant can reapply. It was noted in the bylaw at the end of
the guidelines that a space needs to be inserted between section 9 and 10 and between section 10 and 11.
Mr. McQuaid had expressed a concern about mentioning means and methods of construction at the last meeting.
It has been rephrased under Submission Requirements to read, "Plans including designs and methods of
construction, use of joinery, fasteners, sealants and adhesives may all be relevant. Architectural plans,
elevations and a selection of appropriate details to depict the proposed scope of work as it relates to the
requirements of the bylaw."
Another suggested change was made under APDC Approval Decision Process, in addition to other reasons to
approve a project, to take into account the energy efficiency needs of a building and anything to preserve the
structure itself. In addition, "Legally mandated alterations are also necessary, including those as a result of code
requirements and operational requirements surrounding a change in use." In the same section under #4,
previously it tried to quantify what the cost of repairs would be versus cost of historic value. They modified that
to make it less arbitrary.
Mr. McQuaid referred to wording under The Meaning of Historic Value; specifically Authenticity of materials
and methods of construction. He believed we should be judging the historic appropriateness versus the
authenticity because clearly if you are buying a replica piece to place on the building, it is not authentic material
but is appropriate to place there based on the style of the home and the area. He had suggested renaming that
section to Appropriateness of Materials and changing the first line to say, "Features of a property are deemed
appropriate if they are made from materials visually appropriate to the historical period and style of the
property." Clearly you are not going to find a 200 year old piece of millwork to stick on there.
Mr. Dwyer stated they did discuss that and felt it was covered in the next section titled Appropriateness within
the context. We stood our ground on this because we came up with various scenarios. A few years back the
United Parish had proposed to tear down their steeple and replace it with a fiberglass replica so that a
telecommunications company could install an antennae. The deal fell through but it would be a loss to the
community if it had happened as the the steeple would not be "real" anymore.
Mr. McQuaid stated he could appreciate that but if it is wood or vinyl, you would not know unless you went up
to it and knocked on it. Does it really add value to the commuity if it is wood? The vinyl one is going to last
longer and look better longer. The church over there has viny! siding on it now and most people couldn't tell the
difference. He stated sometimes aspiring to have people be authentic in material is somewhat burdensome and
unrealistic. Mr. Dwyer responded the whole idea of historic value and the purpose of this bylaw is intangible
values. Mr. McQuaid stated he would like to hear from some of the property owners in the district as this could
be a burden to the people who live in the district every day.
===PAGE 4===
Dave Rodgers, 82 Highland Street, stated he resides in the district. He does not have any issue with the
commission members but wanted to register his displeasure of being included in the district. He read a
statement stating more regulations, intervention and costs are being imposed on our residents without any
regard to the common sense and historic responsibility of the residents. Can anyone tell of specific instances
over the past 5-20 years that have negatively impacted our town and thus require this type of regulation? The
residents clearly care for their homes; they maintain them and are proud of them. Frankly they don't wait for
five years to paint the Town Hall, the center of our community. After reviewing the guidelines, he had some
questions. Has the board read the entire guidelines document? The board responded yes. He asked if the board
was familiar with the style guidelines referrenced on page eight. Point was made this was an opportunity for
public comment. Mr. Rodgers stated the reason he asked is because this is referenced in the guidelines by which
all of the designs will be judged. Mr. McQuaid responded what these field guides are saying is these
architectural styles are important to evaluate the properties. He believed the commissioners would use these to
help the applicant better understand their property and so everyone can be on the same page.
Mr. Rodgers noted in the body of the document it states they shall concern mostly with the alterations that are
visible from the public right of way. They also note that even though you may not need a building permit you
still should contact the APDC. He asked if vinyl or aluminum shutters would be allowed. Mr. Dwyer stated
shutters are exempt from the bylaw. Mr. Rodgers asked if vinyl doors and windows are allowed. Mr. Dwyer
stated they will be allowed as long as they don't degrade the historical value of the property. Mr. Rodgers asked
who specifically makes that determination and what part will the homeowner have in that process? Mr. Dwyer
stated the homeowner is responsible for bringing forth that proposal and communicating it to the APDC. The
APDC will make the decision whether it maintains the historical value of the property or if it is a downgrade, in
which case we wouldn't permit it unless it was deemed necessary.
Mr. Rodgers noted there is a significant difference in cost between wood and vinyl windows. Further discussion
ensued about sliding glass doors and greenhouses.
Mr. Rodgers asked about sandblasting brick. Chair McGrath responded sandblasting destroys the brick surface.
Mr. Rodgers asked if metal roofs would be permitted. Mr. McGrath stated it would change the historical
appearance, so no. Rodgers asked about metal chimneys: McGrath responded not viewed from the front. Mi.
Rodgers stated 82 Highland Street has two metal chimneys and has for years. Discussion ensued about existing
structures being exempt because they existed prior to the bylaw acceptance and then moved on to exempt
temporary buildings
Mr. Rodgers moved on to Hearing Procedure wording in #2, presentation by the applicant, which may be
limited "at the discretion of the Chair", #3 and #5, at "the direction of the Chair." Mr. Dwyer believed it was
standard language for hearings. Mr. Rodgers disagreed. Mr. Rodgers pointed out the wording that APDC
makes the determination if the alteration is necessary. Mr. Rodgers expressed concern about that wording and
inspections by the APDC. How many inspectors do we need? Discussion ensued on how and why the district
bylaw was created. Mr. Rodgers moved on to the flow chart, particularly the appeal process in accordance with
General Laws. If you are turned down by the APD you have got to spend more money and go to Superior
Court and fight your case. If someone wants to come in and do something, they have to have an architect draw
up the plans. He was concerned about what kind of money people are going to have to spend. Mr. Rodgers'
final comment was there are several gray areas here that need additional work.
Land Use Director Adam Burney stated the language referring to the Superior Court is in accordance with MA
General Laws and is from the bylaw itself as was suggested by Town Counsel. He added there is no Board
within the land use departments other than the Conservation Commission where an appeal of that board's
decision is not through Superior Court. It is standard practice for municipal boards. He noted if the APDC
denies the application, these guidelines do provide the opportunity to modify your application and resubmit
your application. In so much as features of structures that exist now and them being grandfathered or exempt,
Burney believed within the effective date language, there could be some language added in or a separate section
that alludes to features/structures existing prior to the date of the approval of these regulations shall be
considered conforming until such time as an alteration/modification/demolition is proposed. This would be a
catchall so prior to whatever effective date these become effective you have a statement that eliminates the
concern that someone might have to retroactively fit out their wood stove chimney with a brick exterior.
Discussion ensued on demolition by neglect situations.

Mr. Burney addressed the hearing procedure. This is standard language on many rules and regulations. The
Chair always has the right to conduct the hearing; it's a facilitating of the meeting. Regarding motor homes,
there are subtle differences in each of those four categories based on where it is parked and whether or not it is
blocking the façade.
Nathan Lockwood, 91 Main Street, stated he is a homeowner in the district. He thanked the APDC for working
on the guidelines. He stated it would be really useful to understand the difference between a downgrade and
upgrade to a structure in the guidelines document. Having some clarity, for example, on already existing vinyl
siding and replacing it with vinyl siding if it looks okay would give homeowners some assurance. Also, the
painting of brick is very common. What if a building's brick is already painted? Look at the Ritter Building. He

behind it would be usetul.
Mr. McQuaid referred to the APD bylaw wording and stressed the guidelines should not stray from what the
Town approved.
Jamie Anderson, 944 Mass Ave, noted the primary concern was the front appearance of a house. He stated most
people can see three sides of a house from the road and suggested that be clarified. There is some ambiguity in
the wording relating to siding. He was also concerned about the added cost to provide eight sets of plans from
an architect for replacing siding. Mr. McGrath stated that would not be required for minor repairs. He
recommended a pre-meeting with the APD to find out what would be required for your individual situation.
We are here to be the guiding body. Mr. Dwyer stated the APDC has the right to waive any and all
requirements. That would be part of the administrative review. Not all of them are relevant to every project.
Ms. Bertram noted that the wording in the guidelines is more stringent than what was voted on by the Town as a
whole. It is important to understand what the goal was; to keep a visual historic appearance but also use
contemporary technology in the district. When we are talking about window and doors, to have a homeowner
keep to authentic materials could be costly and maintenance prohibitive. She was concerned about the language
in the current regulations. Mi. Ebersole stated there is more work to be done from this discussion; procedural
and substansive. He suggested one more pass at the guidelines.
Mr. McQuaid stated there were some design standards that were in the bylaw that were not covered completely
in the guidelines. He suggested going back to section 9 of the original bylaw design standards and make sure

4 Quarter Financial Report for FY2017
Finance Director Brochu reported FY17 was a good year.
Revenue
On the revenue side, the total collections for FY17 were $37,110,671.82 or 101.07% of FY17 estimates through
June 30, 2017. Under Local Receipts, we collected 116.36% of the FY17 Local Receipt estimate. Amounts
received that were above target were Departmental Revenue- Schools (192.59% or $64,810.30 more); Licenses
and Permits (124.36% or $60,631.31 more); Motor Vehicle Excise (116.43% or $253,604.29 more); Penalties
and Interest (113.02% or $23,367.87 more); and Miscellaneous Non-Recurring revenue (150.84% or
$156,417.75 more).
Some categories that we received below what was estimated are Fines and Forfeits (63.01% or $13,205.89

Chairman Richard McGrath asked if there were any questions or comments.

they were included in the design standards along with a description of what was historically valuable for
shutters. Previous comments were that it was inappropriate, so it was moved to the glossary or definitions
section. There is a description on how to hang shutters, but it is no longer part of the design standards.
Previously under Reconsideration/Reapplication, it had read the commission had seven days to reconsider any
decision that it made. That was in case any new information came to light or in the case they wanted to
reconsider. After consideration the APDC decided it wasn't necessary as it caused confusion; so it was
removed. Now the decision will be final, but the applicant can reapply. It was noted in the bylaw at the end of
the guidelines that a space needs to be inserted between section 9 and 10 and between section 10 and 11.
Mr. McQuaid had expressed a concern about mentioning means and methods of construction at the last meeting.
It has been rephrased under Submission Requirements to read, "Plans including designs and methods of
construction, use of joinery, fasteners, sealants and adhesives may all be relevant. Architectural plans,
elevations and a selection of appropriate details to depict the proposed scope of work as it relates to the
requirements of the bylaw."
Another suggested change was made under APDC Approval Decision Process, in addition to other reasons to
approve a project, to take into account the energy efficiency needs of a building and anything to preserve the
structure itself. In addition, "Legally mandated alterations are also necessary, including those as a result of code
requirements and operational requirements surrounding a change in use." In the same section under #4,
previously it tried to quantify what the cost of repairs would be versus cost of historic value. They modified that
to make it less arbitrary.
Mr. McQuaid referred to wording under The Meaning of Historic Value; specifically Authenticity of materials
and methods of construction. He believed we should be judging the historic appropriateness versus the
authenticity because clearly if you are buying a replica piece to place on the building, it is not authentic material
but is appropriate to place there based on the style of the home and the area. He had suggested renaming that
section to Appropriateness of Materials and changing the first line to say, "Features of a property are deemed
appropriate if they are made from materials visually appropriate to the historical period and style of the
property." Clearly you are not going to find a 200 year old piece of millwork to stick on there.
Mr. Dwyer stated they did discuss that and felt it was covered in the next section titled Appropriateness within
the context. We stood our ground on this because we came up with various scenarios. A few years back the
United Parish had proposed to tear down their steeple and replace it with a fiberglass replica so that a
telecommunications company could install an antennae. The deal fell through but it would be a loss to the
community if it had happened as the the steeple would not be "real" anymore.
Mr. McQuaid stated he could appreciate that but if it is wood or vinyl, you would not know unless you went up
to it and knocked on it. Does it really add value to the commuity if it is wood? The vinyl one is going to last
longer and look better longer. The church over there has viny! siding on it now and most people couldn't tell the
difference. He stated sometimes aspiring to have people be authentic in material is somewhat burdensome and
unrealistic. Mr. Dwyer responded the whole idea of historic value and the purpose of this bylaw is intangible
values. Mr. McQuaid stated he would like to hear from some of the property owners in the district as this could
be a burden to the people who live in the district every day.

PAYT Program Update-Bags fees, Retail Distribution Program, Extended Contract with WasteZero and
Recycling Textiles
Ms. Lemieux reported the Pay As You Throw extra large trash bag discussion is a continuance from a previous
meeting. If the Town was to do a pilot program introducing the extra large pay as you throw bag, a 40 gallon
clear plastic bag/2.0 mil thickness; the minimum order would be 50 cases at $75.50 per case of 200 bags. This
equates to an initial investment of $3775.00 for the Town. In order to recoup the cost of the bags and hauling
charges, Ms. Lemieux proposed charging $20.00 per roll. The 30 pound weight limit would remain for this size.
These bags could be sold at the DPW or at the retail stores that choose to offer them to customers. It was
suggested to put a survey on the town website to solicit any interest from the community regarding the extra
large bags.
Ms. Lemieux presented a new proposal that would be for a Retail Store Distribution Program with WasteZero.
WasteZero is under the MA state contract and has been our vendor since the PAYT program began. To date the
town has gone year to year with no contract with WasteZero. WasteZero has a Retail Store Distribution
program that would ship and deliver cases of bags directly to our retail store locations. This would eliminate
the DPW as the intermediary to monitor inventory, remove the liability of storing inventory on town premises,
and the town would not be liable for paying the upfront costs of bag inventory as every case ordered by the
retailer is billed to that store.
The contract proposal from WasteZero includes transitioning to the Retail Store Distribution with zero cost in
Year 1. The town could revert back to the previous model if we are not satisfied with the Retail Distribution
model. WasteZero has offered the Town a multi-year contract that will provide longer term stability on pricing
for budgeting and the best pricing available. The contract includes transitioning to the Retail Distribution
===PAGE 2===
Program at zero cost in year 1 and the increases to 4 cents per bag in year 2. Year 3 would increase by the
consumer price index plus 3% (based on the current CPI would be a total of a 3.5% increase).
Ms. Lemieux presented a proposal for recycling textiles. The Town could explore a textile recycling program
with a company called Simple Recycling. There would be a different colored PAYT bag provided to households
just for textiles. Simple Recycling trucks follow the same trash collection route on the same day that trash is
picked up. This is a free service and the Town is paid $20/ton. This program has begun in select communities.
Statistics indicate that 85% of textiles are not recycled or donated. The EPA estimates 68 pounds per person of
clothing are thrown away each year.
When asked what happens to the textiles, Courtney Forester of WasteZero stated some clothing is resold in
thrift stores and materials that aren't able to be reused are repurposed into rags, insulation, animal bedding etc.
Other textiles that are not fashionable but in good shape might be shipped to another country. 5% are
contaminated with grease or oil and are trashed. It was suggested to put a survey on the town website to solicit
any interest from the community regarding the extra large bags.
Discussion ensued on the current size PAYT bags, the small 15 gallon size and the large 33 gallon capacity,
both 1.4 mil thick. The proposed extra large bag would be a little taller and 2.0 mil thick. There is also an option
for drawstring style bags (at no extra charge) versus the tie together option on the current ones.
Ms. Forester went into more detail of the retail store distribution program. This program takes the burden of the
process of ordering, storing inventory and delivering bags to retail locations from the DPW. WasteZero
manages inventory, delivery, payments etc. The other benefit is there is no up front cost to the town.
It was decided to get more pricing information on a thicker mil PAYT trash bag but to proceed with the survey
to solicit interest.

T-Mobile Lease Agreement for Town Hall
Ms. Lemieux reported the Town has a signed lease agreement for the wireless communications equipment in
the interior of the Town Hall attic and exterior on the clock tower with T-Mobile. The original lease was signed
in 2000 and an amended lease agreement was signed in 2012 and according to the amended lease agreement is
scheduled to renew for another five years on August 31, 2017. After review by our current Town Counsel, it
was discovered that the original lease agreement was invalid because the term in that agreement was for a total
of 25 years and was in excess of the ten years maximum that was allowed under the law in 2000. Also, there
are no records that it was put out to bid and this term exceeded the annual town meeting vote on May 13, 2000
that authorized "the Selectmen to enter into one or more ten year leases or licenses of town owned real property
for telecommunications antennas and equipment".
Town Counsel has advised that the lease be put out to bid to abide by procurement laws with a term not to
exceed ten years as authorized by the town meeting vote. The other option is to go back to town meeting to
request authorization for a term not to exceed 30 years. We would still be required to go out to bid under this
option. The benefits of a longer term agreement are that it is more desirable for communications companies and
ensures a long term revenue source for the Town. Since it is unknown whether town offices will continue to
operate out of this building, we could include language in the contract with the selected party that transfers the
lease agreement to subsequent owners or an escape clause that if the property transfers ownership, the lease can
be terminated. According to town counsel Costa we could wait until fall town meeting and operate under the
same conditions. He believes T-Mobile would agree to that. Discussion ensued on how to proceed.

It was decided to research what other communities are doing for this type of lease agreement. A memorandum
