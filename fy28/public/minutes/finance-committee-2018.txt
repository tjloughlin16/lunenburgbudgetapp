1. Terri said that, while the meeting agenda had a 6:00 start time, the agenda header had the start time as
7:00. After discussion with the Town Manager, Terri decided to begin hearing the FY2019 budget
presentations at 6:00. But she won't call the committee to order until 7:00 pm. Only after the

agreed to remain after their presentations to answer questions.
FY 2019 Budget Presentations
2. Public Access Cable Committee (PACC) FY2019 Budget presentation by Steven Walker, Chair of the Public
Access Cable Committee
a. Income:
• Funds come from the franchise fee that Comcast pays quarterly. This fee averages slightly over
$28,000 a quarter.
• Steve projects an FY2018 ending balance in this franchise fee account of $253,500.
• PACC is in negotiations with Comcast over a renewal of the franchise fee including the increase in
that fee from 2.5% to 5% which would be in line with the franchise fee to other towns.
b. Expenses for FY19:
• Operations: $142,600 including salaries, health insurance, and lease of studio space.
• A proposal to Increase staff hours would mean higher operation costs, due in part to increased
cost of benefits.
• Capital: $50,000 - Studio improvements. replacing/upgrading equipment.
• An article on the FY19 ATM warrant requests $192,600 to meet these anticipated expenditures.
3. Monty Tech FY2019 Budget presentation by Sheila Harrity, Superintendent of Monty Tech and Tammy
Crocket, Business Manager
a. FY2019 Assessment for Lunenburg:
• This assessment is largely set by the state required minimum.
• Although the per-student cost increased by about $500 or (see point below), Lunenburg's enrollment
in Monty Tech declined by 10 students to 82 students. Thus, the Town's assessment declined from
$876,381 to $824,011, a decrease of 5.9% or $52,370. For context, Monty Tech is proposing a 3.2%
increase in its total FY19 budget.
• Monty Tech's per student assessment: FY2018 $9,525.88, FY2019: $10,048.91.

3. Review of financial policies: Board discussion about the tax levy and the budget:
George asked for an explanation of the "Tax Levy". Is the Levy is last years base, plus new growth, plus
the annual 2.5% tax increase?
The Town Accountant replied: Back out prior years debt exclusions that was raised within the levy, your
get to the base from the prior year, plus new growth, plus the 2.5%, becomes the new base for the
current fiscal year, and then what ever we have to pay in that year for debt exclusions is added to that
dollar amount to come up for the total levy.
The base can only increase 2.5%. An override is needed to raise taxes over the 2.5%.
Discussion on the needs and objectives of taxing to the levy: We tax to the max levy because of the
demand for service staff levels, the needs of departments we hear about during the budget presentation
every year.
General discussion/review of financial policies: stabilization funds, debt, OPEB, bond rating criteria of
bond rating agencies. Reviewing how our debt load has an effect on our bond rating.
Marc is looking at several other local town budgets and doing a comparison to Lunenburg.

Article 2 Raise and Appropriate Transfer for New Growth to Special Purpose Stabilization
Fund- Town Manager said there is additional local aid as well as additional new growth
and personal property and change in state assessments and cherry sheet offset numbers
for a total of $199,227. The recommendation would be to raise and appropriate transfer
of that amount into the Special Purpose Stabilization Fund. And that fund is to help offset

Dave Passios, 56 Whiting Street, asked, what the committees' thoughts are on new
growth being considered a one-time revenue or something that augments the operating
budget and is there for the future versus free cash that may or may not be there?
Terri said Mr. Passios was correct and John responded that new growth was a result of
the property taxes that occur during the FY the new growth occurs. Then the houses that
generate the new growth that year become base revenue for succeeding years.
Terri replied as well saying Yes it is recurring revenue because right now we are treating
it as it is not going to happen again which is not true, but its basically money in our
pocket that we don't want to spend for the sake of spending. She went on to say the
part that makes the new growth unpredictable is the fact that the town can predict from
past years that we would have $300k budgeted in new growth but then actually have
$330k in new growth, this would leave the town with $30k left over to play around with.
However, it could also go the opposite way. The town could predict and budget for
$300K in new growth but the actual could be $270k in new growth leaving the town in
the hole to find $30k. The town manager also commented that if you do not raise and
appropriate the new growth it is lost.
Mr. Passios commented that he wanted that clarified so he could take it one step further
to ask why we would not take that new growth this year and plug it into facilities
maintenance where we have so many issues that we let ride for so long that we augment
that budget for the FY and get some of the repairs done that are causing these building
to deteriorate rather than putting it into the stabilization fund where it earmarks it for
one thing and one thing only. Town manager did comment that we did put an additional
$40k into the facilities budget this year but since the staff of the DPW is short, she does
not think it would be a good idea to put more money into the facilities budget without
having someone to maintain the buildings.
