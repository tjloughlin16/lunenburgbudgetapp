Briana Proctor, 141 Gilchrest Street I am a 2011 graduate of Lunenburg High School and I also 
coach girls’ basketball and softball at the High School.  I am here to advocate to save the proposed 
administrative cuts to LMHS.  It is incredibly shortsighted to remove an administrator from the 
building with the largest population of students, who have the most complex challenges as they are 
navigating adolescence.  As a coach at the high school, I see the time the administrators spend 
outside of school hours to help monitor sporting events, a responsibility that administrators at the 
Primary and Elementary schools frankly do not have.  Some of these administrators are current and 
future coaches for the school. Students and athletes whose coaching role would likely be lost, should 
they no longer be employed here.  Administrators’ jobs have become very complex given societal 
changes, and they are an integral part of supporting teachers to create a safe and effective learning 
environment.  To cut a school of around 800 students down to three administrators, while leaving 
smaller schools, with less complex problems fully staffed would harm both the students and the 
teachers at LMHS.  Should this committee not be swayed in the LMHS position not to be a saved, I 
hope that you at least inform the person on the chopping block, so the entirety of the staff is not 
living in a constant state of anxiety. 
Laura Barooshian, 389 Mulpus Road I am here to advocate for the fifth grade music program cuts 
and salary cuts.  The cut to the program is going to be devastating on our program.  The upcoming 
fifth grade program is bigger than it has been in the past five years, it 20% bigger.  The fifth grade 
program feeds directly into the Middle and High school band program.  The kids that have allied 
together, the music program, the marching program have worked incredibly hard.  Multiple studies 
are done on the impact that music has on kids as young as four years old.  Their interpersonal skills, 
their leadership skills, their math, it impacts all academic curriculum.  It is really shortsighted, it is 
very small amounts of money to keep our fifth grade program.  I know you are wrestling a very large 
gap between spending and the budget, and I know it needs to come from somewhere, but I just really 
want you to take a hard look at some of the smaller dollar things that should maybe be used with free 
cash.  I ask you to look at which programs are being maybe inequitably harmed.

4. Chair’s Report The select board is meeting at Town Hall in 7 minutes.  They are discussing the FY 
25 budget, FY26 budget capital plan and annual town meeting warrant articles and most likely will 
be discussing the override.  State House news service this morning stated that two weeks before the 
policy is set to expire, House democrats moved Monday to temporarily extend pandemic era laws 
allowing public meetings to offer remote access.  The House Ways and Means Committee began 
advancing a bill that would keep in place the language granting public bodies flexibility to hold 
meetings virtually or in hybrid formats, as well as measures lowering the number of people 
necessary for a quorum at Town Meeting, and allowing representative town meetings to be held with 
remote participation.  Those policies are set to expire March 31st the house bill would push that 
sunset date to June 30, 2027.  There is a citizen’s petition for an override that was submitted by TJ

raise and appropriate $2,099,337 to supplement Lunenburg Public Schools budget for fiscal year

by the difference between the level service budget and the town managers budget.  Additionally the
