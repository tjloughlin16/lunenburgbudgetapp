a. 7:10 PM 49 Cliffview T errace CAD-25-25  <Video link> 
A Public Hearing on a Conservation Administrator’s Determination regarding removal of 11 dead hemlock trees. They were 
probably killed by HWA over the last decade. These trees are a safety hazard to persons and property. The trees will be 
selectively remov ed without disturbing the surrounding trees and vegetation.  
 
Dick Fisher presented th e project.  Herlin's comments were read into the record  as follows: 
(11) hemlock trees were observed to be dead, exhibiting  extensive branch dieback, lack of live foliage, and overall 
structural decline. The condition of these trees is consistent with long -term impacts from Hemlock Woolly Adelgid 
(HW Eleven A) infestation, which has affected hemlock populations throughout the region over the past decade. The 
dead hemlocks were identified throughout the wooded slope and along the shoreline edge. Due to their height, 
location, and deteriorated condition, these trees pose a safety hazard to persons and property, particularly durin g 
wind, snow, or ice events.  
Motion: M. Wilson, to approve as presented with the following stipulations:  
1. Tree removal shall be limited to the eleven (11) dead hemlock trees identified during the site visit.  
2. Trees shall be selectively removed to minimize disturbance to surrounding trees, understory 
vegetation, and soils.   
3. Stumps and root systems shall remain in place unless otherwise authorized.  
4. No equipment staging, stockpiling, or debris disposal shall occur within resource areas.  
5. Any additional tree removal or disturbance beyond what is authorized will require further review by 
the Conservation Agent or Conservation Commission  
Second  T. Doucette 
Vote: A. Burnap – AYE M. Wilson – AYE M. Larouche - AYE T. Doucette – AYE 
 J. Pewtherer – AYE

Tree with the split trunk is deemed  a hazard tree and presents an imminent risk to public safety and property. Removal of

during wind or snow events.  
Additionally, while the tree crew is mobilized on site, the removal of dead, rotted, or hazardous branches from adjacent tree s 
is considered reasonable and appropriate as a preventative safety measure, provided that such work is limited to pruning and 
does not result in additional ground disturbance or expanded tree removal beyond what is necessary to address safety 
concerns.

areas.
