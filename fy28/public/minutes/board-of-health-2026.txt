MEETING MINUTES 
Monday, February 2, 2026 
 
The Board of Health met at 4:30 pm at the Ritter Memorial Building  
 
Members Present:  Matt Proctor, Paul Fortin, Lorraine Rudd and Nick Pirro.   
 
MINUTES APPROVED 
 
None 
 
 12 TOWN ROAD 
 
There is an open housing complaint on this property.  Most of the violations have been completed.  Still 
outstanding are floor repair and hooking up the dishwasher.  More time is required for the floor as the 
existing flooring has been discontinued and is of a difference thickness than what is available now.  This 
requires the whole floor to be replaced.  The dishwasher would need electric available.  Electric is not 
currently available for the dishwasher.  The owner is in the process of hiring an electrician to make 
electric available. 
 
After discussion and on motion by Rudd and second by Fortin, a 30 day extension was granted.  All in 
favor. 
 
922 FLAT HILL ROAD 
 
Larry Greene met with the board on behalf of the owner.  This is a small 1/3 acre property that slopes to 
a wetland.  It is a teardown/rebuild.  This will also need a new well. A Presby system is proposed. 
 
Variances requested: 
 
Offset to groundwater from 5 feet to 3 feet; 
Offset to wetland from 100 feet to 55 feet 
Use of a Presby 
 
Well Setback:  50 feet to 39 feet side yard 
              50 feet to 12 feet front 
 
After discussion and on motion by Pirro and second by Rudd, the board voted to approve the well 
variances.  All in favor. 
 
After discussion and on motion by Fortin and second by Pirro, the board voted t o approve the septic 
variances with the addition of a two bedroom deed restriction.  All in favor. 
 
106 BEAL STREET 
 
Dan Proctor met with the board on behalf of the  owner.  This system is in failure.  The estimated 
groundwater is at 30 inches.  A Presby system is proposed. 
===PAGE 2===
 
Variances requested are: 
Offset from 4 feet to 3 feet 
Use of an IA system 
Sieve analysis 
 
After discussion and on motion by Fortin and second by Pirro the variances were approved.  All in favor. 
 
150 FLYNN STREET 
 
Chris MacKenzie met with the board on behalf of the owner.  This system is in failure.  Groundwater is 
at 60 inches.  This is a small lot on Lake Shirley.  Information in the Board of Hea lth files suggest that 
this is a three bedroom house.  The board has accepted it as such. 
 
Variances requested are: 
 
Use of an IA system

The Board of Health met at 4:30 pm at the Ritter Memorial Building

MINUTES APPROVED 
 
None 
 
 NASHOBA ASSOCIATED BOARDS OF HEALTH 
 
Jim Garreffi, Director and Jenna Montgomery met with the board for their Nashoba Assocates Boards 
of Health annual update.  The discussing included the large increase to member town ’s assessment.  
Causes cited the rising cost of health insurance, increase in salaries, shutting the nursing service as well 
as the town of Littleton leaving Nashoba  and additional discussion about the ongoing State pension 
issues.  Jim Garreffi stated that there are presently 3 bills before the legislature to try and resolve the 
pension issues. 
 
The full presentation can be found on the Town of Lunenburg website, Board of Health Page, titled 
Nashoba Associated Boards of Health 2024 Annual Report. 
 
225 LEOMINSTER SHIRLEY ROAD 
 
No one was presently on behalf of the owner, however, the variance s requested were minor and in an 
effort to not hold up our resident, the hearing was held. 
 
Variances requested are Depth of System Components from 3 feet to 4.8 feet and the use of a sieve 
analysis. 
 
After discussion and on motion by Rudd and second by Jewell, the variances were approved.  All in 
favor. 
 
Having no further business and on motion by Rudd and second by Proctor the meeting was adjourned at 
5:55 pm.  All in favor.

 1491 MASS AVENUE 
 
Dan Proctor met with the board to request an offset to wetlands less than 100 feet.  This is a new building 
lot.  The property is served by town water. 
 
Offsets are 100 feet to 90 feet of the primary system location and 100 feet to 60 feet on the reserve area. 
 
Andrea Schnepf spoke with the Conservation Administrator , Herlin Jamie, who  indicated that she did 
not believe the Conservation with have an issue with the variance. 
 
 After discussion and on motion by Rudd and second by Jewell, a roll call vote was called as follows: 
 
Rudd – yes 
Pirro – yes 
Jewell – yes 
Proctor – recuse 
 
Variance was approved. 
 
3 CHASE ROAD 
 
Owner Tim Robichaud met w ith the board.  There was a housing complaint open on a basement 
apartment.  All issues have been resolved except how the living space can be advertised.  The space was 
rented as a studio apartment with storage space.  The current tenant is using the stora ge space as their 
bedroom.  The storage space has no windows.  The landlord is asking that the apartment be deemed a 
studio apartment with the tenant utilizing the space however they see fit. 
 
After discussion and on motion by Pirro and second by Jewell, t he board voted to make this apartment 
as studio apartment.  All in favor. 
 
41 NEW WEST TOWNSEND ROAD 
 
===PAGE 2===
Carla Laventure, owner, met with the board on an outstanding housing complaint.  All violations have 
been completed with the exception of water seeping into the unit.  A sump pump was installed but there 
is still water seepage. 
 
Mrs. Laventure stated that there was a large snow bank making the situation more difficult and the water 
table is high.  There is a French drain surrounding the property. 
 
Board member suggested making sure the French drain is working properly, sealing the foundation, 
amongst other suggestions. 
 
After discussion and on motion by Jewell and second by Rudd, the board granted an extension until May 
18th at 4:30 pm at which time a solution must be provided.  All in favor. 
 
PARKS COMMISSION USE OF SEPTIC REPAR FUNDS 
 
In August of 2021 the Board of Health voted to allow the use of the Septic Repair Funds (old grant 
money) be used to replace/repair the septic system at Marshall Park.  Alt hough there was a design plan 
for a new septic system approved, the project was never completed.  There are no current plans to move 
forward with any projects at Marshall Park. 
 
After discussion and on motion by Rudd and second by Pirro, the board voted to take back the use of the 
Septic Repair Fund for use by the Board of Health.  All in favor. 
 
Having no further business and on motion by Rudd and second by Proctor the meeting was adjourned at 
5:30 pm.  All in favor.

BURRAGE STREET 
 
Sharon Jordan requested time on our agenda to discuss the Penniman property on Burrage Street.  Mrs. 
Jordan is unwell so Mr. Scott Doucette, a neighbor is present in her place.   Also present were Laur en 
Passerelli Eric Powell and Kim Duffy.

development of the Penniman property on Burrage Street.  There was a request to include this  property 
in the Lunenburg Water district, which was denied. 
 
There are 2 monitoring wells on the property currently that cannot be converted to drinking water wells 
without the approval of either Nashoba Associates Boards of Health of the Lunenburg Board of Health.

which request has been denied in the past.

property can stay in its present undisturbed form as a meadow or open space but development would 
require DEP clearance.

for by the contractor. 
 
The parties present had no further questions.

MEETING MINUTES 
Monday, May 18, 2026 
 
The Board of Health met at 4:30 pm at the Ritter Memorial Building  
 
Members Present:  Perry Jewell, Matt Proctor, Lorraine Rudd and Nick Pirro 
 
MINUTES APPROVED 
 
May 4, 2026 
 
On motion by Pirro and second by Rudd the minutes were approved with a minor change.  All in favor. 
 
7 SEQUOIA DRIVE 
 
Steve Babineau, owner, met with the board.  A new garage with an ADU above is being proposed.  The 
house has a 4 bedroom septic system.  Upon review, there is a question of how many total rooms can be 
in the combined house/ADU.  In the count by Agent Cara Olejarz, there are too many rooms on the total 
property, requiring a deed restriction. 
 
After discussion, it was determined that the ADU is two rooms but what is being used as a storage space 
is in fact heated and can be considered living space.  This requires a deed restriction limiting the house 
to 4 bedrooms and 10 rooms total. 
 
On motion by Pirro and second by Rudd the board voted to allow a 4 bedroom, 10 room total deed 
restriction.  All in favor. 
 
20 PINE ACRES ROAD 
 
The property is in failure.  The system proposed is a pipe and gravel system with the only variance 
required being a ground water offset. 
 
After discussion and on motion by Rudd and second by Pirro, the variance was approved.  All in favor. 
 
23 ROUND ROAD 
 
The owners met with the board.  They are building a new house and replacing the existing shallow well.  
The property is 1.2 acres but is only 100 feet wide.  Even if you put the well in the exact middle of the 
property, you cannot meeting the required 50 feet offset to property line.  They are requesting a well 
variance on either side of 50 feet to 49 feet. 
 
After discussion and on motion by Pirro and second by Rudd, the variances were approved.  All in favor. 
 
Having nor further business the meeting adjourned at 5:30 pm.

OLD PRIMARY SCHOOL – 30 SCHOOL STREET 
===PAGE 2===
 
The Food Pantry is in discussions to find a new location due to the decommis sioning of the Passios 
School.  One of the options and the most available location is the Old Primary School land.  When the 
school was decommission and eventually knocked down, the decision was made to retain the septic 
system for future use as a park.   A Title 5 was completed.  The system is a pump system.  The pumps 
need replacing as well as the property being reconnected to public water.  There is a stub at the end of

food service permit through Nashoba Associated Boards of Health.
