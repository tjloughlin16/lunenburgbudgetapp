LUNENBURG PUBLIC LIBRARY DIRECTOR’S REPORT 
 
   
 
 
   Staff Update 
Muir and Sienne welcomed a baby boy on Thursday, December 18th. I have not received official notice 
of his leave starting yet, but it is understood by myself and the Town Manager, Jen Warren-Dyment, 
to be underway in his continued absence.  
An offer letter has been extended to Minna Scholten for the Library Assistant – Children's Room 
position. She has accepted and start dates are being negotiated. Minna may work some Fridays in 
January for initial training but is anticipating availability to accept the full schedule for the position 
during the week of January 26th. We are excited to welcome her to our team! 
A second offer letter for temporary employment has also been extended to Debra Laffond to start 
immediately. She will work less than 20 hours per week as a regular schedule, and will be hired as a 
SAP (salary admin plan) Staff Librarian. Her assistance will be invaluable in covering desk shifts and 
providing much needed coverage. 
The Teen Librarian search has been suspended during Muir’s leave. I anticipate that we will need to 
repost the position upon his return, as I cannot fill in for the hiring decision process during the 
interim. In the meantime, staff are prepared to address teen behavior as needed, and we have two 
community volunteers to assist with some much-needed programming. One is running a board game 
club on Wednesday afternoons and a Dungeons and Dragons club on Friday afternoons. The other 
volunteer will assist Joslyn with the homeschool craft programs. 
   Programs 
A major success for us recently was the 2nd annual Winter Artisan Showcase on Sunday, December 
7th. We hosted over 40 vendors and were consistently busy during the market hours of 9:00 am to 
1:00 pm. Many vendors reported record sales, and our Friends group received $1061.50 in donations 
from the bake sale and the organizer Kate Ravellette. This is sure to be an annual tradition that will 
continue to grow and improve with time! 
For adults, we held another very popular puzzle swap on November 15th with a record number of 765 
puzzles available to attendees. That same day was also our Small Works art exhibit reception, which 
kicked off the annual exhibit for 2025. There have been a few sales so far, but many more 
compliments on the beautiful artwork in the Wallace room. This is another successful exhibit for the 
library and the local artist community. 
This winter we are also partnering with the Ashland Public Library to host 11 virtual programs via 
Zoom in January and February. These are promoted through a special brochure available at the desk 
and will be included in the newsletter to gain traction with our digital audiences.  
Teen events continue to be sparse with the absence of staff in that department; however, we did 
offer a successful felting program on November 18th and a CPR training class run by a member of the 
Lunenburg Fire Department/EMS on November 29th. Joslyn has taken over one of the tween book 
clubs to keep it running in the interim, while the older teen book club is unfortunately canceled.

LUNENBURG PUBLIC LIBRARY DIRECTOR’S REPORT 
 
   
 
In addition to the extra book club, Joslyn has been providing excellent programs for kids and families 
in the children’s department. Story time attendance remains steady for both sessions, and the two 
main programs for December were substantial hits for the library. The Family Ornament Craft on 
December 4th took place after the town tree lighting, and 76 people stopped by in the cold weather 
to warm up with hot chocolate and make their annual ornament. A few weeks later we hosted the 
family Gingerbread House program to much success, as usual. These popular programs are staples of 
holiday programming, and it’s encouraging to see the engagement continue through the years. 
Coming up: 1/6 Magical Winter Lights (a magic show for families), 1/8 & 1/22 Yoga and Breathwork 
Class, 1/10 Winter Sowing Workshops, 1/20 Teen Nautical Crafting, 1/24 Golden Oldies Sock Hop Party, 
1/30 Winter Salve Workshop, 1/31 Toddler Yoga. 
   Outreach 
We did not attend or participate in any outreach events in November; however, we did spend a good 
deal of time discussing our plans moving forward. Melanie has planned a second event at 
Smokestack Roasters on Sunday, January 18th to reprise the Library of Things petting zoo program 
during a more popular time. Jaci Nicoll, one of the co-owners, is supportive of libraries and assisting 
us in outreach. Developing this partnership is a goal for 2026.  
A follow-up event at Tri Town in February 2026 is also in the works. We want to capitalize on the 
interest we saw in October by delving deeper into some digital resources accessible with a library 
card. Melanie is preparing a presentation on Libby that should find some success with this 
demographic.  
▪ Building & Maintenance 
No changes that I am aware of to report.  
▪ Technology 
CWMARS has suggested that all libraries with a fiber line work towards CIPA compliance. With the 
developments over the years with filtering software, this seems like a reasonable course to enable 
some significant discounts. This work was completed on Thursday, December 4th.
