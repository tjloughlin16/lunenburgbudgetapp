 Open Space Meeting Minutes 
September 22, 2020 
 
Committee Members Present: Karin Menard, Paula Bertram, Brandon Kibbe, Mike Toohill, Sarah Cammer, 
Lynne Man, Bob Pease. 
 
1.  Call to Order  -  Meeting called to order by the Chairman at 6:03 p.m.  Chairman read the notice 
regarding remote meetings which was included on the duly posted agenda. 
 
2.  Review and approve minutes from 8 /20 /2020 meeting  -  The minutes were unanimously accepted 
by roll call vote with the condition that the spelling of Lynne Man's name be corrected. 
 
3. 250 Howard Street  Update and Alternatives Discussion with Prospective Buyers - Previously the 
Open Space Committee had made a recommendation to the Board of Selectmen to exercise the 
Town's right of first refusal.  Brandon Kibbe attended the Board of Selectmen meeting on 9/15/20 to 
discuss this property.  At that time Cesira Newcomb and Joe Normandin, prospective owners, indicated 
they were amenable to a solution that would allow develo pment of the property and conveyance of a 
portion of the property to the Community. Brandon walked the property on Friday with the prospective 
owners.   Records indicate that in 1833 the property was abandoned for agricultural use and became 
forested.   Br andon shared a map with the Committee that included several possible property line 
delineations.  If the Town were to acquire 100 feet from the Mulpus Brook, that would equate to 2.3 
Acres, 200 feet would be 2.74 Acres and 250 ft would be 3.40 Acres. Follo wing discussion, Bob Pease 
made a motion to recommend to the Selectmen that they enter into an agreement with the prospective 
buyers to acquire by gift a portion of the property extending 200 feet beyond the Mulpus Brook.  Paula 
Bertram seconded.  Passed unanimously by roll call vote.  
 
The warrant for the special town meeting closes next week, therefore, a placeholder article for 
acquisition of this property has been placed on the warrant. In order to pass over the article an 
agreement with the prospective buyers must be reached as soon as possible. Bob Pease will reach out 
to the Town Manager and Town Counsel.  Joe Normandin informed the Committee that they will have 
three of the bounds verified and  develop an ANR plan. Granite markers are proposed to del ineate the 
property line following the gift of property to the Town. 
 
4. 671 West Street Parcel - Other General Municipal Lands Next Steps  – Committee received 
correspondence from the Town Manager regarding several parcels that abutters are interested in 
acquiring:  
 
 51 Parmenter Road:  Corner lot about .3 acres that has access to Lake Shirley.   
 247 Highland Street Rear:  3.7 acres that was taken through tax title.  A resident on Chestnut Street 
inquired about purchasing this property.   
 860 Reservoir Road: A n abutter inquired about purchasing this 6.7 acre parcel.   This parcel was 
taken through tax title 
 690 Goodrich Street Rear:   An abutter would like to purchase.   This is a 3.7 acre parcel that was 
taken through tax title. 
 671 West Street:  An abutter wishes to purchase a 100 x 150 foot section of this large parcel.   The 
town voted many years ago to sell off pieces of this large parcel to make it more uniform. 
 
Bob Pease informed the Committee that the Conservation Committee would like to see two parcels

conveyed to the Conservation Commission; 111 Mulpus Road Rear which is a 6 acre landlocked parcel 
abutting conservation land and 970 NW Townsend Road Rear a 3.2 acre landlocked parcel surrounded 
by 166 acres of conservation land.  Committee reviewed parcel s.  Bob Pease made a motion to 
recommend that the Board of Selectmen convey said parcels, Lynne Man seconded.  Motion passed 
unanimously by roll call vote. 
 
The Committee reviewed the 671 West Street parcel request by an abutter for conveyance of 100 x 150 
foot portion of the parcel, this conveyance would not affect frontage.  Bob Pease noted that the 
Conservation Commission is interested in the remainder of the parcel.  Brandon informed the 
Committee that there is an article that was voted several  years ago to allow the Selectmen to convey 
portions of this parcel.  Paula Bertram suggested that the Open Space Committee obtain  a copy of the 
article.  Lynne asked how land becomes “general municipal land”.  Brandon explained that the bulk of 
the property is obtained through nonpayment of taxes, the 671 West Street parcel was purchased by 
the Town for a school which was never built.  The Town has conveyed small portions of this parcel in 
the past.  Bob Pease noted that it is not uncommon for abutters to come forward and the Town 
Manager forwards such requests to the Open Space Committee, Conservation and Parks for their 
recommendation. 
 
Dave Passios noted that the Highland Street property may allow access to other conservation land.  
Sarah Cammer commented that while it is key to preserve existing conservation land it is also important 
that we consider other areas of Town that do not have conservation land/open space. 
 
The general consensus of the committee was that we need to take a comprehensive look at land 
classified as “General Municipal Land (teal colored parcels in the Open Space Plan)  and define a 
process to determine what land should be retained by the Town and what land could potentially be 
conveyed.  Therefore, the consensus of the group was to a ct on the 671 West Street request but to 
delay action on others until such time as a process for such requests is developed.  Bob Pease made a 
motion to recommend to the Board of Selectmen to convey the 100 x 150 foot portion of the parcel to 
the abutter a nd defer action on the other requests.  Karin Menard seconded.  Motion passed 
unanimously by roll call vote.  
 
Bob Pease made a motion to recommend to the Board of Selectmen and the Planning Board that they 
empower the Open Space Committee to work with var ious departments to examine all undesignated 
municipal lands in in order to make recommendations on their potential use or possible conveyance.  
Paula Bertram seconded.  Motion passed unanimously by roll call vote. 
 
Paula Bertram commented that she would l ike to visit the various parcels, other members agreed .  
Sarah Cammer noted that there is an undesignated parcel on Fairview Road that connects to 408 
Lancaster which could offer access to the Catacunemaug Brook.   Brandon noted that parcels that are 
owned by the Town can be visited, just inform neighbors/abutters if questioned that you are on the 
Open Space Committee reviewing Town owned land.   
 
Bob Pease left the meeting at 7:00 p.m. 
 
At the October meeting the Committee will review the list of undesigna ted municipal land and then 
extend an invitation to other Boards to discuss.

5. Bright Farms Connection Update – Brandon informed the Committee that he had walked the property 
with the developers representative and they are amenable to allowing continued  trail access via 
conveyance of a portion of the parcel to the Town.  The northeast corner of the parcel would allow 
connection of the Raino and Saliba conservation parcels via an existing skid path.  Karin Menard made 
a motion that the Town Boards see acquisition of the northeast corner of the land by gift to provide

6. Call for Economic Development Committee Nominations  – Lynne Man indicated she may have 
someone who would be interested in serving, she will reach out to them.  Karin Menard indicated she 
may consider participating if we are unable to find another volunteer.  Paula Bertram w ill send out the

7. Public Comment  – Dave Passios informed the Committee that over the Fall/Winter the Parks 
Commission will be working on development of a master plan for Marshall Park.  They plan on 
investigating grant opportunities and reaching out to the community.  Brandon noted a couple of grant 
opportunities that may be of interested the PARC Grant, a 2 year grant, the 1st year devoted to planning 
and the second to implementation and the Fields Pond Grant.  Members of the Comm ittee offered their 
assistance in putting together grant applications.  Dave also stated that the Parks Commission will be 
investigating the possibility of community gardens.   
 
Sarah Cammer suggested the Town investigate grant opportunities for sidewalks.   Paula noted that 
there are grant opportunities that may be available through MRPC for assistance with sidewalks. 
 
The next meeting of the Open Space Committee is scheduled for 10/13/20 at 6:00 p.m. (May change based on 
availability of Zoom license). 
 
At 7:32 p.m. Karin Menard made a motion to adjourn.  Paula Bertram seconded.  Motion passed unanimously 
by roll call vote. 
 
Respectfully submitted, 
Paula Bertram

Open Space Ad Hoc Committee 
Minutes November 24, 2020 
 
The Committee met via Zoom as scheduled with the following members present:  Karin Menard, Bob 
Pease, Lynne Man, Mike Toohill, Brandon Kibbe and Paula Bertram. 
1. Call to Order - Chairman opened the meeting at 6:03 and read the pandemic notice as posted 
on the agenda. 
2. Review/Approve 10/13/2020 Minutes – Bob Pease made a motion to approve the minutes of 
10/13/2020, seconded by Lynne Man.  Approved unanimously by roll call vote. 
3. Discussion with Nashua River Watershed Associated regarding USDA Forest Legacy Program, 
possible regional Application, ways Lunenburg can be involved – Al Futterman of Nashua River 
Watershed and the Wild and Scenic River Stewardship Project explained the Forest Legacy 
Program to the Committee.  The purpose of the Forest Legacy Program is to identify and 
conserve environmentally important forest areas that are threatened by conversion to non-
forest uses.  The program focuses on threatened unprotected land that is an important natural 
resource and has strategic value.  Land can be sold outright or landowners can enter into a 
conservation restriction with a governmental agency.  Lunenburg has several tributaries to the 
Nashua River and Al requested that the Open Space Committee provide a single point of contact 
to assist with development of priority properties in Lunenburg that may be well suited for the 
program.   Al will provide additional written information about the program to the Committee. 
4. Adding Layers to the CAI AxisGIS website, including a trails layer and a Town properties layer – 
Bob Pease informed the Committee that he is investigating adding a trails layer and possibly a 
town properties layer to the Town’s GIS system with the Assessors and the Land Use Director.  
Groton currently utilized Open Street Maps and MRPC is using Mr. Mapper.  Chris Menard, 
resident, suggested that whatever is done should be compatible with other hiking apps such as 
Open Street Maps. 
5. Revision of Conservation Commission land use policy – Bob Pease informed the Committee 
that the Conservation Commission will be holding a public forum on December 2nd to discuss the 
conservation land use policy.  Items to be discussed include hunting, snowmobile use and trail 
maintenance permission.  The agenda will be posted and residents are encouraged to 
participate remotely. 
===PAGE 2===
6. Continuation of discussion regarding 360 Flat Hill Road Chapter 61A Withdrawal Notice; vote 
on recommendation to the Select Board – Brandon relayed that he met with the Board of 
Selectmen a couple of weeks ago regarding this property and had conversations with several 
people in Town and there is a donor who is willing to make a sizable donation to protect this 
property, he has not heard from North County Land Trust.  The property owner is unable to 
negotiate or discuss a lease back program as the property is under agreement and it would be 
breach in contract.  Committee discussed the feasibility of working with North County Land 
Trust on this parcel; North County may be able to assist with bridging the financing, but we 
would need to locate a buyer for farming rights and possibly subdivide the property to assist 
with cost.  It is not feasible to bring all the pieces together to acquire necessary funding and 
agreements to purchase this property and meet the legal timeline.  Following discussion Paula 
Bertram made a motion to recommend to the Selectmen that the Town waive its right of first 
refusal, Mike Toohill seconded, passed unanimously by roll call vote.  Paula asked that the 
Committee hold an information sharing session so that members can learn about the various 
programs and funding sources that may be available to protect desirable land in the future.    
7. Review updated Town-owned property list.  Discuss prioritization for general municipal 
properties and proposed next steps – Committee members identified several parcels for future 
discussion including Fire Road 12, 380 West Street, 1162 Mass Ave, 247 Highland, 690 Goodrich 
Read, 860 Leominster Road, Parmenter Road and 0 Flynn Road.  Committee members will 
research these properties further and review other parcels under general municipal ownership 
for further discussion at the next meeting. 
 
8. Public Comment – 
 Paula Bertram asked about the status of 250 Howard Street.  Brandon responded that an 
agreement has been signed and that Special Town Meeting voted to approve the acquisition 
and passed over exercising its right of 1st refusal.  The buyer is in the process of putting 
together an ANR plan for approval. 
 Chris Menard suggested that the Town needs to take a proactive approach to protecting 
desirable open space such as 360 Flat Hill and that creation of a special stabilization fund 
should be considered so that a funding source is available when property becomes available.  
Chris will offer the suggestion to the Finance Committee and Paula will relay to Capital 
Planning. 
9. Adjourn – Paula Bertram made a motion to adjourn at 8:23 p.m., Lynne Man seconded.  Voted 
unanimously by roll call vote. 
 
Respectfully submitted, 
 
Paula Bertram

Open Space Ad Hoc Committee 
Minutes December 15, 2020 
 
The Committee met via Zoom as scheduled with the following members present:  Karin Menard, Bob Pease, 
Lynne Man, Mike Toohill (Mike left the meeting at 7:00 p.m.), Brandon Kibbe and Paula Bertram (Paula joined 
the meeting at 6:34 p.m.). 
1. Call to Order - - Chairman opened the meeting at 6:04 p.m.  and read the pandemic notice as posted on the 
agenda. 
 
2. Review/ Approve 11/24/2020 Minutes – Bob Pease made a motion to approve the minutes, Mike 
Toohill seconded.  Brandon suggested a minor change in #3 (Discussion with Nashua River Watershed 
Associated regarding USDA Forest Legacy Program, possible regional Application, ways Lunenburg can be 
involved) in minutes; striking a non-profit trust to conservation restriction with a governmental agency.  
Bob Pease amended his motion, Mike Toohill accepted amendment.  Voted unanimously (Paula did not 
vote as she had not yet joined) 
 
3. Review member recommendations for landowner outreach for potential inclusion in a Forest Legacy 
“aggregation” application as presented by Nashua River Watershed Association at 11/24/2020 
meeting – Al had emailed additional information about the program and requested a list of properties 
that may be appropriate for the participation in the program.  Brandon had explained to Al that we 
would have to cultivate a list by talking to residents etc. Al is on a tight timeline to get the application 
in.  Committee reviewed the land inventory overview map and discussed properties that are in Chapter 
Land aand might be potential candidates. Mike Toohill suggested that we could send a letter to 
landowners to find out if any are interested in participating in the forest legacy program.  Bob Pease 
expressed concern about the bandwidth of the Committee to undertake this task, our goal this year is 
to review Town properties and taking on new projects may not be feasible at this time.  Lynn asked 
what the advantage is to the Town to participate; Brandon responded that the Forest Legacy Program 
is a funding source for Conservation Land.  After discussion it was the consensus of the Committee that 
at this point in time we do not have any identified projects to include in the aggregation project.  
Brandon will inform Al Futterman. 
 
Paula joined the meeting at 6:34 p.m. 
 
4. Continuation of General Municipal Land discussion regarding parcels of interest for potential 
designation for conservation, open space, or recreation purposes – Bob Pease had developed a list of 
properties that might be of interest; he noted that there was a great list in the open space plan that 
Brandon developed.  It had a lot of information regarding various parcels.  Bob compared the list from 
the open space plan to the information we received in the list from the Assessors for 2020 to 
determine what parcels were new and what were no longer town owned and merged the two, he then 
removed land is that already Conservation or Parks and/or in active use by the community and came 
up with a list that he believes Open Space should be analyzing which will be forwarded to the 
===PAGE 2===
Committee.  Paula explained that many of the properties that currently identified as being in tax title 
have not yet been taken by the Town as it has not gone through the entire land court process.  
Brandon noted that the Committee had suggested that regular “education” item be included in future 
agendas and having someone provide an overview of the entire tax title process would be helpful.  In 
the Open Space plan the need for “pocket parks” or community gardens is identified and some of the 
parcels currently in tax title might be ideal for that.  Paula said she will reach out to the Tax Collector, 
Myleen Mallari, and see if she would be willing to provide an overview.  At the last meeting the 
Committee had identified several properties that may be of interest including Fire Road 12, 380 West 
Street, 1162 Mass Ave, 247 Highland, 690 Goodrich Read, 860 Leominster Road, Parmenter Road and 0 Flynn 
Road. Lynn Man said that many of the properties that were identified in November do not appear to be 
Town owned or in Tax title currently.  Brandon reviewed the original list within the Open Space Plan 
and 1162 Mass Ave was in tax title then but may have been redeemed, Fire Road 12 is not on the list 
either, Bob commented that it should be as Conservation is dealing with a town owned land issue in 
this area, he determined it is listed as owner unknown on the current list it is identified as 0 Lake 
Shirley.   
51 Parmenter –  Committee discussed potential use of this 0.31 acre parcel.  Property slopes away 
from the road not particularly attractive from development but could be developed.  An interesting 
access point to the lake for canoes and kayaks; but there is not sufficient room for a boat dock etc. 
Committee discussed parked, the property may be able to accommodate 3 or 4 cars and there is a 
potential for on street parking.  Lynn noted that the area is very residential and parking off street may 
not be desirable to the residents.   Brandon pointed out that this may be a controversial access site as 
it is residential, we would have to take a measured approach and would have to involve the neighbors.  
Bob commented that it may appropriate for this property to be under the jurisdiction of Parks, he 
suggested that the committee look at property identified as 0 Lake Shirley Reservoir which is 3.3 acres.   
0 Lake Shirley Reservoir (off Fire Road 12) – This 3.3 acre property is located in area that is called 
stump cove.  Although there is a portion of the property that accesses the lake apparently it is being 
utilized by the adjacent property owner.  This may be the parcel that was gifted to the Community 
when Lunenburg took control of the Dam to provide public access.   
0 Fire Road 12 – There are two parcels currently identified as 0 Flynn Road, a 0.04 parcel and one that 
is 0.48 acres.  Karin noted that the 0.04 parcel may have been paved by a neighbor.  This could serve as 
rooftop access to the lake (canoes/kayaks etc.).  Paula noted you may be able to put parking of the 
larger parcel and walk to the small one for lake access.  Brandon questioned if making a public area on 
a private road would require the Town to provide road maintenance.  If we decide to pursue, we will 
need to check with Town Counsel.   
860 Reservoir – This 6.7 acre parcel is also owned by the Town and in the vicinity of 0 Fire Road 12, 
there is also a smaller town property that abuts.  Currently this parcel is identified on the Assessors list 
as Cemetery Department, but the CAI database indicates Board of Selectmen, Town of Lunenburg.  
Need to verify that the taking process for this parcel has been completed.     This is a valuable piece of 
property that may be useful to the Community. It may provide parking access to the lake, however 
crossing the road may be dangerous.  Brandon noted that the State of Massachusetts has an Office of 
Fishing and Boating Access and their entire purpose is to provide public access to bodies of water and 
they have a dedicated funding source.  When they develop new access on bodies of water, they have a 
fleet of capabilities engineering, etc.  and usually what they look for in return is that the Town manages 
===PAGE 3===
the access point.  We may want to engage the office of fishing and boating access about land on a large 
great pond that does not have any developed access.  Brandon will provide contact information to 
Paula so that she can reach out.  They would fund the development parking etc.   
For the next meeting, people should prioritize their parcels of interest.      
5. Questions about the Assessor’s database – Bob noted that the properties that are currently listed as 
owner’s unknown do not have consistency in the ownership naming; he will be reaching out the 
assessors to determine why and if there is a difference in where the parcel may be in the taking 
process.  
 
6. Parks update on Marshall Park master planning (if available) – Parks is moving slow in the process.  
Each Commissioner developed a “wish list” which was compiled by the Recreation Director in a 
prioritized list.  Planning funding was requested from the Capital Plan to begin the design process.  The 
Skate Park Group is coming to meet with the Parks Commission to talk about potentially moving the 
skate park project into Marshall Park.  Brandon recommended that Karin get in touch with PARC Grant 
Coordinator for advice and networking.  Liz Murphy who works in Planning in Fitchburg may also be a 
great resource. 
 
7. Conservation update on rules revisions (if available) – Bob informed the Committee that the 
Conservation Commission had a public comment forum on possible changes.  There was limited 
feedback on the majority of potential changes to the rules, other than on hunting.   The opportunity for 
additional public input will be provided.   Town Counsel had indicated the Commission does not have 
the ability to make regulations, but rather they adopt policies.  Rich Bursch, Commissioner, has found 
the Conservation Commissions does have the ability to adopt regulations; further clarification from 
Town Counsel will be sought.  Bob noted that the Commission is reviewing the policy for volunteer 
work on conservation land.  Due to limited staff, volunteer labor is crucial.  Bob pointed out that 
currently the only group that does trail work is the Lunenburg Snow Riders.  
 
8. Set next meeting date – For the next four months we will be meeting the third Tuesday of the month 
at 6:30 p.m.  (rather than 6:00).   
 
9. Public Comment – Paula informed the Committee that at the last Capital Planning Committee she had 
relayed the comment from Chris Menard about creating a funding source for land acquisition.  
Members of the Committee were intrigued by the idea but had questions/concerns on how it could be 
implemented due to the funding mechanism and required action by Town Meeting.  Town Manager is 
going to investigate further.  Brandon mentioned that participated in the Community Preservation Act 
would be ideal.    
 
10. Adjourn – Meeting adjourned at 8:00 p.m. by unanimous roll call vote. 
 
Respectfully submitted, 
Paula Bertram
