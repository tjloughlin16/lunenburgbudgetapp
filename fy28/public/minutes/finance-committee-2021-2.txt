Lunenburg Finance Committee 
Meeting Minutes 
Thursday, September 23, 2021 
 
Location: In-person at Lunenburg Town Hall and broadcast on Zoom 
 
Present: Peter Beardmore, Dave Passios, Jay Simeone, Chris Menard, Evan Watters via Zoom,  
 
Absent: Tom Balish, Tom Gray 
 
Guests: Town Manager Heather Lemieux, Finance Director Karen Brochu, Superintendent of Lunenburg 
Public Schools Kate Burnham, HR Director Michael Cassidy, School Committee Chairperson Carol 
Archambault.  
1. Call to Order 
Peter Beardmore called the meeting to order at 7:01 p.m. and read a statement 
concerning the Open Meeting Law, and how all public meetings are legally being held 
remotely. 
 
This meeting was recorded and is viewable on YouTube at https://youtu.be/gslPh-rXx9M 
2. Pledge of Allegiance 
3. Announcements: None 
4. Public Comment: None 
 
5. Discussion with Schools Superintendent Kate Burnham and HR Director Michael Cassidy: Dr. 
Burnham said the last year and a half has been full of surprises for the schools, and the 
administration has been working on filling the educational, as well as, socio-emotional gaps left 
by the pandemic. The biggest challenge the schools have faced is handling contact tracing as it 
has taken up a majority of the nurses’ time. The schools are working on implementing a “Test 
and Stay” program, which is to be funded by the state, but finding ways to staff the program has 
proved difficult. Currently the schools are using local funds for the program, which should be 
reimbursed with the Elementary and Secondary Schools Emergency Relief (ESSER) formula grant 
funds. The schools are advertising for a new facilities director in the wake of John Londa’s 
retirement, but the applicants’ experience has not met the demand for the position. There is 
one principal at the Middle High School, and though working with two vice principals has been 
working out so far, the administration will advertise for a second principal for the high school. In 
the meantime, the administration will seek out feedback on the current arrangement of school 
leadership. Mr. Passios asked about bussing. Mr. Cassidy replied the schools will go out to bid in 
January, and may need to increase the fleet by two busses over the next three months. Dr. 
Burnham highlighted the challenge the schools face with bussing as there is no way of knowing 
which families will actually use the bus. Mr. Beardmore commented that the committee should 
be out of the realm of ambiguity when it comes time to approve the budget. Mr. Cassidy added 
the administration is going through an assessment of their IT, and that could impact the FY23 
budget. Dr. Burnham added that a multi-year plan will be developed when the assessment is 
complete. 
===PAGE 2===
6. Discussion with Town Manager on general government function: Ms. Lemieux stated that her 
highest priority is IT, and she’s currently working with a consultant to look at infrastructure, 
security, network upgrading, and additional support services. Ms. Lemieux informed the 
committee she is also applying for a grant to fund a network upgrade, which may involve a 
budget amendment. Ms. Lemieux informed the committee that the Town Accountant Clerk will 
be retiring, and she is planning to hire an Assistant Accountant to assist the Finance Director, so 
there will be someone qualified to stand in when the Finance Director is out. Ms. Lemieux stated 
this should not impact the FY23 budget.  Ms. Lemieux also informed the committee that the 
treasurer/collector’s office will be looking for more funding to put liens on properties in order to 
maintain their schedule. Ms. Lemieux will also be looking to create a new department to 
accommodate the town’s facilities as it’s not typical for the Department of Public Works to also 
be responsible for all the town’s buildings. Outside of the omnibus budget, Ms. Lemieux is 
working on a public records management plan, which will entail a centralized catalogue. Ms. 
Lemieux stated her challenges are unanticipated retirements along with the resignation of the IT 
Director. It has been difficult to find the right people for the vacant positions. Other challenges 
include the proposed 40B project, as well as simultaneous road projects (Complete Streets, 
Pleasant Street bridge, D.O.T. work on 2A) 
 
7. Town Manager/Finance Director Report: Ms. Lemieux informed the committee that the 
Principal Assessor submitted receipts for $430,254 in new growth for the town, which came in 
$149,664 higher than what was estimated at Annual Town Meeting, the uptick in funds will 
cancel out the deficit left by school choice and charter assessments. Ms. Brochu informed the 
committee that she is working on the free cash balance sheet, and it looks like there will be $2.6 
million for the town to spend.  
 
8. Old Business 
a. Review Special Town Meeting Draft Articles: The committee decided to hold an 
additional meeting on October 7, 2021 to discuss the articles on the warrant before the 
public hearing on October 14, 2021.  
 
9. New Business: None 
10. Minutes: tabled 
11. Committee Reports: Committee members updated each other on their respective committees. 
12. Public Comment: None 
13. Items for future meetings: Earmarks. Mr. Beardmore suggested if any committee members had 
any thoughts on earmarks to bring them up at the next meeting in order to be put on a future 
agenda. 
14. Next Meetings: October 7, October 14 (STM public hearing), October 28 (Joint meeting 
w/School Committee to discuss Chapter 70) 
15. Adjournment: Mr. Menard motioned to adjourn, Mr. Passios seconded. On vote, motion carried 
unanimously.

Lunenburg Finance Committee 
 
Meeting Minutes 
Thursday, October 14, 2021 
 
Location: In-person at Lunenburg Town Hall and broadcast on Zoom 
 
Present: Acting Chairperson Chris Menard, Peter Beardmore (arrived 15 minutes late), Dave Passios, Jay 
Simeone, Tom Balish, Tom Gray, Evan Watters 
 
Guests: Town Manager Heather Lemieux, Land Use Director Adam Burney, Karen Brochu via Zoom, 
Select Board Member Lou Franco via Zoom, Lunenburg resident Rhonda Lissio 
1. Call to Order 
Chris Menard called the meeting to order at 7:00 p.m. and read a statement concerning 
the Open Meeting Law, and how all public meetings are legally being held remotely. 
This meeting was recorded and is viewable on YouTube at https://youtu.be/ueog2Zsqgtw 
2. Pledge of Allegiance 
 
3. Announcements: None 
 
4. Public Comment: None 
 
5. Public Hearing on Special Town Meeting Warrant Articles: Mr. Menard opened the public 
hearing. Ms. Lemieux, Ms. Brochu, and Mr. Burney went into detail on most proposed warrant 
articles to be heard at Special Town Meeting. Committee members agreed to hold another 
public hearing on November 4, 2021 to discuss those articles still missing key details.  
 
a. Mr. Menard read Article A out loud. Ms. Lemieux explained there would be an increase 
of $4,912 in the Town Accountant’s budget to replace the retiring principal clerk with an 
assistant accountant, as well as adding four hours for the new position. Ms. Lemieux 
also explained the technology budget would increase by $65,352 to cover hiring an 
outside consultant, as well as an upgrade to Microsoft 365, along with network and 
infrastructure upgrades. Ms. Lemieux is in the process of applying for grants to assist 
covering those costs. Whether the town will be awarded the grants will not be known 
until after the Special Town Meeting. Ms. Lemieux also explained an increase in the 
town’s share of Medicare by $39,935, however a reduction in the group health 
insurance along with a reduction in the Monty Tech Assessment will cover that cost. Ms. 
Lemieux also explained that revenue adjustments reflecting new growth along with 
changes in the sewer enterprise budget totaled $156,487 which makes a balanced 
budget for the town.  Mr. Watters and Mr. Simeone asked about whether the schools’ 
technology program came under the town’s budget. Ms. Lemieux explained the schools 
are talking with the town’s technology consultant about proposals, and that the schools’ 
technology will be covered separately from the town.  
===PAGE 2===
b. Article B, concerning payment of prior year expenses totaling $7,519 in technology 
invoices that weren’t known about until after FY21 was closed out. Mr. Beardmore 
asked whether the technology budget was underspent that year. Ms. Brochu answered 
that the FY21 technology budget closed out at $72,000 with $30,000 to cover salaries. 
The committee determined there was enough money left in that budget to cover those 
outstanding invoices. 
c. Ms. Lemieux did not have a number to discuss with the committee concerning the 
Lunenburg Professional Fire Fighters Collective Bargaining Agreement.  
d. Adjusting Elderly Exemption limits on income and assets: Ms. Lemieux explained the 
article aims to adjust the exemption amount from $750 to $1,000 for those single 
seniors with incomes of $31,000 or less and/or assets totaling $52,700 or less, and those 
married seniors with incomes of less than $72,600 and/or assets totaling $72,000 or 
less. Ms. Lemieux explained the adjustment would have an estimated impact of $7,500, 
which could be covered by the overlay account. Mr. Balish and Mr. Beardmore 
commented that the wording of the article doesn’t explain the exemption amount, 
which makes the article appear misleading. Mr. Passios opined that oftentimes it’s the 
asset limit that bumps some a lot of the town’s seniors out of the program. 
e. Easement of land adjacent to Leominster Shirley Rd: Mr. Menard expressed his concern 
about the lack of visibility at the current intersection. Mr. Burney explained that the 
applicant is working with the abutting business to improve visibility by cutting back 
existing vegetation. 
f. Sewer Enterprise budget: Ms. Lemieux explained the fund will provide an increase in 
hours for the assistant to Sewer Business Manager to make it a benefitted position. Mr. 
Passios made clear that those funds would be covered by the Sewer Enterprise fund.  
g. Sewer Commission to transfer $14,875 into their Capital Reserve Stabilization Fund. Ms. 
Brochu explained that there is a certain amount of revenue that gets set aside each year 
to be deposited into Sewer Capital Reserve Stabilization Fund.  
h. Demolition Delay: Ms. Lemieux explained the article was submitted by the Historical 
Commission. The article aims to amend the bylaw. The committee agreed Article H 
would have no financial impact. 
i. Salary Administration Plan: Ms. Lemieux explained that the approved positions by the 
Personnel Committee are to be incorporated into the compensation and classification 
grid.  
j. T.C. Passios Building Committee Report: The committee agreed to support hearing a 
report at Special Town Meeting, with hope that more information, and/or a preview will 
be available at the public hearing scheduled for November 4, 2021.  
k. Mr. Menard suggested discussing both Articles K and L together, and pointed out that 
there are currently no known values on tearing down and rebuilding the T.C. Passios 
building. Mr. Passios said there are figures for Article K. Mr. Beardmore expressed his 
preference to discuss Article K when a comparative analysis among other town building 
projects are complete. Ms. Lemieux informed the board that the debt exclusion for 
Article K is $23,570,000. Mr. Beardmore said he had a discussion with Ms. Lemieux, the 
Town’s financial advisor, and Mr. Menard about debt analysis, and that the voters need 
===PAGE 3===
to informed on how the Turkey Hill Elementary School project, as well as any debt 
incurred from any T.C. Passios project will affect their tax rates.  
Ms. Lemieux added that for Article L, the $250,000 figure may change as she anticipated 
an actual number from the architect before the next time the article will be discussed.  
Lunenburg resident Rhonda Lissio commented she would vote no on both articles, and 
opined that the town has unilaterally decided how to use the T.C. Passios building. Ms. 
Lissio encouraged the town to control its impulses, and to look at its office space as a 
whole instead of rendering two buildings useless and charging taxpayers to renovate, or 
build another 55,000 sq. ft. building, and that the only way to reduce the cost is to 
reduce the size. 
Mr. Menard opined that looking further into how some space can be used would be 
beneficial for a decision on Article L. Mr. Passios rebutted, saying that the needs of the 
town have been assessed several times in the last eight years, and it has come to the 
point where the proposals on the T.C. Passios building need to move forward, and be 
voted on by the town.  
 
m. Flat Hill Culvert: Ms. Lemieux explained that easements are needed to repair a culvert 
on Flat Hill Road.  
n. Ms. Lemieux explained that Article N was voted on already, but the Map and parcel 
numbers were wrong at the time. Article N reflects the correct numbers. 
p. Ms. Lemieux explained that the parks commission submitted Article P to include 
Marshall Park in the sewer service area map. 
q. Mr. Burney explained the Article Q aims to rename some districts in town. 
r. Mr. Burney explained that Article R aims to change what the town’s bylaw says to match 
the town’s water district map. 
s. Mr. Burney explained that Article S would then update the town’s zoning map to reflect 
the change in the water district map. 
t. Mr. Burney explained that Article T aims to establish lot size requirements in the Limited 
Business/Residential District and do away with the Retail Commercial District.  
At 8:25 p.m. Mr. Beardmore motioned to close the public hearing, Mr. Watters seconded. On vote, 
motion carried unanimously. 
 
6. Vote recommendations on Special Town Meeting Warrant Articles: 
a.  Mr. Beardmore motioned to recommend approval on Article A, Mr. Passios seconded. 
On vote, motion carried unanimously. 
b. Mr. Beardmore motioned to recommend approval on Article B, Mr. Balish seconded. On 
vote, motion carried unanimously. 
c. Mr. Beardmore motioned to defer a recommendation until Special Town Meeting on 
Article C, Mr. Watters seconded. Upon discussion committee members agreed they may 
discuss this article again on November 4, 2021. On vote, motion carried unanimously.  
d. Mr. Beardmore motioned to defer a recommendation until Special Town Meeting on 
Article D, Mr. Passios seconded. Upon discussion committee members agreed they may 
discuss this article again on November 4, 2021. On vote, motion carried unanimously.

e. Mr. Beardmore motioned to recommend approval on Article E, Mr. Passios seconded. 
On vote, motion carried unanimously. 
f. Mr. Beardmore motioned to recommend approval on Article F, Mr. Balish seconded. On 
vote, motion carried unanimously. 
g. Mr. Beardmore motioned to recommend approval on Article G, Mr. Watters seconded. 
On vote, motion carried unanimously. 
h. Mr. Beardmore moved to find no financial impact on Article H, Mr. Passios seconded. 
On vote, motion carried unanimously. 
i. Mr. Beardmore motioned to recommend approval on Article I, Mr. Watters seconded. 
On vote, motion carried unanimously.  
j. Mr. Beardmore motioned to recommend approval on Article J, Mr. Balish seconded. On 
vote, motion carried unanimously.  
k. Mr. Beardmore motioned to defer articles K and L to Special Town Meeting, Mr. Passios 
seconded. On vote, motion carried unanimously. 
l. Mr. Beardmore moved that the committee find no financial impact for articles M 
through T, Mr. Watters seconded. On vote, motion carried unanimously. 
 
7. Warrant Letter or Other Content for Special Town Meeting: Mr. Beardmore will write a letter 
from the finance committee to Lunenburg voters concerning the potential financial impact of 
articles K and L to be distributed with the Warrant Articles. 
 
8. Minutes: Mr. Beardmore motioned to approve the minutes of the September 23, 2021, and 
October 7, 2021 meetings. Mr. Passios seconded. Mr. Simeone stated he did not read the 
minutes. Motion carries. 6 Yeas, 1 abstain. 
 
9. Committee Reports: Committee members updated each other on their respective committees. 
 
10. Public Comment: none 
 
11. Items for Future Meetings: Mr. Passios suggested discussing the information the Green 
Communities Committee has collected on replacing the current street lights with L.E.D. street 
lights. The committee agreed to put the item on an agenda in December. 
 
12. Next Meetings: The joint meeting with the school committee regarding Chapter 70 funds will be

1. Call to Order –Joint meeting with Lunenburg School Committee

concerning the Open Meeting Law, and how all public meetings are legally being held 
remotely. 
Ms. Archambault called the Lunenburg School Committee meeting to order. 
This meeting was recorded and is viewable on YouTube at https://youtu.be/Gp102JkcQdU 
2. Pledge of Allegiance 
 
3. Announcements: Mr. Beardmore explained that this joint meeting of the school committee and 
finance committee was to provide an opportunity for both committees to learn more about the 
Commonwealth’s Chapter 70 program, which addresses funding for public elementary and 
secondary schools, and also establishes minimum spending requirements for each school district 
and minimum requirements for each municipality’s share of school costs. 
Mr. Beardmore highlighted that the finance committee has noticed that as the school’s budget 
has risen, Chapter 70 funds have not, and that the intent of this meeting is to determine the 
best routes to improve the town’s standing. 
Mr. Beardmore also informed those in attendance that Lunenburg’s delegates in the state 
legislature were invited to this meeting, and they intended on being present, however, the 
legislative schedule had since changed, and both houses were in session, so none of the 
delegates could be present.  
 
4. Public Comment: none at this time. 
 
5. Chapter 70 Presentation and Discussion: Ms. Archambault introduced Ms. Novick who 
complimented both committees for getting together to learn more and discuss Chapter 70 
funding. Ms. Novick explained the history behind Chapter 70, how it is calculated, and how 
Lunenburg’s figures are formed, as well as the circuit breaker and how it works locally, along 
with how Montachusett Regional Vocational School tuition factors in to Chapter 70 funds.  
===PAGE 2===
Ms. Novick explained that the Commonwealth determines a base foundation for all districts, 
then considers how many low income students, and English language learners there are in each 
district and adds more funds based on those numbers. Ms. Novick explained that the inflation 
rate also affects the base foundation, but pointed out that increase in costs surpasses the rate of 
inflation. Ms. Novick alluded that without higher enrollment, more low income students and/or 
English language learners, inflation is the only other factor that would raise the base foundation. 
According to Ms. Novick, base foundation numbers are determined based on enrollment from 
the prior year. After the Commonwealth determines how much each district is required to 
spend, municipalities are obligated to fund 59 percent of that foundation budget, while the 
Commonwealth provides 41 percent.  In order to determine how much each district is 
responsible for, all taxable property in each municipality is taken into consideration, as well as 
income tax filed, and sets them equal to each other to develop a common calculation to come 
up with a combined effort yield.   
Ms. Novick went on to talk about the Student Opportunity Act, which goes into effect this year 
so won’t be accounted for until next year’s numbers are determined. The act contains four 
financial changes affecting the the base foundation amount concerning health insurance, special 
education, English learning students, and low income students.  
Ms. Novick explained that in FY22 the state determined the town’s contribution to be 
$10,908,378, with Chapter 70 funds coming in at $7,500,000, and that since the base foundation 
can’t meet the cost of operations, the town spent over and above its required amount. Ms. 
Novick also pointed out Lunenburg’s enrollment pattern has had several ups and downs before 
seemingly levelling out.  Ms. Novick also said that several municipalities have passed a 
Proposition 2 1/2 override in order to fund changes to the budget, and suggested looking at the 
base foundation determination and discussing an override with legislature representatives. Ms. 
Archambault asked about charter school reimbursement changes. Ms. Novick explained that 
those changes will be addressed when the Student Opportunity Act takes effect. Mr. Lehtinen 
asked about transportation and the circuit breaker. Ms. Novick explained that the state plans to 
eventually reimburse all funds for transportation, but the amount would come back in phases.  
 
At 8:25 p.m. the school committee adjourned, and the finance committee took a 5 minute 
recess.  
 
6. Public Comment: Resident Dave Rodgers called in and said he found the Chapter 70 talk

Mr. Beardmore added that looking at other municipalities in the same situation as Lunenburg,

Lunenburg Finance Committee 
Meeting Minutes 
Thursday, November 4, 2021 
 
Location: In-person at Lunenburg Town Hall and broadcast on Zoom 
 
Present: Chairperson Peter Beardmore, Chris Menard, Dave Passios, Evan Watters, Thomas Balish, 
Thomas Gray 
 
Absent: Jay Simeone 
 
Guests: Town Manager Heather Lemieux, T.C. Passios Building Design Committee Chairman Greg Roy, 
Select Board Member Michael Ray Jeffreys, Town Accountant Karen Brochu, Lunenburg Resident 
Rhonda Lissio, Lunenburg Resident Dave Rodgers via Zoom 
1. Call to Order 
Mr. Beardmore called the meeting to order at 7:00 p.m. and read a statement 
concerning the Open Meeting Law, and how all public meetings are legally being held 
remotely. 
This meeting was recorded and is viewable on YouTube at https://youtu.be/kUqJBVgp2mk 
2. Pledge of Allegiance 
 
3. Announcements: none 
 
4. Public Comment:  
 
5. Town Manager/Finance Director Reports: none 
 
6. Public Hearing on Special town Meeting Warrant Articles 3, 7, and 8: Mr. Beardmore opened 
the public hearing. Mr. Beardmore explained that the committee resolved to avail themselves to 
public hearing after the Warrant was printed if more information was made available.  
 
a. Article 3: Though Ms. Lemieux anticipated the collective bargaining agreement with the 
Lunenburg Professional Fire Fighters to be ready for discussion, another piece, not 
having to do with finances, still needs to be addressed. Mr. Passios suggested that 
anything could change until an agreement between the two parties is signed.  
 
b. Articles 7 and 8: As a member of the T.C. Passios Building Design Committee, Mr. 
Jeffreys gave a presentation on Article 7 which pertains to renovation the T.C. Passios 
building. Mr. Jeffreys explained that when the committee was tasked with its charge in 
2019 the cost of renovation would be $22,592,000, and as it’s two years later, the cost 
has increased to $28,967,640, citing cost increases across the board from inflation, to 
materials and labor, as well as the job market.  Mr. Jeffreys also proposed a conceptual 
design for a completely new building for $39,637,229, which would house many uses 
===PAGE 2===
including: most town offices, the ACE Program, Lion’s Club Food Pantry, the Extended 
Day Program, storage for schools and the town, as well as conference/meeting space.  
 
As a Select Board member, Mr. Jeffreys then gave a presentation on Article 8, which 
requests $350,000 to advance the design of the proposed new building, if the town 
would decide to choose that option. Mr. Jeffreys pointed out that as the building stands, 
the current tenants who pay rent on the building are what keeps the building in 
operation, and the town does not currently appropriate any funds for the T.C. Passios 
building. Mr. Jeffreys pointed out that a study was done on all town facilities in 2016, 
which revealed the town would soon need a new building to house town offices as 
Town Hall and the Ritter buildings have been rendered inadequate. Mr. Jeffreys shared 
pictures of current storage conditions, and informed those in attendance that the town 
has lost records due to mold from leaking roofs. Mr. Jeffreys drew attention to the 
inadequate accessibility of the Ritter building, as well as an inadequate supply of 
electricity. He also pointed out that by relocating town offices, a preservation of Town 
Hall and the Ritter could be attempted. Mr. Jeffreys also suggested in his presentation 
that not investing in a new building will cost the town millions of dollars a year to 
address the problems it currently faces with the existing conditions. Mr. Jeffreys 
explained that the late information of the increase in costs has caused the Select Board 
to recommend to pass over both Articles 7 and 8 at Special Town Meeting on Nov. 16, 
2021. 
Mr. Beardmore thanked Mr. Jeffrey for the presentations, and asked Ms. Lemieux what 
she knew about the new numbers. She consulted the town’s financial advisor, who was 
not available to give an answer. 
Mr. Beardmore said he encouraged the Select Board to pass over the articles. Mr. 
Beardmore also said he analyzed the new numbers and compared them to the average 
value of homes in Lunenburg and realized that funding either project, without taking 
into consideration funds to be expended to address Turkey Hill Elementary School, 
would put the town in violation of its own financial policies for five years. Mr. 
Beardmore calculated that the average property owner could expect to pay $341 more 
per year at the $28.9 million price tag, and $467 more per year with the $39.6 million 
price tag.  
Mr. Menard approached the situation as a need vs. want scenario, and questioned 
whether busing students from the Primary School to the T.C. Passios for two hours out 
of the day was beneficial. Mr. Jeffreys said that the Extended Day Program is definitely a 
need, and that Superintendent Dr. Kate Burnham had several reasons why it’s important 
for students to separate learning in the classroom from after school activity. Mr. 
Menard agreed that Extended Day is needed, but asked whether it needs to take place 
at the T.C. Passios and suggested the town library as an alternate location citing the 
library’s newest master plan featuring the library as a community space. Mr. Watters 
asked whether the projects have taken into account the possible increase to the 
Extended Day Program.  
===PAGE 3===
Mr. Gray inquired if there were other locations for some uses, could the size of the 
building be decreased in order to lower the cost. Mr. Jeffrey responded that it’s not a 
proportionate relationship. 
Mr. Beardmore concluded that there is a strong possibility that the articles will be 
passed over, and asked where the town should go from there. Mr. Jeffreys reiterated 
that the problems the town faces will not go away, and the T.C. Passios Building Design 
Committee has done the best they can do. Mr. Jeffreys pointed out that the Town 
Manager plans to separate town facilities from the Department of Public Works, which 
will give the town the opportunity to properly address problems with town buildings.  
Mr. Watters motioned to close the public hearing. Mr. Passios seconded. On vote, 
motion carried unanimously.  
 
Public Hearing Public Comment:  
Ms. Lissio commented that she disagrees with the tasks the T.C. Passios Building Design 
Committee were charged with, which was to house the current groups using the T.C. Passios 
building along with municipal offices. Ms. Lissio stated that she disagrees with passing the costs 
of housing the existing programs onto the tax payer. Ms. Lissio also claimed that in her research 
she found only one community that funds an Extended Day Program, and that is Lexington.  
Mr. Rodgers called into the meeting and recognized that the town faces a complex situation 
with prices being a moving target, and that any project will cost more money than anticipated.  
 
7. Discussion and Votes on recommendations for Special Town Meeting Articles:  The committee 
agreed to meet at 6:00 p.m. on November 16, 2021, an hour before Special Town Meeting is 
scheduled to start to address any new information regarding Article 3.  
On Articles 7 and 8 Mr. Beardmore informed the committee their choices are to recommend 
approval, recommend disapproval, defer, or claim no financial impact, and that the committee 
would be setting a precedent to recommend to vote to pass over the articles.  
Mr. Passios opined he would only vote on passing over if the motion was on the floor at Town 
Meeting. Mr. Passios expressed that passing over would not give voters a clear picture of what 
committee members are thinking, and that without debating the articles on the floor, and giving 
the public a chance to respond, there’s no way to tell how the rest of the town thinks on the 
articles. Mr. Passios also expressed his concern over the current working conditions of the 
town’s employees and the possibility of a push back, and asked if not now, when. Mr. Passios 
wants to hear the voice of the townspeople. Mr. Beardmore opined that if committee members 
had more time to consider the numbers they might be able to form an opinion to express, and 
also pointed out that with commodities pricing, along with inflation, and other issues being 
impacted by the pandemic also holds weight in deciding. Mr. Menard agreed with Mr. 
Beardmore and said the committee should have been able to start the discussion six weeks ago, 
and if they did, committee members might have an answer. Mr. Menard agreed to take a stand 
and recommend passing over the articles, and recognized that passing over is unprecedented, 
and pointed out that these are unprecedented times. Mr. Menard also said he would disapprove 
both articles, citing that Article 8 is still incomplete, and the price is too high. Mr. Passios 
pointed out that a yes vote to renovate the T.C. Passios would result in a special election to fill 
===PAGE 4===
the sudden vacant seat on the Select Board. Mr. Watters pondered whether recommending to 
pass over the articles would only lead to another Town Meeting where the committee would 
again seek to pass over the articles. Mr. Watters also said he would vote to disapprove both 
articles because putting all the proposed uses together does not make sense. At the same time, 
Mr. Watters expressed his concern over kicking the can down the road, and pointed out that it’s 
been almost a decade, and the town is still having the same discussion. Mr. Gray said it’s the 
committee’s job to make a recommendation, and currently he couldn’t approve of the article, so 
he would vote to disapprove. Mr. Gray also pointed out that if the prices keep rising, and they 
go up to $50 million, there won’t be much building of anything anywhere. Mr. Gray also drew 
attention to the programs the town currently provides at the T.C. Passios, and pondered what 
kind of town Lunenburg would be if they did not provide those programs. Mr. Beardmore 
claimed he’s been against the articles since they’ve began, and that comparing the options of 
renovation and building new was the right thing to do. Mr. Beardmore prefers to look at 
alternative options for the ACE Program, Extended Day, PACC, and food bank locations within 
the next six months. Mr. Beardmore said he’s not sold on municipal offices and all the other 
programs needing to exist under one roof. Mr. Beardmore said he’d initially vote for 
disapproval, but would entertain a motion to pass over the articles.  Mr. Balish claimed he’d be 
against both articles and would prefer to look at what the town can actually afford, and make a 
decision based on that.  
 
Mr. Menard moved to recommend disapproval of Article 7, Mr. Gray seconded. Motion passes, 
3-2. Mr. Menard –yea, Mr. Balish –yea, Mr. Beardmore –yea, Mr. Passios –no, Mr. Watters –no. 
 
Mr. Menard moved to recommend disapproval of Article 8, Mr. Balish seconded. On vote, 
motion carried unanimously. 
 
Mr. Menard moved to recommend the Finance Committee, as a whole, pass over Articles 7 and 
8, Mr. Beardmore seconded. Motion failed. 4 no, 2 aye. 
Balish –no, Gray –no, Passios –no, Watters –no, Menard –aye, Beardmore-aye  
Mr. Passios explained that a vote of the Finance Committee, as a whole, would not impact the 
way he votes on other committees.  
Mr. Menard reiterated that the committee has just been presented with new data, which hasn’t 
had the opportunity to be discussed enough publicly, and that is his reasoning behind the 
recommendation to pass over.  
Ms. Lemieux clarified for the committee that moving to pass over the articles on the Town 
Meeting Floor is a debatable motion, which can be discussed on the floor. 
 
8. Special Town Meeting Consent Calendar: Ms. Lemieux informed the committee that the Select 
Board voted Articles 5, 9, 10, 11, 12, and 13 as “noncontroversial,” and agreed to vote those

Mr. Menard moved to recommend agreement with the Select Board on the consent calendar,

4. Public Comment: Lunenburg resident Rhonda Lissio informed the committee that she attended a 
Capital Planning meeting, 3 days earlier, where she learned of a $755,500 hazardous abatement 
project requested by the DPW. Ms. Lissio expressed her concern over one Finance Committee member 
who serves on the Capital Planning Committee, and the T.C. Passios Building Design Committee, and 
suggested that member’s duties have been spread too thin, as it hadn’t been brought up to the 
Finance Committee. Ms. Lissio opined that delaying the demolition of the building, and including the 
cost of demolition in the debt exclusion to pass on to the tax payer, would cost the same as paying for 
the demolition outright.  Members of the committee pointed out that this was the first meeting of the 
Finance Committee since Special Town Meeting, and they weren’t aware of items presented in the 
capital plan.  
 
5. Town Manager/Finance Director Report: Ms. Brochu informed the committee that the tax rate for 
FY22 has been certified at $17.19, down from $17.74 in FY21. 
 
Ms. Lemieux updated the committee on Select Board agenda items such as the Green Communities 
Committee Spring 2022 competitive grant, ARPA funding, and consulting with Capital Strategic 
Solutions on how to properly allocate those funds, as well as a tax lien sale to occur on February 16, 
2022. Ms. Lemieux also informed the committee that the Small Bridge Grant Program is opening in the 
first quarter of FY23, and Flat Hill Rd Culvert project will hopefully begin construction in the Fall of 
2022, as the project is an ideal candidate to be awarded the grant funds. Mr. Beardmore asked if the 
decision on the award would be given before town meeting. Ms. Lemieux only knew the decision 
would be made sometime in the Spring. About $428,000 has already been appropriated for the 
project. Ms. Lemieux assured the board she’d discuss the cost with the engineer in order to be 
prepared to fund the project going into Annual Town Meeting if the grant decision has not been made 
clear by then.  
Ms. Lemieux also updated the committee on grants, such as a recycling dividend grant in the amount 
of $6,000, as well as a $50,000 grant for hazardous material abatement for the Old Primary School, as 
well as a MIIA Risk Management grant for $10,000 to purchase licenses for Microsoft 365. File

6. Budget Calendar: Ms. Lemieux has a tentative schedule for department heads to meet with the 
Finance Committee to present their budgets. The five-year forecast is scheduled for January 20, 2022.  
Mr. Menard inquired whether there is an interdepartmental ledger for when monies are exchanged 
between departments, and provided the example of when the parks department has to pay to use the 
schools for town recreational programs. Ms. Brochu said she was not aware of seeing funds 
transferred via the warrant process, but that there is an account for custodians who need to be paid 
for their time in the buildings during those programs. 
 
7. Special Town Meeting: TC Passios building: Mr. Beardmore pointed out that there was a lack of 
financial guardrails when it came to defining the tasks of the TC Passios Design Committee, and that 
reprioritizing the needs the project will serve will lead to the next proposal. Mr. Beardmore explained 
that the committee has a role in the project concerning the town’s borrowing capacity, credit rating, as 
well as considering the burden to the tax payers.  
 
8. Chapter 70: Mr. Beardmore hopes to have state legislative representatives at the January 13, 2022 
meeting to talk more about Chapter 70 funds. Mr. Beardmore said he had a follow-up meeting with 
the Superintendent, Business Manager and School Committee Chair, and found that looking more in 
depth into the calculation of how Chapter 70 funds are obtained, that there is not much the town can 
do to receive an increase in those funds. Mr. Menard suggested considering other charges for the 
town’s legislative representatives. Mr. Beardmore pointed out that as costs continue to rise, the 
challenge to afford those costs will also grow. Committee members agreed that discussing other topics 
with representatives should include: police reform training costs, prevailing wage, records 
management, grants for preserving historical buildings.
