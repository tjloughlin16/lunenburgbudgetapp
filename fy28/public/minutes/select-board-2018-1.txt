2018 MRC District Local Technical Assistance Application (Current Business):
We received notification from Montachusett Regional Planning Commission (MRPC) for requests for
applications for District Local Technical Assistance applications for funding during 2018. Applications are due
by the end of January. In an effort to continue the work the administration has begun to create a partnership
between the Town and the Business community, the application request would be for technical assistance to
implement two of the recommendations made by MRPC in their December 2017 report through the last round
of DLTA funding. The two proposed projects would be to create a brochure that would promote our local
businesses, that could also be put on our website and to create a permitting guidebook. The permitting
guidebook would create a step by step process on how to navigate the permitting process and it would also work
in the new electronic permitting process that will be going live this spring. Both projects would require
continued participation through meetings with the business community, land use departments, and the public,
which was a positive means of increasing citizen engagement on this topic. The application requires a letter
signed by the Chairman that the subject was discussed and decided upon at a public meeting.
Motion: R. Ebersole
To authorize the Town Manager to apply for District Local Technical Assistance to implement the two
MRPC recommendations
2"d: P. Luck
Vote: All in Favor

The Eagle House Senior Community Center is open to all age 50+ regardless of where you live. Copies of our
newsletter, which include monthly current events, can be picked up at the Eagle House, Town Hall, Lunenburg

Mr. Lincoln spoke about the Boston Post Cane program. In 1909, the Boston Post, a publication at the time,
gave out over 400 gold tipped canes to citizens in five hundred different towns in four of the New England
states. The cane was supposed to be given to the oldest male resident in the town. In 1920 coinciding with
womens' voting rights being granted, it was changed to the oldest individual. Lunenburg has managed to keep
that going for 110 years. If anyone in town knows who got the canes before 1969, please let us know. There will
be a ceremony this spring for the next recipient to be awarded.
Eagle House is a dynamic place to be, drop in to see what goes on there. We are always looking for new ideas.

1. Individual Characteristics - most of the Board felt that Heather's sound judgement, professional

was outstanding. Heather has initiated Town Manager Reports which she prepares for each BOS

Vertex Companies Inc. Property Condition Assessments for Town Hall, Ritter Building, TC Passios, Old
Primary School, Brooks House, Turkey Hill Elementary School and Eagle House Senior/Community
Center
Eric Nelson, Vice President, Steve Kirby and Phillip Russo of Vertex presented a summary of the property
condition assessments (PCAs). The written assessments of Turkey Hill Elementary and Eagle House have yet to
be completed so there will only be general comments on those two facilities. The property condition assessment
looks at the current condition of the building. One of the goals for performing these assessments was to
incorporate facilties capital projects into the overall capital plan that will assist in development of the long term
facilties maintenance and repair program. Mr. Nelson stated a property condition assessment is commonly
performed by a single professional such as an architect. We utilized a team approach which included a
registered architect (generalist), mechanical engineer, structural engineer, architect (building envelope) and a
hazardous materials specialist. They provided independent opinions on the various materials, components and
systems of each building for their respective disciplines which were consolidated into a single report. It was
performed following a specific protocol (ASTM E2018-15 provisions); the standard is intended to provide a
framework for the review and make the assessment results repeatable and objective to the degree that it can be.
We categorize all this information into three categories: immediate needs, short term repairs and capital needs.
We agreed with the Town at the onset that we would go out to five years on the categories. We used a facility
condition index (FCI) to evaluate the condition of buildings. Issues identified were immediate, ADA
(Americans with Disabilities Act) and capital needs. The dollar amount listed is the building replacement cost.
The following definitions were used for these ratings:
Good- aggregate of identified issues is less than 5% of total replacement costs estimated for the associated
system;
Fair- aggregate of identified issues is greater than 5% and less than 10% of total replacement costs estimated for
the associated system;
Poor- aggregate of identified issues is greater than 10% of total replacement costs estimated for the associated
system.
He presented a chart showing all the buildings were rated as poor. Lunenburg Primary School had a ranking of
70% poor. Total immediate repairs would amount to $1,027,280. Short term repairs came in at $775,000.
Ritter Memorial Building had a 28% poor ranking. Total immediate repairs would amount to $170,297 and
short term repairs would cost $190,496. There are potential costs for hazardous materials abatement. TC Passios
ranked in the 20 percentile range. Immediate repairs total $794,000. Short term repairs totalled $2,173,000. We
projected windows to be replaced over a period of time. If they were replaced all at once, the capital costs
would increase.

ANNOUNCEMENTS:
Mr. Toale announced the public is invited to the presentation of the Boston Post Cane to Clarence Goguen, 100
years old, on Saturday, March 24th at 2:00 p.m. at Eagle House Community Senior Center. The cane is given to
the oldest recorded resident in town. The tradition of honoring the oldest resident was initiated by the former
Boston Post newspaper in 1909. It was a gold-headed ebony cane with the inscription "Presented by the Boston
Post to the oldest citizen of Lunenburg". It was distributed to many communities in New England, including
Lunenburg, with the town's name engraved on the cane. The first local recipient was Luke Chase, for whom
Chase Road was named. Through the years the cane was passed on to subsequent honorees. For a time, the
Town lost track of the cane's whereabouts but fortunately it was located and the tradition was revived in 1978.
It is now on display in the Senior Center and the recipient is given a pin representing they received the Boston
Post Cane as the oldest citizen. Refreshments will follow the presentation.

Sergeant Hebert and Ms. Thibodeau were sworn in. Sgt. Hebert presented the following report:
On January 10, 2018 at approximately 2:00 a.m. he was on patrol and observed a green 2000 Honda Accord
running in the parking lot with the parking lights on, with no one around. He circled around the building and
could see two people inside. He knocked on the door and owner Melissa Thibodeau opened the door. There was

7 Grade Genius Hour Project Presentation
Griffin Caiozzo, 1091 Massachusetts Avenue, thanked the Board for the opportunity to present tonight. He and
Aidan Moore, Jonah Salvatore, Mason Whitcomb, Aidan Swift and Chris Roy are seeking to build a skate park
in Lunenburg. Griffin stated they are all new skateboarders with no appropriate place to skate. Genius Hour is a
program in school where a group of students meet one hour a week where they can work on a project. He listed
reasons why they want a skate park:
• Provides a safe place for skaters to recreate
• Skating in parks is much safer than in the streets
• Requires very little maintenance
• Low cost for participation makes it accessible for everyone
• Can serve as an attraction for family outings
• Skate parks draw skateboarders away from less appropriate areas
Skate parks support vibrant, healthy communities, just like other athletic facilities
• Skateboarding is a popular spectator sport

Mason Whitcomb stated they had to consider material and construction options for the skate park. They looked
at poured concrete, the strongest material, lasting thirty years and requiring minimal repair. The second option
is a prefabricated slab of concrete with concrete ramps. The ramps weather easily and have a lifespan of around
20 years. The third option is an asphalt pad with metal ramps. These metal ramps can dent and have sharp
edges leading to accidents. The asphalt pad is a rough surface and could hurt skaters who fall on it. Lastly is a
concrete pad with wooden ramps, basically what was in Marshall Park and would only last three to five years.
We presented our project to the Parks Commission but many of the location options are on school property and
not under their jurisdiction. Once we get permission and a location we will begin fundraising and begin to build
the skate park. He explained the difference between a skate park (8,000- 12,000 sq. ft./serving ~ 25,000
residents) and skate spot (3,000 - 5,000 sq. ft./serving ~10,000 residents). Their anticipated maximum load per
day would be 30 riders, needing around 7500 sq. ft. for the skate park.
