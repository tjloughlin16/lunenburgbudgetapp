Appointment:1  LSIC UPDATE  Wendy Gendron and Joanna Bilotta updated the conservation 
commission. Ms Gendron outlined the monitoring requirements  and the results of the testing. The Water 
clarity data was excellen t due to the extended drought, there was a reduction in the introduction of 
sediment from stormwater. Temperature and Dissolved oxygen noted that there was no times where the 
water was below 5 mg/l of oxygen. The remainder of the data noted normal levels for an urbanized lake 
watershed. The herbicide treatment and the targete d aquatic plants were reviewed as well as the areas 
that were targeted and left alone. Clipper was used to aid in the c ontrol of fan wort. The plant survey was 
also reviewed. The public outreach of the LSIC was also reviewed. She further identified the strategy for 
the treatment of the lake including the chemicals  proposed to be used. JR noted that there was good 
progress and that the LSIC has done a wonderful job. RB asked if there was a reason for a spike in 
certain algae’s. Wendy Gendron noted that it was likely due to the drought last year  and further noted 
that there were differences in many lakes through the commonwealth. RB clarified the dates  of the 
surveys with Ms. Gendron. The commission thanked the LSIC as there was no further comment or 
question from the commission or the public.

Pursuant to MGL ch 131, s 40 as amended and the Town of Lunenburg Wetlands Protection and Municipal Bylaws a public hearing

Emily Martin, Dave Cloutier and Olivia Richards and Rob Oliva represented the applicant. 
 
Olivia Richards outlined the work, the current conditions of the site and the location of the wetland resourc e area to the commission. 
She outlined the phasing of the construction. It was noted that the work would take place during the winter and that there was a slight 
increase of flood storage proposed. The erosion control, dewatering and use of a coffer dam was also examined. BP asked if the 
catacunemug brook flows through this culvert. The answer was no, that the bro ok itself was to the north of it.  
 
RB examined the elevations of the inverts and outlets of the new and proposed culvert. He asked if there was a new based proposed 
to the brook. Dave Cloutier from VHB noted that the structure’s intention was to allow a 2 ft natural bed to be established. KJ Noted 
that it is the catecunemug brook does flow through the property and it is noted on the USGS map. Olivia Richards noted that this 
area is where the brook converts to lake area and that during the winter periods th ere was low to no flow recorded and that 
construction should take place with no flow considerations. RB asked for a schedule of construction and the actual calendar period it 
would be designated for. He was concerned of the variability of drawdown versus flow. MM noted to the applicant noted that the 
engineers should make certain they coordinate with the dam keeper to ensure those variability can take place. Joanna Bilotta noted 
that the applicant should take note of the drawdown conditions form the previous years and will work to get the numbers to the 
applicant. MM noted that he could help coordinate with the Shirly Conservation Commission if the drawdown causes the flow 
conditions required by the LSIC’s order of conditions in Shirley to be temporarily adjusted. BP further cautioned on the flow 
variabilities on the brook and asked the applicant to ensure that was accounted for. RB also reminded that a proposed schedule be 
added to the file.  
 
A motion to continue to June 16th was made by RB and seconded by KJ with a unanimous vote to approve the motion by roll call 
vote.

 JR noted he attended a meeting with Adam Costa regarding the proposed 40 B development. 
He noted that the commission should have something into the state for August 16, 2021. BP 
noted that he had reviewed the application materials and reviewed his opinion with the 
conservation commission previously. BP noted the current comments deal only with it 
qualifies as a genuine 40 B and noted deficiencies he spotted with Water and Sewer 
infrastructure. JR noted that Attorney Costa noted that the developer was working towards 
water and sewer availability with the developer aiming for subsequent development.  
 
Andrea Adams expressed concerns with the bridge replaced on Pleasant Street. BP noted that

Appointments:  
 Field change request 16 Ruth Street  
Kenneth Kholstom reviewed the requirement for 6 trees to be planted from a prior determination. He 
noted based on is research new trees in the area planted to closely to the old trees will not be 
beneficial and also expressed concerns about root systems interfering with h is well. He requested 
planting a vegetative buffer instead. The Commission reviewed pictures submitted by the applicant. 
JR noted that the other trees were doing ok and was leaning towards putting more trees in a 
evaluating how they progress. He felt that there would be 5 trees that could be planted. JV reviewed 
the original issue with the applicant and the commission. BP reviewed the chain of events during the 
original filing and site walk. JR noted a precedent where revegetation was required and was 
executed after the house was sold. PA examined what the required number of trees would be under 
current regulation. BP also reviewed the tree replacement regulation. BP examined the lot and noted 
he was satisfied with three or four trees and no vegetative buffer. Mr Kohlstrom also pointed out the 
location of his well with a concrete cover and was concerned about tree roots interfering with the 
roots. JV noted that based on his knowledge of the site and agreed with BP that the smaller 
an\mount of trees was appropriate for shade value. Andrea Adams noted that a shade tolerant shrub 
like laurel would accomplish shade protection and address potential root concerns. CC noted that 
she was in agreement with BP. ML noted that he agreed with allowing a smaller number of trees and 
also noted he liked Andrea Adams suggestion about Laurel noting it may provide the same 
protection for the lake as trees in this area would. BP noted that a vegetative buffer would be 
consistent with adding Laurel. Mr Kohlstrom agreed with BP. BP asked the commission how they felt 
about substituting laurel for trees. JR noted that he would be satisfied with three trees and about six 
mountain laurel. Mr Kholstom offers to plant just mountain laurel in an amount larger than 6. JV 
agreed with using just mountain laurel. CC also agreed. PA noted that she would agree with perhaps 
2 trees and laurel. ML noted that a shrub mix of laurel and blueberry would be beneficial. He also 
asked about potential dwarf trees. JV moved to approve six mountain laurel as within 10 feet of 
water’s edge on the northern side. PA seconded. JR asked for an amended motion to add four trees 
to the north side. JV declined the amendment to the motion. JR converted to a formal motion. There 
was no second and the motion was dropped. The original motion by JV was approved by a yes vote 
of BP, PA, JV, CC and ML and a No by JR.

NEW BUSINESS -   LAND USE MANAGEMENT UPDATE 
 
The Conservation Commission will be holding a hearing for updating land use management plans for the Lane property ( Holman 
Street )and Table Rock ( Saliba property on Howard Street), the meeting is scheduled August 18, 2021 at 7 PM the Bilotta Meet ing 
Room, Second Floor, Town Hall, 17 Main Street and by zoom . The public is invited to attend.  
 
BP noted that a letter was sent out for the updated of the land use management plan. The purpose was 
to clarify the policy on Bow Hunting. The original plan was intended to work in concert with the land 
use regulations and to encourage residents to read the land use regulation.  
 
This was continued until next meeting due to the advertisement noting it was scheduled to the 18 th of 
August.  
 
Tree standards – discussion JR noted that one of the continual problems experienced is what 
defined a tree. He noted a definition based on a course he and ML had taken from a national 
professional organization. Defined as a woody plant with a height of at least 2 meters with a single 
stem or if Multi stemmed at least one vertical stemmed 5 cm in 2 inches in diameter breast height. 
He noted that he feels the commission should adopt this definition. 
 
===PAGE 4===
 
 
BP wanted to know what problem is establishing a definition of a tree? JR noted it would make 
residents and engineers easier to understand.  
 
PA asked if there is a standard for the state of Massachusetts. JR noted that there is none however, 
he stated that the tree is universal. BP and JR discussed the definition of a tree. JR noted that we 
could supplement with a list of tree species. BP felt that we have tree replacement standard and 
reducing the tree standard to 2 inches would be over burdensome. JR noted that this is intended as 
a base definition. JV noted that some guidance could make sense however he recommended the 
minimum for tree replacement be 6 inches. ML noted that if the tree is not on the new England 
species list and could help avoid some confusion. ML noted that he felt 2 inches is pretty restrictive 
is too broad. He noted difficulties on purchasing larger diameter trees and most purchases are 
saplings.CC noted that she would like to see a minimum of a 4 inch diameter tree if a definition is 
adopted. BP noted that the existing regulations call for a minimum of 8 inch diameter DBH. He felt 
our existing regulations are sufficient as is. Jr noted that he was simply attempting to start a 
baseline. BP wants to have a full understanding of the intended and unintended implications. ML 
wanted to know should we think about longevity of trees and dwarf versus semi dwarf. BP used gray 
birch as an example versus a black oak for succession and life span. BP suggested taking the issue 
back up during a meeting with another light agenda and suggested doing a redline on the wetland 
regulations. 
 
Authorization for License to Hay Pleasant Street 
 
BP noted that Steve Debettencourt has been routinely haying the pleasant street property and 
requested the commission authorize BP to pursue a lease agreement. ML asked what was involved 
with the haying and if there was a profit being made. BP noted that there are other locations that 
need to be cut such as the Hunting Hill Property as part of the land use management. It was noted 
in a conversation with Matt Allison and he recommended a long term lease. ML moved to pursue a 
long term lease, JV seconded and the commission unanimously approved.  
 
Old Business-  review of encroachment onto all conservation properties using the assessors ortho 
photographs.  
 
BP reviewed different conservation parcels that 2019 GIS orthos show encroachments in the forms of 
minor items like lawns, shed and driveways. ML suggested that we should send a friendly letter 
showing our evidence and make the owner aware of it and see if it stops. If there is a very large 
encroachment, we may wish to pursue it differently. He feels that the letters and potential follow up 
reminders establishes a record that we are watching. The remainder of commissioners echoed ML’s 
comments. JR felt that we should potentially following the letter up with inviting the owner to a 
meeting to have a face to face discussion. JR also noted that as the addresses were discussed at the 
meeting and notify those owners and invite them to come and comment. BP felt th at we should not do 
that as we may create more stress on the situation than we intended and there was no conclusions 
drawn at this point. BP will review the situation with Adam Costa at the next upcoming meeting.
