Meeting Posted:  Yes 
Time:  6:35 PM 
Place:  Town Hall, 17 Main Street, Lunenburg, MA  01462 
Present:  Gregory Bittner, Matthew Allison, Kenneth Chenis, David Prokowiew, Michael-Ray Jeffreys 
Absent:  Adam R. Burney 
 
The Hearing was to hear and discuss an application for Site Plan Approval  under Section 8.4. and Special Permit under Section 4.7.  of the 
Protective Bylaw of the Town of Lunenburg for construction of an addition for office and warehouse space at 40 Pioneer Drive (Map 146, Parcel 
22) and 98 Leominster Shirley Road (Map 146, Parcel 24).    
 
The Applicant, Ecological Fibers, Inc.  (Steven Quill- owner) was represented by Anthony Cleaves, Whitman & Bingham Associates (WBA).  
Also present on behalf of the Applicant were Walter Bennett, Bennett Building Corp., Leominster, MA and David R obbins, Vice President, 
Ecological Fibers, Inc. 
 
Copies of the Proposed Site & Grading Plan dated May 24, 2017 and Architecturals dated May 31, 2017 were provided to the Board.  (These 
items were part of the original application.)   The application is for a n approximately 68,000 square foot warehouse expansion with associated 
parking.  To accommodate the expansion, the existing parking will be relocated and additional parking will be added.  There are currently two 
existing curb cuts on Pioneer Drive; one to the left of the existing building  for the loading area and one to the right.  The two existing curb cuts 
will continue to be utilized and a new curb cut added to obtain access to the proposed loading docks.  The entrance on the left will access the 
parking lots and the entrance to the right will be for delivery trucks.  The existing domestic water and sewer service will remain.  A new  line off 
the street for fire service will be installed.  The stormwater system will consist of grass swales, catch basins and stormwater basins.   
 
The Board questioned the number of curb cuts under Protective Bylaw of the Town of Lunenburg, § 6.4.3., §§ 4.b.2. “The number of driveways 
permitting entrance to and exit from a lot shall be limited to two per street line.”   
 
D. Prokowiew questioned the parking calculations  as Protective Bylaw of the Town of Lunenburg,  § 6.1., §§ 6.1.1.7. specifies “One (1) space 
for each one thousand (1,000) square feet of gross floor area…”.  A. Cleaves noted his calculations are from the ITE (Institute of Transportation 
Engineers) Manual.  One per 1,000 square feet is for office use, but this is warehouse use.  A. Cleaves stated that p arking calculations for 
warehouse use are not specified in the Protective Bylaw of the Town of Lunenburg .  § 6.1., §§ 6.1.1.9. states  “For uses not specified above , 
the number of required spaces will be determined by the Building Official based on accepted planning standards…”   Current parking space s 
are approximately 60; will be increasing to a total of 95.   
 
D. Prokowiew noted no landscape plan.  A. Cleaves noted that trees are shown on Sheet #2, Proposed Site & Grading Plan.  Tree placement 
within the parking lot complies with Protective Bylaw of the Town of Lunenburg, § 6.4.3.e) 9.a..  Ornamental trees will probably be planted near 
the building entrance and shrubbery plantings by the site drive towards the office.  There are no specifics at this time. 
 
K. Chenis questioned size of vehicles accessing the loading docks.  Eighteen -wheelers; they will pull into t he site, but then back into the 
loading docks.  There will typically be five to six trucks in the morning and six to eight in the afternoon.   
 
M. Jeffreys noted that in conversation with Mr. Quill, he was given to understand that this proposal is for current needs as well as future.   
 
The majority of the snow storage will be towards Leominster Shirley Road.

M. Jeffreys inquired as to a photometric plan.  Wall-mounted lights are shown on the plan.  No light poles are planned for the parking lot.  The 
one residential abutter is located 250 feet from the site.  Lighting will be dark sky compliant as required by the Protective Bylaw of the Town of 
Lunenburg.   
 
Roof runoff will be collected by gutters and drains and piped out to basins. 
 
No specifics on signage are provided at this time.  A. Cleaves noted it could be added to the plans and the Applicant could return to the Board if 
they chose to deviate from the plan. 
 
There are two shifts; one from 7:00 AM to 3:30 PM and the other from 3:30 PM to Midnight. 
 
Departmental comments were noted.  As the site connects to Leominster sewer, Leominster has requested a water and sewer clear ance plan 
for any new connections .  The Lunenburg Fire Chief noted little information on fire protection was available.  He expects to see plans for fire 
protection and recommends review by a fire protection engineer due to the size of the addition.  A. Cleaves not ed that fire protection plans will 
be part of the building permit process. 
 
Peer review was done by Jeffrey Walsh, Graves Engineering, Inc. (GEI).  J. Walsh summarized his July 20, 2017 review letter ( on file in the 
Planning Office).  Comments 1 -6 are for information required under the Protective Bylaw of the Town of Lunenburg  and Board Rules & 
Regulations for Site Plan Approval and Special Permits.   Of note is #4 in which “… all elevations refer to NGVD 1929.  The required horizontal 
datum is Massachusetts State Plane and the required vertical datum is NAVD88.”   Under Hydrology Computations and Stormwater 
Management, J. Walsh noted he was comfortable with the hydrology computations noted in #7.  Per #9, the depth of the wick drain needs to be 
revised.  General Engineering Comments, #10 -  three drainage pipes are proposed  to the right of the building  into the Town’s right of way   It 
would be efficient to have one manhole to which all three pipes connect and only one pipe into the street.  J. Walsh opined having only one 
would benefit the Town.  He was also pleased with the soil testing logs. 
 
A. Cleaves noted in addition to the requested waivers, he will also request a waiver from the required vertical datum of NAVD88 noted in  #4  of 
GEI’s review letter .  1929 datum was used on anything previously done  in Pioneer Park by WBA.  Re #9 – Applicant will change material to 
clean sand.  Re #10 – will add manhole. 
 
Some Board members inquired of J. Walsh if he would recommend a turning movement radii plan and a traffic study.  J. Walsh did not feel it 
was necessary. 
 
G. Bittner requested the datum waiver be documented for the next meeting.   J. Walsh recommended that the waiver be approved in order to 
keep all datum 1929. 
 
Waivers were requested from the following Sections of the Rules and Regulations for Site Plan Approval & Special Permits: 
4.1.f.22 – Photometric Plan 
4.1.h. – Level of Service Analysis 
4.1.h. – Trip Generation 
4.1.h. – Community Impacts 
 
Motion made, Michael -Ray Jeffreys to accept the four requested  waivers, Second, D. Prokowiew , discussion- M. Allison noted the site 
abuts a residential use and questioned if A. Burney would have any input they may not be aware of, all aye.  Waivers approved. 
 
A. Cleaves requested a Motion to contin ue the Hearing to August 14, 2017.  Motion made  to continue Hearing to August 14, 2017 , K. 
Chenis, Second, D. Prokowiew, all aye.  Hearing continued to August 14, 2017, 6:35 PM.

The Hearing  was to hear and discuss an application for Special Permit to relocate a driveway at 675 Reservoir Road, Lot 1A, and 667 
Reservoir Road, Lot 2A (Map 134, Parcels 003 and 049) under Protective Bylaw of the Town of Lunenburg. Section 4.8. Flood Plain District.     
 
G. Bittner noted the August 3, 2017 memorandum from  A. Burney which outlines the  intention of the property owner.  The Applicant/owner, 
Paul Goguen, Reservoir Road LLC, presented his application.   His plan is to straighten out the existing driveway at 675 Reservoir Road to 
increase sight distance when entering onto Reservoir Road.  An Order of Conditions has been issued by the Conservation Commission.  The 
wetlands will be replicated at the driveway that will be removed.  
 
Amedeo Bilotta, 18 Fire Road 7-  Concerned with puddling on Reservoir Road.  Inquired if P. Goguen could put in a drainage system.   
 
P. Goguen noted that A. Bilotta is talking about  a puddling issue at  667 Reservoir Road and that the driveway change is at 675 Reservoir 
Road.  He stated that his driveway is gravel and any runoff from his driveway stays on his property. 
 
Joe Bilotta, 200 Prospect Street- He noted that the slush builds up in the wintertime, but he doesn’t know how it can be fixed  as the driveway is 
already established.  He reiterated what P. Goguen said, that A. Bilotta is talking about an existing driveway at 667 Reservoir Road and not 675 
Reservoir Road. 
 
The Board clarified that the issue before them is 675 Reservoir Road. 
 
K. Chenis inquired if the Conservation Commis sion would approve drainage being put back in u nder the driveway to recreate what the 
previously existing swale used to do.  He went on to state that as this is water on the road, it may be a DPW issue instead of a property owner 
issue.   
 
J. Bilotta noted that the puddling is at the higher end of the road at 667, not the lower end at 675. 
 
G. Bittner noted that the Board cannot force P. Goguen to remediate an issue that was already in place prior to this and relocating this driveway 
will not worsen the situation. 
 
G. Bittner noted correspondence from the Town Treasurer indicating delinquent taxes in a minimal amount.  The Town Manager indicated to G. 
Bittner that if taxes are more than one year delinquent, the Board can physically withhold a special permit.  The Board could still issue a special 
permit, but P. Goguen would not be able to get it until those delinquent taxes are paid.  Those taxes amount to $19.62.  The Board can 
Condition that those taxes are paid as part of its Decision.   
 
Motion, D. Prokowiew, to close the Public Hearing, Second, K. Chenis, all aye.  Hearing closed 7:24 PM

The Board of Selectmen noted its major goals as improving community satisfaction through communication and outreach,
developing an infrastructure plan, and identifying economic development in the Town. Other b/c/c's noted some of their major
goals as follows: Finance Committee- would like to have a sense of Town priorities going into the budget season. Parks
Department- communication and public involvement. Board of Assessors- identify taxable personal property within the Town.
Continue to build the GIS (Geographical Information System) capabilities. Improve the website to make it easier for people to
pay their taxes. Planning Board- continue to address the bylaw. Increase communication with other Town departments and the
public. Public Library- continue to improve services. Stormwater Task Force & Agricultural Commission liaison - standardize
minutes and agendas Town-wide. Charter Review Committee-putting a 4 phased approach in place; 1) community outreach
and information, 2) work groups, 3) draft language, 4) community outreach. PACC (Public Access Cable Committee) - continue
serve the community through media broadcasting. Increase community interaction. Looking for volunteers for both the
Committee and the running of the equipment. Cable Advisory Committee- in negotiations with Comcast as the current contract
Council on Aging- Eagle House is now open on Fridays with no increase to the budget. Would like to increase
evening ciass availability to the public. Architectural Preservation District Commission- working to formulate its guidelines and
regulations for the District. Zoning Board of Appeals-recently revised its 40B guidelines, Capital Planning Committee-forms
have been updated into digital format.
Numerous representatives brought up the fact that if the minutes are being taken by a member, that member does not have the
opportunity to participate in the meeting. A better system needs to be devised.

PUBLIC HEARING PROCESS:
A. Burney put together a handout on the public hearing process. Some of the hearings can be very lengthy and the handout
recommended a time limit for speaking, both for Board members and the audience. He mentioned how individuals can become
perturbed with one another as the evening progresses, especially if the same material is being reiterated. K. Chenis wanted
individual Board member synopsizes and then move on. Those member synopsizes would be a feeder to a Board conversation.
Board members wanted the dialogue session to be free flowing instead of having a time restriction. M. Allison felt that the
current process works well and that instituting time limits is ridiculous.
A. Burney noted there are ways to be firm and respectful without being offensive to the applicants. He went on to note that there
are only a few engineering firms the Board sees frequently. Communities get reputations and when people are looking for sites
a community may get passed over because the applicant doesn't want to deal with a community's regulatory process. He went
on to state that putting together a list of what needs to get addressed is helpful for an applicant. K. Chenis noted that if more
concrete guidance is given to an applicant, there would be less back and forth and the hearing would be more efficient; e.g., the
Board states there is not enough screening for a project, the applicant returns to the next meeting with a plan showing increased
screening, the Board states there is still not enough screening, the applicant again revises the plan and returns to another
meeting, etc. etc. Board members had varying opinions ranging from the Board having taken a stronger stance than in the past
to a tone of cross-examination of an applicant.
M. Jeffreys suggested A. Burney prepares summary memorandums (sample provided) to the Board on every substantial
conversation he has with individuals that come in to the office. It was also his opinion that the Board is unaware of A. Burney's
day to day interactions with individuals that meet with him in the office. K. Chenis noted that if a project is coming before the
Board, a brief summary of any interaction A. Burney had with the project proponents prior to a Board appearance could be
beneficial.
M. Allison felt that prior to accepting an application and scheduling a public hearing, all departmental reviews should be received.
A. Burney noted that the Board cannot get responses to an application that not been submitted. He is not aware of any
community that requires input and review of a project submission prior to acceptance of that application by another department.
There was Board discussion on reviewing the submitted documents at the beginning of a hearing to ensure they provided
adequate information.
One suggestion was for A. Burney, at the end of the hearing, based on Board member comments, compile a list for both the
Board and the applicant as to outstanding concerns.

The Public Hearing was to hear and discuss an application for Site Plan Approval under Section 8.4, of the Protective Bylaw of
the Town of Lunenburg for construction of a parking lot at 9 Electric Avenue (Map 074, Parcel 0009) and a building addition at
Asian Imperial Restaurant, 5 Electric Avenue (Map 074, Parcel 0008).
The Applicant, K& E Realty Trust, Min Xu - Trustee, submitted an application in December 2016 for same reasons noted above.
In February 2017, the Board granted the Applicant's request to withdraw without prejudice in order for her to reevaluate a course
of action with the property.
Min Xu was represented by Rob Oliva, David E. Ross Associates, 6 Lancaster County Road, Harvard, MA. A project overview
was presented. Three Phases are proposed.
Phase 1- first half of the new upper parking area (16 spaces) with associated
drainage, a wall separating the upper parking area from the lower parking area and pedestrian access from the upper parking
area to the lower; Phase 2- if needed, building the back half of the new parking area (15 spaces), and modification to the existing
lower parking area for the proposed driveway at the site rear (would result in loss of some spaces in lower level); and Phase 3-
construction of the building addition and associated parking changes. The parking approved under the Board's 2011 Decision
was for 25 spaces; there are 31 existing with two of them designated handicap. Under Phase 1, 31 will remain with 16 added for
a total of 47, Phase 2 will provide for a net total of 58 spaces. The building expansion and resulting grading in Phase 3 would
result in a loss of spaces, leaving a total of 48. Existing drainage will remain. A row of screening will be added for the immediate
abutter at 13 Electric Avenue.
Requested waivers from Board Rules and Regulations for Site Plan Approval & Special Permits:
§ 4.1.f.(2), Plans at a scale of 1"=40'.
• § 4.1.f.(7), Cut and till calculations.
§ 4.1.f.(16), Existing significant trees.
• §4.1.f.h, Impact Reports.
R. Oliva verbally requested the filing fee be waived as the previous application was withdraw without prejudice. Motion, D.
Prokowiew, to waive filing fee, Second, K. Chenis, Discussion, G. Bitter was not in favor of waiving the filing tee due to the
time expended by staff on the December 2016 application, M. Jeffreys, aye; D. Prokowiew, aye; K. Chenis, aye; M. Allison,
aye; G. Bittner, nay. Motion passed 4-1.
