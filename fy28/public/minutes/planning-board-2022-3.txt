The hearing was to hear a citizen’s petition to amend the Zoning Map by rezoning a portion of the land located at 199 
Massachusetts Avenue (Par cel 074-0051-0000) from Residence A to Commercial District so the entire lot is in the Commercial 
District.   
 
ARTICLE XX  To see if the Town will vote to amend the  Zoning Map by rezoning a portion of the land 
located at 199 Massachusetts Avenue from Residence A to Commercial District so the entire lot is in the 
Commercial District, as shown on a plan on file at the office of the Town Clerk, Select Board and 
Planning Board, or take any other action thereto 
 
Property co-owners Michael and Mark Chapdelaine  (article petitioners), noted they had owned the property for 53 years.  They 
became aware that a portion of the property was zoned Residence A when they began to research leasing a portion of the 
property.  The property consisted of ~17 acres, but o nly about 1/3 of the property was zoned Commercial.  The property ha d 
been used commercially since 1938 by both current and prior owners.  The Chapdelaine’s had approached A. Burney as to 
available options.  A. Burney noted the property had been used commercially f or probably the past 80 -90 years; there was an 
assumption of pre-existing, non-conforming commercial use.  If the Town voted against the Zoning Map amendment, there were 
other options at the petitioners’ disposal ; i.e., a Zoning Board of Appeals special pe rmit.  It would be more beneficial for a “hard 
codified” zoning change.  If the use remain ed pre-existing, non-conforming, other uses could be brought onto the property ; i.e., if 
a non-conforming use was introduced into a residential zone the transfer of one non-conforming to another non-conforming could 
be extremely different and “out of the box”.  The change to a commercial zone would create more structure on future uses.   
 
A. Burney mentioned that the Select Board had noted concerns when reviewing the w arrant article ; a buffer zone for existing 
neighbors, development of the property by a new owner.  A. Burney clarified that Site Plan Approval would still be required, 
which would involve notification of abutters.  The Select Board also noted concern that the abutters had not been notified of the 
zoning change.  A. Burney stressed that was not required within the statute.  The Board utilized the legal advertisement process 
and town meeting.

M. Brenner made Motion to close the hearing for the citizen petition for 199 Massachusetts Avenue, A. Reid Seconded,

The Hearing  was to hear and review  an application for site plan approval under Chapter 250 Section 8.4. of the Code of the 
Town of Lunenburg  for a use of shopping center as granted by the Zoning Board of Appeals at 88 Massachusetts Avenue .  
Applicant/Owner- Anavasi, LLC, 28 Clement Road, Townsend, MA. 
 
Gregory Roy, Dillis & Roy Civil Design Group , represented the applicant.  The applicant had previously received a change of use 
special permit from the Zoning Board of Appeals.  The Zoning Board of Appeals special permit was to divide the space from the 
current three tenant spaces into a total of five tenant spaces , which changed the use to a “shop ping center”.  (A shopping center 
was more than three uses on a site in a building greater than 5,000 square feet. )  Two tenant spaces were currently in use.  At 
the time of this hearing there was not a specific use defined for the larger space.  The appli cant had difficult y marketing the 
spaces and was looking for approval of a use allowed by right in the district to make the property more marketable.  A site plan 
was presented that maximized the number of parking spaces on site.  The large curb cut would be consolidated into a specific 
ingress/egress.  As a result, some parking spaces could be created along the frontage.  Employee parking would be located at 
the rear of the site.  The property line on the northeast side cut through a portion of the parking .  ( Through a land lease, t here 
was an easement conveyed with the property.)  With this application, parking was placed on only the applicant’s property.  There 
were currently three leasable spaces with two in use.   The former Grant Rental unit was larger than any single tenant want ed to 
lease; therefore, the applicant wanted to divide that into two or three units to appeal to a variety of tenants.   
 
M. Brenner was concerned with the snow storage indicated along the frontage of the site.  The Board would condition the height 
and location of the snow storage.

screening, indicating distinctions between proposed and retained vegetation.

The Hearing continuation was to hear and review application s for site plan approval and stormwater management permit under 
Chapter 250 Section 8.4. and Chapter 204 respectively of the Code of the Town of Lunenburg for construction of a 367,000 sq.ft. 
warehouse and distribution building  on Airport Road/Crawford in Fitchburg, MA, with a portion of the development extending into 
Lunenburg at 260 Falulah R oad.  Owner - Fitchburg Redevelopment Authority, Fitchburg, MA.  Applicant -  Fitchburg Land 
Property Owner, LLC, Boston, MA. 
 
Christopher Anderson, Hannigan Engineering, represented the applicant.  The initial hearing was held in May; since that time the 
applicant had been working with the Planning Board and Conservation Commission from Fitchburg, and also the Lunenburg 
Conservation Commission.  The property was currently vacant land.  Access to the site would be on the Fitchburg side from 
Crawford Street/Airport Road.  There would not be any tie -ins to Lunenburg roads.  There would be two a ccess points; one of 
them being specifically for trucks  and the other for employees and pedestrian vehicles .  The majority of employee parking would 
be located in Fitchbur g.  Stormwater was split between two systems.  One in Fitchburg on the westerly side of the building 
consisting of two infiltration basins designed to accommodate the flow from ¾ of the site.  The second system consisted of 
underground storage systems and infiltration basins directing flow to the southerly entrance (truck entrance).  Sewer and water 
would be provided by Fitchburg.  Both Fitchburg and Lunenburg had its own individual peer reviews.  The Plan was revised to 
October 7, 2022.  The Board ha d not yet received an updated review from Graves Engineering, Inc.  It was noted that  in the 
initial review, Graves Engineering, Inc. had concerns with the site slopes.  Several slopes were proposed steeper than 2H:1V 
(two high : one  vertical).  To address that issue, the applicant made the slopes shallower.  The driveway was shifted along 
Airport Road for the pedestrian entrance to increase the sight line distances at the Nashua River bridge (in Fitchburg).
