Executive Summary 
 
The Groton-Dunstable Regional School District is responsible for preparing the annual 
operating budget in accordance with School Committee guidance, state policies and 
guidelines, and the major goals and objectives of the organization. The budget will be 
certified by a vote of the School Committee vote in March 2026, however this may not 
be the final budget as funding in both towns is yet to be determined.

2026 to align with the District Improvement Plan: 
 
Budget Funding:

FY27 Budget Focus on Preservation of Level Services 
 
The goals of the school committee and administration for the budget year continues 
to be based on responding to anticipated staff and student needs.  Budget reductions 
in FY24, FY25 and FY26 resulted in personnel reductions across all job classifications.  
This has led to increased class sizes, reduced class offerings and reduced staff and 
student support.  Existing staff are now tackling not only their own responsibilities, 
but absorbing some additional duties previously performed by a position that was 
reduced.  In FY26, the district has already reduced 3.0 FTE’s by not filling vacancies.  
These positions are not included in the FY27 proposed budget, thus the budget being 
presented is below level services to start deliberations.  It will be programmatically 
devastating to experience another fiscal year that could potentially reduce more staff 
and support services. 
 
Allocation of Human and Fiscal Resources to Achieve Goals and Objectives 
 
The following educational priorities, aligned to district goals, have shaped the 
creation of this budget: 
 
• Administration will provide feedback on how the personnel budget cuts that 
took place in the current fiscal year have impacted student learning, health 
and safety.   
• The use of $300,000 in Excess and Deficiency Funds in order to reduce the 
assessments to both towns.  This is a decrease from $400,000 appropriated for 
the current fiscal year, which is a district goal to wean off of using one time 
revenue sources for operating expenses.  
• The School District is committed to educating the town governments and 
citizens about state funding to the school district (Chapter 70) and how the 
hold harmless status of Groton-Dunstable has put the financial burden of 
funding support on the backs of the towns for decades.  This is what pushes the 
need for an operational override now and in the foreseeable future. 
• The school district will evaluate district fee collection including Athletic, 
Kindergarten, Preschool and Student Activity fees.  The Director of Finance & 
Operations will report to the school committee on current 
processes/procedures and report on collection of fees versus expenditures 
dependent on fees. 
• A comprehensive long-term district capital plan has been drafted using the 
mechanical, electrical and plumbing (MEP) systems reports received last spring.  
Additionally, a motivated new Director of Buildings & Grounds has contributed 
to what repairs and equipment are needed throughout the district.  Thus, the

Significant Financial and Demographic Changes 
 
There are a number of significant cost factors which have influenced the FY27 budget. 
 
The recommended budget is a less than level service budget.  The general fund 
operating budget increased by 6.46% .  Cost drivers include total wages increasing 
5.42%, employee benefits up 7.92%, health insurance up 8.9%, substitute costs 
increasing $200K and increased support for the athletic program.  Maintenance costs 
are increasing by $145K due to deferred maintenance in prior years.  Out of District 
tuition costs and enrollments continue to increase as well.   
 
It should be noted as well that the transportation costs are higher than average.  
Typically, excess regional transportation reimbursements from the prior year are used 
to reduce the assessments to the two Towns.  As actual reimbursement received is 
very close to what is budgeted, there is no surplus to offset assessments.  This takes 
away from the availability of town funds to fund operating expenses. 
 
The total revenue are estimated to be greater than the estimated expenses as 
Administration is recommending rebuilding positive fund balances in School Choice, 
Circuit Breaker and Full Day Kindergarten to reduce expenses to the General Fund.

Organizational Section 
 
District Overview 
 
Groton-Dunstable Regional School District (GDRSD), which serves the towns of Groton 
and Dunstable, was established in 1967 and has an enrollment of 2,290 students at six 
schools, PK-12. 
The schools are Groton-Dunstable Regional High School, Marion Stoddart Middle 
School, Groton-Dunstable Regional Middle School North, Florence Roche Elementary, 
Swallow Union Elementary Schools, and the Boutwell Early Childhood Center. 
 
Mission Statement 
The Groton-Dunstable Regional School District, in cooperation with the parents and 
the community, is committed to providing the best possible education for each 
student. It is our responsibility to promote in each child a spirit of inquiry and to 
instill a self-sustaining desire for continuous growth and service to self, family and 
community. 


===PAGE 15===

Vision Statement 
Guiding students to learn today, lead tomorrow, and reach the world. 
 
● Embrace a growth mindset. In our District, talents and abilities develop through 
effort, purposeful teaching, and persistence. Our students become curious, 
engaged learners ready to become positive contributors to local and global 
communities. 
 
● Eliminate inequities for all students. Our students are an active part of the 
design and delivery of their own education so they become self-directed, 
creative problem solvers. Our universally designed, tiered instructional model 
meets the needs of all students, is based on a comprehensive standards-based 
curriculum and assessed by authentic tasks. 
 
 
● Broaden the meaning of success. We have an expansive definition of student 
success that encompasses academic achievement, integrated arts, athletics, as 
well as social, emotional, and behavioral learning. 
 
● Create environments for innovation. Our schools provide interactive, inclusive 
environments that enhance student success through design, technology, 
creative spaces, and collaboration. 
 
Governance Structure 
 
The School Committee's primary responsibility is to establish those purposes, 
programs, and procedures that will best produce the educational achievement needed 
by our students.  The Committee is charged with accomplishing this while also being 
responsible for wise management of resources available to the school system.  The 
Committee must fulfill these responsibilities by functioning primarily as a legislative 
body to formulate and adopt policy, by selecting an executive officer to implement 
policy, and by evaluating the results.  It must carry out its functions openly, while 
seeking the comments of public, students, and staff in its decision-making processes.

Budget Policies and Regulations 
 
Regional Agreement Regulations 
 
A. Tentative Maintenance and Operating Budget: At the opening of each 
academic year and using, exclusively, the line items and associated 
definitions described in the Chart of Accounts, as amended, established and 
maintained by the Massachusetts Department of Elementary and Secondary 
Education, the Committee shall as promptly as practicable proceed with the 
preparation of a tentative operating and maintenance budget for the ensuing 

===PAGE 18===
18 
 
fiscal year, including therein provision for any installment of principal or 
interest to become due in such fiscal year on any bonds, notes or other 
obligations of the District and any other capital costs to be apportioned to 
the member towns in such fiscal year. The budget process will adhere to all 
legal requirements. The proposed budget will be posted on the District 
website and a copy will be available in each town hall and public library. All 
necessary postings will occur as will a public hearing. 
 
B. Final Maintenance and Operating Budget: Following the public hearing on the 
tentative budget, the Committee may make any such modifications to its 
tentative budget as it may deem necessary or desirable and adopt an annual 
maintenance and operating budget for the ensuing fiscal year not later than 
forty-five days prior to the earliest date on which the business session of the 
annual town meeting in any member town meeting is to be held, but in no 
event, later than March 31, provided that said budget need not be adopted 
earlier than February 1. Said annual maintenance and operating budget shall 
include debt and interest and any other capital costs as separate items, and 
the said Committee shall apportion the amounts necessary to be raised in 
order to meet the said budget in accordance with the provisions of 
subsections IV (D) and IV (E). The amounts so apportioned to each member 
town shall be certified by the District Treasurer to the treasurers of the 
member towns within thirty days from the date on which the annual 
maintenance and operating budget is adopted by the Committee, and each 
town shall call a vote, at the next annual town meeting, whether or not to 
appropriate the amounts so certified to it. The District budget, as adopted by 
two-thirds (2/3) of the Committee, shall require the approval of both of the 
local member municipalities. If a budget is not approved by the member 
towns, the District shall follow the process to approve a final budget as 
stated in MGL Chapter 71, Section 16B, as amended. 
 
C. Annual Report: The Committee shall submit to each member town no later 
than November 1 of each year an annual report containing a detailed 
financial statement for the prior fiscal year, and a statement showing the 
method by which each member town’s proportionate share of the capital and 
operating costs of the District was computed, together with such additional 
information relative to the operation of the Regional District Schools as 
deemed necessary and appropriate by the Committee or as may be requested 
in writing and submitted to the Committee by the Board of Selectmen from 
any member town.

Accounting Practices 
 
The district follows the generally accepted accounting principles (GAAP) for local 
government author ities put forth by the Governmental Accounting Standards Board 
(GASB) in article 34. Additionally, the District maintains compliance with requirement 
from the Department of Elementary and Secondary Education to produce a budget and 
operations summary annua lly through the end of year report. The end  of year report 
will verify compliance with required net school spending, and minimum local 
contributions from each of the District’s member communities. 
 
Finally, the D istrict undergoes an extensive audit process  annually. The process 
examines financial operations, payroll, purchasing and financial reporting.

Revenue Sources and Expenditure Categories 
 
This high level overview of the revenue illustrates the budget by fund. We separate our budget into five different funds. 
The school district has examined all revenue sources and expenses to produce a budget that maximizes all of our revenue 
not just that from our member  districts. In some instances there is a recognition that the district had accumulated 
balances beyond what would fall in the general guidelines for an appropriate balance and in these instances expenditure 
has been planned higher than revenue, which will help to bring these balances to be in line with guidelines for appropriate 
balances  
 
000-General fund: This is the operating budget funded by State Revenue, Assessment and Receipts. 
 
200-Federal and State Grants: These funds support the general fund by picking up salaries, professional development, 
and supplies costs that would have to be charged to the general fund and assessment if the grants were not present. 
 
300-Competitive Grants: This fund has significant fluctuation and is difficult to budget because from year to year we 
never know which grants will be available and which we will be awarded. This makes up a very small portion of the 
budget overall but will aid the district in adding new programs or funding initiatives that are not recurring.  
 
400-Revolving accounts: These accounts are where fees and programs specific revenue are collected and expended. 
Examples are Athletics, Food Service, and Extended Day 
 
600-Capital/Technology Fund: in previous budget cycles capital and technology expenses were included in the general 
fund/operating budget. Per the school committee guidance in an effort to make a more transparent budget we have 
moved these expenses to their fund so that increases and decreases are separate from operating budgets.

Notable changes and components 
0- Health insurance renewal is anticipated to be around 12%, while retiree plans renewed at 5.0% in January.  The 
district debt continues to decrease with total debt being paid off in FY28. Middlesex County Retirement was a 9.15% 
increase.  
1-The 3.0% contractual increase in teaching salaries and a no known retiring staff  produced a increase in the costs for 
professional salaries.   
2-The 3.0% contractual increases drove the increase in Clerical and Support Staff. 
3- There is an increased utilization of substitute teachers in the district.  A younger staff using maternity, paternity, 
FMLA, etc. have driven the substitute expenses up $200K.   
4- The cleaning service contract renewed at a LOWER cost than the previous year. There is also one less NECC contract 
for our ABA programs within the district due to student population.  This was partially offset by a new SEL program 
developed at the middle school. 
5- The requests for supplies and materials were kept at basically level funding in order to allocate budget resources to 
other areas of the budget such as direct services.  However, there is a new unfunded mandated literacy program that 
required $70K in consumable materials.  This will be an ongoing expense. 
6-Educational contingency  and the OPEB line item remain level funded. Utilities continue to increase especial water 
expense as the high school will be going to town water in the summer of 2025.  Before the PFAS was discovered, the 
high school had it’s own private well.  Irrigation is growing as well as we have new grass and athletic fields to 
maintain. 
9-We had an increase in out of district tuitions. We are hopeful that the Circuit Breaker Reimbursment funds continue 
to be funded at the same level to maintain a revolving account balance that can absorb unforeseen increases.
