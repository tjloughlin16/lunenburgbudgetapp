FY25 BUDGET UPDATE 
 Business Manager Nancy Haines reported that the FY25 end-of-year report is in process. 
 
 Haines explained the financial impact of the district’s former Health Trust disbanding. The district 
initially allocated $1 million (from School Choice Revolving and General Fund) to cover potential run-
out claims. A significant portion of those funds was later returned and reallocated appropriately. 
 
 Haines reviewed the School Choice Revolving Fund, noting it is used for health insurance, turf field 
payments, and designated expenses. She reported that declining school choice enrollments, due to 
reduced acceptance of new students, may limit the fund’s sustainability. 
 
 Members discussed FY25 budget strategies, including reducing reliance on one-time funds to 
promote long-term sustainability. 
 
 Superintendent Morgan and Haines outlined FY25 actions taken to control spending and replenish 
Excess & Deficiency (E&D), including: 
o Soft spending freeze 
o Deferral of capital and technology projects 
o Shifting eligible positions to grants 
o Holding select vacancies unfilled 
o Additional cost-saving measures 
 Haines provided an update on supplemental funding and town assessments and said the  district 
received $302,000 in additional state Circuit Breaker reimbursement and additional Chapter 70 funds 
in FY26 to help offset increased assessments and unanticipated special education costs. Managing 
Special Education tuitions and transportation remains a significant ongoing challenge. 
 
 Superintendent Morgan agreed to provide a list of FY25 budget reductions. 
FY26 BUDGET REVIEW AND UPDATE 
 Budget-to-actual updates noted savings in health insurance (opt-out program approximately 
$200,000), tuition/transportation, and retirements. 
 Fuel/heating costs are projected to exceed the FY26 budget by approximately $140,000. 
 Staffing remains fluid due to open positions; a staffing audit is being planned. 
 Deferred capital needs are accumulating for future years. 
 Members discussed continued monitoring of FY26 finances and proactive planning for FY27 budget 
development.

FY27 Revenue & Budget Projections 
Nancy Haines presented a draft FY27 revenue projection based on a 3% budget increase. State aid was 
estimated at $60 per student, though the final amount may range from $30–$150 depending on the 
state budget and the Governor’s proposal. 
Town Assessments & State Aid 
Minimum required contributions for member towns will influence how local assessments are allocated 
but do not affect Chapter 70 funding levels. Current estimates project a 3.94% increase in town 
assessments.

Final town-by-town assessment breakdowns and pupil counts will be updated once the Governor 
releases the January budget proposal, including adjustments for school choice-in and choice-out 
students. 
Excess & Deficiency Funds 
The School Committee has not yet determined the amount of E&D to apply toward the FY27 budget. A 
draft reserve policy is under development, recommending the district maintain a 3–4% reserve. 
Building Utilization 
The district is reviewing potential costs and operational considerations related to reopening Ashby. 
Members also discussed significant unused space across current facilities and explored options for 
classroom reallocations and responding to shifting enrollment. 
Long-Term Facilities Strategy 
Modular classrooms were discussed as a potential cost-effective and quickly deployable solution for 
addressing enrollment pressures and space needs. 
Budget Challenges 
Andrew MacLean shared two revenue handouts and noted that preliminary projections show a $64,000 
deficit at 3% budget growth compared to a $1.5 million deficit at 5%. A discussion followed regarding 
rising health insurance and retirement costs, which continue to strain both town and district budgets 
and signal the likelihood of difficult financial decisions ahead. 
Grants & State Funding 
Superintendent Morgan provided an update, noting that the district continues to pursue additional 
grant opportunities, including Career Technical Education (CTE) pilot grants and MSBA grant extensions. 
He expressed disappointment regarding the delay in pilot grants program. 
Regionalization 
The group discussed the ongoing value of exploring regionalized services across municipalities. 
Superintendent Morgan said the preliminary FY27 budget would be shared once departmental 
submissions are complete, likely after Thanksgiving. Additional discussion is expected at the December 
School Committee meeting. 
Action Items 
Andrew MacLean will send the FY27 revenue projection documents electronically to Nancy Haines and 
Robin Eibye and include additional explanatory notes.

 Fiscal Reserves 
 A financial reserve policy is a means of ensuring the long-term financial health and flexibility of 
 the North Middlesex Regional School District, as well as a tool to support the District's financial 
 decision-making. Financial rating agencies currently recommend that local governments 
 maintain strong and varied financial reserves of up to 8% of the total General Fund Operating 
 Budget (excluding the OPEB fund). 
 Financial reserve policies cover various classes of reserve funds intended to provide for 
 unanticipated expenses (contingency funds) and to supplement the General Fund Operating 
 Budget. 
 ●  Operating reserves, which provide resources for unanticipated expenditures or 
 unexpected revenue losses during the year; 
 ●  Capital Reserves, which provide resources for the repair, replacement, or construction of 
 capital infrastructure. 
 ●  OPEB Reserves, which provide for funding of the other post-employment benefit liability. 
 The District is committed to establishing and maintaining varied reserves that provide for 
 stability and predictability in our financial planning. The District will maintain the following 
 reserve funds and contribute to and maintain these funds as outlined below. 
 The NMRSD School Committee understands that the budget is a good faith contract with the 
 community members from each town. Therefore, the district will strive to create a budget, and 
 fully use those appropriations, that is consistent with the student's best interest in mind. If the 
 EOY balance on any budget exceeds $500,000, it should trigger an examination of the current 
 year's budget for any necessary adjustments. 
 The NMRSD School Committee believes that the budget should be prepared for known 
 expenses, with reserve funds used for unanticipated expenses. Therefore, if the EOY balance 
 on any budget exceeds $500,000 in unexpended expenses, it should trigger an examination of 
 the current year's budget for areas that should be reduced. 
 Excess & Deficiency (E&D) – Reserve Class: Operating and Capital 
 The available E&D balance is calculated and certified each year by the Massachusetts 
 Department of Revenue, Division of Local Services using data submitted by the District. 
 Increases to the E&D balance on an annual basis consist of revenues exceeding budgeted 
 amounts, as well as unspent appropriations. While the allowable use of E&D, by statute, is 
 largely unrestricted; sound financial practice suggests that this reserve provides for the 

===PAGE 24===
 financing of unforeseen, emergency needs such as unanticipated operating or capital costs or 
 loss of revenue and should not be used regularly as a revenue source in future budgets. 
 Current legislation limits the District’s E&D balance to a maximum of 5% of the District’s total 
 General Fund Operating Budget. It is recommended that we maintain between 3 - 4% E&D 
 balance to protect against unforeseen costs. The School Committee may consider budgeting 
 anything in excess of the 3% for: 
 ●  Additional investment in the Stabilization or OPEB reserve accounts. 
 ●  One-time purchases  . 
 ●  Planned future one time expenditures (must be documented)  , 
 ●  Support pilot program costs. 
 ●  Phasing in of new initiatives. 
 ●  Payment toward town debt principal. 
 ●  Applied as funding toward the current year budget 
 ●  Applied as funding source for the following year's budget 
 ●  Other needed use. 
 T  he NMRSD School Committee, on occasion, may approve  by a two-thirds vote the use 
 of existing E&D Funds as additional revenue within a current fiscal year in order to 
 cover unanticipated revenue shortfalls or to fund non-recurring or unforeseen 
 expenditures that are deemed as either extraordinary or unavoidable by District 
 Administration and the School Committee. Any vote to increase the current year Budget 
 through the use of E&D Funds is considered an increase to the district budget and shall 
 be submitted to all member Towns for approval in accordance with DESE regulations. 
 Capital Stabilization Fund –  Reserve Class: Capital 
 A Capital Stabilization Fund is a reserve allowable by Massachusetts General Laws (MGL) 
 Chapter 71, Section 16G ½ to provide the District with the ability to accumulate resources for 
 any purpose in which the District could otherwise borrow funds, as well as any other lawful 
 purpose upon approval of the Massachusetts Department of Revenue, Division of Local 
 Services. Capital stabilization funds may be used to finance capital activities as well as cover 
 unanticipated one-time Capital needs. The District shall maintain a Capital Stabilization fund in 
 accordance with MGL Chapter 71, Section 16G ½.  Additionally, it is the intention of the District 
 that the Capital Stabilization Fund be funded by certified available excess E&D above the 3% 
 recommended minimum balance, one-time revenues, and/or a line item in the District’s General 
 Fund Operating Budget.  In an effort to accumulate resources within the Capital Stabilization 
 Fund, the District may include a line item within the General Fund Operating Budget for 
 appropriation annually until the maximum limit is reached. It is the intention of the School 
 Committee that the Capital Stabilization shall not be further funded once it exceeds 10% of the 
 District’s prior year’s Total General Fund appropriations  with the exception of planned 
 documented savings for a specific one time purpose.  . This fund shall be considered, in addition 
 to funding special capital projects, the reserve (contingency) fund for all unanticipated capital 

===PAGE 25===
 expenses and it shall not be common practice to include contingency capital expenses within 
 general operating budget lines except for planned capital projects. 
 Other Post-Employment Benefits (OPEB) – Reserve Class: Other Post Employment Benefits 
 The District currently maintains an irrevocable Other Post-Employment Benefits Trust Fund in 
 accordance with MGL 32B Section 20, for the purpose of accumulating resources to provide 
 legally required retiree health insurance benefits.  The objective of the OPEB Trust fund is to 
 accumulate assets to fully fund the actuarially calculated liability of providing these future health 
 insurance benefits.  It is in the best financial interest of the District that the OPEB Trust Fund 
 balance not be withdrawn until the point in time at which the actuarially calculated OPEB liability 
 is fully funded. In an effort to accumulate resources within the OPEB Trust Fund, the School 
 Committee may include a contribution to the OPEB Trust in the general fund budget annually 
 until fully funded.
