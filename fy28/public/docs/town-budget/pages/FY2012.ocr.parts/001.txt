   1|                                                                       INMEMORIAM
   2|                                                              PAUL B. PORTER - Paul was born in Fitchburg and grew up in Lunenburg and
   3|                                                              graduated from Lunenburg High School. Following his graduation, Paul joined the U.S.
   4|                                                              Marine Corps and served for four years. He was honorably discharged in 1963 having
   5|                                                              attained the rank of corporal. When he returned home he became a reserve police officer
   6|                                                              with the Lunenburg Police Department and served in that capacity for 46 years.
   7|                                                              Paul was a member of the Lunenburg Planning Board and Zoning Board of Appeals. He
   8|                                                              was president of the Lunenburg Historical Society, member of the Masons and served as a
   9|                                                              Constable. He was member and past president of the Lunenburg Police Relief Association
  10|                                                              and senior vice commander of the American Legion Post 283.
  11|                           Paul was a proud Marine Corps Devil Dog and member and past Commandant of the Marine Corps League Fitchburg
  12|                           Detachment #1076. He was honored by the Department of Massachusetts Marine Corps League as their "Marine of the
  13|                           Year" for 2011-2012.
  14|                           He was affectionately referred to as "The Mayor of Lunenburg" by his daughter Pam and many others. Everyone who
  15|                           knew him loved him.
  16|                                                               RICHARD "NICK" STORES - Nick was born and brought up in New
  17|                                                               York, educated at Pomfret School and Yale University, and moved to Fitchburg in 1962 to
  18|                                                               teach    at Applewild School after teaching in Switzerland for two years. He was
  19|                                                               instrumental in forming the Fitchburg Youth Hockey League which played at Applewild's
  20|                                                               outdoor rink before the Wallace Civic Center was built in the early 1970s.
  21|                                                               Nick moved to Lancaster Avenue in Lunenburg in 1970 and quickly became involved with
  22|                                                               town activities. Whether it was sports or additional education programs he was always
  23|                                                                ready to volunteer for something. He introduced the Math Olympiad program to the 5th
  24|                                                                and 6th grades in the early 1980s and taught the kids the importance of thinking through a
  25|                                                                word problem rather than just concentrating on the "right" answer. Nick served on the
  26|                            School Committee for several years and became the Chairman in 1988. Always concerned about the children of
  27|                           Lunenburg getting the best education available, he worked closely with the Superintendents and other members of the
  28|                            School Committee toward that goal.
  29|                           In 1988 Nick became a Realtor with Century 21 Fontaine in Fitchburg and subsequently bought the franchise. He and
  30|                            his wife, Jeanie, created Century 21 Realty Team and moved the business to Leominster. Covering most of the towns
  31|                            of Northern Worcester County, the philosophy of the company has always focused on service and serving the real
  32|                            estate needs of the community. As a very active member of the Montachusett Area Rotary Club Nick's enthusiasm for
  33|                           "Service Above Self" extended to both local civic projects as well as multiple trips to Central America with Habitat for
  34|                            Humanity. At home, he became a Commissioner of Trust Funds and served as Chairman until his death in 2012

   1|                                                                      REPORT OF THE TOWN MANAGER
   2|                    The Town continued to make important progress in 2012 in many areas, including: School Building; Renewable Energy;
   3|                  . Roadway Improvements and Building Preservation. In addition, we hit a milestone by surpassing 10,000 in population.
   4|                    In 2012, we bid farewell to both Police Chief Dan Bourgeois and Fire Chief Scott Glenny. Together, these two fine
   5|                    gentlemen provided a combined 62 years of service to the Town of Lunenburg! They worked diligently up until the end,
   6|                    particularly in finalizing the Town's plan to join the Nashoba Valley Regional Emergency Communication Center. In 2012,
   7|                    we also welcomed long-time Police Lieutenant Jim Marino as our new Police Chief, and long-time Deputy Fire Chief Pat
   8|                    Sullivan as our new Fire Chief.
   9|                    In 2012, the Town formed a School Building Committee to continue working toward the goal of building a new or renovate
  10|                    an existing school building. Please review the Superintendent of School's report for additional information.
  11|                    A good portion of the first half of 2012 was dedicated to reviewing the FY2013 Budget proposal and Override request. After
  12|                    a significant amount of discussion, the Board of Selectmen, School Committee and Finance Committee agreed that it was
  13|                    time to ask the community to consider an Override. The impact of the $2.2M Operating Override to the Average
  14|                    Homeowner was determined to be just under $500 per year. In the end, the Override was defeated by a large margin, and
  15|                    as a result, several large scale changes were made, including the decision to consolidate four schools into three and to
  16|                    transition all Administrative and Land Use Offices to a 4-day work schedule.
  17|                    A significant amount of time was spent this past year reviewing three different, large solar projects: Borrego at the Town's
  18|                    Landfill; EPG at Pleasant & West Streets; and Mass PV1 on Chase Road. The project at the Town's Landfill site is a town-
  19|                    sponsored project, while the other two are private projects. Unfortunately, the project at the Landfill was determined to be
  20|                    uneconomic. The two private projects are still viable. Both private projects will provide the Town with the opportunity to
  21|                    enter into Power Purchase Agreements, whereby the Town can purchase Net Metering Credits as a discounted price to be
  22|                    used to offset the Town's electric utility costs for municipal buildings.
  23|                    This year, we saw the sale of a town-owned historic building to a private party. The Jones House- the former homestead of
  24|                    American botanist, horticulturalist and pioneer in agricultural science Luther Burbank, was sold in June to Larry & Dawn
  25|                    Marshall. Under the conditions of sale, Mr. & Mrs. Marshall are required to restore the property in keeping with its historical
  26|                    significance. The property's restoration will be monitored by the Town's Historical Commission. To date, the Marshall's
  27|                    have done an excellent job!
  28|                    In June, with the assistance of the Town's Treasurer-Collector, we were able to refund/ refinance our existing 2003 Bond
  29|                    Issue, which resulted in a net savings to the Town of $550,000 over the 11 remaining years of the issue. In the process, the
  30|                    Town's AA- Bond Rating was affirmed.
  31|                    Finally, the Town continues to push forward in the quest to redevelop the Summer Street Corridor. In 2012, Town Meeting
  32|                    authorized funding for an Alternatives Analysis. This was completed and the Board of Selectmen agreed upon a redesign
  33|                  : which includes one travel lane in each direction, with center turn lanes as specific intersections. The next step in the
  34|                      rocess includes bringing the project to 25% design, at which point, it can be included in the region's TIP (Transportation
  35|                      mprovement Plan), which will secure grant funding for construction. The Town owes a big thank you to its legislativ
  36|                     delegation- Senator Flanagan, Representative Benson and Representative DiNatale- who were all instrumental in securing
  37|                     he remaining funding for design in the State's Transportation Bond Bill. As a result of Bond Bill and TIP funding, about
  38|                    95% of this project's funding will come from sources other than the Lunenburg taxpayer.
  39|                    I would be remiss if I did not take this opportunity to thank all of the people in this town who give of their time and many
  40|                    talents to make Lunenburg a better place. Thank you all for your dedicated service.
  41|                    Respectfully submitted,
