   1|                                                                                    In Memoriam
   2|                                                    Jean Elizabeth Hobbs, a dedicated and energetic member of the Lunenburg community for
   3|                        more than 60 years. She graduated from Northbridge High School in 1944 and went on to study at Memorial Hospital
   4|                        School of Nursing in Worcester, graduating in 1947. That same year she married Frederick B. Hobbs and moved to Lunen-
   5|                        burg, where he would later become Police Chief.
   6|                        She was a contributor to the town history, Lunenburg: The Heritage of Turkey Hills, 1728-1978 and played key roles in
   7|                        planning the town's 250th and 275th anniversary celebrations.
   8|                        In the 1960s, she was a Girl Scout leader, Den Mother, and a founding member of the Lunenburg Music Aiders. She served
   9|                        on the Town's 1976 Bicentennial Committee, the Insurance Advisory Committee, Board of Directors for the Memorial Fund
  10|                        and for School Community Linked Services and was a member of the Lunenburg Historical Society. She was a fixture at
  11|                        Lunenburg's Annual Town Meeting, never reluctant to share her thoughts and opinions.
  12|                        Jean was dedicated to elder services. She was Chairwoman of the Lunenburg Council on Aging from 1990 to 2001. She
  13|                        served on boards for the Central MA Agency on Aging, the Central MA Association of Councils on Aging, and the Monta-
  14|                        chusett Opportunity Council and its Nutrition Advisory Board. She also served as a SHINE (Serving Health Insurance
  15|                        Needs of Elders) counselor at Eagle House. In the early years of Fitchburg Access TV, she hosted Senior Lifestyles. She
  16|                        was a familiar face at Eagle House where she contributed hundreds of volunteer hours and was a member of the book club.
  17|                        Jean was instrumental in securing a state grant to expand Eagle House Senior Center in 1998. In 1998 she received a DOVE
  18|                        Award from the Massachusetts Legislature in recognition of her dedicated volunteer service to elders.
  19|                        For many years Jean was the Lunenburg correspondent for the Fitchburg Sentinel and the Times Free Press. From 2001 to
  20|                        2008 she worked for The Lunenburg Ledger and wrote a popular column called "From the Eye of the Eagle" in which she
  21|                        voiced her views on town politics and happenings, colored with a bit of local history.
  22|                                                Lawrence W. Marshall
  23|                        Larry was born in Leominster and attended Leominster High School and Amherst College. He was active in the Boy Scouts
  24|                        of America since his youth and continued his compassion for service with his youngest son's Troop 1798 of Lunenburg;
  25|                        proudly assisting them to achieve the rank of Eagle Scout. He owned various businesses in the area, and was proud to be a
  26|                        well-respected real estate appraiser for over 40 years. He was first an extremely devoted husband and father and enjoyed
  27|                        spending time with his family and gardening. Looked on as a mentor, advisor and friend to many, Larry will be sorely
  28|                        missed and forever remembered and loved.

   8|                                                                            AREA- 26.63 SQUARE MILES
   9|                                                                              ROAD MILES- ABOUT 100
  10|                       Lunenburg was settled in 1718 and incorporated in 1728. It occupies a land area of
  11|                       approximately 26.4 square miles. Lunenburg is located in north central Massachusetts
  12|                       approximately 43 miles from Boston. It is bordered on the west by the Town of Ashby and the
  13|                        City of Fitchburg, on the south by the Town of Lancaster and the City of Leominster, on the east
  14|                       by the Town of Shirley, and on the north by the Town of Townsend.
  15|                       Local legislative decisions are made by an open town meeting consisting of all registered voters
  16|                       in the town. Subject to the legislative decisions made by town meeting, the affairs of the town
  17|                       are generally administered by a five member Board of Selectmen and a Town Manager. Local
  18|                       school affairs are administered by an elected seven member School Committee while local taxes
  19|                       are assessed by a three member Board of Assessors, all are appointed on an at-large basis for
  20|                       staggered three-year terms.
  21|                       Lunenburg is a semi-rural community located in northern Worcester County. Three water bodies
  22|                       account for somewhat of a clustered approach to the town's historical development. Lake
  23|                        Whalom and the former Whalom Amusement Park delineated the Whalom District from the
  24|                       remainder of Lunenburg. This district was developed as a housing base for many factory
  25|                        workers working in the mills and factories of Leominster and Fitchburg. In many ways the
  26|                       characteristics of the Whalom District more closely reflect characteristics of these cities than of
  27|                       the rest of Lunenburg. The Hickory Hills area of town, around Hickory Hills Lake, was
  28|                        developed as a private community comprised largely of vacation cottages. The third body of
  29|                       water is Shirley Reservoir, a public water body also developed with mainly vacation cottages
  30|                       that have now been modified to year-round residences. Commercial and industrial development
  31|                       is permitted in several areas of town, primarily along the two major arteries (Routes 2A and 13),
  32|                       and the town's peripheries, due largely to the close proximity of Leominster's and Fitchburg's
  33|                       sewer and water systems. Although many farms and orchards had slowly developed into single
  34|                       family housing, farming continues on in Lunenburg. At the Annual Town Meeting on May 4*h
  35|                       2013, the Town voted to establish an Agricultural Commission to represent, preserve, and
  36|                       enhance farming in the community and to administer the "Right to Farm" bylaw.

   3|                       The calendar year of 2016 was a year of progress for the Board of Selectmen and Town Manager's
   4|                       Office. During 2016 there are many accomplishments I would like to highlight:
   5|                             • In February the Building Reuse Committee presented the results of the Feasibility Study on the
   6|                                   Building Assessment and Space Needs of the town departments performed by Tappe Associates.
   7|                                   The preferred option of the Committee was to demolish parts of TC Passios School and renovate
   8|                                   portions due to the sufficient space and cost. The Board agreed to move forward on this
   9|                                   alternative with the possibility of renting/leasing a portion of that building. At the Annual Town
  10|                                   Meeting it was voted to transfer the custody and control of the T.C. Passios and Brooks House
  11|                                   from the School Committee to the Board of Selectmen. Further, at the November 2016 Special
  12|                                   Town Meeting the Building Reuse Committee submitted an article to convey for the purpose of
  13|                                  leasing or selling the Ritter Building, Town Hall, old Primary School, Brooks House, and T.C.
  14|                                   Passios. This article did not receive a majority vote but the use of these buildings continues to
  15|                                   be a topic of interest.
  16|                             • During the month of March the Board signed a three year Collective Bargaining Agreement with
  17|                                   the Professional Firefighter's Association and the Board discussed the role and responsibility of
  18|                                   the Selectmen, including being a liaison to various committees. Also in March DPW Director
  19|                                   Jack Rodriquenz presented an updated Pavement Management Plan for roadway improvements
  20|                                   of all town roads that included a proposal for a $4 Million dollar voter authorized debt exclusion
  21|                                   that passed at both a town meeting and ballot vote. The work will be done over a 4 year period
  22|                                   and would be financed over a period at least twice as long. The Town will attempt to put an
  23|                                   additional $50,000 each fiscal year in the Pavement Management Line Item Account, so there

  25|                             • The Summer Street redevelopment and roadway improvement project continued to move
  26|                                   forward with a slated beginning of 2017. The total project costs of $1OM will be funded through
  27|                                   the Regional Transportation Improvement Plan (TIP), which are state and federal transportation
  28|                                   funds.

  30|                                   further work on the Northeast Energy Direct Project, which the Town had declared opposition to
  31|                                  in February of 2015.

  34|                                   November Special Town Meetings for a revised total of $34,068,192.
