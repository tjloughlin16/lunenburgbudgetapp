   1|                                                                                 FINANCE COMMITTEE
   2|                                   (This is a reprint of the message printed in the May 7, 2016 Annual Town Meeting warrant)
   3|                           The Town Budget to be presented at Town Meeting has followed the same process as in recent years. The
   4|                           Town Manager has again provided Department Heads a "target budget' to work from, based on the financial
   5|                           forecast, current service levels and funding opportunities.
   6|                           The Finance Committee is recommending approval of the Town budget in the amount of $33,802,328.00,
   7|                           with an increase in revenues from all sources, totaling $1,758,413 over the 2016 Budget (after recap). A sig-
   8|                           nificant increase is anticipated in State Aid from Chapter 70 (education funding) and general government aid
   9|                           (lottery). While this is welcome news, unrestricted general government aid has yet to reach its previous
  10|                           2006 levels. Furthermore, since the financial forecast was first presented, health insurance costs have come
  11|                           in much greater than expected. For the most part, Town departments have continued to maintain a consistent
  12|                           portion of the budget, but pressures from a few drivers, namely Retirement, Health Insurance and Debt Ser-
  13|                           vice, are beginning to demand a larger share, creating constraints on growth.
  14|                           In breaking down the revenue to the Town, 67% comes from real estate property taxes and 33% comes from
  15|                           other sources such as State Aid, motor vehicle excise or meal taxes, betterments and other fees. The effects
  16|                           of such aid on particular aspects of the budget are difficult to parse out, except for Chapter 70 funding, which
  17|                           accounts for $1 of every $3 budgeted for the schools.
  18|                           Retirement and health insurance consume an ever increasing portion of the total budget. Town employees
  19|                           pay into 1 of 2 retirement systems. Licensed school district employees contribute to the Massachusetts
  20|                           Teacher Retirement System, which requires no additional town funding. Any additional funding of this Re-
  21|                           tirement System is done at the State level, and is reported in notes to the Town Financial Statements as an
  22|                           'On Behalf' payment. Non-licensed school personnel and Town-side employees contribute to the Worcester
  23|                           County Retirement System. The Town makes two payments to this system, an employer contribution for
  24|                           active employees and an assessment to fund the 'Unfunded Liability' of future payments to retirees. The
  25|                           Retirement Assessment has gone up 8.7%, or $84,143, over the 2016 Budget (after recap). Health insurance
  26|                           represents a 9.1% increase in spending and a 0.3% increase in budget share. The Town has been able to ne-
  27|                           gotiate changes to health plans over the past few years to help reduce cost, moving to a 75% Town, 25% em-
  28|                           ployee cost sharing. This year premiums are projected to increase by 12.8%, which affects both the Town
  29|                           budget, and the School Department budget.
  30|                           Even if the funding was available to do so, the Finance Committee and Town Administration have expressed
  31|                           reluctance to add new positions to departments until the issue of future liability to the Town for both retire-
  32|                           ment and other post-employment health benefits (OPEB) are adequately addressed. In the meantime, where
  33|                           it is possible to consolidate current positions to better meet the needs of the Town, such changes have been
  34|                           proposed. For example, consolidating a part time administrative position and a contracted consultant into
  35|                           one full-time position in the Conservation Commission office and adding hours to existing positions in Infor-
  36|                           mation Technology department and in the Assessors' office to meet increased workloads.
  37|                           Debt Service has 14% budget share, up a quarter percent with a 6.6%, or $298,911 increase, the main reason
  38|                           being the new Middle/High School, as well as existing debt from the Primary School, Public Safety Build-
  39|                           ing, and Library. We are still several years out from seeing these projects paid down. The Capital Planning
  40|                           Committee has recommended funding 12 items at $780,975; this budget funds 10 items at $619,475. There
  41|                           is no request or recommendation to borrow money to fund the remaining items.

   1|                       The Jane Doyle Community Playground dedication was held on Wednesday, November 23, 2016. This
   2|                       new playground is located by the Turkey Hill Elementary School Gymnasium. The playground was made
   3|                       possible by the generous donations and support from the Connor Lourens Memorial Fund; the Arnold
   4|                       Family and BEIM Foundation; Turkey Hill Family Lions Club; Lunenburg PTO; the Extended Day
   5|                       Program; and many Lunenburg parents and community members. In addition to the playground,
   6|                       basketball courts with areas for the very popular game "four-square" were constructed. We appreciate
   7|                       everyone who made these wonderful, age-appropriate play areas possible.
   8|                       The Lunenburg School Committee contracted with New England Education Consultants (NEEC) to
   9|                       facilitate a strategic visioning and planning initiative this year. The initial step in this work is a creating a
  10|                       Strategic Framework consisting of a Mission, Core Values, and Vision, all of which serve to set direction
  11|                       for the rest of the planning process. The Strategic Framework is developed members of a Steering
  12|                       Committee working with internal and external stakeholders to help assure the values of the community
  13|                       are well represented and that a connection is made to the public regarding underlying principles for the
  14|                       planning process. The new framework and plan will be adopted by the School Committee by the end of
  15|                       the school year with implementation to begin in the fall. With the opening of the new Lunenburg
  16|                       Middle - High School, the Lunenburg School Committee also committed to develop and implement an
  17|                       adult education program for the benefit of the Lunenburg community. An advisory group was appointed
  18|                       by the School Committee to research and make recommendations by the end of the current school year.

  20|                       Carolyn Tobin. During his four years at LHS Brendan excelled in honors and advanced placement
  21|                       coursework. He has been the President of the Class of 2017 since 9th grade. Brendan is a poised,
