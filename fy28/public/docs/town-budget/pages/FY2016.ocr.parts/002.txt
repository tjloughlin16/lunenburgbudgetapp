   1|                                                                        CONSERVATION COMMISSION
   2|                        The Town of Lunenburg Conservation Commission is a seven member commission appointed to 3 year terms by the
   3|                       Board of Selectmen, whose duties are codified under the Conservation Commission Act MGL c. 40 § 8C. The
   4|                        Commission enforces the Massachusetts Wetland Protection Act MGL c. 131 § 40 and Article XXII of the local
   5|                        bylaws for the protection of wetlands.
   6|                        The year 2016 was, as last year, one of a very busy and active agenda containing both public hearings and meetings
   7|                        for a wide range of projects from commercial development, subdivisions and small single family upkeep projects.
   8|                        The Conservation Commission worked to spell out goals of preparing to use the year 2017 to complete revising its
   9|                        regulations to provide consistency with the original bylaw and to find those sections that may be now out of date and
  10|                        need to be modified. This effort should conclude by the fall of 2017.
  11|                        The Commission held numerous inspections with the Commission Administrator on weekends prior to meetings to
  12|                        familiarize themselves with the upcoming proposals that would appear on their agenda. The Commission is also
  13|                        working with the town on familiarizing itself with the upcoming storm water mandates from the EPA and working
  14|                        towards compliance both by procedure and with any necessary codification.
  15|                       The Conservation Commission in cooperation with the Open Space Committee and the Lunenburg Snow Riders
  16|                       Club has closed out a grant that obtained the former Lane property, known as the Holman Conservation Area, as
  17|                       open space and has worked to place an adequate and ADA-complaint parking area and bridges that would blend in
  18|                        with the trail heads on Holman Street. The Conservation Commission also held public recreational walks through its
  19|                        open space parcels and plans to continue to do so.
  20|                       The Commission has worked closely with the Lake Shirley Improvement Corporation and Hickory Hills Lake
  21|                        Management Group on updates of their management plans and becoming actively involved in formulating the new
  22|                        updates designed to give a better picture of the overall health of the ecosystems and the various ways to ensure
  23|                        continued protection of these valuable resources.
  24|                        The Commission also obtained over $16,000 in grant money from DCR for the completion of forest stewardship
  25|                       planning on the Holman Conservation Area, both Town Forests, the open space on Robbs Hill and the Cowdrey Lot.
  26|                       The Commission retained Gary Gouldrup, a noted forester with many years of experience working in town for the
  27|                        commission and private landowners.
  28|                        This year also marked the hiring of former commission consulting agent Matthew S. Marro as the new Conservation
  29|                        Administrator. With his 18 years of dealing with commission projects and goals, Mr. Marro was ready to hit the
  30|                        ground running on day one. Mr. Marro has worked with the commission this year on office goals and procedures,
  31|                       increased inspections and office productivity as well as special projects. For example, he publishes a monthly article
  32|                       in the Lunenburg Ledger addressing the commission goal of informing the public on local environmental and
  33|                       recreational issues.
  34|                       The Commission thanks all the town agencies for a great year and look forward to many more!

   1|                                                                       DEPARTMENT OF PUBLIC WORKS
   2|                                   The Department of Public Works combines the resources of the Highway, Park, Cemetery, Sewer, and
   3|                        Facilities divisions, and Vehicle Maintenance services into one department organizing and scheduling personnel
   4|                        and equipment to best serve the residents of the community through the effective delivery of an extensive scope
   5|                        of services with a high degree of efficiency.
   6|                        DEPARTMENTAL OBJECTIVES
   7|                                    "Public Works is either working or on call twenty four hours a day three hundred and sixty five days a
   8|                        year to respond to any weather, infrastructure, roadway, or facility related emergency that may arise."
   9|                                    We strive to enhance the awareness of the value of Public Works provides to our Town, and ensure oth-
  10|                        ers are aware of our contribution. This is not for an expectation of praise, but rather to make sure efforts are not
  11|                        forgotten as they merge into the fabric of our everyday service.
  12|                                   Major events over the past years have highlighted the fact that Public Works has a seat at the table and is
  13|                        a major player in emergency management. Town Officials, Police, and Fire acknowledge that Public Works is
  14|                        critical in order to facilitate the required services of others. We must continue to build on this awareness.
  15|                                    This department must also reach out to the public we serve and educate them to the fact that the common
  16|                        services they receive do not just happen, but rather are the efforts of a dedicated staff of individuals, service
  17|                        companies, and vendors. Safe roadways, wastewater control, solid waste collection and recycling operations,
  18|                        tree removal, storm water management facilities and grounds maintenance, winter snow and ice removal, ceme-
  19|                        teries and parks are the expected services supporting everyone in the community.
  20|                                   We will work to engage the public in order to set reasonable expectations and gather support for annual
  21|                        budget requests, capital requests, and specific projects.
  22|                        PAVEMENT MANAGEMENT
  23|                                    The updated Pavement Management Plan, inclusive of an electronic database and a revised Town-wide
  24|                        pavement defect analysis. For a second time, a complete survey of every road was completed to establish a
  25|                        "pavement defect value." The amount of new paving does not keep up with the yearly deterioration factor, and
  26|                        as such the backlog is in excess of nine million dollars. The program remains underfunded however efforts of the
  27|                        Town Manager and the Board of Selectmen have resulted in a separate line within the DPW budget earmarked
  28|                        solely for paving and paving related services.
  29|                                    Road Projects typically extend over two construction seasons and overlap from one fiscal year to the
  30|                        next. During this year the paving schedule included: a large portion of Howard and West St., West Townsend
  31|                        Rd., Northfield and Flat Hill Rd. each funded through the Chapter 90 State Aid program with the exclusion of
  32|                        Flat Hill Rd. completed through the operational budget of the DPW by a dedicated Pavement Management Plan
  33|                        line. Due to a hellacious winter of 2014-2015, the Governor allocated a sum of money to each city/Town in the
  34|                        Commonwealth under the description of "Winter Rapid Recovery Program" to be used exclusively for paving.
  35|                        The completion of Howard St., a portion of Reservoir Rd., Arbor St., Page St., Kilburn St., and West St. all ben-
  36|                        efitted from the program with a curb to curb overlay along the most degraded portions.
  37|                                    Documentation provided through a "pothole" log (a daily record of road work specific to patching and
  38|                        filling of pot holes) provided information that one third of all the available work days during the season are dedi-
  39|                        cated to patching and other temporary repairs to similar roadway failures. As defects are identified we respond to
  40|                        every location in a timely manner. This has proven to be as less than effective method due to the fact that patch-
  41|                        ing is merely a temporary fix. New pot holes appear each spring particularly after a severe winter, and often staff
  42|                        addresses the same areas year after year and must continue to do so until full curb to curb rehabilitation is com-
  43|                        pleted, clearly a revenue driven operation.
  44|                                    The seasonal crack sealing program has been effective in some locations however this application is de-
  45|                        signed as a temporary solution as well with an effective life span of around five years. Many streets that have
  46|                        been "sealed" are well beyond that time frame. The mechanic of the process involves the installation of rubber-
  47|                        ized asphalt under high pressure into cracks or separations in the pavement. The Pavement Management Plan
  48|                        identifies specific areas where this method is the best option based upon the aforementioned pavement defect
  49|                        value This application does not take the place of curb to curb paving, only postpones it and is not applicable in
  50|                        areas of more serious defects.

  19|                        Winter Operations
  20|                                    Snow and ice present a major challenge to our Town. The safety of the general public depends on
  21|                         the ability of the DPW to maintain our roadways in a safe condition despite the severity or the duration of
  22|                         snow and ice events. To that end we have developed a winter operations procedure based upon a detailed
  23|                        plan that must be flexible due to the unpredictability of each storm. A consistently high level of service is
  24|                         expected and must be delivered to every part of Town in a timely manner.
  25|                                    Departmental preparedness begins with successful implementation of the capital plan. Financial
  26|                        support is necessary to provide effective fleet rotation insuring minimal interruption of services due to break-
  27|                         downs and repairs. In the fall, salt and sand are delivered to the Chase Rd. facility. Lunenburg is in a consor-
  28|                         tium with 14 other communities in an effort to procure the lowest possible price for road salt an absolute ne-
  29|                         cessity. Individually the Town also solicits bids for road sand. From late summer to early fall vehicle mainte-
  30|                        nance staff inspects and prepares the trucks, sanders and plows for winter operations. During the construc-
  31|                         tion season road crews address right-of-way issues, overhanging branches, and pavement defects in order to
  32|                        eliminate obstacles to plowing. Each division within the DPW is involved in winter operations. Typically
  33|                         DPW staff combines with 18-20 subcontractors to provide service.
  34|                                    Generally it is not the amount of snow but the duration of the event that determines the overall cost.
  35|                         For example, a fast moving snow storm of 6-8 inches will cost significantly less than a long duration freez-
  36|                        ing rain event even though the accumulation is much less. Historically, the department operates in a deficit
  37|                        due to a yearly allocation that is below the cost of an average winter. It is a dedicated group of individuals
