  14|                      PAVEMENT MANAGEMENT
  15|                      Consistent with the prioritization as determined by our Pavement Management Plan, road improvements were initiated on New
  16|                      West Townsend Rd., Lancaster Ave., and a limited section of Northfield Rd. to the Fitchburg line. The scope of the project
  17|                      included: a leveling course of asphalt followed by a top or finish course, sections of "berm" or curbing, shoulder work, drainage
  18|                      improvements and driveway aprons. As an issue of public safety, a leveling course was installed on Pleasant view Ave.
  19|                       Expanding the scope of our pavement preservation methods we added "crack sealing" to the overall program. This process
  20|                      involves the installation of rubberized asphalt under high pressure to cracks or separations in the pavement. This application
  21|                       can add approximately 5 years or more to the existing surface. The aforementioned Pavement Management Plan also identifies
  22|                      specific areas where this method is the best option.
  23|                       Funding continues to be the major detriment to improving the overall condition of our roadways. The primary source of funding
  24|                      is the Massachusetts Department of Transportation Chapter 90 program. The estimated value of our 90 miles of paved streets is,
  25|                      by industry standards, is calculated at 62 million dollars. The General Highway maintenance line within the operational budget
  26|                      remains stagnant. In order to initiate and sustain a road improvement program a substantial infusion of funds is necessary. A
  27|                      conservative estimate would be approximately 1.2 million dollars per year total or an additional $800,000.00 to existing
  28|                      allocations.
  29|                      Without additional funding our roads Town-wide will continue to deteriorate to a point where potentially the safety of our
  30|                      travelling public could be compromised.
