   1|                                                                           REPORT OF THE TOWN MANAGER
   2|                          Progress was made on many levels within the Town in 2011, from approving a Pavement Management
   3|                          Plan, to entering into a long-term contract for solid waste, to building renovations, and joining a regional
   4|                          emergency dispatch district. In short, many advances were made to ensure that the Town is addressing
   5|                          critical needs.
   6|                          We continue to work on addressing long overdue building issues. By the end of 2011, we had completed
   7|                         interior renovations at the Town Hall, to provide better, more efficient working space for our
   8|                         administrative offices. These renovations also included providing a more secure environment for our
   9|                         technology room and providing ADA compliant restrooms. By the end of 2011, we were also substantially
  10|                         complete with major renovations and expansion at our DPW Facility on Chase Road. Included in the
  11|                         project was a full renovation of the existing building, often referred to as "the Barn." The existing garage
  12|                                         retrofitted with a modern and compliant HVAC system and electrical and lighting systems,
  13|                         which provide for a greatly improved working environment. New office space was also created within the
  14|                         existing space, also provides conference meeting space for the Boards & Committees associated with the
  15|                         Department of Public Works. If you haven't yet had a chance to tour the facility, please stop by and see it.
  16|                         The facility is spectacular. The DPW Building Committee did a fantastic job on a very small budget.
  17|                         We continue to work on a plan to completely rehabilitate the Summer Street corridor. In 2011, the Town
  18|                         approved funding for an Alternatives Analysis to determine which roadway realignment would work best
  19|                         for both residential properties and the enhancement of commercial properties.                                                 The Board elected to
  20|                         pursue a realignment which provides for one lane of travel in each direction, with center turn lanes at
  21|                         certain intersections to enhance vehicular safety. The next step in moving the project forward is to secure
  22|                         funding for design. At the 2012 Annual Town Meeting, the Town will be asked to appropriate funding for
  23|                         Lunenburg's share of the 25% design fee. Achievement of this milestone will allow the project's
  24|                         construction to be programmed in the area Transportation Improvement Plan (TIP). Construction of the
  25|                         project will be fully grant funded through the TIP.
  26|                         This year, we also launched a new Town website, with the assistance of both staff and a citizens task force.
  27|                         The new website is more user friendly, both for the public and staff, and certainly more eye appealing.
  28|                         The Town will continue to make improvements to the website, by adding additional content and
  29|                        functionality.
  30|                         During 2011, the Town entered into a 10-year contract for solid waste services with Casella Waste
  31|                         Systems, Inc. of Auburn, MA. This contract was structured in a way that citizens should see almost no
  32|                        increase in the cost of trash bags through its life. We were also able to modify the pickup schedule to a 5-
  33|                        day per week schedule, which means all trash pickup will occur earlier in the day and the trucks will be
  34|                        off the road during peak commuting times.
  35|                         Finally, the single greatest achievement in 2011 was the formation of the Nashoba Regional Emergency
  36|                        Dispatch District, of which Lunenburg is a founding partner. Creation of this district has been years in
  37|                         coming and will provide the Town, along with its other founding partners- Devens, Harvard and
  38|                         Lancaster- superior, efficient and cost-effective Emergency 911 Dispatch, in a start of the art facility.
  39|                         Creation of the district pools together the resources of all the partnering communities as well as
  40|                        substantial grant funding from the State. It is estimated that each founding member will save
  41|                         approximately $100,000 in annual operating costs, while at the same time meeting a new mandate which
  42|                         requires Emergency Medical Dispatch. If Lunenburg had to meet this mandate on its own, the increase in
  43|                        local operations would have been in excess of $100,000. This effort would not have been possible without
  44|                        the support of the Fire Chief & Department, the Police Chief & Department, the Dispatcher's Union,
  45|                         Board of Selectmen and Finance Committee.
  46|                         I would be remiss if I did not take this opportunity to thank all of the people in this town who give of their
  47|                         time and many talents to make Lunenburg a better place. Thank you all for your dedicated service.

  23|                                                                    PLANNING BOARD OPEN SPACE SUB-COMMITTEE
  24|                        Fiscal Year 2011 marked a very important milestone for the Open Space Sub-Committee. The Commonwealth's Executive Office
  25|                        of Energy and Environmental Affairs Division of Conservation Services approved the revision to the Town's Open Space and
  26|                        Recreation Plan (OSRP). The OSRP gathered input and feedback from Town boards, organizations, and residents about the
  27|                        open space, conservation, and recreation needs. The Plan establishes an inventory of the resources our community already
  28|                        has, and creates an action plan for making improvements. This Plan was first drafted in 1978, and was subsequently revised and
  29|                        updated in 1985, 1990, and 1998.
  30|                        Besides being an important step in addressing our Town's open space and recreation needs, having a state-approved Open
  31|                        Space and Recreation Plan makes the Town eligible to apply for certain State grants; these grants can reimburse the Town for
  32|                        up to 62% of the cost of open space and recreation acquisitions and improvements. Historically, the Town made regular use of
  33|                        these State grants, and much of Town-owned open space in Town was purchased and protected using these funds. However,
  34|                        the Town has not taken advantage of these grants since 1999. The State approval of the newly-revised Plan is valid for seven
  35|                        years.
  36|                        Looking forward, the Open Space Sub-Committee transitioned from a group charged with developing a plan into the Town's first-
  37|                        ever advisory committee responsible for guiding the implementation of the Plan. As such, the Open Space Sub-Committee also
  38|                        began consideration of the first action items outlined in the OSRP. The Plan identifies numerous improvements to our existing
  39|                        open space and recreation infrastructure, policies, and bylaws, and the action items were designed intentionally to be cost-
  40|                        neutral to the Town. The sub-committee will be working with the community and other Town boards and committees in the
  41|                        coming year to begin implementation.

  14|                                                                            CENTRAL MASS MOSQUITO CONTROL
  15|                        The Central Massachusetts Mosquito Control Project (CMCP) currently provides its services to 39 cities and towns throughout Middlesex and Worcester Counties. The Project's headquarters is located at 111 Otis St., Northborough, MA. Project personnel are
  16|                        available to meet with any town board or resident to discuss the Project's procedures and activities. Our phone number is (508) 393-
  17|                                    CMMCP practices Integrated Mosquito Management (IMM), blending state of the art methods and techniques with
  18|                        effective mosquito control. IMM encourages the use of non-chemical means to accomplish the goal of mosquito reduction. expertise, experience, and scientific research to provide our member communities with modern, environmentally sound, cost
  19|                                    The Mosquito Awareness program which we offer to elementary schools in our district is very popular. Project staff meets
  20|                        with students and teachers to discuss mosquito biology, mosquito habitat, and control procedures. Much of the presentation is directed towards what the children and their families can do to prevent mosquitoes from. breeding around their homes. Slides,
  21|                        videos, handouts, and coloring books help to make this an interesting program. As part of our effort to reduce the need for pesticides, our first line of control is our ditch maintenance program. By
  22|                        cleaning clogged and         overgrown waterways, mosquito breeding can be reduced, drainage areas are returned to historical
  23|                        conditions, and water quality is improved. Source reduction, the elimination or reduction of a mosquito breeding source (i.e. water- holding containers), is practiced by alerting residents and business owners about potential mosquito producing sites they have
  24|                        created. They are informed about basic mosquito biology, and the need to eliminate these man-made breeding sources. A new

  10|                       The Firefighters and Emergency Medical Technicians of the Lunenburg Fire Department are dedicated to providing the highest
  11|                       quality, most cost effective fire and emergency medical services to the citizens of the town of Lunenburg.
  12|                       As I begin to write my final annual report as your Fire Chief, I want to remind everyone of the importance of smoke and carbon
  13|                       monoxide detectors. These detectors are required by law but more importantly, can save your life. These detectors will alarm
  14|                       very early in a smoke or carbon monoxide event and will provide you time to escape from the building. Please never go to bed
  15|                       knowing your detectors are not working. YOU MAY NOT WAKE UP! Should you need help to test or install your detectors, call
  16|                       us- we will be glad to help you.
  17|                       This year we saw Tri Town Landing and Emerald Place develop into multi-unit housing. Tri Town currently has two fully
  18|                       occupied 32-unit buildings, Emerald Place has five 24-unit buildings, five 2-unit and two 4-unit buildings. Both developments are
  19|                       fully occupied as of this report. Emerald Place continues to build two and four-unit buildings and Tri Town is projecting
  20|                       construction of one more 32-unit building. Although progressing less rapidly, we still see other building projects in the planning
  21|                       stages in town. These projects combined with our other inspections and plan reviews keep the fire prevention office very busy.
  22|                       Town meeting provided us the funding to replace our brush truck's body (Engine 2). The1988 truck itself had very low mileage
  23|                       and was excellent condition. The body on the truck needed replacement to meet our current needs. The truck was sent out at the
  24|                       end of January and should be back in service the first week in March. This work will make the truck much more functional as a
  25|                       forestry unit. The new body will also be able to be removed and reused when the chassis of the truck needs to be replaced. Town
  26|                        meeting also provided funding for upgrading the infrastructure of the police departments radio system and replacement of some
  27|                       portable radios.
  28|                        Once again this year our department saw an increase in calls for service. As our town grows we will continue to see these
  29|                       requests increase. Our department remains committed to a combination of full and part time call firefighters and EMTs. We are
  30|                        obligated to address weekend and overnight coverage in the station for the safety of our community. Currently we staff the
  31|                        station from 6 am to 6 pm weekdays then depend on our call and career staff to respond to emergency calls after hours and on
  32|                        weekends. Although we have extremely committed and dedicated firefighters and EMT's on call, it still takes time to respond to
  33|                       the station, pick up the equipment and then respond to your call for assistance. When we have firefighters in the station, we can
  34|                       put the first piece of equipment in service in a minute or less. When we respond from our homes, it takes an additional 3 to 6
  35|                       minutes to respond to your call for help. We can accomplish this with the hiring of more full time help or by using our call
  36|                       personnel on a per diem basis. In either case, we need to fund over night and weekend coverage for consistent response times
  37|                       24 x 7. If we were to staff the station over night and on weekends we would consider staffing with two firefighter/EMTs in order to
  38|                       enable the first medical or fire piece of equipment to respond immediately to your call, with the remainder of the call firefighters
