  15|                         This proposed law would expand the state's beverage container deposit law, also known as the Bottle Bill, to require deposits on
  16|                         containers for all non-alcoholic non-carbonated drinks in liquid form intended for human consumption, except beverages primarily
  17|                         derived from dairy products, infant formula, and FDA approved medicines. The proposed law would not cover containers made
  18|                          of paper-based biodegradable material and aseptic multi-material packages such as juice boxes or pouches. The proposed law
  19|                         would require the state Secretary of Energy and Environmental Affairs (EEA) to adjust the container deposit amount every five
  20|                         years to reflect (to the nearest whole cent) changes in the consumer price index, but the value could not be set below five cents.
  21|                         The proposed law would increase the minimum handling fee that beverage distributors must pay dealers for each properly
  22|                         returned empty beverage container, which was 21/4 cents as of September 2013, to 31/2 cents. It would also increase the
  23|                         minimum handling fee that bottlers must pay distributors and dealers for each properly returned reusable beverage container,
  24|                         which was 1 cent as of September 2013, to 31/2 cents. The Secretary of EEA would review the fee amounts every five years
  25|                          and make appropriate adjustments to reflect changes in the consumer price index as well as changes in the costs incurred by
  26|                         redemption centers. The proposed law defines a redemption center as any business whose primary purpose is the redemption
  27|                         of beverage containers and that is not ancillary to any other business. The proposed law would direct the Secretary of EEA to
  28|                          issue regulations allowing small dealers to seek exemptions from accepting empty deposit containers. The proposed law would
  29|                         define small dealers as any person or business, including the operator of a vending machine, who sells beverages in beverage
  30|                         containers to consumers, with a contiguous retail space of 3,000 square feet or less, excluding office and stock room space; and
  31|                          fewer than four locations under the same ownership in the Commonwealth. The proposed law would require that the regulations
  32|                         consider at least the health, safety, and convenience of the public, including the distribution of dealers and redemption centers by
  33|                         population or by distance or both. The proposed law would set up a state Clean Environment Fund to receive certain unclaimed
  34|                          container deposits. The Fund would be used, subject to appropriation by the State Legislature, to support programs such as the
  35|                         proper management of solld waste, water resource protection, parkland, urban forestry, air quality and climate protection. The
  36|                         proposed law would allow a dealer, distributor, redemption center or bottler to refuse to accept any beverage container that is not
  37|                          marked as being refundable in Massachusetts. The proposed law would take effect on April 22, 2015.

  20|              QUESTION 4: LAW PROPOSED BY INITIATIVE PETITION
  21|              This proposed law would entitle employees in Massachusetts to earn and use sick time according to certain conditions.
  22|              Employees who work for employers having eleven or more employees could earn and use up to 40 hours of paid sick time per
  23|              calendar year, while employees working for smaller employers could earn and use up to 40 hours of unpaid sick time per
  24|              calendar year. An employee could use earned sick time if required to miss work in order (1) to care for a physical or mental
  25|              illness, injury or medical condition affecting the employee or the employee's child, spouse, parent, or parent of a spouse; (2) to
  26|              attend routine medical appointments of the employee or the employee's child, spouse, parent, or parent of a spouse; or (3) to
  27|              address the effects of domestic violence on the employee or the employee's dependent child. Employees would earn one hour of
  28|              sick time for every 30 hours worked, and would begin accruing those hours on the date of hire or on July 1, 2015, whichever is
  29|             later. Employees could begin to use earned sick time on the 90th day after hire. The proposed law would cover both private and
  30|              public employers, except that employees of a particular city or town would be covered only if, as required by the state
  31|              constitution, the proposed law were made applicable by local or state legislative vote or by appropriation of sufficient funds to pay
  32|             for the benefit. Earned paid sick time would be compensated at the same hourly rate paid to the employee when the sick time is
  33|              used. Employees could carry over up to 40 hours of unused sick time to the next calendar year, but could not use more than 40
  34|              hours in a calendar year. Employers would not have to pay employees for unused sick time at the end of their employment. If an
  35|              employee missed work for a reason eligible for earned sick time, but agreed with the employer to work the same number of
  36|             hours or shifts in the same or next pay period, the employee would not have to use earned sick time for the missed time, and the
  37|              employer would not have to pay for that missed time. Employers would be prohibited from requiring such an employee to work
  38|              additional hours to make up for missed time, or to find a replacement employee. Employers could require certification of the need
  39|              for sick time if an employee used sick time for more than 24 consecutively scheduled work hours. Employers could not delay the
  40|              taking of or payment for earned sick time because they have not received the certification. Employees would have to make a
  41|              good faith effort to notify the employer in advance if the need for earned sick time is foreseeable. Employers would be prohibited
  42|              from interfering with or retaliating based on an employee's exercise of earned sick time rights, and from retaliating based on an
  43|              employee's support of another employee's exercise of such rights. The proposed law would not override employers' obligations
  44|              under any contract or benefit plan with more generous provisions than those in the proposed law. Employers that have their own
  45|              policies providing as much paid time off, usable for the same purposes and under the same conditions, as the proposed law
  46|              would not be required to provide additional paid sick time. The Attorney General would enforce the proposed law, using the same
  47|              enforcement procedures applicable to other state wage laws, and employees could file suits in court to enforce their earned sick
  48|              time rights. The Attorney General would have to prepare a multilingual notice regarding the right to earned sick time, and
  49|              employers would be required to post the notice in a conspicuous location and to provide a copy to employees. The state
