   2|                                                                  Jane Doyle was born in Fitchburg on April 17, 1941. She was a
   3|                                                                  graduate of the Mary A. Burnham School for Girls and a 1963
   4|                                                                  graduate of Skidmore College with a BSN in Nursing. Jane
   5|                                                                  earned an MS in Nursing from Boston University and an MBA
   6|                                                                  in Health Care Administration from Western New England
   7|                                                                  College in 1984. Jane was a longtime resident of Lunenburg.
   8|                                                                  Jane was a member of the National Teachers Association,
   9|                                                                  National Educational Association, Sigma Theta Tau-Nursing
  10|                          Honor Society and Health Alliance Guild, Lunenburg Woman's Club, Lunenburg
  11|                          Friendly Seniors and the Lunenburg Turkey Hill Family Lions Club. She was the
  12|                          Chairperson of the Lunenburg Community Council, member of the Lunenburg Council
  13|                          on Aging, Past-President of the Lunenburg-Townsend Rotary Club, and recipient of the
  14|                          Paul Harris Fellow Award, a Rotary International Award for Service. In 2007, Jane
  15|                          discovered that there was no local food bank in town and coordinated meetings with
  16|                         fellow Lions' Club members and Fire Chief Glenny to create a space for residents to drop
  17|                          off and pick up food. Her vision lives on today.
  18|                                                          Charles P. Deming, Sr. was a lifelong resident of Lunenburg. Charlie
  19|                         graduated with the Lunenburg High Class of 1952. He worked as a firefighter for the
  20|                         Lunenburg Fire Department for 49 years and was an EMT for the first Lunenburg
  21|                          Rescue Squad. Mr. Deming was employed by General Electric Company for 35 years
  22|                          and was a member of the GE Credit Union's Board of Directors. He served as a
  23|                          Lunenburg Cemetery Commissioner. Charlie was treasurer of the 275th Anniversary
  24|                          Committee and the LHS Athletic Hall of Fame Committee. In 2012, he proudly received
  25|                          the SHS Alumnus of the Year Award. Charlie coached Lunenburg's Little League
  26|                          baseball teams, Biddy League basketball teams and was an EMT for Pop Warner

  28|                          Golf League and Lunenburg Men's Softball League. Charlie was known as a "human
  29|                          GPS"; he could give you directions to anywhere in Massachusetts and knew Lunenburg
  30|                          like the back of his hand; its highways, back roads, forests, fields and waterways. He
  31|                          was a talented woodworker and craftsman; family and friends treasure beautiful
  32|                          reminders of his creativity and generosity.

   1|                                                                                          LUNENBURG
   2|                          Lunenburg was settled in 1718 and incorporated in 1728. It occupies a land area of
   3|                          approximately 26.4 square miles. Lunenburg is located in north central Massachusetts
   4|                          approximately 43 miles from Boston. It is bordered on the west by the Town of Ashby and the
   5|                          City of Fitchburg, on the south by the Town of Lancaster and the City of Leominster, on the east
   6|                          by the Town of Shirley, and on the north by the Town of Townsend
   7|                          Local legislative decisions are made by an open town meeting consisting of all registered voters
   8|                          in the town. Subject to the legislative decisions made by town meeting, the affairs of the town
   9|                          are generally administered by a five member Board of Selectmen and a Town Manager. Local
  10|                          school affairs are administered by an elected seven member school committee while local taxes
  11|                          are assessed by a three member Board of Assessors, all appointed on an at-large basis for
  12|                          staggered three-year terms.
  13|                          Lunenburg is a semi-rural community located in northern Worcester County. Three water bodies
  14|                          account for somewhat of a clustered approach to the town's historical development. Lake
  15|                          Whalom and the former Whalom Amusement Park delineated the Whalom District from the
  16|                          remainder of Lunenburg. This district was developed as a housing base for many factory
  17|                          workers working in the mills and factories of Leominster and Fitchburg. In many ways the
  18|                          characteristics of the Whalom District more closely reflect characteristics of these cities than of
  19|                          the rest of Lunenburg. The Hickory Hills area of town, around Hickory Hills Lake, was
  20|                          developed as a private community comprised largely of vacation cottages. The third body of
  21|                          water is Shirley Reservoir, a public water body also developed with mainly vacation cottages
  22|                          that have now been modified to year-round residences. The remainder of Lunenburg which
  23|                          originally consisted largely of farms and orchards has slowly developed into single family
  24|                          housing. Commercial and industrial development is permitted in several areas of town, primarily
  25|                          along the two major arteries (Routes 2A and 13), and the town's peripheries, due largely to the
  26|                          close proximity of Leominster's and Fitchburg's sewer and water systems.

   1|                                                                      REPORT OF THE TOWN MANAGER
   2|                       The Town continued to make important progress in 2014 in many areas, including: School Building; Open Space
   3|                        Acquisition; Traffic Safety Improvements; Increased Public Transportation; and Organizational Improvements. In 2014, the
   4|                       School Building Committee and School Committee brought forth, to Town Meeting, a comprehensive plan for a new Middle
   5|                       School/ High School Building. This plan was the culmination of months' worth of review, study and public input. The plan
   6|                       and project was approved at Town Meeting and by an overwhelming 2 to 1 majority at the ballot. The building is well under
   7|                       construction and set to open in September 2016. Fifty-eight (58%) of the project's eligible costs will be paid through a grant
   8|                       from the MA School Building Authority.
   9|                       In 2014, both the Conservation Commission and the Open Space Committee prepared and adopted a set of review criteria for
  10|                       evaluating available land for public ownership. Each presented its set of criteria to the Board of Selectmen for acceptance.
  11|                       The criteria established proved helpful in evaluating two separate Chapter Land Right of First Refusal options during the year
  12|                       and also in supporting the Town's application for a Local Acquisitions for Natural Diversity (LAND) grant for the purchase
  13|                       of the William H. & Saundra B. Lane Property at Lunenburg Town Forest. This property, about 167 acres in total, provides
  14|                       critical access to the existing Town Forest at a very affordable price. Purchase of the property, which occurred in January
  15|                       2015, will serve to protect the headwaters of the Mulpus Brook for the long term.
  16|                       The Town began a comprehensive review of its Traffic Rules and Orders in the fall of 2014. As a result of this review, it was
  17|                       determined that a number of existing traffic signs had not been properly authorized, requiring corrective action. Additionally,
  18|                       the Board reviewed several different intersections which were called out as being particularly unsafe. After review, the
  19|                        Board determined to authorize stop signs at the following locations to improve pedestrian and vehicular safety.
  20|                             • Flat Hill Road and Sunset Lane
  21|                             • Highland Street; Main Street and Oak Avenue
  22|                             • Mulpus Road and Townsend Harbor Road
  23|                       The Town also continued to work on the redevelopment of Summer Street, a project slated to begin in 2016. In 2014, the
  24|                       Town committed $230,312 from Chapter 90 Funding to fund the Town's portion of the 75% design. The project is expected
  25|                       to cost about $7M, all of which will be funded though the Regional Transportation Improvement Plan (TIP), which is a grant
  26|                       of State & Federal funds.
  27|                       One of the most exciting projects is 2014 was the implementation of enhanced public transportation within Lunenburg. The
  28|                       Town pays both an annual assessment to the MART and to the MBTA. The assessment to MART pays for local/ regional
  29|                       transportation. The assessment to the MBTA pays for access to the commuter rail system in Fitchburg and Leominster. The
  30|                       Town learned through MART that there is a provision in State Law that would allow the Town to use its MBTA Assessment
  31|                       to purchase additional local transportation, at no additional cost to the taxpayer. Accordingly, the Town undertook a review
  32|                       of residents' transportation needs and began working with MART to provide additional local transportation. The additional
  33|                        services are slated to begin in 2015.
  34|                       One final noteworthy item is the creation of the Land Use Director position which was approved by the 2014 Annual Town
  35|                       Meeting. The Director is responsible for coordinating the activities of all of the Boards and Departments involved in
  36|                       regulating land use and land development in town. The Director will fill the existing role of Planning Director/ Town
  37|                       Planner, a new role of Economic Development Director, and also serve as a coordinator of the activities of all Boards and
  38|                        Departments within the Land Use group. Additionally, the Director will coordinate with both the Sewer Commission and
  39|                       Department of Public Works on utility, storm water management, drainage, road extension or development and/ or other
  40|                       related land development matters. The Town hired its first Land Use Director, Adam Burney, in September 2014.
  41|                       I would be remiss if I did not take this opportunity to thank all of the people in this town who give of their time and many
  42|                       talents to make Lunenburg a better place. Thank you all for your dedicated service.
