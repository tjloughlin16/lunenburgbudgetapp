   2|                                     The Department of Public Works combines the resources of the Highway, Park,
   3|                         Cemetery, Sewer, and Facilities divisions, and Vehicle Maintenance services into one
   4|                         department organizing and scheduling personnel and equipment to best serve the residents of the
   5|                         community through the effective delivery of an extensive scope of services with a high degree of
   6|                         efficiency.
   7|                         DEPARTMENTAL OBJECTIVES
   8|                                     "Public Works is either working or on call twenty four hours a day, three hundred and
   9|                         sixty five days a year to respond to any weather, infrastructure, roadway, or facility related
  10|                         emergency that may arise."
  11|                                     We strive to enhance the awareness of the value of Public Works provides to our Town,
  12|                         and ensure others are aware of our contribution. This is not for an expectation of praise, but
  13|                         rather to make sure efforts are not forgotten as they merge into the fabric of our everyday
  14|                         service.
  15|                                     Major events over the past years have highlighted the fact that Public Works has a seat at
  16|                         the table and is a major player in emergency management. Town Officials, Police, and Fire
  17|                         acknowledge that Public Works is critical in order to facilitate the required services of others.
  18|                         We must continue to build on this awareness.
  19|                                     This department must also reach out to the public we serve and educate them to the fact
  20|                         that the common services they receive do not just happen, but rather are the efforts of a dedicated
  21|                         staff of individuals, service companies, and vendors. Safe roadways, wastewater control, solid
  22|                         waste collection and recycling operations, tree removal, storm water management facilities and
  23|                         grounds maintenance, winter snow and ice removal, cemeteries and parks are the expected
  24|                         services supporting everyone in the community.
  25|                                     We will work to engage the public in order to set reasonable expectations and gather
  26|                         support for annual budget requests, capital requests, and specific projects.
  27|                         PAVEMENT MANAGEMENT
  28|                                     The updated Pavement Management Plan, inclusive of an electronic database and a
  29|                         revised Town-wide pavement defect analysis, identifies the same backlog of work as the original
  30|                         document established in 2000. The backlog is now estimated at close to ten million dollars. A
  31|                         complete survey of every road is underway, but in general, since the level of funding has not
  32|                         significantly increased, pavement conditions continue to deteriorate more steadily than the repair
  33|                         and rehabilitation of Town ways.
  34|                                     Road projects typically extend over two construction seasons and overlap from one fiscal
  35|                          year to the next.
  36|                                     Improvements: milling and overlay on a portion of Goodrich St. from Reservoir Rd., top
  37|                         course on Northfield Rd from Gilchrest St. to the golf course including asphalt berms to control
  38|                         storm water, and an overlay on Fish St. to Leominster Rd. The scope of work included: a
  39|                         leveling course of asphalt installed one season and left over the winter, followed by a top or
  40|                         finish course the next season, sections of "berm" or curbing, shoulder work, minor drainage
  41|                         improvements and driveway aprons.

  43|                         days during the season are dedicated to patching and other temporary repairs to similar

   1|                                                                                 SEWER COMMISSION
   2|                         The five member Sewer Commission was originally established at the Special Town Meeting on May 6, 2006.
   3|                         At the May 2013 Town Election, Carl Luck and Mike Nault were re-elected for three year terms and the officers
   4|                         voted in were: Carl Luck -continuing as Chairman, Jay Simeone- Vice-Chairman and Dave MacDonald - Clerk.
   5|                          The other members are Butch Bilotta and Mike Nault. The Sewer Commission bears the responsibility of
   6|                         overseeing the Town's sewer system, planning for the future, and developing and implementing rules,
   7|                         regulations and policies. The Department of Public Works manages and maintains the operational side of the
   8|                         system. Under the Town Charter responsibility for the day-to day operation, care and maintenance of the
   9|                         sanitary sewers are under the supervision of the Town Manager. The Town of Lunenburg does not operate a
  10|                         sewer treatment plant. Approximately 76% of Lunenburg sewer is discharged to the City of Leominster, and the
  11|                         other 24% is discharged to the City of Fitchburg.
  12|                         There are currently 730 properties connected to the Lunenburg system and another 390 that have access to town
  13|                         sewer but are not yet connected. During Fiscal Year 2014, forty new accounts connected to town sewer. The
  14|                         Commission billed out $656,440 in quarterly sewer bills, and had $53,730 of prior year delinquencies added to
  15|                         the owners' property tax bills for non-payment. Sixteen properties with access to sewer were assessed privilege
  16|                         fees that had not originally been assessed betterments. Privilege fees help to pay down the debt owed by the
  17|                         Town on the original sewer project.
  18|                         The Sewer Commission was very proud to present a balanced budget during the FY15 budget process during
  19|                          FY14. This means that for the second time it was not necessary to transfer any monies from Retained Earnings
  20|                         to operate the Sewer Division of the DPW, which had been the case every year prior to FY14. The goal of the
  21|                         Sewer Commission is to maintain a balanced budget going forward and to build up the Retained Earnings so that
  22|                         it may used for major capital projects to maintain and improve the operating efficiency of the system.
  23|                         In the spring of 2013, the Sewer Commission solicited proposals to convert from in-house service of the 10
  24|                         pumping stations and sewer infrastructure to having an outside contractor perform the service and maintenance.
  25|                          The Sewer Commission selected Small Water Systems Services of Littleton for a one-year contract with the
  26|                         potential for up to three years starting in August 2013. Corey Dowd who had served as the Sewer Technician
  27|                         moved over to the Highway Division of the DPW. As the fiscal year 2014 drew to a close, the Commission has
  28|                         been very satisfied with the personnel, the work and the arrangement with Small Water Systems Services and has
  29|                         already put into motion to add to their responsibilities the maintenance of the 10 generators housed in our
  30|                         pumping stations with the next contract year. The Commission also worked hard over the course of FY14 with
  31|                         Small Water to explore the costs and benefits of installing SCADA systems in the pumping stations which
  32|                          allows for remote checking of alarms, hopefully reducing the number of call outs and expense.
  33|                         The Sewer Commission had several large projects throughout FY14. In September 2013, the revision and
  34|                         implementation of the Sewer Use Regulations became effective. With this document, all bylaws, policies, rules
  35|                         and regulations were compiled into one document. One of the new policies developed and utilized this year was
  36|                         the Illegal Connection Policy for use when a property connects to the town sewer without obtaining the proper
  37|                         permits. A minimum fee of $5000 will be levied to the property owner along with back usage charges if the
  38|                         Commission learns of an illegal connection. The Commission offered "amnesty" to anyone who knowingly was
  39|                         connected and was not paying a sewer bill from the inception of the policy through October 2013. In
  40|                         Lunenburg, the water and sewer is billed separately, so a resident would know that they are illegally connected
  41|                         if they do not have a septic system and do not receive a quarterly sewer bill. The Commission would encourage
  42|                         any resident in that situation come forward to rectify it versus having the illegal connection discovered and be
  43|                          subject to substantial fines. The Commission also worked to enforce the Grease Trap Regulation for all food
  44|                          service establishments that have or should have a grease trap or grease interceptor. Annual permit fees were
  45|                         assessed in October 2013 based on the establishment's level of compliance and the reporting requirements
  46|                         clearly communicated. Lunenburg has 19 grease trap establishments. This regulation will continue to be
