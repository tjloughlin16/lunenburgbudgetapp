   4|                            The Property Tax Work-Off Committee is a five member committee appointed by the Board of Selectmen. The
   5|                            Lunenburg Property Tax Work-Off Program offers income eligible senior citizens age 60+ an opportunity to
   6|                           receive up to $1,000 off their property tax bill in the calendar year rather than the fiscal year. Participants
   7|                            worked in various town departments to earn the tax abatement doing general office duties, custodial, library
   8|                            shelving, and kitchen assistance. For each hour worked a person received $10 and worked up to 100 hours be-
   9|                            tween January 1, 2017 and November 1, 2017 to receive the abatement. Any hours worked beyond that were
  10|                            considered volunteer hours.
  11|                            There were nine Lunenburg residents who participated between January 1, 2017 to November 1, 2017. These
  12|                            individuals, as a whole, accumulated 862 hours towards their abatements.
  13|                           Eligibility Requirements:
  14|                                 Applicants must be 60 years of age or older
  15|                                 Own and occupy a home in Lunenburg as their primary residence for at least one year
  16|                                 Receive a property tax bill in their name or their spouse's name who is also 60 years of age or older
  17|                                 Meet the income guidelines of :
  18|                                       One person household - $40,000 / Two person household - $60,000
  19|                                 All new applicants are required to have a CORI background check and must re-apply and be accepted
  20|                                       annually for participation in the program.
  21|                           Earnings are exempt from state taxes only. Earnings are subject to federal and FICA tax withholdings and are
  22|                           also subject to OBRA if participants are not receiving a pension from either Worcester Regional Retirement or

   2|                       The five member Sewer Commission was established at the May 6, 2006 Special Town Meeting. At the first
   3|                       the new fiscal year, Carl Luck was elected to remain as Chairman, John Reynolds as Vice-Chair and Sue Bunish
   4|                       as Clerk. Both Carl Luck and John Reynolds were elected to 3 year terms at the May 2016 election. There
   5|                       remained two open positions on the Commission after a failure to elect at the May election and Joseph Anderson
   6|                       stepped forward to fill one vacancy after appointment by the Selectmen and Commission on July 5, 2016. On
   7|                       July 19, 2016, Joan Pease was appointed by the Selectmen and Commission to fill the other vacancy. When Sue
   8|                       Bunish submitted her letter of resignation in July 2016, Richard St. Cyr was appointed to fill that vacancy on
   9|                       October 11, 2016. He remained until February 26, 2017, when he resigned to move out of town. With Sue's
  10|                       departure, Joe Anderson was elected to serve as Clerk for the Commission on July 26, 2016. At the May 2017
  11|                       election in, Troy Daniels was elected to the one-year term opened by the departure of Richard St. Cyr by write-
  12|                       in vote.
  13|                       The Commission bears the responsibility of overseeing the Sewer Enterprise Fund, planning for the future and
  14|                       developing and implementing rules, regulations and policies. The Department of Public Works manages and
  15|                       maintains the operational side of the system. Under the Town Charter, responsibility for the day-to day
  16|                       operation and care and maintenance of the sanitary sewers are under the supervision of the Town Manager.
  17|                       Lunenburg does not operate a sewer treatment plant. Approximately 83% of Lunenburg sewer is discharged to
  18|                       Leominster and the other 17% is discharged to Fitchburg. There are currently 862 properties connected to the
  19|                       Lunenburg system and another 381 that have access to Town sewer but are not yet connected. During FY 17, 44
  20|                       new accounts connected to town sewer. The Commission billed out $763,329 in quarterly sewer bills and had
  21|                       $63,087 of prior year delinquencies added to the owners' property tax bills for non-payment. Eighty-six
  22|                       properties gained access to sewer with the completion of the SRF project in the Pratt St., Pleasantview Ave. and
  23|                       Lakeview Ave. areas of town and were assessed a betterment of $13,997.47 to pay down the debt owed by the
  24|                       Town for the SRF project. One additional property was assessed a privilege fee in FY17 for increased usage as
  25|                       a multi-family residence. Privilege fees help to pay down the debt owed by the Town on the original sewer
  26|                       project.
  27|                       The Commission continues contracting with Small Water Systems Services of Littleton (SWSS) for the
  28|                       Operations and Management of the ten pumping stations and sewer infrastructure. The Commission has been
  29|                       very satisfied with the personnel, the work and the arrangement with SWSS, renewing the contract for a second
  30|                       three-year renewable contract when SWSS was awarded the bid in July 2016. The new contract, effective
  31|                       7/1/16, was expanded to include the generators in each pump station and quarterly meter readings for manual
  32|                       and irrigation accounts.
  33|                       In July 2016, with the new Grease Trap regulation in place, the Commission commenced the annual permit
  34|                       program with the new fee structure and requirements for annual permits, compliance and reporting. Lunenburg
  35|                       has eighteen food service establishments with grease traps and interceptors. This regulation continues to be
  36|                       strongly enforced as Fats, Oils and Grease (FOG) can negatively affect the smooth operation and maintenance
  37|                       costs of the sewer system. While the Grease Trap Regulation focuses on commercial and public disposal,
  38|                       individual home owners can also contribute a significant amount of grease into the system which increases
  39|                       everyone's costs. Whether on sewer or septic, grease should not be put down the drain.

  24|                              grow, the demand for emergency services grows along with it. I must continue to state the need for 24 hour staffing in the
  25|                              Station. Funding to maintain a minimum of 2 Firefighter/EMT's or Paramedics in the Station 24 hours a day, 7 days a week is
  26|                              critical to maintain the high level of service our Townspeople expect. This would allow either the first ambulance or first piece of
  27|                              fire apparatus to respond immediately. Not only would this reduce our response times to critical emergencies, it would also
  28|                              reduce the "wear and tear" on our Call members. This coverage would in no way reduce the need for our On Call personnel; it
  29|                              would only supplement their response and reduce response times.
  30|                                       Staffing was slightly increased on July 1, 2017 with the addition of 1 Full Time member. Lt. Christos Lekaditis joined
  31|                              the ranks of our full time staff and functions as our Assistant Fire Prevention and Training Officer. Christos was a Call Firefighter
  32|                              with the Department for 10 years and also served as a Full Time Firefighter with the Devens Fire Department. The addition of
  33|                              this full time position allowed us to slightly increase our coverage during the day time, and to begin covering two nights per week

  35|                              member weekdays when most of our Call Personnel are out of town.
