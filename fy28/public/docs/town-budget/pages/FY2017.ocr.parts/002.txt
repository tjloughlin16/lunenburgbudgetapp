   1|                                                                    DEPARTMENT OF PUBLIC WORKS
   2|                                    The Department of Public Works combines the resources of the Highway, Park, Cemetery, Sewer, and
   3|                         Facilities divisions, and Vehicle Maintenance services into one department organizing and scheduling personnel
   4|                         and equipment to best serve the residents of the community through the effective delivery of an extensive scope
   5|                         of services with a high degree of efficiency.
   6|                         DEPARTMENTAL OBJECTIVES
   7|                                    "Public Works is either working or on call twenty-four hours a day three hundred and sixty-five days a
   8|                         year to respond to any weather, infrastructure, roadway, or facility related emergency that may arise."
   9|                                     We strive to enhance the awareness of the value of Public Works provides to our Town, and ensure oth-
  10|                         ers are aware of our contribution. This is not for an expectation of praise, but rather to make sure efforts are not
  11|                         forgotten as they merge into the fabric of our everyday expectation of service.
  12|                                    Major events, particularly in the form of devastating storms, over the past years have highlighted the fact
  13|                         that Public Works has a seat at the table and is a major player in emergency management. Town Officials, Police,
  14|                         and Fire acknowledge that Public Works is critical in order to facilitate the required services of others. We must
  15|                         continue to build on this awareness.
  16|                                    This department must also reach out to the public we serve and educate them to the fact that the common
  17|                         services they receive do not just happen, but rather are the efforts of a dedicated staff of individuals, service com-
  18|                         panies, and vendors. Safe roadways, wastewater control, solid waste collection and recycling operations, tree re-
  19|                         moval, storm water management facilities and grounds maintenance, winter snow and ice removal, cemeteries
  20|                         and parks are the expected services supporting everyone in the community.
  21|                                     We will continue to engage the public in order to set reasonable expectations and gather support for an-
  22|                         nual budget requests, capital requests, and specific projects.
  23|                         PAVEMENT MANAGEMENT
  24|                                    Historically the amount of funding dedicated to roadway paving and paving related services has been
  25|                         grossly inadequate resulting in a negative pavement condition index. It became a high priority goal of the BOS as
  26|                         Road Commissioners, to achieve a pavement condition of 72%-75% Town-wide. Although State funded Chapter
  27|                         90 funds remained stagnant, the dedicated Pavement Management line in the operational budget grew substantial-
  28|                        ly.
  29|                                     Results of the newly revised Comprehensive Pavement Management Plan displayed clearly the inequity
  30|                         of the distribution of paving funds toward higher use main and collector roads leaving local neighborhoods unat-
  31|                        tended. Using the PMP as evidence, residents went to the polls to vote favorably on a four-million-dollar debt
  32|                         exclusion targeting exclusively the local roads. Expending approximately a million dollars in the initial year re-
  33|                         sulted in a major enhancement of road conditions.
  34|                                    The debt exclusion money combined with state and operational funds brought the total paving value to
  35|                        nearly two million dollars in the first construction season as we attacked the nine-million-dollar backlog of re-
  36|                         pairs.
  37|                                     Our pothole log has and is expected to lessen exponentially as new paving increases, a clear benefit of
  38|                         the dollars expended. There is much work yet to do since we are at the very beginning of the program but expec-
  39|                        tations are high.
  40|                                     The seasonal crack sealing program has been effective in some locations where applicable however this
  41|                         protocol is designed as a temporary solution with an effective life span of around five years. The mechanics of
  42|                        the process involves the installation of rubberized asphalt under high pressure into cracks or separations in the
  43|                        pavement. The Pavement Management Plan identifies specific areas where this method is the best option. This
  44|                        application does not take the place of curb to curb paving, only postpones it and is not applicable in areas of more
  45|                         serious defects.

   3|                          Winter Operations
   4|                                      Snow and ice present a major challenge to our Town. The safety of the general public depends on the
   5|                          ability of the DPW to maintain our roadways in a safe condition despite the severity or the duration of snow and
   6|                          ice events. To that end we have developed a winter operations procedure based upon a detailed plan that must
   7|                          be flexible due to the unpredictability of each storm. A consistently high level of service is expected and must
   8|                          be delivered to every part of Town in a timely manner.
   9|                                      Departmental preparedness begins with successful implementation of the capital plan. Financial sup-
  10|                          port is necessary to provide effective fleet rotation insuring minimal interruption of services due to breakdowns
  11|                          and repairs. In the fall, salt and sand are delivered to the Chase Rd. facility. Lunenburg is in a consortium with
  12|                          14 other communities in an effort to procure the lowest possible price for road salt, an absolute necessity. From
  13|                          late summer to early fall vehicle maintenance staff inspects and prepares the trucks, spreaders and plows for
  14|                          winter operations. During the construction season road crews address right-of-way issues, overhanging branch-
  15|                          es, and pavement defects in order to eliminate obstacles to snow removal. Each division within the DPW is in-
  16|                          volved in winter operations. Typically, DPW staff combines with 18-20 subcontractors to provide service.
  17|                                      Generally, it is not the amount of snow but the duration of the event that determines the overall cost.
  18|                           For example, a fast moving snow storm of 6-8 inches will cost significantly less than a long duration freezing
  19|                          rain event even though the accumulation is much less. Historically, the department operates in a deficit due to a
  20|                          yearly allocation that is below the cost of an average winter.
