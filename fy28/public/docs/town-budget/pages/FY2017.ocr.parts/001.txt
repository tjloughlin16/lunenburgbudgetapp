   1|                                                                              In Memoríam
   2|                                            Lillian S. Griffiths
   3|                       Lillian passed away on September 30th at the age of 99. Lillian grew up on the family farm in
   4|                       Lunenburg with four brothers and five sisters. After distinguishing herself as a scholar-athlete at
   5|                       Lunenburg High and graduating from Fitchburg State in 1941, she began her 33-year career in
   6|                       education as a junior high teacher in Lunenburg. She went on to teach fifth grade, earn her
   7|                       Master's Degree from Boston University. and serve as the Reading Supervisor for grades K-8
   8|                       from 1957 until her retirement in 1977. She was past president of the Lunenburg Teachers'
   9|                       Association and the North Worcester County's Council on Reading. She was an avid golfer and
  10|                       past president of the Oak Hill Country Club Women's Golf Association. She also coached
  11|                       basketball and field hockey at Lunenburg High and was deeply honored to be one of the first ten
  12|                       inductees to the LHS Athletic Hall of Fame in 2007.
  13|                                   Walter Barrett Keeler, age 90, died April 24th. Mr. Keeler was active in the
  14|                       Lunenburg community, taking leadership positions in the United Parish; serving on the School
  15|                       Committee from 1965-1974; helping to found Hickory Hills Landowners Inc. in 1978 which
  16|                       purchased the lake for residents and shareholders; working tirelessly to have Hickory Hills roads
  17|                       taken over by the Town; and culminating in service on the Lunenburg Board of Selectmen from
  18|                       1984-1987.
  19|                       Sarah Powell, 103, passed away on September 13, 2017. She was the recipient of the Boston
  20|                       Post Cane Award in 2015. At that time, she said she was ancient. Sarah's wit will be missed by
  21|                       all, as well as her special ability to say the alphabet backwards!
  22|                       Einard Warpula, 89, passed away on May 17th at home surrounded by family. Einard lived on
  23|                       the same property on Flat Hill Road in Lunenburg for 87 years. Einard owned Warpula Bus
  24|                       Company for 25 years, safely transporting thousands of students in Lunenburg. Upon "retiring,"
  25|                       he drove motor-coaches for Wilson Bus Lines and school buses for local contractors, right up
  26|                       until last December. He was a wealth of knowledge on best-routes, landmarks, parking spots and
  27|                       all things mechanical. Einard loved to drive and trained many others to drive as well. He loved a
  28|                       good story and a good laugh and always found something positive in every situation.

   7|                                                                        AREA- 26.63 SQUARE MILES
   8|                                                                          ROAD MILES- ABOUT 100
   9|                         Lunenburg was settled in 1718 and incorporated in 1728. It occupies a land area of
  10|                         approximately 26.4 square miles. Lunenburg is located in north central Massachusetts
  11|                         approximately 43 miles from Boston. It is bordered on the west by the Town of Ashby and the
  12|                         City of Fitchburg, on the south by the Town of Lancaster and the City of Leominster, on the east
  13|                         by the Town of Shirley, and on the north by the Town of Townsend
  14|                         Local legislative decisions are made by an open town meeting consisting of all registered voters
  15|                         in the town. Subject to the legislative decisions made by town meeting, the affairs of the town
  16|                         are generally administered by a five member Board of Selectmen and a Town Manager. Local
  17|                         school affairs are administered by an elected seven member School Committee while local taxes
  18|                         are assessed by a three member Board of Assessors, all are appointed on an at-large basis for
  19|                         staggered three-year terms.
  20|                         Lunenburg is a semi-rural community located in northern Worcester County. Three water bodies
  21|                         account for somewhat of a clustered approach to the town's historical development. Lake
  22|                         Whalom and the former Whalom Amusement Park delineated the Whalom District from the
  23|                         remainder of Lunenburg. This district was developed as a housing base for many factory
  24|                         workers working in the mills and factories of Leominster and Fitchburg. In many ways the
  25|                         characteristics of the Whalom District more closely reflect characteristics of these cities than of
  26|                         the rest of Lunenburg. The Hickory Hills area of town, around Hickory Hills Lake, was
  27|                         developed as a private community comprised largely of vacation cottages. The third body of
  28|                         water is Shirley Reservoir, a public water body also developed with mainly vacation cottages
  29|                         that have now been modified to year-round residences. Commercial and industrial development
  30|                         is permitted in several areas of town, primarily along the two major arteries (Routes 2A and 13),
  31|                         and the town's peripheries, due largely to the close proximity of Leominster's and Fitchburg's
  32|                         sewer and water systems. Although many farms and orchards had slowly developed into single
  33|                         family housing, farming continues on in Lunenburg. At the Annual Town Meeting on May 4"
  34|                         2013, the Town voted to establish an Agricultural Commission to represent, preserve, and enhance farming in the community and to administer the "Right to Farm" bylaw.

   3|                       The calendar year of 2017 was another year of progress for the Board of Selectmen and Town
   4|                       Manager's Office. During 2017 there were many topics addressed and accomplishments to
   5|                       highlight:
   6|                            • On January 17".             ", the new Town Manager, Heather Lemieux, began. During this month
   7|                                  the Town Manager pursued entering the Commonwealth's Community Compact Program
   8|                                  to implement up to three best practices. By entering the program, the State would
   9|                                   provide grant funds to implement these best practices and it would also enable the Town
  10|                                  to be eligible for an Information Technology Grant and a Regionalization/Efficiency
  11|                                   Grant. At the Board's first meeting in February the Selectmen chose three best practices,
  12|                                  including to "Implement a Citizen Engagement Co-Creation Plan", to "Implement
  13|                                   Stormwater Management Measures",                      ', and to "Implement a Cyber-Security Training
  14|                                   Program". The Town entered the program in February and a ceremony was held with
  15|                                   Lieutenant Governor Karyn Polito on May 4*' to sign the compact. All three best
  16|                                   practices are expected to be completed by June of 2018.
  17|                            • Other noteworthy items that happened in February include a Roads Workshop that was
  18|                                  held to review the list of roads that would be paved for the ensuing year as part of the
  19|                                   $4M debt exclusion for a pavement management plan, as well as a discussion on entering
  20|                                  the Complete Streets Program under Massachusetts Department of Transportation that
  21|                                   would improve accessibility and safety of travel modes for all ages and abilities. Town
  22|                                   Manager Heather Lemieux, Selectmen Chairman Jamie Toale, Land Use Director Adam
  23|                                   Burney and DPW Director Jack Rodriquenz formulated a Complete Streets policy that
  24|                                   was approved by the Board in April of 2017. The policy was submitted to MassDOT and
  25|                                  received a favorable scoring that enabled the Town to receive $37,000 in grant funds to
  26|                                  hire a consultant to assist the Town to put together a list of projects into a Prioritization
  27|                                  Plan. Once completed this would be submitted to MassDOT and the Town would be
  28|                                   eligible to receive up to $400,000 in construction funds to complete projects on the list.
  29|                                  It is estimated the Prioritization Plan will be completed by May of 2018. Also in
  30|                                   February the Selectmen interviewed nine law firms and in April selected Mead, Talerman
  31|                                   & Costa as the Town's new Town Counsel, who took over from KP Law in July. The
  32|                                   Town Manager presented a balanced budget for Fiscal Year 2018 to the Finance
  33|                                   Committee and Selectmen in mid-February for a total of $39,781,168. Part of the Town
  34|                                   Manager's recommendation was to begin to fund the Other Post-Employment Benefits
  35|                                   Trust Fund for the unfunded liability of retiree health insurance in the amount of
  36|                                   $100,000 and to create a Special Purpose Stabilization Account for known large capital
  37|                                  items such as fire trucks with a starting appropriation of $200,000.
