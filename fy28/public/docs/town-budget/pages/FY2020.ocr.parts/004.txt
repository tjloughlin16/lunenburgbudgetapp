   3|              Lunenburg Public School's Instructional Leadership Team and the Reopening Team's Teaching and
   4|              Learning Task Group developed multiple teaching and learning models for the 2020-2021 school year.
   5|              Upon return to school in the fall, students had a choice to return to a hybrid model of learning or remain in
   6|              a fully remote learning model.
   7|              Hybrid & Remote Learning Models
   8|              In our hybrid learning model, approximately 50% of students in the model attend school in person on
   9|              Tuesdays and Thursdays while learning remotely the other days of the week, and the other 50% attend in
  10|              person on Wednesdays and Fridays while learning remotely the other days of the week. High needs
  11|              students are prioritized for in-person learning four days per week. Mondays are a remote learning day for
  12|              all students during which students meet virtually with teachers during a portion of the day and complete
  13|              independent work for the remainder. This allows for classroom configurations to be altered for social
  14|              distancing.
  15|              A percentage of Lunenburg students chose to remain in a fully remote learning model. Specific teachers at
  16|              each grade level have been assigned to fully remote classes which students attend virtually through Google
  17|              Meet and which include a combination of synchronous and asynchronous learning experiences. At
  18|              Lunenburg High School, some remote students are enrolled in select virtual education classes through
  19|              Educere.
  20|              All students in the district were provided with 1:1 devices, Chromebooks for students in grades 3-12 and
  21|              iPads for students in PreK-2.
  22|              Our teachers have worked diligently to create welcoming physical and virtual classrooms and to provide
  23|              robust and rigorous learning and social emotional experiences for students in an environment where
  24|              digitizing and reimagining curriculum is essential but extremely challenging. All teachers use Google
  25|              Classroom as a learning management system in order to post assignments, announcements, instructions,
  26|              videos, presentations, links to virtual meetings through Google Meet, and other instructional materials and
  27|              tools. Some of the instructional and assessment platforms employed by teachers include district-
  28|              purchased subscriptions to iReady, Lexia, IXL, Newsela, Brainpop, Raz-Kids, and Nearpod. In addition,
  29|              teachers are using Google Workplace elements such as Docs, Slides, Sheets, Sites, Forms, and Jamboard to
  30|              design and implement lessons in addition to other tools and resources such as Khan Academy, EdPuzzle,
  31|              Kahoot, CommonLit, Gimkit, and Flipgrid to name a few. Many teachers have also recorded lessons or
  32|              read alouds with Meet and Screencastify to provide students with additional personalized instruction.
  33|              Collaboration has been essential for this dramatic shift in teaching as teams of teachers consistently work
  34|              together to create materials, plan lessons, engage students, and design curriculum for optimum learning
  35|              outcomes for students.
  36|              Live streaming
  37|              In January 2021, our hybrid model changed to incorporate live streaming so that students in the remote
  38|              cohort on Tuesday-Thursday join the in-person cohort for class as one community. Live streaming was
  39|              piloted by select high school classes during the fall and by our 8th grade teachers in December, 2020, and
  40|              best practices were shared with colleagues during professional development and multiple question and
  41|              answer sessions. In preparation for live streaming throughout the district, teachers were provided with new
  42|              laptops, a second monitor, microphones/speakers, docking stations, and tablets.

  30|              regard to literacy instruction. The goal is to prepare students for any reading and writing task they will face
  31|              and to turn kids into life-long, confident readers and writers who display agency and independence.

   1|              Building Culture and Climate continues to be of the utmost importance during this school year. The staff
   2|              has made great efforts to include traditional activities for students while being mindful of the necessary
   3|              safety protocols. Our staff works to maintain a school culture which is positive, inclusive, and supportive.
   4|              Through the continued use of Positive Behavior Intervention and Supports (PBIS). Positive Behavioral
   5|              Interventions and Supports (PBIS) is an evidence-based three-tiered framework for improving and
   6|              integrating all of the data, systems, and practices affecting student outcomes every day. We have worked
   7|              on establishing and teaching the school wide expectations along with having a continuum of encouraging
   8|              expected behavior and discouraging problem behaviors. This system is aligning nicely with The Anchors of
   9|              Emotional Intelligence, a whole-school/district-wide approach to teaching social emotional learning, which
  10|              the staff has been trained in and is using to help students recognize, understand, describe, communicate and
  11|              regulate their emotions. These approaches have been supplemented by the creation of the Behavioral Health
  12|              Support Team (BHST) which provides tiered support strategies for students behavioral and mental health.
  13|               The Primary School is a Title One school and we have continued our tiered instructional system to ensure

   1|                                                                       Turkey Hill Elementary School
   2|                                                                                        2020-2021
   3|              The challenges of 2020 were monumental across the world, COVID-19 an unprecedented calamity with
   4|              profound effects for the world's population. No school in the United States was untouched. The pandemic
   5|              brought with it sweeping changes, some positive, others difficult, but our school community, like our
   6|               country, will never be quite the same.
   7|              Turkey Hill Elementary School is in its fourth year as a grade 3-5 building. There are currently 344
   8|              students, with 111 third graders, 108 fourth graders, and 125 fifth graders. We employ 25 teachers, 100%
   9|              of whom are licensed in their assignment area. Our special areas include physical education, art, music,
  10|              technology/library and social emotional learning. We are fortunate to have a half time assistant principal, a
  11|              full time nurse, a psychologist, BCBA, and a part time adjustment counselor/social worker. Other support
  12|              personnel who work across the district include two EL teachers, a speech and language pathologist, OT, and
  13|              PT.
  14|              January and February of 2020 were unremarkable: the annual TIGER anti-bullying presentation for grades
  15|              3 and 4, professional development in the Calkins Units for reading teachers, EMOTE pilot training (a
  16|              program that tracks student behavior), formative assessments for teacher evaluation, TST (teacher support
  17|              team) meetings and learning walks for administrators. The calm before the storm.
  18|              On a Friday afternoon in mid-March we were called together in an emergency meeting to grapple with
  19|              news about COVID-19. After hours of discussion and searching for the best information at hand, we made
  20|              the decision to close the schools for two weeks. It was a difficult call, but we understood we were working
  21|              in the best interests of our community to keep kids safe. That started us down a road to fully remote
  22|              learning right through June, a reality no one anticipated. Our teachers had a single day, the following
  23|              Monday, to gather their materials, laptops, teacher's guides, plan books, whatever they felt necessary. In a
  24|              remarkably short amount of time they created Google Classrooms and invited their students to it. We were
  25|              hampered by the fact that not every student had equitable access to computers and internet capacity. We
  26|              followed the state guidelines to provide enrichment work; we encouraged students to participate and learn,
  27|              but in the absence of accountability or grades it was an uphill battle. MCAS testing was canceled, as was
  28|              the 5th grade graduation ceremony, the field trips, assemblies, and end-of-year field days. Teachers were
  29|               only allowed to retrieve other items from school via appointments. In an attempt at some semblance of
  30|              school community, the administration created morning messages with birthday shout outs, question-of-the-
  31|              day challenges, as well as kid-humor riddles. Teachers were not obliged to "live meet" with students but
  32|              many did to reassure, answer questions, and provide support. They also worked closely with colleagues to
  33|              present a grade level set of instructions and expectations for consistency. We embraced the challenges as
  34|              they presented, and made it through the year amidst community fear, isolation, huge changes in working
  35|              conditions, childcare issues, and grieving the loss of "live" interactions with students.
  36|              Over the summer, the superintendent created a number of committees to plan for fall reopening. We talked
  37|              through all the scenarios: fully remote, fully in person, hybrid. Ultimately, given the constraints of social
  38|              distancing we settled on a hybrid model with a fully remote option. That meant we would have no more
  39|              than half of our students in two days a week, either Tuesday and Thursday or Wednesday and Friday, and
  40|              Monday became a fully remote day for all students/staff so teachers would have time to collaborate and
  41|              plan in this new setting. About 100 students (of about 350) opted for fully remote classes. One teacher
  42|              from each grade level took on that responsibility. The other 250 students were divided into Cohort A or B.
  43|              Classrooms were reconfigured to hold 12-13 desks, spread six feet apart. Teachers created buffer zones of
  44|              six feet at the front of each room to use their white boards. Hand sanitizer was distributed throughout the
  45|              building, including dispensers at each entrance, and students were obliged to use it upon arrival. Signage
  46|              was posted that gave students safe places outside the classroom to take a water/mask break, to stay to the
  47|              right side of the hallway, to line up six feet apart.

   1|               As I reflected on the 2019-2020 school year, and considered what might be included in the 2020 Annual
   2|               Report, I was reminded of the progress and accomplishments made by our students and staff, during an
   3|               incredibly challenging time. While each year at Monty Tech is exciting and includes many "firsts",
   4|                traditional instruction ended abruptly in 2019-2020, and students and instructors were forced to turn to
   5|               technology to watch, learn and collaborate. The all-important hands-on learning was not possible because
   6|               of the unexpected pandemic, and so for the first time ever, our vocational instructors relied solely on their
   7|               creativity, their understanding of instructional technology, and their technical skills to carry them through
   8|               the end of the year. Our academic teachers collaborated to create lessons and assessments that would
   9|               effectively measure learning in a remote setting, and explored countless apps and platforms to increase
  10|               engagement. Students who looked forward to events like SkillsUSA competitions, prom and graduation,
  11|               grappled with disappointment and the unknown, and Guidance Counselors worked diligently to reach out to
  12|               students who were now more isolated from their peers than ever before. To say 2019-2020 was challenging
  13|               may be an understatement, but to disregard our progress as an educational community would be a disservice
  14|               to everyone who worked so hard to serve our students. While the traditional school year may have been
  15|               abbreviated, so many incredible achievements were recognized in this historic year.
  16|               A project more than 7 years in the making, the Monty Tech Veterinary Clinic, opened and began providing
  17|               affordable veterinary care to pets in need, across our district. Routine wellness care visits, affordable
  18|               medications and vaccinations, diagnostic services, primary care surgical services, stable urgent care,
  19|               compassionate end of life care, and dentistry services were delivered by our talented Veterinary Medical
  20|               Director, Dr. Kayla Sample. Students worked in the new state-of-the-art clinic, learning techniques and
  21|               gaining knowledge that align with veterinary assistant programs across the Commonwealth. In fact, Becker
  22|               College has recognized the Monty Tech Veterinary Science program for its rigor and content, and has
  23|               offered qualified program graduates up to 13 college credits. Further, relying on the school's trusted model
  24|               of school-to-business partnerships, a unique collaboration between Monty Tech and VCA has resulted in co-
  25|               op placements for Seniors in the program.
  26|               In August 2019, Monty Tech accepted a $384,257 award from the Massachusetts Skills Cabinet. This
  27|               award provided funding to update technology and equipment in two shops: Health Occupations and
  28|               Automotive Technology. Health Occupations students worked and learned in updated shop facilities, to
  29|               include new hospital beds and lifts, as well as new diagnostic equipment to train students in the proper
  30|               procedures for testing for cholesterol, blood sugar, lead, lung volume, and more. In addition, the program
  31|               received state-of-the-art virtual dissection technology, bringing lessons in anatomy and physiology to new
  32|               heights. This equipment, provided by Anatomage and most often found on college campuses, immediately
  33|               engaged students, bringing a deeper level of knowledge and understanding to students as they explored the
  34|               human body and its complex systems.
  35|               Students in the Automotive Technology program were introduced to an all-new simulation lab, that now
  36|               includes the latest technology in trainers and simulators aligned with NATEF standards. The shop was
  37|               updated to include new automotive lifts, floor jacks, engine stands, chargers, an air table, and a redesigned
  38|               tool crib with all new hand tools. Further, in 2019-2020, in partnership with Snap On Tools, instructors
  39|               rolled out a more sophisticated technical training program and increased opportunities to earn all-new
  40|               diagnostic credentials, validating their experience and ability to diagnose and service today's computer-
  41|               controlled vehicles.          With new curriculum, technology and the addition of NC3 Automotive Diagnostic
  42|               Certifications to the program, our Automotive Technology program graduates will enter a competitive
  43|               workforce poised for success.
  44|               Students who attend Monty Tech have chosen a high school experience unlike any other. While completing
  45|               all of the same high school requirements of students enrolled in a traditional, comprehensive high school,
  46|               our students are also learning a valuable trade - skills they will carry with them for a lifetime. In the most
  47|               challenging times, our students are called upon to demonstrate creativity, innovation and technical skill
  48|               proficiency, and though face-to-face instruction may have been interrupted, their achievements continued.
  49|               On behalf of our talented educators and administrators, who remain focused on delivering the highest
  50|               quality academic and vocational-technical education possible, I am delighted to present the District's 2019-
  51|               2020 annual report to you.
