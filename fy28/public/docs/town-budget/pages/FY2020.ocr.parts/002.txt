   1|                                           ARCHITECTURAL PRESERVATION DISTRICT COMMISSION
   2|              The year 2020 was a productive year for the Architectural Preservation District Commission (APDC)
   3|              even with the pandemic and the loss of some meeting time in the second part of the year.
   4|              The APDC:
   5|              • facilitated Applications for Certificate to Alter in order to renovate, construct or alter historic homes
   6|                   in the Architectural Preservation District (APD) and communicated with the public answering
   7|                   questions about rules and regulations in the APD,
   8|              • initiated a plan for the APDC to take an annual walk through the APD to assess the physical
   9|                   condition of the APD with the intent of keeping track of the district over time,
  10|              • advocated for the preservation of the old Primary School and the Brooks House
  11|              • designed, purchased, and placed signs indicating the Architectural Preservation District
  12|             • created a plan for the restoration of the Town Pound.
  13|              The commission received the resignation of Jim LaVeck who had been a member since its beginning. His
  14|               long hours of work over two years were instrumental in the formation of the APDC and its rules,
  15|               regulations, and procedures. He was an absolute joy to work with and made difficult work easier for all the
  16|               commission members in the formation of the APDC. His work along with the other members after the two

   1|                                                                            Conservation Commission
   2|              The Conservation Commission is a seven member commission, appointed to 3 year terms by the Select
   3|               Board, whose duties are codified under the Conservation Commission Act MGL c. 40 § 8C. The Commission
   4|              enforces the Massachusetts Wetland Protection Act MGL c. 131 § 40 and Chapter 239 of the local by-laws
   5|               for the protection of wetlands.
   6|              The year 2020 was as last year, one of a very busy and active agenda containing both public hearings and
   7|              meetings for a wide range of projects from commercial development, subdivisions and small single family
   8|              upkeep projects and larger scale industrial/commercial proposals despite the limitations set by the COVID-19
   9|              pandemic. The Conservation Commission's work continues into the year 2021 to work on clarifying the land
  10|              use policy to provide consistency with the original by-law and to find those sections that may be now out of
  11|              date and need to be modified. This effort is estimated to be completed by spring of 2021. The Conservation
  12|              Commission has implemented revisions to the local wetland regulations, which have been in effect for over
  13|              one year to date and have served the interests of protecting valuable community natural resources very fairly
  14|              and effectively.
  15|              The Commission also held numerous inspections with the commission administrator on weekends prior to
  16|              meetings to familiarize themselves with the upcoming proposals that would appear on their agenda. The
  17|              Commission continues to work with the town on familiarizing itself with the upcoming storm water mandates
  18|              from the EPA and working towards compliance both by procedure and with any necessary codification as
  19|              well as aiding with the community implementations the "MS4" regulations.
  20|              The Conservation Commission continues to implement forest stewardship plans using grants as outlined in
  21|              the last two annual reports. The stewardship plan for the Hollis Road property is oriented toward enhancing
  22|              bird wildlife habitat in conjunction with the Mass Audubon Society as part of the DCR "Forestry for the
  23|              Birds" program. The forest stewardship plans provide environmental management plans which include
  24|              timber-stand management. The Commission retained Gary Gouldrup of New England Forestry Consultants
  25|              who is a noted forester with years of experience working in Lunenburg and the adjacent towns/cities serving
  26|              municipalities and private landowners. The Commission continues to hold public forums on the
  27|              implementation of its open space policies and timber-stand management. The Commission expects to
  28|              continue to apply for and receive funding from the Commonwealth to author stewardship plans for our
  29|               Conservation Areas.
  30|              The Conservation Commission also has commenced planning for the forest management of the RAYNO
  31|               property and worked with the Upen Space Committee to complete the acquisition of the Saliba Property on
  32|               Howard Street adding signiticant acreage of open space. Copies of the current environmental stewardship
  33|               plans are available on the Conservation Commission website at:
  34|              https://www.lunenburgma.gov/files-docs/18-05-21/forest-stewardship-plans
  35|              The Commission was awarded a Recreational Trails Program grant to provide color coded trail markers and
  36|              coordinated "You Are Here" trail map signs to expand the safe use of Lunenburg's existing trail network.
  37|              Regional trail system map signs would show how trails are interconnected among multiple properties. The
  38|              project will also provide informational kiosks and emergency access gates. During 2020 trail maps were
  39|               developed for eleven conservation properties. These trail maps will be posted on a Conservation Commission
  40|              Trails website, on informational kiosks on the properties and on "You Are Here" trail map signs at trail heads
  41|               and trail intersections.
  42|              The Commission has also continued to work closely with Lake Shirley and Hickory Hills on the
  43|              implementations of their lake management plans. These plans set goals and action items designed to give a
  44|              better picture of the overall health of the lakes and define various strategies to ensure continued protection of
  45|               these valuable resources.

   1|                                                                 DEPARTMENT OF PUBLIC WORKS
   2|              The Department of Public Works combines the resources of the Highway, Parks, Cemetery, Sewer, Facilities
   3|              Departments, and Vehicle Maintenance services into one department organizing and scheduling personnel and
   4|              equipment to best serve the residents of the community through the effective delivery of an extensive scope of
   5|              services with a high degree of efficiency.
   6|                    "The mission of the Department of Public Works is to protect, preserve, and improve the Town's
   7|                   infrastructure and related assets in a manner which meets and exceeds the current and future social
   8|                   and economic needs of the community, to contribute to a healthy, safe and quality environment for the
   9|                    lown's residents and visitors, and to provide a cost effective and environmentally sound means of
  10|                    alsposing of solid waste and wastewater. "
  11|              DEPARTMENT PHILOSOPHY
  12|              Honesty and integrity are absolutes. They characterize the only forum in which anything worthwhile can be
  13|              accomplished.
  14|              Efficiency: Make every effort to proceed correctly the first time. It may take a bit longer but will not require
  15|             repeated attempts.
  16|              Loyalty up and down the chain of command is essential to success.
  17|              Every employee is essential. Those at the top are support people. The real work is done by the men and
  18|              women who drive the trucks, operate the equipment, turn the wrenches, and provide a seamless flow of
  19|              paperwork.
  20|              DEPARTMENTAL OBJECTIVES
  21|              Public Works Staff is either working or on call twenty-four hours a day three hundred and sixty-five days a
  22|              year prepared to respond to any weather, infrastructure, roadway, or facility related emergency that may arise.
  23|              We strive to enhance the awareness of the value Public Works provides to our Town, and ensure others are
  24|              aware of our contribution. This is not for an expectation of praise, but rather to make sure efforts are not
  25|              dismissed as they merge into the fabric of our everyday expectation of service.
  26|              Major events, particularly in the form of devastating storms, over the past years have highlighted the fact that
  27|              Public Works has a seat at the table and is a major player in emergency management as first responders.
  28|              Town Officials, Police, and Fire acknowledge that Public Works is critical in order to facilitate the required
  29|              services of others. We must continue to build on this awareness.
  30|              This Department must reach out to the public we serve, continuing to make them aware of the fact that the
  31|              common services they receive do not just happen, but rather are the efforts of a dedicated staff of individuals,
  32|              service companies, and vendors. Safe roadways, wastewater control, solid waste collection and recycling
  33|              operations, tree removal, storm water management, grounds maintenance, winter snow and ice removal,
  34|              cemeteries and parks are necessary services supporting everyone in the community. We will continue to
  35|              engage the public in order to set reasonable expectations and gather support for annual budget requests,
  36|              capital requests, and specific projects.
  37|              DEPARTMENTAL GOALS FOR FISCAL 2021
  38|             • Continue to coordinate administrative directives with the activities of all public service departments and
  39|                   organizations within the DPW to improve service levels. Strive always for a higher level of efficiency.
  40|              • Coordinate with those who work daily in Town facilities to best prioritize capital improvements. DPW
  41|                    plays a major role in the enhancement of the work environment of all Town employees.
  42|              • Increase involvement with our insurance provider to make better use of available resources in an effort to
  43|                   obtain credits toward lessening premiums. Safety is our first concern.
  44|              • Encourage and facilitate DPW personnel to obtain and maintain required licensure in order to provide an
  45|                   optimal level of service.
