  32|             The Cooperative Education Program is an extension of the student's technical education that combines
  33|             classroom instruction with on-the-job-training. The Co-op Program provides students with an opportunity to
  34|             further develop academic, technical and employability skills in an industry work environment. All students
  35|             are eligible to participate in the Co-op Program, provided they satisfy state and school grade, attendance and
  36|             performance requirements. All Co-op students have completed the OSHA recognized Career Safe online
  37|             health and safety course.
  38|             The Cooperative Education Program is beneficial for both student and employer. For the student, it is a
  39|             chance to gain on-the-job training that improves both their technical and employability skills. Additionally,
  40|             making industry connections enhances post-graduate employment opportunities. The Co-op Program allows
  41|             students to earn while they learn.

  43|             temporary and potential full-time employees who are already trained, thus reducing employer training costs. Co-
  44|             op work hours may be tailored to suit the needs of partnering employers. Monty Tech was on pace for a record

   1|             Programs and initiatives that we expect to implement in 2020-2021 that will have a positive impact on our
   2|             school and students are as follows:
   3|             Support faculty, staff and students' socio-emotional wellness, and develop a community-wide understanding
   4|             of equity (or lack thereof) and how it contributes to a safe and supportive learning environments for all
   5|             students. We recognize that students and staff have been home and isolated from others for months.
   6|             Learning from home may have presented an unintended result - unequal learning opportunities. There are
   7|             fears and concerns among staff, and there is a heightened sensitivity to what this isolation has meant to our
   8|             student population. Collaborating with community service agencies, we will address efforts to create a
   9|             healthy school climate, and will provide targeted training opportunities to better serve our students. A
  10|             diverse team of professionals (teachers, paraprofessionals, guidance counselors, and support staff) will review
  11|             existing district and school documents and policies, survey staff and students regarding school climate, and
  12|             develop a comprehensive action plan, and infuse those elements into the 2021-2024 District Improvement
  13|              Plan.
  14|             Expand technology resources and infrastructure to support Hybrid and/or Remote Learning Platforms.
  15|             Monty Tech is fortunate to have been able to provide all students with a Chromebook for the past several
  16|             years, and all teachers with Chromebooks or laptops to support curriculum development efforts while away
  17|             from the school. That technology proved to be invaluable during the 2019-2020 school year, and so the
  18|             District is committed to maintaining and expanding access to instructional technology that will enhance at-
  19|             home learning moving forward. During the summer of 2020, we learned that school would not resume with
  20|             all students in-person. A complex schedule (balancing academic and vocational in-person instructional time)
  21|             was negotiated and agreed upon, and students/parents were given the option of returning in a hybrid of fully
  22|             remote model. By purchasing and maintaining ZOOM licenses, increasing bandwidth, reconfiguring surplus
  23|             equipment, and continuing to work with the school's Instructional Technologist to provide a variety of
  24|             workshops to academic and vocational instructors who have come to rely of any one of the two school's
  25|             Learning Management Systems (Google Classroom or Schoology), Monty Tech will be well-positioned and
  26|             prepared to deliver instruction either in-person or remotely, at a moment's notice.
  27|             Modify facilities and infrastructure to ensure students, faculty and staff are working and learning in a safe
  28|             environment. While the school is prepared to provide all faculty and staff with required and requested PPE,
  29|             many upgrades to the school building must be completed prior to and maintained throughout the 2020-2021
  30|             school year. All rooftop units will be upgraded from MERV 8 to MERV 13 rated air filters, which are high
  31|             quality air filters typically used in general surgery or hospital settings, capturing down to 0.3 - 1.0 micron
  32|             particle size. iWave units will be added to the HVAC system, reducing pathogens, allergens, particles, smoke
  33|             and odors in the air, creating a healthy environment without producing any harmful byproducts. Installing
  34|             sanitizing stations, redirecting traffic patterns, and limiting the number of desks/seats in a confined space will
  35|             provide a comfortable setting for the students during their in-person learning days, while limiting access to
  36|             the school (parents and visitors must wait outside) will aide in screening and tracing efforts throughout the
  37|             year.

  10|             ABUTTERS
  11|             The owners of property within 300 feet of the perimeter of the subject property.
  12|             APPLICANT
  13|             The owner of land from which earth will be removed, or the person of lawful standing in the stead of such
  14|             owner. Any person, company, or agent standing instead of the actual property owner must have the owner's
  15|             written permission to do so.
  16|             BOARD
  17|             The Planning Board of the Town of Lunenburg, which shall act as the permit granting authority (PGA).
  18|             EARTH
  19|             This term shall include soil, loam, sand, gravel, clay, peat, rock, or other native earth material in solid form.
  20|             EARTH REMOVAL OPERATION
  21|             Any commercial mining, stripping, quarrying, digging or blasting of earth originating from the Town of
  22|             Luneburg and its transportation into or out of the Town of Lunenburg.
  23|             EROSION
  24|             The process by which the ground surface is worn by forces such as wind, running water, ice, abrasion,
  25|             gravity, transportation or by artificial means.
  26|             GRAVEL
  27|             Loose fragments of rock or coarse aggregate resulting from natural disintegration and abrasion of rock or
  28|             processing of weakly bound conglomerate.
  29|             LOAM OR TOPSOIL
  30|             A soil consisting of a friable mixture of varying proportions of clay, sand, silt, and organic matter.
  31|             PGA
  32|             The permit granting authority, which will be the Planning Board for the purposes of this article.
  33|             PROCESSING
  34|             The sorting or separation of earth materials into distinct categories based on particle size or type usually
  35|             through the use of a screening process, not including stone crushing.
  36|             SILT
  37|             Loose sedimentary material with rock particles usually less than 1/16 mm or less in diameter based on the
  38|              Wentworth scale of measurement.
  39|             A distinct portion of one or more contiguous lots, under the same or different ownerships, on which an earth
  40|             removal operation is conducted, or is proposed to be conducted, under the permit.

  27|               § 140-6 Permit criteria for earth removal operations.
  28|               A. Permits for earth removal operations shall be granted by the PGA only upon its written determination
  29|                     that the proposed use shall not cause substantial detriment to the neighborhood, or the Town, taking into
  30|                     account the characteristics of the site and the proposal in relation to the site and surrounding
  31|                     environment. In addition to any specific factors that may be set forth elsewhere in this article and its
  32|                     associated regulations adopted pursuant to § 140-8, such determination shall include consideration of
  33|                     each of the following:
  34|               (1) Social, economic, or community needs which are served by the proposal;
  35|               (2) Impact of the Earth Removal Operation on traffic flow and safety, including loading and unloading;
  36|               (3) Neighborhood character and the effect of the proposed Earth Removal Operation on that character;
  37|               (4) Impacts on the natural environment, including any proposed remediation for Earth Removal Activities;
  38|               (5) Potential fiscal impact, including impact on Town services, tax base and property values.

   3|               § 140-7 General limitations.
   4|               A. No permit shall be issued for the removal of earth in any location if such an operation:
   5|               (1) Will endanger the public safety, public health or constitute a nuisance.
   6|               (2) Will produce noise, dust, or other noxious effects observable at the lot lines of the property in amounts
   7|                     objectionable or detrimental to the normal use of adjacent properties.
   8|               (3) Will result in the transportation of materials in such manner as to cause traffic congestion, dust,
   9|                       spillage, noise, or other nuisances or hazards, particularly on residential streets.
  10|               (4) Will result in the transportation over ways which will be unduly injured thereby.
  11|               (5) Will result in a change of topography and cover which will be disadvantageous to the most appropriate
  12|                       final use of the land or to the use of lands adjacent to the site.
  13|               (6) Will cause irreparable harm to or loss of important wildlife, wildlife habitat or rare plant species
  14|                       indigenous to the area.
  15|               (7) Will result in stormwater damaging abutting properties.
  16|               B. Applicants are encouraged to submit a written statement regarding how each of the previous items will
  17|                     be addressed and/or mitigated.
  18|               § 140-8 Regulations.
  19|               The PGA may adopt and periodically amend rules and regulations for the implementation of this article, by
  20|               majority vote. Such rules and regulations may set forth performance standards for earth removal operations,
  21|               impose filing and consultant fees, define additional terms not inconsistent with the article, and establish
  22|               administrative procedures. Proposed Rules and Regulations shall be adopted by the PGA at a public hearing.
  23|               Notice of the hearing shall be published by the PGA in a newspaper of general circulation in each of two
  24|               successive weeks, the first publication being not less than 14 days before the day of said hearing. Failure by
  25|               the PGA to adopt such rules and regulations or a legal declaration of their invalidity by a court of law shall
  26|               not act to suspend or invalidate the effect of this article.
  27|               § 140-9 Permit procedures.
  28|               A. Any person wishing to obtain a permit to remove material on a property in the Town, or to use any
  29|                     public way within the Town for transporting such material, shall file a completed application for a
  30|                     permit together with any required supporting data and maps with the PGA and the filing fee, as
  31|                     established in the regulations of the PGA.
  32|               B. Any permit issued is nontransferable and shall automatically expire upon completion of the earth
  33|                     removal project for which it was issued or at such time as may be specified in said permit. In no case
  34|                     shall a permit be issued initially for a period longer than one year. A permit may be renewed for up to
  35|                     two years or lesser time as determined by the PGA after evidence is presented that all conditions of the
  36|                     expiring permit have been complied with and the work has been performed in good faith. There is no
  37|                     limit on the number of permit renewals an applicant can apply for. A public hearing may be required by
  38|                     the PGA, at its discretion, for renewal of permits.
  39|               C. Upon approval of all permits will expire on the following June 30. Thereafter all renewals will be for a
  40|                           period of 1 or 2 years running from July 1 to June 30.

   9|                   transmission of the notice of the hearing to the abutters, and the costs will be billed to the applicant. Final
