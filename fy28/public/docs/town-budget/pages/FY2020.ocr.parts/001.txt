   2|                                         Elisabeth "Sis" (Ryan) Montuori, 87, of Lunenburg, died peacefully July 28, 2020 surrounded by her
   3|                                         loving family. Her husband of 65 years, Lawrence "Lawrie" Montuori, died January 16, 2020. Sis worked
   4|                                         alongside Lawrie as bookkeeper of Lunenburg Oil Co, the business they operated together for 36 years. She
   5|                                         was a talented seamstress, working out of her home. Sis was a faithful member of St. Boniface Parish and St.
   6|                                         Boniface Women's Guild. For years, she oversaw St. Boniface's annual Red Cross Blood Mobile, earning
   7|                                         awards for the high volume of donations. She volunteered on the T.C. Passios Elementary School
   8|                                         Community Council, Lunenburg High School Football Boosters and the Council on Aging. Sis had a passion
   9|                                         and talent for cooking, interior decorating, entertaining, and gardening. She had an unconditional love of life,
  10|                                         family and friends. She leaves a son, three daughters, a half-brother, eight grandchildren, six great-
  11|                                         grandchildren and several nephews and nieces.
  12|                                          Bessie H. (Platt) Lindsay, 90, passed peacefully on July 30, 2020. Bessie was married

  14|                                          daughter of the late William and Shirley Platt and one of seven siblings.
  15|                                          Bessie was proud of and dedicated to her community. Her volunteerism was extensive including at the Eagle
  16|                                          House and the Lunenburg Senior Center and the Lunenburg Friendly Seniors, where she was also a member.
  17|                                          Bessie was incredibly active and loved to have fun with family and friends!

  29|                                           from 1963 to 1969 and then attended Northeastern University. He returned to the Lunenburg Football field

   9|                                                                          ROAD MILES- ABOUT 100
  10|              Lunenburg was settled in 1718 and incorporated in 1728. It occupies a land area of approximately 26.4
  11|              square miles. Lunenburg is located in north central Massachusetts approximately 43 miles from Boston. It is
  12|              bordered on the west by the Town of Ashby and the City of Fitchburg, on the south by the Town of
  13|              Lancaster and the City of Leominster, on the east by the Town of Shirley, and on the north by the Town of
  14|              Townsend
  15|              Local legislative decisions are made by an open town meeting consisting of all registered voters in the town.
  16|              Subject to the legislative decisions made by town meeting, the affairs of the town are generally administered
  17|              by a five member Select Board and a Town Manager. Local school affairs are administered by an elected
  18|              seven member School Committee while local taxes are assessed by a three member Board of Assessors, all
  19|              are appointed on an at-large basis for staggered three-year terms.
  20|              Lunenburg is a semi-rural community located in northern Worcester County. Three water bodies account for
  21|              somewhat of a clustered approach to the town's historical development.                                               Lake Whalom and the former
  22|              Whalom Amusement Park delineated the Whalom District from the remainder of Lunenburg. This district
  23|              was developed as a housing base for many factory workers working in the mills and factories of Leominster
  24|              and Fitchburg. In many ways the characteristics of the Whalom District closely reflect characteristics of
  25|              these cities more than of the rest of Lunenburg. The Hickory Hills area, around Hickory Hills Lake, was
  26|              developed as a private community comprised largely of vacation cottages. The third body of water is Shirley
  27|              Reservoir, a public water body also developed with mainly vacation cottages that have now been modified to
  28|              year-round residences.
  29|              Commercial and industrial development is permitted in several areas of town, primarily along the two major
  30|              arteries (Routes 2A and 13) and the town's peripheries, due largely to the close proximity of Leominster's
  31|              and Fitchburg's sewer and water systems. Although many farms and orchards had slowly developed into
  32|              single family housing, farming continues on in Lunenburg. In 2013 the Annual Town Meeting voted to
  33|              establish an Agricultural Commission to represent, preserve, and enhance farming in the community and to
  34|              administer the "Right to Farm" bylaw.
  35|              The 2015 Lunenburg Town Meeting established the Architectural Preservation District ("District") bylaw to
  36|              regulate construction, demolition or renovation of existing "Contributing Property" structures as defined by
  37|              the bylaw within an established bounded district. The bylaw was introduced at Town Meeting along with the
  38|              Village District Bylaw, which created an overlay zoning district to encourage mixed-use commercial and
  39|              residential development. While the Village District Bylaw encourages the economic vitalization of
  40|              downtown Lunenburg, the Architectural Preservation District Bylaw ensures that it will not come at the
  41|              expense of the town's historical character and integrity.

   1|                                                               REPORT OF THE TOWN MANAGER
   2|                                                                                 Fiscal Year 2020
   3|             This year, as I write this report on the achievements of fiscal year 2020, my mind immediately goes back to
   4|             March of 2020 when all our lives were turned upside down. We can all relate that this past year has been a
   5|             roller coaster and everyone has faced personal and professional challenges that no one could have foreseen.
   6|             From a local government standpoint, there were a myriad of obstacles and uncertainties facing cities and
   7|             towns across our nation that our local and school leadership team also faced, such as providing a continuity
   8|              of services to our residents and businesses during a pandemic, food insecurity for our youth and elders, re-
   9|             opening measures that adequately protected our employees, students and the public, remote learning for
  10|             students, remote public meetings, a vast array of changes in state and federal legislation that affected our
  11|             daily operations, and the list goes on. The town department heads and school leadership teams and our staff
  12|             came together during this time and met these challenges head on. As a town we witnessed attitudes of
  13|             perseverance, innovation, and an overall desire to help our residents, students, and businesses throughout
  14|             this pandemic. I want to recognize the immense thought and effort that town and school staff put into how
  15|             we provided services this past year and how everyone rose to the challenge.
  16|             During fiscal year 2020, in addition to all the responses that were needed in light of COVID-19, there were
  17|             many topics addressed and accomplishments to highlight:
  18|             • In July of 2019, as part of the annual goal setting process, the Selectmen reviewed their goals from FY19
  19|                   and formalized their goals for FY20. The goals the Board identified for FY2019 included identifying,
  20|                   defining, discussing, and finalizing the criteria to be used in determining the use and/or re-use of the
  21|                   Town Hall, Ritter, old Primary, Brooks House, and TC Passios and to propose at least one project to the
  22|                   2020 Annual Town Meeting regarding the use and/or reuse of the old Primary School. The Board
  23|                   identified the criteria used in determining the use and/or re-use of the aforementioned buildings in the
  24|                   fall of 2019 and discussed the future use of the old Primary throughout the year and continued to narrow
  25|                   down the focus for the future use of this building into 2020. The Board made progress on determining
  26|                   the future use of the old Primary School property as a future open space/passive recreation space with
  27|                   additional parking that is lacking in the center of town.
  28|             • The Town Manager's goals for FY 2020 included developing and implementing steps towards the
  29|                   redevelopment/reuse of 925 Massachusetts Avenue, implementation of a recycling contamination
  30|                   enforcement program, and planning/implementing accessibility improvements. All of the action items
  31|                   identified for each of the Town Manager's goals were completed.
  32|             • In July and August the Board met multiple times to finalize a Special Event Policy. The Board also did
  33|                   their annual review of all their policies and adopted a new Buy Recycled Policy and made various
  34|                   updates to the existing policies. In September the Board adopted a Social Media Policy.
  35|             • This year was another productive year for infrastructure projects in Lunenburg. Besides the continuation
  36|                   of the $4M Pavement Management Plan on local roads, the Town continued to work with the
  37|                   engineering firm on the Pleasant Street Bridge Project that is expected to be complete by the spring of
  38|                   2022. The DPW also undertook culvert repairs on Northfield Road and Sunset Lane and repairs to the
  39|                   Townsend Harbor Bridge. The Summer Street Road Reconstruction project that was awarded in 2018
  40|                   was substantially complete by the fall of 2019.

   9|                    Municipal Vulnerability Preparedness (MVP) Program, which provides support for cities and towns to
  10|                    plan for climate change and implement key adaptation actions for resiliency. The planning grant applied
  11|                    for is a necessary step in order to apply for construction costs to implement projects that are identified
  12|                    during the planning phase. The Town received a Municipal Vulnerability Preparedness (MVP) Planning
  13|                    Grant and hired a consultant to guide the planning process, including holding the required all day
  14|                    workshop, listening session, and to complete the vulnerability assessment and develop an action oriented
  15|                    climate resiliency plan. Communities who complete the MVP program become certified as an MVP
  16|                    community and are eligible for action grant funding to implement projects in their action plan. At the
  17|                    conclusion of this planning phase the Town submitted the action plan to the State, which was
  18|                    subsequently approved.

  20|                    in the fall that equated to a zero dollar impact to Fiscal Year 2020, mainly due to attrition in the Police
  21|                    Department during Fiscal Year 2020. The agreement included a 2.5% COLA for year one, 2% for year
  22|                    two, and 2% for year three and increased the educational incentive over three years. The costs for year
  23|                    two were estimated at $81,000 and year three costs were estimated at $74,000, with the total cost of the
  24|                    agreement to $139,000.

  14|                     Town Clerk and Board of Health Administrative Assistant, additional funding for Facilities and Public

  19|                    FY21. The pandemic also required us to postpone the Annual Town Meeting from May until June and it
  20|                    was held on Saturday, June 13" with all public health and CDC safety protocols in place. The total
  21|                    omnibus budget for FY21 that was approved in June was $38,658,115. By the fall Special Town
  22|                    Meeting in November 2020, with the first quarter revenues trending in a normal trajectory, all the items
  23|                    that were deferred were able to be funded with the exception of the additional hours for the Assistant
  24|                    Town Clerk and the additional $50,000 for Pavement Management.

  26|                    public rights of way throughout the year. In February the engineering firm that completed the ADA

  28|                    funding the non-compliant ramps, sidewalks, and crosswalks. The Town Manager applied for and

  30|                     Transition Plan by Montachusett Regional Planning Commission. The Town also received an earmark in
  31|                    FY20 for the repair of the handicapped ramp at the Town Hall in the amount of $27,000 that was put out
  32|                    to bid in April and was completed by the beginning of July.

  35|                    HVAC system in the older portion of the Eagle House Senior Community Center, energy efficiency
  36|                    improvements at the Public Safety Building, and repair of a portion of the Eagle House Senior
  37|                     Community Center roof. The removal of the underground fuel tanks located at the DPW and the
  38|                    installation of a new above ground tank commenced in September 2020.

  41|                    the unknown environmental hazards have not taken the property for back taxes so that the onus of the
  42|                    potential clean-up costs was not borne by the Town. The Town Manager submitted a Brownfields Site
  43|                     Assessment Grant through Montachusett Regional Planning Agency for the costs of a Phase II
  44|                    environmental site assessment, which was approved in the amount of $30,000.

   2|                     Continuity of Operations Plan and a Financial Trend Monitoring System. The State notified the Town
   3|                     Manager in May that both applications received funding, totaling $50,000. The pandemic highlighted
   4|                     the lack of a written Continuity of Operations Plan to ensure that there would be no interruption in
   5|                     services and back-ups for all personnel and programs were identified. Due to the uncertain economic
   6|                     times caused by the pandemic and the impact it had on the budget process, the implementation of a
   7|                     Financial Trend Monitoring System that would review certain key financial indicators and positive or
   8|                     negative trends was considered another important best practice. The consultant for both projects, the
   9|                     UMass Collins Center, began working with the Town in June. Both projects are expected to be complete
  10|                     by June of 2021.

  15|               The most prominent event of 2020 was the issuance of a State of Emergency by Governor Baker on March
  16|               10, 2020 and the impact COVID-19 had on the operations of the town, the school, how it affected our
  17|               residents and the businesses in town. There were countless actions needed in response to the different
  18|               actions and COVID-19 related legislation that affected our day to day operations, but I will limit it to the

  21|                     Coronavirus, we were forced to take swift, decisive actions to ensure we could continue providing
  22|                     services to the public and our businesses in the event of a shutdown. On Tuesday, March 17"h the Town
  23|                     Manager, Chairman of the Select Board, Police Chief, Fire Chief, and Board of Health Agent provided a

  25|                     on the Coronavirus.

  28|                     began using Zoom as our remote meeting platform, which meant a different method of scheduling all
  29|                     meetings, public access to be trained to broadcast Zoom meetings, and training for all board, committee,
  30|                     and staff members that would be using this platform.
  31|               • On March 24* the Select Board declared a local state of emergency and voted to delay the Annual Town
  32|                     Meeting from May until June 13" and on March 28'* the Board voted to file a Court Order to extend the
  33|                     deadline for nomination papers for the Annual Town Election. On March 31" the Board voted to
  34|                     postpone the Annual Town Election until June 20, 2020.
  35|               • Town Offices closed to the public on March 16" but the employees remained in the building. During this
  36|                     time an assessment was done to determine what steps were needed and what IT equipment was needed

  38|                    to occur in short order. On March 23d non-public safety employees began working remotely and
