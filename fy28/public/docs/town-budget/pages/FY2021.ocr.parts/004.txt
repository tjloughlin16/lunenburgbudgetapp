   3|                 I am pleased to present my annual report for the Lunenburg Public Schools, a district that I have the honor
   4|                 and privilege to lead. The COVID-19 pandemic has continued to present public education challenges, but
   5|                 the administration, faculty and staff continue to focus on what our students need in order to be successful. I

   7|                 future needs with the Town Manager, Heather Lemieux. As the leader of the district, 1 am fortunate to
   8|                 work with a School Committee that is truly dedicated to serving the best interests of our students and
   9|                 appreciative that the Select Board and Finance Committee work collaboratively with the School Committee
  10|                 and School Department to problem-solve in order to better meet the educational needs of the children of
  11|                 Lunenburg.
  12|                  Throughout the pandemic, the district has remained committed to making progress as outlined in on our
  13|                 Strategy for Continuous Improvement, which is aligned with our Mission and Vision:
  14|                 The Mission of the Lunenburg Public Schools is to provide all students with the skills, confidence, and
  15|                 passion for life-long learning so that they may find their own paths to successful careers, active citizenship,
  16|                 and rewarding lives.
  17|                  The Lunenburg Public Schools is committed to becoming a leader in education by focusing on its values of
  18|                 attending to the personal well-being of its students, providing high-quality curricula and student-centered
  19|                 instruction for deeper learning at all levels, and promoting the development of attributes and skills
  20|                 necessary for continuous learning for all.
  21|                 The Strategy is a living document which is reviewed annually and revised as needed. Annually in August,
  22|                 the District Leadership Team reviews the Strategy, its objectives and action plans. The Strategy objectives
  23|                  for the next three to four years are:
  24|                  Transforming Teaching and Learning: To ensure teaching and learning are student-centered and inclusive
  25|                 by: (1) establishing effective teams of educational leaders to accomplish the essential work for
  26|                 transforming practice, and (2) hiring and developing excellent educators who are continually learning and
  27|                  adapting practice to meet the needs of all students.
  28|                  Cultivating Climate & Culture: To create a culture that values data use and fosters continuous learning in a
  29|                  climate that is safe, caring, inclusive and reflective of our core values.
  30|                  Communication & Operations: To improve operational efficiencies, including communication, in order to
  31|                 increase the organization's capacity to execute the district's mission and vision.
  32|                  Equity, Access, Achievement & Opportunity for All Students: To ensure all students are provided equal
  33|                  access to a high-quality education, that is collaborative, interactive, and relevant with an appropriate leve!
  34|                  of support and challenge so that they can become well-rounded, resilient, confident, and capable young
  35|                  adults adequately prepared for future challenges.

  37|                  Improvement, the district continues to create teams to lead our work. Over the last three years we
  38|                  established and have maintained the Behavioral Health Advisory Council, Professional Development
  39|                  Committee, and Equity Advisory Council. During the 2020-2021 school-year we strengthened our general
  40|                  education academic referral process and created a behavioral health referral process. These teams review
  41|                  student cases referred by staff or administration and determine which academic or social/emotional/
  42|                  behavioral interventions may be needed. Intervention plans are developed, implemented and reviewed for
  43|                  impact and progress. In the spring of 2021, K-8 curriculum coordinator positions were created to assist

  12|                safely report to school as long as results were negative. This helped us keep more students in school for in-
  13|                 person learning.

  15|                 no available MCAS data. The district began administering these standardized assessments in October 2020

  17|                 administration to include grades 6-8. These assessments provide the schools valuable information which

   1|                                                                          School Facilities Department
   2|                After 23 years of dedicated service to the School District and Town, John Londa retired from the positon
   3|                Director of Facilities in October of 2021. As was the previous school year, the focus of school maintenance
   4|                and the Facilities Team is to support operating schools in a COVID-19 environment, preventative
   5|                maintenance on heating, ventilation and energy conservation measures and well as life safety.
   6|                COVID-19
   7|                With the school year welcoming in a global pandemic, the district prepared for opening by using tools and
   8|                lessons learned in 2020/2021. The Facilities department reinstalled partitions in school offices, cafeterias,
   9|                upgraded custodial equipment for disinfection, prepared supplies for the teaching environment such as
  10|                additional disinfectant spray bottles and microfiber wipers as well as using robust cleaning protocols to add
  11|                additional disinfection periods during and after the school day. Additionally the Facilities team provided
  12|                COVID-19 assistance with the distribution of masks and kits and testing operations as needed. Indoor air
  13|                 quality assessment and regular changing of air filters were completed and measures taken to confirm that the
  14|                ventilation systems were performing well against the original design.
  15|                Security Upgrades
  16|                 As part of ALICE safety training held on school campuses each school year, modifications were made on the
  17|                Primary and Turkey Hill schools' door hardware. This was needed to provide means to lock classroom and
  18|                office doors in the event of a lockdown other emergency requiring the locking of doors. Work was provided
  19|                by Oak Security.
  20|                Fire Alarm Upgrades
  21|                 Under a State grant for $75,000 the fire alarm devices and main fire alarm panel at the Primary School were
  22|                replaced. This allowed for the new smoke and CO2 detectors to be replaced in every classroom, offices,
  23|                mechanical and storage rooms and created a more consistent and reliable fire alarm signal. Work was provide
  24|                by Norel Fire Services.
  25|                 Water Testing
  26|                 After completing 3 years of district-wide water testing for lead and copper in the drinking water at all schools
  27|                in June 2020, lead was detected in a number of sources at Turkey Hill and the TC Passios building. The
  28|                source of the lead is believed to be solder from joining copper pipes. The teaching staff, parents and students
  29|                 have been made aware of the need to flush water sources at the start of the school day. A fixture replacement
  30|                 program was implemented to remove and replace fixtures as required. Follow up testing occurred at all
  31|                schools in June 2021. In the continued effort to improve water quality within the schools the Facilities
  32|                 department has started a State of Massachusetts grant application to replace existing drinking water stations
  33|                 under the SWIG program or School Water Improvement Grant. If approved the grant will allow up to $3000
  34|                per replacement cost per water station.
  35|                 Energy Savings
  36|                 As a Green Community Grant participant since 2013, the Town has benefited from various awards allowing
  37|                 upgraded energy efficient, Energy Star rated appliances at Turkey Hill Elementary at a cost of $15,092. A
  38|                 LED lighting conversion for Lunenburg Middle/High School was completed in February 2021 with
  39|                 $2,780.12. A replacement domestic hot water heater for the Primary School was complete by April 2021 with
  40|                 $9,900 in green community grant funds. The Facilities Department has applied for the next round of grant
  41|                 funding and will look to continue the effort to reduce cost and environmental impact.
  42|                 Capital Program
  43|                The FX 22 Capital program received funding to begin air conditioning design for Lunenburg Primary School. The subsequent assessment, preliminary design and cost estimate projected a cost of $232,738.31 to provide
  44|                 necessary electrical wiring and equipment to provide air conditioning to the school. The assessment and
  45|                 preliminary design was awarded to DiGiorgio Associates for $10,000.
  46|                 Respectfully Submitted,
  47|                 John C. Murphy, Director of Facilities & Grounds

   2|               Just like the preceding year, 2021 has continued to bring unexpected challenges and solutions related to the
   3|                health epidemic; our school district has adjusted and responded to those challenges to the best of our ability,
   4|                upon the advice of health and academic experts.
   5|               In late January of 2021 we moved to a live-streaming model. That meant that students were all fully remote
   6|                on Mondays, but could opt to attend in person for two days a week. Students who remained fully remote all
   7|                week "attended" class via a Google meet. There was a teacher at each grade who handled the fully remote
   8|                groups. At the start of April we moved to a full return to school model or a fully remote model, dropping the
   9|                hybrid model. The majority of students returned to school, fully masked. Stringent guidelines remained in
  10|                place: 6 feet of space when possible, masks even during recess time, desks and high touch surfaces frequently
  11|                wiped down, socially distanced lunchrooms, and scheduled mask and water breaks outside. We continue to
  12|                have hand sanitizer throughout the building and students use water filling stations for bottles, not water
  13|                bubblers. The number of students who could take the bus was limited, causing a significant increase in drop-
  14|                offs and pick-ups.
  15|                We canceled the fifth grade trip to Nature's Classroom in Beckett that is usually slated for October; however,
  16|                in May we invited Nature's Classroom staff to our school. They conducted several days of outdoor activities
  17|                with fifth graders.
  18|                We participated in the MCAS tests in April and May, per DESE's directive. All three tests (Math, ELA, and
  19|                science) were shortened to one session instead of two per subject. Remote students participated remotely,
  20|                using the Google Meet as a way to monitor the fidelity of the test taking.
  21|                In June the fifth grade celebrated their "completion ceremonies". Local public access television was kind
  22|                enough to record the events so parents could enjoy the assemblies via video.
  23|                All Chromebooks were collected during the last school week to be cleaned and repaired. Each grade level
  24|                held a field day outside for students that included fun activities, games, and picnic lunches. Fully remote
  25|                students were invited to join.
  26|                In late August we reopened to a full in-person model. There is no longer the option for a remote or hybrid
  27|                schedule. Students and staff are still obliged to wear masks inside the building, but they can be removed while
  28|                outside, including recess. We established six lunchtimes so all students can eat in the cafeteria while
  29|                maintaining six feet of distance. We also established three feet of space between desks to lessen the possibility
  30|                of "close contacts" in the classroom.
  31|                We made the decision to pilot a "no homework" policy this year. This is based on our experience last year,
  32|                during the COVID remote time, when there was no homework. Our intention is that students do their best
  33|                 work in school and use after school as an opportunity to be social with friends, take on a new sport or hobby,
  34|               . and not feel stressed about more worksheets or academic demands. We do encourage all students to spend
  35|                time each evening enjoying independent reading. We recognize that there is inequity for students who go
  36|                 home to a variety of situations some of which are more or less conducive to homework, and have chosen to
  37|                mitigate that obstacle. We will review our test scores at year's end to make a decision regarding next year.
  38|                Turkey Hill Elementary School currently has 363 students. There are six sections of grade 3, with a total of
  39|                134 students. There are five sections of grades 4 and grades 5, with totals of 113 students and 116 students
  40|                respectively. There are 26 teachers, 100% of whom are licensed in their assignment area. Our special areas
  41|                include physical education, art, music, technology/library and social emotional learning. We are fortunate to
  42|                have a full time assistant principal, two full time nurses, a psychologist, BCBA, and a full time adjustment
  43|                counselor/social worker.

   1|                When schools across the country shuttered in March of 2020, no one could imagine the long-term effects the
   2|                pandemic would have on our school systems. The summer of 2020 was spent purchasing and installing air filtration
   3|               systems and plexiglass barriers, sourcing mass quantities of PPE and working with local departments of public health
   4|               to determine how to safely bring students and staff into the building. When guidance from the state's Department of
   5|                Elementary and Secondary Education was released, school leaders were faced with rolling out a learning and
   6|               instruction model that had never been attempted before. We would offer a Hybrid Learning Model, and a rotating
   7|               daily schedule that would allow half of the student body to come into the building, while the other half would learn
   8|               Still, the beginning of the 2020-2021 school year was fraught with concern for student, faculty and staff safety and a
   9|               deep-rooted knowledge of how students learn best - in-person. School leaders worked tirelessly to develop a schedule
  10|               that would bring as many students back into the building as possible, safely, while attempting to balance academic and
  11|                vocational in-person learning time. Instructors were provided with additional technology supports and added
  12|                professional development days, to write and create curriculum that could be delivered remotely. This, in itself, would
  13|                be a challenge. How do you teach a student how to prepare and present a meal, or demonstrate fine-motor skills
  14|               required to give a haircut, in a virtual learning environment? How do you assess a student's understanding of
  15|               residential electrical wiring, without having the ability to observe and manipulate his/her work? How can you teach
  16|                students to safely restrain an animal, with no pet interaction? I am proud to say that the Monty Tech instructors rose to
  17|               this incredible challenge, and for much of the 2020-2021 school year, delivered an effective educational program,
  18|                using the resources that were available to them.
  19|                While face-to-face instruction was provided, that, too, looked and felt different to students and staff. On those days
  20|                when students were scheduled to attend in-person lessons, they found things had changed. Traffic patterns were
  21|                rerouted, and signage on floors, hallways and doors reminded them to wear a mask and maintain distance. Seating on
  22|                busses and in the cafeteria was assigned and closely monitored. A place that had always encouraged connection with
  23|                fellow classmates was now insisting on distance. Though it may have been uncomfortable for both students and staff,
  24|                the school wide adherence to these new rules seemed to be a quiet demonstration of their understanding. And on May
  25|                3, 2021, their compliance was rewarded - Monty Tech was allowed to return to full, in-person learning. With just
  26|                over a month remaining in the 2020-2021 school year, students and staff welcomed the change, anxious for the halls
  27|               and classrooms to return to a more normal capacity. While masks, social distancing and numerous hand sanitizing
  28|                stations were still present, the return of students into the building was a welcome sight for all.
  29|                In 2020-2021, it was disappointing that so many traditional annual events had to be cancelled, including VIP, Tour
  30|                Day, College Fair, and Career Awareness Night; however, where possible, the district continued to operate at a high
  31|               level. Examples include:
  32|                To help our faculty and staff return to work, daycare issues had to be addressed, and, as a result, an important
  33|               relationship with the Boys and Girls Club of Fitchburg and Leominster developed. The club provided daycare and
  34|                homework help, Monday through Friday, to school age children of Monty Tech faculty and staff.
  35|                Thanks to more than $670,000 in competitive grant awards, Monty Tech was able to renovate and expand the
  36|                welding/metal fabrication shop and retrofit one science lab. The school launched an all new evening training program
  37|                that provided no-cost training and job placement to qualified participants. With grant funds, we also established a
  38|                Safe and Supportive Schools Team to review school policies and procedures and target those in need of improvement,
  39|                with an overarching goal of providing all students with safe and appropriate learning environments.
  40|                In an effort to build connections with our school supporters and to "show off" the talents of our students, we held our
  41|                first Holiday Sale, which quickly sold out! More than 400 charcuterie trays, designed and handcrafted by our
  42|                 Cabinetmaking students were sold, and all proceeds went to the Class of 2021 Scholarship Fund.
  43|                The 2020-2021 school year was unexpected, challenging, and at times, disappointing, yet there were so many
  44|                moments to be proud of. I remain humbled and honored to lead such an outstanding organization, and thank you for
  45|                your continued support.

   1|               Vocational Projects in the District Communities
   2|                2020-2021 challenged vocational instructors to rewrite curriculum, so that any lesson may be delivered effectively
   3|               remotely. For so many years, the value of a Monty Tech vocational education has been our commitment to hands-on
   4|               learning, which, due to the pandemic and an inability to safely have all students in the building, was limited. However,
   5|               instructors across the school worked with local municipalities, community service agencies, and within the building to
   6|               find meaningful opportunities for students to demonstrate their technical skills and proficiency. While projects may
   7|                have been smaller and even taken a bit longer to complete in 2020-2021, their educational value was immense, and we
   8|                are forever grateful to our vocational instructors for their diligence in reimaging vocational education under these
   9|                challenging and unforeseen circumstances.
  10|               Advanced Manufacturing: Beginning 2020-2021, what was formerly known as Machine Technology would now be
  11|                called Advanced Manufacturing, a name that better aligns with local workforce needs, job titles and qualifications, and
  12|                may aide in job placement for qualified students and program graduates. In FY21, program instructors also adopted the
  13|                newly revised frameworks, and reviewed and developed curriculum to address any changes noted. 124 NC3
  14|                Certifications were earned by students, now skilled in Advanced & Precision Measuring Instrument (MI). Advanced
  15|                fusion CAD/CAM software training was completed, and Makerbot Method Carbon was also added to the training
  16|                program. All Freshman students successfully completed safety training, earning an OSHA 10-hour general industry
  17|                certification. Eight Seniors earned co-op placements, working and learning in area machine shops. (Total student
  18|               enrollment: 46)
  19|                Auto Body Collision Repair Technology: Monty Tech Auto Body students and instructors prioritized earning valuable
  20|               credentials in 2020-2021. Students earned the OSHA 10 hour general industry card, EPA 6H Rule spray certification, I
  21|               -Car Pro Level One in Refinishing and I-Car Pro Level One in Non-Structural Repair. Five Seniors also earned co-op
  22|                placements, and were reported to be valuable contributors in local repairs shops in the area. Due to the pandemic, the
  23|                program restricted shop work to that of Monty Tech staff, but remained surprisingly busy throughout the year. Students
  24|               performed quite well at the SkillsUSA District competition, earning a gold medal and a bronze in Collision Repair;
  25|                which several students for the State competition. (Total student enrollment: 62)
  26|                Automotive Technology: Automotive Technology students benefited from the addition of two new certification
  27|               opportunities (digital multimeters and scan tool applications) that were made available to students, using the newly
  28|                purchased Snap-On NC3 certified training equipment. These industry-recognized credentials and the new equipment
  29|                will enhance the students' level of competency within the automotive industry for years to come. Due to the pandemic,
  30|               hands-on learning opportunities and in-house repairs were limited to faculty, staff, school, and student vehicles only;
  31|                yet, surprisingly, the shop remained very busy. Students performed repairs that included transmission replacements,
  32|                intake manifolds, brake work, and various other technical repairs, demonstrating technical skills that are required for
  33|                any service technician in our area. Three Seniors and two Juniors earned co-op placements, while instructors continued
  34|               to utilize e-learning program options to keep the remaining students on track and engaged during the hybrid
  35|               schedule. (Total student enrollment: 66)
  36|                Business Technology: Monty Tech's Business Technology program continues to benefit from a unique partnership with
  37|                Workers Credit Union. The opportunity to participate in teller training and financial literacy workshops has been met
  38|                with great enthusiasm from students, who completed nine modules and earned a financial literacy
  39|               certificate. Instructors, in collaboration with their post-secondary colleagues, continue to refine and develop new.
  40|               articulation agreements. The agreement with Mount Wachusett Community College will provide qualified students with
  41|                college credits at no cost to the student. Students are eligible to receive 24 credits from Mount Wachusett Community
  42|                College. Unfortunately, due to the pandemic, the Gear House School Store and the Monty Tech Greenhouse were not
  43|                operational. Program instructors hope to reopen these important training facilities in the coming year, allowing all
  44|                freshmen, sophomores, and juniors to be successfully trained in the day-to-day operations of retail sales. Four Seniors
  45|                and three Juniors were out on co-op placements during the 2020-2021 school year. (Total student enrollment: 64)
