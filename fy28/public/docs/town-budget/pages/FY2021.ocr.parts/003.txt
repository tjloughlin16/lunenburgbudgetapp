  19|                  In the early 1990's with the structure in need of repair/rebuilding, the Lake Shirley Association turned
  20|                   over ownership of the dam to the Town. After completion of a total reconstruction, responsibilities for
  21|                  maintenance, monitoring, and record keeping was completed by a small number of volunteers from the
  22|                  Lake Association. In 2004-2005 custodianship of the dam, compliance with State dam regulations, the
  23|                   establishment of an operations manual as well as an emergency action plan was placed under the umbrella
  24|                  of the Department of Public Works. As with the storm water program and other EPA/DEP mandates,
  25|                  compliance with current and future regulations is critical. We rely on the expertise and support of a
  26|                   professional consultant to assist with predetermined mandates. The most recent inspection resulted in a
  27|                  positive report and we remain in total compliance.
  28|                  Winter Operations
  29|                  Snow and ice present a major challenge. The safety of the public depends on the ability of the DP W to
  30|                  maintain our roadways at a high level of safety despite the severity or the duration of snow and icing
  31|                  events. To that end, we have developed a winter operations procedure based upon a detailed plan with
  32|                   flexibility to accommodate the unpredictability of each storm. The expectation of immediate service is
  33|                  high and must be delivered to every part of Town in a timely manner.
  34|                   Departmental preparedness begins with successful implementation of the Capital Plan. Financial support
  35|                  is necessary to provide effective fleet rotation ensuring minimal interruption of service due to breakdowns
  36|                   and repairs. In the fall salt is delivered to the DPW facility. Lunenburg is in a consortium with 14 other
  37|                   communities in an effort to procure the lowest possible price. From late summer to early fall vehicle
  38|                  maintenance staff inspects and prepares the trucks, spreaders, and plows for winter operations. During the
  39|                   construction season road crews address right-of-way issues, overhanging branches, and pavement defects
  40|                  in order to eliminate obstacles to snow removal. Each division within the DPW is involved in winter
  41|                  operations. Typically, DPW staff combines with subcontractors to provide comprehensive snow and ice
  42|                  operations.
  43|                   Generally, it is not the amount of snow but the duration of the event that determines the overall cost. For
  44|                   example, a fast moving snow storm of 6-8 inches will cost significantly less than a long duration freezing
  45|                  rain event even though the accumulation is much less. Historically and as allowed by State law, the
  46|                   department's snow removal accounts operate in a deficit due to a yearly allocation that is below the cost
  47|                   of snow removal operations for an average winter.
  48|                   The Town and its residents are fortunate to have a dedicated group of DPW personnel and private
  49|                   contractors who tirelessly and successfully challenge the worst nature has to offer regardless of severity or
  50|                   duration.
