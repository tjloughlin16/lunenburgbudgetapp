   1|                                                                                 Board of Assessors
   2|                   The Board of Assessors is responsible for establishing the value of property, both real and personal, for
   3|                   taxation purposes for the town. Assessed values are subject to a five year revaluation and interim year
   4|                  adjustments for certification by the Department of Revenue. The Assessors grant all statutory exemptions
   5|                  for Senior and Surviving Spouse, Blind and Veterans. They act on all abatement applications to property
   6|                   values. The Assessors are responsible for defending all appeals to the Appellate Tax Board. They also
   7|                   administer all special assessments qualifying farm and forest management programs. Assessments in
   8|                   Massachusetts are based on full and fair cash value as of January 1st
   9|                   The Assessors analyze all sales for the prior 18 months to determine new values for the interim adjustments
  10|                   in the next fiscal year. Market value has seen increases in the sale of homes in town. Properties have been
  11|                   selling over the assessed valuations reflecting an overall increase to market value of the town. The next town
  12|                   wide recertification of all properties will be in FY 2024. Home sales have been strong and the town may
  13|                   experience some adjustments in valuations for FY 2023.
  14|                   Each property in town has a property record card on file with all the components that go into the valuation
  15|                   of the parcel such as land size, building description, sales information, etc. We recommend taxpayers
  16|                  review their property field card annually to ensure all the information is up to date and correct. The field
  17|                   cards are available in our office or online through the Assessor's Data on the Town website.
  18|                   The Department of Revenue certified the results of assessment data to be within their statistical
  19|                   requirements and the tax bills went out on time. The tax rate went from $18.12 to $17.74 for FY 2021.
  20|                   The office continues the process of mandated cyclical inspections of all properties throughout the town.
  21|                   This is achieved by inspections, visiting the properties, and by means of sales verification, building permits,
  22|                   occupancy permits and abatement inspections. The Board of Assessors strives to maintain accuracy in the
  23|                   assessment to sales ratio as well as assessment uniformity.
  24|                   The office staff strives to continue to provide good service that the Lunenburg taxpayers have come to
  25|                   expect.

   1|                                           ARCHITECTURAL PRESERVATION DISTRICT COMMISSION
   2|               In 2021, the APDC again focused its efforts primarily on the preservation of the old Primary School building.
   3|               Two workshops were held in conjunction with the Select Board to try to find compromise solutions between
   4|               preservation and future development on the site. Chairman McGrath investigated legal remedies for the
   5|               Town's refusal, through lack of Town Meeting appropriation, to fund maintenance for the building and sought
   6|               information on non-profit companies and developers that had experience developing properties like 30 School
   7|               St. The APDC also attracted the support of the Historical Commission in the effort. An application was
   8|               submitted to Mass Historic to include the old Primary School as inventory in the Lunenburg Historic District
   9|               in the National Register of Historic Places.
  10|               In other business, Certificates to Alter were approved for roof work on several contributing properties. The
  11|               commission advised the Town Manager on appropriate repairs to the Ritter Building and Town Hall. Damage
  12|               to the WWII bronze plaque in Memorial Park was assessed and repairs planned. A Facebook page was
  13|               launched to keep interested parties informed of recent developments. Road signs marking the Architectural
  14|               Preservation District perimeter were replaced and correctly relocated. Finally, the APD unsuccessfully
  15|               opposed Warrant Article 24 at the spring Annual Town Meeting, which allows the APDC's decisions
  16|               regarding town-owned property to be appealed by vote at Town Meeting.

  18|               The Building Department is located on the second floor of the Ritter Memorial Building located at 960

  20|               serves as the Building Commissioner as well as the Zoning Officer. Lisa Normandin serves as the
  21|                Administrative Assistant to the Building Commissioner. Jack Biery serves as the Electrical Inspector, and
  22|                Gary Williams serves as the Plumbing and Gas Inspector. The Building Department strives to ensure all work
  23|                done in the Town of Lunenburg is performed to state mandated regulations and codes, as well as local Zoning
  24|                By-Laws. This is to ensure that the buildings built or worked on and all other work done in town has a
  25|               minimum level of state regulated safety. In addition to Building, Plumbing, Gas, and Electrical Permits, the
  26|                Building Department also oversees annual inspections for places of assembly, safety inspections for liquor
  27|               serving establishments, and forms of intent required to issue business certificates from the Town Clerk.

   1|                                                                    CONSERVATION COMMISSION
   2|               The Conservation Commission is a seven member commission, appointed to 3 year terms by the Select Board,
   3|               whose duties are codified under the Conservation Commission Act MGL c. 40 § 8C. The Commission
   4|               enforces the Massachusetts Wetland Protection Act; MGL c. 131 § 40 and Chapter 239 of the local bylaws for
   5|               the protection of wetlands. The Commission also manages and controls town owned lands retained for
   6|                conservation and passive recreation use.
   7|               The year 2021 was as last year, one of a very busy and active agenda containing both public hearings and
   8|               meetings for a wide range of projects from commercial development, subdivisions and small single family
   9|               upkeep projects and larger scale industrial/commercial proposals despite the limitations of the continuing
  10|                COVID-19 Pandemic. The Conservation Commission's work continues into the year 2022 to implement the
  11|               recently updated land use policy. This effort was completed by spring of 2021. The Conservation Commission
  12|               continues to implement revisions to the local wetland regulations, which have been in effect for over two years
  13|               to date and have severed the interests of protecting valuable community natural resources very fairly and
  14|                effectively.
  15|                The Commission also held numerous inspections with the commission administrator on weekends prior to
  16|               meetings to familiarize themselves with the upcoming proposals that would appear on their agenda. The
  17|                Commission continues to work with the Town on familiarizing itself with the continually changing storm
  18|               water mandates from the EPA and working towards compliance both by procedure and with any necessary
  19|                codification as well as aiding with the community implementations the "MS4" permit regulations.
  20|                The Conservation Commission continues to implement forest stewardship plans using grants as outlined in the
  21|               last two annual reports. The upcoming management efforts will be concentrated on the RAYNO property on
  22|                Northfield Road and the Hawes property on Hollis Road. The stewardship plan for the Hollis Road property is
  23|                oriented toward enhancing bird wildlife habitat in conjunction with the Mass Audubon Society as part of the
  24|                DCR "Forestry for the Birds" program. The forest stewardship plans provide environmental management
  25|                plans which include timber stand management. The Commission retained Gary Gouldrup of New England
  26|                Forestry Consultants who is a noted forester with years of experience working in Lunenburg and the adjacent
  27|               towns/cities serving municipalities and private landowners. The Commission continues to hold public forums
  28|                on the implementation of its open space policies and timber-stand management. The Commission expects to
  29|                continue to apply for and receive funding from the Commonwealth of MA to author stewardship plans for our
  30|                 Conservation Areas.
  31|                The Commission was awarded and is implementing a Recreational Trails Program grant providing color coded
  32|                trail markers and coordinated "You Are Here" trail map signs to expand the safe use of Lunenburg's existing
  33|                trail network. Regional trail system map signs would show how trails are interconnected among multiple
  34|                properties. The project will also provide informational kiosks and emergency access gates. During 2021, trail
  35|                maps were developed for, and continue to be refined in detail, for eleven conservation properties. These trail
  36|                maps are posted on the new Conservation Commission Trails website, on informational kiosks on the
  37|                properties, and on "You Are Here" trail map signs at trail heads and trail intersections. The Commission
  38|                would like to express our gratitude for all of the hard working volunteers that work so enthusiastically in
  39|                executing the project.
  40|                The Commission has also continued to work closely with Lake Shirley Improvement Corporation and Hickory
  41|                Hills Landowners Inc. on the implementations of their lake management plans. These plans set goals and
  42|                action items designed to give a better picture of the overall health of the lakes and define various strategies to
  43|                 ensure continued protection of these valuable resources.

   1|                                                                 DEPARTMENT OF PUBLIC WORKS
   2|               The Department of Public Works combines the resources of the Highway, Parks, Cemetery, Sewer, Facilities
   3|               Departments, and Vehicle Maintenance services into one department organizing and scheduling personnel and
   4|               equipment to best serve the residents of the community through the effective delivery of an extensive scope of
   5|               services with a high degree of efficiency.
   6|                     "The mission of the Department of Public Works is to protect, preserve, and improve the Town's
   7|                    infrastructure and related assets in a manner which meets and exceeds the current and future social
   8|                    and economic needs of the community, to contribute to a healthy, safe and quality environment for the
   9|                     Town's residents and visitors, and to provide a cost effective and environmentally sound means of
  10|                     disposing of solid waste and wastewater. "
  11|               DEPARTMENT PHILOSOPHY
  12|               Honesty and integrity are absolutes. They characterize the only forum in which anything worthwhile can be
  13|               accomplished.
  14|               Efficiency: Make every effort to proceed correctly the first time. It may take a bit longer but will not require
  15|               repeated attempts.
  16|               Loyalty up and down the chain of command is essential to success.
  17|               Every employee is essential. Those at the top are support people. The real work is done by the men and
  18|               women who drive the trucks, operate the equipment, turn the wrenches, and provide a seamless flow of
  19|               administrative support.
  20|               DEPARTMENTAL OBJECTIVES
  21|               Public Works staff is either working or on call 24 hours a day 365 days a year prepared to respond to any
  22|               weather, infrastructure, roadway, or facility related emergency that may arise.
  23|               We strive to enhance the awareness of the value Public Works provides to our Town, and ensure others are
  24|                aware of our contribution. This is not for an expectation of praise, but rather to make sure efforts are not
  25|               dismissed as they merge into the fabric of our everyday expectation of service.
  26|               Major events, particularly in the form of devastating storms, over the past years have highlighted the fact that
  27|               Public Works has a seat at the table and is a major player in emergency management as first responders.
  28|               Town Officials, Police, and Fire acknowledge that Public Works is critical in order to facilitate the required
  29|                services of others. We must continue to build on this awareness.
  30|                This department must reach out to the public we serve, continuing to make them aware of the fact that the
  31|               common services they receive do not just happen, but rather are the efforts of a dedicated staff of individuals,
  32|               service companies, and vendors. Safe roadways, wastewater control, solid waste collection and recycling
  33|               operations, tree removal, storm water management, grounds maintenance, winter snow and ice removal,
  34|                cemeteries and parks are necessary services supporting everyone in the community. We will continue to
  35|                engage the public in order to set reasonable expectations and gather support for annual budget requests, capital
  36|               requests, and specific projects.

   9|                It had become a high priority goal of the Select Board as Road Commissioners to achieve a pavement
  10|                 condition of 72%-75% town-wide. While State supported Chapter 90 funds remain stagnant, the dedicated
  11|                Pavement Management line in the operational budget has grown substantially. Additionally, with the
  12|                infusion of funds facilitated by a four million dollar debt exclusion supported by residents, the
  13|                 Comprehensive Pavement Management Plan has been hugely successful providing smooth and safe
  14|                 transportation for our residents and all those travelling through our community.
  15|                 The debt exclusion funding combined with state and operational funds has nearly tripled the total paving
  16|                value of years past as we attack a nine-million-dollar backlog of repairs.
  17|                 Time spent addressing pot holes and other roadway deficiencies diminish substantially as new paving
  18|                increases. A clear benefit of the dollars expended. There still remains work to do, but expectations are

  23|                 Complete Streets Design to enhance walkability in the Town Center, with the expectation of the

  27|                 FLEET MAINTENANCE
  28|                 The DPW Maintenance Division is responsible for the repair and preventative maintenance to all town
  29|                 vehicles and equipment and is carried out by a single, skilled and dedicated fleet mechanic with support by
  30|                 department personnel. High performance police units, first responder fire and rescue apparatus, park,
  31|                 cemetery and school vehicles as well as over 30 pieces of highway related machinery are serviced by the
  32|                 Maintenance Division. Outsourcing of highly technical repairs has become more frequent and therefore
  33|                more costly. Due to the volatile global market, the overall cost of fuel and petroleum based products
  34|                 continues to be a moving target and therefore difficult to estimate annually.
  35|                 Use of a fleet maintenance software program continues to be a valuable tool. Each repair is logged by
  36|                 department and vehicle identification number and stored in a comprehensive database. Every vehicle can be
  37|                 referenced for part numbers, required services, as well and repair costs over the life of the equipment.
  38|                 Preventative maintenance continues to be a most valuable component of the program. Work will continue
  39|                 to update and evaluate a vehicle replacement schedule based upon American Public Works Association

  19|               Compliance with our EPA NDES MS4 Permit remains and will continue to remain an important function of

  21|               DPW works to meet the obligations of the permit and required reporting each year.

  23|               not limited to: catch basing cleaning, street sweeping, pipe jetting, pipe replacement and swale cleaning. The

  33|               request but must prioritize work based upon the perceived degree of danger to residents and property. With
  34|               the establishment of a Hazardous Tree Survey, a more efficient approach to defects has been established
  35|               through prioritization. Currently, the amount of work far exceeds the funding available for it. As this is the
  36|               case, the DP W will continue to act in a reactionary manner as needs arise. All work aside from brush
  37|               removal, chipping, and the felling of small diameter trees will continue to be outsourced due to the necessity

  41|                the Town by removing those trees that may cause an interruption of electrical service to the community.
