   1|                                                                                 In Memoriam
   2|                   Emily M. (Moore) Foster, 96, a longtime Lunenburg resident, passed away peacefully on June 28, 2021.
   3|                 From Cub Scout Den Mother to beloved Children's Librarian to Meals on Wheels volunteer to busy Senior
   4|                 Center member, Emily was active in the Lunenburg community, and enjoyed golf and bowling leagues and
   5|                  the "Bookworms" club. Emily loved her "Story Hour" kids from her 20+ years as the Children's Librarian
   6|                       of the Ritter Memorial Library in Lunenburg. After retirement, she became an active member of the
   7|                 Lunenburg Friendly Seniors and the Eagle House Senior Center. She also worked for years as a poll worker
   8|                            for elections in Lunenburg and assisted in the yard sales for the United Parish of Lunenburg.
   9|                                                                 Robert M. Herrick, "Mickey" Herrick. 73, passed away on May 9th,
  10|                                                                 2021, surrounded by his immediate family. He was a graduate of
  11|                                                                 Lunenburg High School during which time he was Vice President of his
  12|                                                                 class and an accomplished athlete. Mickey continued his love of sports
  13|                                                                 having played men's softball on numerous local teams throughout his life.
  14|                                                                 He truly cherished the lifelong friendships and memories that were made
  15|                                                                 while he was playing. Mickey was a prominent figure in the community
  16|                                                                 and a local business owner for over 45 years. He was most known for
  17|                                                                 "Mickey's Place,", a Lunenburg staple which served wonderful food and
  18|                                                                 spirits for over 28 years. When not cracking jokes across the bar, Mickey
  19|                                                                 could be found coaching T-ball, Little League and LHS Varsity Softball,
  20|                                                                 and serving on the Trustee Board over the years. He was a passionate
  21|                 sports fan and loved spending his free time falling asleep in his chair watching a Boston Red Sox or New
  22|                 England Patriots game, Howard Stern, or anything political. More than anything, Mickey loved to spend
  23|                 time with his wife, Kathleen, children and grandchildren.

   8|                                                                        AREA-26.63 SQUARE MILES
   9|                                                                          ROAD MILES—ABOUT 100
  10|                Lunenburg was settled in 1718 and incorporated in 1728. It occupies a land area of approximately 26.4
  11|                 square miles. Lunenburg is located in north central Massachusetts approximately 43 miles from Boston. It
  12|                is bordered on the west by the Town of Ashby and the City of Fitchburg, on the south by the Town of
  13|                 Lancaster and the City of Leominster, on the east by the Town of Shirley and on the north by the Town of
  14|                 Townsend.
  15|                 Local legislative decisions are made by an open town meeting consisting of all registered voters in the town.
  16|                 Subject to the legislative decisions made by town meeting, the affairs of the town are generally administered
  17|                 by a five member Select Board and a Town Manager. Local school affairs are administered by an elected
  18|                 seven member School Committee while local taxes are assessed by a three member Board of Assessors, all
  19|                 are appointed on an at-large basis for staggered three-year terms.
  20|                 Lunenburg is a semi-rural community located in northern Worcester County. Three water bodies account
  21|                 for somewhat of a clustered approach to the town's historical development. Lake Whalom and the former
  22|                 Whalom Amusement Park delineated the Whalom District from the remainder of Lunenburg. This district
  23|                 was developed as a housing base for many factory workers working in the mills and factories of Leominster
  24|                 and Fitchburg. In many ways the characteristics of the Whalom District closely reflect characteristics of
  25|                 these cities more than of the rest of Lunenburg. The Hickory Hills area around Hickory Hills Lake, was
  26|                 developed as a private community comprised largely of vacation cottages. The third body of water is
  27|                 Shirley Reservoir, a public water body also developed with mainly vacation cottages that have now been
  28|                 modified to year-round residences.
  29|                 Commercial and industrial development is permitted in several areas of town, primarily along the two major
  30|                 arteries (Routes 2A and 13) and the town's peripheries, due largely to the close proximity of Leominster's
  31|                 and Fitchburg's sewer and water systems. Although many farms and orchards had slowly developed into
  32|                 single family housing, farming continues on in Lunenburg. In 2013 the Annual Town Meeting voted to
  33|                 establish an Agricultural Commission to represent, preserve and enhance farming in the community and to
  34|                 administer the "Right to Farm" bylaw.
  35|                 The 2015 Lunenburg Town Meeting established the Architectural Preservation District ("District") bylaw to
  36|                 regulate construction, demolition or renovation of existing "Contributing Property" structures as defined by
  37|                 the bylaw within an established bounded district. The bylaw was introduced at Town Meeting along with
  38|                 the Village District Bylaw, which created an overlay zoning district to encourage mixed-use commercial and
  39|                 residential development. While the Village District Bylaw encourages the economic vitalization of
  40|                 downtown Lunenburg, the Architectural Preservation District Bylaw ensures that it will not come at the
  41|                 expense of the town's historical character and integrity.

   1|                                                                 REPORT OF THE TOWN MANAGER
   2|                                                                                FISCAL YEAR 2021
   3|               Like many others, there was hope going into fiscal year 2021 that COVID-19 would start to become a thing of
   4|                the past. However, after over a year into the pandemic, it was still a challenge that we had to surmount while
   5|               providing services to our residents and simultaneously keeping everyone safe. There wasn't a department that
   6|                was immune to COVID- whether it was being a "close contact", having to quarantine or isolate became a
   7|               regular occurrence.
   8|                Despite the lingering challenge that COVID-19 continued to pose in continuity of services, fiscal year 2021
   9|               was a year of progress. Below I would like to highlight the topics that were addressed and the
  10|               accomplishments within the Office of the Town Manager:
  11|               • In July of 2020, as part of the annual goal setting process, the Selectmen reviewed the Town Manager's
  12|                     goals from FY20 and voted to accept the Town Manager's Goals for FY21. The Town Manager's goals
  13|                     for FY 2021 included continuing to take the necessary steps towards the redevelopment/reuse of 925
  14|                     Massachusetts Avenue, looking at the feasibility of creating an independent facilities department and
  15|                     improve the long term maintenance and repair plan for town facilities, and succession planning. Progress
  16|                     was made on different action items and two of the goals have carried forward into FY 2022.
  17|               • In regards to 925 Massachusetts Avenue, the Town Manager continued to work with the BETA Group on
  18|                     environmental site assessments for that property and with MassDevelopment on any possible funding
  19|                     available to clean-up the property. During FY 2021 the Phase II environmental site assessment was
  20|                     completed and the Town Manager requested additional funding for a Phase III environmental site
  21|                     assessment that would identify the costs of cleanup of the identified hazardous materials and removal of
  22|                     underground tanks. The Town Manager also applied for a Brownfields Redevelopment Fund grant for
  23|                      $250,000 in September of 2020, but unfortunately this property did not yet qualify for that funding. The
  24|                      Town Manager also worked with the DPW Director to hire a surveyor to do survey the property.
  25|                • As it related to one of the action items under the Town Manager's Annual Goals for Facilities, in April the
  26|                      Town Manager announced that a new Facilities Management software called eMaint would be utilized for
  27|                      facility work orders, scheduling preventative maintenance, and overall asset management. This was paid
  28|                      for by a grant through our insurance carrier.
  29|                • The Select Board finalized their Goals for FY 2021 in September of 2020. One of the Select Board goals
  30|                      for FY 2021 was to identify, define and propose at least one project to the 2021 Annual Town Meeting
  31|                     regarding the use and/or re-use of the Old Primary School building, and/or the land on which it sits. The
  32|                      Board held a site visit to the property at the end of October 2020 to review the property and boundaries. In
  33|                      January the Board met with the Architectural Preservation District Commission and the Historical
  34|                      Commission about the Select Board plans to see happen with that property and how to navigate a path
  35|                      forward. The Town Manager and the Select Board worked with Monty Tech on conceptual drawings to
  36|                      demolish the existing old Primary School building and create an a passive recreation/open space area for
  37|                      the Farmers Market, band concerts and other community events. At the May 2021 Annual Town Meeting
  38|                      the Town approved funding to hire a Landscape Architect to prepare conceptual plans for this site and a
  39|                      cost estimate for the overall project.
  40|                 • Another one of the Select Board goals for FY 2021 was to review, discuss and revise (where needed) the
  41|                      re-appointment process of all annual Select Board appointments. In February the Board held a workshop
  42|                      to formalize their process into an appointment policy. Ultimately the Board mostly deferred to the original
  43|                      Annual Select Board Appointment Process but defined the timeline and notifying chairs of the committees
  44|                      of persons wishing to be appointed/reappointed.

   1|                • Fiscal Year 2021 was also a year for retirements for two long serving town employees, Police Chief
   2|                     James Marino and DPW Director Jack Rodriquenz. Chief Marino retired on July 31st after working for
   3|                     the Town of Lunenburg for over forty years. On October 30th, Jack Rodriquenz retired after over twenty
   4|                      five years working for the Town. The Town was fortunate to have both Jim and Jack leading the Police
   5|                      and DPW Departments, respectively and wish them all the best in their retirement. Lieutenant Thomas
   6|                      Gammel was appointed the Acting Police Chief in August and after conducting a search to fill the
   7|                      position, was selected to fill the position permanently in October. Similarly, the DPW Director position
   8|                      was posted in September and after conducting a search the Town Manager appointed Robert Oliva to fill
   9|                     the position at the beginning of November.
  10|                • Also in July the Select Board met with Sean O'Neill of Bright Farms (MA Greenhouse LLC) relative to a
  11|                      proposed major greenhouse that they were looking to build on the land behind Walmart on Massachusetts
  12|                      Avenue and their request for a Tax Increment Financing (TIF) Agreement. The proposal included an
  13|                     initial investment of $10 million and the creation of jobs that would be offered first to Lunenburg
  14|                      residents. After negotiating with Bright Farms over the terms of a TIF agreement, this was voted upon
  15|                      favorably at the November Special Town Meeting. The final TIF provided a 10 year tax discount to the
  16|                      company with a 100% discount in real estate taxes on the new construction in year 1 and decreased by
  17|                      10% each year of the agreement until year 10 where the Town would be receiving 90% of the real estate
  18|                      taxes. In Year 11 Bright Farms would be required to pay the full tax assessment. At the November 2020
  19|                      Special Town Meeting the Town approved the TIF agreement as presented. Ultimately the corporate
  20|                      offices of Bright Farms chose another location that better fit their business model and the project did not
  21|                      move forward.
  22|                • At the beginning of this fiscal year the Town Manager reported that she worked with the Open Space
  23|                      Chairman, the Finance Director, DPW, and Conservation Administrator to ensure that all requirements of
  24|                      the PARC and LAND Grants to purchase the Saliba property were done in order to close on the property
  25|                      in time to submit for reimbursement of these grants. The Town Manager submitted all the required
  26|                      documentation for the LAND and PARC grants, totaling $500,000, to ensure the Town received this
  27|                      reimbursement to purchase this park and open space property.
  28|                • The Town Manager and Select Board conducted an annual review of the Select Board's policies in
  29|                      August and finalized the review in September.
  30|                • This year was another productive year for infrastructure projects in Lunenburg. Besides the continuation
  31|                      of the $4M Pavement Management Plan on local roads, the Town began the reconstruction of the Pleasant
  32|                      Street Bridge that was completed in the fall of 2021. As part of paving in the area of Whalom Road, the
  33|                      ADA accessible ramps were incorporated. The Complete Streets design work was finalized during the
  34|                      fiscal year and the construction portion went out to bid in March of 2021. This project wrapped up in the
  35|                      fall of 2021. The Town Manager also worked with the DPW Director on continuing the discussions about
  36|                      the drainage issues on Flat Hill Road and engaging McCarty Engineering to look at drainage solutions.
  37|                      On another section of Flat Hill Road, a culvert project that had received funding in the FY21 Capital Plan
  38|                      had to be delayed due to a revised estimated construction cost for the project due to rising construction
  39|                      and materials cost due to the pandemic that increased the total cost from $428,000 to $750,000. The Town
  40|                      Manager and the DPW Director worked with the engineer to apply for the Small Bridge Grant Program.
  41|                      and seek earmark requests from the State. To date the Town is waiting on the whether the Small Bridge
  42|                      Grant application that was submitted will be approved for funding.

  44|                      Density Bonus Payment per MGL c. 40R (Smart Growth Zoning District) from the Department of
  45|                      Housing and Community Development. These funds would close out at the end of FY21 and positively
  46|                      impact the certified free cash available in FY22.
