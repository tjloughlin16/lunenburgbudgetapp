   5|             Lunenburg was settled in 1718 and incorporated in 1728. It occupies a land area of approximately 26.4
   6|             square miles. Lunenburg is located in north central Massachusetts approximately 43 miles from Boston. It
   7|             is bordered on the west by the Town of Ashby and the City of Fitchburg, on the south by the Town of
   8|             Lancaster and the City of Leominster, on the east by the Town of Shirley and on the north by the Town of
   9|             Townsend.
  10|             Local legislative decisions are made by an open town meeting consisting of all registered voters in the town.
  11|             Subject to the legislative decisions made by town meeting, the affairs of the town are generally administered
  12|             by a five member Select Board and a Town Manager. Local school affairs are administered by an elected
  13|             seven member School Committee while local taxes are assessed by a three member Board of Assessors, all
  14|             are appointed on an at-large basis for staggered three-year terms.
  15|             Lunenburg is a semi-rural community located in northern Worcester County. Three water bodies account
  16|             for somewhat of a clustered approach to the town's historical development. Lake Whalom and the former
  17|             Whalom Amusement Park delineated the Whalom District from the remainder of Lunenburg. This district
  18|             was developed as a housing base for many factory workers working in the mills and factories of Leominster
  19|             and Fitchburg. In many ways the characteristics of the Whalom District closely reflect characteristics of
  20|             these cities more than of the rest of Lunenburg. The Hickory Hills area around Hickory Hills Lake, was
  21|             developed as a private community comprised largely of vacation cottages. The third body of water is
  22|             Shirley Reservoir, a public water body also developed with mainly vacation cottages that have now been
  23|             modified to year-round residences.
  24|             Commercial and industrial development is permitted in several areas of town, primarily along the two major
  25|             arteries (Routes 2A and 13) and the town's peripheries, due largely to the close proximity of Leominster's
  26|             and Fitchburg's sewer and water systems. Although many farms and orchards had slowly developed into
  27|             single family housing, farming continues on in Lunenburg. In 2013 the Annual Town Meeting voted to
  28|             establish an Agricultural Commission to represent, preserve and enhance farming in the community and to
  29|             administer the "Right to Farm" bylaw.
  30|             The 2015 Lunenburg Town Meeting established the Architectural Preservation District ("District") bylaw to
  31|             regulate construction, demolition or renovation of existing "Contributing Property" structures as defined by
  32|               he Village District Bylaw.
  33|             residential development. While the Village District Bylaw encourages the economic vitalization of
  34|             downtown Lunenburg, the Architectural Preservation District Bylaw ensures that it will not come at the
  35|             expense of the town's historical character and integrity.

   3|            • In July of 2020, as part of the annual goal setting process, the Selectmen reviewed the Town Manager's
   4|                goals from FY20 and voted to accept the Town Manager's Goals for FY21. The Town Manager's goals
   5|                for FY 2021 included continuing to take the necessary steps towards the redevelopment/reuse of 925
   6|                 Massachusetts Avenue, looking at the feasibility of creating an independent facilities department and
   7|                improve the long term maintenance and repair plan for town facilities, and succession planning. Progress
   8|                 was made on different action items and two of the goals have carried forward into FY 2022.
   9|            • In regards to 925 Massachusetts Avenue, the Town Manager continued to work with the BETA Group on
  10|                 environmental site assessments for that property and with MassDevelopment on any possible funding
  11|                 available to clean-up the property. During FY 2021 the Phase II environmental site assessment was
  12|                 completed and the Town Manager requested additional funding for a Phase III environmental site
  13|                 assessment that would identify the costs of cleanup of the identified hazardous materials and removal of
  14|                 underground tanks. The Town Manager also applied for a Brownfields Redevelopment Fund grant for
  15|                 $250,000 in September of 2020, but unfortunately this property did not yet qualify for that funding. The
  16|                 Town Manager also worked with the DPW Director to hire a surveyor to do survey the property.
  17|            • As it related to one of the action items under the Town Manager's Annual Goals for Facilities, in April the
  18|                 Town Manager announced that a new Facilities Management software called eMaint would be utilized for
  19|                 facility work orders, scheduling preventative maintenance, and overall asset management. This was paid
  20|                 for by a grant through our insurance carrier.
  21|            • The Select Board finalized their Goals for FY 2021 in September of 2020. One of the Select Board goals
  22|                 for FY 2021 was to identify, define and propose at least one project to the 2021 Annual Town Meeting
  23|                 regarding the use and/or re-use of the Old Primary School building, and/or the land on which it sits. The
  24|                 Board held a site visit to the property at the end of October 2020 to review the property and boundaries. In
  25|                 January the Board met with the Architectural Preservation District Commission and the Historical
  26|                 Commission about the Select Board plans to see happen with that property and how to navigate a path
  27|                 forward. The Town Manager and the Select Board worked with Monty Tech on conceptual drawings to
  28|                 demolish the existing old Primary School building and create an a passive recreation/open space area for
  29|                 the Farmers Market, band concerts and other community events. At the May 2021 Annual Town Meeting
  30|                 the Town approved funding to hire a Landscape Architect to prepare conceptual plans for this site and a
  31|                 cost estimate for the overall project.
  32|             • Another one of the Select Board goals for FY 2021 was to review, discuss, and revise (where needed) the
  33|                 re-appointment process of all annual Select Board appointments. In February the Board held a workshop
  34|                 to formalize their process into an appointment policy. Ultimately the Board mostly deferred to the original
  35|                 Annual Select Board Appointment Process but defined the timeline and notifying chairs of the committees
