   9|                                  Snow and ice present a major challenge to our Town. The safety of the general public
  10|                       depends on the ability of the DPW to maintain our roadways to a high level of safety despite the
  11|                       severity or the duration of snow and ice events. To that end we have developed a winter operations
  12|                       procedure based upon a detailed plan with a flexibility to accommodate the unpredictability of
  13|                       each storm. The expectation of immediate service is high and must be delivered to every part of
  14|                       Town in a timely manner.
  15|                                  Departmental preparedness begins with successful implementation of the capital plan.
  16|                       Financial support is necessary to provide effective fleet rotation insuring minimal interruption of
  17|                       services due to breakdowns and repairs. In the fall, material is delivered to the Chase Rd. facility.
  18|                       Lunenburg is in a consortium with 14 other communities in an effort to procure the lowest possible
  19|                       price for road salt, an absolute necessity. From late summer to early fall vehicle maintenance staff
  20|                       inspects and prepares the trucks, spreaders and plows for winter operations. During the
  21|                       construction season road crews address right-of-way issues, overhanging branches, and pavement
  22|                       defects in order to eliminate obstacles to snow removal. Each division within the DPW is involved
  23|                       in winter operations. Typically, DPW staff combines with up to 18 subcontractors to provide
  24|                       service.
  25|                                  Generally, it is not the amount of snow but the duration of the event that determines the
  26|                       overall cost. For example, a fast moving snow storm of 6-8 inches will cost significantly less than
  27|                       a long duration freezing rain event even though the accumulation is much less. Historically, the
  28|                       department operates in a deficit due to a yearly allocation that is below the cost of an average
  29|                       winter.

  10|                       The Town also remains committed to incorporating steady increases (tied to motor vehicle excise tax increases) in the
  11|                       Pavement Management Plan to ensure that recent improvements paid for through debt exclusion will be adequately
  12|                       maintained going forward. $50,000 of the DPW budget increase addresses this.
  13|                        As we attempt to manage costs associated with the roads, the Town has also begun to set aside monies for other
  14|                       infrastructure and building needs by establishing and funding a Special Purpose Stabilization Fund. This budget deposits
  15|                       $200,000 into that fund.

  17|                       year. This makes room for short term borrowing within the general budget to help replace the aging ladder truck, which
  18|                       has been on the Capital Planning radar for quite some time. The new debt will not hit the budget until 2020, so is not
  19|                       reflected in the budget presented here, but is planned for going forward.

  21|                       low. This year, the increase in premiums is coming in at about 7 percent, which affects both the 'Unclassified' portion of

  28|                       notes to the Town Financial Statements as an 'On Behalf' payment. Non licensed school personnel and Town side

  30|                       employer contribution for active employees and an assessment to fund the 'Unfunded Liability' of future payments to
  31|                       retirees. It is the future liability assessment that is driving the increase.
