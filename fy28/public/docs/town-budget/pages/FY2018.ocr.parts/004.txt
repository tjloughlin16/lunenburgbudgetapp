   2|                                                                        Superintendent's Message
   3|                          It is a pleasure for me to present my first annual report for the Lunenburg Public Schools.
   4|                          This report reflects some of what I have learned about the promises and needs of a district I
   5|                         am proud to lead. During the last 6 months, I have been executing my Entry Plan. I have
   6|                          conducted forums and one-on-one interviews. I have reviewed many documents. I have at-
   7|                         tended numerous school and community events and activities. I have been able to observe
   8|                          the district processes and procedures, tour the facilities and grounds, and most importantly,
   9|                         spend time in classrooms observing teaching and learning. While there is much to be proud
  10|                          of, there is also good work to be done to improve the efficiency of operations, strengthen
  11|                         curriculum and instruction, and support all learners.
  12|                         I want to acknowledge that this community has demonstrated its support for the schools by
  13|                         supporting two building projects in the last 15 years. The Lunenburg Primary School and
  14|                         Lunenburg Middle/High School are beautiful, modern facilities for teaching and learning.
  15|                         A modern facility is; however, but one factor in providing students with a quality education.
  16|                         Rigorous programming designed to meet the academic, social and emotional needs of all of
  17|                         our students is foundational to a quality learning experience. In addition to meeting a stu-
  18|                         dent's basic needs, we must ensure that the curriculum is well-designed; materials are avail-
  19|                         able to implement that curriculum; and that teachers have the knowledge and skills requisite
  20|                         for effective instructional practice. All of this must be supported by the use of data to assess
  21|                         and monitor student learning. Today's classrooms also require the seamless integration of
  22|                         instructional technology to create engaging lessons, provide differentiated instruction and
  23|                         assessment, and facilitate analysis of achievement data to monitor the growth and support
  24|                         the success of all of our students.
  25|                         I also want to acknowledge that I have experienced a collaborative approach to planning and
  26|                         problem-solving with the Town Manager, Heather Lemieux. I am very grateful for that. I
  27|                         appreciate the effort on the part of the School Committee and the Board of Selectmen to
  28|                         strengthen communication and find opportunities to work together. I trust that, working
  29|                         together, we will be able to find ways to meet the needs of the children of Lunenburg.
  30|                         Through my transition, the district continues to be focused on implementation of the District
  31|                         Strategic Plan (2018-2023). Our Strategic Goals are:
  32|                         Goal 1: Social-emotional learning, prevention programs and secure schools provide safe
  33|                         environments, promote kindness and celebrate cultural differences.
  34|                          Goal 2: Civic learning and engagement enable all students to become knowledgeable citi-
  35|                          zens and active community members.
  36|                          Goal 3: Curricula and assessments in major subject areas are aligned with recent Massa-
  37|                         chusetts State Frameworks and fully implemented.
  38|                          Goal 4: Implementation of digital literacy and computer science curriculum is expanded;
  39|                         and, instructional technologies are more available.
  40|                          Goal 5: The processes for annual professional development ensure opportunities for well-
  41|                         targeted individual, school-based, and district-wide professional growth.
  42|                          Goal 6: Strategies to improve instructional support for struggling learners are enhanced.
  43|                          Goal 7: Enrichment opportunities are expanded to include the arts and other areas of high
  44|                         student interest.
  45|                          Goal 8: Collaboration and communications within and among the schools and the commu-
  46|                         nity are improved.

   9|                          The MCAS data analysis conducted at the district leadership level indicates a need for con-

  15|                          This year DESE implemented a new accountability system. The new system still allows the
  16|                          DESE to identify schools requiring assistance or intervention, as well as those demonstrating
  17|                          success. Highlights of the new system include: (1) the inclusion of additional accountability
  18|                          indicators; (2) a focus on raising the performance of each district's or school's lowest per-
  19|                          forming students in addition to the performance of the district or school as a whole; and (3)
  20|                          the discontinuation of accountability and assistance levels (Levels 1 to 5).
  21|                          Under the new accountability system, annual performance determinations for districts and
  22|                          schools will be calculated using Indicators: English language arts (ELA), Mathematics, and
  23|                          Science achievement; ELA and Mathematics mean student growth percentile (SGP); four-
  24|                          year cohort graduation rate, extended engagement rate, annual dropout rate; student progress
  25|                          towards attaining English language proficiency; chronic absenteeism, percentage of 11th &
  26|                          12th graders completing advanced coursework.
  27|                          Indicators are weighted. Substantial weight for achievement, growth, English language pro-
  28|                          ficiency, and graduation rate indicators are required by federal law. Federal law also requires
  29|                          that these indicators combined should have much greater weight than the additional indica-
  30|                          tors. Under the new accountability system, the weighting of achievement and growth is based
  31|                          on ratios instead of percentages, maintaining the achievement to growth ratio of 3 to 1.
