   2|             The Board of Assessors is responsible for establishing the value of property, both real and personal, for

   4|             adjustments for certification by the Department of Revenue. The Assessors grant all statutory exemptions for

   7|             special assessments qualifying farm and forest management programs. Assessments in Massachusetts are
   8|             based on full and fair cash value as of January 1st.

  11|             market value of the town. The next town wide recertification will occur this year for FY 2024.

  13|             the parcel - such as land size, building description, sales information, etc. We recommend that the taxpayers

   2|               The Town of Lunenburg Conservation Commission is a seven member commission, appointed to three year terms by
   3|              the Select Board, whose duties are codified under the Conservation Commission Act MGL c. 40, § 8C. The
   4|               Commission enforces the Massachusetts Wetland Protection Act MGL c. 131, § 40 and Chapter 239 of the local bylaws
   5|               for the protection of wetlands. The Commission also manages and controls town-owned lands retained for conservation
   6|               and passive recreation use.
   7|              The year 2022 was as last year, one of a very busy and active agenda containing both public hearings and meetings for a
   8|              wide range of projects from commercial development, subdivisions and small single family upkeep projects and larger
   9|               scale industrial/commercial proposals.
  10|               The Commission held numerous inspections with the commission administrator on weekends prior to meetings to
  11|               familiarize themselves with the upcoming proposals that would appear on their agenda. The Commission continues to
  12|              work with the Town on familiarizing itself with the continually changing storm water mandates from the EPA and
  13|              working towards compliance both by procedure and with any necessary codification as well as aiding with the
  14|              community implementations the "MS4" regulations.
  15|               The Conservation Commission continues to implement forest stewardship plans developed using grants as outlined in
  16|              the last two annual reports. The upcoming management efforts will be concentrated on the Rayno property on
  17|              Northfield Road and the Hawes property on Hollis Road. The stewardship plan for the Hollis Road property is oriented
  18|              toward enhancing bird wildlife habitat in conjunction with the Mass Audubon Society as part of the DCR "Forestry for
  19|              the Birds" program. The forest stewardship plans which provide environmental management including timber-stand
  20|              management were developed by Gary Gouldrup of New England Forestry Consultants. The Commission continues to
  21|              hold public forums on the implementation of its open space policies and timber-stand management. The funding from
  22|              the sale of harvested timber provides the seed money to make it possible for the Commission to apply for grants to
  23|              The Commission continues to implement a Recreational Trails Program grant providing color-coded trail markers and
  24|              coordinated "You Are Here" trail map signs to expand the safe use of Lunenburg's existing trail network. Regional
  25|              trail system map signs show how trails are interconnected among multiple properties. The project also provides
  26|              informational kiosks and emergency access gates. During 2022 trail maps were developed for and continue to be
  27|              refined in detail for eleven conservation properties. These trail maps are posted on the new Conservation Commission
  28|              Trails website, on informational kiosks on the properties, and on "You Are Here" trail map signs at trail heads and trail
  29|              intersections. The Commission would like to express our gratitude for all the hard-working volunteers that work so
  30|              enthusiastically in maintaining and improving Lunenburg's conservation lands. The Lunenburg Snow Riders continue
  31|              to support the maintenance and improvement of the trail system on Lunenburg's conservation lands. The club replaced

  33|               five new trail head kiosks, improve the cart paths on the Lane Conservation Area, level and de-stump Small Town

  11|             Departmental Mission:
  12|             The mission of the Department of Public Works is to provide high quality, cost effective, efficient, and safe services to
  13|             protect, preserve and improve the Town's roadway infrastructure and related assets in a manner which meets and
  14|             enhances the current and future social and economic needs of the community; to contribute to a healthy, safe and quality
  15|             environment for the Town's citizens and visitors; and to provide environmentally sound means of solid waste and
  16|             wastewater disposal.
  17|             Departmental Goals and Objectives
  18|             • Be prepared to respond to any weather, roadway infrastructure, or accident-related emergency that may arise on a
  19|                   twenty-four hour per day, three hundred and sixty-five day per year basis.
  20|             • Maintain and enhance a highly qualified and dedicated staff of public works professionals.
  21|             • Provide preventative maintenance in a proactive manner to reduce reactive maintenance as follows:
  22|             • Maintain and update the sewer system and associated pumping stations.
  23|             • Maintain and repair roadways in accordance with the new (2022) pavement management plan.
  24|             • Maintain and repair sidewalks in accordance with the 2020 PROW ADA Transition plan.
  25|             • Increase the number of roadsides mowed by developing a roadside maintenance plan.
  26|             • Increase the number of catch basin and stormwater structures cleaned and the number of miles of roadways swept
  27|                   per the June 2020 Operations and Maintenance Plan prepared by Comprehensive Environmental, Inc.
  28|             • Establish a comprehensive evaluation of bridges and culverts, and prioritize by condition, use, and plans for
  29|                   rehabilitation.
  30|             • Increase training offered by our insurance provider to obtain credits to lessen premiums.
  31|             • Identify new sources (Federal, State, and other grants) of funding to supplement the operational budget.
  32|             • Continue to maintain a high level of courteous communication and productive interaction between members of
  33|                   boards and commissions as well as Town residents.
  34|             • Strive to modernize the operations of the Department by obtaining and using the most up to date equipment and
  35|                   methods, including asset management.
