   8|                                                                     TOTAL AREA- 26.63 MILES
   9|               Lunenburg was settled in 1718 and incorporated in 1728. It occupies a land area of approximately 26.4
  10|               square miles. Lunenburg is located in north central Massachusetts approximately 43 miles from
  11|               Boston, It is bordered on the west by the Town of Ashby and the City of Fitchburg, on the south by the
  12|               Town of Lancaster and the City of Leominster, on the east by the Town of Shirley and on the north by
  13|               the Town of Townsend
  14|                Local legislative decisions are made by an open town meeting consisting of all registered voters in
  15|               the town. Subject to the legislative decisions made by town meeting, the affairs of the town are
  16|                generally administered by an elected five member Select Board and a Town Manager, Local school
  17|                affairs arc administered by an elected seven member School Committee while local taxes are
  18|               assessed by an elected three member Board of Assessors.
  19|                Lunenburg is a semi-rural community located in northern Worcester County. Lake Whalom and the
  20|                former Whalom Amusement Park delineated the Whalom District from the remainder of Lunenburg. This
  21|                district was developed as a housing base for many factory workers working in the mills and factories of
  22|                Leominster and Fitchburg. In many ways the characteristics of the Whalom District closely reflect
  23|                characteristics of these cities more than of the rest of Lunenburg. The Hickory Hills Lake area was
  24|                developed as a private community comprised largely of vacation cottages. Shirley Reservoir is a public
  25|                water body also developed with mainly vacation cottages that have now been modified to year-round
  26|                residences.
  27|                Commercial and industrial development is permitted in several areas of town, primarily along the two
  28|                major arteries (Routes 2A and 13) and the town's peripheries, due largely to the close proximity of
  29|                Leominster's and Fitchburg's sewer and water systems. Although many farms and orchards had     sigh many la m and orchard hay of
  30|                 slowly developed into single family housing, farming continues on in Lunenburg, In 2013 the Annual
  31|                 own Meeting voted to establish an Agricultural Commission to represent, preserve and enhance
  32|                farming in the community and to administer the "Right to Farm" bylaw,
  33|                 The 2015 Lunenburg Town Meeting established the Architectural Preservation District ("District")
  34|                 at Town Meeting along with the Village District Bylaw, which created an overlay zoning district to ry the bylay notin an entrated buddie the byly wary
  35|                 encourage mixed-use commercial and residential development. While the Village District Bylaw
  36|                 encourages the economic vitalization of downtown Lunenburg, the Architectural Preservation
  37|                 District Bylaw ensures that it will not come at the expense of the town's historical character and
  38|                integrity.

   2|                                                                                             FY22
   3|               It is my pleasure to submit the annual report on the activities of the Office of the Town Manager during
   4|               Fiscal Year 2022. Fiscal Year 2022 started off like most other years however, there were lingering after
   5|               effects of the pandemic. The pandemic brought about a shift in the employment marketplace, causing
   6|               vacancies to last longer and a smaller applicant pool to fill positions. The State extended the allowance of
   7|               virtual public meetings, which most boards and committees continued to use as a matter of convenience and,
   8|               at times, for personal health reasons. The federal stimulus funds that were part of the American Rescue Plan
   9|                Act made their way down to cities and towns and this would result in many discussions in how best to use
  10|               these windfall monies. Driver shortages, supply chain issues, and increased demand for items disrupted our
  11|               Pay As You Throw program, severely impacted the delivery schedule of vehicles/equipment the Department
  12|               of Public Works, Fire Department, and Police Department ordered and spiked costs for construction projects.
  13|               Despite the ripple effects of the pandemic, the Town continued to provide a high level of services and was
  14|               another year of progress. Below I would like to highlight the topics that were addressed and the
  15|               accomplishments within the Office of the Town Manager:
  16|               • In July of 2021, as part of the annual goal setting process, the Selectmen reviewed and voted to accept the
  17|                     Town Manager's annual goals for FY22. The Town Manager's goals for FY22 included continuing to
  18|                     take the necessary steps towards the redevelopment/reuse of 925 Massachusetts Avenue, working with
  19|                     the Select Board on creating a means tested senior citizen tax relief program, and action items related to
  20|                     enhancing facilities maintenance and management. A majority of the action items related to these three
  21|                     goals were completed. In regards to 925 Massachusetts Avenue, the Town Manager continued to work
  22|                     Joe McLaughlin of BETA Group, MassDEP, and MassDevelopment about possible cleanup funding. In
  23|                     the end we worked with the EPA and this project was put on the list of projects in line for federal funding
  24|                     through Targeted Brownfields Assessment funding. The funding would pay for removal of the
  25|                     underground tanks, limited soil removal and make the Town eligible for future cleanup funds. This work
  26|                    is slated to be completed in FY 2023. In addition, a survey was completed for the property and after
  27|                     much due diligence to eliminate any possibility that the Town would be legally responsible for cleanup,
  28|                     the Town foreclosed on the property, which was a necessary step to receive the federal funding from the
  29|                     EPA.As it related to one of the action items under the Town Manager's annual goals for Facilities, the
  30|                     Town Manager followed the Town Charter process for an Administrative Organization Plan and after a
  31|                     public hearing process, received approval at the May 2022 Annual Town Meeting to separate Facilities
  32|                     from the Department of Public Works. The third goal, to assist the Select Board with a means tested
  33|                     senior citizen tax relief program was initiated and the Principal Assessor presented information from
  34|                     other communities with similar programs to the working group. Ultimately additional time was needed to
  35|                     develop the framework of the program and its effects on taxpayers, so this goal was carried forward to
  36|                     FY 2023.
  37|               • In July 2021, the Select Board set their goals for the upcoming year: they amended their FY21 goal/work
  38|                     on the old Primary School at 30 School Street with additional action items such as engaging with a
  39|                     landscape architecture firm to discuss a vision of multi-use, passive recreation/ green space project at the
  40|                     site and all necessary steps to bring the associated costs to the 2022 Annual Town Meeting. Another goal
  41|                     was to work with the Town Manager and Finance Director to establish a means-tested senior citizen
  42|                     property tax exemption or abatement. The last goal was to develop a policy designed to encourage
  43|                     volunteerism in Town, specifically for town boards, committees, and commissions. A Request for
  44|                     Proposals was put out twice for selection of a landscape architect for 30 School Street and ultimately
  45|                     architect Ray Dunetz was chosen for the project. Various staff and community meetings led to a
  46|                     conceptual design that requested additional funding at the May 2022 Annual Town Meeting, but funding
  47|                     was only approved for abatement and demolition of the structure and the associated engineering costs.
  48|                     The goal of developing a policy designed to encourage volunteerism was discussed at a workshop but the
  49|                     Board was split on the language of the policy so no action was taken during fiscal year 2022 on this goal.
