   3|             Annual Town Meeting is an important event for community members to come together and vote on warrant articles that
   4|             directly impact how we manage our town. Amongst these articles is the FY23 budget, capital plan, authorizations for
   5|             various enterprise and revolving funds, and appropriations relating to stabilization funds and reserves. Throughout the
   6|             year the Town Manager and department heads carefully review expenses and have thoughtful conversations about how
   7|             to best serve and manage the town. While developing the budget they considered funding, contracts, and mandates, as
   8|             well as what the community wants and needs to thrive. The Town Manager formulated a recommended budget, which
   9|             she presented to the Finance Committee on February 17th. Over six subsequent Finance Committee meetings,
  10|             department heads presented overviews and plans relating to their budgets and capital requests. A Public Hearing was
  11|             held on March 31st, as required by the Town Charter, and the Finance Committee voted on recommendations pertaining
  12|             to financially relevant articles.
  13|             Armed with all the information gathered during this process, the Finance Committee recommends approval of the Town
  14|             Manager's omnibus budget, as outlined in this warrant.
  15|             Overall, our financial condition is good. Revenues are stable, aided by strong collections, increases in state aid, new
  16|             growth, and a variety of federal and state grants that the town has secured. On the expense side, while not meeting every
  17|             need outlined by the department heads, this budget does begin to address several emerging and previously unmet needs.
  18|              The total in requested expenditures is $49,953,531.76. While we are pleased with our current fiscal standing, we do face
  19|             challenges ahead; especially with regard to a number of our municipal buildings, which we outline in more detail below.
  20|             Notably, our certified free cash this year is $2,666,962, which is substantially higher than in previous years, a temporary
  21|             benefit resulting from appropriations that were not fully expended in FY21 as a result of COVID-related restrictions and
  22|             impacts. Town fiscal policy directs portions of free cash to accounts such as stabilization, the OPEB allocation (a fund to
  23|             pay for future retiree benefits), and the capital plan.
  24|             Accordingly, this year's recommended capital plan (funded by free cash, tax revenues, and unspent capital

  26|             incurring any additional debt) into the capital infrastructure that supports our town. As of the printing of this warrant, the
  27|             Finance Committee is seeking to answer a few specific questions pertaining to the capital plan. We will have a
  28|             recommendation on the capital plan, Article 6, at Town Meeting on May 7.

   2|             The Commonwealth of Massachusetts has the most comprehensive system of assistance offered to Veterans,
   3|             families, and surviving spouses in all of the United States. Every city or town in Massachusetts, in accordance
   4|             with chapter 115 of the Massachusetts General Laws, must have available to its citizens a Veterans' Services
   5|             Officer (VSO), either full- or part-time, as determined by the community population, in order to serve the
   6|             needs of its Veterans. Lunenburg has a part-time VSO at the Ritter Building at 960 Massachusetts Ave.
   7|             Working with the Town census of 2021, shows Lunenburg in excess of 618 residents who claimed either
   8|             military service or veteran status. On a monthly basis, the veterans and widows of veterans in Lunenburg
   9|             receive in excess of $328,330.74 in federal benefits (Veterans Administration), which they mostly put back
  10|             into the community by paying their taxes and through discretionary spending.
  11|             Veterans, families, or widows who quality can be provided monthly financial assistance (reimbursements) for
  12|             specitic living expenses such as heating fuel, medical care, and pharmaceutical co-payments. With the VSO's
  13|             approval, the Town of Lunenburg provides the financial assistance and is then reimbursed by the state's
  14|             department of Veterans' Services (DVS). It is the function of the Veterans' Services Office to identify
  15|             qualifying members of the community and assist with enrollment into the DVS program. Additionally, certain
  16|             Veterans and/or widows may also be eligible for federal benefits from the Veterans Administration (VA).
  17|             Only Massachusetts offers both of these services to every community.
  18|             During Fiscal Year 2022 (July 2021- June 2022), Lunenburg served an average of 10 recipients (Veterans,
  19|             families, or widows) per month. Lunenburg paid out $64,435.43 in benefits, or an average of $5,369.62 per
  20|             recipient per month. Lunenburg was reimbursed $48,326.57 by DVS, which is 75% of what was given as
  21|             assistance, the maximum allowable share.
  22|             Some examples of Veterans' Service Office assistance services include:
  23|                Enrollment in chapter 115, State Veterans benefits
  24|                   Assistance obtaining military service records or replacement medals/decorations
  25|             •     Tuition waivers at Massachusetts state colleges
  26|                  Housing assistance, including Veterans Shelters and Veterans Homes
  27|             •  Payments of "Welcome Home" bonuses for deployed Veterans
  28|                   Assistance obtaining certain Massachusetts automobile and motorcycle license plates
  29|            • Employment assistance
  30|             The Veterans' Service Office also provides information, advice, and assistance to any citizen interested in
  31|             learning about their qualifications for federal benefits, such as:
  32|             •  VA disability payments and property tax exemptions
  33|             • Tax-exempt annuities for parents or spouses of those killed in Action or those who are declared 100%
  34|                  disabled from service-connected conditions.
  35|            •   VA pensions, Aid and Attendance assistance, and death benefits
  36|             • VA medical care for both physical and mental needs

  30|             Nashoba Valley Regional Dispatch District:
  31|             The Nashoba Valley Regional Dispatch District (NVRDD) is a Regional Emergency Communications Center located in
  32|             Devens, MA. NVRDD serves as the 9-1-1 public safety answering point and dispatch center for Police, Fire and EMS
  33|             for the communities of Lunenburg, Bolton, Devens, Harvard, Lancaster and Lunenburg. NVRDD was one of the first
  34|             Regional Emergency Communications Centers in the State of Massachusetts, opening its doors on June 18, 2013.
  35|             NVRDD serves a total population of just over 35,000 residents and thousands more visitors to the orchards, farms,
  36|             businesses, and attractions within its six communities. In 2022, NVRDD handled 33,500 phone calls coming in on 911
  37|             and business lines. NVRDD also coordinated the response to over 89,900 calls for service. NVRDD's staff are highly
  38|             skilled in call-handling and dispatching, and are all able to perform both functions simultaneously. These first of the first
  39|             -responders provide a vital and seamless link between the community and their local police, fire, and emergency medical
  40|             services. While the focus is on handling emergencies, our staff also serves the public and our responders by providing
  41|             other administrative and non-emergency dispatch tasks. NVRDD staffs the communications center with multiple
  42|             dispatchers and supervisors each shift to handle calls and requests.
