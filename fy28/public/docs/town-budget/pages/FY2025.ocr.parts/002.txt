  23|                                                                               CONSERVATION
  24|            The Town of Lunenburg Conservation Commission is a seven-member commission, each appointed to 3-year
  25|            terms by the Select Board, whose duties are codified under the Conservation Commission Act MGL chapter 40
  26|            s 8C. The Commission enforces the Massachusetts Wetland Protection Act MGL chapter 131 s 40 and Chapter
  27|            239 of the local bylaws for the protection of wetlands. The Commission also manages, and controls town
  28|            owned lands retained for conservation and passive recreation use.
  29|            The fiscal year 2025-26 was a very busy and active agenda containing both public hearings and meetings for a
  30|            wide range of projects from commercial development, subdivisions and small single family upkeep projects and
  31|            larger scale industrial/commercial proposals. The Commission also held numerous inspections on weekends
  32|            prior to meetings to familiarize themselves with the upcoming proposals that would appear on the agenda.
  33|            During this period the Conservation Commission supported 28 NOIs, 36 Administrative Determinations, 18
  34|            RDAs, 34 COCs, and numerous other requests.
  35|            The Conservation Commission continues to implement forest stewardship plans developed using grants as
  36|            outlined in the last annual reports. The funding from the sale of harvested timber provides the seed money to
  37|            make it possible for the Commission to apply for grants to maintain and improve Lunenburg's Conservation
  38|            Lands. The Conservation Commission continues to execute on the awarded MASS Recreational Trail Program
  39|            Grant that was awarded in 2023. The purpose of this grant was to fund the engineering design and permitting
  40|            work to create parking access in Table Rock and Rayno Conservation Areas. The grant scope also covered
  41|           improving Accessible Access to the Cowdrey Conservation Area as accessed from Townsend Harbor Road. In
  42|            early 2025, the permits were issued for Table Rock and Rayno Areas. This effort is ongoing to complete the
  43|            engineering design and permitting work in the Cowdrey. Members of the commission applied for two additional
  44|            grants for the upcoming year.

  12|            Borrowing
  13|            The town employs two types of borrowing:
  14|                 • Regular Debt, typically for smaller projects within the Tax Levy Limit.
  15|                  • Excludable Debt, requiring Town Meeting and ballot approval, which does not permanently increase
  16|                       taxes once the debt is repaid, the tax rate decreases.
  17|            According to the Massachusetts Division of Local Services, "Municipal debt is an important tool for city and
  18|            town governments to finance the purchase of expensive capital assets and to construct and maintain municipally
  19|            owned infrastructure. However, debt cannot become overburdensome to either the community's property tax
  20|            paying population or to the budget. Capital purchasing and/or improvements should ideally be planned with its
  21|            priorities analyzed and reviewed by a capital planning committee seeking to coordinate community planning
  22|            with debt affordability and aiming to present its recommendations to municipal leaders and to the public."
  23|            The Town policy states that:
  24|                  • Principal and Interest on Regular Debt remain below 4%.
  25|                  • Excludable Debt remains below 11%.
  26|                  • Combined debt remains below 14%.
