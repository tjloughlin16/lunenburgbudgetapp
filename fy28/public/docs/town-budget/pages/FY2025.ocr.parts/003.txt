  33|            This level of activity was similar to 2024, with a slight decrease in fires, alarms and hazardous conditions, but
  34|            an increase in EMS responses. The closure of Nashoba Valley Medical Center continues to strain the Region's
  35|            EMS Services. Ambulances traveling to distant hospitals or "holding the wall" waiting for a bed locally have
  36|            resulted in increased out of service times for all services in the area. For our Department, this has resulted in a
  37|            36% increase in mutual aid EMS responses and an average of 15 additional minutes in turnaround time for an
  38|            ambulance to become available. It is not uncommon for one of the ambulances to be "holding the wall" in the
  39|            local Emergency Department for 20 minutes or more waiting for a bed.
  40|            The Department consists of 10 Career and 30-35 On Call/Per Diem Firefighters and Emergency Medical
  41|            Technicians, plus a full-time Administrative Assistant. The Station is staffed by three Firefighter/EMT's or
  42|            Paramedics. This coverage is provided by a combination of our Career Staff and Per Diem Firefighters. The
  43|                                                                                               71

   1|            greatest issue we continue to confront is having enough part time staff to cover the 96 hours of Per Diem
   2|            coverage each week. This is particularly true of having enough Part time Firefighter/Paramedics. The addition

   8|            town that cares so deeply about its children.
   9|            My tenure began with a commitment to listen. Before setting a new course, I needed to understand the heart of
  10|            Lunenburg. Through three comprehensive surveys and countless conversations, I heard from 491 students, 106
  11|            staff members, and 186 families.
  12|            What I learned was powerful. I discovered a district with a strong heartbeat; where staff take immense pride in
  13|            their colleagues and where families value the safety and opportunities our schools provide. However, I also
  14|            heard clear calls for growth. While 70% of our students feel supported, we learned that a sense of belonging is
  15|            fragile; too many students told us they do not yet feel fully accepted for who they are. Families expressed a
  16|            need for stability in the budget and a focus on student mental health, while staff highlighted that years of fiscal
  17|            constraints have impacted trust.
  18|            These findings became the compass for our work this year. They confirmed that our mission must be to move
  19|            our "One Community" spirit from a theme to a daily practice: welcoming all, lifting each other up, learning with
  20|            purpose, and leading with heart.
  21|            Guided by what I learned, we established four strategic goals to steer the district through the 2025- 2026 school
  22|            year.
  23|            First, we focused on Improving Instruction. With the appointment of Dr. Kimberly Sarfde as our Director of
  24|            Teaching and Learning, we raised the bar for classroom excellence. Our goal has been to ensure every educator
  25|            receives high-quality, actionable feedback that drives student achievement. A central pillar of this work is our
  26|            steadfast commitment to early literacy, with the explicit goal that every child will be reading fluently by the end
  27|            of third grade. We are seeing the "joy of rigor" emerge in every classroom, where high expectations meet high
  28|            engagement.
  29|            Second, we prioritized Fiscal Leadership and Transparency. A critical step in this direction was the hiring of
  30|            Christopher McNamara as our Business Manager. Mr. McNamara has made strong gains in implementing
  31|            accounting practices that are clear and publicly communicated, allowing effective insight for town elected
  32|            officials and citizens. Understanding the financial pressures facing the town, we developed a budget process
  33|            defined by "radical clarity." We presented distinct budget scenarios-level-service, town-allocation, and
  34|            restoration which fostered an honest, collaborative dialogue about what we value and what we fund.
  35|             Third, we dedicated ourselves to Building a Culture of Belonging. We strove to create an environment where
  36|            kindness is the norm and every student sees themselves as a valued member of the community.
  37|            Looking back on the past year, we have achieved milestones.
  38|             We completed universal screenings in mathematics and literacy for grades K-5 to ensure no child slips through
  39|            the cracks. Our educators modeled the instructional excellence we strive for. To support this work, we provided
  40|            targeted professional development in the Open Sci Ed curriculum and the development of literacy skills.

   9|            Literacy Instruction and Student Support Systems
  10|            Lunenburg Public Schools continues to implement a coherent, research-aligned literacy system designed to
  11|            improve outcomes for all students while maximizing the impact of limited and increasingly uncertain resources.
  12|            Rather than relying on isolated or short-term initiatives, the district has focused on aligning curriculum,
  13|            assessment, professional learning, and intervention into an integrated system that can be sustained over time.
  14|            High-quality Tier 1 classroom instruction serves as the foundation of the district's literacy framework. Research
  15|            consistently demonstrates that strong daily instruction is the most effective way to improve literacy outcomes at
  16|            scale, particularly in districts with limited supplemental staffing. At the elementary level (Grades K-5), HMH
  17|            Into Reading provides a structured, evidence-based approach that integrates foundational skills, vocabulary
  18|            development, knowledge-building, and comprehension.
  19|            In Grades K-2, instruction emphasizes early reading development—including phonological awareness, phonics,
  20|            spelling, and fluency—while intentionally embedding oral language and comprehension from the outset. These
  21|            practices are designed to prevent reading difficulty rather than respond after gaps widen. In Grades 3-5,
  22|            instruction shifts toward consolidating fluent reading and strengthening vocabulary, background knowledge,
  23|            and comprehension of increasingly complex texts.
  24|            At the middle school level, the district has devoted significant time to deepening implementation of Amplify
  25|            English Language Arts. Educators engage in sustained Professional Learning Community (PLC) work focused
  26|            on text complexity, pacing, instructional routines, and scaffolding to ensure students consistently engage with
  27|            rigorous, grade-level material while receiving appropriate instructional support.
  28|            Assessment data are used instructionally to guide teaching and support decisions. Elementary students are
  29|            universally screened using Dynamic Indicators of Basic Literacy Skills (DIBELS), enabling educators to
  30|            identify risk early, adjust instruction, and provide targeted intervention through the district's Multi-Tiered
  31|            System of Supports (MTSS). Intervention time and intensity are determined by data and refined through regular
  32|            progress monitoring and collaborative review.
  33|            Beginning in February 2026, Lunenburg Primary School will engage in a short, high-impact partnership with
  34|            the HILL for Literacy. This classroom-based work will focus on modeling effective instruction and providing
  35|            real-time feedback to teachers, intentionally strengthening internal instructional capacity that can be sustained
  36|            despite declining external funding.
  37|            Mathematics Instruction and Conceptual Understanding
  38|            Mathematics instruction across grades K-12 emphasizes conceptual understanding, reasoning, and application
  39|            rather than rote memorization of procedures. Programs and practices are aligned with the Massachusetts
  40|            Mathematics Frameworks and DESE expectations for high-quality instruction.
  41|            In Grades K-5, mathematics instruction is delivered through HMH Into Math, with a strong emphasis on
  42|            number sense, multiple representations, mathematical discourse, and problem-solving. Students regularly
  43|            engage with visual models, hands-on materials, and structured discussion to develop deep and lasting

===PAGE 96=== (ocr, 41 lines)
   1|            understanding of mathematical concepts.
   2|            At the middle school level, mathematics instruction is delivered through EdGems, an inquiry-based program
   3|            that emphasizes reasoning, sense-making, and productive struggle. Instruction encourages students to articulate
   4|            their thinking, engage with complex problems, and develop confidence as mathematical learners.
   5|            At the high school level, mathematics instruction follows a coherent sequence aligned with state frameworks
   6|            and college- and career-readiness expectations. Students complete required coursework in Algebra, Geometry,
   7|            and Algebra II, with additional opportunities in Precalculus, Statistics, Calculus, Business Math, and Advanced
   8|            Placement courses. Multiple pathways ensure access to rigorous, standards-aligned instruction that supports a
   9|            range of postsecondary goals.
  10|            Universal screening for early numeracy using the Universal Screener for Number Sense (USNS) supports
  11|            MTSS decision-making in the elementary grades. Data are reviewed collaboratively to guide instruction,
  12|            intervention, and progress monitoring.
  13|            Science Instruction and Inquiry-Based Learning
  14|            Science instruction across grades K-12 is aligned with the Massachusetts Science and Technology/Engineering
  15|            Frameworks and the Next Generation Science Standards. Instruction emphasizes inquiry, investigation, and the
  16|            application of scientific concepts.
  17|            In Grades K-2, science instruction is delivered through locally designed, standards-aligned units that promote
  18|            curiosity, observation, questioning, and hands-on exploration, supporting early scientific thinking and language
  19|            development.
  20|            In Grades 3-5, science instruction utilizes OpenSciEd materials, which engage students in real-world
  21|            phenomena and integrate science and engineering practices, disciplinary core ideas, and crosscutting concepts.
  22|            This approach supports sustained investigation, explanation, and sense-making over time.
  23|            Middle school science is delivered through Glencoe/McGraw Hill Science, incorporating hands-on
  24|            investigations, laboratory experiences, literacy connections, and assessments aligned with state standards.
  25|            At the high school level, students complete coursework in Biology and have additional opportunities in
  26|            Chemistry, Physics, Environmental Science, Anatomy and Physiology, Biotechnology, and Advanced
  27|           Placement sciences. Instruction emphasizes laboratory experiences, scientific inquiry, and the application of
  28|            scientific concepts.
  29|            Data-Informed Instruction, MTSS, and Professional Collaboration

  13|            to student needs.

  15|            continued instructional improvement. Despite these efforts, the loss of federal funding will further limit
  16|            flexibility and place additional pressure on already constrained instructional systems.

  18|            All instructional work described in this report has been carried out amid repeated budget reductions, staffing

  22|            and funding. Additional reductions—particularly the loss of federal Title funds-would compromise
  23|            instructional systems and place current gains at risk.

  10|                                                                       Turkey Hill Elementary School
  11|                                                                                 Town Report 2025
  12|            Turkey Hill Elementary School is an elementary school serving students in third grade through fifth grade. As
  13|            of Feb 2026, Turkey Hill Elementary School serves 355 enrolled students. There are five sections of grade 3,
  14|            grade 4, and grade 5. Grade 3 has 111 students. Grade 4 has 136 students and Grade 5 has 108 students.
  15|            Our unified arts areas include physical education, art, music, library, and STEM. We are also served by a full
  16|            time assistant principal, full time nurse, a psychologist, a full time adjustment counselor/social worker, and 21
  17|            full time teachers. Other support personnel who work across the district include an ELA coach (through
  18|            December), a Math Specialist, a Director of Teaching and Learning, two EL teachers, a Speech and Language
  19|            Pathologist, OT, and PT. In addition, we have a half-time interventionist targeted support for small groups of
  20|            students.
  21|            Staff continually work on the development of the whole child by supporting students' development and growth
  22|            in the areas of social emotional learning, literacy, math, social studies and science. We are able to meet student
  23|            needs with the support of the district curriculum of teaching and learning and the two coaches which enables
  24|            teachers to have the opportunity to meet at grade levels to develop and provide evidence based instruction,
  25|            common learning experiences, and student data analysis and share effective teaching strategies to support
  26|            student academic growth. Students continue to have benchmark assessments three times throughout the school
  27|            year (beginning, middle and end of year) that are used to help us celebrate students' academic growth and
  28|            effort. The data provided by these assessments also helps refine our focus for interventions. We began the
  29|           implementation of the OpenSci Ed curriculum with initial training and a trial unit. The implementation will
  30|            continue next school year.
  31|            This fall our fifth grade students had their annual trip to Nature's Classroom. It was a successful week with good
  32|            weather, community building, and the creation of fond memories of a unique time together, thanks to the
  33|            combined efforts of our entire fifth grade staff.
  34|            We held an open house in September. It was a well-attended event and for many parents their first opportunity
  35|            to be physically present in our building. It was accompanied by a book fair facilitated by our librarian, Mrs.
  36|            McNamara. Parent conferences in January offered both in person and virtual options and appointments were
  37|            fully booked within a few days.
  38|            We held our annual Penny Wars fundraiser, it was a huge success and the money raised will be put toward the
  39|            purchase and installation of a shade structure in our playing field. As always, our PTO assisted in the weeklong
  40|            collection. We could not be successful without their efforts. We were able to bring back a fifth grade band and
  41|            all students will be performing in an upcoming spring concert under the direction of our music teacher, Mr.
  42|            Anthony Balester. Turkey Hill also held its annual Turkey Trot in November. Led by Mr. Derek Guimond, the
  43|            Turkey Trot was a wonderful experience.

  21|                                                                                 Town Report 2025
  22|            Lunenburg Middle School provides a rigorous academic curriculum as well as strong programs that support
  23|            students' social and emotional growth. These programs ensure that each child becomes a self-motivated,
  24|            creative, lifelong learner in a safe, stimulating environment that encourages diversity and open honest
  25|            communication. The dedicated and accomplished staff of professionals within the school embraces the
  26|            philosophy of educating the whole child academically, socially, and emotionally. This philosophy is a means by
  27|            which all students become positive community members that possess the skills to be successful in high school
  28|            and beyond.
  29|             Lunenburg Middle School is a co-located middle - high school serving grades six through eight. Our effective,
  30|            student centered middle school embraces interdisciplinary teams of teachers that enable teachers to work with
  31|            smaller groups of students. Smaller groups of students allow teachers to learn and engage students in their
  32|            preferred learning style, making them more confident and successful.
  33|            Our effective middle school model is student centered and provides opportunities to explore a variety of
  34|            different interests and talents through special areas and enrichment classes and extracurricular activities. One of
  35|            our goals is for students to discover something they are passionate about and want to continue exploring as they
  36|            move through high school and beyond. We will continue to seek out opportunities for students that foster
  37|            critical thinking and problem-solving skills that prepare them for high school and beyond or where their passion
  38|            may lead them to.
  39|            Our grade eight team of teachers continue to implement a schedule that mirrors the high school schedule. Our
  40|            philosophy is to ease the difficult transition from middle school to the first year of high school by developing
  41|            student's independent skills they will require to be successful in high school. Grade eight teachers function as
