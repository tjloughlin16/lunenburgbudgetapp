   1|             In Memoriam
   2|                 John Francis Lattanzi was born to John and Frances (Lynch) Lattanzi on April 1, 1936, in Yonkers,
   3|                New York. He grew up in Yonkers and Washington, New Jersey. In his youth John developed a life-
   4|               long affinity for mischief, playing rousing piano by ear, and a love of outdoor pursuits. He graduated
   5|                 from Lafayette College in 1958, served as an officer in the U.S. Army, then embarked on a 30-year
   6|                                                             career with the American Can Company.
   7|              He met his life-long partner, Christine Uhl, in 1961. They married, purchased a camp on Sebec Lake in
   8|                Maine and started their family soon after. John and Christine raised their family in Cranford, NJ and
   9|                                                 Geneva, NY before moving to Lunenburg, MA in 1982.
  10|              Over the past 43 years, John served his community in various roles on the Lunenburg Personnel Board,
  11|              Conservation Commission, and Historical Society. John was a member of Saint Boniface and Our Lady
  12|                          of the Lake Churches, the Lunenburg Sportsman Club and the Sebec Lake Association.
  13|              John leaves behind his beloved wife of over 61 years, and their five children; John Lattanzi Jr. and his
  14|              wife Karen of Honolulu, HI; Jean Proctor and husband Dan of Lunenburg, MA; Peter Lattanzi and wife
  15|              Dina of Cumberland, ME; Paul Lattanzi and wife AnnMarie or Yarmouth, ME; and James Lattanzi and
  16|               wife Allison of Fitchburg, MA. John also leaves behind 15 grandchildren and his younger sister, Jane
  17|                                                                       O'Connell, of Durham, NH.

   1|             In Memoriam
   2|            Robert Joseph Saiia of Lunenburg, MA, affectionately known as Bob or Bobby, passed away peacefully on
   3|            April 3, 2025, after a long battle with dementia. Bob was a loving husband to Donna and a devoted father to his
   4|            daughter Tricia Back and son Michael Saiia.
   5|            Born in Buffalo, New York (1944), Bob was proud of the days he spent working at The Bankers Life Insurance
   6|            Company in Buffalo, NY~ later Principal Financial Group, where he was known for his dedication and hard
   7|            work. As his career progressed, he was promoted and moved his family to Westerville, Ohio, where he became
   8|            a proud Buckeye fan. His colleagues remember him as a kind and supportive mentor who always had a joke to
   9|            share. He always held dear the memories of his days as a camp counselor at Camp Weona, just outside of
  10|            Buffalo, NY. He also had a passion for coaching, whether it be his own children or once they were grown,
  11|            volunteering as a football coach at Lunenburg High School. Bob also was very proud of the time he served on
  12|            the Lunenburg Planning Board.
  13|            Known as the life of the party, Bob valued every friendship, especially his lifelong friends from Frontier High
  14|            School, where he graduated in 1962, affectionately calling themselves, HAAC-Hamburg Attitude Adjustment
  15|            Club. Each and every friendship he made throughout his 81 years of life left an impact on him and he couldn't
  16|            go anywhere without making a new friend. His infectious laughter and warm spirit made him a beloved friend
  17|            to all.
  18|            Bob loved listening to Jazz, Frank Sinatra and Andrea Bocelli. He loved traveling to Italy and Europe with his
  19|            wife Donna, but his favorite place to travel was Indiana where he could spend time with his children and
  20|            grandchildren. He was the best Grandpa around to his two grandchildren, Renee and Anthony; they were his
  21|            pride and joy and he would do just about anything to put a smile on their faces. He was always their biggest
  22|            cheerleader and supporter and loved attending all their various events and activities. Bob adored his time with
  23|            his family and his dogs, loved sports and he especially loved celebrating his Italian heritage. He took great pride
  24|            in his roots and enjoyed sharing stories of his family's traditions and history.
  25|            Bob is survived by his devoted wife, Donna Saiia, daughter Tricia Back (Robert Back), son Michael Saiia,
  26|            grandchildren, Renee Back (19) and Anthony Back (18), stepchildren Christina Gallagher and Michael
  27|            Gallagher, sister Kathy Krasowski, nieces Tracy Ensminger (Jeff Ensminger) and Julie Reinstein (Craig
  28|            Reinstein), Linda Saiia Ross, and his five great nephews and one great niece.

   8|               the Town of Townsend.
   9|               Local legislative decisions are made by an open town meeting. Subjet to the legislative decisions
  10|               made by town meeting, the affairs of the town are generally administered by an elected five member
  11|               Select Board and a Town Manager and local school affairs are administered by an elected seven
  12|               member School Committee.
  13|                Lunenburg is a semi-rural community located in northern Worcester County. Lake Whalom and the for-
  14|                mer Whalom Amusement Park delineated the Whalom District from the remainder of Lunenburg. This
  15|                district was developed as a housing base for many factory workers working in the mills and factories of
  16|                Leominster and Fitchburg. In many ways the characteristics of the Whalom District closely reflect charac-
  17|               teristics of these cities more than of the rest of Lunenburg. The Hickory Hills Lake area was developed as
  18|                a private community comprised largely of vacation cottages. Shirley Reservoir is a public water body also
  19|                developed with mainly vacation cottages that have now been modified to year-round residences.
  20|                Commercial and industrial development is permitted in several areas of town, primarily along the two
  21|               major arteries (Route 2A and 13) and the town's peripheries, due largely to the close proximity of
  22|                Leominster's and Fitchburg's sewer and water systems. Although many farms and orchards have slowly
  23|                developed into single family house, farming continues on in Lunenburg. In 2013 the Annual Town
  24|                Meeting voted to establish an Agricultural Commission to represent, preserve and enhance farming in
  25|                the commujnity and to administer the "Right to Farm" bylaw.
  26|                In 2014 Lunenburg Town Meeting established the Architectural Preservation District ("District") bylaw
  27|               to regulate construction, demolition or renovation of existing "Contributing Property" structures as
  28|                defined by the bylaw with an established bounded district. The bylaw was introduced at Town Meeting
  29|                along with the Village District Bylaw, which created an overlay zoning district to encourage the
  30|                economic vitalization of downtown Lunenburg, the Architectural Preservation District Bylaw ensures
  31|                that it will not come at the expense of the town's historical character and integrity.

   1|                                                           REPORT OF THE TOWN MANAGER
   2|                                                                                 Fiscal Year 2025
   3|            Fiscal Year 2025 saw significant transition in the Office of the Town Manager. The Office of the Town
   4|            Manager was led by three individuals over the course of the year: Heather Lemieux, who departed in the
   5|            summer of 2024 after eight years of dedicated service; Carter Terenzini, who served ably as Interim Town
   6|            Manager from August 2024 through early February 2025; and Jennifer Warren-Dyment, who was appointed as
   7|            Town Manager and began on February 4, 2025. Throughout these transitions, department heads, Town staff, the
   8|            Select Board, and the many volunteers who serve on our boards and committees provided the continuity and
   9|            commitment that kept Lunenburg moving forward.
  10|            Below are the highlights of the key topics and accomplishments from FY25 within the Office of the Town
  11|            Manager.
  12|            Leadership Transition
  13|            Town Manager Heather Lemieux submitted her notice to the Select Board in June of 2024. Her last meeting
  14|            with the Select Board was the evening of July 23, 2024, during which she offered heartfelt remarks on her
  15|            service to the community. She expressed gratitude to residents, board members, staff, business owners, and
  16|            state officials, reflecting on her seven and a half years of service. She outlined major accomplishments,
  17|           including long-term staffing plans for public safety, significant capital improvements, enhanced ADA
  18|            accessibility, and infrastructure projects such as Complete Streets initiatives, Summer Street improvements, and
  19|            bridge funding. She emphasized that her proudest achievements were the relationships built during her tenure.
  20|            She clarified that her resignation was not requested by the Board and attributed her decision, in part, to a loss of
  21|            trust. She concluded by thanking her family for their support and reiterated her commitment to having served
  22|            Lunenburg with dedication and integrity.
  23|            Carter Terenzini was engaged as Interim Town Manager beginning in August 2024. He brought extensive
  24|            municipal management experience, including several prior engagements in the Interim role, and assumed the
  25|            position immediately. As Interim Town Manager, Mr. Terenzini not only kept the day-to-day operations
  26|            running smoothly, but also advanced a number of capital projects, completed the obligation of ARPA funds,
  27|            navigated multiple vacancies, and made notable strides in improving employee engagement. At his final Select
  28|            Board meeting on January 28, 2025, Interim Town Manager Terenzini presented his concluding report and was
  29|            thanked by the Board for filling vacant positions, committing ARPA funds, and fostering greater employee
  30|            participation during his tenure. Board members expressed appreciation for his work navigating challenging
  31|            circumstances.
  32|            With the Interim position filled, the Select Board stood up a Town Manager Screening Committee and hired the
  33|            search firm of Community Paradigm Associates. Per the Town Charter, The Town Manager shall be appointed
  34|            by the Select Board from names submitted to it by a Town Manager Screening Committee which shall consist of
  35|            1 member of the Select Board, designated by it, 1 member of the School Committee, designated by it, 1 member
  36|            of the planning board, designated by it, 1 member of the Finance Committee designated by it and the Town
  37|            Moderator, who shall serve as the chair and call all meetings of the Town Manager Screening Committee.
  38|            Whenever the position of Town Manager shall be vacant, the Town Manager Screening Committee shall be
  39|            convened and shall solicit candidates, review applications, interview and rate candidates and submit the names
  40|            of qualified candidates to the Select Board who shall appoint a Town Manager only from the names so
  41|           prepared and submitted to it.
  42|            The Screening Committee, Chaired by Town Moderator Tim Murphy, also included Tom Alonzo of the Select
  43|            Board, Peter Beardmore of the School Committee, Chris Menard of the Finance Committee and Matt Brenner
  44|            of the Planning Board. The Committee worked with Community Paradigm to develop the Position Statement
  45|            for the Town Manager Role, screen applications, interview semi-finalist candidates, and recommend a list of
  46|            finalists to the Select Board for their consideration. Much of the work of the Screening Committee is conducted

===PAGE 21=== (ocr, 40 lines)
   1|            in Executive Session.
   2|            Three finalists were publicly interviewed by the Select Board on January 14, 2025. They included Angeline
   3|            Lopes Ellison, Jennifer Warren-Dyment, and John Woodsmall. The next evening, the Board moved (4-1) to
   4|            appoint Ms. Warren-Dyment as Town Manager pending successful negotiation. On January 28, 2025, the Select
   5|            Board approved the contract with Ms. Warren-Dyment as Town Manager. Ms. Warren-Dyment began her first
   6|            day on February 4, 2025, and began immediately engaging with department heads, the Select Board, and the
   7|            budget process that was already well underway.
   8|             Select Board Composition and Goals
   9|            Throughout the majority of FY25, the Select Board was composed of Chair Caesar Nuzzolo, Vice-Chair
  10|            Michael-Ray Jeffreys, Clerk Amanda Moore, Tom Alonzo, and Renee Emkey. The Board met regularly,
  11|            typically the first three Tuesdays of the month, and addressed a wide range of Town business including the
  12|            annual and special town meetings, policy updates, the Town Manager transition, budget discussions, licensing
  13|            matters, and appointments.
  14|            In the fall of 2024, the Select Board engaged in goal-setting discussions with Interim Town Manager Terenzini.
  15|            The Board established the following goals (each goal including a number of additional objectives):
  16|                  1) Establish policies in collaboration with the Town Manager and Assistant Town Manager/HR Director to
  17|                       improve the quality of the Town as an employer, addressing the issues of recruitment, retention, and
  18|                        overall work environment
  19|                  2) Revamp the Select Board and Town Manager Goal-Setting Process
  20|                  3) Increase Transparency and Availability of Information to the Public
  21|                  4) Work through the Town Manager, Assistant Town Manager/HR Director and the department heads to
  22|                       initially create, to periodically review and to develop and secure plans, policies and procedures
  23|             Financial Matters
  24|            FY26 Budget Development
  25|            Interim Town Manager Terenzini began the FY26 budget process, working with the departments to develop
  26|            budget requests and with Finance Director/Town Accountant Ezequiel Ayala to develop revenue projections.
  27|            Town Manager Warren-Dyment continued the FY26 budget process after her arrival. She held meetings with all
  28|            departments in January and worked to understand the picture of revenues and expenditures and made initial
  29|            revenue adjustments.
  30|            By March 2025, the FY26 budget picture had come more fully into sharper focus. The FY26 budget presented
  31|            to the Select Board on March 19, 2025, included reductions across a number of areas, including cuts to the
  32|            Pavement Management Plan (which were offset by Chapter 90 funding).
  33|            On May 3, 2025, a $49,374,465,00 balanced budget was presented and approved at Town Meeting. The budget
  34|            was subsidized by a $500,000 appropriation of free cash to the Lunenburg Public Schools (LPS) to fund FY25
  35|            and avoid an override allowing town and school leaders to have time to jointly plan for FY27.
  36|            The need for additional state funding was a theme that persisted through the development of the FY26 budget
  37|            with town and school leaders advocating for increased Chapter 70 and Unrestricted General Government Aid
  38|            funding.
  39|            Budget Task Force
  40|            The Budget Task Force, formed by the Select Board, brought forward recommendations that were reviewed at 17

===PAGE 22=== (ocr, 44 lines)
   1|            the February 11, 2025 meeting. The Task Force concluded there were three structural causes to the state of
   2|            disrepair in Lunenburg: 1) Proposition 2 ½ funding; 2) lack of competitive employment packages; and 3) an
   3|            overly complex financial structure for the town.
   4|            Recommendations of the Task Force included 1) consolidating town investment accounts and funds; 2) a
   5|            targeted diagnostic audit; 3) implement the prudent investment rule; 4) review the viability of the Community
   6|            Preservation Act; 5) Green grants for HVACs; 6) solar on old town dump; 7) solar on school lots; 8) Brooks
   7|            House lease; 9) review trash and recycling programs; 10) designate additional roads as town roads; 11)
   8|            implement a high deductible health plan; 12) increase health insurance opt outs; 13) payroll and purchase order
   9|            streamlining; 14) standardize and improve workflow for town finances; 15) review school solar contracts; 16)
  10|            create regular review process for school fees; 17) create regular review process for town fees; 18) change
  11|            warrant delivery; 19) consolidate town and school snow removal and ground maintenance; and 20) determine
  12|            viability of split tax rate.
  13|            Other Financial Mentions
  14|            Town Manager Lemieux had obtained a grant to work with the Division of Local Services (DLS) to draft an
  15|            update Lunenburg's financial policies and procedures which were not adopted prior to her departure. Interim
  16|            Town Manager Terenzini shared the draft and encouraged feedback and commentary. This project was not
  17|            completed in FY2S.
  18|            As of March 2025, the Town had received over $225,000 in cumulative opioid settlement funds. Town Manager
  19|            Warren-Dyment began preliminary discussions with the Police Chief and Fire Chief on the potential use of
  20|            these funds. No funds were expended in FY25.
  21|            A final tax rate of $14.36 per thousand was established for FY25. Free cash was certified at $2,270,060.
  22|             Infrastructure, Facilities, and Capital Projects
  23|            Flat Hill Road Bridge
  24|            The Flat Hill Road Bridge replacement over Catacoonamug Brook was the most significant and most
  25|            challenging capital project of FY2025. After years of planning, the project, funded by a previously secured
  26|            $500,000 Small Bridge Grant and supplemental MassDOT funding, was awarded to Kenefick Corporation. The
  27|            road was closed beginning March 7, 2025, with an anticipated reopening of April 25, 2025 and a detour route
  28|            put in place and communicated widely to the public. However, the water level in the lake had already risen
  29|            significantly by the time work was to begin, and Kenefick's water control plan proved inadequate for the
  30|            conditions. No work was performed on the structure, and Kenefick submitted a request for a one-year extension,
  31|            which was denied. The bridge remained closed for the remainder of FY25 after a MassDOT Underwater Bridge
  32|            Inspection Team inspection found additional section in the structure.
  33|            Town Building Projects
  34|                 • The Town Hall Roof Replacement and Chimney Restoration project was one of the major capital
  35|                       investments of FY25. The contract was issued in December 2024 under Interim Town Manager
  36|                       Terenzini, with the chimneys prioritized first as they were anticipated to generate the most change
  37|                       orders. Winter weather slowed progress, but by March 2025 both Town Hall chimneys were complete
  38|                       and the roofing work had begun. By April 2025 the Town Hall roof was nearly complete. The contractor
  39|                       then transitioned to the Ritter Memorial Building, completing chimney repointing, flashing repairs, slate
  40|                       tile replacement, and gutter and trim work. The project was substantially complete by May 2025.
  41|                 • The Library Teen Room expansion was completed during FY25. The project included construction of
  42|                       two new offices (Phase 1), expansion of the teen room itself (Phase 2), and replacement of the children's
  43|                       story craft room floor.

  12|            ARPA Fund Obligations
  13|             Completing the obligation of all American Rescue Plan Act (ARPA) funds before the federal deadline was one
  14|             of Interim Town Manager Terenzini's most urgent and consequential priorities during FY2025. The Town had
  15|             received approximately $3.5 million in ARPA Coronavirus State and Local Fiscal Recovery Funds (SLFRF),
  16|             and the federal requirement had evolved from a definition of "committed" funds to requiring that all dollars be
  17|             under contract by December 31, 2024. This created a compressed and challenging timeline.
  18|             The ARPA-funded project portfolio being moved to contract during FY25 included a wide range of municipal
  19|             investments including supplementing existing projects on prior year capital plans as well as additional facilities
  20|             and infrastructure side projects, public safety upgrades, school projects, Water District projects, software
  21|             upgrades, and funding for community programs.
  22|             By the end of December, all funds were obligated. When Interim Town Manager Terenzini presented his final
  23|             report to the Select Board on January 28, 2025, he cited the successful commitment of ARPA funds as one of
  24|             the signature accomplishments of his tenure. Chair Nuzzolo drafted a press release to communicate to the
  25|             public the full scope of ARPA investments the Town had made on behalf of the community.
  26|             Personnel
  27|             Staffing remained an ongoing challenge and priority throughout FY25. Key staffing actions during FY25
  28|             included: the completion of the Police Chief search and appointment; continued recruitment of the Land Use
  29|             Director; the search and recruitment of the Senior Center Director; the filling of the Assistant to the Sewer
  30|             Business Manager position; the recruitment for the Conservation Agent; a vacancy in the position of Executive
  31|             Assistant to the Town Manager and Select Board to name a few.
  32|            One of the most significant recruitments of FY25 was the search for a new Police Chief following Chief
  33|            Thomas Gammel's retirement. Applications for the Police Chief position closed in early February 2025. A
  34|            Screening Committee comprised of the Fire Chief, COA Director, Superintendent, a Chief from another
  35|            community, a Town Manager from another community, and Town Manager Warren-Dyment reviewed all
  36|            applications and conducted interviews on March 12, 2025. Promoted from Lieutenant and then Interim Chief,
  37|            Jeffrey Thibodeau, was sworn in as Lunenburg's Police Chief at a well-attended swearing-in and pinning
  38|            ceremony held on April 18, 2025. Select Board Chair Caesar Nuzzolo served as Master of Ceremonies and
  39|            PACC produced a video of the ceremony for those unable to attend in person.
  40|            After 30 years of service, Sue Doherty retired as COA Director on December 31, 2024. After a recruitment
  41|            process, Deb Goodsell was hired to take over the helm at the Center. Deb brought significant experience to the
  42|            position, having served in Clinton for ten years as their Director.
  43|            After a sustained vacancy in the role of Land Use Director that was vacated by Adam Burney, Christopher
  44|            Reilly was hired in September 2024. The position was vacated again in April 2025 and recruitment continued

   5|            Under the umbrella of Massachusetts state and local town laws, Lunenburg's Assessing Organization will work
   6|            with town operations and our citizens to meet our vision for delivering a fair and equitable taxation. The
   7|            Lunenburg Board of Assessors will evolve and administer a series of "Best Practices" methodologies consistent
   8|            with our Vision. These practices will ensure equitable treatment and compassion for all of our citizens.
   9|            The Board of Assessors has empowered an Assessor Organization that has the capability to evaluate and assess
  10|            all property at its full and fair market value. This is accomplished through a rigorous measurement of property,
  11|            collection of appropriate data, analysis and maintenance of all property tax data records. Additionally, the
  12|            Assessor Department administers a motor vehicle excise tax, real estate and motor vehicle tax abatements, and
  13|            all statutory tax exemptions as allowed by law. The Assessor Organization will work cohesively with other
  14|            town departments.
  15|            The last year has seen a lot of progress in restoring the fully functional Assessors' Office.
  16|                 • We continue to be fully staffed after several years of being understaffed. Our staff includes a full time
  17|                       Principal Assessor, a 19-hour Data Collector and a 32-hour Assessing Administrative Assistant.
  18|                 • The Assessing office and files have been reorganized from the disarray in 2023.
  19|                 • Assessing Policies and Procedures have been established.
  20|                 • A sales inspection program has been established to follow-up on sales and review property data in MLS.
  21|                       Permit inspections are conducted weekly and a cyclical program has been established with the
  22|                       inspection of over 500 properties annually to comply with the DOR Directives from the 2024
  23|                       revaluation.
  24|                 • We successfully improved several exemptions by loosening eligibility income and asset limits
  25|                       qualification for Seniors and increasing exemptions amounts. We also worked on developing a new
  26|                       "Senior Means Tested Tax Exemption" for implementation in the fall of 2025 (FY2026).
  27|            The Board of Assessors' efforts at restoring the trust of Lunenburg taxpayers include scheduling all our
  28|            meetings during evenings (with both PACC coverage and Zoom access) so everyone can participate more
  29|            easily. The Board has also developed a Property Tax program for use at the Senior Center and has held a yearly
  30|            senior tax exemption presentation to create awareness of the available real estate tax exemptions for the seniors.
  31|            We continue to improve visibility and access by redesigning of the Website, including analytics and
  32|            explanations provided the Massachusetts DOR / Division of Local Services, Massachusetts Association of
  33|            Assessing Officers, and UMass among others. The office staff strives to provide exceptional service that the
  34|            Lunenburg taxpayers deserve.
