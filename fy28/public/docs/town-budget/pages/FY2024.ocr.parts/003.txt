   1|                                                                           VETERANS' SERVICES
   2|             The Commonwealth of Massachusetts has the most comprehensive system of assistance offered to Veterans,
   3|             their families and surviving spouses in all of the United States. Every city or town in Massachusetts, in accord-
   4|             ance with chapter 115 of the Massachusetts General Laws, must have available to its citizens a Veterans' Ser-
   5|             vices Officer (VSO), either full or part-time, as determined by the community population, in order to serve the
   6|             needs of its veterans. If they want to File VA Claims they must be State Certified thru the VA and are called
   7|             Veteran Agents. Lunenburg has a part-time Veteran Agent at the Ritter Building at 960 Massachusetts Ave.
   8|             The Office hours are Thursdays from 9:00 AM thru 12:00 PM.
   9|             Veterans, families, or widows who qualify can be provided monthly financial assistance (Chapter 115 Bene-
  10|             fits), which are reimbursements for specific living expenses such as heating fuel, medical care, and pharmacy
  11|             co-payments. With the Veteran Agent's approval, the Town of Lunenburg provides the financial assistance,
  12|             but then the town is reimbursed by the State's Department of Veterans' Services (DVS). It is the function of
  13|             the Veterans' Services Office to identify qualifying members of the community and assist with enrollment into
  14|             the DVS program. Additionally, certain Veterans and/or widows may also be eligible for federal benefits from
  15|             the Veterans Administration (VA). Only Massachusetts offers both of these services to every community.
  16|             During Fiscal Year 2024 (July 1, 2023- June 30, 2024), Lunenburg is serving an average of 7 recipients
  17|             (Veterans, Families, or Widows) per month on Chapter 115. Lunenburg paid out $44,454.57 in benefits, or an
  18|             average of $3,704.55 per month. Lunenburg was reimbursed $37,233.82 by DVS, which is typically 75% of
  19|             what was given as assistance, the maximum allowable share. There are a couple of services that the Town is
  20|             reimbursed 100%.
  21|             Some examples of Veterans' Service Office assistance services include:
  22|             • Enrollment in chapter 115, State Veterans benefits
  23|             • Assistance obtaining military service records or replacement medals/decorations
  24|             • Tuition waivers at Massachusetts state colleges
  25|             • Housing assistance, including Veterans Shelters and Veterans Homes
  26|             • Payments of "Welcome Home" bonuses for deployed Veterans
  27|             • Assistance obtaining certain Massachusetts automobile and motorcycle license plates
  28|             • Employment assistance

   2|             The Planning Board consists of 5 members elected for terms of 5 years each. It makes careful studies of the resources,
   3|             possibilities, and needs of the Town and plans for, and reviews, a variety of proposed short and long range development.
   4|             Included in this is making and maintaining a comprehensive, land use related Master Plan, setting forth in graphic and
   5|             textual form policies to govern the future growth and development of the entire Town, and that effort continued in FY24.
   6|             Related to that, the Board also oversees the statutory power to regulate the subdivision of land and the creation of public
   7|             ways within the Town by the adoption and administration of rules and regulations governing such development. More
   8|             broadly, the Board makes recommendations to Town Meeting on all matters affecting land use and growth, including the
   9|             Zoning By-Laws of the Town. Under its statutory and local permitting authority, it reviewed a variety of applications for
  10|             development and initiatives for regulatory amendments over 23 meetings in FY24, as outlined below. All applications to
  11|             the board are supported by a fee structure and use of peer review services paid for by the applicants, to offset administra-

  13|             SUBDIVISIONS
  14|             In FY24, under its Subdivision Rules and Regulations, the board held one public hearing to amend the application dead-
  15|             lines. Submitted applications included Subdivision Approval Not Required (ANR) plans, which are allowed by the Sub-
  16|             division Control Act and are an important part of the board's regulatory authority over divisions of land. Eleven (11)
  17|             ANR Plans not requiring approval under the Lunenburg Subdivision Rules and Regulations, but still requiring Board
  18|             endorsement, were approved in FY24. One (1) Definitive Plan application was also reviewed and approved. Under relat-
  19|             ed Zoning Bylaws Regulations, 1 Common Driveways application was approved.

  22|             disturbs 1 acre or more of land. This Bylaw safeguards public health, safety, welfare and the environment by minimizing
  23|             adverse impacts of contaminated stormwater both off-site and downstream. These requirements are more likely to affect
  24|             large commercial projects and multi-family dwellings, rather than average homeowner wanting to put in a shed, garden,

  39|                  pursue additional funding to install the remaining LPS classrooms with mini-split units. Once both phases are com-

  41|                  rebates from Unitil. Added electrical costs for operation will be offset to some degree by solar net metering credits.

  43|                   School ($33,250). Benefits of both projects 1) create a barrier that does not allow window glass to immediately shat-

  20|             The Lunenburg Public Schools Information Technology (IT) Department played a critical role in addressing the technol-
  21|             ogy needs both inside and outside of the classroom. The Department also focused heavily on compliance for E911 on the
  22|             phone systems. The following projects were completed as part of an emphasis on E911 compliance:
  23|             1. Re-cabling the Turkey Hill Elementary School. This upgrade from CAT5 cabling allowed an increase in the overall
  24|             network speeds as well as support for a phone system.
  25|             2. Installation of a new phone system and phones in every room at the Turkey Hill Elementary School. This helped get
  26|             Turkey Hill Elementary School in line with multiple E911 compliance laws by having phones which can dial out in eve-
  27|             ry instructional area.
  28|             3. Phone server CPU upgrades at both the Lunenburg Middle High School and the Lunenburg Primary School.
  29|             4. Switch to SIP Trunking phone plan which will finish the compliance in which every phone has the ability to call out
  30|             and the emergency personnel will receive both the building as well as the room number of the caller.
  31|             Solutions supporting cyber safety were also addressed this year. PhishID Phishing Protection was implemented which
  32|             scans and detects malicious login pages for staff. The district also implemented KnowB4 which sends out phishing simu-
  33|             lation emails as well as weekly and monthly cyber tips to all staff members to help protect against cyber criminals.
  34|             Technology continued to be a prevalent tool in the classroom. In response, the district continued to support devices for
  35|             each student by providing new devices to 6th and 9th graders as well as introducing new devices in grades k-5 to replace
  36|             end of life equipment.
  37|             The IT Department continued to manage and respond to all help desk requests with an extra focus on following best
  38|             practices, student technology usage and excellent customer service. The team will continue to direct efforts towards im-
