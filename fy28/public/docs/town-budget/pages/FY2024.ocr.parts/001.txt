   1|                                                                         In Memoriam
   2|                                 James J. Larkin, affectionately known as Jim, left the world on March 31, 2024, leav-
   3|                                 ing a legacy of love and laughter. Born on June 24, 1940, in Newton, MA, Jim's reli-
   4|                                    able good nature and funny wit made him a beacon of joy to all who met him. Jim
   5|                                    was a devoted husband to his wife Sheila of 45 years, who survives him. He was a
   6|                                   very positive role model and loving stepfather to Darrell Maki. He was preceded in
   7|                                  death by his parents, Robert and Mary (Lynch) Larkin and his brother Robert Larkin
   8|                                                                                                Jr.
   9|                                  Jim was a dedicated systems engineer in the technology sector at GenRad and Poly-
  10|                                    com before his retirement. He was a passionate member of the Maserati Club, the
  11|                                     Mid State Antique Auto Club, and the Antique Auto Club of America. May God
  12|                                                 grant him a front row seat at the Antique Auto shows in Heaven.
  13|                                   His interest in family genealogy and the history of Lunenburg led him to become a
  14|                                  curator for the Lunenburg Historical Society, a role he cherished, especially Whalom
  15|                                  Park memorabilia. He served in the US Navy from 1961-1966. Jim was also an avid
  16|                                  metal detector, always looking for hidden treasures. Jim, his siblings, and family fre-
  17|                                                    quently shared many stories of their bird watching sightings.

   8|                                                                     TOTAL AREA- 26.63 MILES
   9|                Lunenburg was settled in 1718 and incorporated in 1728. It occupies a land area of approximately 26.4
  10|                  quare miles. Lunenburg is locatea in north central Massachusetts approximately 43 miles from Bo in, It is bordered on the west by the Town of Ashby and the City of Fitchburg, on the south by th
  11|               Town of Lancaster and the City of Leominster, on the east by the Town of Shirley and on the north by
  12|                the Town of Townsend.
  13|                Local legislative decisions are made by an open town meeting. Subject to the legislative decisions
  14|                made by town meeting, the affairs of the town are generally administered by an elected five member
  15|                Select Board and a Town Manager and local school affairs are administered by an elected seven
  16|               member School Committee.
  17|                Lunenburg is a semi-rural community located in northern Worcester County. Lake Whalom and the for-
  18|                mer Whalom Amusement Park delineated the Whalom District from the remainder of Lunenburg. This
  19|                district was developed as a housing base for many factory workers working in the mills and factories of
  20|                Leominster and Fitchburg. In many ways the characteristics of the Whalom District closely reflect charac-
  21|               teristics of these cities more than of the rest of Lunenburg. The Hickory Hills Lake area was developed as
  22|                a private community comprised largely of vacation cottages. Shirley Reservoir is a public water body also
  23|                developed with mainly vacation cottages that have now been modified to year-round residences.
  24|                 Commercial and industrial development is permitted in several areas of town, primarily along the two
  25|                major arteries (Routes 2A and 13) and the town's peripheries, due largely to the close proximity of
  26|                Leominster's and Fitchburg's sewer and water systems. Although many farms and orchards have
  27|                slowly developed into single family housing, farming continues on in Lunenburg, In 2013 the Annual
  28|                Town Meeting voted to establish an Agricultural Commission to represent, preserve and enhance
  29|                farming in the community and to administer the "Right to Farm" bylaw,
  30|                 The 2015 Lunenburg Town Meeting established the Architectural Preservation District ("District")
  31|                 bylaw to regulate construction, demolition or renovation of existing "Contributing Property" struc-
  32|                 tures as defined by the bylaw within an established bounded district. The bylaw was introduced at
  33|                 Town Meeting along with the Village District Bylaw, which created an overlay zoning district to en-
  34|                 courage mixed-use commercial and residential development. While the Village District Bylaw en-
  35|                 courages the economic vitalization of downtown Lunenburg, the Architectural Preservation District
  36|                 Bylaw ensures that it will not come at the expense of the town's historical character and integrity.

   1|                                                           REPORT OF THE TOWN MANAGER
   2|                                                                                   Fiscal Year 2024
   3|              Note: The Fiscal Year 2024 Report of the Town Manager was not authored by Town Manager Heather Lemieux. The content
   4|              herein is based on a review and synthesis of periodic Town Manager Reports provided to the Select Board.
   5|              Fiscal Year 2024 (FY24) was a year of meaningful progress for the Town of Lunenburg, marked by the advancement of long-
   6|              planned capital and infrastructure projects, significant strides in restoring the public's confidence in the property tax assessment
   7|              process, important legislative wins for our senior residents, a coordinated response to multiple storm events, and continued work
   8|             to address staffing needs across Town departments. Despite ongoing challenges, the dedicated staff, appointed and elected offi-
   9|              cials, and volunteers of the community continued to deliver a high level of service to Lunenburg residents. Notably, Town Man-
  10|              ager Heather Lemieux tendered her resignation, after over seven years of service, to the Select Board on June 4, 2024.
  11|              Below are the highlights of the key topics and accomplishments from Fiscal Year 2024 within the Office of the Town Manager.
  12|              Town Manager Goals
  13|              As in prior years, the Town Manager's goals for FY2024 centered on three primary areas: continuing to advance the redevelop-
  14|              ment/reuse of 925 Massachusetts Avenue, review of financial policies and address long-term staffing and unknown infrastructure
  15|              needs to help inform the budget process, and employee recruitment, recognition, and retention. Progress was made across all three
  16|              areas during the fiscal year.
  17|              925 Massachusetts Avenue Redevelopment
  18|              The redevelopment of 925 Massachusetts Avenue remained a top Town Manager goal throughout FY2024, with substantial pro-
  19|              gress made toward readying the site for disposal and future reuse.
  20|             In the fall of 2023, the EPA-funded contractor completed the removal of the hydraulic lift and underground holding tank at the
  21|              site, with hazardous materials submitted for proper off-site disposal. The Town's environmental consultant, Joe McLaughlin of the
  22|              BETA Group, continued to coordinate with the EPA and MassDEP on follow-up environmental work. The EPA approved addi-
  23|              tional Brownfields funding to conduct soil borings, monitoring well installations, and groundwater sampling to address remaining
  24|              data gaps beneath the building.
  25|              In February 2024, the Town Manager met with MassDevelopment and their consultant from BSC Group, engaged through the
  26|              Real Estate Technical Assistance program, to develop conceptual plans, perform a market analysis, and draft a Request for Pro-
  27|              posals for the disposal of the property. A site visit took place in March 2024, with work on a strict timeline to expend the technical
  28|              assistance funds by June 30, 2024. The Historical Society also expressed interest in preserving the cupola atop the 925 Mass Ave
  29|              building, and the Town Manager referred the request to the APDC for review and incorporation into the property's disposition
  30|              plans. Conceptual plans yielded two options: maintaining the same footprint of the building if it were to be a renovation and one
  31|              that would be new construction.
  32|              Infrastructure, Facilities, and Capital Projects
  33|              Flat Hill Culvert Project
  34|              The Flat Hill Road Culvert replacement was a major project in FY2024. Funded in part by a $500,000 Small Bridge Grant, the
  35|               roject went out to bid in early 2024 with the lowest responsive and responsible bid coming in at $830,230. The Town Manager
  36|               uccessfully secured an additional $100,000 from MassDOT to cover higher-than-estimated costs, bringing total available funding
  37|              to approximately $975,000. Construction was planned to begin in the fall of 2024 when lake water levels are lowered, with an
  38|              estimated two-month road closure.
  39|              Page Street Culvert Project
  40|              The Page Street Culvert replacement, performed by Powell Stone & Gravel, was completed in September 2023 on time and under
  41|              budget. The road was closed briefly to allow excavation and installation of the new pipe, and the guardrail was installed shortly
  42|              thereafter.

  25|               The LAAC Generator project, included in the FY23 Capital Plan, was bid in the fall of 2023. Four bids were received and the

  27|               completed in the spring of 2024.

  30|               Manager was issued in early 2024. After the Park Commission evaluated respondents and conducted interviews, the Town final-

  32|               and completion of PARC Grant-funded construction by June 2025. The Parks Commission also engaged a firm for the prelimi-

  34|               Fund grant. The Town also finalized the Right of Entry Agreement with the Lunenburg Skate Park organization, with construc-

  45|               report was submitted in November 2023. MassDOT subsequently developed a work order to address additional low-cost safety

   2|               Tax Assessment and Billing
   3|               FY2024 continued to involve significant complexity in tax assessment and billing. With the Town's property revaluation not
   4|               completed until February 2024, the Bureau of Local Assessment approved a valuation delay, and the Town issued third quarter
   5|               preliminary tax bills using a Pro Forma Recap approved by DOR in December 2023. The Treasurer/Collector and Interim Princi-
   6|               pal Assessor, Rena Swezey, worked extensively to test billing processes in Munis before generating final bills, and a detailed tax
   7|               bill insert was prepared to explain the unusual billing cycle to taxpayers.
   8|               After the FY24 fourth quarter actual tax bills were mailed, the Assessors Department discovered a software error in Munis that
   9|               caused an incorrect total taxable value to be printed on bills for property owners with exemptions. The payable amount on every
  10|               bill was correct. The Town proactively sent correction letters to all affected taxpayers with copies of the accurate Vision Property
  11|               Cards, and a ticket was submitted to Tyler Technologies to resolve the issue.
  12|               FY25 preliminary tax billing also required outside contractor assistance due to persistent software challenges with Tyler Tech-
  13|               nologies. These bills incorporated the estimated additional tax levy from the override approved by voters in May 2024.
  14|               The Town's New Growth for FY24 came in at approximately $630,000, exceeding the original estimate by $230,000 and provid-
  15|               ing a meaningful addition to the tax base.
