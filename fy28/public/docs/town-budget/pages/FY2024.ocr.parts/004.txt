   3|             Teaching and learning is at the center of all of our work. This past year, we worked diligently to provide high-quality
   4|             curricular materials and instruction to students, provide strategic interventions and challenges to our learners, and to pro-
   5|             vide professional development opportunities for our educators to support their efforts.
   6|             Providing educators with high quality, evidence based, and inclusive curricular materials is a priority for the state of
   7|             Massachusetts' Department of Elementary and Secondary Education (DESE) and for our district. Access to these types
   8|             of curricular materials can "prompt improvement in student outcomes" as evidenced by research. To this end in 2023-
   9|             2024, educators:
  10|             → implemented the second year of our new English language arts and math curriculums, HMH Into Reading and Into
  11|                   Math, at grades K-5. HMH online professional development was provided on both the platform and content. Pro-
  12|                   fessional Learning Community (PLC) meetings occurred to review assessment data, make adjustments to instruc-
  13|                   tional practice based on the data, improve instruction through coaching and modeling, and module planning based on
  14|                   priority standards.
  15|             → began implementation of Amplify, our new middle school language arts curriculum. Educators taught all of the mod-
  16|                  ules at least once and made adjustments and refinements to practice. The specific focus in the first year was looking
  17|                  at each of the program components and determining which were best implemented through the online platform, and
  18|                  which were better to do on paper or through the use of Amplify's supplemental materials that supported the curricu-
  19|                  lum. Additional work was done on pacing to ensure that the critical units were taught and mastered.
  20|             → completed a process for selecting a new high-quality math program for our middle school as well as developed an
  21|                  implementation process. Middle school math teachers created the criteria by which they would select a new math
  22|                  program. Once that document was finalized, a curriculum review of all high-quality middle school math programs
  23|                   was completed in PLC meetings. Three programs were selected for a pilot of one month each, using all components
  24|                   of the program's units. Grade levels documented their feedback and a group meeting was held to discuss the merits
  25|                   of each program. It was unanimously decided to choose the EdGems program based on its quality, and combination
  26|                   of differentiated online and paper content that was easily accessible to teachers and students.
  27|             MTSS
  28|             A multi-tiered system of support (MTSS) was implemented at Lunenburg Primary and Turkey Hill Elementary School.
  29|              The key components of MISS include universal screening and progress monitoring tools: a process to analyze data, plan
  30|             for interventions, and monitor student progress; a tiered system of support where every student receives core instruction
  31|             and some students receive strategic or intensive instruction; and a collaborative team-based approach. In short, MTSS is
  32|             a way of doing things within the schools that impacts every student. At each school, teams of educators came together to
  33|             plan for and check student growth. They worked with our literacy and math specialists to analyze multiple data points so
  34|             that strategic and intensive interventions for support and/or enrichment were provided. Student progress was monitored
  35|             and adjustments were made as necessary.
  36|             Lunenburg Public Schools utilizes multiple assessments to evaluate student achievement and growth. Students in grades
  37|             K-8 completed a fall, winter and spring iReady assessment in both reading and mathematics and the personalized learn-
  38|             reading and mathematics. The Dynamic Indicators of Basic Literacy Skills (DIBELS) is used in grades K-3 to deter-
  39|             mine how students are performing on the critical skills necessary for reading: phonemic awareness, phonics, fluency,
  40|             vocabulary, and comprehension. In mathematics grades K-5, the Forefront Universal Screener for Number Sense was
  41|             used to determine students' early numeracy competency.
  42|             Structured common planning time for teachers in grade level continued for grades K-8. Our district literacy coaches met
  43|             with teachers during common planning time to support curriculum development and help foster student growth. High
  44|             school department heads structured department meeting time to facilitate the necessary curriculum work needed to move
  45|             each department forward and improve learning for students.

   2|             Lunenburg Middle School provides a rigorous academic curriculum as well as strong programs that support students'
   3|             social and emotional growth. These programs ensure that each child becomes a self-motivated, creative, lifelong learner
   4|             in a safe, stimulating environment that encourages diversity and open honest communication. The dedicated and ac-
   5|             complished staff of professionals within the school embraces the philosophy of educating the whole child academically,
   6|             socially, and emotionally. This philosophy is a means by which all students become positive community members that
   7|             possess the skills to be successful in high school and beyond.
   8|             Lunenburg Middle School is a co-located middle - high school serving grades six through eight. Our effective, student
   9|             centered middle school embraces interdisciplinary teams of teachers that enable teachers to work with smaller groups of
  10|             students. Smaller groups of students allow teachers to learn and engage students in their preferred learning style, making
  11|             them more confident and successful.
  12|             Our effective middle school model is student centered and provides opportunities to explore a variety of different inter-
  13|             ests and talents through special areas and enrichment classes and extracurricular activities. One of our goals is for stu-
  14|             dents to discover something they are passionate about and want to continue exploring as they move through high school
  15|             and beyond. We will continue to seek out opportunities for students that foster critical thinking and problem solving
  16|             skills that prepare them for high school and beyond or where their passion may lead them to.
  17|             Our grade eight team of teachers continue to implement a schedule that mirrors the high school schedule. Our philoso-
  18|             phy is to ease the difficult transition from middle school to the first year of high school by developing student's inde-
  19|             pendent skills they will require to be successful in high school. Grade eight teachers function as one inclusive team
  20|             where students take classes with a variety of different teachers as they would in a high school setting. This differs from
  21|             our two interdisciplinary team of teachers that have been a successful model in grades six and seven.

   1|                                     LETTER FROM LEADERSHIP
   2|                                                            In the long-storied history of the Montachusett Regional Vocational Technical School
   3|                                                            District, I am confident that the 2023-2024 school year will stand out as one of the more
   4|                                                           memorable years in its workforce training legacy. Personally, it marked my second year
   5|                                                           as Superintendent and tenth, overall at Monty Tech. Thus, as a proud district leader, and
   6|                                                           in support of        the ground-breaking efforts of our students, faculty, staff                           and
   7|                                                           administration, it is my honor to highlight some of those initiatives with you, our school
   8|                                                           community, in this annual report.
   9|                                                            Throughout 2023-2024, Monty Tech took significant steps to address a growing concern
  10|                                                           facing vocational education in the Commonwealth - the overall lack of capacity to meet
  11|                                                           the demand for vocational training. In Monty Tech's case, well over 800 8th-grade
  12|                                                           students will typically apply for the approximately 350 available seats in the incoming
  13|                         freshmen class. Unable to simply accept more students here, Monty Tech implemented a creative strategy using a
  14|                          state-funded Lab Modernization grant to lease a nearby vacant warehouse with the intent of converting it into a
  15|                          contemporary workforce training facility that, when completed, will provide students training in House Carpentry,
  16|                          Electrical and Plumbing trades. The district navigated the design and permitting process that allowed for the initial
  17|                          renovation to begin, and formed meaningful partnerships with Fitchburg, Gardner and Narragansett School
  18|                          Districts.     The Department of Elementary and Secondary Education (DESE) approved the partnerships?
  19|                          applications, and as a result, the Montachusett Vocational Partnership (MVP) Academy was slated to begin in 2024-
  20|                          2025, serving as many as 84 high school students in its first year.
  21|                          While the MVP Program will result in the largest expansion of workforce training in our region, it represents only
  22|                          one dedicated effort to offer such instruction to a variety of constituencies. Monty Tech remains committed to
  23|                          offering career skills training to adult-students who are unemployed or underemployed by providing no-cost
  24|                          offerings in a number of fields. In the fall of 2023, Monty Tech was awarded a $720,000 Career Technical Initiative
  25|                          grant, so that even more adult learners would have an opportunity to earn valuable industry-recognized credentials,
  26|                          hands-on learning experiences, and receive job placement services upon program completion.
  27|                          Monty Tech also identified a previously underserved population, young adults with disabilities, and successfully
  28|                          delivered one of its most rewarding programs to date - the Supportive Training & Education Program (STEP) in
  29|                          the spring of 2024. Initiated through an award of $133,900 in funds made available through the Commonwealth
  30|                          Corporation, Monty Tech developed a 12-week program that included hands-on and professional skills training as
