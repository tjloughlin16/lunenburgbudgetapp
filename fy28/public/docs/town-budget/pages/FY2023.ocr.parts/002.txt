   1|                                                                        BOARD OF ASSESSORS
   2|                    The Mission Statement of the Board of Assessors is to accurately determine the value of all Real
   3|             and Personal Property within the Town of Lunenburg, as outlined in Massachusetts General Law
   4|             Chapter 59. The assessed value in every community in the Commonwealth must reflect fair market
   5|             value as of January 1 prior to the start of any given fiscal year.
   6|                    The Assessors are also responsible for the administration of all property tax data records. It
   7|             maintains accurate parcel ownership data based on recorded property transactions at the Worcester
   8|             North Registry of Deeds. The Assessors process and administer auto excise as well as personal real
   9|             estate tax exemptions for the Senior and Surviving Spouse, Blind, and Disabled Veterans.
  10|                    The Board of Assessors is elected and consists of three members. Each Assessor must complete a
  11|             required DOR Training (Course 101, Assessment Administration: Law, Procedures, and Valuation for
  12|             assessing board members). In the Town of Lunenburg, the Assessor's office staff consists of a full
  13|             time Principal Assessor and a full time Assessors Clerk.
  14|             The last 18 months have been tumultuous for the Board of Assessors.
  15|                    There has been a 100% turnover of the Board of Assessors' membership. 1 new Member was
  16|                    elected in May 2023, while 2 new Members were appointed to fill vacant slots until May 2024.
  17|                    The Principal Assessor resigned and was replaced with an Interim Lead Assessor. After a lengthy
  18|                    search process, a full time Lead Assessor has been identified and will start late March 2024.
  19|                    The Town has issued a historic number of Property Tax abatements in FY 2023. We handled a 30
  20|                    x increase in Abatement applications (440 vs 14). The overwhelming majority of the abatements
  21|                    were settled in a timely fashion.
  22|                    FY 2024 is a state-mandated 5-Year Revaluation for the every property in Lunenburg. The Prop-
  23|                    erty Tax abatement effort led to an unprecedented delay in completing that revaluation and de-
  24|                    termining the tax rate for FY 2024. This resulted in a delay in distribution of the final FY 2024 tax
  25|                    bills. Because of the unusual and significant work load, two outside agencies, Vision Government
  26|                    Solutions, Inc. and Municipal Financial Services (MFS), have been engaged to provide assessment
  27|                    personnel for the abatement process and DOR filings for the 5-year Revaluation.
  28|                    The Assessor Operations continue be resource-constrained. Currently, we only have 1 Interim
  29|                    Lead Assessor, and are temporarily short the Assessor Clerk. In the MFS report on the FY2023
  30|                    Abatement process, it was recommended that Lunenburg add a part time person to help per-
  31|                    form the normal Assessor Office work load. This included site inspections, office coverage, data
  32|                    analytics and records maintenance. Currently, the resource constraint is being partially ad-
  33|                    dressed by the external contractors previously mentioned.
  34|                    The Board of Assessors is committed to restoring the trust of Lunenburg tax-payers. We hope to
  35|             accomplish this through improving transparency, better out-reach, and enhancing visibility and ac-
  36|             cess.
  37|                    We have begun this by scheduling all meetings during evenings (with both PACC coverage and
  38|             Zoom access), so everyone can participate more easily. We created a Data Collection Manual for use
  39|             during the 5-year Revaluation. We are also putting the finishing touches on a guide for "How to Read
  40|             Your Vision Property Card", that will be available in hard-copy and via the internet.

   1|                                                                 CONSERVATION COMMISSION
   2|               The Town of Lunenburg Conservation Commission is a seven-member commission, each appointed to 3-year terms by the
   3|               Select Board, whose duties are codified under the Conservation Commission Act MGL chapter 40 s 8C. The Commission
   4|              enforces the Massachusetts Wetland Protection Act MGL chapter 131 s 40 and Chapter 239 of the local bylaws for the pro-
   5|              tection of wetlands. The Commission also manages, and controls town owned lands retained for conservation and passive
   6|              recreation use.
   7|               During 2023 the commission had a very busy and active agenda of public hearings for a wide range of projects from com-
   8|              mercial development, subdivisions and small single family upkeep projects and larger scale industrial/commercial pro-
   9|               posals.
  10|               The Commission also held many inspections with the Commission Administrator on weekends prior to meetings to famil-
  11|              iarize themselves with the upcoming proposals on the agenda. The Commission continues to work with the town on famil-
  12|              iarizing itself with the continually changing storm water mandates from the EPA and working towards compliance both by
  13|               procedure and with any necessary codification as well as aiding with the community implementations the "MS4" regula-
  14|               tions.
  15|               The Conservation Commission continues to implement forest stewardship plans developed using grants as outlined in the
  16|              last three annual reports. The upcoming management effort will be concentrated on the Rayno property on Northfield
  17|              Road. The Hollis Road property stewardship plan is oriented toward enhancing bird wildlife habitat with the Mass Audu-
  18|              bon Society as part of the DCR "Forestry for the Birds" program. The funding from the sale of harvested timber provides
  19|              the seed money to make it possible for the Commission to apply for grants to maintain and improve Lunenburg's conserva-
  20|               tion lands. In 2023, the Conservation Commission was the beneficiary of the Shawn H. Kelley Estate which will further
  21|               support ongoing maintenance and improvements.
  22|              The Pollinator Habitat portion of the Hollis Rd property provides for a diversity of native flowers throughout the growing
  23|              season. The Habitat promotes insect egg laying and nesting sites for pollinators. The Habitat provides food sources for
  24|               birds and numerous insects including native bees and butterflies. This unique "Hidden Gem" helps control soil erosion and
  25|               oxygenates our air.
  26|              The Commission has made significant progress on "You Are Here" trail map signs at trail heads and trail intersections to
  27|               expand the safe use of Lunenburg's existing trail network. In April of 2023, Conservation partnered with Parks to coordi-
  28|               nate a month of Earth Day events across Lunenburg. The joint project improved 13 properties and brought 361 man-hours
  29|              of improvements to Lunenburg. In June of 2023, the Conservation Commission was awarded a MassTrails grant for fund-
  30|              ing planning of parking areas in Table Rock and Rayno Conservation Area, and improved access to the Harold Harley
  31|               Wildlife Viewing Platform in the Cowdrey Conservation Area. The Lunenburg Snow Riders continue to support the
  32|               maintenance and improvement of the trail system on Lunenburg's conservation lands. As part of their Recreational Trails
  33|               grant award from 2022, the Snow Riders recently completed the Lane Conservation Area trail improvement project with
  34|               aid from Baer Construction, Dan Deleo Plumbing, Gatsby Grounds, M. Proctor Masonry, and the assistance of 17 volun-
  35|              teers, including numerous volunteer tractors. The Commission would like to express our gratitude for all the hard-working
  36|               volunteers that work so enthusiastically in maintaining and improving Lunenburg's conservation lands!
  37|              The Commission has also continued to work closely with Lake Shirley and Hickory Hills on their lake management plans.
  38|              These plans set goals and action items designed to give a better picture of the overall health of the lakes and define various
  39|               strategies to ensure continued protection of these valuable resources.
  40|              During the year 2023, there were changes of personnel along creating staffing shortages. In July, Conservation Administra-
  41|              tor Anna Petrie resigned. Approximately May, Conservation Administrative Assistant, Rebecca Manley resigned. In July,
  42|              Ashleigh Denio was hired to backfill Rebecca. In October, the Land Manager, Adam Burney resigned. Adam was the front
  43|              -line management support for the Conservation Commission staff and had been providing direction to the interim Conser-
  44|              vation Administrator. There have been several interim administrators hired to bridge the gap in support since Anna's depar-
  45|              ture. There has also been an interim clerical resource provided to mitigate the backlog in Meeting Minutes. There is still a
  46|              backlog of Conservation Administrator actions, and in some cases significant delays in the issuance of documentation, ex-
