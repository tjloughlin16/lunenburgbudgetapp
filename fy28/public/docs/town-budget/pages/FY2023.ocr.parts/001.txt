   1|                                                                                IN MEMORIAM
   2|              Stanley Barney passed away on July 12, 2023. Stan was born to the late Wilbur Walker Barney and Therese
   3|              Annette (LaChance) Barney on August 14, 1950.
   4|              Stan is survived by his loving wife Suzanne (Anderson) Barney, daughter Stephanie Barney and her partner
   5|              Alyssa Sharpe, sister Marie Barney, brother Michael G. Barney and wife Janice, nephew Michael R. Barney
   6|              and his fiancée Jillian Doucette, and several nieces, nephews, cousins and extended family. A son, Patrick M.
   7|              Barney, predeceased him in 2019.
   8|              Stan was blessed with a circle of lifelong friends, many of whom were at his side at the time of his passing.
   9|              In his litetime, Stan was involved in a wide range of activities: working at the family business (Barney's Res-
  10|              taurant and Dairy Bar), caring for animals, gardening, woodworking, and serving as captain of the Blue
  11|              Knights Football Team (#61!). He was an avid Boston sports fan, and was a Celtics season ticket holder for
  12|              many years. An avid gardener, Stan maintained beautiful flower and vegetable gardens. He gave away most of
  13|              the produce and flowers he grew, including pumpkins, jars of pickles he made with his mother and sister, and
  14|              fresh cut flowers. He loved the outdoors and even built his own lumber mill at his residence. Stan loved fishing
  15|              and snowmobiling with his friends. Stan was a life member of the Eastwood Club.
  16|              A 1968 graduate of Lunenburg High School, Stan attended Merrimack College and graduated from Fitchburg
  17|              State University. He married the former Suzanne Anderson of Great Barrington Massachusetts on October 6,
  18|              1973. Devoted to the Town of Lunenburg, Stan served for 38 years on the Lunenburg Police Department,
  19|              moving through the ranks to Sargent in 2000 and finally Lieutenant in 2013. He cherished serving the commu-
  20|              nity as a police officer along with his son. Stan was also a lifetime member of the Lunenburg Police Relief As-
  21|              sociation, serving as its President in 1984. He also served several terms as a Cemetery Commissioner for the
  22|              Town of Lunenburg, overseeing the maintenance and care of town cemetery grounds.
  23|              Stan was also employed by the law enforcement division of the Massachusetts Society for the Prevention of
  24|              Cruelty to Animals from 1986 to 1991, serving throughout 40 cities and towns. Devoted to animal rescue, Stan
  25|              built a barn/stable at his Lunenburg residence and provided a safe residence for rescued horses, including draft
  26|              horses and Clydesdales.

   8|                                                                     TOTAL AREA- 26.63 MILES
   9|                Lunenburg was settled in 1718 and incorporated in 1728. It occupies a land area of approximately 26.4
  10|                  quare miles. Lunenburg is locatea in north central Massachusetts approximately 43 miles trom Bo in, It is bordered on the west by the Town of Ashby and the City of Fitchburg, on the south by th
  11|               Town of Lancaster and the City of Leominster, on the east by the Town of Shirley and on the north by
  12|                the Town of Townsend.
  13|                Local legislative decisions are made by an open town meeting consisting of all registered voters in
  14|               the town. Subject to the legislative decisions made by town meeting, the affairs of the town are gen-
  15|                erally administered by an elected five member Select Board and a Town Manager, Local school af-
  16|               fairs arc administered by an elected seven member School Committee while local taxes are assessed
  17|                by an elected three member Board of Assessors.
  18|                Lunenburg is a semi-rural community located in northern Worcester County. Lake Whalom and the for-
  19|               mer Whalom Amusement Park delineated the Whalom District from the remainder of Lunenburg. This
  20|                district was developed as a housing base for many factory workers working in the mills and factories of
  21|                Leominster and Fitchburg. In many ways the characteristics of the Whalom District closely reflect charac-
  22|               teristics of these cities more than of the rest of Lunenburg. The Hickory Hills Lake area was developed as
  23|                a private community comprised largely of vacation cottages. Shirley Reservoir is a public water body also
  24|                developed with mainly vacation cottages that have now been moditied to year-round residences.
  25|                Commercial and industrial development is permitted in several areas of town, primarily along the two
  26|                 major arteries (Routes 2A and 13) and the town's peripheries, due largely to the close proximity of
  27|                 Leominster's and Fitchburg's sewer and water systems. Although many farms and orchards had slow-
  28|                ly developed into single family housing, farming continues on in Lunenburg, In 2013 the Annual
  29|                 Town Meeting voted to establish an Agricultural Commission to represent, preserve and enhance
  30|                farming in the community and to administer the "Right to Farm" bylaw,
  31|                 The 2015 Lunenburg Town Meeting established the Architectural Preservation District ("District")
  32|                 bylaw to regulate construction, demolition or renovation of existing "Contributing Property" struc-
  33|                tures as defined by the bylaw within an established bounded district. The bylaw was introduced at
  34|                 Town Meeting along with thẹ Village District Bylaw, which created an overlay zoning district to en-
  35|                 courage mixed-use commercial and residential development. While the Village District Bylaw en-
  36|                 courages the economic vitalization of downtown Lunenburg, the Architectural Preservation District
  37|                 Bylaw ensures that it will not come at the expense of the town's historical character and integrity.

   3|               It is my honor to submit the annual report on the activities of the Office of the Town Manager during Fiscal Year 2023.
   4|                There was a substantial amount of progress during Fiscal Year 2023, yet the year proved to be a continuation of the
   5|               effects of the pandemic and a mid-year tax assessment crisis that affected lakefront property owners occurred that im-
   6|               pacted the capacity to do more during Fiscal Year 2023. Since 2020, the Town has struggled with staffing levels in var-
   7|               ious departments, including attracting and retaining employees. The Town Manager and Assistant Town Manager have
   8|                worked on various ways to address staffing, including recruitment strategies and employee engagement. An interim
   9|               valuation adjustment through the Assessors triggered hundreds of abatement applications to be filed and the effects
  10|               were widespread. In the aftermath of the increases in lakefront valuations, certain processes and corrective actions have
  11|                been undertaken by the Select Board, Town Manager and Board of Assessors that will prevent a similar occurrence
  12|               from happening again.
  13|               Below I would like to highlight the topics that were addressed during Fiscal Year 2023 and the accomplishments with-
  14|               in the Office of the Town Manager:
  15|               • In July of 2022, as part of the annual goal setting process, the Selectmen reviewed the and voted to accept the
  16|                     Town Manager's annual goals for FY23. The Town Manager's goals for FY 2023 included continuing to take the
  17|                     necessary steps towards the redevelopment/reuse of 925 Massachusetts Avenue, enhancement of long term plan-
  18|                     ning in the areas of capital assets, staffing and infrastructure, and focusing on employee recruitment, recognition,
  19|                     and retention. A majority of the action items related to these three goals were completed.
  20|               • In regards to 925 Massachusetts Avenue, the Town Manager continued to work Joe McLaughlin of BETA Group,
  21|                     MassDEP, the EPAand MassDevelopment about possible cleanup funding. In FY 2023 the EPA paid for removal
  22|                     of the fuel tanks and canopy as part of the Targeted Brownfields Assessment funding. The Town Manager also
  23|                     applied for funding through MassDevelopment's Real Estate Technical Assistance funding to create conceptual
  24|                     plans, perform market analysis, and develop a Request for Proposals in order to dispose of the property. As it relat-
  25|                     ed to one of the action items under the Town Manager's annual goals for road and bridge planning, the DPW Di-
  26|                     rector worked with BETA Group to update the Town's five year pavement management plan and identified the
  27|                     costs of creating a similar plan for bridges and culverts, that would be undertaken in FY24.
  28|               • In July 2022, the Select Board set their goals for the upcoming year: they amended their FY22 goal/work on the
  29|                     old Primary School at 30 School Street, work with the Town Manager and Finance Director to establish a means-
  30|                     tested senior citizen property tax exemption or abatement, and lastly to review and update Select Board related by-
  31|                     laws and policies.

  33|                     DPW Director and engineering firm on submitting was awarded to the Town for the Flat Hill Culvert project. This

   1|                        At the January 10th meeting, a large number of residents around Lake Shirley, Hickory Hills, and Lake
   2|                    Whalom attended the Select Board meeting to speak during public comment about their 3"d and 4" quarter
   3|                    tax bills increasing significantly. Many residents voiced that their property valuations increased more than
   4|                    50%. On January 17* the Select Board held a special meeting at the Middle/High School Auditorium and
   5|                    hundreds attended. The meeting was scheduled to allow the public to ask questions to the Board, Principal
   6|                    Assessor, and Board of Assessors. Ultimately the Select Board voted to conduct an investigation into the
   7|                    effects that the pandemic had on property values in 2021, the corresponding impact on the FY 2023 tax
   8|                    assessments, and the process by which the Town conducted property valuations and distributed tax assess-
   9|                    ments to residents. The Chairman of the Board led the investigation, with the assistance of the Vice-
  10|                    Chairman, and in coordination and collaboration with the Town Manager. The following six months of
  11|                    the fiscal year were significantly impacted by the large increase in lake property valuations, including the
  12|                    need to hire an outside consulting firm to conduct the over 400 property inspections that resulted in
  13|                    $1,317,690 in abatements that were issued in the fourth quarter of FY23. The consultant issued a report to
  14|                    the Board of Assessors in FY23, and reported that the Principal Assessor found that two-thirds of the sales
  15|                    that occurred in calendar year 2021 had an ASR (Assessment to Sale Ratio) of .40, indicating that a major
  16|                    adjustment was needed in order to bring values in line with the Department of Revenue guidelines (.90-
  17|                    1.10). When the Principal Assessor made this interim year adjustment, it created an increase in land val-
  18|                    ues in some cases of around 135%. The consultant recommended creating a new adjustment factor that
  19|                    addressed that not every lakefront property is the same, including "Good, Average, Below Average". The
  20|                    Board of Assessors endorsed this recommendation and this determined the revised valuation and the
  21|                    abatements issued.

  24|                    Post-Employment Benefits Trust Fund for the unfunded liability of retiree health insurance in the amount

  26|                    the amount of $100,000, $250,000 towards the Special Purpose Stabilization Fund, and use $1,500,000 in
  27|                    free cash towards the Overlay account to fund the "worst case scenario" for FY23 property tax abate-

  29|                    in hours for different positions including the Assistant Town Manager, the COA Outreach Coordinator,
