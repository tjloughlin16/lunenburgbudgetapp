   8|                        While the Montachusett Regional Vocational Technical School District educational community is certainly
   9|                       proud of the achievements of our talented students, faculty, and staff, we continue to have an eye toward the
  10|                       future, always committed to improving our vocational and academic programming, strengthening key
  11|                       partnerships, and maintaining facilities that contribute to student success and achievement. As we look
  12|                       ahead, there are a number of programs and initiatives that we expect will have a positive impact on our
  13|                       school and students for years to come.
  14|                       Expand partnerships with area businesses: To support the school's popular Business Technology program,
  15|                       specifically addressing the financial elements found within the program's frameworks, school leaders began
  16|                       exploring opportunities to bring banking to Monty Tech students. In collaboration with Workers' Credit
  17|                        Union, school officials are pleased to announce a developing partnership that will result in a full-service
  18|                       branch located on the school's campus, offering students opportunities to develop authentic financial literacy
  19|                       skills and experience while still in high school. Additionally, students studying in the school's Business

  21|                       banking institutions across North Central Massachusetts.
  22|                       Expand partnerships with area colleges and universities: Students in Monty Tech's very busy Health
  23|                       Occupations program may just be getting busier in the coming years. While the program already offered a
  24|                       number of industry-recognized credentials, school leaders began working with college partner, Mount
  25|                        Wachusett Community College, to determine if a college-level Emergency Medical Technician (EMT)
  26|                       training program could be embedded into the senior year of study. We are pleased to announce that this
  27|                       collaboration has resulted in an agreement that will effectively allow seniors to earn 8 college credits, at no
  28|                       charge, and the opportunity to sit for the certification exam in June 2016. By providing additional career
  29|                       pathways in the medical field, and access to college level coursework while still enrolled in high school,
  30|                       school administrators and teachers are confident that program graduates will not only be more qualified for
  31|                       entry level positions, they will be more apt to find an area of interest within the medical field, and continue to
  32|                       refine the technical skills they developed while at Monty Tech.
  33|                       Expand Access to Instructional Technology: A Monty Tech education is effective because efforts are made
  34|                       to continually access our programming, instructional equipment, and our educational resources. Through
  35|                       this assessment, it has become clear that while so many students have access to technology away from the
  36|                       school, many do not. It is important, then, to arm every student with the tools necessary to succeed not only
  37|                       in the workplace, but in the classroom, as well. The district is pleased to announce a large-scale technology
  38|                       initiative that began in August 2015, when every educator at Monty Tech was provided with a Chromebook
  39|                       and individualized professional development on how to best incorporate this added technology into daily
  40|                       instruction. So many teachers have embraced the concept, and continued to explore learning opportunities
  41|                       that have resulted in classroom instruction that better reflects our 21" Century school. The Monty Tech
  42|                       technology initiative continues, and in August 2016, every student at Monty Tech will also be provided with
  43|                       a Chromebook to support his/her learning, more efficiently communicate with teachers, and to promote
  44|                        collaboration among student peers.

   3|                       Section XXXII ARCHITECTURAL PRESERVATION DISTRICT
   4|                        Section 1: Purpose
   5|                        This bylaw is enacted under the Home Rule Amendment of the Massachusetts Constitution for the purposes of:
   6|                       (a) Preserving, restoring and protecting groups of historically and/or architecturally significant buildings and the
   7|                       characteristics of their neighborhoods that are important to the town's architectural, cultural, economic, political
   8|                       and/or social history;
   9|                       (b) Fostering wider public knowledge and appreciation of such neighborhoods and Contributing Properties;
  10|                       (c) Limiting the detrimental effect of alterations (substantial & minor), additions, and demolitions on the character
  11|                        of such Contributing Properties and their neighborhood settings;
  12|                       (d) Reviewing proposed alterations (substantial and minor) including demolition of or to any Contributing Property
  13|                       located within the APD, for appropriateness and compatibility with the existing buildings, setting and neighborhood
  14|                        character; and
  15|                       (e) Facilitating the protection of the APD through a regulatory review.
  16|                        To achieve these purposes, the Town of Lunenburg may designate APDs to be administered as set forth in this
  17|                        bylaw.

  20|                        ADDITION: Any addition to a principal or accessory Contributing Property that is enclosed by walls and which
  21|                       increases the existing gross floor area.

  23|                       bylaw, a process to review proposed alterations to Contributing Properties and identify the need for Binding Review.

  27|                       Contributing Property, which retains the character of the property as determined by the Architectural Preservation
  28|                       District Commission. Examples include, but are not limited to windows, doors, roof material and trim work.
  29|                       ALTERATION, SUBSTANTIAL: A change to a Contributing Property or part thereof, such as removal,
  30|                       reconstruction, restoration, replication, rehabilitation, addition, or demolition; and/or a change to a site that includes,
  31|                       enlarging or moving a building and other similar activities.
  32|                       ARCHITECTURAL PRESERVATION DISTRICT ("APD"): An area of historic and /or architectural
  33|                       significance determined by the Town's residents to be worthy of preservation.
  34|                        BINDING REVIEW: Pursuant to Section 8 of this bylaw, a mandatory process which applies to substantial
  35|                       alterations of contributing properties, including demolition, in the APD.
  36|                        BUILDING: A combination of materials having a roof, the purpose of which is the shelter of persons, animals,
  37|                       property, or processes, including any other permanent structure.
  38|                        CERTIFICATE TO ALTER: A document issued by the APDC allowing Alterations pursuant to Section 8 of this
  39|                       bylaw.

   8|                       PREAMBLE
   9|                       The Lunenburg Town Center region has tremendous importance in symbolizing the unique character and heritage of
  10|                       Lunenburg. As residents engage with the historic buildings and streetscape of this area in the course of their routine daily

  14|                       of people who will be drawn to share in it by expanding the businesses and other attractions in the area. There is also a key
  15|                       challenge to preserving this critical embodiment of our town. Appropriate new uses or remedies must be found to the

  17|                       Town planning experience and best practices, as exemplified in the state's model bylaw for Village Center Districts,
  18|                       demonstrates some key ingredients to sustaining and enriching this type of environment in small towns. One of these is
  19|                       careful stewardship through clear guidelines for development consistent with what the area means to the community.
