   1|                                                                              In Memoriam
   2|                        Marion M. Benson, 83
   3|                        Marion passed away on December 27, 2015 in Leominster. She was very active in the Town of
   4|                        Lunenburg where she lived for 40 plus years. She was an active volunteer in both church & civic
   5|                       groups, including the School Committee. After her return to Lunenburg in 1981, she began
   6|                       volunteering on the Planning Board, where she quickly became Chairperson. She volunteered for
   7|                       two decades on the Board at which time the position evolved into full-time Planning Director; a
   8|                       position she held until 2014. In May 2015, her many years of service were recognized at Annual
   9|                       Town Meeting by a Ritter Building conference room being named in her honor.
  10|                                             August "Gus" S. Misner, 78
  11|                                             Gus passed away on July 15, 2015 in Togus, Maine following a long battle with cancer.
  12|                                            He was born in Fitchburg on December 17, 1936 where he attended school and played
  13|                                            football. He served in the U.S. Army from 1955-1964, was stationed in the Arctic and was
  14|                                            an aide to President Eisenhower. He later became a police officer in Lunenburg for several
  15|                                             years until ill health forced his retirement.
  16|                                         Senator Mary Padula, 89
  17|                              Mary Louise Padula, nee Harley, passed away on October 29, 2015. Born in Fitchburg and
  18|                              nearly a lifetime resident of Lunenburg, Mary served as Town Clerk, Tax Collector and
  19|                              Treasurer for 30 years. Upon leaving the Town Hall, she represented the Second Worcester
  20|                              and Middlesex District in the Massachusetts Senate for 8 years, and as part of Governor
  21|                              Weld's cabinet, served as Secretary of Housing & Community Development for 5 years.
  22|                              She served as President of the Massachusetts Treasurer and Tax Collector Association and
  23|                              as Assistant Minority Leader in the Senate and on many boards and committees. She was
  24|                             recognized by Fitchburg State College as one of the 100 citizens who made the greatest
  25|                             difference in North Central Massachusetts in the last 100 years.
  26|                                                David M. Shea, 67
  27|                                                David Shea passed away on August 8, 2015 after a courageous battle with cancer. David
  28|                                                was an atmospheric scientist with an environmental consulting company for 37 years.
  29|                                                He was a longtime resident of Lunenburg. David was an original founder of the
  30|                                                Lunenburg Recycling Commission and a member of the Board of Health for 30 years.
  31|                                                He was a passionate golfer who enjoyed playing as well as organizing an annual golf
  32|                                                tournament. David was a soccer coach and a high school soccer referee for many years.
  33|                                                David was known for his great sense of humor and his unending energy. He seemed to
  34|                                                know everyone and was referred to many times as the "Mayor of Lunenburg."

   1|                                                                                     LUNENBURG
   2|                       Lunenburg          was settled in 1718 and incorporated in 1728. It occupies a land area of
   3|                       approximately 26.4 square miles. Lunenburg is located in north central Massachusetts
   4|                       approximately 43 miles from Boston. It is bordered on the west by the Town of Ashby and the
   5|                       City of Fitchburg, on the south by the Town of Lancaster and the City of Leominster, on the east
   6|                       by the Town of Shirley, and on the north by the Town of Townsend.
   7|                        Local legislative decisions are made by an open town meeting consisting of all registered voters
   8|                       in the town. Subject to the legislative decisions made by town meeting, the affairs of the town
   9|                       are generally administered by a five member Board of Selectmen and a Town Manager. Local
  10|                       school affairs are administered by an elected seven member School Committee while local taxes
  11|                       are assessed by a three member Board of Assessors, all are appointed on an at-large basis for
  12|                       staggered three-year terms.
  13|                       Lunenburg is a semi-rural community located in northern Worcester County. Three water bodies
  14|                       account for somewhat of a clustered approach to the town's historical development. Lake
  15|                       Whalom and the former Whalom Amusement Park delineated the Whalom District from the
  16|                       remainder of Lunenburg. This district was developed as a housing base for many factory
  17|                       workers working in the mills and factories of Leominster and Fitchburg. In many ways the
  18|                       characteristics of the Whalom District more closely reflect characteristics of these cities than of
  19|                       the rest of Lunenburg. The Hickory Hills area of town, around Hickory Hills Lake, was
  20|                       developed as a private community comprised largely of vacation cottages. The third body of
  21|                       water is Shirley Reservoir, a public water body also developed with mainly vacation cottages
  22|                       that have now been modified to year-round residences. Commercial and industrial development
  23|                       is permitted in several areas of town, primarily along the two major arteries (Routes 2A and 13),
  24|                       and the town's peripheries, due largely to the close proximity of Leominster's and Fitchburg's
  25|                       sewer and water systems. Although many farms and orchards had slowly developed into single
  26|                       family housing, farming continues on in Lunenburg. At the Annual Town Meeting on May 4*
  27|                       2013, the Town voted to establish an Agricultural Commission to represent, preserve, and
  28|                       enhance farming in the community and to administer the "Right to Farm" bylaw.
  29|                                                 Lunenburg Centre, Lunenburg, Mass.

   1|                                                                    REPORT OF THE TOWN MANAGER
   2|                        The Town continued to make important progress in 2015 in many areas, including: School Building;
   3|                         Building Reuse; Increased Public Transportation; and updating the Pavement Management Plan. The MS/
   4|                        HS building is well under construction and set to open in September 2016. Fifty-eight (58%) of the
   5|                        project's eligible costs will be paid through a grant from the MA School Building Authority. As of
   6|                         December 31, 2015, approximately $30M of the Town's total share had been permanently financed in order
   7|                        to take best advantage of the low interest rates.
   8|                        The Board of Selectmen (BOS) appointed Building Reuse Committee continued its work throughout 2015.
   9|                        In the summer, the Committee issued a Request for Proposals for a Building Reuse and Space Needs Study.
  10|                        Town's goal for the Project was to assess the existing conditions of four (4) buildings owned by the Town
  11|                        and determine the most cost effective building(s) to accommodate the Town's current and anticipated space
  12|                        needs for Town administrative operations currently housed at Town Hall and remote sites. The building(s)
  13|                        that are determined not to be advantageous to the Town may be deemed as "surplus property" and disposed
  14|                        of in accordance with M.G.L. c. 30B§16. The Assessment and Space Needs study include the following
  15|                        four (4) buildings: Town Hall; Ritter Memorial Building: Thomas C. Passios Elementary; and the Brooks
  16|                        House. In late summer, the Town hired Tappé & Associates, the same firm serving as Architect on the MS/
  17|                        HS School Project, to complete this study. The results of the study were presented to the BOS in 2016.
  18|                        The BOS worked diligently with the Department of Public Works to update its existing Pavement
  19|                        Management Plan. The updated plan built upon the foundation of the existing plan but included additional
  20|                        evaluation criteria. The original plan was developed in 2009 and funding decisions from that point up until
  21|                        FY16 were based upon that plan. Beginning in FY2017, all funding decisions will be based upon the
  22|                        revised plan which established a PCI, or Pavement Condition Index, for each roadway and a Prioritize List
  23|                        which takes into account both the functional classification of the roadway and the needed repair. The
  24|                        Board further determined that a PCI of 70 would be considered a generally acceptable pavement condition
  25|                        and worked on determining the best funding mechanism for the PMP Program to insure that maintenance
  26|                        needs would be addressed uniformly across all roadway classifications. To that end, the Board agreed to
  27|                        put forth a debt exemption question to the voters, seeking an additional $4M in tax dollars to complete the
  28|                        large backlog of needed maintenance & repair work. The work would be completed over a period of 3 - 4
  29|                        years and would be financed over a period of at least twice as long. Thereafter, the Town would endeavor
  30|                        to include additional funding within the Operating Budget to provide for a sufficient level of funding to
  31|                        maintain roads at a PCI of 70.
  32|                         The Town also continued to work on its largest and most comprehensive roadway project, the
  33|                        redevelopment of Summer Street, a project slated to begin in fall of 2016. In 2014, the Town committed
  34|                        $230,312 from Chapter 90 Funding to fund the Town's portion of the 75% design. The project is expected
  35|                        to cost about $7M, all of which will be funded though the Regional Transportation Improvement Plan
  36|                        (TIP), which is a grant of State & Federal funds. The project was approved for TIP funding for FFY2017.
  37|                         One of the most exciting projects which continued into 2015 was the implementation of enhanced public
  38|                        transportation within Lunenburg. The Town pays both an annual assessment to the MART and to the
  39|                         MBTA. The assessment to MART pays for local/ regional transportation. The assessment to the MBTA
  40|                        pays for access to the commuter rail system in Fitchburg and Leominster. The Town learned through
  41|                        MART that there is a provision in State Law that would allow the Town to use its MBTA Assessment to
  42|                        purchase additional local transportation, at no additional cost to the taxpayer. Accordingly, the Town
  43|                        undertook a review of residents' transportation needs and began working with MART to provide additional
  44|                        local transportation. The additional services began in spring 2015, though ridership was low. The Town
  45|                        was able to revise its transportation offerings to include limited service along the full length of Route 2A,
  46|                        from the town center to the Shirley line. The Town will continue to monitor ridership and make
  47|                        adjustments as needed to best service the public.
  48|                        I would be remiss if I did not take this opportunity to thank all of the people in this town who give of their
  49|                        time and many talents to make Lunenburg a better place. Thank you all for your dedicated service.
