   9|                       The Lunenburg Property Tax Work-Off Program offers income eligible senior citizens who are age 60
  10|                       plus an opportunity to receive up to $1,000 off their property tax bill in the calendar year rather than the
  11|                       fiscal year. Participants worked in various town departments to earn the tax abatement doing general
  12|                       office duties, custodial, library shelving, and kitchen assistance.
  13|                       For each hour worked a person received $10 and worked up to 100 hours between January 1, 2015 and
  14|                       November 1, 2015 to receive the abatement. Any hours worked beyond that were considered volunteer
  15|                       hours.

  19|                             • Applicants must be 60 years of age or older

  21|                             • Receive a property tax bill in their name or their spouse's name who is also 60 years of age or
  22|                                   older
  23|                             • Meet the income guidelines of :
  24|                                   One person household - $40,000 / Two person household - $60,000
  25|                             • All new applicants are required to have a CORI background check and must re- apply and be

   1|                                                                                 SEWER COMMISSION
   2|                       The five member Sewer Commission was originally established at the Special Town Meeting on May 6, 2006.
   3|                       At the first Sewer Commission meeting after the May 2014 Town election, when the Commission elects the
   4|                        officers for the next year, Carl Luck was elected to remain as Chairman, Dave MacDonald to serve as Vice-
   5|                       Chair and Mike Nault to serve as Clerk. Other members on the Commission were Butch Bilotta and Troy
   6|                        Daniels, who was elected to the Sewer Commission in the May 2014 Town Election as a write-in candidate to
   7|                        fill the vacancy left by Jay Simeone who did not run for re-election. The Sewer Commission bears the
   8|                       responsibility of overseeing the Town's sewer system, planning for the future, and developing and implementing
   9|                       rules, regulations and policies. The Department of Public Works manages and maintains the operational side of
  10|                       the system. Under the Town's Charter, responsibility for the day-to day operation, care and maintenance of the
  11|                       sanitary sewers are under the supervision of the Town Manager. The Town of Lunenburg does not operate a
  12|                       sewer treatment plant. Approximately 81% of Lunenburg sewer is discharged to the City of Leominster, and the
  13|                       other 19% is discharged to the City of Fitchburg.
  14|                        There are currently 764 properties connected to the Lunenburg system and another 378 that have access to Town
  15|                        Sewer but are not yet connected. During FY15, 36 new accounts connected to Town Sewer. The Commission
  16|                       billed out $715,400 in quarterly sewer bills, and had $41,759 of prior year delinquencies added to the owners'
  17|                       property tax bills for non-payment. Nine properties with access to sewer were assessed privilege fees that had
  18|                       not originally been assessed betterments. Privilege fees help to pay down the debt owed by the Town on the
  19|                       original sewer project.
  20|                       In FY15, it was required by the State Department of Revenue that the Sewer Enterprise and the Sewer
  21|                       Betterment Funds be combined for a consolidated statement, and for the first time, the Commission had to
  22|                       project the year-to-year income over expense as pertains to betterment income versus debt service. With these
  23|                       revisions to the budget, it was necessary to request a transfer from Retained Earnings for $77,401.80 (Article 17,
  24|                        May 2015 Town Meeting) to cover both general expenses and debt service for the next fiscal year. The goal of
  25|                       the Sewer Commission is to maintain a balanced budget going forward and to build up the Retained Earnings so
  26|                       that it may be used for major capital projects to maintain and improve the operating efficiency of the system.
  27|                       In August 2013, the Sewer Commission began contracting with Small Water Systems Services of Littleton
  28|                       (SWSS) for the Operations and Management of the 10 pumping stations and sewer infrastructure. The Sewer
  29|                       Commission has been very satisfied with the personnel, the work and the arrangement with SWSS, renewing the
  30|                       contract again for FY15. Since the start of the contract, additional responsibilities have been added to the SWSS
  31|                       contract, including installing and monitoring the SCADA system at the Leominster Road station in March 2015
  32|                       and taking on the maintenance of the 10 generators housed in our pumping stations.
  33|                       The Sewer Commission continued work on preparing for the major sewer project in the Pratt Street/Lakeview
  34|                       Ave sections of town, using the State Revolving Fund (SRF) program throughout FY15, to connect 80 plus
  35|                       homes in areas of need as determined by the Comprehensive Wastewater Management Plan to public sewer.
  36|                        Forty-two of these homes were in close proximity to Whalom Lake and were of concern to the homeowners,
  37|                        Board of Health and environmentalists. The proposed sewer extensions had attained a satisfactory rating by the
  38|                       State to be assured of an SRF loan and the Commission presented the project at the spring Town Meeting in
  39|                       May 2014, obtaining approval to move the project forward. The Commission worked continuously with the
  40|                       engineering firm, Wright Pierce, throughout the year on design and engineering. In the spring of 2015, the
  41|                       Commission put the project out to bid and in May 2015 awarded the construction bid to CJP Construction out of
  42|                        Millis, MA, with construction to commence in the summer of 2015.
  43|                       Also in the spring of 2015, the Commission, working with Weston & Sampson Engineering, continued their
  44|                       work to find sources of I/I (Inflow/Infiltration) by performing 171 house-to-house inspections in the Mass
  45|                       Ave./Leominster Rd. areas in the spring of 2015. These efforts were funded by the Sewer Bank Fee, established
  46|                       in 2013 as required by the Inter-Municipal Agreement with the City of Leominster, collecting $220. per
  47|                       bedroom on new connections. Weston & Sampson worked to ensure proper connections from these 171

  18|                       an "on call" basis. This adds several minutes or more to our response time as personnel must respond to the

  20|                            The addition of the Weekend Day coverage began on July 1, 2015 and has significantly decreased response

  22|                       even though we have seen an almost 48% increase in our call volume during that same time period. We still rely
  23|                       on our On Call Personnel to be the primary responders 50% of the week.
  24|                           2015 was our busiest year to date, with a 1.5% overall increase in activity from 2014 and a 5.5% increase in
  25|                       EMS related calls. As our community continues to grow I can only expect the demand for emergency services to
  26|                       grow along with it. While our Call Force continues to do an amazing job, the ever increasing call volume is
  27|                       straining our ability to provide the high level of service townspeople have come to expect. This leads me to
  28|                       continue to call for 24 hour staffing in the station. Funding to maintain 2 Firefighter/EMT's in the station 24
  29|                       hours a day, 7 days a week would allow either the first ambulance or first piece of fire apparatus to respond
  30|                       immediately. Not only would this reduce our response times to critical emergencies, it would also reduce the
  31|                       "wear and tear" on our Call Staff. This coverage would in no way reduce the need for our On Call personnel; it
  32|                       would only supplement their response and reduce response times.

  36|                       lifesaving skills. Personnel train regularly both here at the station and at classes sponsored by other agencies.
