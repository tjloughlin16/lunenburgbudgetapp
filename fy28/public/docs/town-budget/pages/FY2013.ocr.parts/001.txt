   1|                                                                 IN MEMORIAM
   2|                                                     Richard J. Bishop was born in Fitchburg, graduated Lunenburg High School,
   3|                                                     attended Fitchburg State College, graduating in 1956 and receiving his Master's
   4|                                                     degree in 1962. He taught sixth grade in 1956 only to be drafted into the U.S. Army
   5|                                                     serving from1957 until 1959. He returned to teaching in Lunenburg and led many
   6|                                                     of his students on new adventures when he instituted the Hiking Club, Biking Club
   7|                                                     and Skiing Club. Dick's final class was in in 1993 when he retired after 36 years of
   8|                                                      dedicated services. Dick was a true "Lunenburger" in every sense of the word
   9|                             serving as Town Moderator for 22 years as well as being involved in Little League, Biddy Basketball
  10|                             and the Community Council. Dick served 50 years in Boy Scouts rising to the rank of Eagle Scout
  11|                             and also enjoyed 50 years in the Lunenburg Grange. Richard received the Joel Miller Outstanding
  12|                             Educator Award from Fitchburg State College, the district Award of Merit from the Boy Scouts of
  13|                             America. In 1966 he was recognized as one of the Ten Outstanding Young Men of the Year in the
  14|                             greater Worcester Area.
  15|                                                     Claire Marie (Cosgrove) Ebersole was born in Malden, Massachusetts and was a
  16|                                                     resident of Lunenburg for 57 years. Mrs. Ebersole was a Librarian at Memorial
  17|                                                     Middle School in Fitchburg MA from its opening in1968 to 1984. She was an
  18|                                                      English Teacher in Vermont in the early 1940's. She graduated from Milton High
  19|                                                      School in 1937, Regis College in 1941 and Boston University in 1942 with a
  20|                                                     Master's Degree in English. She was a very active member of the Lunenburg
  21|                             Historical Society, a Red Cross volunteer, a Lunenburg Library Trustee and a Cub Scout Den
  22|                              mother. She served on the high school curriculum advisory committee. Over the years she
  23|                             volunteered countless hours at the Library and Historical Society. She was an avid Red Sox fan.
  24|                              She was a Veteran of World War Il, serving as a Lieutenant, jg in the U.S. Navy WAVES from 1943 to
  25|                              1945 and was awarded the American Campaign Medal and World War I Victory Medal.

   1|                                                                            REPORT OF THE TOWN MANAGER
   2|                                    The Town continued to make important progress in 2013 in many areas, including:                   School Building; Regional
   3|                                    Service Delivery; Municipal Electric Aggregation; and Building Reuse.                In 2013, we renewed our consultant
   4|                                    contract for our Municipal Electric Aggregation Plan with Colonial Power Group. Under the Aggregation Plan,
   5|                                    the Town, on behalf of rate payers, bids electric supply service with various suppliers. If the Town can secure
   6|                                    a better rate that what Unitil can offer as its default rate, then the Town will make that rate available. If not,
   7|                                    then Unitil's default rate stands. Since the inception of the Aggregation Plan in January 2012, the Town has
   8|                                    almost always been able to offer a more competitive rate. In total, Lunenburg residents and business
   9|                                    collectively have saved $183,301.72 under the Aggregation Plan!
  10|                                    In 2013, the School Building Committee continued its work on the Feasibility Study & Preliminary Design
  11|                                    Report, with the end goal being to bring to Town Meeting a recommendation on either building a new or
  12|                                   renovating an existing school building. Please review the Superintendent of School's report for additional
  13|                                    information.
  14|                                    In June 2013, the Town's E-911 Service was transitioned over to Nashoba Valley Regional Dispatch District
  15|                                    (NVRDD), a newly created district which provides E-911 Service to the towns of Harvard, Lancaster,
  16|                                    Lunenburg and to Devens. Lunenburg is the largest community in the district and it was the first town to
  17|                                    transition service. The decision to transition to the district was made after years of study and review of two
  18|                                    separate regional options. New state requirement to provide Emergency Medical Dispatch in addition to the
  19|                                   increasing number of calls for service would have necessitated an increase in staffing levels, resulting in a
  20|                                    higher cost to the Lunenburg taxpayer. Ultimately, it was determined that by opting for a regional service, we
  21|                                    could meet the new state requirements within our existing budget. From an end-user point of view, there has
  22|                                    been no change in service levels. In FY2015, the District anticipates expanding to include an additional town
  23|                                    which will reduce the annual assessment to each of the existing district members.
  24|                                    In October 2013, the Town received word that its Bond Rating had been increased from AA- to AAt, a
  25|                                    significant jump of two grades. Since 2009, the Town's bond rating has increased four grades from A to AA+.
  26|                                   The increase was made in recognition of the Town's overall improved financial condition, adherence to
  27|                                   adopted financial policies, and fiscal prudence. The increase in Bond Rating comes at a great time and will
  28|                                    In November 2013, the Board of Selectmen accepted a $25,000 grant from the Stanton Foundation which will
  29|                                   enable the Lunenburg Police Department to implement a K-9 Program. The grant will cover the cost of
  30|                                   purchasing, training and caring for the dog for the first 3 years of the program. The K-9 Unit will be used in
  31|                                   narcotics detection, assisting in locating missing persons, and in following suspects involved in crime.
  32|                                   In 2013, the Board of Selectmen established a Building Reuse Committee, specifically tasking it with the
  33|                                   following: identify space requirements for potential combined municipal operations; identify costs to convert
  34|                                   municipal space; identify 5 and 10 year capital costs to retain existing buildings including, Town Hall, Ritter
  35|                                   Memorial and Eagle House; identify appropriate options for sale or lease of facilities; and identify potential
  36|                                   reuse or market for any buildings deemed surplus as a result of consolidation. The Committee made its report
  37|                                   of findings to the Board in December. As a result, additional tasks were identified, including: communicating
  38|                                   findings and recommendations so they can be considered during School Building discussion; develop a "go
  39|                                                plan for the marketable properties; create a Request for Proposals Template after discussion with
  40|                                   stakeholders; develop consolidation plans for municipal functions and a renovations plan for Town Hall; and
  41|                                   make bottom-line cost and investment decisions and review sale and revenue projections. The Committee will
  42|                                   continue its work in 2014.
  43|                                   I would be remiss if I did not take this opportunity to thank all of the people in this town who give of their time
  44|                                   and many talents to make Lunenburg a better place. Thank you all for your dedicated service.
