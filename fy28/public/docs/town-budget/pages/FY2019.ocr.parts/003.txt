   3|                           The chance find of a gravestone that had been used as a walkway paver led to the Historical
   4|                           Society's research and rediscovery of the Arnold Litch Cemetery near Pleasant Street. The

   8|                          prepared new granite bases on which to reset the monuments. They have yet to be erected as the
   9|                          search continues for the exact location of the burial plot.
  10|                           The 18th century Battles House at 68 Elm Street was slated for demolition to make way for the
  11|                          development of new homes. The Historical Commission invoked the demolition delay bylaw to seek
  12|                          alternatives. By some combination of pressure from the Commission, cooperation of the property
  13|                          owner, and good fortune, the house was sold intact with its 19th century barn to a new owner who
  14|                          apparently intends to restore or renovate the old house.
  15|                          Several of the road signs marking the Lunenburg Historic District had gone missing over the
  16|                          years and plans were laid to order new signs to be installed by the DPW.
  17|                          The weathervane that once sat atop Town Hall was missing for some time. Commissioner Bill Lakso
  18|                          researched the design of the old weathervane and commissioned a new one to be constructed. The new
  19|                          weathervane had yet to be installed at the close of the year.
  20|                          With some regrets, the Commission approved the total demolition of the historic house at 822 New
  21|                          West Townsend Road. The owner had previously intended to restore the building but after stripping
  22|                          the interior discovered that decay and previous alterations had made the structure worthless.
  23|                          Recognizing the owner's predicament, the Commission approved the demolition application without
  24|                          delay.
  25|                          Lunenburg continues to own historical buildings in the town center. The old Primary School and to a
  26|                          lesser extent, the Brooks House, are falling into disrepair. The Town has no immediate municipal use

  28|                          buildings languish in limbo was a major motivation for Chair Lantry to begin planning for the

  12|                           under the supervision of the Town Manager who delegates the responsibility to manage and maintain

  14|                           Town of Lunenburg does not operate a sewer treatment plant. Approximately 78% of Lunenburg
  15|                           sewer is discharged to the City of Leominster, and the other 22% is discharged to the City of

  20|                           supervision of the town's sewer system. The Commission developed a scope of services for a new
  21|                           O&M contract and put it out for bid in April 2019. Small Water Services (SWSS) won the bid and
  22|                           the Commission began negotiations with SWSS for the new contract effective 7/1/19.

  24|                           access to Town Sewer but are not yet connected. During FY19, 24 new accounts connected to Town
  25|                           Sewer. The Commission billed out $835,329.23 in quarterly sewer bills, and had $44,556 of prior
  26|                           year delinquencies added to the owners' property tax bills for non-payment. Five properties were
  27|                           assessed a privilege fee in FY19. Two properties assessed were residential. Two assessments were
  28|                           for 24-unit apartment buildings and one other property was a 3-unit condominium building.
  29|                           Privilege fees help to pay down the debt owed by the Town on the original sewer project.
  30|                           Throughout FY19, the Commission continued to work with several different groups regarding their
  31|                           interest in sewer extensions, specifically at Lancaster Avenue, Upland Avenue and Chase Road. The
  32|                           Commission is always willing to discuss a potential sewer extension.
  33|                           In FY 19, the Commission worked on updates to the Grease Trap Regulations, Connection Charges
  34|                           Policy, Irrigation Meter Policy and Single Meter Policy. On October 30 and November 27, 2018,
  35|                           the Sewer Commission held public hearings to inform residents of the proposed changes. The
  36|                           Commission voted to accept the updated policies on November 27, 2019. For the new Irrigation
  37|                           Meter policy, the Commission investigated options for new updated wireless irrigation meters and
  38|                           worked with the Lunenburg Water District to select the new meter which can be read remotely by
  39|                           the Water District on their regular rounds. With the updated Irrigation Meter policy that was
