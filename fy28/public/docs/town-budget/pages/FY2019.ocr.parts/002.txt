   1|                                                                   CONSERVATION COMMISSION
   2|                        The Town of Lunenburg Conservation Commission is a seven-member commission, appointed to 3-year terms by
   3|                        the Board of Selectmen, whose duties are codified under the Conservation Commission Act MGL chapter 40 $
   4|                        8C. The Commission enforces the Massachusetts Wetland Protection Act MGL chapter 131 § 40 and Chapter 239
   5|                        of the local bylaws for the protection of wetlands.
   6|                        The year 2019 was as last year, one of a very busy and active agenda containing both public hearings and
   7|                        meetings for a wide range of projects from commercial development, subdivisions and small single family upkeep
   8|                        projects and larger scale industrial/commercial proposals along the Electric Avenue section of Lunenburg. The
   9|                        Conservation Commission worked to spell out goals of preparing to use the year 2020 to work on clarifying the
  10|                        land use policy to provide consistency with the original bylaw and to find those sections that may be now out of
  11|                        date and need to be modified. This effort is estimated to be completed by summer of 2020. The Conservation
  12|                        Commission completed revisions to the local wetland regulations which are now in effect and were presented to
  13|                        the Town Clerk for posting.
  14|                        The Commission also held numerous inspections with the Commission Administrator on weekends prior to
  15|                       meetings to familiarize themselves with the upcoming proposals that would appear on their agenda. The
  16|                        Commission continues to work with the Town on familiarizing itself with the upcoming storm water mandates
  17|                        from the EPA and working towards compliance both by procedure and with any necessary codification as well as
  18|                       aiding with the community Notice of Intent to the EPA under the "MS4" regulations.
  19|                        The Conservation Commission implemented its forest stewardship planning after receiving grants continuing the
  20|                        efforts outlined in last year's annual report. These stewardship plans, adding the Hollis Road property, is oriented
  21|                       for enhancing bird wildlife habitat in conjunction with the Mass Audubon Society as part of the DCR "Forestry
  22|                        for the Birds" and will aid the Commission in implementing environmental management plans which include
  23|                        timber stand management. The Commission retained Gary Gouldrup of New England Forestry Consultants, a
  24|                        noted forester with many years of experience working in Lunenburg and all of the adjacent towns/cities in this
  25|                        region serving those communities and private landowners. The Conservation Commission also held public
  26|                        recreational walks through its open space parcels and plans to continue to do so. The Commission has, and
  27|                        continues to hold public forums on the implementation of its open space policies and timber-stand management.
  28|                        As a result of some of the meetings and forums held, the Commission continues to apply for and receive funding
  29|                        from the Commonwealth to author stewardship plans for our Conservation Areas.
  30|                        Copies of the current environmental stewardship plans are available on the Conservation Commission website at:
  31|                        http://www.lunenburgma.gov/content/204/222/default.aspx
  32|                        This past year, the Commission successfully completed the collection of a single $11,000 fine for wetland
  33|                        violations on one single property. Aside from the collection of the fine, the Commission ordered and oversaw the
  34|                        successful remediation of the wetland resource areas being restored to pre-alteration condition.
  35|                       The Commission has also completed the successful implementation of the installation of handicap parking and
  36|                        handicap accessible trails at the Small Town Forest, Cowdrey and Lane Properties. The funding was provided by
  37|                        a grant from the Mass Office on Disability.

  39|                        of their management plans becoming actively involved in continuation of formulating updates designed to give a

  12|                                        Honesty and integrity are absolutes. They characterize the only forum in which anything
  13|                            worthwhile can be accomplished.
  14|                                        Efficiency: Make every effort to proceed correctly the first time. It may take a bit longer but
  15|                            will not require repeated attempts.
  16|                                        Loyalty up and down the chain of command is essential to success.
  17|                                        Every employee is essential. Those at the top are support people. The real work is done by
  18|                            the men and women who drive the trucks, operate the equipment, turn the wrenches, and provide a

  18|                         Tree Removal
  19|                                      Storm damage together with the natural attrition creates a backlog of tree felling and pruning
  20|                          that far exceeds the allotted budget. The Department is unable to fulfill each resident request and must
  21|                          prioritize work based upon the calculated degree of danger to residents and property. With the
  22|                          establishment of a Hazardous Tree Survey, a more efficient approach to defects has been established
  23|                          through prioritization. Sadly, the amount of work far exceeds the available revenue. Until there is a
  24|                          substantial infusion of funds, the DPW will act in a reactionary mode. All work aside from brush
  25|                          removal, chipping, and the felling of small diameter trees is outsourced due to the necessity of a bucket
  26|                          truck or crane and a licensed professional. Each spring DPW staff initiates the activity of removing and
  27|                         disposing of woody debris within the right-of-way together with a town-wide program of curbside

  29|                          Town to remove those trees that may cause an interruption of electrical service to the community.

  20|                          Winter Operations
  21|                          Snow and ice present a major challenge. The safety of the public depends on the ability of the DPW to
  22|                          maintain our roadways at a high level of safety despite the severity or the duration of snow and icing
  23|                          events. To that end, we have developed a winter operations procedure based upon a detailed plan with a
  24|                          flexibility to accommodate the unpredictability of each storm. The expectation of immediate service is
  25|                          high and must be delivered to every part of town in a timely manner.
  26|                                     Departmental preparedness begins with successful implementation of the capital plan.
  27|                          Financial support is necessary to provide effective fleet rotation insuring minimal interruption of
  28|                          services due to breakdowns and repairs. In the fall, salt is delivered to the Chase Rd. facility.
  29|                          Lunenburg is in a consortium with 14 other communities in an effort to procure the lowest possible
  30|                          price. From late summer to early fall vehicle maintenance staff inspects and prepares the trucks,
  31|                          spreaders and plows for winter operations. During the construction season road crews address right-of-
  32|                          way issues, overhanging branches, and pavement defects in order to eliminate obstacles to snow
  33|                          removal. Each division within the DPW is involved in winter operations. Typically, DPW staff
  34|                          combines with up to 14 subcontractors to provide service.
  35|                                      Generally, it is not the amount of snow but the duration of the event, that determines the
