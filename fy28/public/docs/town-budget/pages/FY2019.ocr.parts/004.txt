  29|                        Under the Department of Elementary and Secondary Education's accountability system, annual
  30|                        performance determinations for districts and schools are calculated using Indicators: English language
  31|                        arts (ELA), Mathematics, and Science achievement; ELA and Mathematics mean student growth
  32|                        percentile (SP); four-year cohort graduation rate, extended engagement rate, annual dropout rate;
  33|                        student progress towards attaining English language proficiency; chronic absenteeism, and percentage of
  34|                        11th & 12th graders completing advanced coursework
  35|                        Indicators are weighted. Substantial weight for achievement, growth, English language proficiency, and
  36|                        graduation rate indicators is required by federal law. Federal law also requires that these indicators
  37|                        combined should have much greater weight than the additional indicators. Under the accountability
  38|                        system, the weighting of achievement and growth is based on ratios instead of percentages, maintaining
  39|                        the achievement to growth ratio of 3 to 1.

   3|                           The focus of school maintenance for FY20 continues on preventative maintenance on
   4|                           heating and ventilation systems and energy conservation measures. The focus of repairs has
   5|                           been on Turkey Hill Elementary School with minor improvements such as switch
   6|                           replacement, painting, and other minor items.
   7|                           Security Upgrades
   8|                           The district applied for a Safer Schools and Community Grant in April 2019 and was
   9|                           awarded $55,500 for 32 additional security cameras. The project was bid with Johnson
  10|                           Controls Security Solutions being awarded the work at $45,286.70. Work began in
  11|                           November 2019 and is expected to be complete by January 2020. The number of additional
  12|                           cameras by school was Lunenburg Middle High School, 14, Turkey Hill Elementary School,
  13|                           10, Lunenburg Primary School, 8. Additional memory is being planned for the remainder of
  14|                           the grant funds.
  15|                           Water Testing
  16|                           In the second year of widespread water testing for lead and copper in the drinking water at
  17|                           all schools, in June 2019 lead was detected in a number of sources at Turkey Hill and
  18|                           Passios Elementary School. The source of the lead is believed to be solder from joining
  19|                           copper pipes. A number of high copper readings at Lunenburg Primary School suggest that
  20|                           the water district's corrosion control program needed to be reviewed. The water district has
  21|                           recommended that the ph level of the water needs to be raised. The teaching staff, parents and students have been made aware of the need to flush water sources at the start of the
  22|                           school day. Follow-up testing will occur at all schools in June 2020.
  23|                           Energy Savings
  24|                           The LED lighting conversion for Turkey Hill was completed in April 2019 with $36,233 in
  25|                           Green Community grant funds which paid $14,347.53 for materials and $21,855.47 to
  26|                           Duoba Electrical Contracting to install. The Green Community grant was supplemented by
  27|                           $1719 from the school budget and the remainder of $14,500 from a Unitil Corporation
  28|                           energy rebate.
  29|                           Capital Program
  30|                           The FY20 capital program continued to fund additional asbestos abatement at Turkey Hill
  31|                           Elementary School. In July 2019 Air Quality Experts, for $154,704.29, removed spray-on
  32|                           fireproofing above the ceiling on the south stairway and the second floor hallway of the
  33|                           classroom wing. This project included removal of the splined ceiling and the floor tiles in
  34|                           those areas. Turkey Hill Elementary School is expected to continue to have additional
  35|                           asbestos removal in the coming year with three first floor classrooms still to be abated. A
  36|                            request tor $ 123,000 tor F Y21 was submitted into the town's capital planning process to
  37|                            abate rooms 102, 113 and 114. The school department also requested funds to begin air
  38|                           conditioning design for the Lunenburg Primary School classroom wing and the turkey Fill
  39|                           administration area. Three split air conditioning systems would be added to cover the main

   1|                                                                          Lunenburg Primary School
   2|                        The Lunenburg Primary School is an early childhood education center for students in preschool to
   3|                        second grade. Our students range from three years old to eight years old. Our school community is
   4|                        made up of students, teachers, parents and support staff working together to maintain a learning
   5|                        environment that focuses on the academic and social emotional needs of the whole child.
   6|                        The October 1, 2019 student enrollment reported to the Department of Elementary and Secondary
   7|                        Education was 421 students. Currently, our Preschool program services 45 three and four year old
   8|                        students. We have six Kindergarten classes servicing 123 students. Class sizes in our Kindergartens are
   9|                        at 20-21 students. This year we have six first grade classrooms servicing 147 students with class sizes
  10|                        ranging between 23-25. Five second grade classrooms have 119 students with class sizes averaging at
  11|                        24 students.
  12|                        Our professional development has centered on literacy instruction and Social Emotional Learning this
  13|                        year. In early October we received notification from Governor Charles D. Baker that the school was
  14|                        awarded a FY20 Early Grades Literacy grant of $37,705. This grant funded teacher participation in an
  15|                        extensive professional development opportunity that included three statewide literacy institutes and
  16|                        follow-up regional meetings. Along with a consultant who works with teachers to provide clear, specific
  17|                        examples of how the early literacy aligns with our goals and supports the staff in a coaching model.
  18|                        We explored the development of writing in young children (birth-9); the role language plays in literacy;
  19|                        and the content of the Massachusetts ELA/Literacy Framework Standards for Language. Teachers
  20|                        discussed strategies to engage early grades students in writing, and thinking about the content they are
  21|                        learning in order to write with purpose and reason. The grant team shared information with grade level
  22|                        teams throughout the year. Our second grade team has worked with Mary Canner from Teachers21 and
  23|                        they have focused on implementing, Lucy Calkins: Units for Study for teaching reading, this Readers'
  24|                        Workshop model helps teachers provide their students with instruction, opportunities for practice, and
  25|                        concrete doable goals to help them meet and exceed any set of high standards. The main goal of this
  26|                        design is to prepare students for any reading and writing task they will face and to turn kids into life-
  27|                        long, confident readers and writers who display agency and independence.
  28|                        Our staff works to maintain a school culture which is positive, inclusive, and supportive. In September
  29|                        and November all of the Primary staff participated in two full days of professional development on
  30|                        Positive Behavior Intervention and Supports (PBIS). Positive Behavioral Interventions and Supports
  31|                        (PBIS) is an evidence-based three-tiered framework for improving and integrating all of the data,
  32|                        systems, and practices affecting student outcomes every day. The system supports all students through a
  33|                        multi-tiered framework and this year's focus was the Universal Tier (Tier 1). We have worked with
  34|                        Maureen Gilman of Seaside Educational Consultants to develop universal school-wide positive
  35|                        expectations that are practiced throughout our school community. We have worked on establishing and
  36|                        teaching the school wide expectations along with having a continuum of encouraging expected behavior
  37|                        and discouraging problem behaviors. This system is aligning nicely with The Anchors of Emotional
  38|                        Intelligence, a whole-school/district-wide approach to teaching social emotional learning which the staff
  39|                        has been trained in and is using to help students recognize, understand, describe, communicate and
  40|                        regulate their emotions.
  41|                        The Primary School is a Title One school and we have continued our tiered instructional system to
  42|                        insure that all students have the services they need to be successful. Common assessments are
  43|                        completed in the fall, winter, and spring for all children in grades K-2 in the areas of reading, writing,
  44|                        and math. These common assessments serve to monitor the effectiveness of teacher instruction and the
  45|                        progress of students. Struggling students are identified for additional support in Reading, Math, Speech,
  46|                        Occupational and Physical Therapy.
