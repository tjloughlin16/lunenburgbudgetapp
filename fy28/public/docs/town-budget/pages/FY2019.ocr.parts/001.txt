   8|                                                                        AREA- 26.63 SQUARE MILES
   9|                                                                          ROAD MILES- ABOUT 100
  10|                          Lunenburg was settled in 1718 and incorporated in 1728. It occupies a land area of
  11|                          approximately 26.4 square miles. Lunenburg is located in north central Massachusetts
  12|                          approximately 43 miles from Boston. It is bordered on the west by the Town of Ashby and
  13|                          the City of Fitchburg, on the south by the Town of Lancaster and the City of Leominster, on
  14|                          the east by the Town of Shirley, and on the north by the Town of Townsend.
  15|                          Local legislative decisions are made by an open town meeting consisting of all registered
  16|                          voters in the town. Subject to the legislative decisions made by town meeting, the affairs of
  17|                          the town are generally administered by a five member Board of Selectmen and a Town
  18|                          Manager. Local school affairs are administered by an elected seven member School
  19|                          Committee while local taxes are assessed by a three member Board of Assessors, all are
  20|                          appointed on an at-large basis for staggered three-year terms.
  21|                          Lunenburg is a semi-rural community located in northern Worcester County. Three water
  22|                          bodies account for somewhat of a clustered approach to the town's historical development.
  23|                          Lake Whalom and the former Whalom Amusement Park delineated the Whalom District
  24|                          from the remainder of Lunenburg. This district was developed as a housing base for many
  25|                          factory workers working in the mills and factories of Leominster and Fitchburg. In many
  26|                          ways the characteristics of the Whalom District more closely reflect characteristics of these
  27|                          cities than of the rest of Lunenburg. The Hickory Hills area of town, around Hickory Hills
  28|                          Lake, was developed as a private community comprised largely of vacation cottages. The
  29|                          third body of water is Shirley Reservoir, a public water body also developed with mainly
  30|                           vacation cottages that have now been modified to year-round residences. Commercial and
  31|                          industrial development is permitted in several areas of town, primarily along the two major
  32|                          arteries (Routes 2A and 13), and the town's peripheries, due largely to the close proximity of
  33|                           Leominster's and Fitchburg's sewer and water systems. Although many farms and orchards
  34|                          had slowly developed into single family housing, tarming continues on in Lunenburg.

   1|                                                           REPORT OF THE TOWN MANAGER
   2|                        Each year that I sit down to write this report I pour over the minutes of the previous year to
   3|                        recollect all of the achievements we were able to complete. Some may think the wheels of
   4|                        government turn slowly, but for those of us that have chosen local government as our careers
   5|                        and know the various steps and stakeholders involved to get things done, it is amazing all that
   6|                        we are able to accomplish. The calendar year of 2019 was no exception. The Town Manager
   7|                        and Board of Selectmen continue to work hard on serving the best interests of the Town and
   8|                        providing a high level of services while remaining fiscally responsible to the taxpayers.
   9|                        During 2019 there were many topics addressed and accomplishments to highlight:
  10|                        • As part of the annual goal setting process, the Selectmen reviewed their goals from FY19
  11|                              and formalized their goals for FY20. The goals the Board identified for FY2019 included
  12|                             identifying, defining, discussing, and finalizing the criteria to be used in determining the
  13|                             use and/or re-use of the Town Hall, Ritter, old Primary, Brooks House, and TC Passios and
  14|                             to propose at least one project to the 2020 Annual Town Meeting regarding the use and/or
  15|                             reuse of the old Primary School. The Board identified the criteria used in determining the
  16|                              use and/or re-use of the aforementioned buildings in the fall of 2019 and discussed the
  17|                             future use of the old Primary throughout the year and continued to narrow down the focus
  18|                              for the future use of this building into 2020.
  19|                        • The Town Manager's goals included developing and implementing steps towards the
  20|                             redevelopment/reuse of 925 Massachusetts Avenue, implementation of a recycling
  21|                              contamination enforcement program, and planning/implementing accessibility
  22|                              improvements.
  23|                        • There were multiple projects that addressed accessibility at various town facilities, town
  24|                              property, and public rights of way throughout the year. In January the Town Manager
  25|                             announced receipt of an ADA Project Grant from MA Office on Disability for $7,500 for
  26|                              accessibility improvements to the Lane, Cowdrey, and Small Town Forest properties. Also
  27|                              in January the Veterans Service Office was relocated from the Eagle House to the Ritter
  28|                              Memorial Building to be more accessible for veterans as the previous office was located on
  29|                              a second floor without a handicap lift so the Veterans Officer was relocated to the Ritter
  30|                              Memorial Building lower level that has a lift. The Town Manager coordinated the use of
  31|                              Montachusett Technical students to renovate the second floor Town Hall restroom to
  32|                              become a handicapped accessible bathroom and this was started in February and completed
  33|                              by May. In March the Town Manager applied for and ultimately received a District Local
  34|                              Technical Assistance grant to update the Town's ADA Self-Evaluation and Transition
  35|                              Plan. The Town was one of three communities that participated in a Public Rights of Way
  36|                              study through our regional planning agency that used a consultant to review the town's
  37|                              sidewalks, ramps, and other ADA requirements. Around June the handicapped parking
  38|                              spot at Town Hall was moved from an uneven brick sidewalk to a concrete level spot, the
  39|                              chairlift door at Town Hall was fixed, and the uneven granite steps at the Ritter Memorial
  40|                              Building were repaired.
  41|                                                                                                21

   1|                          • In June the Town Manager applied for and received the Recycling IQ Kit Grant, which
   2|                               provided funding for educational materials and funds to hire Recycling Inspectors to do
   3|                               curbside checks of recycling bins in order to reduce the town's contamination rate. The
   4|                               nine week program targeted the areas in town with higher contamination rates and focused
   5|                               on educating the public on what materials can and cannot be recycled. At the conclusion
   6|                               of the program, the town's contamination rate was cut by twenty percent.
   7|                          • The redevelopment and/or reuse of 925 Massachusetts Avenue, an old vacant gas station
   8|                               with potential environmental hazards, has been a property the Town has sought to clean-
   9|                               up for over a decade but due to the unknown environmental hazards have not taken the
  10|                               property for back taxes so that the onus of the potential clean-up costs was not borne by
  11|                               the Town. After much research and consultation with the State and legal counsel, we were
  12|                               able to gain an Administrative Search Warrant that gave the Town the ability to apply for
  13|                               grant funding to perform an environmental assessment and right to access the property for
  14|                               the inspection and sampling needed. In September the Court granted the Administrative
  15|                               Search Warrant and the Town Manager submitted a Brownfields Site Assessment Grant
  16|                               through Montachusett Regional Planning Agency for the costs of the environmental site
  17|                               assessment, which was approved. The environmental site assessment is expected to be
  18|                               complete by the summer of 2020 and the project will be eligible for clean-up costs
  19|                               through MassDevelopment.
  20|                         • In the fall of 2019 the Town Manager worked with a consultant to apply for grant funding
  21|                               through the Municipal Vulnerability Preparedness (MVP) Program, which provides
  22|                               support for cities and towns to plan for climate change and implement key adaptation
  23|                               actions for resiliency. The planning grant applied for is a necessary step in order to apply
  24|                               for construction costs to implement projects that are identified during the planning phase.
  25|                               In 2020, the Town was notified we received this grant.
  26|                         • After successfully applying for District Local Technical Assistance from our regional
  27|                               planning agency to create a business brochure and create a permitting guidebook, both
  28|                               were completed in January. The Business Brochure was created with assistance from the
  29|                               Lunenburg Business Association and Land Use Director in an effort to highlight the
  30|                               town's natural resources and increase awareness of these locations to residents who many
  31|                               not know where public access to trails and conservation lands are located. The Permitting
  32|                               Guidebooks was created to assist customers with projects of any type and scale to simplify
  33|                               how they can file permits. Another project that was applied for and received through our
  34|                              regional planning agency was for a Walkability and Parking Study. This study focused on
  35|                               traffic data, crash data, an inventory of bike and pedestrian infrastructure for the Main
  36|                               Street area and Whalom Lakefront area. The report was finalized in November and
  37|                              identified a number of recommendations to address improving the walkability and parking
  38|                              in both the Main Street and Whalom area.
