   7|                    Lunenburg was settled in 1718 and incorporated in 1728. It occupies a land area of
   8|                    approximately 26.4 square miles. Lunenburg is located in north central Massachusetts
   9|                    approximately 43 miles from Boston. It is bordered on the west by the Town of Ashby and
  10|                    the City of Fitchburg, on the south by the Town of Lancaster and the City of Leominster, on
  11|                    the east by the Town of Shirley, and on the north by the Town of Townsend.
  12|                    Local legislative decisions are made by an open town meeting consisting of all registered voters in the town. Subject to the legislative decisions made by town meeting, the affairs of
  13|                     the town are generally administered by a five member Board of Selectmen and a Town
  14|                    Commit wait focal at as are and by a they a dead on means alo appointed on an at-large basis for staggered three-year terms.
  15|                     Lunenburg is a semi-rural community located in northern Worcester County. Three water bodies account for somewhat of a clustered approach to the town's historical development
  16|                     Lake Whalom and the former Whalom Amusement Park delineated the Whalom District
  17|                    from the remainder of Lunenburg. This district was developed as a housing base for many
  18|                    factory workers working in the mills and factories of Leominster and Fitchburg. In many
  19|                     ways the characteristics of the Whalom District more closely reflect characteristics of these
  20|                     cities than of the rest of Lunenburg. The Hickory Hills area of town, around Hickory Hills
  21|                     third body of water is Shirley Reservoir, a public water body also developed with manly Lake, was developed as a private community comprised largely of vacation cottages. ne
  22|                     vacation cottages that have now been modified to year-round residences. Commercial and
  23|                    industrial development is permitted in several areas of town, primarily along the two major
  24|                     arteries (Routes 2A and 13), and the town's peripheries, due largely to the close proximity of
  25|                     Leominster's and Fitchburg's sewer and water systems. Although many farms and orchards
  26|                     had slowly developed into single family housing, farming continues on in Lunenburg.

   6|                       identifying, defining, discussing, and finalizing the criteria to be used in determining the

   8|                       to propose at least one project to the 2020 Annual Town Meeting regarding the use and/or
   9|                       reuse of the old Primary School. The Board identified the criteria used in determining the
  10|                       use and/or re-use of the aforementioned buildings in the fall of 2019 and discussed the
  11|                       future use of the old Primary throughout the year and continued to narrow down the focus
  12|                       for the future use of this building into 2020.
  13|                   • The Town Manager's goals included developing and implementing steps towards the
  14|                       redevelopment/reuse of 925 Massachusetts Avenue, implementation of a recycling
  15|                       contamination enforcement program, and planning/implementing accessibility
  16|                       improvements.
  17|                   • There were multiple projects that addressed accessibility at various town facilities, town
  18|                       property, and public rights of way throughout the year. In January the Town Manager
  19|                       announced receipt of an ADA Project Grant from MA Office on Disability for $7,500 for
  20|                       accessibility improvements to the Lane, Cowdrey, and Small Town Forest properties. Also
  21|                       in January the Veterans Service Office was relocated from the Eagle House to the Ritter
  22|                       Memorial Building to be more accessible for veterans as the previous office was located on
  23|                       a second floor without a handicap lift so the Veterans Officer was relocated to the Ritter
  24|                       Memorial Building lower level that has a lift. The Town Manager coordinated the use of
  25|                       Montachusett Technical students to renovate the second floor Town Hall restroom to
  26|                       become a handicapped accessible bathroom and this was started in February and completed
  27|                       by May. In March the Town Manager applied for and ultimately received a District Local
  28|                       Technical Assistance grant to update the Town's ADA Self-Evaluation and Transition
  29|                        Plan. The Town was one of three communities that participated in a Public Rights of Way
  30|                       study through our regional planning agency that used a consultant to review the town's
  31|                       sidewalks, ramps, and other ADA requirements. Around June the handicapped parking
  32|                       spot at Town Hall was moved from an uneven brick sidewalk to a concrete level spot, the
  33|                       chairlift door at Town Hall was fixed, and the uneven granite steps at the Ritter Memorial
  34|                        Building were repaired.
