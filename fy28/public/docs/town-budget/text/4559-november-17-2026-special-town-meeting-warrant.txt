ARTICLE 4.  TRANSFER OF FUNDS TO THE SPECIAL EDUCATION RESERVE FUND 
(Submitted by School Committee)   
 
To see if the Town will raise and appropriate, or tran sfer from available funds, a sum of money to the 
Special Education Reserve Fund; or take any other action relative thereto. 
 
FINANCE COMMITTEE RECOMMENDATION:  
SELECT BOARD RECOMMENDATION: Recommend Disapproval (4-1-0). 
VOTE REQUIRED: 2/3rds if raise and appropriate and majority if transfer 
 
Summary:  At the November 18, 2025 Special Town Meeting the “Special Education Reserve 
Fund” was established but was not funded.   The Fund is intended to be a tool to help level the tax 
impact of fluctuating expenses to fund legally mandated Special Education services. These funds 
are used to help fund unanticipated or unbudgeted costs of special education, out of district tuition, 
transportation costs, and recovery high school tuition.  
 
The balance in the Fund cannot exceed 2 percent of the annual net school spending of the School 
District.  Funds can only be expended or transferred out from the Reserve Fund after a majority 
vote of the School Committee and a majority vote of the Select Board, as is specified in M.G.L. 
Chapter 40, Section 13E. Monies accumulated in this fund carry over from one fiscal year to 
another. 
 
The proposed motion under this article funds the Special Education Reserve Fund with a transfer 
of $300,000 from Certified Free Cash.

all applicable laws, regulations, permits, and approvals, including review and approval by the 
Architectural Preservation District Commission, if required, and to request that the Select Board continue

Summary:  This is a non-binding, advisory vote intended to provide direction to the Select Board 
on pursuing demolition of the T.C. Passios Building. 
 
An affirmative vote would express support for demolition and request that the Select Board

ARTICLE 8.  ADVISORY VOTE ON FUNDING MECHANISM FOR STORMWATER 
MAINTENANCE AND MS4 PERMIT COMPLIANCE  
(Submitted by Stormwater Taskforce)   
 
To see if the Town will vote on the preferred funding mechanism for the expenditures necessary to 
achieve and maintain compliance with the federal Clean Water Act National Pollutant Discharge 
Elimination System (NPDES) Municipal Separate Storm Sewer System (MS4) General Permit 
requirements, by selecting one of the following primary methods advanced by the Stormwater Task 
Force:  
 
 either 
 
 1. Stormwater Enterprise Fund / Utility Fee: The future establishment of a dedicated 
Stormwater Enterprise Fund under Massachusetts General Laws Chapter 44, Section 
53F½, to be funded through a stormwater utility fee assessed on real property owners 
based strictly on the amount of impervious surface area (such as roofs, driveways, and 
parking lots) on their properties. 
  
 or  
  
 2. Town's Annual Operating Budget: Traditional annual appropriation from the Town’s 
annual operating budget, to be funded through property taxes where a property owner's 
contribution is based entirely on the assessed value of the property rather than its 
stormwater runoff contribution.  
 
said vote to be advisory only; or take any other action relative thereto. 
===PAGE 6===
 
 
 
 
 
FINANCE COMMITTEE RECOMMENDATION:  
SELECT BOARD RECOMMENDATION: No position (4-1-0). 
 
Summary: This is a non-binding, advisory vote intended to provide explicit direction to the 
Stormwater Task Force as it evaluates how the Town will fund requirements under the MS4 
Stormwater Permit and ongoing stormwater maintenance.  
 
Please note: This vote does NOT establish a stormwater enterprise fund or any new fees under 
Option 1. It is strictly an advisory vote to inform the Stormwater Task Force which funding option 
the community prefers they pursue. If Option 1 is favored, a formal proposal to legally establish the 
enterprise fund and utility structure would be presented to voters at a future Town Meeting.  
 
The Town is required by the U.S. Environmental Protection Agency (EPA) and the Massachusetts 
Department of Environmental Protection (MassDEP) to implement a comprehensive stormwater 
management program to reduce pollution entering local water bodies. Compliance requires 
predictable, long-term funding for infrastructure maintenance, street sweeping, catch basin 
cleaning, illicit discharge detection & elimination, reporting, and public education.  
 
Town Meeting is being asked to provide guidance on the two distinct funding pathways:  
 
• Option 1 (Stormwater Utility): Directs the Stormwater Task Force to pursue a 
dedicated enterprise fund framework where fees would be determined by the 
amount of impervious area on a given property with a flat fee for all residents (est. 
<$100 / year). Funds would be legally restricted solely to stormwater operations and 
capital projects.  
 
• Option 2 (Town's Annual Operating Budge t): Directs the Stormwater Task Force to 
pursue funding operations through the Town's annual operating budget. Under this 
option, funding is derived from property taxes based strictly on the assessed value of 
the property. Choosing this option means stormwater maintenance and compliance 
must compete annually with schools, public safety, and other Town departments for 
limited tax dollars. The funding required for stormwater maintenance and MS4 
compliance has historically been insufficient and will likely require a Proposition 2 
1/2 override in the future.
