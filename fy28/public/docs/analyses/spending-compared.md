# How Lunenburg compares, and what matching would cost

**Where Lunenburg sits among all 318 Massachusetts school districts on spending for each pupil — and what it would cost, in dollars, to move.**

Analysis, September 2026. Every figure is DESE’s, for FY2025, and the only arithmetic here is a multiplication.

---

## The short version

Lunenburg spent **$18,027** for each pupil in FY2025, against a statewide median of **$23,520** — **310 of 318 districts**.

**To reach the median, Lunenburg would have to spend $39,182,050. It spends $30,031,242. The difference is $9,150,808 — 30% more than it spends now.**

To reach merely the **first quartile** — the line below which a quarter of districts sit — would take $35,282,503, or $5,251,261 more.

---

## What the target is a target FOR

DESE’s per-pupil figure is **all funds**, and that is not the number Town Meeting votes on. Of Lunenburg’s $30,031,242:

| | FY2025 | share |
|---|---:|---:|
| General fund — what the town appropriates | $26,986,344 | 89.9% |
| Grants and revolving funds | $3,044,898 | 10.1% |
| **All funds** | **$30,031,242** | **100%** |

So **$39,182,050 is a target for total spending, not for the appropriation.** A district can close part of such a gap with grants nobody in town votes on, and 10.1% of what Lunenburg already spends arrives that way. What share of the distance to the median a town could close without raising its own levy is not established here, and this project holds no document that settles it.

## “Regionalise and we would get more money”

It is one of the most common things said about this deficit, so it is worth setting out what these figures do and do not settle.

**Regional districts do not systematically spend more.** Five of the districts above are regional, and they span nearly the whole range — from the one that spends almost exactly what Lunenburg does to the one that spends most:

| district | structure | per pupil | against Lunenburg |
|---|---|---:|---:|
| Montachusett Regional Vocational Technical | regional vocational, many towns | $25,827 | +$7,800 |
| Groton-Dunstable | regional, two towns | $22,664 | +$4,637 |
| North Middlesex | regional, three towns | $21,528 | +$3,501 |
| Ayer Shirley School District | regional, two towns — regionalised FY2012 | $20,428 | +$2,401 |
| Ashburnham-Westminster | regional, two towns | $18,142 | +$115 |
| **Lunenburg** | single town, K–12 | **$18,027** | — |

Ashburnham-Westminster is regional and spends **$115** more for each pupil than Lunenburg. Being regional plainly does not decide the number.

**And the one local case cannot be measured here, which is worth saying plainly.** Ayer and Shirley regionalised for FY2012, next door and in living memory. This archive holds `Ayer` for FY2009–FY2011 and `Ayer Shirley School District` from FY2012 — and nothing for Shirley on its own beforehand. So the figure before regionalisation is one town and the figure after is two, which is a different denominator rather than a change. Comparing them would produce a confident number about the wrong thing.

### What the town itself has to raise

Spending per pupil is what a district SPENDS, whoever paid for it. The argument is about what the TOWN pays, and Chapter 70 states that directly: the **required local contribution**, which is the minimum a town must put in, beside the aid the state sends. Per foundation pupil, FY2026:

| district | structure | the town must raise | state sends |
|---|---|---:|---:|
| Ashburnham Westminster | regional | $7,040 | $7,309 |
| North Middlesex | regional | $7,800 | $7,225 |
| **Lunenburg** | single town | **$8,319** | $5,758 |
| Ayer Shirley | regional | $9,639 | $5,113 |
| Groton Dunstable | regional | $10,459 | $5,291 |
| Harvard | single town | $10,464 | $2,787 |
| *Every district in the state* | | *$9,253* | *$8,132* |

**The regional districts hold both ends of that table** — the lowest local burden for each pupil and the highest. Being regional does not decide it.

What decides it is in the formula’s own inputs, which this archive holds: `equalized_valuation`, `property_local_effort` and `income_local_effort`. **The required contribution is computed from a town’s property wealth and its residents’ income.** Regionalising changes neither; it averages them with whichever town you join. Join a poorer one and the share for each pupil can fall, and the aid rise. Join a wealthier one and it goes the other way.

Two things that table is not. It is the **required minimum**, not what Lunenburg actually raises, which is higher. And its denominator is **foundation enrolment**, the formula’s own count — not the FTE pupils the spending tables above divide by. The two are not interchangeable.

### Chapter 70 is not the only mechanism

It is the largest, and it is the one these figures cover. There is at least one more, and the town’s own record names it. School Committee minutes of 16 April 2025, reporting on a state bill:

> “Key provisions to the bill were a full 100% reimbursement for FY25 regional transportation up from 82%, a 75% reimbursement increased from 44% for FY out of district special education transportation cost through circuit breaker”

**Regional districts are reimbursed for transportation under c.71 §16C and single-town districts are not.** At 82% rising to 100% that is not a rounding error, and it is money Lunenburg cannot receive in its present form.

**This archive holds no dollar figure for it, for any district.** So the honest position is: the largest term in the comparison — the required local contribution — is set by a town’s wealth and not by its structure, and at least one further term exists that favours regional districts and cannot be sized from anything held here. Anyone claiming to know the net is claiming more than the record supports, in either direction. Registered in `money-gaps.csv`.

The decision itself is on the record too. A School Committee member, 24 January 2024: “Lunenburg decided to not regionalize and to stay a small school, so scheduling in our middle high school is so hard and there isn’t really a clear path to fix that.” That is evidence about what was decided and why it still matters to people — not evidence about what it cost.

---

## What this does not show

- **That spending more would buy more.** This is a price, not a promise. Nothing here measures what any district gets for its money, and the two districts that spend least on this list are virtual schools, which are not comparable.

- **That the median is a target anybody has set.** No document says Lunenburg should spend the median. It is a position in a distribution, and it is used here because a rank of 310 of 318 is not a quantity a resident can act on.

- **That this is the appropriation.** See above, and rule 11. The general fund is 89.9% of it.

- **Why Lunenburg spends less.** A figure is a fact; a reason is a hypothesis. This page measures the distance and does not explain it.
