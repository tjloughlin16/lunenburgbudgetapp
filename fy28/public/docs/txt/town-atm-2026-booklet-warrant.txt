CAPITAL IMPROVEMENT PROGRAM- A blueprint for planning a community’s capital expenditures 
that comprises an annual capital budget and a five -year capital program.  It coordinates community 
planning, fiscal capacity and physical development.   
CHERRY SHEET/STATE AID – Revenue allocated by the Commonwealth to cities, towns, and regional 
school districts.  Estimates of local aid are transmitted to cities, towns, and districts annually by the “Cherry 
Sheets”.  Most Cherry Sheet aid programs are considered general fund revenues and may be spent for any 
purpose, subject to appropriation. 
DEBT EXCLUSION- An action taken by a community through a referendum vote to raise the funds 
necessary to pay debt service costs for a particular project from the property tax levy but outside of the 
limits under Proposition 2 ½.  By approving a debt exclusion, a community calculates its annual levy limit 
under Proposition 2 ½, then adds the excluded debt service cost.  The amount is added to the levy limit for 
the life of the debt only and may increase the levy above the levy ceiling. 
EXCESS LEVY CAPACITY- The difference between the levy limit and the amount of real and personal 
property taxes actually levied in a given year.  Annually, the Selectmen must be informed of excess levy 
capacity at the Tax Rate Hearing and this is submitted to DLS when setting the tax rate. 
FREE CASH  – Remaining, unrestricted funds from operations of the previous fiscal year including 
unexpended free cash from the previous year, actual receipts in excess of revenue estimates shown on the 
tax recapitulation sheet, and unspent amounts in budget line -items.  Unpaid property taxes and certain 
deficits reduce the amount that can be certified as free cash.  The calculation of free cash is based on the 
balance sheet as of June 30, which is submitted by the Town Accountant.  Free Cash is not available for 
appropriation until certified by the Director of Accounts. 
LOCAL RECEIPTS- Locally generated revenues, other than real and personal property taxes.  Examples 
include motor vehicle excise, investment income, meals tax, fees, rentals, and charges.  Annual estimates 
of local receipts are shown on the tax rate recapitulation sheet. 
OVERLAY – (Allowance for Abatements and Exemptions) An account established annually to fund 
anticipated property tax abatements, exemptions and uncollected taxes in that year.   
RESERVE FUND - This fund is used by the Finance Committee to provide for extraordinary or unforeseen 
expenditures.  The total amount appropriated in any one year may not exceed 5% of the tax levy.  No direct 
drafts against this fund can be made, but transfers from the fund can be authorized by the Finance 
Committee. 
RETAINED EARNINGS-  An equity account reflecting the accumulated earnings of an enterprise fund, 
which may be used to fund capital improvements, reimburse the general fund for prior year subsidies, 
reduce user charges, or provide for enterprise revenue deficits (operating losses). 
STABILIZATION FUND – A fund designed to accumulate amounts for capital and other future spending 
purposes, although it may be appropriated for any lawful purpose.  Communities may establish one or more 
stabilization funds for different purposes and may appropriate any amounts into them.  A two -thirds vote 
of town meeting is required to establish, amend the purpose of, or appropriate money from a stabilization 
fund.  A majority vote of town meeting is required to appropriate money into a stabilization fund.

FY2027 Budget Overview 
 
This year, Town Meeting voters will be asked to consider three distinct budget scenarios: 
 
• A balanced (no override) budget, which reflects available revenues under Proposition 2½ and 
results in service reductions across both municipal and school operations;  
• A Tier 1 override budget, which supports a defined level of services ($2.4M override); and  
• A Tier 2 override budget, which supports an additional level of service restoration beyond 
Tier 1 ($3.3M override).  
 
This structure is intended to provide transparency and allow voters to evaluate service levels 
alongside the financial implications of each option. 
 
It is important to be clear about process: 
• Town Meeting votes to appropriate a budget, establishing spending authority;  
• The ballot election determines whether an override is approved, providing the revenue 
required to fund that appropriation.  
 
The Annual Town Election is scheduled for May 16, 2026 and any override question must be 
approved at the ballot in order to take effect.

Each of the three budget scenarios carries a different tax impact for property owners. The balanced 
budget is developed within the constraints of Proposition 2½ and does not require an override; 
however, it reflects reductions necessary to align expenditures with available revenue.  The Tier 1 
and Tier 2 budgets require voter approval of a Proposition 2½ override and would result in an 
increase to the property tax levy to support the additional services funded at each level.  Estimated 
tax impact information is available on the Town’s website and has been provided to assist voters in 
their evaluation of both the service implications and tax impacts.  
 
How Does a “Tiered” Override Work? 
 
Under Massachusetts law, a community may present multiple override options to voters in a tiered 
format. Each tier represents a different level of funding. 
 
Operationally: 
• Town Meeting may adopt contingent budget appropriations tied to override outcomes;  
• At the ballot, voters consider each override question separately;  
• If multiple tiers are presented, the outcome is implemented based on the highest level of 
override approved by voters.  
 
The decisions before the community this year are significant. They involve considerations of service 
levels, staffing, educational programming, balanced against the tax impact required to support those 
services.  There is no scenario without consequence. The balanced budget reflects the limits of 
existing revenues. The override options reflect the cost of maintaining, or restoring, services.  
 
This booklet, along with the supplemental materials available online, is intended to provide voters 
with the information necessary to make decisions both at the Town Meeting and at the ballot box at 
the Town Election.

Lunenburg Finance Committee Annual Report for FY27  
On May 2, 2026, members of the public will gather in the Lunenburg Middle/High School and 
participate in the centuries-long tradition of New England style town government. As part of this 
tradition, the Finance Committee has reviewed all monied articles, recommended updates, held public 
hearings, and voted on our official recommendations. Every warrant article is important, and we never 
know where long discussions may occur, however, this warrant contains two budgetary articles that are 
worth highlighting: 
• Article 10 - Omnibus Budget article that provides most funds for the operations of town (both 
municipal and schools).  This year’s Omnibus Budget, often referred to as the Balanced Budget, 
will result in significant reductions in School and Town services 
• Article 11 – Override article that contains two tiers.  Tier 1, also referred to as “Core Services” 
results in a cut to some services in town but retains those deemed critical by the Select Board and 
School Committee.  Tier 2, also referred to as “Restorative” build upon the “Core Tier” to restore 
all services that the Boards believe retain the level of services that the town should expect to 
have.  Recognizing that choices at town meetings and ballot box are important, and that these tax 
increases are significant, the Select Board placed a two-Tier override in front of the voters. 
Town Meeting alone is not sufficient to make your voice heard in the direction of Lunenburg.  A 
proposition 2 ½ override requires a vote at the ballot on May 16.  Our elected and appointed Boards and 
Committees need volunteers.  Finally, our Massachusetts representatives must be held accountable for 
their failures to fund the mandates they put on our town and our neighboring communities.  
Tri-Board Process 
Starting in the summer of 2025, the Select Board, School Committee and Finance Committee joined 
together as an ad hoc group referred to as the Tri-Board.  The goals of the Tri-Board were to develop a 
plan for the FY27 budget, a 3-year financial forecast, and to identify and discuss consolidation and 
efficiencies (and the timeline for the same).  A secondary benefit was to increase collaboration and work 
off the same data.  This process was difficult at times, but the Finance Committee wants to thank the 
other participants for their time and effort through this process.  Not all goals were met, but a foundation 
is in place for future progress. 
Town Revenue Projections 
Lunenburg’s revenue comes from multiple sources, primarily from the Property Tax. This year’s budget 
builds on the FY26 Levy Limit of $32,910,811. By law, the levy limit increases automatically by 2.5%, 
resulting in an increase of $822,770.28. Additionally, the town estimates New Growth of $400,000, 
leading to a total increase of 3.72%. Previously approved Excluded Debt of $2,199,352.52 is then 
applied, resulting in a Maximum Allowable Levy of 36,332,933.80. 
FY2027 Net State Aid is estimated at $10,335,568, based on the Governor's budget, with potential 
increases pending State legislators' negotiations. Chapter 70 Governor’s budget is set at $9,349,335 
(1.28% increase from FY26), reflecting ongoing challenges with state aid for education. The Chapter 70 
program is the primary source of state aid for funding local public elementary and secondary schools. 
From FY13-FY23 state aid increased at an annual rate of 5.3%, but in the last four years, it has slowed 
to approximately 1.4%, increasing the financial burden on the town to raise appropriate funds. From a 
Lunenburg resident’s perspective, State Aid is funded through income and sales taxes.

Proposition 2 ½ Override and Operations 
Many Town Meeting articles relate to operational budgets. Lunenburg, like most municipalities in the 
Commonwealth, faces increased health insurance costs (8.8% increase), stagnant state aid, necessary 
salary increases to remain competitive, and inflation. Last year we reported that the vast majority of 
department staffing plans have been maintained, placed on hold, or reduced. This year’s balanced budget 
has now put Lunenburg in the position that many services will be cut without an override.  The School 
Committee, Superintendent, Select Board and Town Manager have had difficult conversations and 
decisions to arrive at a Balanced budget and Tiered override budgets.

To see if the Town will vote, to Amend the Code of Lunenburg, Chapter 12, entitled “Boards,

as follows:  
 
Article IX Open Space Committee 
 
§ 12-24. Membership and Appointment 
 
A. There shall be established an Open Space Committee consisting of seven (7) voting members 
who reside in Lunenburg who shall be sworn to the faithful performance of their 
duties.   Membership shall include:  
• One member or designee of the Planning Board  
• One member or designee of the Conservation Commission  
• One member or designee of the Parks Commission  
• One member or designee of the Agricultural Commission  
• Three at-large community members.  
   
B. The Open Space Committee at-large members shall be appointed by the Select Board.  The four 
Board/Commission members shall be appointed by their relevant Board/Commission.  Committee 
members shall serve two (2) year staggered terms, with the majority of the first members 
appointed for a two (2) year term and the remaining initial members appointed to a one (1) year 
term.  Thereafter, each member shall serve for two (2) years or until a successor has been 
appointed.  The positions will not be term-limited.  
   
39

===PAGE 41===
   
 
   
 
C. The membership shall consist of individuals with experience, training, or a dedicated interest in 
open space preservation, land planning, conservation, real estate acquisition, mapping, 
fundraising, communications, and such other professions as could be helpful to the Committee’s 
work.    
   
§ 12-25. Duties 
 
The Open Space Committee shall have the following responsibilities and duties:    
   
A. Assist and advise other Town boards/committees and departments as they may request 
such assistance with regard to the review of applications for land development permits with the 
goal of securing open space areas for the community.   
 
B. Serve as an information source and advocate for the acquisition of land for conservation, open 
space, agricultural, recreation, and related purposes and make recommendations for its 
purchase to the Select Board.    
 
C. Update and implement Lunenburg’s Open Space Plan and Recreation Plan pursuant 
to Massachusetts Department of Conservation and Recreation guidelines.    
 
D. Maintain a publicly-owned open space inventory.    
 
E. Work with Lunenburg residents, Town Departments, Town Boards and Committees, state and 
federal officials and agencies, and private non-profit land conservation organizations to further 
the preservation of Lunenburg's open space resources.    
 
F. Encourage philanthropy and private efforts to preserve open space.    
 
G. Develop community education programs and communication tools regarding the value and use of 
open space areas.    
   
§ 12-26. Officers 
 
The Open Space Committee shall choose its own officers and may adopt rules of procedure regarding its 
functioning and operation in compliance with Massachusetts Open Meeting Law.    
 
§ 12-27. Guiding Principles 
 
In performing its work, the Open Space Committee shall be guided by the Lunenburg Master Plan and 
the Lunenburg Open Space and Recreation Plan.    
 
Or take any other action relative thereto.

follows: 
 
§200-18 Waivers 
 
A. Except as otherwise stated herein, the Sewer Commission reserves the right to waive, in its sole 
discretion, any provision(s) of this bylaw and/or the rules and regulations adopted hereunder in situations 
that meet all the following criteria: 
  
1. Emergency circumstances threaten the safe and continued use of a property that a waiver may 
resolve; Unusual or extraordinary circumstances threaten the safe and continued use of a 
property that a waiver may resolve;  
 
2. Alternatives to the waiver present an undue hardship to any person or entity affected thereby; 
 
3. The Commission deems it to be in the best interest of the Town of Lunenburg and not 
otherwise inconsistent with the purpose and objectives hereof; 
Or take ay other action relative thereto.  
 
FINANCE COMMITTEE RECOMMENDATION: No Direct Financial Impact (6-0-0). 
SELECT BOARD RECOMMENDATION: Recommend Approval (5-0-0). 
VOTE REQUIRED: Majority  
 
Summary: This article amends Section 200-18 of the Sewer Bylaw (Waivers) by replacing the term 
“emergency circumstances” with “unusual or extraordinary circumstances”.  All other waiver 
provisions remain unchanged.  The Sewer Commission determined that the term "emergency 
circumstances" is too restrictive as there may be non-emergency circumstances that qualify for a 
waiver.  In December 2025, the Sewer Commission adopted a new Regulation for accessory 
dwelling units that include the waiver language in the bylaw but replaced the term “emergency 
circumstances” with “unusual or extraordinary circumstances”.  The purpose of this Article is to 
have consistent waiver language in the regulations and the bylaws.
