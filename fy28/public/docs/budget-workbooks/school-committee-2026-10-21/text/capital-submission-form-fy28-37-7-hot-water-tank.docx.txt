2. DESCRIPTION AND JUSTIFICATION
Description and scope:        Age and deterioration: The existing hot water holding tank is aging and may be approaching or exceeding its expected service life, increasing the likelihood of failure. 
  Risk of leaks or catastrophic failure: An aging tank can develop corrosion, leaks, or structural failure, potentially causing significant water damage and disruption to school operations. 
  Reliability: A new tank would provide a more dependable source of hot water for student and staff bathrooms, kitchens, custodial operations, and other building needs. 
  Reduced maintenance costs: Replacing an aging tank can reduce the frequency and expense of repairs, emergency service calls, and replacement parts. 
  Energy efficiency: Newer hot water storage systems typically have improved insulation and efficiency, which can reduce heat loss and lower energy consumption. 
  Operational continuity: A tank failure could leave portions of the school without adequate hot water, potentially affecting sanitation and daily operations. 
  Health and sanitation: Reliable hot water is essential for maintaining appropriate cleaning and sanitation practices throughout the school. 
  Preventive maintenance: Replacing the tank before a major failure allows the district to plan and budget for the work rather than responding to an emergency. 
  Protection of school facilities: A failed tank could release a large volume of water and damage floors, walls, equipment, and other building components. 
  Long-term cost savings: Investing in a new tank can reduce emergency repair costs, water damage risks, and energy expenses over the life of the equipment.
