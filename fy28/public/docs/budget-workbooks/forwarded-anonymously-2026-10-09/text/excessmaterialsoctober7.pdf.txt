MEMORANDUM 
 
TO: Lunenburg School Committee 
 
FROM: Dr. Kimberly Sarfde, Director of Teaching and Learning 
 
DATE: September 16, 2026 
 
SUBJECT: Declaration of Surplus/Excess Materials 
 
Executive Summary 
This memorandum requests formal authorization from the Lunenburg School Committee to 
declare a district-wide inventory of outdated, non-compliant, damaged, and mold-contaminated 
literacy materials as surplus property no longer usable for school purposes. 
 
This inventory encompasses legacy core curricula—specifically Houghton Mifflin Harcourt 
(HMH) Into ReadingVersion 2 (V2)—alongside non-aligned supplemental and intervention 
programs (such as Leveled Literacy Intervention / LLI) and any instructional resources 
incorporating three-cueing strategies. The district utilizes HMH Into ReadingVersion 3 (V3), 
which earned a favorable review from The Reading League's Curriculum Evaluation Guidelines 
for its alignment with evidence-based literacy practices. Systematically declaring these materials 
excess is required to maintain statutory compliance with state and federal requirements and to 
ensure a safe learning environment across our schools. 
 
1. Comprehensive Legal & Regulatory Compliance Framework 
Retaining or utilizing non-aligned core or supplemental reading materials creates serious 
statutory and regulatory non-compliance across state and federal mandates: 
 
● Massachusetts Early Literacy Law (An Act relative to teacher preparation and student 
literacy): State law mandates that all school districts adopt evidence-based early literacy 
instruction grounded in the science of reading. The statute explicitly prohibits curricula, 
intervention suites, and supplemental materials that rely on implicit or incidental word 
reading, visual memorization, or guessing strategies—specifically banning three-
cueing/MSV (Meaning, Structure, Visual). Legacy core programs (such as Into Reading 
V2) and cueing-based intervention tools (such as LLI) fail to meet these benchmarks and 
cannot legally be deployed in district classrooms. 
 
● Individuals with Disabilities Education Act (IDEA): Under 20 U.S.C. § 
1414(d)(1)(A)(i)(IV), specialized instruction and core materials must be grounded in 
peer-reviewed research. IDEA adopts federal definitions requiring explicit, systematic 
instruction across the five essential components of reading (phonemic awareness, 
phonics, vocabulary, fluency, and comprehension). Retaining non-aligned materials 
directly impairs specialized instruction and creates significant legal exposure to Free 
Appropriate Public Education (FAPE) violations for students with Specific Learning 
Disabilities (e.g., dyslexia). 
 
● Federal ESSA & Title I Standards: Under Every Student Succeeds Act (ESSA) Title I, 
Part A regulations (20 U.S.C. § 6368), districts receiving federal funding must utilize Tier 
1 scientifically based reading research (SBRR) materials. Removing non-compliant 
inventory eliminates audit liabilities during Department of Elementary and Secondary

Education (DESE) Tiered Focused Monitoring reviews. 
 
2. Health, Safety, and Environmental Hazards 
District physical audits confirmed materials that must be declared unusable due to health and 
safety concerns: 
 
● Mold Contamination Mitigation: Portions of surplus legacy materials stored in basement 
and facility storage areas have sustained moisture damage, resulting in active mold and 
mildew growth. 
 
● Indoor Air Quality & Student Safety: Contaminated paper inventory compromises indoor 
air quality and presents severe health hazards (allergens, respiratory triggers) to 
students and staff. These materials cannot be resold or transferred; they must be 
condemned and safely destroyed/recycled. 
 
● Facility Optimization: Purging hazardous and non-compliant items frees up valuable 
storage and instructional space across district buildings. 
 
3. Recommended Action 
Motion: 
 
"Move that the Lunenburg School Committee declare all identified inventory of non-compliant 
literacy materials, three-cueing resources, legacy core programs (including HMH Into Reading 
Version 2), and non-aligned intervention suites (including LLI), alongside physically damaged or 
mold-contaminated textbooks and teacher resources, as surplus property no longer usable for 
school purposes, and authorize the administration to proceed with appropriate disposal or 
recycling."
