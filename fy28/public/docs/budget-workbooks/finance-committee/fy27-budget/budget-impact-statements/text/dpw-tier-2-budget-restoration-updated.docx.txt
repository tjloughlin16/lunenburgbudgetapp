March 19, 2026
Restoration Budget (Tier 2) for DPW
Prepared by: Bill Bernard, DPW Director
Pavement Management Plan
$202,282 Budget Request
This is a desirable request.
In Fiscal Year 2016 Town Meeting voted to approve a $4,000,000 debt exclusion to fund the Pavement Management Plan (PMP) along with using Operating Budget funds and Chapter 90 funds for FY 2016 through FY 2022 with the goal of achieving a minimum PCI (Pavement Condition Index) of 70-75.  In Fiscal Year 2023, a new PMP was developed, and the PCI was calculated to be 84.75; therefore, the 2016 plan was a great success.
In Fiscal Year 2016 the estimated annual cost to meet the goal of a PCI score of 70-75 was $1.0M - 1.2M.  The chart below shows that we have spent on average $1.05M per year since 2016; thus, the estimated annual cost was correct.

The plan in 2016 to fund paving with the Debt Exclusion also addressed funding for future years beyond 2022.  The plan was to add $50,000 per year to the Operating Budget for paving until there was 1.2M available per year for paving.  The $1.2M is a combination of Chapter 90 and Operating Budget.  Note: $50,000 is the annual increased revenue from Motor Vehicle Excise Taxes.  In 2017 the Operating Budget for paving was $250,000 and the Chapter 90 funding was $425,000.  Chapter 90 Funding had been around $425,000 for the previous 5 years, so they anticipated it to remain around $425,000 for the next 13 years (it has remained there to date, note: there have been some supplements to it in the past 2 years).  Therefore, to reach $1.2M per year by adding $50,000 per year, the goal would be reached in 2029.
The Preliminary Operations Budget for Pavement Management in FY 2027 is $279,000. The Chapter 90 allotment for FY 2027 is $718,718.  Together this is $997,718 or $202,282 less than our goal of $1.2M.  Accordingly, the budget request for the Restoration Budget (Tier 2) for Pavement Management is $202,282.
The PMP for FY’s 2023-2027 calculated a backlog of needed pavement repairs to be $5.9M.  Since 2023 we have spent $2.7M on pavement repairs, leaving a backlog of $3.2M.  However, the roadways have continued to deteriorate; thus, the actual backlog is likely closer to $5M.  The Town is engaging a consultant to develop a new PMP for FY’s 28-32 in the next 6 months.  This will recalculate the actual backlog of pavement repairs.
As we now have a PCI of over 80 people may think we do not need to spend $1.2M per year to maintain that PCI.  However, in 2017 the price per Ton of Hot Mix Asphalt was $55.80 and in 2026 the price per Ton of Hot Mix Asphalt is $100 or a 79% increase of $44.20 per ton.  Therefore, due to the substantial increase in costs, $1.2M per year is not unreasonable to maintain our PCI.
Chapter 90 cannot be the exclusive funding source for pavement management.
