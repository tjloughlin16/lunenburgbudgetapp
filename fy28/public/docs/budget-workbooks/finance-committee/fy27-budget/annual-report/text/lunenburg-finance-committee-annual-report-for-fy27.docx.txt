
Lunenburg Finance Committee Annual Report for FY27 
On May 2, 2026, members of the public will gather in the Lunenburg Middle/High School and participate in the centuries-long tradition of New England style town government. As part of this tradition, the Finance Committee has reviewed all monied articles, recommended updates, held public hearings, and voted on our official recommendations. Every warrant article is important, and we never know where long discussions may occur, however, this warrant contains two budgetary articles that are worth highlighting:
Article XX - Omnibus Budget article that provides most funds for the operations of town (both municipal and schools).  This year’s Omnibus Budget, often referred to as the Balanced Budget, will result in significant reductions in School and Town services
Article YY – Override article that contains two tiers.  Tier 1, also referred to as “Core Services” results in a cut to some services in town but retains those deemed critical by the Select Board and School Committee.  Tier 2, also referred to as “Restorative” build upon the “Core Tier” to restore all services that the Boards believe retain the level of services that the town should expect to have.  Recognizing that choices at town meetings and ballot box are important, and that these tax increases are significant, the Select Board placed a two-Tier override in front of the voters.
Town Meeting alone is not sufficient to make your voice heard in the direction of Lunenburg.  A proposition 2 ½ override requires a vote at the ballot on May 16.  Our elected and appointed Boards and Committees need volunteers.  Finally, our Massachusetts representatives must be held accountable for their failures to fund the mandates they put on our town and our neighboring communities. 
Tri-Board Process
Starting in the summer of 2025, the Select Board, School Committee and Finance Committee joined together as an ad hoc group referred to as the Tri-Board.  The goals of the Tri-Board were to develop a plan for the FY27 budget, a 3-year financial forecast, and to identify and discuss consolidation and efficiencies (and the timeline for the same).  A secondary benefit was to increase collaboration and work off the same data.  This process was difficult at times, but the Finance Committee wants to thank the other participants for their time and effort through this process.  Not all goals were met, but a foundation is in place for future progress.
Town Revenue Projections
Lunenburg’s revenue comes from multiple sources, primarily from the Property Tax. This year’s budget builds on the FY26 Levy Limit of $32,910,811. By law, the levy limit increases automatically by 2.5%, resulting in an increase of $822,770.28. Additionally, the town estimates New Growth of $400,000, leading to a total increase of 3.72%. Previously approved Excluded Debt of $2,199,352.52 is then applied, resulting in a Maximum Allowable Levy of 36,332,933.80.
FY2027 Net State Aid is estimated at $10,335,568, based on the Governor's budget, with potential increases pending State legislators' negotiations. Chapter 70 Governor’s budget is set at $9,349,335 (0% increase from FY26), reflecting ongoing challenges with state aid for education. The Chapter 70 program is the primary source of state aid for funding local public elementary and secondary schools. From FY13-FY23 state aid increased at an annual rate of 5.3%, but in the last four years, it has slowed to approximately 1.4%, increasing the financial burden on the town to raise appropriate funds. From a Lunenburg resident’s perspective, State Aid is funded through income and sales taxes.
Additional revenue sources include local receipts (Excise Tax, Meals Tax, etc.) and enterprise funds such as Sewer. Notably, Chapter 90 funding for municipal capital improvements has increased over the last couple of years due to a five-year state-level bond bill.  
Proposition 2 ½ Override and Operations
Many Town Meeting articles relate to operational budgets. Lunenburg, like most municipalities in the Commonwealth, faces increased health insurance costs (8.8% increase), stagnant state aid, necessary salary increases to remain competitive, and inflation. Last year we reported that the vast majority of department staffing plans have been maintained, placed on hold, or reduced. This year’s balanced budget has now put Lunenburg in the position that many services will be cut without an override.  The School Committee, Superintendent, Select Board and Town Manager have had difficult conversations and decisions to arrive at a Balanced budget and Tiered override budgets.

School Department Operations
A balanced budget will result in catastrophic losses, including a reduction of 1 teacher each in grades 1-4; 1 Certified Occupational Therapist; .2 Music teacher at THES ending 5th grade band; 1 Interventionist at Primary; .5 Athletic Trainer; 1 Custodian through Attrition. In Addition, Athletic and band transportation will be ended; Girl’s Lacrosse and Championship Boy’s Golf along with the Ski Team will be cut and Athletic Fees will be raised.  Finally supply budgets will be diminished and computer services and leasing will be cut back.
Tier 1 (Core Budget) will result in the addition of 1 Paraprofessional at the Primary; 1 Interventionist at THES; 1 Bridge Program Teacher, 1 Paraprofessional, .4 Music Teacher, and 1 World Language Teacher all at the High School Level; along with 1 English Language Learning Teacher, an Assistant Business Manager and a .5 State Reporting Secretary,  An Interventionist at the High School and an Interventionist at THES will not be added
Tier 2 (Restorative Budget) will result in all the additions of Tier 1 as well as adding an Interventionist at the High School and an Interventionist at THES
