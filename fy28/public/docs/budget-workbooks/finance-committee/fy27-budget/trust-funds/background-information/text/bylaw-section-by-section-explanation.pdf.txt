Town of Lunenburg, Massachusetts 
Chapter 12, § 12-9 — Distribution of Trust Funds in the Absence of a Quorum 
Section-by-Section Explanation 
Prepared: January 29, 2026 
This document explains each part of the Town of Lunenburg’s General By-Law, Chapter 12, 
§ 12-9, entitled ‘Distribution of Trust Funds in the Absence of a Quorum.’ It is written for 
presentation, Town Meeting debate, Attorney General (AG) review under M.G.L. c.40, § 32, 
and institutional recordkeeping. It summarizes what each section does, why it is needed, 
and what legal authority supports it. 
Overview of the By-Law 
This by-law is a continuity and risk-management measure. It does not transfer management 
or investment authority away from the Board of Commissioners of Trust Funds (BCTF). 
Instead, it creates a narrow, conditional process allowing distributions when the BCTF 
cannot act due to lack of a quorum. The framework rests on: (1) M.G.L. c.41, § 45 (BCTF 
authority); (2) Charter § 3-11 (local confirmation of BCTF role); (3) M.G.L. c.40, § 32 (AG 
approval process for by-laws); and (4) State Ethics Commission guidance on the Rule of 
Necessity for conflicts in emergencies. 
§ 1. Purpose and Findings 
States the limited, temporary objective: ensure distributions occur without undermining 
donor intent or the BCTF’s statutory/charter authority. This framing helps AG reviewers see 
the by-law as supplemental, not a reallocation of management. 
§ 2. Definitions 
Defines ‘absence of a quorum’ to prevent discretionary or convenience-based use. Ensures 
the Select Board only acts when the BCTF truly cannot form a quorum at a duly posted 
meeting. 
§ 3. Triggering Conditions 
Requires objective documentation of quorum failure (two duly noticed meetings or a 
sustained lack of quorum), plus written certification by the Town Manager or Town Clerk. 
This creates a defensible record for audit, AG review, and potential litigation. 
§ 4. Scope of Delegated Authority 
(a) Authorizes distributions of interest, income, and—where the trust permits—principal, 
but only within the bounds of the governing instrument and BCTF’s lawful remit. 
(b) Clarifies interest may be expended in addition to income/principal when allowed, with a 
non-depletion rule to maintain a reasonable continuing balance. 
(c) Requires preservation of corpus/minimum balances under (1) trust terms, (2) current

BCTF policies (remaining in full force and effect), and (3) fiduciary standards set by any 
engaged trust manager. 
(d) Confirms no expansion of Select Board authority; investment/management remain with 
the BCTF. 
§ 5. Application and Review Process 
Creates a transparent workflow: written application; Treasurer/Collector verification of 
balances and restrictions; Town Counsel legal review for donor compliance; public Select 
Board deliberation and vote; notice to all BCTF members. This aligns operations with 
fiduciary best practices. 
§ 6. Voting Procedures When the BCTF Lacks a Quorum 
(a) Restates the BCTF’s three-member, elected composition for context. 
(b) Requires inclusion of the sole elected BCTF member in deliberations to preserve 
institutional knowledge. 
(c) Sets a heightened safeguard: unanimous 5–0 Select Board approval when only one 
elected BCTF member remains and no emergency exists. 
(d) Establishes an emergency fallback: seat the sole elected BCTF member as a voting 
participant; the standard defaults to a majority of Select Board present, with the seated 
Commissioner counted, provided a Select Board quorum exists. 
(e) If the remaining BCTF member is appointed (not elected), they may advise but not vote; 
the Select Board proceeds by unanimous 5–0 or may defer in an emergency to a date when 
all five members are present. 
(f) Addresses conflicts/abstentions in emergencies: temporary substitution by Finance 
Committee members only if lawful and confirmed in writing by Town Counsel; otherwise, 
invoke the Rule of Necessity with counsel’s written advice; minutes must capture the 
emergency, conflicts, counsel opinion, and the path used. 
(g) Reaffirms open meeting posting and the general rule that a majority of a quorum may 
act unless a higher standard applies. 
§ 7. Transition and Pending Matters 
Prevents applications from lapsing solely because the BCTF lacks a quorum; permits the 
Select Board to act temporarily (subject to trust terms) and requires immediate reversion to 
the BCTF once a quorum is restored. 
§ 8. Fiscal Handling and Records 
Directs the Treasurer/Collector to execute approved distributions and keep trust-by-trust 
accounting (preserving corpus/earnings distinctions) and requires a written Select Board 
determination filed with the Town Clerk for each action. 
§ 9. Emergencies 
===PAGE 3===
Allows a special meeting when delay would frustrate donor intent or harm beneficiaries, 
while still requiring compliance with § 3 and posting/meeting requirements. 
§ 10. Construction; Severability 
Directs liberal construction to effect donor intent and continuity while preserving the 
BCTF’s authority; ensures that if one clause is invalidated, the remainder remains effective. 
§ 11. Effective Date 
Ties effectiveness to AG approval or lapse of 90 days after submission by the Town Clerk, 
with required posting/publishing—exactly as set out in M.G.L. c.40, § 32.
