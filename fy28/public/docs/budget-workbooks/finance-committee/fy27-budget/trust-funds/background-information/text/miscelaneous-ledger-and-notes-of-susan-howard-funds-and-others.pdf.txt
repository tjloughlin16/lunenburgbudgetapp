June 5, 1972
Jean Tenander, Library Manager
Ritter Memorial Library
Lunenburg, Massachusetts 01462
Dear Jean:
Enclosed is as much information as I have been able to come up with with regard to
the varlous Library Trust Funds. Although some of the bequests refer to special committees to
administer the funds, it has been assumed that in general the terms of such bequests would
not be violated by the Trust Fund Commissioners' serving as such committees.
With the exception of Numbers 4, 5, and 9, the exact terms of the bequests are
given in excepts from the wills or in votes by the Town. The exceptions are as follows:
In the case of Number 4, Lucy Goodrich, I have not been able to locate the
original deposit book, a will, or any minutes related thereto. There is a slight chance
that there may be something in the Town's safety deposit box, which i seldom go to,
but I doubt it. I did find and note her birth date and, assuming that the bequest was
SOSAN
by the person for whom the account is named and this is not always the case
How record of probate in the yeur of her death (December 23, 1883) may give wording of
Library Bequest shows date of deposit as February 27, 1917, which may mean that an
heir or friend of Susan made the Library Bequest in her honor. Also, Cunningham
History gives a Susannah Taylor Howard born February 16, 1830 (which birth date
coincides with information on burial card referred to above, which shows age at time
of death on October 30, 1902 as being 72 years, 8 months, 14 days) who lived unmar-
ried in 1874 with a sister in Manchester, New Hampshire. Cunningham also shows that
the sister died before Susan. The only brother or sister who appeared to have died ofter
she was William, who died on February 21, 1920 at age 78. If he made the bequest it
must have been three years before his death, as opposed to being a condition of his will.
I have to add that this poor fellow could hardly have been expected to do things in the
usual manner, as according to Cunningham he "lost his right arm by the premature dis-
charge of a cannon, while firing a salute on July 4, 1860, and fell from a staging on
===PAGE 2===
on a building while painting in 1872 breaking one leg, and worse than breaking the other."
He lived unmarried in Fitchburg and if I were going to check a probate other than Susan's
own, I think it would be his, even though the dates don't coincide.
In the case of Number 9, Adin and May Estabrook, the vote is in broad form
but I would tend to think that the will probably spells out the ferms of the bequest.
Note May died last in 1949, and it is her probate which should be checked in view
of deposit date in 1950.
Of the remaining nine bequests, eight permit the use of the income only, and all of the
eight specify in one manner or another that such income be used for the purchase of books. The
ninth bequest (Number 8) In the name of Ebenezer Bailey in the amount of $500.00, clearly states
that principal and income may be used for (any) library purposes in a manner deemed fitting by the
Library Trustees (here finally is your chance, Jeanil). As far as the argument as to whether or not
amount spent on books should be over and above the amount specified in the budget is concerned,
I'm afrid it's a moot question, as in all probability amounts budgeted would have been the same
whether or not there were, in addition, bequests for the same purpose.
References in deposit books with regard to manner of withdrawing principal relate to
withdrowals for reinvestment purposes and should not be construed as authorizing use of principal.
If your husband should check any probates and/or come up with any additional information,
could I please have a copy for the Trust Fund files?
Yours very truly,
Mary L. Padula, Treasurer
