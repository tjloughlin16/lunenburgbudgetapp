Town of Lunenburg, Massachusetts  
Policy on Delegation of Trustee Powers 
by the Board of Commissioners of Trust 
Funds 
Authority: M.G.L. c.41, §45; M.G.L. c.203E, §807; M.G.L. c.44, §54 (and, if accepted, §54(b)). 
1) Purpose and Applicability 
This policy establishes standards and procedures for delegation of trustee duties or powers 
by the Board of Commissioners of Trust Funds (the “Commission”) in administering 
municipal trust funds created for the benefit of the Town or its inhabitants. It applies to all 
trust funds unless a donor instrument provides otherwise. M.G.L. c.41, §45 places the 
management of all trust funds with the Commission (except Boston), unless a donor has 
directed otherwise. [Source: c.41, §45] 
2) Legal Framework (Summary) 
2.1 Delegation by Trustee (M.G.L. c.203E, §807). A trustee may delegate duties and 
powers if prudent, and must use reasonable care, skill, and caution in selecting an agent, 
setting the scope/terms, and periodically reviewing the agent’s actions. The agent owes a 
duty of reasonable care, and a trustee who complies with §807(a) is not liable for the 
agent’s acts. Acceptance of a delegation submits the agent to Massachusetts court 
jurisdiction. [Source: c.203E, §807] 
2.2 Commission’s Statutory Role (M.G.L. c.41, §45). The Commission “shall have the 
management of all trust funds” given or bequeathed for the benefit of the town or its 
inhabitants, unless a donor provides otherwise. [Source: c.41, §45] 
2.3 Treasurer’s Custody/Investment Role (M.G.L. c.44, §54). Trust funds are in the 
custody of the Treasurer and invested per §54 (or, if the Town accepts §54(b), funds may be 
managed as a pool under c.203C prudent investor rules, subject to donor terms). These 
requirements are in addition to trustee duties under c.203E. [Sources: c.44, §54; §54(b)] 
Interpretive Note: Delegation under §807 cannot override donor instructions or statutory 
roles (e.g., Treasurer’s custody/investment under c.44, §54). The Commission remains the 
managing trustee; the Treasurer remains the custodial/investment officer as prescribed by 
statute.

3) Policy Statements 
3.1 General Delegation Authority. The Commission may delegate discrete administrative 
or professional functions (e.g., investment advisory, operational processing, due diligence) 
when the Commission determines delegation is prudent and in the best interests of the 
trusts. All delegations must be in writing, specify scope, standards, duration, reporting, and 
provide for termination. [Source: c.203E, §807] 
3.2 Non-Delegable Core Duties. The Commission retains ultimate responsibility for 
honoring donor intent and trust purposes; making management decisions expressly 
assigned to the Commission by statute or donor instrument; and ensuring compliance with 
c.44, §54 (and §54(b), if adopted). Delegations shall not abrogate these duties. [Sources: 
c.41, §45; c.44, §54] 
3.3 Selection and Oversight of Agents. Prior to delegating, the Commission shall evaluate 
the proposed agent’s qualifications and independence, establish written terms, and 
implement periodic reviews to monitor compliance and performance, documenting findings 
in Commission minutes. [Source: c.203E, §807] 
3.4 Documentation Requirements. Each delegation instrument must identify the affected 
trust(s); describe delegated tasks; state performance standards; set reporting 
(frequency/content); acknowledge the agent’s duty of reasonable care and submission to 
MA jurisdiction; and include expiration and revocation conditions. [Source: c.203E, §807] 
4) Special Circumstance: Temporary Delegation to the Select Board When 
Quorum Is Lacking 
4.1 Policy Question. May the Commission temporarily delegate to the Select Board during 
a lack of quorum, where (a) the Commission has an adopted policy/practice or (b) a by-law 
expressly authorizes such temporary delegation? 
4.2 Findings.  
• Permissive Delegation Under §807: The Uniform Trust Code allows delegation if prudent 
with oversight, including to another municipal body acting as an agent. [Source: c.203E, 
§807] 
• Commission’s Statutory Management: Because c.41, §45 vests management in the 
Commission, any delegation to the Select Board must be narrowly tailored, time-limited, 
and structured so the Select Board acts only as agent; it may not supplant the Commission 
as managing trustee. [Source: c.41, §45] 
• Treasurer’s Role Preserved: Delegation shall not transfer the Treasurer’s 
custody/investment responsibilities under c.44, §54 (and any accepted §54(b)). 
Coordination with the Treasurer is required for actions implicating deposits or investments. 
[Sources: c.44, §54; §54(b)]

4.3 Conclusion (Conflict Check). If authorized by a Lunenburg by-law or formal 
Commission policy that contemplates quorum failure, a temporary, limited delegation to the 
Select Board is compatible with state law provided it meets §807’s prudence/oversight 
standards, respects donor instructions, and does not alter the Treasurer’s statutory 
functions. If no by-law exists, the Commission may still rely on §807 for a narrowly defined, 
time-limited delegation during a quorum lapse with the same safeguards; adoption of a by-
law or formal policy is recommended for clarity. [Sources: c.203E, §807; c.41, §45; c.44, 
§54] 
5) Required Procedure for Temporary Delegation (Quorum Failure) 
1. Written Findings: Document the lack of quorum, the time-sensitive need, and risks to 
donor intent/beneficiaries if delayed. [Source: c.203E, §807] 
2. Delegation Order: Adopt a written Delegation Order to the Select Board specifying scope 
(limited tasks), standards (donor terms; Commission policies; c.44, §54/§54(b)), term (until 
next Commission meeting or ≤60 days), reporting, and acceptance (Select Board vote 
acknowledging §807(d) jurisdiction). [Sources: c.203E, §807; c.44, §54] 
3. Treasurer Coordination: If the delegated action touches custody or investments, 
coordinate with the Treasurer to ensure §54 compliance. [Source: c.44, §54] 
4. Ratification & Review: Place the agent’s report on the next Commission agenda; review 
for compliance under §807, and ratify or direct corrective actions as needed. [Source: 
c.203E, §807]

7) Quick Reference: Conflicts & Safeguards 
• Donor terms control: If a will/trust instrument speaks to governance or prohibits 
delegation, follow the donor. [Source: c.41, §45] 
• Commission remains manager: Delegation creates an agent, not a replacement governing 
body. [Source: c.41, §45] 
• Treasurer’s statutory role: Custody/investment duties remain with the Treasurer 
(coordinate accordingly). [Sources: c.44, §54; §54(b)] 
• §807 compliance: Prudence in selection, written scope/terms, and periodic review. 
[Source: c.203E, §807] 
• Local by-law/policy: Prefer explicit authorization for quorum‑failure contingencies. 
[Source: c.41, §45]

Appendix A – Executive Summary 
Executive Summary – Delegation Policy for Lunenburg Trust Funds 
This executive summary outlines the authority of the Lunenburg Board of Commissioners of 
Trust Funds to delegate certain trustee powers under Massachusetts law. Massachusetts 
General Laws (M.G.L.) Chapter 203E, Section 807 expressly permits trustees to delegate 
duties and powers when prudent, provided they exercise reasonable care in selecting and 
supervising the agent. [1] Meanwhile, M.G.L. Chapter 41, Section 45 grants municipal trust 
fund commissions the statutory responsibility to manage all trust funds given to the town 
unless donor instructions dictate otherwise. [2] Additionally, M.G.L. Chapter 44, Section 54 
establishes that trust funds remain in the custody of the municipal treasurer, who must 
invest the funds according to statute or, if adopted locally, the prudent investor standard 
under Section 54(b). [3] Within this legal framework, the Commission may temporarily 
delegate limited tasks to the Select Board during circumstances such as a lack of quorum, 
provided the delegation is prudently limited, time‑bound, documented, and does not 
infringe on donor intent or the treasurer’s statutory role. A formal policy or by‑law is 
recommended to clarify such emergency delegation authority.
