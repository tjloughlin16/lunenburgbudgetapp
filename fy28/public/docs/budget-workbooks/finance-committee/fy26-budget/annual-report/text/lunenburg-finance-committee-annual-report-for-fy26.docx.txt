Lunenburg Finance Committee Annual Report for FY26
On May 3, 2025, members of the public will gather in the Lunenburg Middle/High School and participate in the centuries-long tradition of New England style town government.  As part of this tradition, the Finance Committee has reviewed all monied articles, recommended updates, held public hearings and has voted on our official recommendations. This warrant contains five budgetary articles that are worth highlighting:
Article M is the Omnibus Budget article that provides most funds for the operations of town (both municipal and schools).
Article N seeks to provide one-time, free cash funding to the School Department.  This breaks from Lunenburg’s traditional practices and from municipal government best practice but may be necessary to fund essential school services.
Article JJ is a Citizens Petition requesting a Proposition 2 ½ override in the amount of $2,099,337.
Article K is a Debt Exclusion article requesting $13.8M in bonds to fund enhancements to Marshall Park.
Article L is a Debt Exclusion article requesting $21,409,100 - $22,301,145 in bonds to renovate Town Hall into a Meeting House, renovate Riter Memorial and add a Town Hall addition onto Ritter Memorial.
The first three articles are related and align to Operational budgets and service levels for the town.  This report will summarize the impact to planned operations and with detailed presentations given at Town Meeting.  The last two articles are debt exclusion articles and are the results of years of planning and design work by two Commissions.
Town Revenue Projections
Lunenburg’s revenue comes from multiple sources with the primary source being Property Tax.  This year’s budget builds on the FY25 Levy Limit of $31,602,108 that includes increases from the successful FY25 Proposition 2 ½ override.  By law the Levy limit automatically increases by 2 ½ percent which results in an increase of $790,052.70.  The town then estimates New Growth of $400,000.  The 2 ½ levy limit increase plus new growth result in an increase on 3.76%.  Finally, previously approved Excluded Debt of $2,213,677 is applied resulting in a Maximum Allowable Levy of $35,005,838. 
FY2026 Net State Aid is estimated at $9,803,871 and is based on the Governor's budget.  There is a chance that this number could increase if our State Representatives successfully fight for additional revenue from the state budget.  From a Lunenburg resident’s perspective, State Aid comes from our income taxes and Sales Tax.  One important aspect of State Aid is the Chapter 70 Governor’s budget is $9,109,185. The final sources of revenue for the town include local receipts (e.g. Excise Tax, Meals Tax, etc.) and other available funds derived mostly from enterprise funds such as Sewer.  Outside of the omnibus budget sources of revenue come in the form of grants.  One positive news from the state is that Chapter 90 funding to municipalities for the implementation of capital improvements on local public ways has increased from $418,227 to $690,000.  This increase comes from a 5-year bond bill at the state level.
In FY25, the town appropriated $50,000 for grant writing services.  With the changes in town managers, including an interim town manager, these funds were not spent.  The Finance Committee continues to recommend that grant services are maximized by the town and that this budget item remains intact.
Operations
Many of the articles at Town meeting relate to operational budgets of our town departments.  Lunenburg, along with most municipalities in the Commonwealth, faces significant headwinds related to increased health insurance costs (14.84% increase), stagnant state aid contributions, necessary salary increases to remain competitive and inflation.  As a result of these headwinds, department staffing plans are either held level, placed on hold or result in staffing reductions. The Fire Department and the School Department have the most significant impacts this year, although all departments are feeling pressure.
School Department Operations
The School Committee and the Superintendent have presented to the Finance Committee a budget that aligns with the Town Managers.  This budget represents $25,787,474 via the omnibus budget.  The schools do receive grants to augment this budget, but from a Town Meeting perspective the omnibus budget is what is voted on by the public.  The Lunenburg Schools website has a significant amount of data associated with the budget.  The primary issue associated with this budget is that it will eliminate 28 Full Time Equivalents and 39 actual positions.
A citizen’s petition article in the amount of $2,099,337 has been proposed to restore the budget to level funding. Fire Department Operations
The fire department currently operates three shifts to cover 24/7 operations in the town resulting in a 48-hr, 6 day per week shift.  Sunday shifts are covered by call and per diem staff and 62% of the coverage is by Career Firefighters.  Massachusetts has 197 Fire Departments that provide 24/7 coverage, and Lunenburg is only one of six that staff with three shifts.  The other 191 departments operate with a fourth shift.  This results in Lunenburg being an unattractive town when trying to hire and retain first responders.  This fourth shift plan is placed on hold for yet another year but should be addressed in the future.
Borrowing
The town utilizes two types of borrowing to finance certain town expenses.  Regular Debt is typically used for smaller projects and is included within the Tax Levy Limit.  Excludable Debt is outside of the Tax Levy Limit and requires both Town Meeting approval and approval at the ballot.  Excludable Debt , unlike a Proposition 2 ½ Override, is not a permanent increase in taxes.  Once the debt matures, the Tax Rate goes down accordingly.  The town has a policy that the Principal and Interest on Regular debt is lower than 4%, Excludable Debt is lower than 11% and combined are lower than 14%. 
This year’s budget has a reduction in regular debt of $393, 881.14.  This reduction is going towards 1) the FY26 Capital Program in the amount of $244,576 and 2) offsetting operating budget in the amount of $149,305.
Two Excludable Debt and one Regular Debt projects are on the Warrant and the Ballot.  Marshall Park improvements and Municipal Building are proposed to be funded by Excludable Debt.  Article I seeks funds to design and prepare bid solicitations for the High School Track and Field Complex and is proposed to be funded by Regular debt.
