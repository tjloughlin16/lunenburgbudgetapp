 RE: Open Meeting Law Complaint 
 
Dear Attorney Cunningham: 
 
This office received a complaint from Arshan Gailus on March 5, 2025, alleging that the 
Arlington Affordable Housing Trust Fund Board of Trustees (the “Board”) violated the Open 
Meeting Law, G.L. c. 30A, §§ 18-25.
1  The complaint was originally filed with the Board on 
February 12, and you responded on behalf of the Board by letter dated March 3.2  In his 
complaint, Mr. Gailus alleges that the Board failed to meet to review an Open Meeting Law 
Complaint, which he filed with the Board on December 23, 2024 (“the December 23 
Complaint”).   
 
Following review, we resolve this complaint by informal action, in accordance with 940 
CMR 29.07(2)(a).3  Within 14 business days of receiving an Open Meeting Law complaint, the 
chair must distribute the complaint to all members of the public body, the public body must meet 
to review the complaint, and the public body must then respond to the complainant in writing 
and provide our office with a copy of the complaint and the response.  See G.L. c. 30A, 
§ 23(b); 940 CMR 29.05(5).  Once a public body has reviewed a complaint during a meeting, its 
decision to simply refer the complaint, rather than discuss its substance, is the public body’s

prerogative.  See OML 2019-40; OML 2017-96.  The Open Meeting Law does not require a 
public body to substantively discuss the merits of the complaint in detail.  See OML 2021-128.  
A public body may review a complaint during a meeting and refer the complaint to a designee, 
such as legal counsel, to respond, or a designee may receive and draft a response to the 
complaint in advance of a meeting for the public body to approve during a meeting, but the 
public body may not remove itself entirely from the process.  See OML 2020-164.  The 
complainant alleges, and the Board does not deny, that Arlington Town Counsel responded to the 
December 23 Complaint without the Board first having met to review the complaint.  By failing 
to meet to review the December 23 Complaint before a response was sent on its behalf, the 
Board violated the Open Meeting Law.4   
     
We order that the Board meet to review the December 23 Complaint within 25 days of 
the date of this letter, if it has not already.  We also order the Board’s immediate and future 
compliance with the Open Meeting Law.  We now consider the complaint addressed by this 
determination to be resolved.  This determination does not address any other complaints that may 
be pending with our office or the Board.  Please feel free to contact the Division at (617) 963 - 
2540 if you have any questions.
