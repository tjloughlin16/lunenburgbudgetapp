RE: Open Meeting law Complaint 
Dear Attorney Angelini: 
This office received a complaint on June 23, 2014 from Peter and Jill Mann, alleging that 
the Narragansett Regional School Committee (the "Committee") violated the Open Meeting 
Law, G.L. c. 30A, §§ 1 8-25. The complaint alleges that the Committee failed to discuss the 
Manns' May 8, 2014 retaliation complaint in detail during its May 21, 2014 meeting. The Open 
Meeting Law complaint was filed with the Committee on May 22, 2014. The Committee 
responded in a letter dated May 23, 2014. 
Following our review, we find that the Committee did not violate the Open Meeting Law 
when discussing the Mann's May 8, 2014 retaliation complaint during its May 21, 2014 meeting. 
In reaching a determination, we reviewed the May 22, 2014 complaint filed with the Committee; 
the Committee's May 23, 2014 response; and the June 23, 2014 complaint filed with our office. 
Additionally, we reviewed the minutes of the Committee's May 21, 2014 meeting. 
FACTS 
We find the facts as follows. On May 8, 2014, the Manns filed a complaint with the 
Committee alleging that Superintendent Ruth Miller made a false accusation to the Phillipston 
Police Chief on May 6, 2014. The Superintendent had expressed concern that the Manns might 
show up to the Committee's May 7, 2014 meeting, and that the meeting could require police 
presence. The complaint alleged that the Superintendent's accusation was made in retaliation for 
an Open Meeting Law complaint that the Manns filed on March 29, 2014.

The Committee held a meeting on May 21, 2014. During the "Public Forum" portion of 
the meeting, Mr. Mann addressed the Committee and asked to discuss the retaliation complaint. 
Committee Chair Rae-Ann Trifilo replied that the complaint was to be discussed later in the 
meeting. When the Committee reached the agenda topic "5/8 Formal Complaint by Mr. and 
Mrs. Mann" under "New Business/Superintendent's Report," Chair Trifilo addressed the Manns' 
May 8, 2014 retaliation complaint. Chair Trifilo reported that the Committee had received the 
complaint and that "given information provided by both the Phillipston Police Chief and Ms. 
Miller [Superintendent of Schools]," the Committee found the complaint to be unfounded and 
stated that it would be "passed over."1 Chair Trifilo then asked if there was any discussion on 
the matter, of which there was none. The Chair then moved to "pass over" the complaint. The 
motion was seconded and the Committee voted unanimously to pass over, and thus take no 
action on, the complaint. 
DISCUSSION 
During its May 21, 2014 meeting, the Committee discussed the Manns' retaliation 
complaint. The basis of the present Open Meeting Faw complaint is that the Committee did not 
discuss the retaliation complaint in sufficient detail. The Open Meeting Faw does not require a 
public body to discuss any topic, nor does it establish requirements for the level of detail a public 
body's discussions must include. See OMF 2013-64; OMF 2012-23. The law simply requires 
that a public body post a timely notice listing the topics the chair reasonably anticipates will be 
discussed, and that such discussions take place during meetings. See G.F. c. 30A, §§ 20(a), (b); 
id. Accordingly, we find that the Committee's May 21, 2014 discussion of the Manns' May 8, 
2014 retaliation complaint did not violate the Open Meeting Faw. We express no opinion as to 
whether any other law may have required the Committee to discuss the complaint in greater 
detail. 
CONCFUSION 
For the reasons stated above, we find that the Committee did not violate the Open 
Meeting Faw. We now consider the complaint addressed by this determination to be resolved. 
This determination does not address any other complaints that may be pending with the 
Committee or with our office. Please feel free to contact our office at (617) 963-2540 if you 
have any questions.
