RE: Open Meeting Law Complaint 
Dear Mr. Nason: 
The Attorney General's Office reviewed a complaint filed by Monica Ryan, dated 
February 2, 2012, alleging that the Lee Energy Efficiency Committee (the "Committee") violated 
the Open Meeting Law, G.L. c. 30A, §§ 18-25. Specifically, the complaint alleges that the 
Committee is a public body subject to the Open Meeting Law and that it failed to create and 
approve meeting minutes for at least two years. The complaint was filed with the Committee on 
or about January 4, 2012, and the Committee responded by letter from the Town Administrator' 
on January 17, 2012. 
Following our review, we fmd that the Committee violated the Open Meeting by failing 
to create and approve minutes of its meetings from July 1, 2010 through January 4, 2012, as 
required by G.L. c. 30A, §§ 22(a), (c). $ee OML 2012-19; OML 2011 -39. 2  In reaching this 
determination, we reviewed the January 4, 2012 complaint; the Committee's January 17, 2012 
response; and the February 2, 2012 complaint filed with our office. 
The Town Administrator acknowledged the violation, stating in his January 17, 2012 
letter that "Ms. Ryan complains that the Energy Efficiency Advisory Committee has not created 
and maintained minutes of its meetings and she is correct." In proposing steps to remedy the 
violation, the Town Administrator stated that 1) the Committee will create minutes of all future 
meetings; 2) that Committee has created minutes of its December 20, 2011 and January 3, 2012 
meetings; and 3) he will ask the Committee to elect a chairperson. 
For purposes of clarity in this letter, we will refer to you in the third person.

We appreciate the Committee's willingness to admit its error and commitment to taking 
remedial action. In addition to the remedial steps proposed by the Committee, we order 
immediate and future compliance with the Open Meeting Law, and order the Committee to 
create and approve minutes of all Committee meetings dating back to July 1, 2010, to the best of 
its ability. This should occur within ninety (90) days of receipt of this letter. 
As a final matter, we note that the complainant alleges the Committee holds meetings at 
times that are difficult for members of the public to attend, and that the Committee posts notices 
barely more than 48 hours before a meeting. While we encourage the Committee to meet at 
times that are convenient for both the public and members of the Committee, we find that the 
Committee did not violate the Open Meeting Law in either regard. 
We now consider this matter closed. Please contact me if you have any questions 
regarding this letter.
