RE: Open Meeting Law Complaint 
Dear Chair Carlino: 
This office received a complaint from Sharon MacDonald, dated April 10, 2012, alleging 
that the Lee Board of Selectmen (the "Board") violated the Open Meeting Law, G.L. c. 30A, §§ 
18-25. Ms. MacDonald originally filed her complaint with the Board on March 2, 2012, and the 
Board responded by letter dated March 12, 2012. In her complaint, Ms. MacDonald alleges that 
the approved minutes from the Board's October 19, 2010 meeting do not adequately reflect the 
discussions from that meeting. She requests relief in the form of the Board amending, and 
approving as amended, the October 19, 2010 meeting minutes to include the allegedly omitted 
information. 
In reaching our determination, we reviewed Ms. MacDonald's original March 2, 2012 
complaint; the Board's March 12, 2012 response; and Ms. MacDonald's April 10, 2012 
complaint filed with our office. We also reviewed the Board's May 24, 2012 response to our 
May 16, 2012 letter requesting additional information, including the October 19, 2010 meeting 
notice and agenda; October 19, 2010 meeting minutes approved at the Board's February 7, 2012 
meeting; revised October 19, 2010 minutes approved at the Board's March 6, 2012 meeting; and 
a video recording of the October 19, 2010 meeting. Additionally, we reviewed the March 6, 
2012 meeting minutes and a Berkshire Record article titled "Lee Cultural Council facing 
questions about use of funds" that appeared in the newspaper's February 17-23, 2012 edition. 
Following our review, we find that the Board did not violate the Open Meeting Law 
because the minutes of its October 19, 2010 meeting were sufficiently detailed and, in response 
to the complaint, the Board amended the minutes on March 6, 2012 to add additional detail.

FACTS 
Based upon our review of the material listed above, the facts are as follows. The Board 
met in open session on October 19, 2010. During the "Pending Business" portion of the 
meeting, Matthew Carty and Philip Smith, Co-Chairs of the Lee Cultural Committee (LCC), 
petitioned the Board to support a warrant article asking the Town's Finance Committee to 
approve up to $10,000, with the LCC raising matching funds, for various LCC projects. Both 
Mr. Carty and Mr. Smith reminded the Board of the LCC's goal of securing sufficient funding, 
with the Board's permission, to hire "a consultant as well as financ[e] various cultural events 
throughout the year" to make the Town more of a cultural destination like neighboring towns. 
The minutes indicate that there was Idliscussion on putting another article on the special town 
meeting warrant," and that Town Administrator Robert Nason "asked about meeting to approve 
this new article." The "Board discussed continuing this meeting," and Board Chair David 
Consolati stated that "he would like to see a concrete proposal." Selectwoman Patricia Carlino 
then advised the Co-Chairs to "bring the proposed language to the Selectmen's office tomorrow 
morning" and the Board subsequently agreed to meet the next day, October 20, 2010 at 4:00 pm 
in the Selectmen's office.' 
The video recording of the October 19, 2010 meeting reflects discussion surrounding 
funding for the LCC and the proposed warrant article. It also shows Selectman Gordon Bailey 
explaining that the Massachusetts Cultural Council (MCC) sent an email stating that the LCC 
must determine where its funding came from and the intent of its donors, and ensure that any 
earmarked funds were put to the donor's intended use. Selectman Bailey asked the Council if it 
had "any suggested language" for the warrant article and how the LCC would spend the money. 
In response, LCC member, Shaun Mahoney, stated that she did "have proposed language, but not 
with her" and explained what purposes the funds would serve. Board Chair Consolati then 
expressed concern that there was nothing concrete about the LCC's proposal and Ms. Mahoney 
stated that the LCC had every intention to provide detail in the warrant article. The Board agreed 
to continue this discussion the following afternoon. 
On March 6, 2012, following its receipt of this complaint, the Board amended the 
minutes of the October 19, 2010 meeting by adding this sentence: "Selectman Bailey 
acknowledged receipt of an e-mail from the State Cultural Council concerning the use of 
donations previously received by the Lee Cultural Council." The Board did not add any 
reference to Ms. Mahoney. 
DISCUSSION 
The Open Meeting Law requires a public body to "create and maintain accurate minutes 
of all meetings, including executive sessions, setting forth the date, time and place, the members 
present or absent, a summary of the discussions on each subject, a list of documents and other 
exhibits used at the meeting, the decisions made and the actions taken at each meeting, including 
In his March 12, 2012 response to the complaint, Chair Consolati writes that the members of the Board did not 
meet on October 20, 2010 to discuss the proposed warrant language. We remind the Board that notice must be 
posted for all meetings, including continuations of previously posted meetings, in accordance with the requirements 
of the Open Meeting Law. See  G.L. c. 30A, § 20(b).

public bodies to create and maintain accurate minutes of all meetings, we generally only review 
minutes for substantial compliance with the accuracy requirement. To substantially comply with 
the Law's requirement, the minutes should contain enough detail and accuracy so that a member 
of the public who did not attend the meeting could read the minutes and have a clear 
understanding of what occurred. See OML 2013-14; OML 2012-29; OML 2011-55. 2  
After reviewing both the initially approved and then revised minutes of the Board's 
October 19, 2010 open session, as well as the video recording of that meeting, we find that the 
Board's October 19, 2010 minutes substantially complied with the requirement that it provide an 
accurate and sufficiently detailed summary of the discussion that occurred. While the minutes 
do not include every remark or opinion presented at the meeting, and could have included more 
detail regarding the Board's discussion of the LCC's presentation and Ms. Mahoney's remarks to 
the Board, the Board included a sufficiently detailed summary of the discussion that occurred. 
See G.L. c. 30A, § 22(a). We therefore find no violation of the Open Meeting Law. 
CONCLUSION 
For the reasons stated above, we find that the Board did not violate the Open Meeting 
Law. 
We now consider the complaint addressed by this determination to be resolved. This 
determination does not address any other complaints which may be pending with our office or 
the Board. Please feel free to contact me if you have any questions or believe any facts in this 
letter to be inaccurate.
