RE: Open Meeting Law Complaint 
Dear Mr. McWane: 
Our office received a complaint from Richard Boroff, dated January 2, 2014, 
alleging that the Hamilton Finance and Advisory Committee (the "Committee") violated 
the Open Meeting Law, G.L. c. 30A, §§ 18-25. The complaint was originally filed with 
the Committee on or about October 21, 2013, and you responded on behalf of the 
Committee on October 31, 2013.1 In his complaint, Mr. Boroff alleges that the Committee 
met on Saturday, October 18, 2013 to conduct business and make recommendations on 
Special Town Meeting warrant articles without posting notice at least 48 hours in advance 
of the meeting. 
Following our review, we find that the Committee violated the Open Meeting Law 
by holding a meeting without posting a notice at least 48 hours before the meeting. 
Because the Chair was warned via email by two individuals prior to the meeting that the 
meeting lacked sufficient notice, and chose to proceed nevertheless, we find this to be an 
intentional violation. 
In reaching this determination, we reviewed the October 21, 2013 complaint filed 
with the Committee; the Committee's October 31, 2013 response; and the January 2, 2013

FACTS 
We find the facts as follows. On Wednesday, October 16, 2013, Town Manager 
Michael Lombardo reviewed the agenda for the Committee's Saturday, October 19, 2013 
meeting with finance department staff and discussed the need to post a notice that evening. 
However, according to Mr. Lombardo, the staff was preoccupied with year-end account 
closings, preparation for a pre-audit meeting, the upcoming Committee meeting, and 
preparation for a November 4, 2013 Special Town Meeting. Thus, the notice was not 
posted until the next morning. The notice was posted to the Town website2 at 
approximately 9:12 AM on Thursday, October 17, 2013. The noticed listed six topics for 
discussion, including "Special Town Meeting Warrant - Review and Vote Article 
Recommendations." The notice listed the meeting time as 8:30AM on Saturday, October 
19, 2013. 
On Friday, October 18, 2013, Michelle Bailey emailed Chair McWane, Town 
Manager Lombardo, and one other individual, with the subject line, "Fin Com Meeting on 
Saturday morning does not meet OML posting requirement." In the body of the email, Ms. 
Bailey wrote that "this meeting was posted at 9:12am on Oct. 17, the notice (excluding 
Saturday hours, which start at 12:01am) is 38 hours and 48 minutes, well shy of the 
required 48-hours. Please reschedule this meeting to a time that allows for the proper 
posting to be made." 
On the morning of October 19, 2013, Richard Boroff also sent an email to Chair 
McWane, Town Manager Lombardo, and several others, writing that "[a]s you have been 
informed by Michelle Bailey, your meeting posting does not [meet] the State OML 
requirements. Please Reschedule." 
On the morning of Saturday, October 19, 2013, Mr. Boroff arrived early for the 
meeting. He approached Town Manager Lombardo and asked him if he had received the 
emails from Mr. Boroff and Ms. Bailey. Town Manager Lombardo stated that he had 
received them and took responsibility for the posting being late. When Chair McWane 
arrived, Mr. Boroff approached Chair McWane regarding the notice's untimeliness. 
Although there is disagreement between Mr. Boroff and Chair McWane as to what was 
said between them, it appears that Chair McWane understood that the notice for the 
meeting did not meet the Open Meeting Law's 48-hour requirement, but decided to 
continue with the meeting anyway. According to Chair McWane's October 31, 2013

DISCUSSION 
The Open Meeting Law requires that, except in an emergency, "a public body shall 
post notice of every meeting at least 48 hours prior to such meeting, excluding Saturdays, 
Sundays and legal holidays." G.L. c. 30A, § 20(b). In an emergency, a public body shall 
post notice as soon as reasonably possible prior to such meeting. Id. An emergency is 
defined as "a sudden, generally unexpected occurrence or set of circumstances demanding 
immediate action." G.L. c. 30A, § 18; OML 2011-45.3 
The notice for the Committee's Saturday, October 19, 2013 meeting was posted at 
9:12 AM on Thursday, October 17, 2013. Because Saturdays are excluded from the 48 
hour posting requirement, the notice fell short of the required time period by over nine 
hours. Before the meeting, two different individuals e-mailed the Chair about the 
deficiency with the notice posting. Additionally, the complainant attended the meeting and 
informed the Chair before its start that the meeting was not in compliance with the Open 
Meeting Law. The Chair nevertheless proceeded with the meeting and the Committee took 
a series of votes on warrant articles for the Special Town Meeting. 
In responding to the complaint. Chair McWane explains that "[w]e agreed to 
proceed with the meeting based on the need for Finance Committee guidance on warrant 
articles to be presented to the [Board of Selectmen] meeting on Monday, October 21. The 
October 21 meeting was the only scheduled meeting of the [Board of Selectmen] before 
the [November 4] Special Town Meeting." Although the Committee was working to meet 
a deadline, the circumstances were not of the sort that justify an emergency meeting. An 
emergency is defined as "a sudden, generally unexpected occurrence or set of 
circumstances demanding immediate action." G.L. c. 30A, § 18; OML 2011-45. Where a 
public body has advance notice of circumstances that are likely to require deliberation, it is 
not an emergency. Emergency meetings are reserved for circumstances that are 
unanticipated and require an immediate response to, for example, protect public safety or

health. See OML 2011-45. Here, the Special Town Meeting was scheduled for November 
4, 2013. The Committee could have rescheduled its meeting for some time during the 15 
days before the Special Town Meeting, and still have provided the public with the requisite 
48 hours' notice. While it may have been inconvenient for the Committee and for the 
Board of Selectmen, which may have decided to schedule an additional meeting to review 
the Committee's recommendations, this is what the law required. Additionally, it is clear 
from these bodies' actions following the filing of this complaint that they were able to 
convene a properly posted meeting during that period, as they held a joint meeting to ratify 
the Committee's votes just prior to the Special Town Meeting. We therefore find that the 
Committee violated the Open Meeting Law. 
While we note the Committee's efforts to respond to the complaint by 
acknowledging the violation and ratifying its votes during a subsequent open meeting, we 
nevertheless find this to be an intentional violation of the Open Meeting Law. An 
intentional violation means an "act or omission by a public body or a member thereof, in 
knowing violation of [the Open Meeting Law]." 940 CMR 29.02. Evidence of an 
intentional violation includes a violation of the law where "the public body or public body 
member (a) acted with specific intent to violate the law." Id. The Committee acted with 
specific intent to violate the law because the Chair was informed via email and in person, 
prior to the October 19, 2013 meeting, that the meeting lacked proper notice, yet chose to 
proceed with the meeting in violation of the Open Meeting Law. This type of intentional 
violation cannot be fully addressed by subsequent remedial measures. Accordingly, we 
refer this matter to a hearing pursuant to 940 CMR 29.07(3) and recommend that the 
Committee be assessed a fine of $500 pursuant to G.L. c. 30A, § 23(c) ("Upon the finding 
of a violation, the attorney general may issue an order to ... impose a civil penalty upon the 
public body of not more than $1,000 for each intentional violation").4 We invite counsel 
for the Committee to contact this office to discuss the hearing process. 
CONCLUSION 
We find that the Committee violated the Open Meeting Law by holding a meeting 
without providing the public with 48 hours' notice. We find this violation to be 
intentional, and refer this matter to a hearing pursuant to 940 CMR 29.07(3). Additionally, 
we order the Committee's immediate and future compliance with the Open Meeting Law.
