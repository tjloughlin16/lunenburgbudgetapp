 RE:  Open Meeting Law Complaint 
 
Dear Director Curry:  
 
 This office received a complaint from Ms. Jacquelyn Wehtje on April 4, 2022, alleging 
that the Fitchburg Board of Health (the “Board”) violated the Open Meeting Law, G.L. c. 30A, 
§§ 18-25.1  The complaint was originally filed with the Board on March 4, and you responded, 
on behalf of the Board, by letter dated March 25.2  The complaint alleges that the Board violated 
the Open Meeting Law when it delayed and limited public participation during a meeting held on 
March 3.3

We resolve this matter by informal action, in accordance with 940 CMR 29.07(2)(a), 
after reviewing the original complaint, the Board’s response to the complaint, the complaint filed 
with our office requesting further review, the notice, open session minutes, and a video recording 
of the meeting held on March 3.4  We find that the Committee did not violate the Open Meeting 
Law in the way alleged.  
 
The Open Meeting Law does not require that a public body allow public participation in 
its meetings, but rather provides that “[n]o person shall address a meeting of a public body 
without permission of the chair, and all persons shall, at the request of the chair, be silent.”  G.L. 
c. 30A § 20(f); see also OML 2021-27; OML 2020-161; OML 2017-189; OML 2014-23; OML 
2012-78.5  We do, however, encourage public bodies to allow for as much public participation as 
time and circumstances permit.  See OML 2015-12; OML 2014-129; OML 2012-59. 
 
Here, the Board duly posted notice for a meeting to be held on March 3.  The notice listed 
the following seven topics in this order: “Public Forum,” “COVID-19 Updates,” “Petitions,” 
“Order Review,” “Meeting Minutes,” “Commission Against Discrimination Discussion” and 
“Adjourn.”  Certain of these topic headings included specific item descriptions that further 
advised the public of the topics to be discussed.  Specifically, Order Review included the 
additional subtopic of “Order #2021-3 Mandating Vaccination and Testing in Fitchburg Public 
Schools.”   
 
The March 3 meeting was held as planned and the Board first heard from the 
Superintendent of Schools rather than accept public comments as the first order of business 
because the Superintendent could only attend the first portion of the meeting.  The 
Superintendent recommended that testing and masking for extracurricular activities be voluntary, 
except for the high school boys’ basketball and hockey teams until the conclusion of the 
playoffs.  The Board then voted to rescind a previous order mandating COVID-19 testing for 
Fitchburg students and staff participating in extracurricular activities and voted to “sustain” a 
separate order mandating COVID-19 testing and vaccination for Fitchburg Public Schools 
employees.  Immediately thereafter, public attendees objected that they had not yet been heard;  
the Board then accepted comments from members of the public, including the complainant, who 
expressed frustration that the Board had taken action on vaccination and testing prior to 
accepting public comments.   
 
The Open Meeting Law permits the Chair of the Board to establish parameters for public 
participation.  See OML 2013-2; OML 2012-23.  Moreover, once a meeting begins, the public 
body controls its own agenda.  Accordingly, the Law does not prohibit a public body from 
removing, postponing, or declining to discuss a topic listed on a meeting notice or taking topics 
in a different order than listed on the notice, as the Board did here when it heard from the 
Superintendent before the public.  See OML 2014-35; OML 2021-201.  It is also not a violation 
of the Open Meeting Law for a public body to vote on a topic without first taking comments
