RE: Open Meeting Law Complaints 
Dear Attorney Doucette: 
This office received five complaints from John Femino on February 27, 2017 and April 
11, 2017, alleging that the Woburn Zoning Board of Appeals (the "Board") violated the Open 
Meeting Law, G.L. c. 30A, §§ 18-25. The complaints were originally filed with the Board on or 
about December 14, 2016; December 15, 2016; December 16, 2016; March 9, 2017; and March 
10, 2017,1 and you responded, on behalf of the Board, by letters dated January 25, 2017 and 
April 6, 2017.2 In his December 14, 2016 complaint, Mr. Femino alleges that the Board's 
October 19, 2016 meeting minutes are inaccurate. In his other complaints, Mr. Femino alleges 
that the recording of the Board's October 19, 2016 and January 18, 2017 meetings were edited 
and that the notes used to create the minutes from that meeting were destroyed. Mr. Femino 
also alleges that the Board failed to provide documentation to substantiate statements made by 
the Board chair during its January 18, 2017 meeting. 
Following our review, we resolve this matter by informal action, in accordance with 940 
CMR 29.07(2)(a), and find that the Board did not violate the Open Meeting Law. The December 
14, 2016 complaint alleges that the October 19, 2016 minutes are inaccurate. Our office and the 
Board sought further clarification from the complainant regarding which parts of the minutes he 
alleged to be inaccurate. In response to both requests, the complainant declined to offer such 
clarification until the Board provided him with an unedited recording of the meeting.3

The Open Meeting Law requires that a public body "create and maintain accurate 
minutes of all meetings, including executive sessions, setting forth the date, time and place, the 
members present or absent, a summary of the discussions on each subject, a list of documents 
and other exhibits used at the meeting, the decisions made and the actions taken at each meeting, 
including the record of all votes." G.L. c. 30A, § 22(a). When reviewing minutes for 
compliance with the Open Meeting Law, we look for substantial compliance with the accuracy 
requirement. See OML 2013-64.4 By substantial compliance, we mean that the minutes should 
contain enough detail and accuracy so that a member of the public who did not attend the 
meeting could read the minutes and have a clear understanding of what occurred. See OML 
2012-106. While minutes must include a summary of the discussion on each topic, a transcript is 
not required, and the minutes do not need to include every remark or opinion presented. See 
OML 2012-29; OML 2011-55. Based upon our review, we find that the Board's October 19, 
2016 meeting minutes comply with these requirements as they contain sufficient detail about 
each discussion topic. As the complainant was unable to identify any portion of the minutes that 
he believed to be inaccurate, we find that the Board did not violate the Open Meeting Law.5 
Furthermore, we decline to review the remaining four complaints filed with the Board. 
The Division of Open Government interprets and enforces the Open Meeting Law, which 
governs requests for minutes and the determination of whether documents were used during a 
meeting, only. Any complaints involving public records requests and/or municipal records 
retention may be resolved by contacting the Supervisor of Records within the Secretary of the 
Commonwealth's Office.
