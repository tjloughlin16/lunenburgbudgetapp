RE: Open Meeting Law Complaint 
Dear Attorney Assad; 
This office received a complaint from Patrick Higgins, dated April 1, alleging that the 
Fall River School Committee (the "Committee") violated the Open Meeting Law, G.L. c. 30A, 
§§ 18-25.' The complaint was originally filed with the Committee on March 14, and the 
Committee responded by letter dated March 31. In his complaint, Mr. Higgins alleges that the 
Committee violated the Open Meeting Law by failing to: (i) follow proper procedure before 
convening in executive session, and (ii) approve meeting minutes in a timely manner. 
Following our review, we find that the Committee failed to approve minutes of its 
November 23 and December 14, 2015 meetings in a timely manner. Flowever, we find that the 
Committee approved the minutes of its January 11 meeting in a timely manner, and we also find 
that the Committee followed proper procedure before convening in executive session during its 
March 14 meeting. In reaching a determination, we reviewed the original complaint, the 
Committee's response, and the request for further review filed with our office. We also 
reviewed the minutes, both open session and executive session, of a Committee meeting held on 
March 14. Finally, we reviewed the notice of the Committee's March 14 meeting. 
We find the facts as follows. The Committee is a seven-member public body, with 
Mayor Jassiel Correia serving as its chair. The Committee typically meets once per month; it 
held meetings on January 11 and February 22. On March 10, the Committee posted notice of a 
meeting to be held on March 14. The notice stated that the Committee would convene in 
executive session during the meeting to discuss four topics:

The Committee held the March 10 meeting as planned. During the meeting, as the 
Committee prepared to enter executive session, Mayor Correia asked the Committee's legal 
counsel to state the reasons for entering executive session.2 The Committee's legal counsel then 
read aloud the reasons for the executive session in a similar manner to how they were stated on 
the meeting notice. While in executive session, the Committee, among other discussions, 
approved the minutes of executive sessions held on November 23 and December 14, 2015, as 
well as January 11. 
DISCUSSION 
I. The Committee Followed Proper Procedure Before Entering its March 14 
Executive Session. 
While meetings of a public body typically must be held in public view, a public body 
may enter into a closed executive session for any of ten statutory purposes. G.L. c. 30A, § 21(a). 
Before entering executive session, the public body chair must first state the purpose for the 
executive session, stating all subjects that may be revealed without compromising the purpose 
for which the executive session was called. See G.L. c. BOA, § 21(b)(3). 
The complaint alleges that the Committee violated the Open Meeting Law because the 
Committee's attorney, rather than the chair, stated the purposes for entering the March 14 
executive session. While the Open Meeting Law specifically provides that the chair must state 
the purposes for executive session, see OML 2016-75, we have concluded that it is permissible 
for the chair the delegate the responsibility for making the required statement to legal counsel. 
See OML 2016-108.J Here, because Mayor Correia specifically asked the Committee's legal 
counsel to state the purposes for the executive session, we find no violation of the Open Meeting 
Law. 
II. The Committee Failed to Approve Certain Minutes in a Timely Manner. 
Public bodies must create and maintain minutes of all meetings. G.L. c. 30A, § 22(a). 
Minutes of open session meetings must be created and approved in a timely manner. See G.L. c.

30A, § 22(c); OML 2012-91. Although the Open Meeting Law does not define "timely manner," 
a public body should approve minutes at the next meeting where possible. See OML 2012-91. 
For bodies that meet regularly, we have concluded that a delay of two to three months in 
approving minutes does not comply with the law. See OML 2016-62. 
Here, the complaint alleges that the Committee failed to approve minutes in a timely 
manner for its November 23 and December 14, 2015, and January 11 meetings. With respect to 
the 2015 meetings, we agree. Even though the Committee typically meets once per month, the 
Committee held multiple meetings before the minutes were approved on March 14. Even if the 
Committee believed that the executive session purpose warranted continued non-disclosure, the 
Committee was obligated to approve final minutes in a timely manner. See OML 2016-57. The 
delay in approving the November 23 and December 14, 2015 minutes violated the Open Meeting 
Law. With respect to the January 11 meeting minutes, because the Committee held only one 
intervening meeting and because it approved those minutes approximately two months after the 
meeting, we find no violation. 
CONCLUSION 
For the reasons stated above, we find that the Committee violated the Open Meeting Law 
by failing to approve certain meeting minutes in a timely manner. We order the Committee's 
immediate and future compliance with the Open Meeting Law, and we caution that similar future 
violations may be considered evidence of intent to violate the law. 
We now consider the complaint addressed by this determination to be resolved. This 
determination does not address any other complaints that may be pending with our office or the 
Committee. Please feel free to contact the Division at (617) 963-2540 if you have any questions.
