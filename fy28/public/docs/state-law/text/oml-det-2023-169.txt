 RE: Open Meeting Law Complaint 
Dear Co-Chairs Lazaris and Barnett: 
This office received a complaint from Patrick Higgins on August 11, 2023, alleging that 
the Somerset Cultural Council (the “Council”) violated the Open Meeting Law, G.L. c. 30A, §§ 
18-25.  The complaint was originally filed with the Council on or around June 8, 2023, and you 
responded, on behalf of the Council, by letter dated June 26.
1  The complaint alleges that the 
notice for the Council’s June 12 meeting failed to include a list of the specific grant applicants 
that the chair anticipated discussing.   
After review, we find that the Council did not violate the Open Meeting Law.  First, there 
is no indication on the notice for the Council’s June 12 meeting that any discussion of grant 
applicants was planned and the Council states that it did not discuss grant applicants at its June 
12 meeting.  The complainant has provided no information to the contrary, nor any explanation 
for why he asserts the notice for the June 12 meeting should have listed any grant applicants.  
When reviewing a meeting notice for sufficiency, unless it is clearly insufficient on its face, we 
do not review a meeting notice standing alone.  See OML 2015-109; OML 2014-155.
2  Rather, 
we review what was actually discussed at the meeting to determine if the notice was sufficiently 
specific to make the public aware of the discussion topics.  Where a public body does not 
actually engage in any deliberation on a particular topic, it cannot violate the Open Meeting Law 
with respect to the sufficiency of the meeting notice.  See OML 2016-76.  Moreover, the 
complaint was filed with the Council on June 8, four days prior to the June 12 meeting;
