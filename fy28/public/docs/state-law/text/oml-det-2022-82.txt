RE:  Open Meeting Law Complaint 
 
Dear Attorney Corbo: 
 
This office received a complaint from John Gaskey on January 24, 2022, alleging that the 
Carver Planning Board (the “Board”) violated the Open Meeting Law, G.L. c. 30A, §§ 18-25.1  
The complaint wase originally filed with the Board on January 25, and you responded to the 
complaint, on behalf of the Board, by letter dated February 14.  The complaint alleges that the 
Chair of the Board failed to disseminate a prior Open Meeting Law complaint to all members of 
the Board. 
 
We resolve this matter by informal action, in accordance with 940 CMR 29.07(2)(a), 
after reviewing the original complaint, the Board’s response to the complaint, and the complaint 
filed with our office requesting further review.  We also reviewed the notice and a video 
recording of the Board meeting held on January 11.2  We find that the Board violated the Open 
Meeting Law in the way alleged.   
  
An Open Meeting Law complaint was filed with the Board on January 4 and alleged that 
three out of the five members of the Board – Chair Bruce Maki, Kevin Robinson, and Ellen 
Sordillo – violated the Open Meeting Law by deliberating outside of a meeting and failing to 
follow the Town’s by-laws with respect to the approval of a solar array project.   
 
The Open Meeting Law requires that within 14 days of receipt of a complaint, the public 
body review the complaint’s allegations; take remedial action, if appropriate; and send to the

Attorney General a copy of the complaint and a description of any remedial action taken.  G.L.  
c. 30A, § 23(b); 940 CMR 29.05(5).  A public body must meet to review the complaint and 
formulate a response, or meet to delegate that authority, and respond to the complaint within 14 
business days.  See G.L. c. 30A, § 23(b); OML 2017-69; OML 2012-90.3  Upon the filing of 
an Open Meeting Law complaint with a public body, the chair “shall disseminate copies of the 
complaint to the members of the public body.”  940 CMR 29.05(3).  The purpose of this 
requirement is to make sure all members of the public body are aware of the allegations in the 
complaint.  See OML 2016-88.  Although the Board briefly reviewed the complaint during its 
January 11 meeting and authorized Town legal counsel to respond to the complaint, two Board 
members abstained from the vote to forward the complaint to legal counsel for response because 
they were unfamiliar with the complaint and in fact only knew about the complaint because it 
was listed on the notice.  The Board admits that Chair Maki did not disseminate the complaint to 
all Board members.  Rather, the Chair only disseminated the complaint to the members who 
were specifically named in the complaint.   
 
For the reasons stated above, we find that the Board violated the Open Meeting Law by 
failing to disseminate an Open Meeting Law complaint to all members of the Board.  We order 
immediate and future compliance with the law’s requirements, and we caution that similar future 
violations could be considered evidence of intent to violate the law.   
 
We now consider the complaint addressed by this determination to be resolved.  This 
determination does not address any other complaints that may be pending with our office or the 
Board.  Please feel free to contact our office at (617) 963-2540 if you have any questions 
regarding this letter.
