 RE: Open Meeting Law Complaint 
 
Dear Attorney Braun: 
 
This office received a complaint from Patrick Higgins on November 23, 2023,
1 alleging 
that the Somerset Zoning Board of Appeals (the “Board”) violated the Open Meeting Law, G.L. 
c. 30A, §§ 18-25.  The complaint was originally filed with the Board on or about October 26, and 
you responded on behalf of the Board by letter dated November 22.   
 
The complaint alleges that the Board posted a notice for its October 26 meeting that 
indicated the Board would discuss two identified Open Meeting Law complaints and included 
the language “and referral to Town Counsel,” which the complainant asserts is improper, since 
the Board must first review the complaint during a meeting before it may refer the complaint to 
legal counsel for a response.  As we have explained to the complainant on numerous occasions, a 
public body may choose to refer a complaint to legal counsel during a meeting, rather than 
discuss its substance.  See, e.g., OML 2023-59; OML 2021-120; OML 2019-40; OML 2017-149; 
OML 2017-148.2  Here, the meeting notice could have stated only that the Board would review 
the identified complaints; the Board opted to provide additional notice to the public and greater 
transparency by also indicating on the notice the likely outcome that the Board would vote to 
refer the complaints to legal counsel for a response.  See OML 2019-107 (explaining that the 
Open Meeting Law does not generally require meeting notices to indicate that a vote will be 
taken on a topic included on the notice, although including the additional detail provides greater 
clarity to the public).  We commend the Board for posting a meeting notice that included more 
specificity and clarity than the Law requires, and advise the complainant not to file complaints 
on this well-settled issue in the future.

The complaint also alleges that two amended notices for the October 26 meeting 
continued to include the posting notation “James Pimental, Clerk 10/24/23” despite that the 
amended notices were posted on October 26.  We find no error, where the notices also indicated 
the date and time of the amendments.  See 940 CMR 29.03(1)(d). 
 
For the reasons stated above, we find that the Board did not violate the Open Meeting 
Law as alleged. We now consider the complaint addressed by this determination to be resolved.  
This determination does not address any other complaints that may be pending with our office or 
the Board.  Please feel free to contact the Division at (617) 963-2540 if you have any questions.
