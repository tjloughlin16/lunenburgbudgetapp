Dear Attorney Miyares: 
This office received a complaint from Michael Goodwin, dated May 28, 2014, alleging 
that the Hopkinton Board of Selectmen (the "Board') violated the Open Meeting Law, G.L. c. 
30A, §§ 18-25. The complaint alleges that a quorum of the Board improperly deliberated behind 
closed doors during a portion of the Board's February 27, 2014 meeting. The complaint was 
originally filed with the Board on March 21, 2014 and counsel responded on behalf of the Board 
by letter dated April 4, 2014.2 
Following our review, we find that the Board did not violate the Open Meeting Law. In 
reaching this determination, we reviewed the March 21, 2014 complaint filed with the Board; the 
Board's April 4, 2014 response; and the May 28, 2014 complaint filed with our office. 
Additionally, we reviewed the notice, minutes and a video recording of the Board's February 27, 
2014 meeting. 
FACTS 
We find the facts as follows. The Board is a five-member public body. On February 21, 
2014, the Board posted notice for a 5:30 PM meeting on February 27, 2014. The agenda listed 
"Police Chief Interviews" as one topic for discussion. During the Board's February 27, 2014 
meeting, the Board interviewed the four finalists for the Town's Police Chief position. In

DISCUSSION 
The Open Meeting Law defines "deliberation" as "an oral or written communication 
through any medium, including electronic mail, between or among a quorum of a public body on 
any public business within its jurisdiction." G.L. c. 30A, § 18. A "quorum is a "simple majority 
of the members of the public body, unless otherwise provided in a general or special law, 
executive order or other authorizing provision." G.L. c. 30A, § 18. Because the Board here 
consists of five members, three constitutes a quorum. All deliberation must occur during a 
noticed meeting. G.L. c. 30A, §§ 18, 20(b). 
The complaint alleges that during the Board's brief recess during its February 27, 2014 
meeting, "a quorum of members went into a small office.. .and intentionally closed the door." 
The Board acknowledges that one Board member entered the Town Manager's Office with the 
Town Manager and the Assessment Center Consultant and at one point another Board member 
knocked on the closed door and spoke briefly with the others inside the office. Even assuming 
that the second member participated in the discussion, it did not involve a quorum of the Board. 
See G.L. c. 30A, § 18. We therefore find no violation of the Open Meeting Law. 
CONCLUSION 
For the reasons stated above, we find that the Board did not violate the Open Meeting 
Law. We now consider the complaint addressed by this determination to be resolved. This 
determination does not address any other complaints that may be pending with our office or the 
Board. Please feel free to contact the Division at (617) 963-2540 if you have any questions.
