 RE: Open Meeting Law Complaint 
 
Dear Mr. Watson: 
This office received a complaint from Steven Baker on September 20, 2021, alleging that 
the Groveland Board of Selectmen (the “Board”) violated the Open Meeting Law, G.L. c. 30A, 
§§ 18-25.1  The complaint was originally filed with the Board on August 30, and you responded, 
on behalf of the Board, by letter dated September 16.  The complaint alleges that the Board 
failed to properly post notice of the meeting that was held on August 30.    
 
Following our review, we find that the Board violated the Open Meeting Law in the way 
alleged.  In reaching this determination, we reviewed the original complaint, the Board’s 
response to the complaint, and the complaint filed with our office requesting further review.  We 
also reviewed the notice and open session minutes of the Board’s August 30 meeting, as well as 
a video recording of that meeting.2  In addition, we communicated with the complainant on 
October 1 and October 6.   
 
FACTS 
 
We find the facts as follows.  The official method of posting notice for the Town of 
Groveland is the town website, http://www.grovelandma.com/.

On Thursday, August 26 at 11:45 A.M., the Board posted notice with the Town Clerk for 
a meeting to be held on Monday, August 30 at 5:30 P.M.  Because the town website was 
inaccessible at that time, the notice was posted inside Town Hall on a bulletin board outside of 
the Town Clerk’s Office and to the front door of Town Hall.  The website was restored and 
became accessible on Thursday night.  On Saturday, August 28 at 8:49 A.M., the Board posted 
notice of the August 30 meeting to the Town website.    
 
The August 30 meeting was held as planned and the Board discussed the noticed topics, 
which included approving several warrants and special permit requests, and discussing the 
interview process for a Town Administrator, the COVID vaccination rate for the school district, 
historical designation for Washington Street, issues with the Bagnall School, implementation of a 
waste management contract, and designated hours for an Administrative Coordinator.   
  
DISCUSSION 
 
A public body must post notice of every meeting at least 48 hours in advance, not 
including Saturdays, Sundays, and legal holidays.  G.L. c. 30A, § 20(b).  The notice must be 
printed in a legible, easily understandable format and must contain the date, time and place of the 
meeting, as well as a listing of topics that the chair reasonably anticipates will be discussed.  
G.L. c. 30A, § 20(b).  The list of topics shall have “sufficient specificity to reasonably advise the 
public of the issues to be discussed at the meeting.”  940 CMR 29.03(l)(b).  We generally 
consider a topic to be sufficiently specific when a reasonable member of the public could read 
the topic and understand the anticipated nature of the public body’s discussion.  See OML 2015-
35.3   
 
The complaint alleges that notice for the Board meeting held on August 30 was not 
properly posted because it was not posted to the Town’s website.  For local public bodies, notice 
shall be filed with the municipal clerk and posted in a manner conspicuously visible to the public 
at all hours in or on the municipal building in which the clerk’s office is located.  G.L. c. 30A, § 
20(c).  A municipality may designate a website as an alternative notice posting location for all 
public bodies within the municipality.  940 CMR 29.03(2)(b).  The Board states that the official 
posting location of the Town “is the Clerk’s board within Town Hall and to post in a 24/7 visible 
location (Front door).”  However, in accordance with the Attorney General’s regulations, the 
Town notified our office on August 23, 2010, that it was adopting its website as the Town’s 
official notice posting method.  See 940 CRM 29.03(1)(c).  Although the Open Meeting Law and 
the Attorney General’s regulations do not prohibit a public body from posting meeting notices in 
multiple locations, a municipality may only have one official notice posting location and if it has 
adopted its website as its notice posting location, then that is the official location for purposes of 
the Open Meeting Law.  See 940 CMR 29.03(2)(b)(3) & (c); OML 2021-94; OML 2021-63; 
OML 2020-140.  
 
We understand that the Town website was inaccessible on Thursday, August 26, which is 
why the Board posted notice of the August 30 meeting to the front door of Town Hall.  However,

once a municipality has adopted its website as its official method of posting notice, it is the 
method that must be used to post meeting notices.  Other notice posting methods cannot replace 
web posting during a website outage.  Once the website became inaccessible, the municipal clerk 
or other individual responsible for posting notice to the website had six business hours to restore 
the website.  See 940 CMR 29.03(7).  Although the website was restored some time Thursday 
night, it was not restored in time for the Board to post notice 48 hours prior to its August 30 
meeting.  See G.L. c. 30A, § 20(b) (notice of every meeting must be posted at least 48 hours in 
advance, not including Saturdays, Sundays, and legal holidays).  We must look to the official 
notice posting location, here the website, to determine whether a public body has complied with 
the Law.  See OML 2021-17.  Because the Board did not post notice of its August 30 meeting 
until Saturday, August 28, we find that the Board violated the Open Meeting Law by failing to 
properly post notice 48 hours prior to a meeting.  See OML 2019-102.   
 
CONCLUSION 
 
For the reasons stated above, we find that the Board violated the Open Meeting Law by 
failing to properly post notice of its August 30 meeting.  We order the Board’s immediate and 
future compliance with the Open Meeting Law, and caution that future similar violations may be 
considered evidence of intent to violate the law. 
 
We now consider the complaint addressed by this determination to be resolved.  This 
determination does not address any other complaints that may be pending with our office or the 
Board.  Please feel free to contact our office at (617) 963-2540 if you have any questions 
regarding this letter.
