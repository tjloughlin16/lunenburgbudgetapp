This office received a complaint on February 3, 2011 from Peter Waasdorp (the 
"Complainant"), alleging a violation of the Open Meeting Law by the Falmouth Board of 
Selectmen (the "Board"). Specifically, the Complainant alleges that the Board failed to provide 
him with requested executive session minutes. The complaint was originally filed with the 
Board on January 4, 2011.1  We received your response on behalf of the Board in a letter dated 
January 14, 2011.2  
We appreciate the patience of all parties involved while we worked to resolve this 
complaint. Following our review, we find that, by failing to create and maintain minutes of 
executive sessions, the Board violated the Open Meeting Law, G.L. c. 39, §§ 23A-C.

2008; June 23, 2008; August 11, 2008; September 8, 2008; October 6, 2008; November 24,

DISCUSSION 
The Attorney General assumed responsibility for the interpretation and enforcement of 
the Open Meeting Law as it applies to local public bodies on July 1, 2010. Prior to that date, the 
district attorneys had responsibility for enforcement of the Open Meeting Law at the municipal 
level. 
The Complainant requested executive session minutes for meetings that were held in 
2008 and 2009, therefore we apply the previous version of the Open Meeting Law in reviewing 
this matter. The Open Meeting Law, as it existed prior to July 1, 2010, stated that a 
"governmental body shall maintain accurate records of its meetings, setting forth the date, time, 
place, members present or absent and action taken at each meeting, including executive 
sessions." G.L. c. 39, § 23B. The Board failed to meet this requirement for numerous meetings 
held prior to July 1, 2010 by not creating minutes from its executive sessions in a timely manner. 
However, we acknowledge that the Board has made significant efforts to re-create the missing 
executive session minutes requested by the Complainant. The Board also stated in its November 
18, 2011 letter to our office that "Nile town recognizes that the inability to prepare and approve

The current version of the Open Meeting Law, which was in effect at the time the 
Complainant requested the meeting minutes, states that a public body must respond to any 
request for executive session minutes within 10 days of receipt and must release any executive 
session minutes that no longer warrant continued non-disclosure. See G.L. c. 30A, § 22(g)(2). 
The Board also did not comply with this requirement, because certain minutes did not exist and 
others could not easily be located. Going forward, we advise the Board that its minutes should 
be maintained in a way that ensures it is able to locate and release all available executive session 
meeting minutes promptly. 
In addition, with regard to the executive session minutes currently being withheld by the 
Board, we remind the Board that the Open Meeting Law allows a public body to withhold 
executive session meeting minutes only as long as disclosure of such minutes would defeat the 
lawful purpose for the executive session. See G.L. c. 30A, § 22(f). Once the purpose for the 
executive session has been served, the public body is required to release those minutes. Id. 
Public bodies must therefore review their executive session minutes periodically to determine 
whether or not continued non-disclosure is warranted. See G.L. c. 30A, § 22(g)(1). Such 
determination must be announced at the Board's next open meeting following the review, and 
must be included in the meeting minutes. Id. 
CONCLUSION 
We find that the Board violated the Open Meeting Law, G.L. c. 39, §§ 23A-C, by failing 
to create and maintain minutes of its executive sessions. Because the Board took appropriate 
remedial action by recreating all the minutes that it could, and has committed itself to 
compliance going forward, we order no further relief.
