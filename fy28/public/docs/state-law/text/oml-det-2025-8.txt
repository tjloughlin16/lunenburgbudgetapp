This office received a complaint from Jude Ahern on September 13, 2024,
1 alleging that 
the Wellfleet Selectboard (the “Board”) and Finance Committee (the “Committee”) violated the 
Open Meeting Law, G.L. c. 30A, §§ 18-25. The complaint was originally filed with the Board 
and Committee on or about August 9, and you responded on behalf of both bodies by letter dated 
September 13. The complaint alleges that the bodies violated the Open Meeting Law during a 
joint meeting held on July 15 when they failed to allow for public comment even though the 
meeting notice listed public comment as a topic. 
 
Following our review, we find that the Board and the Committee did not violate the Open 
Meeting Law as alleged. In reaching this determination, we reviewed the Open Meeting Law 
complaint, the bodies’ response, the request for further review, the notice for the July 15 
meeting, and a portion of a video recording of the July 15 meeting.

A public body must post notice of every meeting at least 48 hours in advance, not 
incl
uding Saturdays, Sundays, and legal holidays. G.L. c. 30A, § 20(b). The notice must contain 
the date, time, and place of the meeting, and list the topics that the chair reasonably anticipates 
will be discussed. G.L. c. 30A, § 20(b). The Law does not prohibit a public body from removing, 
postponing, or declining to discuss a topic listed on a meeting notice. See OML 2022-109; OML 
2012-23.4 Moreover, while the Open Meeting Law requires that the meeting notice list all topics 
reasonably anticipated for discussion 48 hours in advance of the meeting, a public comment 
period is not itself a topic for the public body’s discussion and therefore does not need to be 
listed on the meeting notice. See G.L. c. 30A, § 20(b); OML 2022-62; OML 2022-45. 
Additionally, the Law does not require that a public body allow public participation at all, but 
rather gives the chair of the public body discretion to decide whether to permit public comment 
during a meeting. See G.L. c. 30A, § 20(g) (“No person shall address a meeting of a public body 
without permission of the chair, and all persons shall, at the request of the chair, be silent.”); 
OML 2022-237; OML 2017-189; OML 2014-23; OML 2012-78. We do, however, encourage 
public bodies to allow for as much public participation as time and circumstances permit. See 
OML 2024-222; OML 2015-12; OML 2014-129; OML 2012-59. 
 
The complaint alleges that th
 e Board and Committee violated the Open Meeting Law 
when they failed to allow for public comment during the July 15 meeting even though public 
comment had been listed on the meeting notice. Because the bodies were not required to permit 
public comment during the meeting and were not required to discuss all matters listed on the 
meeting notice, we find that the Board and the Committee did not violate the Open Meeting Law. 
See OML 2022-215; OML 2015-174; OML 2012-23.  
 
For the reasons stated above, we find that the Board and the Committee did not violate 
the Open Meeting Law as alleged. We now consider the complaint addressed by this 
determination to be resolved. This determination does not address any other complaints that may 
be pending with the Board, the Committee, or our office. Please feel free to contact our office at 
(617) 963-2540 if you have any questions regarding this letter.
