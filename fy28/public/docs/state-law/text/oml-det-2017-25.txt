RE: Open Meeting Law Complaint 
Dear Attorney Emerson: 
This office received a complaint from Joaquin Soares on November 30, alleging 
that the Stoughton School Committee (the "Committee") violated the Open Meeting Law, 
G.L. c. 30A, §§ 18-25.1 The complaint was originally filed with the Committee on or 
about October 21, and you responded, on behalf of the Committee, in a letter dated 
November 3. In his complaint, Mr. Soares alleges that the Committee failed to publish 
certain meeting minutes to its website.2 
After reviewing the complaint, the Committee's response, the request for further 
review filed with our office, and the November 7 meeting minutes, we resolve this 
complaint by informal action in accordance with 940 CMR 29.07(2)(a). We find that the 
Committee did not violate the Open Meeting Law. 
The Open Meeting Law requires public bodies to "create and maintain accurate 
minutes of all meetings, including executive sessions." G.L. c. 30A, § 22(a). These 
meeting minutes must be reviewed and approved in a timely manner. G.L. c. 3OA, § 22(c). 
Although the Open Meeting Law does not define "timely manner," we recommend that 
minutes of a meeting be approved at the next meeting, whenever possible. See OML 
2014-15; OML 2012-91.3 The Law does not require that minutes be published to a 
website, however.4 See G.L. c. 30A, § 22; OML 2016-17.

The Committee approved the July 26 meeting minutes on September 13, and 
approved the September 13 and September 27 meeting minutes on November 1. We find 
this to be an appropriate amount of time for approving minutes. See OML 2016-157; 
OML 2016-62. The minutes were then posted to the Committee's website. In the 
meantime, we note that anyone can request a copy of open session minutes from the 
Committee, which must be provided if they exist, whether in draft or final form, within 10 
days. G.L. c. 30A, § 22(c). As a member of the Committee, we encourage the 
complainant to work with his fellow Committee members to resolve any Open Meeting 
Law concerns before filing an Open Meeting Law complaint. 
For the reasons stated above, we find that the Committee did not violate the Open 
Meeting Law. We now consider the complaint addressed by this determination to be 
resolved. This determination does not address any other complaints that may be pending 
with our office or the Committee. Please feel free to contact our office at (617) 963-2540 
if you have any questions regarding this letter.
