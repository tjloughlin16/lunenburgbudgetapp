Dear Ms. Hazen: 
This office received a complaint from Timothy Toomey on November 30, 2015, 
alleging that the Rowley Board of Registrars (the Board) violated the Open Meeting Law, 
G.L. c, 30A, §§ 18-25, The complaint was originally filed with the Board on or about 
November 16, 2015, and you responded, on behalf of the Board, to the complaint by letter 
dated November 19, 2015. In his complaint, Mr. Toomey alleges that the Board did not 
properly post notice of its meeting held on November 13, 2015.' 
Following our review, we find that the Board did not violate the Open Meeting Law. 
In reaching a determination, we reviewed the original complaint, the Board's response to the 
complaint, and the complaint filed with our office requesting further review. We also 
reviewed the original and revised notices of the Board's November 13, 2015 meeting. 
Finally, we spoke with you by telephone on February 4, 2016. 
FACTS 
We find the facts as follows. The Board held a meeting on Friday, November 13, 
2015. The Board initially posted notice of this meeting on Monday, November 9, 2015, and

DISCUSSION 
The Open Meeting Law was enacted "to eliminate much of the secrecy surrounding 
deliberation and decisions on which public policy is based." Ghiglione v. School Committee 
of Southbridge. 376 Mass. 70, 72 (1978). The Open Meeting Law requires that public bodies 
post notice of each meeting at least 48 hours in advance, and include in that notice a "listing 
of topics that the chair reasonably anticipates will be discussed at the meeting." G.L. c. 30A, 
§ 20(b). 
The complaint alleges that the Board did not properly post notice of its Friday, 
November 13, 2015 meeting during which it held a public hearing. We find that the Board 
properly posted notice of this meeting at least 48 hours in advance of the meeting, on 
Monday, November 9, 2015, and subsequently revised the notice twice the following day, on 
November 10, 2015 at 11:35 AM and 4:01 PM. See G.L. c. 30A, § 20(b), (c); 940 CMR 
29.03. The subsequent revisions were made in an attempt to clarify the sole topic listed on the 
original notice, however the Board added an additional related topic to the final version, 
which was posted less than 48 hours before the meeting. Board counsel had advised the chair, 
just minutes before she posted it, to further clarify the notice by adding this related topic. 
Although the final version of the notice was posted fewer than 48 hours before the meeting, 
because the added topic was not anticipated by the chair until mere minutes before it was 
posted, we find no violation. See G.L. c. 30A, § 20(b); OML 2015-192.4 Additionally, the 
original notice and the two revised versions indicated the date and time that the Town Clerk 
posted them, in compliance with the Open Meeting Law. See 940 CMR 29,03(b). 
Accordingly, we find that the Board did not violate the Law. 
CONCLUSION 
For the reasons stated above, we find that the Board did not violate the Open Meeting 
Law. We now consider the complaint addressed by this determination to be resolved. This 
determination does not address any other complaints that may be pending with our office or
