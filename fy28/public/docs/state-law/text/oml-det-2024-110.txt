This office received a complaint from Susan Delmolino on December 12, 2023, alleging 
that the Hadley Finance Committee (the “Committee”) violated the Open Meeting Law, G.L. c. 
30A, §§ 18-25.  The complaint is dated October 18, 2023, and was sent by email on that date, 
although it is unclear whether the Committee or its Chair received the complaint until early 
January 2024, after our office inquired about the status of the Committee’s response.  The 
Committee responded to the complaint on January 26, 2024.  The complaint alleges that the 
Committee posted insufficiently specific notices for its October 2 and October 11, 2023, 
meetings.

Following our review, we find that the Board did not violate the Open Meeting Law.  In 
reaching this determination, we reviewed the complaint, the Committee’s response, and the 
Complainant’s request for review of the complaint.  We also reviewed the notices for the 
Committee’s October 2 and October 11 meetings as well as video recordings of both meetings.

The Open Meeting Law requires th at a public body post notice of each meeting “at least 
48 hours prior to such meeting, excluding Saturdays, Sundays and legal holidays” and that every 
notice include, among other things, “a listing of topics that the chair reasonably anticipates will 
be discussed at the meeting.”  G.L. c. 30A, § 20(b).  Public bodies must list topics for discussion 
with “sufficient specificity to reasonably advise the public of the issues to be discussed at the 
meeting.”  940 CMR 29.03(l)(b).  The Open Meeting Law does not require a public body 
to anticipate the course of deliberation or the outcome of a discussion, nor does the “specificity” 
requirement generally require a meeting notice to indicate that a vote will be taken on a listed 
topic.  See G.L. c. 30A, § 20(b); 940 CMR 29.03(l)(b); OML 2023-186; OML 2019-157; OML 
2017-186; OML 2014-58; OML 2012-75.4   A notice need not include every detail about an 
anticipated discussion, but the law requires that notice topics include “sufficient specificity” so 
that the public can decide whether to attend the meeting and observe the discussions regarding 
the specific topics anticipated for discussion.  See OML 2023-108; OML 2018-22; OML 2018-7.  
 
When reviewing a meeting notice for sufficiency, unless it is clearly insufficient on its 
fac
e, we do not review a meeting notice standing alone.  See OML 2015-109; OML 2014-155. 
Rather, we review what was actually discussed at the meeting to determine if the notice was 
sufficiently specific to make the public aware of the discussion topics.  We have explained that 
when a public body anticipates reviewing all town meeting warrant articles or an entire budget, it 
is not required to list all the warrant articles or all budgetary items on its meeting notice.  See 
OML 2017-185; OML 2012-83.  However, when a public body anticipates discussing particular 
Town Meeting warrant articles or budgetary items, it must specifically list them in its meeting 
notice.  See OML 2024-27; OML 2013-8; OML 2011-7. 
 
After review, we are satisfied that the Committee anticipated reviewing the full warrant 
during its October 2 meeting.  We acknowledge that the Committee’s discussion focused on the 
many warrant articles that included budgetary components, consistent with the Committee’s role, 
while it quickly reviewed or skipped over unrelated articles such as proposed zoning bylaw 
amendments.  The complaint is concerned specifically with the Committee’s discussion and vote 
to recommend that the Select Board revise Article 4 to reduce the amount to be approved at 
Town Meeting for architectural and design work for a new Department of Public Works building 
from $3 million to $225,000, to cover costs only through the schematic design phase.  Although 
that particular expenditure generated much discussion and resulted in a recommendation to 
revise the article, it was only one of several capital expenditures that were discussed, along with 
several other warrant articles with financial implications.  We find no indication that the Chair 
anticipated in advance that the Department of Public Works building expenditure in particular 
would generate extended discussion or recommended changes.  Where the Committee reviewed 
the warrant in full, we are satisfied that the notice for the Committee’s October 2 meeting was
