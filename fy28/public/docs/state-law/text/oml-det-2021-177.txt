 RE: Open Meeting Law Complaint 
Dear Attorney Hall: 
This office received a complaint from Edward Davison on July 12, 2021, alleging that the 
Belmont Select Board (the “Board”) violated the Open Meeting Law, G.L. c. 30A, §§ 18-25. The 
complaint was originally filed with the Board on April 8, and you responded on behalf of the 
Board by letter dated April 28.1 In his complaint, Mr. Davidson alleges that the Board failed to 
approve meeting minutes in a timely manner for meetings held on January 21 and February 8.2 
We resolve this complaint by informal action in accordance with 940 CMR 
29.07(2)(a), and find that the Board violated the Open Meeting Law by failing to timely approve 
minutes for the January 21 meeting. However, we find not violation with respect to the February 
8 meeting minutes. In reaching this determination, we reviewed the original complaint, the 
Board’s response to the complaint, and the complainant’s request for further review. We 
reviewed the Board’s February 8 executive session minutes, as well as open session minutes 
from January 21, January 22, January 25, January 27, February 1, February 8, February 11, 
February 22, March 1 and March 15. We also reviewed the Board’s website to confirm the dates 
of various Board meetings.

 As the Board met very frequently in late January, the Board had 30 days from the date of 
the January 21 meeting to approve minutes for that meeting. The Board first attempted to 
approve the minutes for the January 21 meeting at its March 1 meeting, which was the eighth 
subsequent meeting.4 In its response, the Board explains that the Board met ten times between 
January 21 and March 15 which created a high volume of meeting minutes, which the Board 
relied upon the Town Administrator to create. The responsibility for complying with the Open 
Meeting Law rests with public bodies and members of such bodies, not with individual public 
employees, such as administrative staff. See OML 2018-105. If staff resources are not available 
to create minutes for a public body to approve, then public body members must take on this 
responsibility to ensure compliance with the Law. See OML 2018-111. When it became apparent 
that the Board would meet ten times in a short period, the Board should have taken steps to 
ensure that the Town Administrator would have adequate assistance to complete the minutes in a 
timely manner, including having members of the Board assist in the creation of minutes if 
necessary. The Board first considered approving the minutes of the January 21 meeting at its 
March 1 meeting, which was the eighth subsequent meeting, and already 30 days after the 
January 21 meeting. The Board then delayed the final approval until the March 15 meeting, 47 
days after the January 21 meeting. Accordingly, we find that the Board violated the Open 
Meeting Law by failing to approve the January 21 meeting minutes in a timely manner. 
 On the other hand, we find that the Board had good cause to delay approval of the 
February 8 meeting minutes for five days beyond the 30-day period. Because the Board met 
three times in less than 30 days following its February 8 meeting, it had 30 days, or until March 
10, by which to approve the February 8 minutes. See 940 CMR 29.11(2); OML 2018-82. The 
Board argues that it had good cause to take an extra five days to approve both open and 
executive session minutes of the February 8 meeting. We agree. Previously, we have not found a 
violation of the Open Meeting Law where a public body failed to approve meeting minutes due 
to a significant obstacle, and the public body made a good faith effort to approve the meeting 
minutes within a reasonable period of time. See OML 2020-136; OML 2015-190 (finding no 
violation when the public body had repeatedly made good faith efforts to approve minutes 
through multiple rounds of edits in an effort to reach a consensus among its members); OML 
2015-75 (finding no violation when a Board member’s absence from meetings and several

 The Board explains that, at a March 1 meeting, which was the third meeting since the 
February 8 meeting, the Board reviewed both the open and executive session February 8 
minutes, but discovered some errors in the drafts. Both sets of draft minutes were corrected and 
approved at the Board’s meeting on March 15, which was thirty-five days after the February 8 
meeting. We find that the Board engaged in a good faith effort to approve both sets of February 8 
meeting minutes after finding errors in the initial drafts at the March 1 meeting. The errors in the 
draft meeting minutes created a significant obstacle which reasonably prevented the Board from 
approving both sets of February 8 meeting minutes at the March 15 meeting. The Board then 
made a good faith effort to correct the meeting minutes and approved them two weeks later, at 
the meeting on March 15, which was only a few days beyond the required 30 days. For these 
reasons, we find that the Board had good cause for the brief delay in its approval of the open and 
executive session minutes of the February 8 meeting. We therefore find that the Board did not 
violate the Open Meeting Law.  
 For the reasons stated above, we find that the Board violated the Open Meeting Law by 
failing to timely approve minutes for the January 21 meeting. We order the Board’s immediate 
and future compliance with the Open Meeting Law and caution the Board that a determination 
by our office of a similar violation in the future may be considered evidence of intent to violate 
the Open Meeting Law. 
 We now consider the complaint addressed by this determination to be resolved. This 
determination does not address any other complaints that may be pending with our office or the 
Board. Please feel free to contact the Division at (617) 963 - 2540 if you have any questions.
