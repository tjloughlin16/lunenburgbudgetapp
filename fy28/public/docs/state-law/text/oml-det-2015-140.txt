RE: Open Meeting Law Complaint 
Dear Attorney Randazzo: 
This office received a complaint from Ronald Beaty, dated July 31, alleging that the 
Dennis Board of Selectmen (the "Board") violated the Open Meeting Law, G.L. c. 30A, §§ 18-
25.1 The complaint was originally filed with the Board on July 6, and the Board responded by 
letter dated July 20. In his complaint, Mr. Beaty alleges that the Board posted an insufficiently 
specific meeting notice. 
Following our review, we resolve this complaint by informal action in accordance with 
940 CMR 29.07(2)(a), and find that the Board did not violate the Open Meeting Law. As we 
have noted in prior determinations resolving complaints filed by Mr. Beaty, a public body does 
not need to list "open session" in its meeting notice, unless an executive session is the only topic 
for discussion. See OML 2015-110; OML 2015-105.2 Here, the notice listed six topics besides 
the anticipated executive session, thus it was clear that an open session meeting would occur. 
The Board, therefore, did not violate the Open Meeting Law by failing to list open session as a 
distinct topic. 
The complaint also alleges that the Board failed to indicate what real property would be 
discussed during executive session, stating only that an executive session was anticipated "[t]o 
discuss the purchase, exchange, lease or value of real estate." When noticing an executive 
session pursuant to Purpose 6, a public body must identify the property to be discussed if doing 
so would not compromise the purpose for which the executive session was called. See OML 
2014-136; OML 2013-97. Here, the property at issue had not previously been discussed during 
an open session and identifying it on the notice would have compromised the Board's
