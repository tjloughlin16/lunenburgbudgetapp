This office received a complaint from Neil Gordon on March 28, 2025,
1 alleging that the 
Brookline Moderator’s Committee for Forms of Government (the “Committee”) violated the 
Open Meeting Law, G.L. c. 30A, §§ 18-25. The complaint was originally filed with the 
Committee on January 15, and, after an extension of time granted by our office, you responded 
on behalf of the Committee by letter dated March 5. We understand the complaint to allege that 
1) the Committee failed to create minutes for five meetings held in 2024;
2 2) the Committee 
failed to approve meeting minutes; and 3) draft minutes for four Committee meetings are 
insufficiently detailed.
3 
 
 
1 All dates are in 2025, unless otherwise stated. 
2 The complaint alleges that the Committee failed to “file” minutes for its meetings held on February 26, March 20, 
April 10, May 22, and June 12, 2024. The Committee appears to have construed this as an allegation that it failed to 
create minutes for these meetings and acknowledges that it did fail to create the  minutes. For clarification, we note 
that the Open Meeting Law does not require that public bodies publish their meeting minutes to a website or 
otherwise disseminate meeting minutes absent a request. See G.L. c. 30A, § 22; OML 2025-17; OML 2017-25; 
OML 2016-17. Nonetheless, the Attorney General encourages public bodies to post meeting minutes to a website as 
this furthers the Open Meeting Law’s goal of government transparency, and we commend the Committee for its 
commitment to posting its meeting minutes online. 
3 In the request for further review, the Complainant raises concerns about whether the Committee properly reviewed 
the complaint prior to a response being provided. We decline to review allegations raised for the first time in a 
request for further review and not in a complaint filed with the public body. See OML 2021-99; OML 2020-3; OML 
Declination 4-22-15 (Natick Economic Development Committee).

Following our review, we resolve this complaint by informal action, in accordance with 
940 CMR 29.07(2)(a).4 The Committee acknowledges, and we find, that the Committee violated 
the Open Meeting Law by failing to create minutes for its meetings held on February 26, March 
20, April 10, May 22, and June 12, 2024; and by failing to timely approve minutes for its 
meetings held on September 18, October 9, October 30, and November 18, 2024. The Committee 
represents that it now understands its obligation to create and maintain accurate minutes of all 
meetings, as well as the requirement to timely approve open session meeting minutes. See G.L. 
c. 30A, § 22(a) & (c); 940 CMR 29.11(2).5 
 
Finally, with respect to the allegation that the Committee created insufficiently detailed 
meetin
g minutes, because the minutes were in draft form at the time the complaint was filed, we 
find that the Committee did not violate the Open Meeting Law by creating insufficiently detailed 
minutes. See OML 2024-42; OML Declination 2-28-2024 (Dennis Community Preservation 
Committee); OML 2018-42 (“Under the Open Meeting Law, a public body is responsible for the 
sufficiency of final, approved minutes as it may revise draft minutes at any point prior to their 
approval to correct inaccuracies or add detail.”); OML 2014-13; OML 2014-7.
6 Although we 
find no violation here, we remind the Committee that when reviewing minutes for compliance 
with the Open Meeting Law, we look for substantial compliance with the accuracy requirement. 
See OML 2017-104; OML 2013-64. By substantial compliance, we mean that the minutes 
should contain enough detail and accuracy so that a member of the public who did not attend the 
meeting could read the minutes and have a clear understanding of what occurred. See OML 
2019-145; OML 2012-106. Although a transcript of the discussions is not required, the minutes 
must include a substantive summary of the discussion on each topic. See OML 2020-57; OML 
2020-18; OML 2019-167. 
 
For t
he reasons stated above, we find that the Committee violated the Open Meeting Law 
by failing to create and approve meeting minutes. We order the Committee’s immediate and 
future compliance with the Open Meeting Law and caution that similar future violations may be 
considered evidence of an intent to violate the Law. We understand that since the filing of the 
complaint, the Committee has created and approved the meeting minutes at issue; therefore, we 
do not order further remedial action.
