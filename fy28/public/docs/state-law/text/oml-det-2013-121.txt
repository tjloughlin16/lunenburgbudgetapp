RE: Open Meeting Law Complaint 
Dear Attorney Frank: 
This office received a complaint from Patrick Fliggins, dated March 29, 2013, alleging 
that the Swansea Planning Board (the "Board") violated the Open Meeting Faw, G.F. c. 30A, §§ 
18-25. Mr. Higgins' complaint alleges that the Board failed to approve minutes for its April 2, 
2012 meeting in a timely manner. The complaint was filed with the Board on or about March 
19, 2013. The Board responded by letter dated March 26, 2013. 
Following our review, we find that the Board violated the Open Meeting Faw by failing 
to approve the minutes for its April 2, 2012 meeting in a timely manner. We acknowledge that 
the delay was due, in part, to the Board's uncertainy as to how it could approve the meeting 
minutes after the composition of the Board changed. 
In reaching this determination, we reviewed the March 19, 2013 complaint filed with the 
Board; the Board's March 26, 2013 response; the March 29, 2013 complaint filed with our 
office; and the notices and minutes from the Board's March 18, 2013 and April 1, 2013 
meetings. We also spoke with you by telephone on July 30, 2013.1 
FACTS 
Fhe Board is a five member public body. On April 2, 2012, the Board held a meeting, 
which three of the five Board members attended. Shortly thereafter, the Town held its annual 
elections, and one of the three Board members who had attended the April 2, 2012 meeting did 
not run for reelection. The two remaining Board members who had attended the April 2, 2013 
meeting were unsure whether they alone could vote to approve the minutes of that meeting at the

next duly called meeting. The Board ultimately postponed approving the April 2, 2012 minutes, 
pending referral to Town Counsel. 
In the Summer or Fall of 2012, the Town Counsel suggested that the two Board members 
who attended the April 2, 2012 meeting approve the minutes, and the two absent members who 
were on the Board at that time listen to the tape recordings and endorse that the minutes 
accurately reflected the deliberations and votes taken at the April 2, 2012 meeting. 
In late March 2013, after receiving the complaint, the Board informed Town Counsel that 
it could not locate the recording of the April 2, 2012 meeting. On April 1, 2013, the Board 
moved to approve the April 2, 2012 minutes. The two members who were not present at the 
April 2, 2012 meeting abstained, and the Board invoked the "rule of necessity" to have the two 
remaining members approve the minutes. The fifth member, who had been elected after the 
April 2, 2012 meeting, did not attend the April 1, 2013 meeting. 
DISCUSSION 
The Open Meeting Law requires that the "[mjinutes of all open sessions shall be created 
and approved in a timely manner." G.L. c. 30A, § 22(c). The Open Meeting Law does not 
define "timely manner," but we recommend that minutes be approved at the next meeting, 
whenever possible. See OML 2012-91. The Board approved the minutes from the April 2, 
2012 meeting almost a year later, even though the Board generally meets monthly. We therefore 
find that the Board did not comply with the Law's requirements regarding the timely creation 
and approval of minutes. 
We understand that the Board was uncertain about how to approve the minutes for the 
April 2, 2012 meeting, given the change in Board membership. However, this does not excuse 
the Board from its obligation under the Open Meeting Law to create and approve minutes of its 
meetings in a timely manner. G.L. c. 30A, § 22(c). The manner in which the Board ultimately 
approved the meeting minutes was appropriate, however approval should have occurred more 
promptly. In the future, we strongly encourage the Board to contact the Attorney General's 
Division of Open Government if it has questions about compliance with the Open Meeting Law. 
CONCLUSION 
We find that the Board violated the Open Meeting Law by failing to approve the minutes 
for its April 2, 2012 meeting in a timely maimer. We order immediate and future compliance 
with the Open Meeting Law, and caution that future similar conduct may be considered evidence 
of an intentional violation of the Open Meeting Law.
