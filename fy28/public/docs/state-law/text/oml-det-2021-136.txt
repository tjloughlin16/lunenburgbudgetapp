 RE: Open Meeting Law Complaint 
Dear Attorney Dupere: 
This office received a complaint from Jeffrey Gosselin on April 8, 2021, alleging that the 
Westfield School Committee (the “Committee”) violated the Open Meeting Law, G.L. c. 30A, 
§§ 18-25. The complaint was originally filed with the Committee on March 4, and you 
responded on behalf of the Committee by letter dated March 18.1 In his complaint, Mr. Gosselin 
alleges that, at a February 22 meeting, the Committee discussed and voted on a topic which was 
not included on the meeting notice.2 
We resolve this matter by informal action, in accordance with 940 CMR 29.07(2)(a), 
and find that the Committee did not violate the Open Meeting Law. In reaching this 
determination, we reviewed the original complaint, the Committee’s response to the complaint, 
and the complainant’s request for further review. We also reviewed the Committee’s notice for 
the February 22 open session meeting.  
The Open Meeting Law requires public bodies to post notice no later than 48 hours in 
advance of any meeting, excluding Saturdays, Sundays and legal holidays, and to include on the 
meeting notice “a listing of topics that the chair reasonably anticipates will be discussed at the

Even if true, this allegation would not constitute a violation of G.L. c. 30A, §§  18-25. The Open Meeting Law does 
not require that a public body allow public participation during its meetings, but rather provides that “[n]o person 
shall address a meeting of a public body without permission of the chair, and all persons shall, at the request of the 
chair, be silent.” G.L. c. 30A § 20(f). The Open Meeting Law, therefore, permits the chair of a public body to decide 
whether to allow for public participation, and if so, to set limitations on participation. See OML 2017-189; OML 
2014-23; OML 2012-23. We do, however, encourage public bodies to allow for as much public participation as time 
and circumstances permit. See OML 2015-12; OML 2014-129. Accordingly, we decline to review this allegation.

meeting, although the Attorney General strongly recommends that public bodies postpone 
discussion and action on unanticipated topics that are controversial or may be of particular 
interest to the public if the topic can wait until a subsequent meeting. See OML 2020- 172; OML 
2019-131; OML 2018-119. 3 The Committee argues that on February 22, 2021, the Committee 
was entitled to discuss a topic that was not listed on the meeting notice, because that topic was 
not anticipated by the chair 48 hours in advance of the meeting. We agree.  
At approximately 1:20 p.m. on Thursday, February 18, the Committee posted a meeting 
notice for a meeting to be held on Monday, February 22 at 7:00 p.m. The meeting notice did not 
include any mention of stipends for Committee members. The Committee explains in its 
response that, over the weekend of February 20 and 21, City Councilor William Onyski called 
Committee Vice Chair Timothy O’Connor requesting that the Committee vote regarding a 
stipend for Committee members, since the City Council’s Legislative and Ordinance Committee 
would soon be meeting to discuss the stipend issue for Committee members. The Committee 
discussed the stipend issue near the end of its February 22 meeting.  
As the Vice Chair was only informed about the stipend issue over the weekend, he was 
not aware of the topic 48 hours in advance of the meeting, “excluding Saturdays, Sundays and 
legal holidays.” Likewise, since the Chair was told about the stipend issue after the Vice Chair 
first learned of it, the Chair did not anticipate that the stipend issue would be discussed at the 
February 22 meeting until less than 48 hours in advance of the meeting, excluding Saturdays, 
Sundays and legal holidays.   
In the absence of any evidence to the contrary, we credit the Committee’s account that 
the Chair did not anticipate a discussion of the stipend issue 48 hours prior to the February 22 
meeting. See OML 2019-26; OML 2018-26; OML 2017-2. Therefore, we find that the 
Committee did not violate the Open Meeting Law when it discussed an unanticipated topic – the 
stipend issue – during the February 22 meeting. Although we do not find a violation of the Law, 
in the future, we strongly encourage the Committee to postpone discussion about topics that are 
controversial or may be of particular interest to members of the public if those topics were not 
listed on the meeting notice. See OML 2019-145. 
 For the reasons stated above, we find that the Committee did not violate the Open 
Meeting Law. We now consider the complaint addressed by this determination to be resolved. 
This determination does not address any other complaints that may be pending with our office or 
the Committee. Please feel free to contact the Division at (617) 963 - 2540 if you have any 
questions.
