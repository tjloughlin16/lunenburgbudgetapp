This office received a complaint from James Conley on September 24, 2022, alleging that 
the Kingston Board of Selectmen (the “Board”) violated the Open Meeting Law, G.L. c. 30A, 
§§ 18-25.  The complaint was originally filed with the Board on or around August 24, and the 
Board responded on September 23.
1  The complaint alleges that the Board voted on a matter 
during an August 23 meeting despite not specifying on the meeting notice that the Board would 
take action on the matter. 
 
Following our review, we find that the Board did not violate the Open Meeting Law.  In 
reaching this determination we reviewed the original complaint, the response to the complaint, 
and the Complainant’s request for further review.  We also reviewed the notice and minutes for 
the Board’s August 23 meeting. 
 
The complaint alleges that the Board violated the Open Meeting Law when it took 
official action by voting on a motion to change the speed limit on Main Street (Route 106) in the 
Town of Kingston, despite the meeting notice only specifying that a “discussion” would take 
place.  Specifically, the notice for the Board’s August 23 meeting included the topic “Discussion 
on Removing the Regulatory Speed Limit and Implement a Statutory Speed Limit on Main 
Street.”  During the meeting, the Board discussed the Board’s authority to reconsider a change to 
a 35-mile-per-hour speed limit for a portion of Main Street so as to reduce the speed limit to 25 
miles per hour, based on the section of road satisfying the “thickly settled” criteria.  The Board 
discussed enforcement considerations, read emails from residents on the matter, and allowed 
residents in attendance to speak.  Board members then shared their views on the matter before 
voting 2-3-0 against rescinding the 35-mile-per-hour speed limit.

The Open Meeting Law was enacted “to eliminate much of the secrecy surrounding 
delibe
rations and decisions on which public policy is based.”  Ghiglione v. School Committee of 
Southbridge, 376 Mass. 70, 72 (1978).  To that end, a public body must post notice of every 
meeting at least 48 hours in advance, not including Saturdays, Sundays, and legal holidays. G.L. 
c. 30A, § 20(b).  Notices must include “a listing of topics that the chair reasonably anticipates 
will be discussed at the meeting.”  Id.  The listing of topics shall have sufficient specificity to 
reasonably advise the public of the issues to be discussed at the meeting.  940 CMR 29.03(1)(b).  
We generally consider a topic to be sufficiently specific when a reasonable member of the public 
could read the topic and understand the anticipated nature of the public body's discussion.  See 
OML 2018-7; OML 2015-35.2  Where a public body’s discussion naturally flows from its 
consideration of the posted agenda topic, the notice was sufficient to inform the public of the 
discussion that occurred.  See OML 2018-14; OML 2014-44; OML 2014-38. 
 
Here, the complaint does not allege that the topic itself was described with insufficient 
speci
ficity on the meeting notice; rather the complaint alleges that the notice was insufficient 
where it specified that a “discussion” would take place yet a vote was taken.  Although it is good 
practice for a notice to specify that a vote will be taken when a vote is indeed anticipated in 
advance, the Open Meeting Law does not require this.  See OML 2015-66.  It is reasonably 
foreseeable that a public body may take action and hold a vote following discussion of a topic 
listed on a meeting notice; the Open Meeting Law does not require that the notice separately 
indicate the possibility of a vote.  See OML 2021-153; 2019-36; 2015-66; OML 2012-75.  Here, 
the Board’s vote naturally flowed from its discussion on the listed topic.  See OML 2012-32; 
OML 2013-152.  Accordingly, the Board did not violate the Open Meeting Law when it voted on 
the motion to reduce the speed limit on Main Street. 
 
For t
he reasons stated above, we find that the Board did not violate the Open Meeting 
Law.  We now consider the complaint addressed by this determination to be resolved.  This 
determination does not address any other complaints that may be pending with our office or the 
Board.  Please feel free to contact the Division at (617) 963-2540 if you have any questions.
