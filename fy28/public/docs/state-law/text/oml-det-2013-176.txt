This office received two complaints from D. John McCarthy, dated May 9, 2013 and July 2, 
2013, respectively, alleging that the Wilbraham Board of Selectmen (the "Board") violated the 
Open Meeting Law, G.L. c. 30A, §§ 18-25. The complaints were originally filed with the Board on 
or about April 12, 2013 and April 25, 2013, and then-Chair Robert Boilard responded, on behalf of 
the Board, to the original complaints by letters dated April 25, 2013 and May 24, 2013.1 In his May 
9, 2013 complaint, Mr. McCarthy alleges that the Board engaged in improper deliberation outside 
of a publicly posted meeting about a revision to a draft contract. In his July 2, 2013 complaint, Mr. 
McCarthy alleges that the Board did not comply with the Open Meeting Law in responding to his 
April 12, 2013 complaint. 
We reviewed the April 12, 2013 and April 25, 2013 complaints; your responses to the 
complaints on behalf of the Board, dated April 25, 2013 and May 24, 2013; and the May 9, 2013 
and July 2, 2013 complaints filed with our office requesting further review. We reviewed the 
executive sessions minutes of the Board's meetings held on May 7, 2012; December 17, 2012; 
January 7, 2013; February 19, 2013; and March 20, 2013; as well as the notice and open and 
executive session minutes from the Board's April 29, 2013 meeting. Finally, we spoke by 
telephone with Mr. Boilard, who was Chair of the Board at the time of these complaints, with 
current Chair James Thompson, and with Attorneys Michael Hassett2 and James Toomey. 
Following our review, while we do not find any evidence that the Board engaged in 
improper deliberation, we find that the Board violated the Open Meeting Law by failing to comply 
with certain requisite procedures before responding to Mr. McCarthy's April 12, 2013 Open 
Meeting Law complaint.

FACTS 
Based upon our review of the material listed above, the facts are as follows. For almost a 
year, the Board and the Hamden-Wilbraham Regional School Committee (the "Committee") were 
engaged in negotiating a contract to resolve the status of the Memorial School property that is 
owned by the Town and leased to the School District. According to the contract, the Committee 
agreed that the lease would terminate and the Memorial School property would revert back to the 
Town. Since the beginning of the negotiations, the Board expressed to Attorney Hassett its 
intention that the net proceeds from the lease would be applied to the infrastmctural needs of the 
Town, rather than the school district. 
On April 4, 2013, Attorney Toomey sent a draft of the contract to Attorney Hassett before 
presenting the draft to the Committee at its meeting that evening. This draft included language, as 
set forth in paragraph four, that the net proceeds from the lease would be specifically applied to the 
infrastmctural needs of the "Town of Wilbraham Schools." Between April 4, 2013 and April 9, 
2013, when the next Committee meeting was scheduled. Attorney Flassett indicated to Attorney 
Toomey that the word, "Schools" in paragraph four was unacceptable to the Board. Accordingly, 
Attorney Toomey omitted "Schools" from the draft, as presented to the Committee during its 
meeting on April 9, 2013. 
In response to this change to the draft contract, Mr. McCarthy, a member of the Committee, 
filed an Open Meeting Law complaint with the Board on April 12, 2013, alleging that the Board 
made revisions to the contract outside of a meeting. Even though Mr. Boilard listed this complaint 
as a topic for discussion for the Board's April 29, 2013 meeting, he individually responded to the 
complaint prior to the meeting on April 25, 2013. As a result, the Board did not discuss the 
complaint at the April 29, 2013 meeting because, Mr. Boilard asserted, a discussion between the 
Board "had simply not taken place.. .the response needs no more than a statement that the alleged 
discussion had not taken place." Accordingly, Mr. Boilard assumed that he, in his capacity as the 
chair, could respond to the complaint before discussing it with the rest of the Board. 
DISCUSSION 
The Open Meeting Law requires that all meetings of a public body be properly noticed and 
open to members of the public, unless an executive session is convened. See G.L. c. 30A, §§ 20(a)-
(b), 21. The Law's purpose is "to eliminate much of the secrecy surrounding deliberations and 
decisions on which public policy is based." Ghiglione v. School Committee of Southbridge. 376 
Mass. 70, 72 (1978). A "meeting" is defined, in relevant part, as "a deliberation by a public body 
with respect to any matter within the body's jurisdiction." G.L. c. 30A, § 18. The law defines 
"deliberation," in relevant part, as "an oral or written communication through any medium, 
including electronic mail, between or among a quorum of a public body on any public business 
within its jurisdiction." Id. "Quorum" is defined as "a simple majority of the members of the 
public body, unless otherwise provided in a general or special law, executive order or other 
authorizing provision." Id 
Because this is a three-member Board, two members constitute a quorum. We do not find 
evidence to support the assertion that a quorum of the Board improperly deliberated about the 
revision to the draft contract. Between April 4, 2013 and April 9, 2013, Attorneys Hassett and

Toomey continued to negotiate about the language of the draft contract. Attorney Hassett alone 
indicated to Attorney Toomey that the Board would not agree to the word, "Schools" in paragraph 
four, based on its longstanding position that the Town should retain the lease proceeds, rather than 
the school district. Accordingly, we find that the Board did not violate the Open Meeting Law. 
However, the Board violated the law by not reviewing the April 12, 2013 complaint and 
meeting to issue or authorize a response as required by 940 CMR 29.05(5) and G.L. c. 30A, § 23(b). 
See also OML 2013-173; OML 2013-151.3 The Open Meeting Law requires that within 14 
business days after receiving an Open Meeting Law complaint, a public body shall review the 
complaint's allegations; take remedial action, if appropriate; and send to the Attorney General a 
copy of the complaint and a description of any remedial action taken. G.L. c. 30A, § 23(b); 940 
CMR 29.05(5). The public body shall simultaneously notify the complainant that it has sent such 
materials to the Attorney General and shall provide the complainant with a copy of the description 
of any remedial action taken. 940 CMR 29.05(5). Mr. Bollard individually responded to the 
complaint by letter, prior to any review by the Board, and failed to provide our office with a copy of 
this response. We therefore find that the Board did not comply with the requirements of G.L. c. 
3OA, § 23(b) and 940 CMR 29.05(5). 
For the reasons stated above, while we do not find any evidence that the Board engaged in 
an improper deliberation, we find that the Board violated the Open Meeting Law by failing to 
comply with certain requisite procedures before responding to Mr. McCarthy's April 12, 2013 Open 
Meeting Law complaint. We therefore order immediate and future compliance with the Open 
Meeting Law, and caution that future similar conduct may be considered evidence of intent to 
violate the Open Meeting Law. 
We now consider the complaint addressed by this determination to be resolved. This 
determination does not address any other complaints which may be pending with our office or the 
Board. Please contact our office at (617) 963-2540 if you have any questions regarding this letter.
