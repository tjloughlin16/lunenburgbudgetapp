RE: Open Meeting Law Complaint 
Dear Attorney Troy; 
This office received a complaint filed by Richard Conron, dated September 19, alleging 
that the Bourne Board of Selectmen (the "Board") violated the Open Meeting Law, G.L. c. 30A, 
§§ 18-25.1 Specifically, the complaint alleges that the Board discussed and voted on an 
anticipated topic that was not noticed with sufficient detail prior to its July 22 meeting. The 
complaint was filed with the Board on August 5.2 The Board responded through counsel by 
letter dated September 12. 
Following our review, we find that the Board did not violate the Open Meeting Law. In 
reaching this determination, we reviewed the complaint filed with the Board, the Board's 
response, and the complaint filed with our office. We also reviewed the notice for and minutes 
of the Board's July 22 meeting. Finally, we spoke with Mr. Conron by telephone on October 2. 
FACTS 
We find the facts as follows. The Board posted notice for its July 22 meeting on July 18.

The notice included the following topic under the category of Selectmen's Business: "Discussion 
and Disposition of non-documented unaccompanied children at Camp Edwards Cape Cod." 
During the Board's July 22 meeting, the Board engaged in significant discussion 
regarding a proposal by the Governor of the Commonwealth of Massachusetts to house 
undocumented children at a facility at Camp Edwards. Following the discussion and public 
comment, the Board voted to "oppose the settlement of the children on Joint Base Cape Cod" 
and to draft a letter to the Governor reflecting the Board's position. 
DISCUSSION 
The Open Meeting Law requires that public bodies post notice of every meeting at least 
48 hours prior to such meeting, excluding Saturdays, Sundays and legal holidays, and include in 
the notice "the date, time, place of such meeting and a listing of topics that the chair reasonably 
anticipates will be discussing at the meeting." G.L. c. 30A, § 20(b). Public bodies are required 
to list topics in a meeting notice with "sufficient specificity to reasonably advise the public of the 
issues to be discussed at the meeting." 940 CMR 29.03(1 )(b). We generally consider a topic to 
include sufficient specificity when a reasonable member of the public could read the topic and 
understand the anticipated nature of the public body's discussion. See OML 2012-10.4 
The notice for the Board's July 22 meeting listed a topic as "Discussion and Disposition 
of non-documented unaccompanied children at Camp Edwards Cape Cod." During the meeting, 
the Board proceeded to discuss exactly that topic and voted to take a position. The law does not 
require a public body to anticipate the course of deliberation or the outcome of a discussion, only 
that it identify the discussion topic with sufficient specificity to reasonably advise the public of 
the issues to be discussed at the meeting. See G.L. c. 30A, § 20(b); 940 CMR 29.03(l)(b); OML 
2014-58; OML 2014-44; OML 2014-38. Here, the Board clearly met that obligation. It is 
reasonably inferable that a public body may take action on any topic listed in a meeting notice. 
See OML 2013-134; OML 2012-75. Therefore, the Board was not required to inform the public 
that it could take a vote or other action with respect to the topic at issue. 
CONCLUSION 
For the reasons stated above, we find that the Board did not violate the Open Meeting 
Law. With the issuance of this determination, we now consider this matter closed. Please 
contact our office at (617) 963-2540 if you have any questions.
