This office received a complaint from George Harris on June 23 alleging that the 
Wayland Audit Committee (the "Committee") violated the Open Meeting Law, G.L. c. 30A, §§ 
18-25.' The complaint was originally filed with the Committee on April 5; and you responded, 
on behalf of the Committee, on April 27.2 The complaint alleges that the Committee failed to 
timely approve minutes from Committee meetings held between August 2013 and November 
2016, and failed to provide those minutes upon request. 
Following our review, we find that the Committee violated the Open Meeting Law by not 
approving certain meeting minutes in a timely manner and by failing to timely respond to a 
request for meeting minutes. In reaching this determination, we reviewed the original complaint, 
the Committee's response to the complaint, and the complaint filed with our office requesting 
further review. We also reviewed open session minutes from the Committee's June 23, 2014; 
December 15, 2014; March 10, 2015; April 19; and July 17 meetings. In addition, we reviewed 
a video recording of the April 19 meeting.3 Finally, we spoke with Mr. Hams by telephone on 
August 15, and spoke with you by telephone on August 18.4

FACTS 
We find the facts as follows. The Committee approved the minutes from its April 28, 
2014 meeting at a June 23, 2014 meeting. During a December 15, 2014 meeting, the Committee 
approved minutes from its November 10, 2014 meeting. During a March 10, 2015 meeting, the 
Committee approved minutes from its December 1, 2014 and December 15, 2014 meetings, as 
well as executive session minutes from a November 10, 2014 meeting. 
On March 21, the complainant requested from the Committee copies of all approved 
minutes of meetings "from April 1, 2014 to the present." The Committee did not respond to this 
request. On April 5, the complainant filed an Open Meeting Law complaint in which he 
identified eighteen sets of meeting minutes that the Committee failed to create and approve in a 
timely manner. Specifically, those minutes were from Committee meetings held on August 26, 
2013; April 14, 2014, April 28, 2014, September 8, 2014, September 29, 2014, October 27, 
2014, November 10, 2014, December 1, 2014, December 15, 2014; February 3, 2015, March 10, 
2015, July 30, 2015, December 1, 2015, December 9, 2015; March 15, 2016, May 9, 2016, June 
30, 2016, and November 28, 2016. 
During its April 19 meeting, the Committee discussed the Open Meeting Law complaint 
filed by the complainant. Chair Claris Cullen stated that the August 26, 2013, April 14, 2014, 
and February 3, 2015 meetings were cancelled, and thus no minutes existed for those meetings. 
He believed that certain minutes identified by the complainant had been previously approved but 
could not ascertain which ones; he recommended the Committee approve all minutes identified 
in the complaint. The Committee then approved the minutes. Although the Committee 
presumably approved all the minutes identified in the Open Meeting Law complaint, the 
Committee did not publically identify those minutes, either at the April 19 meeting or in its 
minutes. 
During its July 17 meeting, the Committee again discussed the Open Meeting Law 
complaint filed by the complainant. Mr. Cullen stated that Committee meetings posted for 
August 26, 2013, April 14, 2014, October 27, 2014, and February 3, 2015 were cancelled. The 
Committee then specifically identified and approved minutes from Committee meetings held on 
April 28, 2014, September 8, 2014, September 29, 2014, October 27, 2014, November 10, 2014, 
December 1, 2014, December 15, 2014; March 10, 2015, July 30, 2015, December 1, 2015, 
December 9, 2015; March 15, 2016, May 9, 2016, June 30, 2016, and November 28, 2016. 
DISCUSSION 
The Open Meeting Law requires that a public body "create and maintain accurate 
minutes of all meetings, including executive sessions, setting forth the date, time and place, the 
members present or absent, a summary of the discussions on each subject, a list of documents 
and other exhibits used at the meeting, the decisions made and the actions taken at each meeting, 
including the record of all votes." G.L. c. 30A, § 22(a). The meeting minutes must be reviewed 
and approved in a timely manner. G.L. c. 30A, § 22(c). Although the Open Meeting Law does

not define "timely manner," we recommend that minutes of a meeting be approved at the next 
meeting, whenever possible. See OML 2014-15; OML 2012-91.5 
The complaint alleges that eighteen sets of minutes from Committee meetings held 
between August 2013 and November 2016 were not approved in a timely manner. We find that 
four meetings were cancelled and thus no minutes exist for the August 26, 2013, April 14, 2014, 
October 27, 2014, and February 3, 2015 Committee meetings. In addition, we find that the 
Committee approved the April 28, 2014, November 10, 2014, December 1, 2014, and December 
15, 2014 meeting minutes in a timely manner where the minutes were approved either at the next 
meeting or within the next two scheduled meetings. Moreover, the complainant concedes that 
these minutes were timely approved. 
We next determine whether the remaining ten sets of meeting minutes were approved in a 
timely manner. For bodies that meet regularly, such as weekly or bi-monthly, we have 
concluded that a delay of two to three months in approving minutes does not comply with the 
law. See OML 2016-62; OML 2013-31. Here, the Committee meets on an irregular basis, 
meeting ten times in 2014 but only four times in 2015 and five times in 2016. The Committee's 
first meeting in 2017 was on April 19, at which time it approved the ten sets of meeting minutes 
at issue. Of those ten, we find that two, the June 30, 2016 and November 28, 2016 minutes, were 
approved in a timely manner because they were approved at the Committee's next meeting or 
within the next two scheduled meetings, even though this was five to ten months later. See OML 
2013-173 (where a Committee meets only annually, the Committee may approve minutes at its 
next regularly scheduled meeting, even where that meeting is not for another year). 
However, we find that the Committee violated the Open Meeting law by not timely 
approving the minutes from meetings held on September 8, 2014, September 29, 2014, March 
10, 2015, July 30, 2015, December 1, 2015, December 9, 2015, March 15, 2016, and May 9, 
2016. Although these minutes were also approved on April 19, the Committee had met between 
three and eighteen times before April 19. Thus, where the Committee had several opportunities 
to approve these eight sets of minutes but failed to do so, we find that these minutes were not 
approved in a timely manner. However, we commend the Committee for taking remedial action 
in response to the complaint by promptly approving the backlog of minutes, and for posting draft 
minutes to the Town website. 
Finally, the complaint alleges that the Committee failed to make open session meeting 
minutes available upon request. The Open Meeting Law requires that "[t]he minutes of an open 
session, if they exist and whether approved or in draft form, shall be made available upon request 
by any person within 10 days." G.L. c. 30A, §§ 22(a), (c). Here, the complainant requested 
access to minutes on March 21, but minutes were not made available until on or around April 20 
when they were posted to the Town website. Accordingly, the Committee violated the Open 
Meeting Law by failing to timely respond to a request for minutes. The Open Meeting Law does 
not require that public bodies post their meeting minutes to a website. See OML 2013-103. We 
note, however, that the Committee has chosen to post its meeting minutes on its website and has 
notified the complainant that minutes from its meetings are available at that location.

CONCLUSION 
We find that the Committee violated the Open Meeting Law by not approving its 
September 8, 2014, September 29, 2014, March 10, 2015, July 30, 2015, December 1, 2015, 
December 9, 2015, March 15, 2016, and May 9, 2016 meeting minutes in a timely manner and 
by failing to timely respond to a request for meeting minutes. We find that the Committee did 
not violate the Open Meeting Law where it approved in a timely manner its April 28, 2014, 
November 10, 2014, December 1, 2014, December 15, 2014, June 30, 2016 and November 28, 
2016 meeting minutes. We order the Committee's immediate and future compliance with the 
law's requirements, and we caution that similar future violations could be considered evidence of 
intent to violate the law. 
We now consider the complaints addressed by this determination to be resolved. This 
determination does not address any other complaints that may be pending with the Committee or 
with our office. Please feel free to contact our office at (617) 963-2540 if you have any 
questions regarding this letter.
