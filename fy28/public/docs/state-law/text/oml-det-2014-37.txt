RE: Open Meeting Law Complaint 
Dear Attorney McCusker: 
This office received a complaint from Rodney Grace, dated February 26, 2014, 
alleging that the Westwood Zoning Board of Appeals (the Board) violated the Open Meeting 
Law, G.L. c. 30A, §§ 18-25. The complaint was originally filed with the Board on or about 
January 6, 2014, and you responded, on behalf of the Board, to the original complaint by 
letter dated January 22, 2014. In his complaint, Mr. Grace alleges that based on a Board 
decision issued on December 18, 2013, the Board included a condition to a variance that was 
neither listed as a topic on the meeting notice nor discussed during its November 20, 2013 
meeting. 
We reviewed the January 6, 2014 complaint filed with the Board; your January 22, 
2014 response, on behalf of the Board; and the February 26, 2014 complaint filed with our 
office, requesting further review. We also reviewed the notice and a transcript from the 
Board's November 20, 2013 meeting. 
Following our review, we find that the Board did not violate the Open Meeting Law. 
FACTS 
We find the facts to be as follows. On November 20, 2013, the Board convened a 
meeting in open session, which consisted, in part, of a public hearing, to discuss an 
application for a variance from Section 6.5.1 of the Zoning Bylaws. The meeting notice, 
posted on November 4, 2013, explained that the Board would hold a public hearing "to 
consider an application filed by Mr. Vincent Gadoury on behalf of PAS Realty for a variance 
from Section 6.1.5.1 of the Westwood Zoning Bylaw, entitled Off-Street Parking, 
Commercial Uses in order to reduce the number of required parking spaces from six to 
four....Property located in Local Business District A (LBA). Land affected: 5 Church Street

Map 21, Lot 032." At the time the notice was posted, it was unclear whether the Board would 
grant the variance or which conditions might be imposed, if granted. 
During the meeting, the Board held a public hearing and then engaged in a discussion 
about the variance with abutters to the property. Following this discussion, the Board granted 
the variance subject to certain conditions, which were read aloud. One condition required that 
any changes or expansion of the business and the building shall come before the Board. On 
December 18, 2013, the Board issued a written Decision (the Decision). Condition 11 
provided, "[s]hould the building be expanded, a determination shall be made by the Building 
Commissioner as to the necessity (sic) the owner appearing before the Zoning Board of 
Appeals." According to Attorney McClusker, the Westwood Zoning Bylaws permit the 
Building Commissioner to first determine whether a building change is de minimis before it is 
referred to the Board for its review.1 Furthermore, Attorney McClusker states that the 
language of Condition 11 is template language used by the Board. 
DISCUSSION 
The Open Meeting Law requires that all meeting notices "shall be printed in a legible, 
easily understandable format and shall contain the date, time, and place of such meeting and a 
listing of topics that the chair reasonably anticipates will be discussed at the meeting." G.L. c. 
30A, § 20(b). The Open Meeting Law does not require that the chair of a public body guess 
what will occur during a meeting, but the chair does need to list those topics that he or she 
reasonably anticipates will be discussed. See OML 2011-36; OML 2011-23; OML 2011-13.2 
We generally consider the description of a topic sufficiently specific when a reasonable 
member of the public could read the topic and understand the anticipated nature of the public 
body's discussion. See OML 2014-23; OML 2013-112. 
The complainant alleges that the Board violated the Open Meeting Law by including a 
condition in its written decision that was neither listed as a topic on the meeting notice nor 
discussed during its November 20, 2013 meeting. We find that the Board's November 20, 
2013 meeting notice was sufficiently specific to notify the public of the discussion that 
occurred concerning the particular property and bylaw at issue in the application before the 
Board. Furthermore, during the meeting, the Board outlined the conditions imposed on the 
variance before granting it. Although Condition 11, as described in the Decision, did not 
precisely reflect the condition described during the meeting, we do not find any evidence that 
the Board discussed Condition 11 outside of a properly posted meeting. Rather, the difference 
in language appears to be the result of the use of template language in the Board's decisions. 
Therefore, the Board did not violate the Open Meeting Law.

CONCLUSION 
For the reasons stated above, we find that the Board did not violate the Open Meeting 
Law. We now consider the complaint addressed by this determination to be resolved. This 
determination does not address any other complaints which may be pending with our office or 
the Board. Please feel free to contact our office at (617) 963-2540 if you have any questions.
