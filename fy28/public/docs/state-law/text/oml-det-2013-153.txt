RE: Open Meeting Law Complaint 
Dear Attorney Chavin: 
This office received an Open Meeting Law complaint from Brenda Smith, dated March 
22, 2013, alleging that the Savoy Board of Selectmen (the "Board") violated the Open Meeting 
Law, G.L. c. 30A, §§ 18-25. The complaint specifically alleges that the Board engaged in 
unlawful deliberation by agreeing outside of a posted meeting to change the date and location of 
the Special Town Meeting that was held on February 26, 2013. The complaint was filed with the 
Board on February 25, 2013. The Board responded by letter through Counsel, dated March 11, 
2013. 
Following our review, we find that the Board violated the Open Meeting Law by 
deliberating outside of a posted meeting when the Board members signed a warrant to change the 
date and location of the Special Town Meeting. In reaching this determination, we reviewed the 
February 25, 2013 complaint filed with the Board; the Board's March 11, 2013 response; and the 
March 22, 2013 complaint filed with our office. Additionally, we reviewed the notice for the 
postponed February 19, 2013 Special Town Meeting and the notice for the February 26, 2013 
Special Town Meeting. Finally, we reviewed the minutes of the Board's January 29, 2013 and 
February 12, 2013 meetings. 
FACTS 
During the Board's January 19, 2013 meeting, the Board agreed to schedule a Special 
Town Meeting for February 19, 2013, to be located at the Savoy Fire Station. The three 
members of the Board signed the warrant for the Special Town Meeting during the meeting. On 
February 5, 2013, the Town of Savoy's Fire Chief notified the Board that she had concerns about 
holding the Special Town Meeting at the Fire Station because parking the fire trucks outside in 
the cold could cause water pumps to freeze. Board Chair John Tynan then directed the Town

DISCUSSION 
The Open Meeting Law was enacted "to eliminate much of the secrecy surrounding 
deliberations and decisions on which public policy is based." Ghiglione v. School Committee 
of Southbridge, 376 Mass. 70, 72 (1978). Except in an emergency, "a public body shall post 
notice of every meeting at least 48 hours prior to such meeting." G.L. c. 30A, § 20(b). Notices 
shall contain "the date, time and place of such meeting and a listing of topics that the chair 
reasonably anticipates will be discussed at the meeting." Id. A "meeting" is "a deliberation by a 
public body with respect to any matter within the body's jurisdiction." G.L. c. 30A, § 18. A 
"deliberation" is "an oral or written communication through any medium, including electronic 
mail, between or among a quorum of a public body on any public business within its jurisdiction; 
however, that 'deliberation' shall not include the distribution of a meeting agenda, scheduling 
information or distribution of other procedural meeting or the distribution of reports or 
documents that may be discussed at a meeting, provided that no opinion of a member is 
expressed." Id A "quorum" is "a simple majority of the members of the public body, unless 
otherwise provided in a general or special law, executive order or other authorizing provision."

After the Fire Chief raised concerns about using the Fire Station as the location for the 
February 19, 2013 Special Town Meeting, the Board Chair consulted with Town Counsel and 
decided to move the Special Town Meeting to the Senior Center on February 26, 2013. The 
Town Manager drafted a new wan-ant, and the three Board members then came to Town Flail to 
sign the new warrant, which was then posted on February 6, 2013. No public meeting of the 
Board was posted or publicly announced. 
The Board argues in its March 11, 2013 letter that "if all the Select Board did was 
reschedule the Special Town Meeting, due to circumstances beyond their control, and further 
scheduled it to the next available time while further providing ample notice to the public, neither 
Selectmen Tynan nor the Select Board violated the Open Meeting Law." The Board argues that 
because the definition of "deliberation" excludes "distribution of a meeting agenda, scheduling 
information, or distribution of other procedural meeting," that the Board did not violate the 
Open Meeting Law by communicating to reschedule the Special Town Meeting. G.L. c. 3OA, § 
18 (emphasis added). The exemption to deliberation refers to discussions among a quorum of 
the Board regarding scheduling of its own meetings. It does not include discussions of 
scheduling other events that are within the Board's jurisdiction to schedule, such as a Special

Town Meeting. See OML 2012-67.' 
The Board members made a collective decision by signing the warrant to change the date 
and location of the Special Town Meeting. This decision was public business within the Board's 
jurisdiction, and constituted a written communication between and among a quorum of the 
Board. See G.L. c. 30A, § 18. Because the Board engaged in deliberation, the Board should 
have posted notice for a meeting and signed the warrant during that meeting. We do not find this 
to be an intentional violation of the law, however, and we note that in its March 11, 2013 
response to the complaint, the Board states that it was following advice of counsel when it acted 
outside of a meeting. 
CONCLUSION 
We find that the Board violated the Open Meeting Law by deliberating outside of a 
posted meeting to change the date and location of the Special Town Meeting. We order the 
Board's immediate and future compliance with Law, and caution the Board that future similar 
violations may be considered evidence of an intentional violation.
