This office received a complaint from Gerrit Blauvelt on January 31, 2021, alleging that 
the North Adams Airport Commission (the “Commission”) violated the Open Meeting Law, 
G.L. c. 30A, §§ 18-25.  The complaint was originally filed with the Commission on December 
15, 2020, and Chair Jeffrey Naughton responded to the complaint, on behalf of the Commission, 
by letter dated December 29, 2020.  The complaint alleges that the Commission failed to 
maintain minutes for meetings held between 1982 and 2017.1   
 
Following our review, we find that the Commission violated the Open Meeting Law in 
the way alleged.  In reaching this determination, we reviewed the original complaint, the 
Commission’s response to the complaint, and the complaint filed with our office requesting 
further review.

FACTS 
 
We find the facts as follows.  On September 24, 2020, the complainant requested from 
the Commission minutes of meetings held between 1960-1962, 1989-1990, and 1995-2004.2  
Commission Secretary Tina Samson3 responded to the request on October 9, 2020, with copies 
of minutes of meetings held between 1960 and 1962 but advised that minutes of other 
Commission meetings did not exist.  On October 20, 2020, in response to follow-up emails from 
the complainant regarding the existence of Commission meeting minutes, North Adams Public 
Records Access Officer Jessica Lincourt advised that “some past airport commission secretaries 
chose to record the minutes, while others did not.  Therefore, the records of the minutes are 
sporadic at best.” 
  
On or about October 21, 2020, having not received any further responsive minutes from 
the Commission, the complainant appealed to the Supervisor of Records (the “Supervisor”) 
within the Office of the Secretary of the Commonwealth.  On December 2, 2020, the Supervisor 
closed the appeal because the Commission could not locate any additional meeting minutes.  
However, the Supervisor raised concerns with the records management practices of the 
Commission and reminded the Commission that meeting minutes must be retained permanently.  
See Municipal Records Retention Schedule 01.1074.   
 
On December 15, 2020, this complaint was filed with the Commission.  The Commission 
responded on December 29, 2020, suggesting that the complaint was untimely because the 
complainant was “aware of the lack of minutes as of October 15, 2020,” but the complaint was 
not filed until December 15, 2020 – 61 days later.  The Commission’s response further states that 
it will “continue to comply with MGL OML guidelines and the North Adams City Ordinance . . . 
and will catalogue and archive past minutes which have been recovered.”  See North Adams City 
Ordinance Section 2-2.1 (every board and commission shall file minutes of each of its meetings, 
whether or not approved, with the City Clerk within two weeks of said meeting).  
 
DISCUSSION 
 
We first address the Commission’s suggestion that the complaint is untimely because the 
complainant was “aware of the lack of minutes as of October 15, 2020,” but the complaint was 
not filed until 61 days later.  Here, the complainant appealed the Board’s response to his request 
for minutes to the Supervisor, rather than file an Open Meeting Law complaint.  However, he 
filed this complaint on January 13, 2018, within 30 days of receiving the Supervisor’s decision.  
Where the complaint was filed within 30 days of the Supervisor’s decision, we find that it was 
timely filed.  See G.L. c. 30A, § 23(b); 940 CMR 29.05(3) (complaints alleging violations of the 
Open Meeting Law must be filed with the public body within 30 days of the alleged violation, or 
within 30 days of the date the alleged violation could reasonably have been discovered); OML 
2017-102.

Next, we address the substance of the complaint - that the Commission failed to maintain 
minutes for meetings held between 1982 and 2017.  The Attorney General assumed 
responsibility for the interpretation and enforcement of the Open Meeting Law as it applies to 
local public bodies on July 1, 2010.  Prior to that date, the district attorneys had responsibility for 
enforcement of the Open Meeting Law at the municipal level.  Moreover, at the time that certain 
of the alleged violations occurred, specifically the maintenance of minutes between 1982 and 
2010, the revised Open Meeting Law, G.L. c. 30A, §§ 18-25, was not yet in effect.  Therefore, 
we apply the version of the Open Meeting Law that was in effect at the time of the meetings at 
issue.  The Open Meeting Law, as it existed prior to July 1, 2010, stated that a public body “shall 
maintain accurate records of its meetings, setting forth the date, time, place, members present or 
absent and action taken at each meeting, including executive sessions.”  G.L. c. 39, § 23B.   
 
The current version of the Open Meeting Law requires that a public body “create and 
maintain accurate minutes of all meetings, including executive sessions, setting forth the date, 
time and place, the members present or absent, a summary of the discussions on each subject, a 
list of documents and other exhibits used at the meeting, the decisions made and the actions 
taken at each meeting, including the record of all votes.”  G.L. c. 30A, § 22(a).  Moreover, the 
Open Meeting Law requires public bodies to create and approve meeting minutes, whether for 
open or executive session, in a timely manner.  G.L. c. 30A, § 22(c).  “Timely manner” means 
“within the next three public body meetings or within 30 days, whichever is later, unless the 
public body can show good cause for further delay.”  940 CMR 29.11; see OML 2018-48.   
 
The Commission suggests that, prior to 2017, the City of North Adams did not require 
public bodies to retain meeting minutes.  Regardless of what the City of North Adams requires, 
at all relevant times, the Open Meeting Law required that a public body create and maintain 
accurate minutes of all meetings.  See G.L. c. 30A, § 22(a); G.L. c. 39, § 23B.  We find that the 
Commission failed to meet this requirement for numerous meetings by not creating or 
maintaining minutes of its meetings for a period of approximately 35 years and thus violated the 
Open Meeting Law.   
 
In past determinations, when a public body has failed to create minutes of meetings, we 
have ordered that public body to create minutes for all previous meetings to the best of its ability, 
through whatever means are available, including the memories and individual notations of the 
attendees.  See OML 2020-160; OML 2018-23; OML 2017-120.4  We acknowledge the 
difficulty in doing so here where the number of missing meeting minutes is voluminous, the 
period of time that the Commission failed to maintain minutes exceeds three decades, and the 
Commission is currently comprised of mostly different members than those who served 
previously.  Nevertheless, we order the Commission to create minutes for as many meetings as it 
deems possible, starting with the most recent, using any means available to it.  See OML 2020-
53 (noting that, although “time ha[d] passed since [the] meeting occurred, the Board should add 
the requisite level of detail to the minutes to the best of its ability”); OML 2020-69 (ordering a 
school committee to create and approve minutes for meetings of a subcommittee held over a 
three-year period).  Although we order the Commission to create minutes to the best of its

CONCLUSION 
 
 For the reasons stated above, we find that the Commission violated the Open Meeting 
Law by failing to create and maintain meeting minutes.  We order immediate and future 
compliance with the law’s requirements and we caution that similar future violations could be 
considered evidence of intent to violate the law.  In addition, we order the Commission to create 
minutes for as many meetings as it deems possible to the best of its ability, through whatever 
means are available, including the notes and recollections of the attendees.  Within 90 days of 
receipt of this letter, the Commission shall certify to our office that it has complied with this 
order to create meeting minutes.  If the Commission is unable to create specific minutes, it must, 
within 90 days of receipt of this letter, explain to this office in writing what efforts it made to 
create those minutes. 
 
We now consider the complaint addressed by this determination to be resolved.  This 
determination does not address any other complaints that may be pending with the Commission 
or with our office.  Please feel free to contact our office at (617) 963-2540 if you have any
