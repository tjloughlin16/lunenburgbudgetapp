This office received a complaint from Linda Johansen on March 24, 2017 alleging 
that the Townsend Board of Selectmen (the "Board") violated the Open Meeting Law, 
G.L. c. 30A, §§ 18-25. The complaint was originally filed with the Board on February 
13, 2017; and you responded, on behalf of the Board, by letter dated March 6, 2017. The 
complaint alleges that the Board failed to timely approve open session minutes from its 
meetings held between March and December 2016, and failed to provide open session 
minutes upon request. 
Following our review, we find that the Board violated the Open Meeting Law by 
not approving its meeting minutes in a timely manner and by failing to properly respond 
to a request for meeting minutes. In reaching this determination, we reviewed the 
original complaint, the Board's response to the complaint, and the complaint filed with 
our office requesting further review. We also reviewed the open session minutes from 
the Board's February 28, 2017 and March 7, 2017 meetings. Finally, we spoke with Ms. 
Johansen by telephone on July 5, 2017. 
We find the facts as follows. In early January 2017, the complainant 
electronically requested copies of open session minutes from all Board meetings held in 
2016. She was advised that approved minutes were posted to the town website, 
www.townsend.ma.us. After reviewing the website, the complainant identified twenty-
one sets of minutes from Board meetings held between March and December 2016 that 
were missing from the website. On January 18, 2016, the complainant requested copies 
of the open session minutes from the Board meetings held between March and December

DISCUSSION 
The Open Meeting Law requires that a public body "create and maintain accurate 
minutes of all meetings, including executive sessions, setting forth the date, time and 
place, the members present or absent, a summary of the discussions on each subject, a list 
of documents and other exhibits used at the meeting, the decisions made and the actions 
taken at each meeting, including the record of all votes." G.L. c. 30A, § 22(a). The 
meeting minutes must be reviewed and approved in a timely manner. G.L. c. 30A, § 
22(c). Although the Open Meeting Law does not define "timely manner," we 
recommend that minutes of a meeting be approved at the next meeting, whenever 
possible. See OML 2014-15; OML 2012-91.1 
The complaint alleges that the Board failed to approve open session minutes from 
Board meetings held between March and December 2016 in a timely manner. The Board 
denies that it violated the Open Meeting Law claiming that "all 2016 outstanding 
minutes" were approved at the Board's February 28, 2017 meeting. We find that the 
Board violated the Open Meeting Law where it took between two and eleven months to 
approve its meeting minutes. Here, the Board took at most eleven months to approve 
minutes from meetings held in March 2016; and over two months at the very least to 
approve minutes from meetings held in December 2016. Absent exceptional 
circumstances, it is not timely for a public body that meets weekly or bi-weekly to take 
two to three months to approve minutes of its meetings. See G.L. c. 30A, § 22(c); OML 
2016-62; OML 2015-43. However, we commend the Board for taking remedial action in 
response to the complaint by promptly approving the backlog of minutes. 
In response to the complaint, the Board contends that significant staffing 
turnovers and "a great deal of work directed at the office" in 2016 resulted in the Board 
neglecting to compile and approve minutes. The Board also suggests that, even if 
minutes were not timely approved, videos of the meetings were available the following 
day on the Town's You Tube channel. We commend the Board for making a video of 
each meeting promptly available and we understand the demands on a volunteer Board 
with limited staff, however the Board is still obligated to create and approve minutes of 
its meetings in a timely manner. See G.L. c. 30A, § 22(a); OML 2015-86. If staff

resources are not available to create minutes for the Board to approve, then Board

Finally, the complaint alleges that the Board failed to provide copies of open 
session meeting minutes upon request. The Open Meeting Law requires that "[t]he 
minutes of an open session, if they exist and whether approved or in draft form, shall be 
made available upon request by any person within 10 days." G.L. c. 30A, § 22(a), (c). 
Here, the complainant requested copies of draft minutes on January 18, 2017, but minutes 
were not provided until February 16, 2017. Accordingly, the Board violated the Open 
Meeting Law by failing to properly respond to a request for minutes. The Open Meeting 
Law does not require that public bodies post their meeting minutes to a website. See 
OML 2013-103. We note, however, that the Board has chosen to post its meeting 
minutes on its website and has notified the complainant that minutes from its meetings 
are available at that location. 
CONCLUSION 
We find that the Board violated the Open Meeting Law by not approving its 
meeting minutes in a timely manner and failing to properly respond to a request for 
meeting minutes. We order the Board's immediate and future compliance with the law's 
requirements, and we caution that similar future violations could be considered evidence 
of intent to violate the law. 
We now consider the complaint addressed by this determination to be resolved.
