This office received a complaint from Mary Rice, dated August 13, 2013, alleging 
that the Hinsdale Select Board (the Board) violated the Open Meeting Law, G.L. c. 30A, 
§§ 18-25. The complaint was originally filed with the Board on or about June 5, 2013, and 
you responded, on behalf of the Board, to the original complaint by letter dated July 11, 
2013. In her complaint, Ms. Rice alleges that the Board deliberated outside of a properly 
posted meeting about the terms of the Police Chiefs contract, which was executed during 
the Board's May 8, 2013 meeting. 
We reviewed the June 5, 2013 complaint; your July 11, 2013 response to the 
complaint, on behalf of the Board; and the August 13, 2013 complaint filed with our office 
requesting further review. Finally, we spoke by telephone with Selectman Bruce Marshall 
as well as with the Board's Assistant, Kerri Streibel. 
Following our review, we find that the Board did not violate the Open Meeting 
We find the facts to be as follows. The Board consists of three members, thus two 
constitutes a quorum. In June 2012, the Hinsdale Police Chief resigned, and the Board 
appointed Nancy Daniels to unofficially serve as Interim Police Chief. In early 2013, the 
Board began the process of appointing a new police chief. During its meeting held on 
January 16, 2013, the Board discussed the police chief position. The meeting notice, 
posted on January 11, 2013, listed "Police Chief position" among the discussion topics.

DISCUSSION 
The Open Meeting Law requires that all meetings of a public body be properly 
noticed and open to members of the public, unless an executive session is convened. See 
G.L. c. 30A, §§ 20(a)-(b), 21. The Law's purpose is "to eliminate much of the secrecy 
surrounding deliberations and decisions on which public policy is based." Ghiglione v. 
School Committee of Southbridge. 376 Mass. 70, 72 (1978). A "meeting" is defined, in 
relevant part, as "a deliberation by a public body with respect to any matter within the 
body's jurisdiction." G.L. c. 30A, § 18. The law defines "deliberation" as "an oral or 
written communication through any medium, including electronic mail, between or among 
a quorum of a public body on any public business within its jurisdiction." Id 
We do not find evidence to support the allegation that a quorum of the Board 
improperly deliberated about the draft contract between its meetings held on January 23, 
2013 and May 8, 2013. The Board discussed and agreed to the final terms of the contract 
during an executive session on January 23, 2013. Following this meeting, Ms. Streibel 
typed up the final draft of the contract for Selectman Marshall's review. The complainant 
has not identified, and we have not found any evidence of, other communication between 
Board members outside of a meeting regarding the contract. During its May 8, 2013 
meeting, the Board executed the final contract. Because a quorum of the Board is two 
members, and Ms. Streibel is not a member of the Board, the communication between her 
and Selectman Marshall was permissible. Accordingly, we find that the Board did not 
violate the Open Meeting Law.

CONCLUSION
