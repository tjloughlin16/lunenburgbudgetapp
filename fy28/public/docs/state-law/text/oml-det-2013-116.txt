RE: Open Meeting Law Complaint 
Dear Attorney Corbo: 
This office received an Open Meeting Law complaint from Peter Golrick, dated 
December 28, 2012, alleging that the Montague Planning Board (the "Board") violated the Open 
Meeting Law, G.L. c. 30A, §§ 18-25. Specifically, the complaint alleges that the Board failed to 
timely post notice for its November 27, 2012 meeting; that there was an error in the description 
for a topic listed in the notice; and that the chair failed to publicly announce that the meeting was 
being recorded. The complaint was filed with the Board on November 28, 2012. The Board 
responded by letter dated December 18, 2012. 
Following our review, we find that the Chair violated the Open Meeting Law by failing to 
inform the attendees that the November 27, 2012 meeting was being recorded. The violation 
was de minimis, though, as the complainant drew substantial attention to the fact that he was 
recording, and those in attendance were aware that he was doing so. We find that the Board did 
not violate the Open Meeting Law with respect to the remaining allegations. 
The Board properly posted notice for its November 27, 2012 meeting, both on the 
Town's bulletin board and on the Town's website, on November 19, 2012. See G.L. c. 30A, §§ 
20(b),(c). Additionally, because generally a typographical error in a meeting notice will not 
constitute a violation of the Open Meeting Law, we therefore find that the alleged error in 
identifying an address in that same notice did not violate the Law. See G.L. c. 3OA, § 20(b); 940 
CMR 29.03(l)(b). 
Finally, the complaint alleges that after Mr. Golrick informed the Chair that he would be 
recording the November 27, 2012 meeting, the Chair failed to publicly announce that the

meeting was being recorded. The Open Meeting Law states that, "[a]fter notifying the chair of 
the public body, any person may make a video or audio recording of an open session of a 
meeting of a public body ... At the beginning of the meeting the chair shall inform other 
attendees of any such recordings." G.L. c. 30A, § 20(e). According to the Board's December 
18, 2012 response to the complaint, Mr. Golrick positioned recording equipment in the room so 
that it was clear to all in attendance that he was recording, and everyone heard Mr. Golrick 
inform the Chair that he was recording. While Mr. Golrick's recording was open and obvious, 
the Law nevertheless requires that the chair of a public body state at the beginning of the meeting 
whether anyone is recording. This clear rule eliminates the need for chairs to determine in each 
individual instance whether a recording is obvious, or whether the statement made by the person 
who is recording was sufficiently clear or audible to attendees. We therefore find that the Chair 
violated the Open Meeting Law, but find that the violation was de minimis, as all attendees were 
aware of the recording, and order no further relief. We remind the Chair of his obligation to 
inform attendees when an individual is recording a meeting. 
We appreciate the patience and cooperation of the parties during this investigation, and 
now consider this matter closed. Please contact our office at (617) 963-2504 if you have any 
questions regarding this letter.
