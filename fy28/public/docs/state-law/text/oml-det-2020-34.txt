RE: Open  Meeting Law Complaint
Dear Attorney Callanan:
This office received a complaint from Gerard Mackin on May 15, 2019, alleging that the 
Weymouth School Committee Collective Bargaining Subcommittee (the “Subcommittee”) 
violated the Open Meeting Law, G.L. c. 30A, §§ 18-25.' The complaint was originally filed 
with the Subcommittee on or about April 13, and you responded to the complaint, on behalf of 
the Subcommittee, by letter dated May 7. The complaint alleges that the notice for the 
Subcommittee’s March 27 meeting lacked sufficient detail where it did not list the specific 
executive minutes to be approved.
We resolve this complaint by informal action in accordance with 940 CMR 29.07(2)(a), 
after reviewing the original complaint, the Subcommittee’s response to the complaint, the 
complaint filed with our office requesting further review, and the notices and open session 
minutes of the Subcommittee’s March 27 and April 23 meetings.
The Open Meeting Law requires a public body to post notice of a meeting at least 48 
hours in advance, including a “listing of topics that the chair reasonably anticipates will be 
discussed at the meeting.” G.L. c. 30A, § 20(b). Public bodies are required to list topics on a 
notice with “sufficient specificity to reasonably advise the public of the issues to be discussed at 
the meeting.” 940 CMR 29.03(l)(b); OML 2014-47.1 2 We generally consider a topic sufficiently 
specific when a reasonable member of the public could read the topic and understand the 
anticipated nature of the public body’s discussion. See OML 2015-35.

The complaint alleges that the March 27 meeting notice should have identified the 
specific executive session meeting minutes to be approved. Notices should generally include this 
detail. See OML 2017-92; OML 2013-199. Here, the March 27 notice did not identify the 
specific minutes to be approved, and instead included a generic statement that the Subcommittee 
would approve executive session minutes. The Subcommittee does not contend that it was 
unaware of the exact minutes to be approved during its March 27 meeting. Compare OML 
2015-25 (when the chair does not anticipate which minutes will be ready for review until the 
meeting, a generic topic may be sufficient). As such, we find that the Subcommittee violated the 
Open Meeting Law by failing to list on its notice the specific executive session minutes to be 
approved.
However, the Subcommittee took prompt remedial action in response to the complaint.
On April 18 at 4:56 P.M., the Subcommittee posted a notice for a meeting to be held on April 23 
at 4:00 PM. The notice listed four topics, including “Review of Unit A executive session 
minutes.” The notice further included the dates for nine sets of executive session minutes and 
stated that this topic was a “reauthorization of previous meeting’s March 27 agenda item.” The 
April 23 meeting was held as planned and the Subcommittee re-approved the nine sets of 
executive session minutes that it had initially approved during its March 27 meeting.3 Where, as 
occurred here, a public body takes corrective action in response to a complaint, we strongly 
encourage complainants to consider whether filing a complaint with our office is necessary. See 
OML 2017-191; OML 2016-159.
For the reasons stated above, we find that the Subcommittee violated the Open Meeting 
Law but we order no further relief where the Subcommittee took prompt remedial action. We 
now consider the complaint addressed by this determination to be resolved. This determination 
does not address any other complaints that may be pending with our office or the Subcommittee. 
Please feel free to contact our office at (617) 963-2540 if you have any questions regarding this 
letter.
