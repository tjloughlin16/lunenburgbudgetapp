This office received a complaint from David Dunn, dated May 30, 2014, alleging that the 
Lancaster Board of Selectmen (the "Board") violated the Open Meeting Law, G.L. c. 30A, §§ 
18-25. Specifically, the complaint alleges that: 1) the Board refused to permit the local cable 
access station from recording its April 1, 2014 meeting; 2) the Board has been deliberating 
outside of noticed meetings; and 3) that the Board has held discussions in executive session that 
were not appropriate for executive session. The complaint was originally filed with the Board on 
or about April 23, 2014. The Board responded to the complaint through its attorney by letter 
dated May 20, 2014.1 
In his May 30, 2014 complaint filed with our office, Mr. Dunn also alleges that the Board 
violated the Open Meeting Law prior to or during its December 31, 2013 meeting. We decline to 
review this allegation because 1) the allegation was not first raised in a complaint filed with the 
public body; and 2) the allegation is untimely. See G.L. c. 30A, § 23(b); 940 CMR 29.05(3). 
Additionally, we decline to review Mr. Dunn's allegations that the Board has deliberated outside 
of open meetings and held discussions in executive session that were not appropriate for 
executive session, as our office will not conduct broad audits of public bodies based on 
generalized allegations without specific dates or date ranges. See OML 2013-180; OML 
Declination 2-25-13.2 
Following our review of the remaining allegations, we find that the Board did not violate 
the Open Meeting Law by requesting that Sterling/Lancaster Community Television Corporation 
("SLCTV") not record its April 1, 2014 meeting. In reaching this determination, we reviewed 
the April 23, 2014 complaint filed with the Board; the Board's May 20, 2014 response; and the

FACTS 
According to a 2009 agreement, SLCTV has contracted with the Board to "[c]ablecast 
all public meetings of the Lancaster Board of Selectmen." Prior to the Board's April 1, 2014 
open meeting, an SLCTV member began setting up equipment to record the meeting. The Board 
Chair requested that the SLCTV member not record the meeting, and the SLCTV member 
complied. 
DISCUSSION 
The Open Meeting Law states that "[ajfter notifying the chair of the public body, any 
person may make a video or audio recording of an open session of a meeting of a public body, or 
may transmit the meeting through any medium, subject to reasonable requirements of the chair 
as to the number, placement and operation of equipment used so as not to interfere with the 
conduct of the meeting. At the beginning of the meeting the chair shall inform other attendees of 
any such recordings." G.L. c. 30A, § 20(e). While any member of the public may record an 
open meeting, a public body is not required to make an audio or video recording of its own 
meeting. OML 2013-8. 
The complaint does not allege that anyone was denied the right to record or broadcast the 
Board's April 1, 2014 meeting. Rather, it asserts that the Board asked an SLCTV member not to 
record the meeting and the member complied. As SLCTV was acting as an agent of the Board, 
recording the meeting pursuant to a contract, the Board's request was akin to a decision by the 
Board itself not to record the meeting. Because a public body is not required to broadcast or 
record its meetings, we find that the Board did not violate the Open Meeting Law. See OML 
2013-40; OML 2013-8; OML 2012-57. We make no finding as to whether the Board was 
required by contract to permit SLCTV to record or broadcast the meeting.4
