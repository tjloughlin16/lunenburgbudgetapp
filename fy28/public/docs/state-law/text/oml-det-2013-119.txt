RE: Open Meeting Law Complaint - Carver Board of Selectmen 
Dear Mr. Franey: 
This office received a complaint from Mr. Jon Fortier, dated January 5, 2013, alleging 
that the Carver Board of Selectmen (the "Board") violated the Open Meeting Law, G.L. c. 30A, 
§§ 18-25. Specifically, the complaint alleges that the notice for the Board's October 16, 2012 
meeting did not include enough specificity to reasonably inform the public that it would 
deliberate and vote to disband the Audit Committee. Additionally, the complaint alleges that the 
notice for the Board's September 25, 2012 meeting did not include enough specificity to 
reasonably inform the public that the Board would deliberate and vote to reduce the Building 
Commissioner's position from full-time to part-time. The complaint was originally filed with the 
Board as two complaints, both dated October 22, 2012. The Board responded to both by letter 
dated November 20, 2012. 
Following our review, we find that Board did not violate the Open Meeting Law. In 
reaching this determination, we reviewed two complaints filed with the Board, dated October 22, 
2012; the Board's November 20, 2012 response; and the January 5, 2013 complaint filed with 
our office. Additionally, we reviewed the notices and minutes of the Board's September 25, 
2012 and October 16, 2012 meetings. 
FACTS 
The notice for the Board's September 25, 2012 meeting included a number of topics. 
One category of topics was "Items for Discussion and Possible Vote." Under that category were 
nine topics, including "Building Commissioner Position." During the September 25, 2012

Following significant discussion, the Board voted 3-2 to make the building commissioner a part-

The notice for the Board's October 16, 2012 meeting included the category of topics,

DISCUSSION 
The Open Meeting Law requires that meetings of a public body be properly noticed and 
open to members of the public, unless an executive session is convened. See G.L. c. 30A, §§ 
20(a)—(b), 21. Meeting notices must include "the date, time and place of such meeting and a 
listing of topics that the chair reasonably anticipates will be discussed at the meeting." G.L. c. 
30A, § 20(b). The list of topics must have sufficient specificity to reasonably advise the public 
of the issues to be discussed at the meeting. 940 CMR 29.03(l)(b); OML 2011-7.1 
The notice for the Board's September 25, 2012 meeting included "Building 
Commissioner Position" as a topic under "Items for Discussion and Possible Votes." This topic 
was sufficiently specific to reasonably advise the public that the Board would be discussing, and 
possibly voting to make changes to, the building commissioner position. See 940 CMR 
29.03(l)(b); OML 2013-102; OML 2012-83. 
The notice for the Board's October 16, 2012 meeting included "Audit Committee Update 
- Chairman Bruce Kaiser (possible vote)" as a topic under "Appointments." During the meeting, 
the Board met with the Audit Committee Chair and entertained a lengthy discussion about the 
progress and status of the Audit Committee, and then voted 3-0-1 to disband the Audit 
Committee and reconstitute it with new membership. The topic listed in the notice was 
sufficiently specific to reasonably advise members of the public that the Board would be 
discussing appointments to the Audit Committee and could take a vote. 
We encourage public bodies to include in their meeting notices as much detail as possible 
about the nature of any anticipated votes.
