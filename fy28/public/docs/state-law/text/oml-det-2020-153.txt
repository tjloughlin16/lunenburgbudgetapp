Dear Attorney Murphy: 
 
This office received a complaint from Mike Offner on August 10, 2020, alleging that the 
Brookline Select Board (the “Board”) violated the Open Meeting Law, G.L. c. 30A, §§ 18-25.1  
The complaint was originally filed with the Board on July 8, and you responded, on behalf of the 
Board, by e-mail dated July 27.  The complaint alleges that a topic listed on the notice for the 
Board’s July 7 meeting was insufficiently detailed.  
 
 We resolve this matter by informal action, in accordance with 940 CMR 29.07(2)(a), 
after reviewing the original complaint, the Board’s response to the complaint, the complaint filed 
with our office requesting further review, and the notice and open session minutes of the Board’s 
July 7 meeting.  We find that the Board did not violate the Open Meeting Law as alleged.   
 
The Open Meeting Law requires that, except in an emergency, “a public body shall post 
notice of every meeting at least 48 hours prior to such meeting, excluding Saturdays, Sundays 
and legal holidays.”  G.L. c. 30A, § 20(b).  The notice must be printed in a legible, easily 
understandable format and must contain the date, time and place of the meeting, as well as a 
listing of topics that the chair reasonably anticipates will be discussed.  Id.  The list of topics 
shall have “sufficient specificity to reasonably advise the public of the issues to be discussed at 
the meeting.”  940 CMR 29.03(l)(b).  We generally consider a topic to be sufficiently specific

when a reasonable member of the public could read the topic and understand the anticipated 
nature of the public body’s discussion.  See OML 2015-35.2   
 
The notice for the Board’s July 7 meeting listed 13 topics, the first of which was 
Organization of the Select Board – Deliberation by the Board and Vote on Select Board 
Leadership.  The complaint alleges that this topic as listed on the July 7 notice was insufficiently 
detailed where the topic did not specifically include reference to the deliberation and vote of a 
vice chair position.  During the July 7 meeting, the Board discussed the creation of a new vice 
chair position, the responsibilities of a vice chair, and then voted to create a position of vice 
chair.  The Board then appointed by majority vote member Bernard Greene to serve as chair and 
member Heather Hamilton to serve as vice chair. 
 
We find that the Board did not violate the Open Meeting Law with respect to the 
sufficiency of the July 7 meeting notice.  The Open Meeting Law does not require a public body 
to list all tangential topics related to a discussion nor does the law require a public body to 
anticipate the course of deliberation or the outcome of a discussion, only that it identify the 
discussion topic with sufficient specificity to reasonably advise the public of the issues to be 
discussed at the meeting.  See G.L. c. 30A, § 20(b); 940 CMR 29.03(l)(b); OML 2015-73; OML 
2014-127; OML 2014-58; OML 2014-38.  Here, the topic as listed sufficiently identified the 
discussions that occurred, which were essentially about the leadership of the Board.    
 
 We now consider the complaint addressed by this determination to be resolved.  This 
determination does not address any other complaints that may be pending with the Board or with 
our office.  Please feel free to contact our office at (617) 963-2540 if you have any questions 
regarding this letter.
