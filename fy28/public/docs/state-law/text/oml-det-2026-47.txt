RE:  Open Meeting Law Complaint 
 
Dear Mr. Ullucci, 
 
This office received a complaint from Patrick Higgins on November 6, 2025, alleging 
that the Somerset Board of Selectmen (the “Board”) violated the Open Meeting Law, G.L. c. 
30A, §§ 18-25.  The complaint was originally filed with the Board on October 20, 2025, and you 
responded, on behalf of the Board, by letter dated November 5, 2025.  The complaint alleges that 
the Board convened an executive session on October 16, 2025, at 5:01 P.M. in violation of the 
Open Meeting Law where the notice stated the start time of the meeting as 6:00 P.M.  
 
Following our review, we find that the Board did not violate the Open Meeting Law in 
the way alleged.  In reaching this determination, we reviewed the original complaint, the Board’s 
response to the complaint, the complaint filed with our office requesting review, and the notice 
and open session minutes of the Board meeting held on  October 16, 2025.

DISCUSSION 
 
The Open Meeting Law was enacted “to eliminate much of the secrecy surrounding 
deliberations and decisions on which public policy is based.”  Ghiglione v. School Committee of 
Southbridge, 376 Mass. 70, 72 (1978).  The Open Meeting Law requires that, except in an 
emergency, “a public body shall post notice of every meeting at least 48 hours prior to such 
meeting, excluding Saturdays, Sundays and legal holidays.”  G.L. c. 30A, § 20(b).  The notice 
must be printed in a legible, easily understandable format and must contain the date, time and 
place of the meeting, as well as a listing of topics that the chair reasonably anticipates will be 
discussed.  Id.   
 
A public body may enter an executive, or closed, session for any of the ten purposes 
enumerated in the Open Meeting Law provided that it has first convened in an open session, that 
a majority of members of the body have voted to go into executive session, that the vote of each 
member is recorded by roll call and entered into the minutes, and the chair has publicly 
announced whether the open session will reconvene at the conclusion of the executive session.  
G.L. c. 30A, §§ 21(a), (b); see also OML 2014-94.

The complaint alleges that the Board held an “illegal” executive session at 5:01 P.M. on 
October 16, 2025, as the notice stated the start time of the meeting as 6:00 P.M.  The Open 
Meeting Law requires that a public body post notice with an accurate and clear start time.  See 
OML 2016-97; OML 2013-161; OML 2012-59.  A public body may not begin a meeting earlier 
than the posted start time.  See OML 2021-53; OML 2013-161.  Here, the masthead on the notice 
included the “Date & Time of Meeting” as Thursday, October 16, 2025, at 6:00 P.M.  However, 
directly below this statement was additional information that specified the “Location of Meeting” 
as 5:00 P.M. at the Somerset Town Office Building.  The notice then listed the specific topics to 
be discussed, with the first three topics as “Call to Order: Open Session: 5:00 PM,” then 
“Executive Session: 5:01 PM,” and “Call to Order: Open Session: 6:00 PM.”   
 
Because the notice posted for the October 16, 2025, meeting listed a 5:00 P.M. start time 
in multiple locations, and, indeed, the meeting began at that time, we find it unlikely that any 
member of the public was genuinely confused by the one 6:00 P.M. notation listed on the notice.  
Thus, we find that the Board did not violate the Open Meeting Law.  However, we encourage the 
Board to be more attentive to preventing errors that may cause confusion as to meeting times.  In 
addition, consistent with our office’s previous guidance, the Board made reasonable efforts to 
honor the times at which it anticipated different items of discussion to occur during the October 
16, 2025, meeting where the Board first convened in open session at 5:01 P.M., moved to 
convene in executive session at 5:02 P.M., and then reconvened in open session at 6:03 P.M.  
See OML 2014-35.   
 
CONCLUSION 
 
For the reasons stated above, we find that the Board did not violate the Open Meeting 
Law.  We now consider the complaint addressed by this determination to be resolved.  This 
determination does not address any other complaints that may be pending with our office or the 
Board.  Please feel free to contact our office at (617) 963-2540 if you have any questions 
regarding this letter.
