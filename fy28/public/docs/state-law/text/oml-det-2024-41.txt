 RE: Open Meeting Law Complaints 
 
Dear Attorney Mead: 
 
This office received a complaint from Stephen Morgan on September 1, 2023, alleging 
that the Ashland Sustainability Committee (the “Committee”) violated the Open Meeting Law, 
G.L. c. 30A, §§ 18-25.  The complaint was originally filed with the Committee on or about 
August 11, and you responded, on behalf of the Committee, by letter dated August 31.1  The 
complaint alleges that the Committee failed to timely approve the minutes of meetings held 
between May 9 and August 1. 
 
We resolve this matter by informal action, in accordance with 940 CMR 29.07(2)(a), 
after reviewing the original complaints, the Committee’s response to the complaint, and the 
complaint filed with our office requesting further review.  We find that the Committee violated 
the Open Meeting Law by failing to timely approve minutes of its May 9, May 23, and June 20 
meetings.   
 
The Open Meeting Law requires that a public body “create and maintain accurate 
minutes of all meetings, including executive sessions, setting forth the date, time and place, the 
members present or absent, a summary of the discussions on each subject, a list of documents 
and other exhibits used at the meeting, the decisions made and the actions taken at each meeting, 
including the record of all votes.”  G.L. c. 30A, § 22(a).  The meeting minutes must be reviewed 
and approved in a timely manner.  G.L. c. 30A, § 22(c).  “Timely manner” means “within the 
next three public body meetings or within 30 days, whichever is later, unless the public body can

show good cause for further delay.”  See 940 CMR 29.11; OML 2018-48.2  Whenever possible, 
we recommend that minutes of a meeting be approved at the next meeting.  See OML 2018-67; 
OML 2017-133.  
 
As a preliminary matter, we reject the Committee’s assertion that the complaint was not 
timely filed, and therefore this office should decline to review allegations, as to minutes of 
meetings held more than 30 days before the complaint was filed with the Committee on August 
11.  Complaints alleging violations of the Open Meeting Law generally must be filed with the 
public body within 30 days of the alleged violation.  G.L. c. 30A, § 23; 940 CMR 29.05(4).  The 
Open Meeting Law imposes a continuing obligation to create and maintain minutes of all 
meetings.  See G.L. c. 30A, § 22(a); OML 2020-69 (reviewing a complaint regarding a 
committee’s failure to approve meeting minutes for a three-year period); OML 2019-133; OML 
2018-115 (reviewing a complaint regarding the failure to create and maintain meeting minutes 
for meetings held four years prior); OML 2013-45.  Therefore, we consider the complaint to be 
timely as the alleged violations continued each day until the minutes were approved.   
 
Turning to the merits of the complaint, the Committee was required to approve the 
minutes of meetings held on May 9, May 23, June 20, July 13, July 19, and August 1 within the 
later of the next three meetings or 30 days.  See 940 CMR 29.11; OML 2018-152.  As of the date 
the complaint was filed, the minutes of the May 9, May 23, and June 20 meetings had not been 
approved, and the Committee violated the Open Meeting Law by failing to timely approve those 
meeting minutes, as the Committee acknowledges.  In response to the complaint, the Committee 
approved all outstanding meeting minutes and pledged to do so in a timely manner going 
forward. 
 
We remind the Committee that the responsibility for complying with the Open Meeting 
Law rests with public bodies and members of such bodies, not with individual public employees, 
such as administrative staff.  See OML 2018-105.  We understand the demands on a volunteer 
public body with limited or no staff; however, the Committee is still obligated to timely approve 
minutes.  See OML 2018-111; OML 2017-115.  If staff resources are not available to create 
minutes for the Committee to approve, then Committee members must take on this responsibility 
to ensure compliance with the law.  See OML 2017-115.   
 
As a remedy for the Committee’s failure to timely approve meeting minutes, the 
complainant requests that all minutes be completed and released, and that the Committee 
establish a process to ensure future timely approval of meeting minutes, both of which the 
Committee represents has been done.  The complainant also requests a “sanction for multiple 
repeat similar violations.”  Although the Committee has been found in violation of the Open 
Meeting Law on three prior occasions, see OML 2023-77, OML 2023-12, and OML 2018-7, we 
have not previously found the Committee in violation of the Open Meeting Law for failing to 
timely approve meeting minutes.  Therefore, we decline to find that this violation was intentional 
and decline to recommend a civil penalty.

For the reasons stated above, we find that the Committee violated the Open Meeting Law 
by failing to timely approve meeting minutes.  We order the Committee’s immediate and future 
compliance, and caution that a similar violation in the future may be considered evidence of an 
intent to violate the Law and may result in a civil penalty of up to $1,000 per violation.   
 
We now consider the complaint addressed by this determination to be resolved.  This 
determination does not address any other complaints that may be pending with our office or the 
Committee.  Please feel free to contact our office at (617) 963-2540 if you have any questions 
regarding this letter.
