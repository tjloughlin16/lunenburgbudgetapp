Dear Attorney Frank: 
This office received two related complaints from Patrick Higgins alleging that the 
Swansea Recreation Commission (the "Commission") violated the Open Meeting Law, G.L. c. 
30A, §§ 18-25. The first complaint was originally filed with the Commission on October 25, 
2017, and, after the Commission failed to respond, this complaint was filed with our office on 
November 15, 2017. The second complaint was filed with the Commission on November 15, 
2017, and, after the Commission again failed to respond, the second complaint was filed with our 
office on December 7, 2017. After being repeatedly contacted by our office, the Commission 
responded to both complaints on February 2, 2018. In his complaints, Mr. Higgins alleges that 
the Commission violated the Open Meeting Law by: (i) failing to respond to a request for 
meeting minutes; and (ii) failing to respond to an Open Meeting Law complaint. 
Following our review, we find that the Commission violated the Open Meeting Law in 
the ways alleged in the complaints. We find that the failure to respond to a request for meeting 
minutes within the time frame required by law was intentional. In reaching this determination, 
we reviewed the complaints and the requests for further review filed with our office. We also 
spoke with the Commission's legal counsel by telephone on January 8, 10, and 29, 2018.1 
We find the facts as follows. On October 12, 2017, the complainant sent an e-mail to the 
Commission as well as the town clerk, requesting, "all meeting notices, open session minutes 
and executive session minutes for the [Commission] for the years 2015, 2016 and 2017, whether 
accepted or draft. I DO NOT WISH TO PURCHASE COPIES OF THESE DOCUMENTS, 
ONLY TO SIT DOWN AT A TABLE OR DESK AND REVIEW THEM." (Emphasis in

DISCUSSION 
I. The Commission Failed to Respond to a Request for Meeting Minutes. 
Public bodies must create and maintain accurate minutes of all meetings. G.L. c. 30A, § 
22(a). The minutes of an open session, if they exist and whether approved or in draft form, shall 
be made available upon request by any person within 10 days. G.L. c. 30A, § 22(c). 
A public body must respond to a request for executive session minutes within 10 days. 
G.L. c. 30A, § 22(g)(2). If the public body or a designee has determined, prior to the request, 
that certain executive session minutes may be released, it should respond to the requester 
directly, notifying him or her of the availability of those minutes. See OML 2013-105; OML 
2013-99.2 And if, at the time of a request, the public body has not conducted a review of the 
minutes to determine whether continued nondisclosure is warranted, the body must perform such 
a review and release the minutes, if appropriate, no later than its next meeting or within 30 days, 
whichever occurs first. G.L. c. 30A, § 22(g)(2). In such circumstances, the body must still

Here, the complainant requested to review meeting minutes in an October 12, 2017 email, 
and the Commission failed to respond within ten days. While it appears that the Commission 
referred the request to town counsel, and town counsel provided his analysis to the town clerk, 
no information was given to the complainant until November 7, 2017, and no formal responses 
came until February 2, 2018. Because the Commission did not respond to the October 12, 2017 
request within ten days, we find that the Commission violated the Open Meeting Law. See OML 
2017-159.3 The Commission eventually responded to the request, stating that "minutes will be 
made available at the Town Clerk's office at a time and date convenient to all," although it does 
not appear that such an appointment has yet taken place. 
We must determine whether this violation was, as the complainant urges, an intentional 
one. G.L. c. 30A, § 23(c). An intentional violation is an "act or omission by a public body or a 
member thereof, in knowing violation of [the Open Meeting Law]." 940 CMR 29.02. An 
intentional violation may be found where the public body has previously been advised that 
certain conduct violates the Open Meeting Law. Id. Here, the Commission was clearly advised 
in a determination issued by our office on September 27, 2017 that a failure to respond to a 
request for minutes within the statutory time frame violated the Open Meeting Law. See OML 
2017-153.4 Because the Commission had been notified of its obligations prior to the events 
addressed in this letter, we find that this violation was intentional. While it is a defense to the 
imposition of a penalty that a public body acted in good faith reliance on the advice of legal 
counsel, see G.L. c. 30A, § 23(g), that is not what occurred here. Town counsel provided advice 
to the town clerk, but did not advise the Town Clerk not to respond to the complaint, which is 
what occurred. 
11. The Commission Failed to Respond to an Open Meeting Law Complaint. 
Within 14 business days after receiving the complaint, unless an extension has been 
granted by the Attorney General, the public body shall meet to review the complaint's 
allegations; take remedial action, if appropriate; and send to the complainant a response and a 
description of any remedial action taken. 940 CMR 29.05(5). Here, the Commission failed to 
respond to the October 23, 2017 complaint. It was only on February 2, 2018, after being 
contacted by our office, that the Commission responded to both complaints. This failure to 
respond to an Open Meeting Law complaint within fourteen business days violated the Open 
Meeting Law. See OML 2017-171.

CONCLUSION 
For the reasons stated above, we find that the Commission violated the Open Meeting 
Law. We find that the Commission's failure to respond to the complainant's request for meeting 
minutes was intentional. While we may recommend the imposition of a monetary penalty, see 
940 CMR 29.07(3)(d), such a remedy is not appropriate here, where the Commission referred the 
request to town counsel, understanding that the request would be resolved by either town counsel 
or the town clerk responding on the Commission's behalf. Despite this understanding, the 
Commission is responsible for ensuring that a timely response is provided to all requests for 
meeting minutes. We therefore order the Commission, within thirty (30) days of receipt of this 
letter, to review the Attorney General's training video on Meeting Minutes. Upon completion of 
this task, the Commission must notify our office of compliance in writing, pursuant to 940 CMR 
29.07(4).5 With respect to the Commission's failure to respond to an Open Meeting Law 
complaint, we order the Commission's immediate and future compliance with the law, and we 
caution that similar future violations may be considered evidence of intent to violate the law. 
This determination does not address any other complaints that may be pending with our 
office or the Commission. Please feel free to contact the Division at (617) 963 - 2540 if you 
have any questions.
