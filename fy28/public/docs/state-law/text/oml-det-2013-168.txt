This office received a complaint from Suzanne Corbett dated March 8, 2013, alleging 
that the Ashfield Board of Health (the "Board") violated the Open Meeting Law, G.L. c. 3 OA, §§ 
18-25. Ms. Corbett's complaint was originally filed with the Board on or about January 3, 2013, 
and the Board responded by letter dated January 14, 2013. In her complaint, Ms. Corbett alleges 
that for its meetings held on December 4, 2012 and December 11, 2012, the Board failed to list 
topics on its notices with sufficient specificity and changed its normal meeting time from 
evening to afternoon.1 
We reviewed the January 3, 2013 complaint and supplemental material submitted 
therewith; the Board's January 14, 2013 response; and the March 8, 2013 complaint filed with 
our office, requesting further review. We also reviewed the Board's notices and minutes from its 
December 4, 2012 and December 11, 2012 meetings. Finally, we spoke by telephone with Chair 
Duncan Colter. 
Following our review, we find that the Board violated the Open Meeting Law by failing 
to include sufficient detail about anticipated discussion topics in its meeting notices. However,

FACTS 
Based upon our review of the material listed above, the facts are as follows. During its 
December 4, 2012 and December 11, 2012 meetings, the Board planned to discuss a $2,500 
grant from the Food and Drug Administration (the "FDA") to be used for food service 
inspections (the "grant"). The Franklin Regional County of Governments' Health Agent, Glen 
Ayres, who had submitted the application for the grant, attended both meetings. The December 
4, 2012 meeting notice, posted on November 29, 2012, listed the following topics for discussion: 
"Health Agent Reports; Inspection Reports; Minutes of 11/27/12; FDA Grant- Glen Ayres; Mail; 
Old Business; New Business; Public Comments- if time allows; Executive Session- if needed." 
During its December 4, 2012 meeting, Mr. Ayres responded to questions about the use of the 
grant and the process for holding a public hearing to discuss the grant, which the Board 
scheduled for December 11, 2012. 
On December 11, 2012, the Board convened a meeting, opened a public hearing and 
engaged in a lengthy discussion about the grant. The Board's notice, posted on December 6, 
2012, listed the following topics for discussion; "Public Hearing for FDA- BOH Policy Grant, 
Old Business, New Business." According to the meeting minutes, the Board then voted to close 
the hearing before turning to other Board business under the headings, "Old Business" and "New 
Business." 
DISCUSSION 
The Open Meeting Law requires that all meeting notices "shall be printed in a legible, 
easily understandable format and shall contain the date, time, and place of such meeting and a 
listing of topics that the chair reasonably anticipates will be discussed at the meeting." G.L. c. 
30A, § 20(b); 940 CMR 29.03. The Open Meeting Law does not require that the chair of a 
public body guess what will occur during a meeting, but the chair does need to list those topics 
that he or she reasonably anticipates will be discussed. See OML 2011-36; OML 2011-23; OML 
2011-13.3 We generally consider a topic to include sufficient specificity when a reasonable 
member of the public could read the topic and understand the anticipated nature of the public 
body's discussion. See OML 2013-152. 
The topics, "FDA Grant- Glen Ayres" and "Public Hearing for FDA- BOH Policy Grant" 
were sufficiently specific to describe the topic to be discussed. However, we find that several 
recurring topics on the Board's December 4, 2012 and December 11, 2012 meeting notices, 
including "New Business," "Old Business," and "Executive Session- if needed" were 
insufficiently detailed for purposes of the Open Meeting Law, as discussed in OML 2013-14 and 
OML 2013-164. We appreciate that Chair Colter generally lists his name and telephone number 
on each notice in order to respond to any inquiries about the meeting or its notice, but the Law

requires public bodies to list topics in a meeting notice with "sufficient specificity to reasonably

Additionally, the complainant alleges the Board changed its normal meeting time and 
held its December 4, 2012 and December 11, 2012 meetings at times that are difficult for 
members of the public to attend. While we encourage the Board to meet at times that are 
convenient for both the public and members of the Board, we find that the Board did not violate 
the Open Meeting Law by holding these two meetings at a time other than its customary meeting 
time. The Board complied with the Open Meeting Law by accurately noting the time of these 
meetings in its meeting notices. See OML 2012-99. 
CONCLUSION 
For the reasons stated above, we find that the Board violated the Open Meeting Law by 
failing to include sufficient detail about anticipated discussion topics in its meeting notices. We 
order immediate and future compliance with the Open Meeting Law, and caution that future 
similar conduct may be considered evidence of intent to violate the Law. 
We now consider the complaints addressed by this determination to be resolved. This 
determination does not address any other complaints which may be pending with our office or
