This office received a complaint from Patrick Higgins on January 18, 2024, alleging that 
the Dartmouth Board of Public Works (the “Board”) violated the Open Meeting Law, G.L. c. 
30A, §§ 18-25.  The complaint was originally filed with the Board on January 10, 2024, and 
Timothy J. Barber, Director of Public Works, responded, on behalf of the Board, by letter dated 
January 18, 2024.  The complaint alleges that the Board violated the Open Meeting Law by 
failing to announce that the meeting held on December 21, 2023, was being recorded; failed to 
conduct roll call votes during that meeting; and created insufficient minutes of the meeting held 
on December 21, 2023.   
 
We resolve this matter by informal action, in accordance with 940 CMR 29.07(2)(a), 
after reviewing the original complaint, the Board’s response to the complaint, the complaint filed 
with our office requesting further review, and the notices and video recordings of the Board 
meetings held on December 21, 2023, and January 18, 2024.1  We find that the Board violated 
the Open Meeting Law by failing to announce that the meeting held on December 21, 2023, was

The Board held a meeting on December 21, 2023, in which all three members 
participated remotely.  Because Board members participated remotely during the December 21, 
2023, meeting, all votes taken during that meeting must be by roll call vote, even unanimous 
votes.  See 940 CMR 29.10(7)(c); OML 2016-132; OML 2015-145.

Board failed to conduct roll call votes during the meeting, we find that the Board violated the 
Open Meeting Law.   
 
In addition, the December 21, 2023, meeting was recorded and broadcast by Dartmouth 
Community Media and therefore the Chair was required to inform attendees that the meeting was 
being recorded.  See OML 2023-74; OML 2012-24.  The Open Meeting Law requires that 
“[a]fter notifying the chair of the public body, any person may make a video or audio recording 
of an open session of a meeting of a public body, or may transmit the meeting through any 
medium, subject to reasonable requirements of the chair as to the number, placement and 
operation of equipment used so as not to interfere with the conduct of the meeting.  At the 
beginning of the meeting, the chair shall inform other attendees of any recordings.” G.L. c. 30A, 
§ 20(f).  The requirement that the chair inform attendees of any recording includes any recording 
made by members of the public body itself, including those made for public broadcasting or 
administrative purposes, such as assisting in the drafting of meeting minutes.  See OML 2023-
111; OML 2019- 129; OML 2016-155; OML 2013-136.  Because the Chair did not make such 
an announcement, we find that the Board violated the Open Meeting Law.  We commend the 
Board’s commitment to ensure future compliance. 
 
Finally, the complaint alleges that the minutes of the December 21, 2023, meeting are 
insufficient where they do not include a list of documents or exhibits used by the Board during 
the meeting.  The Open Meeting Law requires that a public body “create and maintain accurate 
minutes of all meetings, including executive sessions, setting forth the date, time and place, the 
members present or absent, a summary of the discussions on each subject, a list of documents 
and other exhibits used at the meeting, the decisions made and the actions taken at each meeting, 
including the record of all votes.”  G.L. c. 30A, § 22(a).  At the time the complaint at issue here 
was filed, the minutes of the December 21, 2023, meeting had not been approved and were in 
draft form.  A public body may revise draft minutes at any point prior to their approval to correct 
inaccuracies or add detail.  See OML 2014-62.  Accordingly, because these minutes remained in 
draft form at the time the complaint was filed, we decline to review their sufficiency.  See OML 
2019-11; OML Declination 12-18-20 (Fall River Housing Authority Board of Commissioners).  
 
For the reasons stated above, we find that the Board violated the Open Meeting Law by 
failing to conduct roll call votes and failing to announce that the meeting was being recorded.  
We order the Board’s immediate and future compliance with the law’s requirements, and we 
caution that similar future violations could be considered evidence of intent to violate the law.
