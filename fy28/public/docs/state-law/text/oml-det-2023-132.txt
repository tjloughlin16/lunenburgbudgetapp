 RE: Open Meeting Law Complaint 
 
Dear Ms. Metcalf: 
 
This office received a complaint from Patrick Higgins on May 26, 2023, alleging that the 
Foxborough Select Board (the “Board”) violated the Open Meeting Law, G.L. c. 30A, §§ 18-25. 
The complaint was originally filed with the Board on May 9, 2023, and John Coderre, Former 
Town Manager, responded to the complaint, on behalf of the Board, by letter dated May 24, 
2023.
1 The complaint alleges that the Board failed to approve minutes of the meeting held on 
March 28 in a timely manner. 
 
We resolve this matter by informal action, in ac cordance with 940 CMR 29.07(2)(a), 
after reviewing the original complaint, the Board’s response to the complaint, and the complaint 
filed with our office requesting further review. In addition, we reviewed the notices and minutes 
of Board meetings held on March 28, April 11, April 25, and May 9, the notice of a Board 
meeting to be held on May 8, and the notices and minutes of the Foxborough Advisory 
Committee meetings held on March 29, April 5, and April 12. Finally, we communicated with 
you on August 9. We find that the Board did not violate the Open Meeting Law in the way 
alleged. 
 
The O
pen Meeting Law requires that a public body “create and maintain accurate 
minutes of all meetings, including executive sessions, setting forth the date, time and place, the 
members present or absent, a summary of the discussions on each subject, a list of documents 
and other exhibits used at the meeting, the decisions made, and the actions taken at each meeting, 
including the record of all votes.” G.L. c. 30A, § 22(a). The meeting minutes must be reviewed

The com
plaint suggests that the Board met on May 8 prior to Town Meeting and held 
joint meetings with the Advisory Committee on March 29, April 5, and April 12, such that these 
four meetings should count for the purpose of calculating timely approval of the March 28 
meeting minutes. The Board maintains that it only held meetings on March 28, April 11, April 
25, and May 9. During the May 9 meeting, the Board approved the minutes of the March 28 
meeting. Thus, the Board contends that the March 28 meetings minutes were approved in a 
timely manner where the minutes were approved within three meetings. 
 
The Open Meeting Law defines “meeting” as “a deliberation by a public body with 
respe
ct to any matter within the body’s jurisdiction.” G.L. c. 30A, § 18. The Open Meeting Law 
does not include an exception to the definition of “meeting” for meetings that are held jointly by 
two or more public bodies. See OML 2023-129; OML 2022-144. Moreover, the Open Meeting 
Law and regulations do not distinguish between “regular” meetings and other types of meetings 
such as workshops or public hearings, for purposes of timely approval of minutes. See OML 
2017-103 (public body should have created complete minutes for its “workshop” meetings 
because topics discussed were matters within the public body's jurisdiction); OML 2019-115 (no 
exception for “single topic” meetings or meetings not “normally scheduled”). 
 
We ackno
wledge that the Board posted notice for a meeting to be held on May 8 prior to 
Town Meeting to “Review/Act/Amend or Take Position on any Town Meeting Article(s),” but 
that meeting was cancelled. Thus, we find that the Board did not meet on May 8. We also note 
that the Board posted notices of meetings to be held on March 29, April 5, and April 12 for the 
purpose of attending Advisory Committee meetings. The Board asserts, and we find no evidence 
to the contrary, that a quorum of the Board did not attend any of those three meetings. Therefore, 
we find that the Board did not hold joint meetings with the Advisory Committee on March 29, 
April 5, or April 12. Since the Board only held meetings on March 28, April 11, April 25, and 
May 9, the minutes of the Board’s March 28 meeting were approved in a timely manner when 
those minutes were approved on May 9, the third consecutive Board meeting.
