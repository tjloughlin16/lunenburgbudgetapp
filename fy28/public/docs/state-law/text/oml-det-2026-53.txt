Following our review, we find that the Commission did not violate the Open Meeting 
Law in the way alleged. In reaching this determination, we reviewed the original complaint, 
the Commission’s response to the complaint, and the request for further review filed with our 
office. We also reviewed the notice, minutes and a video recording4 of the Commission meeting 
held on August 13. 
 
FACTS 
 
We find the facts as follows. On August 11, the Commission posted notice of a meeting 
to be held on August 13 at 7:00 p.m. The notice included several topics for discussion but made 
no reference to the Property.  
 
On August 13, prior to the meeting, the Town’s Land Use Coordinator, Thomas Fallon, 
received a phone call from a representative for the Property sharing updates regarding the 
Property.  
 
The August 13 meeting was held as scheduled. During the meeting, Mr. Fallon provided 
the Commission with updates on several properties that were included in the meeting notice.  
Following these updates, under a section of the meeting identified in the minutes as “New 
Business Unforeseen by the Chair,” the Commission Chair Michael Garland prompted Mr. 
Fallon: “I have you marked down on my agenda for tonight [to present on] 47, 49 Rockland 
Road.” Mr. Fallon then gave a brief update to the Commission regarding the Property.  
 
DISCUSSION 
 
The Open Meeting Law requires that, except in an emergency, “a public body shall post 
notice of every meeting at least 48 hours prior to such meeting, excluding Saturdays, Sundays 
and legal holidays.” G.L. c. 30A, § 20(b). Meeting notices must include, among other things, a 
list of the topics that the chair, and any other individual authorized to create the notice, 
reasonably anticipates 48 hours (excluding weekends and legal holidays) in advance of the 
meeting will be discussed at the meeting. See G.L. c. 30A, § 20(b); OML 2024-101; OML 2022-
206. A public body may discuss topics that were not included on the meeting notice if those 
topics were not reasonably anticipated 48 hours (excluding weekends and legal holidays) in 
advance of the meeting. See OML 2025-101; OML 2021–131; OML 2018-119. Although not 
required, the Attorney General’s Office recommends that public bodies, as a matter of best 
practice, table discussion of unanticipated topics that might be of significant interest to the public 
in order to include such topics on a meeting notice for a subsequent meeting if the topic can wait 
until a subsequent meeting. See OML 2019-131; OML 2018-119. 
 
The Commission contends that it did not violate the Open Meeting Law because Mr. 
Fallon’s August 13 update regarding the Property was unanticipated where he received a phone 
call from the applicant’s representative shortly before the August 13 meeting. Absent sufficient 
evidence to the contrary, we credit the Commission’s account of the facts and find that the topic
