 RE: Open Meeting Law Complaint 
 
Dear Attorney Maser: 
 
This office received a complaint from Edward Davison on May 18, 2021,1 alleging that 
the Belmont Fire Chief Screening Committee (the “Committee”) violated the Open Meeting 
Law, G.L. c. 30A, §§ 18-25. The complaint was originally filed with the Town of Belmont (the 
“Town”) on March 12. Because the Committee completed its work and disbanded on or about 
March 10, the Belmont Select Board (the “Board”) responded to Mr. Davison’s complaint by 
letter dated March 30. The complaint alleges that on January 6 the Committee improperly 
discussed interview questions while in executive session under G.L. c. 30A, § 21(a)(8) (“Purpose 
8”).   
 
Following our review, we find that the Committee violated the Open Meeting Law as 
alleged. In reaching this determination, we reviewed the Open Meeting Law complaint, the 
Board’s response, the request for further review, and the minutes for the Committee’s executive 
session held on January 6. 
 
The Open Meeting Law permits a public body to enter executive session for any of ten 
enumerated purposes. G.L. c. 30A, § 21(a). These ten exceptions to the general rule that 
meetings of a public body shall be conducted in the open are narrowly construed. See McRea v. 
Flaherty, 71 Mass. App. Ct. 637, 641 (2008). One permissible basis for holding an executive

session is “[t]o consider or interview applicants for employment or appointment by a preliminary 
screening committee if the chair declares that an open meeting will have a detrimental effect in 
obtaining qualified applicants . . . .” G.L. c. 30A, § 21(a)(8). 
 
On January 6 the Committee met in executive session and discussed interview questions 
as well as the schedule and process for conducting interviews for a new fire chief. In its response 
the Board acknowledges that during the January 6 executive session the Committee “discussed 
handouts distributed by its consultant, Municipal Resources, Inc. (MRI) and the questions that 
the Committee would ask the applicants for the position of Fire Chief.” However, the Board 
asserts that “the clear majority of the deliberations in the January 6, 2021 executive session were 
appropriate under Purpose 8.” We disagree.  
 
Based on our review of the January 6 executive session minutes, no part of the 
discussions held on that day were appropriate for executive session under Purpose 8 or any other 
executive session purpose. The Open Meeting Law allows public bodies to convene in executive 
session under Purpose 8 in order to undertake two activities, to consider or interview applicants. 
G.L. c. 30A, § 21(a)(8); OML 2016-105.2 We have consistently found that the review of 
interview questions is not appropriate for executive session. See OML 2021-70; OML 2019-7; 
OML 2016-105. Therefore, we find that the Committee violated the Open Meeting Law when it 
discussed interview questions, schedule, and process in executive session on January 6. It is our 
understanding that the minutes of the Committee’s January 6 executive session have been 
released to the public; therefore, we do not order any additional remedial action.  
 
We now consider the complaint addressed by this determination to be resolved. This 
determination does not address any other complaints that may be pending. Please feel free to 
contact our office at (617) 963-2540 if you have any questions regarding this letter.
