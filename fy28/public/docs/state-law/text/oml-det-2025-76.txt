 RE: Open Meeting Law Complaint 
 
Dear Thomas M. Rodrigues, Jr.: 
 
This office received a complaint from Patrick Higgins on April 4, 2025, alleging that the 
Swansea School Building Subcommittee (the “Committee”) violated the Open Meeting Law, 
G.L. c.30A §§ 18-25. The complaint was originally filed with the Committee on March 21, 2025, 
and you responded on behalf of the Committee by letter dated April 3, 2025. The complaint 
alleges that the Committee failed to timely approve minutes of a meeting held on May 16, 2024.  
 
We resolve this matter by informal action, in accordance with 940 CMR 29.07(a), and 
find that the Committee violated the Open Meeting Law in the way alleged. In reaching this 
determination, we reviewed the Open Meeting Law complaint, the Committee’s response, the 
request for further review filed with our office, and the “Agendas and Minutes” folders posted on 
the Swansea Public Schools website.
1 
 
The Open Meeting Law requires that a public body create and maintain accurate minutes 
of all meetings and that those minutes be approved in a timely manner. G.L. c. 30A § 22. 
“Timely manner” means “within the next three public body meetings or within 30 days,

whichever is later, unless the public body can show good cause for delay.” 940 CMR 29.11(2). 
Whenever possible, we recommend that minutes of a meeting be approved at the following 
meeting. See OML 2023-33.
2  
 
The Committee held meetings on May 16, 2024, July 31, 2024, September 30, 2024, 
January 29, 2025, and March 20, 2025. For the May 16, 2024, meeting minutes to be timely 
approved, the Committee was required to approve the minutes by January 29, 2025, which was 
the third meeting thereafter. However, the Committee acknowledges, and we agree, that it failed 
to timely approve the minutes of the meeting held on May 16, 2024, where it did not approve the 
minutes until its March 20, 2025, meeting, which was the fourth meeting thereafter. As such, we 
find that the Committee violated the Open Meeting Law by failing to approve these minutes in a 
timely manner.

We note that at the time the complaint was filed on March 21, 2025, the Committee had 
approved the May 16, 2024, meeting minutes.
4 In instances where a public body acknowledges a 
violation and all appropriate corrective action has been taken, we urge complainants not to 
consume scarce state resources by requesting review where the transparency purpose of the 
complaint has already been addressed by the corrective action taken. See OML 2012-46; OML 
2017-191; OML 2023-113; OML 2024-20; OML 2024-224. Indeed, the Open Meeting Law 
complaint process is designed to allow public bodies to take corrective action and address 
concerns raised in a complaint without this office’s involvement. See G.L. c. 30A, § 23(b) (“At 
least 30 days prior to the filing of a complaint with the attorney general, the complainant shall 
file a written complaint with the public body, setting forth the circumstances which constitute the 
alleged violation and giving the body an opportunity to remedy the alleged violation.”). 
 
For the reasons stated above, we find that the Committee violated the Open Meeting Law 
by failing to approve the minutes of its May 16, 2024, meeting in a timely manner. We order the 
Committee’s immediate and future compliance with the Open Meeting Law, and we caution that 
similar future violations could be considered evidence of intent to violate the law.
