RE: Open Meeting Law Complaint 
Dear Attorney Corbo: 
This office received a complaint from Jeanne Golrick, dated February 12, 2014, alleging 
that the Montague Board of Selectmen (the "Board") violated the Open Meeting Law, G.L. c. 
30A, §§ 18-25. Specifically, the complaint alleges that on November 13, 2013, the Board held 
an executive session meeting without posting proper notice. The complaint was originally filed 
with the Board on November 27, 2013. The Board responded to the complaint through its 
attorney by letter dated December 19, 2013.1 
We appreciate the patience of the parties while we considered this matter. Following our 
review, we find that the Board violated the Open Meeting Law by failing to properly post notice 
for its November 13, 2013 executive session meeting. In reaching our determination, we 
reviewed the November 27, 2013 complaint filed with the Board; the Board's December 19, 
2013 response; and the February 12, 2014 complaint filed with our office. We also reviewed the 
notice and amended notice for a joint meeting of the Board and the Town Finance Committee 
held on November 13, 2013, and the executive session minutes of the Board meeting held the 
same day. Finally, we reviewed the minutes of the Board's December 9, 2013 meeting. 
FACTS 
On November 12, 2013, Montague Town Counsel informed the Town Administrator of 
an immediate need to hold a Board meeting in executive session to discuss litigation strategy. At 
that time, the Board was involved in a litigation matter. The Board had previously posted notice 
for a joint meeting with the Finance Committee, to be held at 6:00 PM on November 13, 2013.

The need for the executive session discussion arose within 48 hours of this posted meeting, and 
was not anticipated at the time of the original posting. 
Prior to the joint meeting on November 13, 2013, the Town Administrator learned that 
two of the three Selectmen were unable to remain at the joint meeting past 7:00 PM. The Town 
Administrator, therefore, attempted to schedule the Board's executive session for 8:00 PM, an 
hour after the joint meeting would be adjourned, when the two Board members would be able to 
return. The Town Administrator prepared an amendment to the joint meeting notice on 
November 12, 2013. The amendment was posted on the Town's website on November 12, 2013, 
but it was not sent to the Town Clerk for posting on the Town Hall bulletin board, which is the 
official posting location for the Town of Montague. The amendment read: 
8;00PM BOARD OF SELECTMEN, EXECUTIVE SESSION: under G.L. c. 
30A, §21(a)(3) to discuss strategy with respect to litigation. Town v. Conley eviction 
matter currently in the Western Division of the Housing Court. This was not anticipated 
within the 48 hour posting requirements. 
The Board met with the Committee as noticed from 6:00 PM to 7:00 PM, adjourned, and 
then met again at 8:00 PM in open session to vote to enter into executive session. Because the 
minutes of that executive session have not been publicly released, we do not recount their 
content in detail here. However, it is clear from our in camera review of the minutes that the 
Board discussed the Conley matter. 
On December 9, 2013, the Board met again in open session and discussed the present 
Open Meeting Law complaint. During the meeting, the Town Administrator explained to the 
Board that he had prepared the amendment adding the executive session to the notice for the 
November 13, 2013 meeting, and had posted the notice to the Town's website but inadvertently 
failed to send the notice to the Town Clerk. The Town Administrator then asked the Board to 
ratify its vote to enter executive session on November 13, 2013. The Board then voted 
unanimously to: 
ratify the decision of the Board of Selectmen to enter into an Executive Session on 
November 13, 2013 for the following purpose under G.L. c. 30A, § 21(a)(3) to discuss 
strategy with respect to litigation. Town v. Conley eviction matter currently in the 
Western Division of the Housing Court because the matter in open session would have a 
detrimental effect on the Town's litigating position. 
DISCUSSION 
The Open Meeting Law requires that "[ejxcept in an emergency, in addition to any notice 
otherwise required by law, a public body shall post notice of every meeting at least 48 hours 
prior to such meeting excluding Saturdays, Sundays and legal holidays." G.L. c. 30A, § 20(b). 
For meetings of a local public body, notice must be filed with the municipal clerk and posted in a 
manner conspicuously visible to the public at all hours in or on the municipal building in which 
the clerk's office is located. G.L. c. 30A, § 20(c). Alternatively, the municipality may adopt an 
alternative notice posting method, such as posting to the town's website. 940 CMR 29.03(2)(b).

The Board failed to post proper notice for its 8:00 PM meeting on November 13, 2013. 
Notice was posted for a 6:00 PM joint meeting with the Finance Committee, however there was 
a delay of a full hour between the conclusion of that meeting and the start of the Board's 
executive session meeting. While there may be circumstances where an hour-long recess of a 
meeting may be appropriate, several factors here lead us to conclude that the Board's 8:00 PM 
executive session was a separate meeting and not merely a continuation of the joint meeting with 
the addition of an unanticipated topic. In addition to the significant period of time between the 
two meetings, only one of the two bodies involved with the joint meeting planned to participate 
in the executive session, and the topic discussed during the executive session was unrelated to 
matters discussed during the earlier meeting. We therefore find that this was a separate meeting, 
and it was not sufficient notice for the Board to simply add the executive session as a topic listed 
in the previously posted joint meeting notice. G.L. c. 30A, § 20(b). Because the Board failed to 
post notice for the 8:00 PM meeting in the official posting location for the Town, 48 hours in 
advance of the meeting, we find that it violated the Open Meeting Law. 
The Board publicly ratified its November 13, 2013 vote to enter into executive session at 
its properly posted December 9, 2013 meeting. In its response to the complaint, the Board 
acknowledged that the manner in which the executive session meeting was posted did not meet 
the requirements of the Open Meeting Law. The Board states that it will continue to work 
diligently to ensure that future notices are properly posted so as to meet the requirements of the 
Open Meeting Law. We commend the Board for its efforts to take remedial action to address its 
violation of the Open Meeting Law and for its commitment to future compliance. 
We find that the Board violated the Open Meeting Law by failing to properly post its 
November 13, 2013 executive session meeting. We order the Board's immediate and future 
compliance, and caution the Board that a similar future violation may be considered evidence of 
an intentional violation of the Law. 
We now consider this matter closed. If you have any questions regarding this letter, 
please do not hesitate to contact our office at (617) 963-2540.
