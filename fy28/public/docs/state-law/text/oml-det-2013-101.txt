RE: Open Meeting Law Complaint 
Dear Attorney Goldberg: 
This office received an Open Meeting Law complaint from Bernard Brahm on September 
24, 2012, alleging that the Provincetown Board of Selectmen (the "Board") violated the Open 
Meeting Law, G.L. c. 30A, §§ 18-25. The complaint specifically alleges that the notice posted 
for the Board's June 11, 2012 and June 25, 2012 meetings failed to state the specific topic of 
discussion, listing "general review of the status of pending litigation matters" without revealing 
the specific litigation matters to be discussed. The complaint was originally filed with the Board 
on July 6, 2012. The Board responded by letter, through counsel, on August 15, 2012, following 
an extension of time to respond granted by this office. 
After reviewing the complaint and the Board's response, we have decided to resolve this 
complaint by informal action, in accordance with 940 CMR 29.07(2)(a). We find that the 
Board violated the Open Meeting Law by failing to include sufficient detail about its executive 
session topic in the notice for its June 11, 2012 meeting. See G.L. c. 30A, § 20(b); OML 2012-
118; 2012-55.1 As this violation occurred prior to the issuance of our decision in OML 2013-65, 
we do not find it to be an intentional violation. Additionally, we find that the notice for the 
Board's June 25, 2012 meeting was sufficient because the Board discussed imminent litigation 
during that meeting, and revealing the specific matter could have been detrimental to the Board's 
litigating position. See G.L. c. 30A, § 21(a)(3). 
The Board acknowledged its error with respect to the June 11, 2012 meeting notice in its 
August 15, 2012 response to the complaint. Furthermore, during its August 13, 2012 meeting,
