RE: Open Meeting Law Complaint
Dear Attorney Macy:
This office received a complaint from Patrick Higgins on September 18, 2018, 
alleging that the Fall River City Council Committee on Finance (the “Committee”) violated 
the Open Meeting Faw, G.L. c. 30A, §§ 18-25. The complaint was originally with the 
Committee on or about August 15, 2018, and you responded to the complaint, on behalf of the 
Committee, in a letter dated September 18, 2018. In his complaint, Mr. Higgins alleges that 
the Committee failed to approve certain meeting minutes in a timely manner.1
We resolve this complaint by informal action in accordance with 940 CMR 
29.07(2)(a), after reviewing the original complaint, the Committee’s response to the 
complaint, and the complaint filed with our office requesting further review. We find that the 
Committee violated the Open Meeting Law in the manner alleged.
The Open Meeting Law requires that a public body “create and maintain accurate 
minutes of all meetings, including executive sessions, setting forth the date, time and place, 
the members present or absent, a summary of the discussions on each subject, a list of 
documents and other exhibits used at the meeting, the decisions made and the actions taken at 
each meeting, including the record of all votes.” G.L. c. 30A, § 22(a). The meeting minutes 
must be reviewed and approved in a timely manner. G.L. c. 30A, § 22(c). The Open Meeting 
Law itself does not define “timely manner.” However, the Attorney General’s Open Meeting 
Law regulations provide that “timely manner” means “within the next three public body 
meetings or within 30 days, whichever is later, unless the public body can show good cause

for further delay.” 940 CMR 29.11(2). Whenever possible, we recommend that minutes of a 
meeting be approved at the next meeting. See OML 2014-15; OML 2012-91.2
The complaint alleges that the Committee failed to timely approve the minutes for 
meetings held on September 26, 2017; December 19, 2017; and June 26, 2018. In this 
instance, the Committee was required to approve its meeting minutes by the third meeting 
after each meeting, as that was the later of the two time periods. See 940 CMR 29.11; OML 
2018-111. Because the Committee reviewed and approved the September 26 and December 
19, 2017, meeting minutes on August 15, 2018, despite having met more than three times in 
the interim, it did not approve them in a timely manner.3 However, we find no violation with 
respect to the Committee’s June 26, 2018, meeting minutes, which were timely approved, as 
the August 15 meeting was the second meeting thereafter.
For the reasons stated above, we find that the Committee violated the Open Meeting 
Law. We order the Committee’s immediate and future compliance with the Open Meeting 
Law, and we caution that similar future violations may be considered evidence of intent to 
violate the law. We now consider the complaint addressed by this determination to be 
resolved. This determination does not address any other complaints that may be pending with 
the Committee or with our office. Please feel free to contact our office at (617) 963-2540 if 
you have any questions regarding this letter.
