The Open Meeting Law mand
 ates that, unless a lawful executive session has been 
convened, “all meetings of a public body shall be open to the public.”  G.L. c. 30A, § 20(a).  
Access must include the opportunity to be physically present for a meeting that is held in person 
as well as to see and hear what is being discussed by the members of the public body.  See OML 
2014-3; OML 2013-189; OML 2012-66.4  For a meeting to be truly “open to the public,” it must 
be held at a location that is accessible to persons with disabilities.  See G.L. c. 30A, § 20(a); 
OML 2025-3; OML 2023-43; OML 2015-38; OML 2012-49 (meetings of public bodies must be 
held in locations that are compliant with the Americans with Disabilities Act (“ADA”)).  Public 
bodies must hold public meetings in locations where both the building and the meeting room are 
accessible.  See 28 C.F.R. § 35.150 (2011); Dep’t of Justice, Americans with Disabilities Act: 
Title II Technical Assistance Manual, at 5.1000 (1993); see also OML 2019-90.  We have 
consistently explained that meetings of public bodies that are held in person must be held in 
locations that are compliant with the ADA and state law, even in the absence of any particular 
request or known accessibility need.  See, e.g., OML 2024-130 (explaining that public body 
violated the Open Meeting Law when meeting was held in a non-ADA compliant location, even 
though there is no evidence any person was unable to attend as a result); OML 2025-3 (same); 
OML 2023-43 (same).
5  
 
The Board does not dispute that the elevator was out of service during the March 12 
meeting and that without a working elevator the meeting location was inaccessible to individuals 
with mobility impairments.  The Board also acknowledges that the elevator was out of service 
during several other periods of time in the preceding months.  Instead, the Board explains that to 
address the unreliable elevator service, the Board began offering both in-person and Zoom access 
to its meetings.  The Board has since acknowledged—and we reiterate—that offering a Zoom 
option for a meeting that is otherwise held in person does not obviate the need to hold meetings 
in ADA-compliant locations.   
 
The complaint also alleges that the February 26 meeting was inaccessible because of a 
lack of compliant parking at Town Hall.  On this point, we recommended that the complainant 
pursue a complaint with the Massachusetts Architectural Access Board (AAB) and we advised 
that we would defer to the judgment of the AAB.  On September 23, the AAB issued its decision 
finding violations of Massachusetts building accessibility requirements both as to the elevator 
and the parking at Town Hall.  Therefore, based on that decision, we find that the February 26 
meeting also was insufficiently accessible in violation of the Open Meeting Law.

Finally, the complainant asserts that because of the accessibility challenges he 
experien
ced when trying to attend the February 26 and March 12 meetings, he missed or was 
unable to fully participate in public comment during the meetings.  We do not find an additional 
violation of the Open Meeting Law on these grounds.  The Open Meeting Law does not require 
that a public body allow public participation, but rather gives the chair of the public body 
discretion to decide whether to permit public comment during a meeting. See G.L. c. 30A, 
§ 20(g) (“[n]o person shall address a meeting of a public body without permission of the chair,
and all persons shall, at the request of the chair, be silent.”); see also OML 2020-77; OML 2017-
189. Therefore, concerns regarding restrictions or limitations  on public comment do not
constitute violations of the Open Meeting Law.
For the re
asons stated above, we find that the Board violated the Open Meeting Law by 
holding meetings on February 26 and March 12 that were insufficiently accessible to the public.  
We order the Board’s immediate and future compliance with the Open Meeting Law and caution 
that similar future violations may be considered evidence of an intent to violate the Law.   
We now consider the complaint addressed by this determination to be resolved.  This 
determination does not address any other complaints that may be pending with the Board or with 
our office.  Please feel free to contact our office at (617) 963-2540 if you have any questions
