violation with respect to the other allegations listed in the complaint. In reaching a

FACTS 
We find the facts as follows. The Board held meetings on September 22, 2015; 
October 13, 2015; October 27, 2015; November 10, 2015; November 17, 2015; December 15, 
2015; and January 26, 2016. Following each meeting, draft minutes were distributed to the 
Board members in preparation for approval at upcoming meetings. The Board discussed 
those draft minutes during several meetings, but was unable to reach an agreement and thus 
did not approve most of them until February 9, 2016.6 The Board attempted to approve its 
November 10, 2015 meeting minutes during this meeting, but decided to "table" the motion. 
The Board tried to approve them again during its April 13, 2016 meeting, but the motion 
again failed. The February 9, 2016 meeting was recorded by the local cable access channel, 
as is usual practice. Although the minutes indicate that the Board chair announced that the 
meeting was recorded; the video-recording shows that the chair made no such statement. Per 
its usual practice, the Board posted a sign on its meeting table that read, "This meeting is

preparation for the Board's discussion and approval during its meetings.

documents discussed during the meeting. Prior to its vote to approve the minutes, the Board

DISCUSSION 
I. The Board Complied with the Notification Requirement for Recording its 
February 9. 2016 Meeting. 
The Open Meeting Law requires that "[ajfter notifying the chair of the public body, 
any person may make a video or audio recording of an open session of a meeting of a public 
body, or may transmit the meeting through any medium, subject to the reasonable 
requirements of the chair as to the number, placement and operation of equipment so as not to 
interfere with the conduct of the meeting. At the beginning of the meeting the chair shall 
inform other attendees of any such recordings." G.L. c. 30A, § 20(f). The requirement that 
the chair inform attendees of any recording includes any recording made by members of the 
public body itself, including those made for public broadcasting or administrative purposes, 
such as assisting in the drafting of meeting minutes. See OML 2012-24.8 
The complainant alleges that the chair failed to announce that the February 9, 2016 
meeting was being recorded. Here, the local cable access station recorded the meeting. 
Although the chair did not verbally announce that the meeting was being recorded, the Board 
displayed a sign on the table where its sits that reads, "This meeting is being recorded." 
Where a public body regularly records or broadcasts its own meetings, it may satisfy the 
requirement of notification by prominently displaying a sign at the meeting location that 
informs attendees about the recording. See OML 2013-136. This relieves the chair from 
making a verbal announcement about the body's own recordings at the beginning of each 
meeting. Id. Because the Board posted a sign notifying the public that the meeting was 
recorded, the Board did not violate the Open Meeting Law.

II. The Board Violated the Open Meeting Law by Failing to Approve Minutes in a 
Timely Manner. 
The Open Meeting Law was enacted "to eliminate much of the secrecy surrounding 
deliberations and decisions on which public policy is based." Ghiglione v. School Committee 
of Southbridge, 376 Mass. 70, 72 (1978). Public bodies have an obligation to "create and 
maintain accurate minutes of all meetings." G.L. c. 3 OA, § 22(a). These meeting minutes 
must be reviewed and approved in a timely manner. G.L. c. 30A, § 22(c). Although the Open 
Meeting Law does not define "timely manner," we recommend that minutes of a meeting be 
approved at the next meeting, whenever possible. See OML 2014-15; OML 2012-91. For 
bodies that meet regularly, we have concluded that a delay of two to three months in 
approving minutes does not comply with the law. See OML 2016-62. 
The complainant alleges that the Board failed to timely approve certain meeting 
minutes and failed to post those minutes to the Town website. First, the Board did not violate 
the Open Meeting Law by failing to post minutes to a website because that is not a 
requirement under the law. See G.L. c. 30A, § 22; OML 2016-16. However, with respect to 
the approval of minutes, we find that, although the minutes had been drafted and shared with 
the Board for approval within weeks of each meeting, the Board was unable to arrive at a 
decision to approve them. Instead, unable to agree on the drafts, the Board repeatedly decided 
to "table" its discussion of the minutes for future meetings. The complainant participated in 
this delay. It was not until the Board's February 9, 2016 meeting that it was able to approve 
all of the minutes at issue, except for the November 10, 2015 minutes. Again, the Board 
attempted to approve the November 10, 2015 meeting minutes on April 13, 2016 without 
success. Although we do not penalize public bodies for delaying the approval of minutes 
after good faith efforts to do so, see OML 2015-190, and we hesitate to find a violation where 
a complainant participates in the delay, the Board stalled on approving certain minutes for 
months. For this reason, we find that the Board violated the Open Meeting Law.9 We order 
the Board to approve the November 10, 2015 meeting minutes within thirty (30) days, if it has 
not done so already. 
III, The Board's October 13. 2015 and October 27, 2015 Meeting Minutes are 
Substantially Accurate. 
When reviewing meeting minutes, we look for substantial compliance with the 
accuracy requirement. See OML 2016-101; OML 2013-64. The minutes should contain 
enough detail and accuracy so that a member of the public who did not attend the meeting 
could read the minutes and have a clear understanding of what occurred. See id. 
Furthermore, the minutes do not need to include every remark or opinion presented. See 
OML 2016-40; OML 2012-29; OML 2011-55. We find that, although the complainant is 
correct that certain statements are omitted from the Board's October 13, 2015 and October 27, 
2015 meeting minutes, the minutes are substantially accurate so that a member of the public 
who was not present for the meeting could understand the nature of the discussions on each

IV. The Board Violated the Open MeetinR Law by Failing to Include a List of 
Documents in its Minutes. 
Although the minutes we reviewed were substantially accurate, they failed to include 
the requisite list of documents used during the meeting. See G.L. c. 30A, § 22(a) ("A public 
body shall create and maintain accurate minutes of all meetings, including...a list of 
documents and other exhibits used at the meeting[.]") During its February 9, 2016 meeting, 
the Board approved the minutes subject to the addition of the document list. However, it does 
not appear that the lists were, in fact, added to the minutes. Because the Board used 
documents during each meeting at issue, we order the Board to revise the minutes, as planned, 
to include such a list. We commend the Board for pledging to comply with this requirement 
going forward. 
V. The Board Violated the Open Meeting Law by Failing to Provide Minutes 
Within Ten (10) Days of a Request. 
The minutes of an open session, if they exist and whether approved or in draft form, 
shall be made available upon request by any person within 10 days. G.L. c. 30A, § 22(c). 
The complainant submitted her request for "the minutes of all meetings of the Ashland Board 
of Health exactly as presented for votes during the Tuesday, February 9, 2015 (sic) [Board] 
meeting" on February 16, 2016, days after the Board had approved them on February 9, 2016. 
Although the complainant arguably already had copies of the requested minutes as a Board 
member, the Open Meeting Law does not differentiate between requests or requesters, but 
rather allows any person to obtain minutes within 10 days. By failing to provide the minutes 
to the complainant after her request, the Board violated the Open Meeting Law. See G.L. c. 
30A, § 22(c). We therefore order the Board to provide the requested minutes to the 
complainant within 10 days of its receipt of this determination. 
CONCLUSION 
For the reasons stated above, we find that the Board violated the Open Meeting Law in 
several ways with respect to the minutes from the following meetings: September 22, 2015; 
October 13, 2015; October 27, 2015; November 10, 2016; November 17, 2015; December 15, 
2015; and January 26, 2016. Specifically, we find that the Board violated the law by failing 
to timely approve certain meeting minutes; by failing to include a list of documents in those 
meeting minutes; and by failing to timely respond to a request for minutes. We order 
immediate and future compliance with the law's requirements, and we caution that similar
