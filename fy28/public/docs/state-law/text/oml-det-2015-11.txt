RE: Open Meeting Law Complaint 
Dear Attorney Saint Andre: 
This office received three related complaints from Mr. Scott Nassa and Mr. Robert C. 
Haarde, alleging that the Sudbury Board of Selectmen (the "Board") violated the Open Meeting 
Law, G.L. c. 30A, §§ 18-25. Mr. Nassa originally filed his two complaints with the Board on 
October 14, 2014. When no response was received from the Board, he requested further review 
from our office on November 20, 2014. Mr. Haarde's complaint was originally filed with the 
Board on October 15, 2014. When no response was received from the Board, he requested 
further review from our office on November 25, 2014. All three complaints allege that the Board 
failed to follow proper procedure in responding to other Open Meeting Law complaints that were 
previously filed against the Board. 
Following our review, we find that the Board violated the Open Meeting Law as alleged. 
In reaching a determination, we reviewed the original complaints and the requests for review 
filed with our office. We received no response to the complaints, despite our efforts to obtain a 
response from the Board's former counsel. 
FACTS 
We find the facts as follows. The Board is a five-member public body that meets at 
regular intervals to discuss the governance of the Town of Sudbury (the "Town"). Mr. I laarde is 
a member of the Board. Mr. Nassa filed five related Open Meeting Law complaints with the 
Board on September 1, 2014.1 Having received no response to his complaints, Ms. Nassa 
attended a meeting of the Board held on October 14, 2014, and inquired why he had received no 
response. Board Chair Leonard Simon stated that the complaints had been referred to Town 
Counsel. The Board had not reviewed the complaints and had not voted to authorize Town 
Counsel to act on them.

DISCUSSION 
The Open Meeting Law requires that within 14 days of receipt of a complaint, a public 
body must review the complaint's allegations; take remedial action, if appropriate; and send to 
the Attorney General a copy of the complaint and a description of any remedial action taken. 
G.L. c. 30A, § 23(b); 940 CMR 29.05(5). Here, Mr. Nassa filed his complaints with the Board 
on October 14, 2014, and Mr. Haarde filed his complaint with the Board on October 15, 2014. 
The Board, therefore, should have reviewed the complaints and responded no later than October 
24, 2014 and October 25, 2014, respectively. The Board did not respond to these complaints at 
all, which violated the Open Meeting Law. See OML 2014-78; OML 2014-15. Furthermore, it 
appears that the Chair referred the complaints to Town Counsel without first disseminating 
copies for review by the Board. Proper procedure would have been for the Board to review the 
complaints before authorizing legal counsel to respond on its behalf. See 940 CMR 29.05(4); 
OML 2012-95; OML 2012-90; OML 2011-6. The Board's failure to do so violated the law. 
For the reasons stated above, we find that the Board violated the Open Meeting Law as 
alleged. We order immediate and future compliance with the Open Meeting Law, and we 
caution that future similar violations may be considered evidence of intent to violate the law. 
We now consider the complaints addressed by this determination to be resolved. This 
determination does not address any other complaints that may be pending with our office or the 
Board. Please feel free to contact the Division at (617) 963 - 2540 if you have any questions.
