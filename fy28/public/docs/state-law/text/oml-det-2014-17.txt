This office received a complaint from David Reinhart, dated December 10, 2013, 
alleging that the Fitchburg Municipal Airport Commission (the "Commission")'violated the 
Open Meeting Law, G.L. c. 30A, §§ 18-25. The complaint alleges that the Commission entered 
into executive session for an improper purpose during its October 22, 2013 meeting.1 The 
complaint was originally filed with the Commission on or about November 6, 2013. We 
received your response on behalf of the Commission in an email dated December 19, 2013.2 
Following our review, we find that the Commission entered into executive session for an 
improper purpose during its October 22, 2013 meeting. In reaching this determination, we 
reviewed the November 6, 2013 complaint filed with the Commission; the Commission's 
December 19, 2013 response; and the December 10, 2013 complaint filed with our office. 
Additionally, we reviewed the Commission's October 22, 2013 open and executive session 
minutes. 
FACTS 
The Commission is a three-member public body subject to the Open Meeting Law. The 
Commission met on October 22, 2013. During the meeting, the Commission voted to enter into 
executive session "For the Purpose of Reviewing:- a. Financial Audit Draft Findings, b. 
Proposed Tenant Litigation, c. GALE - re Avigation Easement."3

Because the Commission has not publicly released the minutes from this executive 
session, we do not recount their content in detail here. However, one of the three topics the 
Commission discussed in executive session was the Airport Audit. The Commission discussed 
the latest information on the Audit and discussed the Audit's conclusions. 
DISCUSSION 
A public body may enter executive—or closed—session for any of ten enumerated 
purposes. G.L. c. 30A, § 21(a). The Open Meeting Law requires that "before the executive 
session, the chair shall state the purpose for the executive session, stating all subjects that may be 
revealed without compromising the purpose for which the executive session was called." G.L. c. 
30A, § 21(b)(3). 
Prior to entering into executive session on October 22, 2013, the Commission did not 
state which of the 10 purposes it believed permitted the executive session, but rather identified 
only the subject matter of the executive session discussion. With respect to the Commission's 
discussion of the Airport Audit, there is no executive session purpose that covers general 
discussion of an audit. See OML 2012-104 (finding that a public body may not discuss a draft 
audit report in executive session).4 Discussion of an audit may be appropriate for executive 
session if it is related to litigation, complaints, or charges against an individual or where there is 
a law requiring that the audit be kept confidential. See G.L. c. 30A, §§ 21(a)(1), 21(a)(3), 
21(a)(7). However, there is no suggestion that any of those bases were applicable here. Because 
there is no purpose that generally allows a public body to enter into executive session to discuss 
an audit, the Commission violated the Open Meeting Law by holding this discussion outside of 
public view. 
CONCLUSION 
We find that the Commission violated the Open Meeting Law by meeting in Executive 
Session for an improper purpose to discuss the Airport Audit during its October 22, 2013 
meeting. We order the Commission's immediate and future compliance with the Open Meeting 
Law and caution the Commission that a similar future violation may be considered evidence of 
an intentional violation of the law. 
Additionally, we order the Commission to publicly release at its next regularly scheduled 
meeting the portion of the October 22, 2013 executive session minutes that covers the discussion 
of the Airport Audit. Because this was not a lawful executive session held in compliance with 
G.L. c. 30A, § 21, the minutes may not be withheld in part or whole under the Public Records 
Law. See G.L. c. 30A, § 22(f) (stating that the minutes of an executive session may be withheld 
from disclosure to the public as long as publication may defeat the lawful purposes of an
