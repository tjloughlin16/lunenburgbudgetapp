Dear Chair Shearer: 
This office received two complaints from Mr. Joseph Graveline, dated July 21, 2012 and 
August 28, 2012, alleging that the Northfield Zoning Board of Appeals (the "Board") violated 
the Open Meeting Law, G.L. c. 30A, §§ 18-25. Mr. Graveline alleges in his July 21, 2012 
complaint that the Board convened in executive session for an improper purpose on two 
occasions. In his August 28, 2012 complaint, Mr. Graveline alleges that the Board discussed and 
took action on an item without listing it on the meeting notice. He filed his complaints with the 
Board on May 29, 2012 and June 7, 2012, respectively, and the Board responded to those 
original complaints by letters received by our office on June 19, 2012 and June 28, 2012, 
respectively. 
In reaching our determination, we reviewed Mr. Graveline's May 29, 2012 complaint; the 
Board's June 19, 2012 response; and Mr. Graveline's July 21, 2012 complaint filed with our 
office. We reviewed Mr. Graveline's June 7, 2012 complaint; the Board's June 28, 2012 
response; and Mr. Graveline's August 28, 2012 complaint filed with our office. Additionally, we 
reviewed the Board's response to our August 3, 2012 letter requesting additional information, 
received August 22, 2012. We reviewed the April 30, 2012; May 17, 2012; May 21, 2012; and 
June 4, 2012 meeting notices and agendas; and open session minutes from the Board's April 30, 
2012 and June 4, 2012 meetings. Finally, we reviewed the March 29, 2012 Application to the 
Board for Special Permit by Bill Willey; the February 2, 2012 Board Notice granting Bill Willey 
& Vincent Pemice a special permit to operate an auto repair shop on Timothy and Beth Ives' 
property; the February 3, 2010 Board Notice of Special Permit granting Timothy and Beth Ives a 
special permit for their property; email correspondence between Town Administrator Tom 
Hutcheson and Dianne Comwell, and between Mr. Hutcheson and Town Counsel, Joel B. Bard; 
correspondence between Building Commissioner James Hawkins and the Ives, dated February

Following our review, we find that the Board did not violate the Open Meeting Law on 
either April 30, 2012 or May 17, 2012 because, while the complainant alleges that the Board 
entered executive session for an improper purpose, the Board did not enter into executive session 
on those dates. Additionally, the Board did not violate the Open Meeting Law on June 4, 2012 
because the Board properly listed those topics that the Chair reasonably anticipated the Board 
would discuss. Finally, we find that the notice for the Board's April 30, 2012 meeting violated 
the Open Meeting Law because it did not specify that the Board would also meet in open session. 
FACTS 
Based upon our review of the material listed above, the facts are as follows. The 
complainant, Joseph Graveline, filed a series of complaints against Board Chair Ed Shearer. 
Chair Shearer requested that the Northfield Selectboard (the "Selectboard") provide him access 
to the Town's counsel to advise  him in responding to the complaints. The Selectboard denied 
Chair Shearer's request. Following that denial, Chair Shearer informed the Selectboard that he 
would call an executive session of the Board to discuss other options for legal representation. 
The Board posted notice on April 26, 2012 for its April 30, 2012 meeting. The notice 
stated that the meeting would be held entirely in executive session "to discuss personnel matters 
of interest to the Board." The notice did not list any other topics. On April 30, 2012, the Board 
held the meeting entirely in open session. The Board writes in its June 19, 2012 letter that, prior 
to holding its April 30, 2012 meeting, the Board realized that the April 26, 2012 notice was 
"worded improperly." The April 30, 2012 minutes reflect that during the meeting, the Board 
discussed the proper procedures for posting notice and holding an executive session in 
accordance with the Open Meeting Law, reviewed past Board decisions, and decided on dates for 
two upcoming Board hearings.2 The Board adjourned the meeting with instructions for 
Marguerite Lentz, Board Clerk, to post notice for the next Board meeting and include an 
executive session. 
On May 2, 2012, the Board posted notice for its May 17, 2012 meeting. The notice listed 
the only topic as "[ejxecutive [s]ession for reason #1: 'to discuss a complaint against an 
individual.'" On May 16, 2012, Vivien Venskowski, Administrative Assistant to the Board, 
cancelled the May 17, 2012 meeting, and no meeting was held on that date. 
On May 21, 2012, the Board held a public hearing to discuss the renewal of a special 
permit for Bill Willey and his "auto repair shop on property owned by Tim & Beth Ives." The 
Board originally granted the special permit to Mr. Willey on February 2, 2010 to operate an auto 
repair shop at the Ives' property at 90 East Street in Northfield. The May 21, 2012 hearing 
included two hours of public comments regarding Mr. Willey's application.

DISCUSSION 
1.  The Board did not Enter into Executive Session for an Improper Purpose on Either 
April 30. 2012 or May 17. 2012. 
The Open Meeting Law requires that meetings of public bodies occur in open session, 
unless a public body enters executive session for one or more of ten specific purposes outlined in 
G.L. c. 30A, § 21. One such purpose. Purpose 1, allows a public body to enter into executive 
session to discuss "complaints or charges brought against, a public officer, employee, staff 
member or individual." G. L. c. 30A, § 21(a)(1). 
The complainant alleges that the Board met in executive session for an improper purpose 
on April 30, 2012. The notice for the Board's April 30, 2012 meeting specified that the "meeting 
will be held in [ejxecutive [sjession to discuss personnel matters of interest to the Board." As 
the Board acknowledged during its April 30, 2012 meeting, this is not a valid purpose for 
executive session. Recognizing its error, the Board cancelled the April 30, 2012 executive 
session, and instead held the meeting in open session. During that meeting, the Board reviewed 
the proper protocol for posting notice and holding an executive session. Because the Board 
never entered into executive session during its April 30, 2012 meeting, the Board did not violate 
the Open Meeting Law. 
The complainant also alleges that the Board entered into an improper executive session 
on May 17, 2012. The Board posted notice for the May 17, 2012 executive session more than 48 
hours prior to the meeting, and listed Purpose 1 as the purpose for the executive session. 
However, the Board cancelled the May 17, 2012 meeting on May 16, 2012. Therefore, the 
Board did not violate the Open Meeting Law because the Board did not meet on May 17, 2012. 
2.  The Board did not Violate the Open Meeting Law on June 4. 2012 because the Board 
Properly Listed those Topics that the Chair Reasonably Anticipated the Board would 
Discuss. 
A public body must post notice of every meeting at least 48 hours prior to the meeting in 
an easily understandable format containing the date, time, and place of the meeting, and a list of 
topics that the chair reasonably anticipates will be discussed. G.L. c. 30A, § 21(b). Public 
bodies must list topics in a meeting notice with "sufficient specificity to reasonably advise the 
public of the issues to be discussed at the meeting." 940 CMR 29.03(l)(b). We generally

consider a topic sufficiently specific when a reasonable member of the public could read the 
topic and understand what the nature of the public body's discussion will be.  See OML 2012-69. 
The Board posted notice for its June 4, 2012 public hearing on May 29, 2012 and 
included the following discussion topic; "[c]ontinuation of Public Hearing Bill Willey 90 East 
Street, Northfield (Property owned by Beth & Timothy Ives [sic]." Minutes from the June 4, 
2012 meeting reflect discussions pertaining solely to Bill Willey and the property at 90 East 
Street in Northfield, as listed in the notice. The Board discussed issuing a renewed Special 
Permit to both Bill Willey and the Ives, as the property owners, as a way to increase 
accountability for the property. Because the Board's discussion during the June 4, 2012 meeting 
concerned the posted, anticipated agenda item, we do not find a violation of the Open Meeting 
Law.3 
3.  The Notice for the Board's April 30. 2012 Meeting was Insufficient Because it did 
not Specify that the Board Would Meet in Open Session. 
The notice for the Board's April 30, 2012 meeting stated that the meeting would be held 
entirely in executive session "to discuss personnel matters of interest to the Board." Although 
not raised in the complaint, we note that this meeting notice was insufficient because it failed to 
specify that an open session would occur. Because the Open Meeting Law requires that a public 
body convene in open session before entering executive session, any public body entering 
executive session should reasonably anticipate that it will convene in open session to 1) state the 
purpose for its executive session, 2) take a vote to enter executive session, and 3) announce 
whether it will re-convene in open session following the executive session. G.L. c. 30A, § 
21(b)(l)-(4); OML 2013-9. When the only business to be discussed during an open session is 
the procedural requirements for entering executive session, public bodies should list "open 
session" on the meeting notice, in addition to the executive session topic, to inform members of 
the public that the public body will, in fact, hold an open meeting that they are permitted to 
attend. See OML 2013-9. Though the April 30, 2012 meeting was ultimately held entirely in 
open session, a member of the public had no notice that the Board would meet in open session 
because the meeting notice only stated that the "meeting will be held in [ejxecutive [sjession." 
See OML 2013-9. The notice therefore did not comply with the Open Meeting Law. 
CONCLUSION 
For the reasons stated above, we find that the Board did not violate the Open Meeting 
Law as alleged by the complainant because the Board did not enter into executive session on 
either April 30, 2012 or May 17, 2012. The Board also did not violate the Open Meeting Law 
during its June 4, 2012 meeting because its discussion concerned only the topic listed in a 
properly posted meeting notice. Finally, we find that the notice for the Board's April 30, 2012 
meeting violated the Open Meeting Law because it did not specify that the Board would also 
meet in open session. We order the Board's immediate and future compliance with the Open
