This office received a complaint from Laura Ortiz on August 29, 2022, alleging that the 
Board of Elementary and Secondary Education (the “Board”) violated the Open Meeting Law, 
G.L. c. 30A, §§ 18-25.  The complaint was originally filed with the Board on May 22, and you 
responded to the complaint, on behalf of the Board, by letter dated June 20, following an 
extension of time granted by this office.
1  The complaint alleges that the meeting notice that the 
Board posted for its May 24 meeting failed to include attachments corresponding with two topics 
that were listed on that meeting notice.
2  
 
We resolve this matter by informal action, in accordance with 940 CMR 29.07(2)(a), 
after reviewing the original complaint, the Board’s response to the complaint, the complaint filed 
with our office requesting further review, and the notice and minutes of the Board meeting held 
on May 24.  We find that the Board did not violate the Open Meeting Law in the way alleged.

The Open Meeting Law requires that public bodies post notice 48 hours in advance of a 
meeting and include a “listing of topics that the chair reasonably anticipates will be discussed at 
the meeting.”  G.L. c. 30A, § 20(b).  Public bodies are required to list topics in a meeting notice 
with “sufficient specificity to reasonably advise the public of the issues to be discussed at the 
meeting.”  940 CMR 29.03(l)(b).  We generally consider a topic sufficiently specific when a 
reasonable member of the public could read the topic and understand the anticipated nature of 
the public body’s discussion.  See OML 2018-7; OML 2015-35.
3  The Open Meeting Law does 
not require that a public body attach supporting documents to a meeting notice or that it publicly 
post a “meeting packet” that contains such documents.  See OML 2022-24; OML 2022-19; OML 
2021-181.  To the contrary, the meeting notice itself must contain all information that the Open 
Meeting Law requires; posting attachments does not cure an otherwise defective notice.  See 
OML 2021-138; OML 2021-57; OML 2019-22. 
 
Here, the Board duly posted notice of a meeting to be held on May 24.  The notice 
included ten topics, including “Update of Boston Public Schools” and “State Student Advisory 
Council End of Year Report for 2021-2022.”  On May 23, the Board posted to its website a 
Review Report pertaining to the Boston Public Schools and the State Student Advisory Council 
Year End Report.  We find that the notice for the Board’s May 24 meeting described the two 
topics at issue, Boston Public Schools Update and State Student Advisory Council Year End 
Report, with sufficient specificity.
4  The Board was not required to include attachments 
pertaining to these topics, even if its usual practice was to do so.  Nonetheless, we note that the 
Board attached and posted to its website relevant documents it planned to discuss with respect to 
the two topics the day prior to the May 24 meeting.

meeting.  But see G.L. c. 30A, § 22(c) (minutes of an open session, if they exist and whether approved or in draft 
form, shall be made available upon request by any person within 10 days) ; G.L. c. 66, § 10 (Public Records Law 
requires Board to permit inspection or provide copy of any public record, such as documents used during a meeting, 
within 10 business days of request).  We encourage public bodies, whenever feasible, to provide meeting attendees 
with copies of all documents it intends to discuss.  Particularly where viewing such documents would be helpful to 
following the conversation during the meeting, providing copies of documents that the Board intends to discuss 
promotes transparency and public access to government deliberations.  See  OML 2016-94.

For the reasons stated above, we find that the Board did not violate the Open Meeting 
Law.  We now consider the complaint addressed by this determination to be resolved.  This 
determination does not address any other complaints that may be pending with our office or the
