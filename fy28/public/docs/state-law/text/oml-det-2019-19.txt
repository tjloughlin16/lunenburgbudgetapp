RE: Open Meeting Law Complaint
Dear Mr. Jennings:
This office received a complaint from Timothy Gagnon on February 5, 2019 alleging that 
the Diman Regional Vocational Technical School District Committee (the “Committee”) 
violated the Open Meeting Law, G.L. c. 30A, §§ 18-25. The complaint was originally filed with 
the Committee on December 11, 2018; and you responded, on behalf of the Committee, by letter 
dated December 26, 2018. The complaint alleges that the Committee did not allow public 
participation at its December 6, 2018, meeting when the Committee voted not to renew the 
Superintendent’s contract.
We resolve this matter by informal action, in accordance with 940 CMR 29.07(2)(a), 
after reviewing the original complaint, the Committee’s response to the complaint, the complaint 
filed with our office requesting further review, and the notice and open session minutes of the 
Committee’s December 6, 2018 meeting. We find that the Committee did not violate the Open 
Meeting Law as alleged.
The Open Meeting Law states that “[n]o person shall address a meeting of a public body 
without permission of the chair, and all persons shall, at the request of the chair, be silent.” G.L. 
c. 30A § 20(f). The law does not require that a public body allow public participation, but rather 
gives the chair of the public body discretion to decide whether to permit public comment during 
a meeting. See OML 2017-189; OML 2014-23; OML 2012-78.1 We do, however, encourage 
public bodies to allow for as much public participation as time and circumstances permit. See 
OML 2015-12; OML 2014-129; OML 2012-59.
1 Open Meeting Law determinations and declinations may be found at the Attorney General’s website, 
www.mass.gov/ago/openmeeting.

The Committee met on December 6, 2018 and appointed a treasurer, two instructors, and 
a nighttime attendant, and approved several donations and expenditures. The Committee then 
convened in executive session to discuss contract negotiations with nonunion personnel, 
including the Superintendent. When the Committee reconvened in open session, it voted not to 
renew the Superintendent’s contract. The Committee then adjourned the meeting. It is not a 
violation of the Open Meeting Law for a public body to vote on a topic without first taking 
comments from the public. See OML 2013-64; OML 2012-59; OML 2012-23. Moreover, the 
Committee did not violate the Open Meeting by limiting public participation during the meeting, 
as it is within the Chair’s discretion to do so. See OML 2017-181.
The complaint further alleges that the Committee violated its Code of Ethics. The 
Division of Open Government’s statutory authority concerns compliance only with the Open 
Meeting Law, G.L. c. 30A, §§ 18-25. Even if true, this allegation in the complaint would not 
constitute a violation of the Open Meeting Law. As such, we decline to review this portion of 
the complaint. See OML Declination 1-25-16 (Nahant Housing Authority Board of 
Commissioners); OML Declination 5-21-12 (Hampden-Wilbraham Regional School 
Committee). We offer no opinion on whether any actions taken by the Committee could be a 
violation of some other law or regulation outside the scope of the Division’s review.
For the reasons stated above, we find that the Committee did not violate the Open 
Meeting Law. We now consider the complaint addressed by this determination to be resolved.

this determination.
