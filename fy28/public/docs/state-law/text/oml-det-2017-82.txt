RE: Open Meeting Law Complaint 
Dear Attorney Crotty: 
This office received a complaint from Karen Vilandry, dated April 4, 2017, alleging that 
the Fairhaven Planning Board (the "Board") violated the Open Meeting Law, G.L. c. 30A, §§ 18-
25. The complaint was originally filed with the Board on March 21, 2017, and the Board 
responded by letter dated March 30, 2017. In her complaint, Ms. Vilandry alleges that the Board 
posted an insufficiently detailed meeting notice. 
Following our review, we find that the Board did not violate the Open Meeting Law. In 
reaching a determination, we reviewed the original complaint, the Board's response, and the 
request for further review filed with our office. We also reviewed the notice of a Board meeting 
held on February 21 21, 2017. 
FACTS 
We find the facts as follows. The complaint concerns the notice of a Board meeting held 
on February 21, 2017. The notice contained, among other anticipated topics, a section described 
as, "PUBLIC FIEARING: Continued Public Hearings from January 10, 2017." The notice then 
listed nine subtopics, described as follows:

The Board met on February 21, 2017 as planned. During the meeting, the Board 
discussed the relevant special permit applications, which sought permission to install 
replacement utility poles at the addresses listed on the notice. Ultimately, the Board voted in 
favor of all special permits listed on the notice. 
The Open Meeting Law requires public bodies to post notice of all meetings at least 48 
hours in advance, not including Saturdays, Sundays, and legal holidays. G.L. c. 30A, § 20(b). 
Such notice must include "a listing of topics that the chair reasonably anticipates will be 
discussed at the meeting." Id, Topics must be listed with sufficient detail such that a reasonable 
member of the public could read the notice and understand what the body will discuss. 940 
OMR 29.03(l)(b); OML 2015-35. 
Here, the complaint alleges that the February 21, 2017 meeting notice was insufficiently 
detailed because it did not provide information on the substance of the special permits sought. 
We agree that the inclusion of more information, such as the purpose for the permits sought, 
would have been useful to members of the public. However, because the notice identified the 
party seeking the special permits, Verizon Wireless, and provided further identifying information 
in the form of the lot numbers of the property under discussion, we find that the Board met its 
minimum obligations under the Open Meeting Law. See OML 2017-68; OML 2016-137; OML 
2015-73. Further, we note that the special permits had been discussed at a previous public 
hearing, and the public was thus aware that the Board was considering the applications. 
Accordingly, we find no violation with respect to the complaint's allegation. However, we 
advise the Board to include additional detail on future meeting notices, such as the nature of the 
special permits under consideration. 
For the reasons stated above, we find that the Board did not violate the Open Meeting 
Law. We now consider the complaint addressed by this determination to be resolved. This 
determination does not address any other complaints that may be pending with our office or the 
Board. Please feel free to contact the Division at (617) 963 - 2540 if you have any questions.
