Dear Mr. Fitzgerald: 
This office received a complaint from Michael Bonetti on December 28, alleging that the 
Boston Zoning Board of Appeal (the "Board") violated the Open Meeting Law, G.L. c. 30A, §§ 
18-25.1 The complaint was originally filed with the Board on November 16, and the Board 
responded by letter dated December 11. The complaint alleges that the Board did not allow 
members of the public to address the Board during a November 14 meeting. 
Following our review, we resolve this complaint by informal action in accordance with 
940 CMR 29.07(2)(a), and find that the Board did not violate the Open Meeting Law. In 
reaching this determination, we reviewed the complaint filed with the Board, the Board's 
response, and the complaint filed with our office. We spoke separately with Mr. Bonetti and his 
attorney, Matthew J. Dunn, by telephone on January 19, 2018. 
The Open Meeting Law does not require that a public body allow public participation in a 
meeting. See OML 2014-23; OML 2012-23.2 The Open Meeting Law provides that, "[n]o 
person shall address a meeting of a public body without permission of the chair, and all persons 
shall, at the request of the chair, be silent." G.L. c. 30A, § 20(g). Thus, the Board was not 
required by the Open Meeting Law to permit members of the public to address the Board during 
its November 14 meeting. We note, however, that according to the Board, both Mr. Bonetti and 
his attorney were permitted to address the Board during the meeting. To the extent that the 
complaint alleges that the Board violated certain public hearing requirements, we decline to 
address that issue as the Division is tasked with enforcing the Open Meeting Law. We do not 
enforce public hearing requirements.
