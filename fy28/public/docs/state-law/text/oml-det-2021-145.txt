 RE: Open Meeting Law Complaint 
Dear Attorney Austin: 
This office received a complaint from Mary Jane and Phillip Sarocco on June 4, 2021, 
alleging that the Weston Zoning Board of Appeals (the “Board”) violated the Open Meeting 
Law, G.L. c. 30A, §§ 18-25. The complaint was originally filed with the Board on April 22, and 
you responded on behalf of the Board by letter dated May 6.1 In their complaint, the 
complainants allege that the Board met in executive session on April 8 without a proper statutory 
purpose, and took votes in executive session which should have taken place in open session. 
Following our review, we find that the Board did not violate the Open Meeting Law. In 
reaching this determination, we reviewed the original complaint, the Board’s response to the 
complaint, and the complainant’s request for further review. We also communicated by email 
with Attorney Austin on several occasions in June and July of 2021, and with complainant’s 
counsel, Attorney Benjamin Tymann, in August of 2021.2 
 The Open Meeting Law requires that all meetings of a public body be properly noticed 
and open to members of the public, unless an executive session is convened. See G.L. c. 30A, §§ 
20(a), 21(a). One permissible reason to convene in executive session is “to discuss strategy with 
respect to collective bargaining or litigation if an open meeting may have a detrimental effect on 
the bargaining or litigating position of the public body and the chair so declares.” G.L. c. 30A, § 
21(a)(3) (“Purpose 3”). Purpose 3 offers the narrow opportunity to discuss strategy with respect 
to litigation that is pending or clearly and imminently threatened or otherwise demonstrably 
likely; the mere possibility of litigation is not sufficient to invoke Purpose 3. See Doherty v.

School Committee of Boston, 386 Mass. 643, 648 (1982); Perryman v. School Committee of 
Boston, 17 Mass. App. Ct. 346, 352 (1983); OML 2012-05.3 The public body invoking Purpose 
3 must have a bargaining or litigating position to protect, and must declare in open session that 
having the discussion in open session may have a detrimental effect on that position. See G.L. c. 
30A, § 21(a)(3); OML 2011-47. Under Purpose 3, a public body has a litigating position to 
protect if there is pending litigation in which the public body has a litigating position, or 
if litigation is clearly and imminently threatened or otherwise demonstrably likely. See OML 
2020-108; OML 2020-53. 
 The Board met in executive session under Purpose 3 on April 8 to discuss three pending 
litigation matters.4 The Board maintains that holding these discussions in open session would 
have been detrimental to the Board’s litigating position with respect to each matter. In order to 
determine whether the Board properly invoked Purpose 3 to enter these executive sessions, we 
questioned Attorney Austin in detail about the nature of the Board’s discussions, though we did 
not review the Board’s executive session minutes as the Board asserts that they consist of 
attorney-client privileged communications. As the meeting minutes of this executive session 
have not been released to the public, we do not discuss the contents of Board’s discussions in 
detail. Attorney Austin provided information about the Board’s discussion of each of the three 
litigation matters, all of which were pending lawsuits at the time of the April 8 meeting. Based 
on the information provided by Attorney Austin, we are satisfied that the Board’s discussions 
were limited to strategy with respect to pending litigation. Therefore, we find that the Board 
properly entered executive session under Purpose 3 to discuss these three matters on April 8. 
 Finally, the complainants assert that the Board was required to vote in open session to 
approve any settlement agreements that it discussed during the executive session on April 8. We 
disagree. The Board was not required, as the complainants suggest, to announce or ratify the 
settlement agreement in open session as the Board convened in executive session under Purpose 
3. See OML 2018-70; see also Doherty v. School Committee of Boston, 386 Mass. 643, 647 
(1982) (holding that closed door votes are permissible where a body meets in executive session 
to protect its litigating position). Therefore, we find that the Board did not violate the Open 
Meeting Law in this respect.  
 We note that, once an agreement has been executed, we recommend as best practice that 
the public body publicly announce the agreement in open session and describe the terms reached. 
Ideally, this should occur either during the same meeting or at the public body’s next scheduled 
meeting. See OML 2015-52; OML 2013-84. 
 For the reasons stated above, we find that the Board did not violate the Open Meeting 
Law. We now consider the complaint addressed by this determination to be resolved. This 
determination does not address any other complaints that may be pending with our office or the 
Board. Please feel free to contact the Division at (617) 963 - 2540 if you have any questions.
