 RE: Open Meeting Law Complaint 
 
Dear Chair Furnia: 
 
This office received a complaint from Dakota Richards on September 24, 2024, 1 alleging 
that the Granby Housing Authority Board of Commissioners (the “Board”) violated the Open 
Meeting Law, G.L. c. 30A, §§ 18-25. The complaint was originally filed with the Board on or 
about August 16, and you responded on behalf of the Board by letter dated September 26. 2 We 
understand the complaint to allege that 1) the Board deliberated outside of a properly noticed 
meeting, and 2) certain comments made during the Board’s July 22 meeting regarding a 
litigation matter violated the Open Meeting Law. 
 
Following our review, we find that the Board did not violate the Open Meeting Law as 
alleged. In reaching this determination, we reviewed the Open Meeting Law complaint, the 
Board’s response, the request for further review, the notice for the Board’s July 22 meeting, and 
portions of a video recording of the meeting.3

FACTS 
 
The Board posted notice of a meeting to be held on July 22. Included on the meeting 
notice was the topic “Warrant Report.” The Board convened the July 22 meeting as scheduled. A 
few minutes into the meeting Board Chair Joseph Furnia4 explained that he and another 
individual, who he did not name during the meeting, had visited some new apartments that were 
under construction.  
 
Later in the meeting, while the Board reviewed the warrant, a member highlighted that 
one of the items on the warrant was for costs associated with a legal matter involving the 
Complainant. Thereafter a brief discussion ensued regarding the status of the legal matter. The 
entire exchange lasted approximately 40 seconds.  
 
DISCUSSION 
 
The Open Meeting Law was enacted “to eliminate much of the secrecy surrounding 
deliberations and decisions on which public policy is based.” Ghiglione v. Sch. Comm. of 
Southbridge, 376 Mass. 70, 72 (1978). Accordingly, all deliberations of a public body must 
occur at a meeting for which proper notice has been posted and which is open to the public, 
unless a lawful executive session has been convened. G.L. c. 30A, § 20. The Open Meeting Law 
defines deliberation as “an oral or written communication through any medium, including 
electronic mail, between or among a quorum of a public body on any public business within its 
jurisdiction.” G.L. c. 30A, § 18. For purposes of the Open Meeting Law, a quorum is generally a 
simple majority of the members of the public body. Id. 
 
Except in cases of an emergency, meeting notices must be posted at least 48 hours in 
advance of the meeting, not including Saturdays, Sundays, and legal holidays. G.L. c. 30A, 
§ 20(b). Notices must include “a listing of topics that the chair reasonably anticipates will be 
discussed at the meeting.” Id. The listing of topics shall have sufficient specificity to reasonably 
advise the public of the issues to be discussed at the meeting. 940 CMR 29.03(1)(b). We 
generally consider a topic to be sufficiently specific when a reasonable member of the public 
could read the topic and understand the anticipated nature of the public body’s discussion. See 
OML 2018-7; OML 2015-35.5 A public body need not list each and every detail about a topic, 
nor anticipate the course of deliberation or the outcome of a discussion. See OML 2022-98; 
OML 2020-132; OML 2019-134.  
 
First, the complaint alleges that members of the Board, along with the Executive 
Director, deliberated while visiting apartments prior to the Board’s July 22 meeting. As evidence 
to support this allegation the complaint points to the comments made by Chair Furnia at the 
beginning of the July 22 meeting explaining that he and another individual had visited

apartments that were under construction.6 In its response, the Board explains that the only 
individuals present during the visit were Chair Furnia and the Executive Director of the Housing 
Authority. Absent sufficient evidence to the contrary, we credit the Board’s account of the facts. 
See OML 2023-121; OML 2017-93; OML 2016-141; see also LaPointe v. License Board of 
Worcester, 389 Mass. 454 (1983). As such, we find that the Board did not improperly deliberate 
as alleged where a quorum of the Board was not present at the visit and there has been no 
allegation or suggestion that deliberation occurred via serial communication. 7 
 
Next, the complaint alleges that the Board violated the Open Meeting Law during the 
July 22 meeting when it discussed a legal matter involving the Complainant. We disagree. We 
find that the brief exchange regarding the legal matter naturally flowed from the Board’s 
consideration of the notice topic related to the warrant and therefore the notice was sufficient to 
inform the public of the discussion that occurred. See OML 2021-39; OML 2018-72; OML 
2014-44; OML 2014-38. 
 
CONCLUSION 
 
For the reasons stated above, we find that the Board did not violate the Open Meeting 
Law as alleged. We now consider the complaint addressed by this determination to be resolved. 
This determination does not address any other complaints that may be pending with the Board or 
with our office. Please feel free to contact our office at (617) 963-2540 if you have any questions 
regarding this letter.
