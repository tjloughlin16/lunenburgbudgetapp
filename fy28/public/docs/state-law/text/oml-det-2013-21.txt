RE: Open Meeting Law Complaint 
Dear Attorney McQuillan: 
This office received a complaint filed by Jack Burke on January 18, 2012, alleging that 
the Methuen City Council (the "Council") violated the Open Meeting Law, G.L. c. 30A, §§ 18- 
25. The complaint alleges that a Council member and a Council member-elect contacted a 
quorum of the Council outside of a meeting to solicit votes for the Council vice-chairmanship. 
The complaint was first filed with the Council on or about January 6, 2012. We received the 
Council's response to the original complaint, dated January 11, 2012. 
Following our review, we find that the Council did not violate the Open Meeting Law. In 
reaching this determination, we reviewed the January 6, 2012 complaint filed with the Council; 
the Council's January 11, 2012 response; and the January 18, 2012 complaint filed with our 
office. We also reviewed the minutes of the Council's January 3, 2012 meeting. 
FACTS 
The Methuen City Council is a nine member public body, thus a quorum of the Council is 
five members. 
On November 8, 2011, Methuen elected its 2012 City Council members. Three members 
of the 2011 Council were reelected, and six members were newly elected. The new Council was 
===PAGE 2===
sworn in on January 3, 2012. The complaint alleges that between November 8, 2011 and 
December 12, 2011, two members of the 2012 Council contacted a quorum of the 2012 Council 
to campaign for the position of Council vice-chair, which would be elected by the 2012 Council 
members at their inaugural meeting on January 3, 2012. One was Joyce Campagnone, who was 
at the time a sitting Council member. The other was Sean Fountain, who at the time was not a 
sitting member of the Council but a Councilor-elect. In its January 11, 2012 response to the 
complaint, the Council does not address the allegation directly, but "objects to the fact that it 
must formally respond to a complaint predicated completely upon rumor, hearsay and innuendo." 
The letter calls the claims raised by the complaint "baseless" but does not specifically address 
the allegations that Councilor Campagnone and Councilor-elect Fountain contacted a quorum of 
the 2012 Council regarding the vice-chair election. 
DISCUSSION 
The Open Meeting Law was enacted "to eliminate much of the secrecy surrounding 
deliberations and decisions on which public policy is based." Ghiglione v. School Committee of 
Southbridge, 376 Mass. 70, 72 (1978). A "deliberation" is defined as "an oral or written 
communication through any medium, including electronic mail, between or among a quorum of 
a public body on any public business within its jurisdiction." G.L. c. 30A, § 18. The Open 
Meeting Law defines a "meeting" as "a deliberation by a public body with respect to any matter 
within the body's jurisdiction." Id. The law requires that meetings of a public body be properly 
noticed and open to members of the public, unless an executive session is convened. See G.L. c. 
30A, §§ 20(a)—(b), 21. 
The Council's response to the complaint did not directly address the factual allegations 
contained therein. We found it unnecessary to investigate whether Councilor Campagnone or 
Councilor Fountain actually communicated with other members of the Council regarding the 
vice-chair position prior to being sworn in on January 3, 2012, however, because even if they did 
the Council did not engage in improper deliberation. Between November 8, 2011 and January 3, 
2012, there were only three members of the Council serving who could count toward a quorum 
of the 2012 Council (which was five). The other six members had not been sworn in, and thus 
were not members of a public body. The alleged communications, therefore, could not have 
been "between or among a quorum of a public body." See G.L. c. 30A, § 18; OML 2012-63. 1  
CONCLUSION 
For the reasons stated above, we find that the Council did not violate the Open Meeting 
Law. We now consider this matter closed. If you have any questions regarding this 
determination, please do not hesitate to contact me.
