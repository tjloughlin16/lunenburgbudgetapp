RE: Open Meeting Law Complaint 
Dear Mr. Gagne: 
This office received a complaint from John Michael Dupuis, Esq., dated November 18, 
2013, alleging that the West Bridgewater Board of Selectmen (the "Board") violated the Open 
Meeting Law, G.L. c. 30A, §§ 18-25. The complaint was originally filed with the Board on 
October 17, 2013, and the Board responded to the original complaint by letters dated October 25, 
2013 and December 11, 2013. In his complaint. Attorney Dupuis alleges that the Board's 
September 17, 2013 meeting failed to comply with the Open Meeting Law because 1) the notice 
failed to identify with sufficient specificity the topics that the Board intended to discuss in 
executive session; and 2) the minutes failed to state with sufficient specificity the topics that the 
Board voted to discussed in executive session. 
Following our review, we have decided to resolve this complaint by informal action, in 
accordance with 940 CMR 29.07(2)(a). We find that the Board failed to state the purpose for an 
anticipated executive session with sufficient specificity both in the notice for and in the minutes 
of its September 17, 2013 meeting. See G.L. c. 30A, §§ 20(b), 21(b)(3); 940 CMR 29.03(l)(b); 
OML 2013-28; OML 2011-56.1 We commend the Board for acknowledging the violation and 
taking remedial action in response to the complaint. 
The notice for the Board's September 17, 2013 meeting listed a topic as "Executive 
Session pursuant to MGL Chapter 3 OA, Section 21 (a), Exception 2 To conduct strategy sessions 
in preparation for negotiations with nonunion personnel or to conduct collective bargaining 
sessions or contract negotiations with [a] non union person. Exception 3. To discuss strategy 
with respect to.. .litigation if an open meeting may have a detrimental effect on the .. .litigating

position of the public body and the chair so declares." This same statement of the executive 
session purpose was included in the minutes of the Board's September 17, 2013 meeting. 
Public bodies must list topics in a meeting notice with "sufficient specificity to 
reasonably advise the public of the issues to be discussed at the meeting." 940 CMR 
29.03(l)(b). Executive session topics must include as much detail as possible without 
compromising the purpose for which the executive session was called. District Attorney for N. 
Dist. v. School Comm. of Wayland. 455 Mass. 561, 567 (2009) ("[a] precise statement of the 
reason for convening in executive session is necessary under the open meeting law because that 
is the only notification given to the public that the school committee would conduct business in 
private, and the only way the public would know if the reason for doing so was proper or 
improper."); see G.L. c. BOA, §§ 20(b), 21(b)(3); 940 CMR 29.03(l)(b). Here, although the 
Board listed the executive session purposes in its notice, it failed to identify the specific non-
union personnel or collective bargaining unit with whom the Board would be negotiating under 
Purpose 2, or identify the litigation matter that the Board planned to discuss under Purpose 3. 
See OML 2012-118; OML 2011-56. Because this additional detail would not have compromised 
the purpose for the executive session discussion, it should have been included in the meeting 
notice and recorded in the meeting minutes. Id.; OML 2013-140; 2011-56. 
Furthermore, before entering into executive session, the chair must also state the purpose 
for the executive session, stating all subjects that may be revealed without compromising the 
purpose for which the executive session was called. G.L. c. 30 A, § 21(b)(3). During the 
Board's September 17, 2013 meeting, the Chair similarly failed to identify the specific non-
union personnel or collective bargaining unit with whom the Board would be negotiating under 
Purpose 2, and failed to identify the litigation matter that the Board planned to discuss under 
Purpose 3. See OML 2012-118; OML 2011-56. 
In response to this complaint, the Board acknowledged the violation, and during its April 
16, 2014 meeting, amended the September 17, 2013 minutes to read, in relevant part, that the 
Board entered into executive session; 
Pursuant to Exception 4: Police training; Suicide Awareness Preparatory Class, namely 
that for one full day all Police Officers of rank were not in the Town of West Bridgewater 
due to attending this class; Pursuant to Exception 3: Strategy session in negotiations with 
Collective Bargaining in relation to role of Finance Committee, namely reviewing a letter 
from Town Counsel which defines the role of the Finance Committee in relation to the 
Board of Selectmen in upcoming union contract negotiations in a way that would not 
compromise the bargaining power of the Selectmen with pending union contracts; 
Litigation strategy session involving pending MCAD case; Pursuant to Exception 3: 
Strategy session to potentially engage Town Counsel in surrounding community 
litigation, namely the Town believes it should be named as a Surrounding Community as 
defined in MGL Chapter 23K in the Raynham Park quest for a Class II slots license 
through the Massachusetts Gaming Commission, the negotiations have stalled and the 
Town needs to determine the best course of action to force a favorable settlement; 
Strategy session involving possible litigation involving real estate property. Seconded by 
Mrs. Maloney and so voted unanimously.

We applaud the Board's efforts to be responsive to the complaint, and find that with 
regard to its minutes the Board took sufficient remedial action by amending them to include 
more than the necessary degree of detail required by the Open Meeting Law. G.L. c. 30 A, § 
21(b)(3). Furthermore, the Board has acknowledged its failure to provide sufficient specificity in 
the notice for its September 17, 2013 meeting and in its verbal statement prior to entering 
executive session on that date. We remind that Board of the importance of adhering to these 
requirements, which are intended to give the public prior notice of what the body plans to discuss 
behind the closed doors. See OML 2012-68. 
We order the Board's immediate and future compliance with the law, and caution that 
future similar violations may be considered evidence of intent to violate the law. We now 
consider the complaint addressed by this determination to be resolved. This determination does 
not address any other complaints which may be pending with our office or the Board. Please feel 
free to contact our office at (617) 963-2540 if you have any questions or believe any facts in this 
letter to be inaccurate.
