Re: Open Meeting Law Complaint 
Dear Attorney Suarez: 
This office received a complaint from Don Warner Saklad on August 26, alleging that the 
Boston City Council (the Council) violated the Open Meeting Law, G.L. c. 30A, §§ 18-25.1 The 
complaint was originally filed with the Council on July 22, and Attorney David Waterfall 
responded, on behalf of the Council, by letter dated August 4. The complaint alleges that the 
Council convened in executive session during its July 22 meeting without stating the purpose for 
that session. 
Following our review, we have decided to resolve this complaint by informal action, in 
accordance with 940 CMR 29.07(2)(a), and find that the Council did not violate the Open 
Meeting Law. The Council did not convene in executive session during its July 22 meeting. 
Rather, the Council recessed the meeting for several minutes to allow two Councilors to 
privately discuss a matter with the Council President while the other ten Councilors remained in 
their seats. The Open Meeting Law requires that, prior to convening in executive session, a 
public body follow certain procedural steps in open session, including announcing the purpose 
for which it intends to convene in executive session. See G.L. c. 30A, § 21(b). There is no 
similar requirement that a public body announce why it is taking a recess from its meeting. See 
id. at §§ 18-25. Because the Council did not convene in executive session during this meeting, it 
was not required to announce the reason for its recess, and therefore did not violate the Open 
Meeting Law.
