RE: Open Meeting Law Complaint 
Dear Attorney Feodoroff: 
This office received a complaint from Stephen Morgan on October 20 alleging that the 
Ashland Board of Health (the "Board") violated the Open Meeting Law, G.L. c. 30A, §§ 18-25.' 
The complaint was originally filed with the Board on September 25, and you responded, on 
behalf of the Board, by letter dated October 4. The complaint alleges that the Board failed to 
properly respond to an Open Meeting Law complaint within 14 business days. 
After reviewing the original complaint, the Board's response to the complaint, the 
complaint filed with our office requesting further review, and the notices of the Board's 
September 14, September 19, and September 26 meetings, we resolve this complaint by 
informal action in accordance with 940 CMR 29.07(2)(a). We find that the Board violated the 
Open Meeting Law as alleged. 
The complaint concerns the Board's failure to respond to an August 31 Open Meeting 
Law complaint in the timeframe required by the Law. The Board responded to the August 
complaint by letter dated September 27 - 20 business days after the complaint was filed. A 
public body must respond to an Open Meeting Law complaint within 14 business days, unless 
the public body requests an extension from the Director of the Division of Open Government. 
G.L. c. 30A, § 23(b); 940 CMR 29.05(5). The Board acknowledges that it failed to timely 
respond to the complaint. The Board anticipated reviewing the complaint's allegations at its 
September 19.meeting but realized that the September 19 notice was insufficiently detailed with 
respect to the Open Meeting Law complaint topic. The Board, therefore, postponed discussion 
on this topic until its next meeting but failed to request an extension. We commend the Board 
for following the Open Meeting Law's meeting notice requirements.

Because the Board failed to respond to an Open Meeting Law complaint within 14 
business days, we find that the Board violated the Open Meeting Law. We order the Board's 
immediate and future compliance with the Open Meeting Law, and we caution that similar future 
violations may be considered evidence of intent to violate the law. We now consider the 
complaint addressed by this determination to be resolved. This determination does not address 
any other complaints that may be pending with our office or the Board. Please feel free to 
contact our office at (617) 963-2540 if you have any questions regarding this letter.
