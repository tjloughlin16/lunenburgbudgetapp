RE: Open Meeting Law Complaint 
Dear Attorney Riley: 
This office received a complaint from Mr. Richard Squailia, dated January 9, 2015, 
alleging that the West Stockbridge Board of Health (the "Board") violated the Open Meeting 
Law, G.L. c. 3OA, §§ 18-25. The complaint was originally filed with the Board on October 20, 
2014, and the Board responded to the original complaint by letter dated January 6, 2015. In his 
complaint, Mr. Squailia alleges that the Board deliberated outside of an open meeting. 
Following our review, we find that the Board did not violate the Open Meeting Law. In 
reaching a determination, we reviewed the original complaint; the Board's response; and the 
request for further review filed with our office. We also reviewed meeting agendas and minutes 
for all meetings of the Board held between January 2012 and October 2014.1

of West Stockbridge. On August 29, 2014, a letter was sent to Mr. Squailia concerning the 
expansion of the sewer system in West Stockbridge. This letter reads, in pertinent part, "To 
protect public health and the environment all properties eligible to tie into the municipal sewer

Olander, Mr. Moffet drafted, among others, the August 29, 2014 letter sent to Mr. Squailia. Mr. 
Moffet acted without consulting the other Board members. 
DISCUSSION 
The Open Meeting Law requires that meetings of a public body be properly noticed and 
open to members of the public, unless an executive session is convened. See G.L. c. 30A, §§ 
20(a)-(b), 21. A "public body" is defined, in relevant part, as any "multiple-member board, 
commission, committee or subcommittee within the executive or legislative branch or within any 
county, district, city, region or town, however created, elected, appointed or otherwise 
constituted, established to serve a public purpose." G.L. c. 30A, § 18. 
Here, the complaint alleges that the Board deliberated outside of an open meeting to 
decide to send the August 29, 2014 letter, and to decide on its contents. Mr. Moffett 
communicated with Mr. Olander, who is not a Board member, regarding certain notice letters, 
including the one sent to Mr. Squailia. Without consulting the other Board members, Mr. Moffet 
then drafted the letters and sent them out. There was no communication between or among a 
quorum of the Board's members. See G.L. c. 30A, § 18; OML 2012-20. Accordingly, we find 
that there was no improper deliberation and therefore no violation of the Open Meeting Law. 
See OML 2014-122; OML 2013-133. 
While the issue was not raised in the complaint, we note our serious concerns about the 
sufficiency of the agendas and meeting minutes provided by the Board. For example, several 
agendas do not indicate the time or location of the meeting. Additionally, several sets of minutes 
contain only sparse descriptions of the discussions that took place. Public bodies must post a 
notice 48 hours in advance of a meeting, including "the date, time and place of such meeting and 
a listing of topics that the chair reasonably anticipates will be discussed at the meeting." G.L. c. 
3 OA, § 20(b). Topics must be listed on a meeting notice with "sufficient specificity to 
reasonably advise the public of issues to be discussed at the meeting." 940 CMR 29.03(l)(b). 
We generally consider a topic to include sufficient specificity when a reasonable member of the 
public could read the topic and understand what the nature of the public body's discussion will

be. See OML 2011-7. Public bodies are also required to "create and maintain accurate minutes 
of all meetings, including executive sessions, setting forth the date, time and place, the members 
present or absent, a summary of the discussions on each subject, a list of documents and other 
exhibits used at the meeting, the decisions made and the actions taken at each meeting, including 
the record of all votes." G.L. c. 30A, § 22(a). Meeting minutes should contain enough detail and 
accuracy that a member of the public who did not attend the meeting could read the minutes and 
have a clear understanding of what occurred. See OML 2013-90. We take this opportunity to 
remind the Board of its obligations under the Open Meeting Law.2 
CONCLUSION 
For the reasons stated above, we find that the Board did not violate the Open Meeting 
Law. We now consider the complaint addressed by this determination to be resolved. This 
determination does not address any other complaints that may be pending with our office or the 
Board. Please feel free to contact the Division at (617) 963 - 2540 if you have any questions.
