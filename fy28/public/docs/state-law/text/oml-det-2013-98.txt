RE: Open Meeting Law Complaint 
Dear Attorney Coyle: 
This office received a complaint from Paul Cesan, dated April 18, 2013, alleging that the 
Southwick Board of Selectmen (the "Board") violated the Open Meeting Law, G.L. c. 30A, §§ 
18-25. Mr. Cesan's complaint alleges that the Board failed to approve minutes from three 
November 2012 meetings in a timely manner.1 The complaint was filed with the Board on or 
about January 18, 2013. The Board responded by letter dated February 8, 2013. 
In reaching a determination, we reviewed the January 18, 2013 complaint filed with the 
Board; the Board's February 8. 2013 response; and the April 18, 2013 complaint filed with our 
office. Additionally, we reviewed the minutes of the Board's November 5, 2012; November 19, 
2012; and November 26, 2012 meetings; and the notice for the Board's January 14, 2013 
meeting. Following our review, we find that the Board violated the Open Meeting Law by 
failing to approve meeting minutes in a timely manner. However, we find that the Board took 
the appropriate remedial action in response to the complaint and order no further relief. 
FACTS 
The Board held meetings on November 5, 2012; November 19, 2012; and November 26, 
2012. On January 14, 2013, the Board reviewed the minutes from the November 5, 2012 and 
November 19, 2012 meetings. A Board member requested that revisions and amendments be 
made, so the Board did not approve the minutes at that time.

On January 18, 2013, the complainant filed a complaint with the Board stating that at the 
time of the complaint, none of the minutes from the three November 2012 meetings had been 
approved. 
At the next meeting of the Board, on January 28, 2013, the Board approved the minutes 
from its November 5, 2012 and November 19, 2012 meetings. At the following meeting, on 
February 4, 2013, the Board approved the minutes from its November 26, 2012 meeting. 
The Open Meeting Law requires that the "[mjinutes of all open sessions shall be created 
and approved in a timely manner." G.L. c. 30A, § 22(c). The Open Meeting Law does not 
define "timely manner," but we recommend that minutes be approved at the next meeting, 
whenever possible. See OML 2013-31; OML 2012-91.2 The Board approved the minutes of its 
November 2012 meetings more than two months after the meetings occurred, even though the 
Board generally meets on a weekly basis. We understand the demands on a volunteer Board 
with limited staff, however the Board is still obligated to create and approve minutes of its 
meetings in a timely manner. See G.L. c. 30A, § 22(c). Therefore, we find that the Board did 
not comply with the Law's requirements regarding the timely approval of minutes. However, the 
Board took appropriate remedial action by approving the minutes for its November 2012 
meetings upon receiving this complaint. Where, as occurred here, a public body takes corrective 
action in response to a complaint filed with that body, we strongly encourage complainants to 
reconsider whether further review by this office remains necessary. 
For the reasons stated above, we find that the Board violated the Open Meeting Law by 
failing to approve meeting minutes in a timely manner. Because the Board took appropriate 
action in response to the complaint, we order no additional relief. 
We now consider this matter closed. If you have any questions regarding this 
determination, please contact our office at (617) 963-2540.
