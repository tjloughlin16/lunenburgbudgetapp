RE: Open Meeting Law Complaint 
Dear Mr. Stewart: 
This office received a complaint from Mr. Robert Mitchell, dated March 3, 2012, alleging 
that the Templeton Board of Selectmen (the "Board") violated the Open Meeting Law, G.L. c. 
30A, §§ 18-25. Specifically, the complaint alleges that the notice for the Board's February 16, 
2012 was not properly posted 48 hours before the meeting.' The complaint was filed with the 
Board on or about February 18, 2012, and the Board responded by letter on February 27, 2012. 
Following our review, we find that Board did not violate the Open Meeting Law. In 
reaching this determination, we reviewed the February 18, 2012 complaint filed with the Board; 
the Board's February 27, 2012 response; and the March 3, 2012 complaint filed with our office. 
Additionally, we reviewed the notice and minutes for the Board's February 16, 2012 meeting. 
FACTS 
Notice of the Board's February 16, 2012 meeting was posted on a glass bulletin board 
outside the Town Office Building on February 14, 2012. The notice was then added to the 
Town's website on February 15, 2012. At the time of the posting, Templeton had not filed 
written notice of adoption of an alternative notice posting method with the Attorney General's 
Office. 
An additional allegation states that one member of the Board may have had a conflict of interest in a vote taken 
during the February 16, 2012 meeting. Because this allegation does not implicate the Open Meeting Law, we 
decline to review it.

DISCUSSION 
The Open Meeting Law requires that all meetings of a public body be properly noticed. 
G.L. c. 30A, § 20(b). Notice of every meeting must be posted "at least 48 hours prior to such 
meeting, excluding Saturdays, Sundays and legal holidays," and every notice shall include "the 
date, time and place of such meeting and a listing of topics that the chair reasonably anticipates 
will be discussed at the meeting." Id. For meetings of a local public body, notice must be filed 
"with the municipal clerk and posted in a manner conspicuously visible to the public at all hours 
in or on the municipal building in which the clerk's office is located." G.L. c. 30A, § 20(c). The 
Attorney General's Regulations provide that municipalities may choose an alternative method of 
posting notice, including posting notice to the municipal website. 940 CMR 29.03(2)(b). 
The Town of Templeton has not filed written notice of adoption of an alternative notice 
posting method with the Attorney General's Office. See 940 CMR 29.03(1)(c). Therefore, its 
meeting notices must be posted "in a manner conspicuously visible to the public at all hours in or 
on the municipal building in which the clerk's office is located." G.L. c. 30A, § 20(c). The 
bulletin board outside the Town Office Building meets this requirement. The Board posted 
notice on the bulletin board 48 hours before its February 16, 2012 meeting. Any additional 
notice, such as posting to a website, is a matter of courtesy to the public and not required by the 
Open Meeting Law. We therefore find that the Board complied with the Open Meeting Law's 
notice posting requirements for its February 16, 2012 meeting. 
CONCLUSION 
For the reasons stated above, we find that the Board did not violate the Open Meeting 
Law. We now consider this matter closed. Please contact me if you have any questions 
regarding this letter.
