This office received a complaint from Elvio Rodrigues on April 10, 2024, alleging that 
the Yarmouth Housing Authority Board of Commissioners (the “Board”) violated the Open 
Meeting Law, G.L. c. 30A, §§ 18-25.  The complaint was originally filed with the Board on 
March 11, 2024, and you
1 responded, on behalf of the Board, by letter dated April 11, 2024.2  
The complaint alleges that the Board failed to properly post notice of meetings held on April 11, 
2023, and March 5, 2024.    
 
Following our review, we find that the Board violated the Open Meeting Law by failing 
to properly post notice of a meeting held on March 5, 2024.  We find that the Board did not hold 
a meeting on April 11, 2023.  In reaching this determination, we reviewed the original complaint, 
the Board’s response to the complaint, and the complaint filed with our office requesting further 
review.  We also reviewed the notice and open session minutes of the Board meeting held on 
March 5, 2024.

The Open Meeting Law requires that, except in an emergency, “a public body shall post 
notic
e of every meeting at least 48 hours prior to such meeting, excluding Saturdays, Sundays 
and legal holidays.”  G.L. c. 30A, § 20(b).  The notice must be printed in a legible, easily 
understandable format and must contain the date, time and place of the meeting, as well as a 
listing of topics that the chair reasonably anticipates will be discussed.  Id.  The list of topics 
shall have “sufficient specificity to reasonably advise the public of the issues to be discussed at 
the meeting.”  940 CMR 29.03(l)(b).   
 
For local public bodies, notice shall be filed with the municipal clerk and posted in a 
manne
r conspicuously visible to the public at all hours in or on the municipal building in which 
the clerk’s office is located.  G.L. c. 30A, § 20(c).  The governing board of a local housing 
authority “shall be deemed a local public body.”  G.L. c. 30A, § 18.  Alternatively, a 
municipality may designate a website as its official notice posting location for all public bodies 
within the municipality.  940 CMR 29.03(2)(b).  The Town of Yarmouth has adopted its website, 
http://www.yarmouth.ma.us/, as the official method of posting notice.   
 
The c
omplaint alleges that the Board did not properly post notice of meetings held on 
April 11, 2023, and March 5, 2024.  Complaints alleging violations of the Open Meeting Law 
must be filed with the public body within 30 days of the alleged violation.  G.L. c. 30A, § 23(b).  
If the alleged violation could not reasonably have been known at the time it occurred, then the 
complaint must be filed within 30 days of the date it should reasonably have been discovered.  
940 CMR 29.05(3).  When an alleged violation occurs during an open meeting, the alleged 
violation is reasonably discoverable at the time it occurs.  See OML 2014-85; OML 2012-52.
3  
We need not decide here whether the allegation regarding the April 11, 2023, meeting was 
timely filed as the Board did not actually hold a meeting that day because of a lack of quorum.  
Therefore, where no meeting occurred, the Board could not violate the Open Meeting Law by 
either failing to post notice or posting an insufficient notice.  See OML 2023-87.   
 
However, we find that the Board violated the Open Meeting Law by not properly posting 
notice of the March 5, 2024, meeting.  Here, the Board’s Executive Director faxed a notice for 
the March 5, 2024, meeting to the Town Clerk’s Office on March 1, 2024, at 2:34 P.M.  While 
we acknowledge that the notice was sent to the Clerk’s Office 48 hours prior to the meeting, the 
notice did not get posted.  Nevertheless, the Board met on March 5, 2024, for approximately 15 
minutes and approved one set of meeting minutes and a payroll warrant, accepted 
correspondence, and received updates from the Executive Director.   
 
The Board suggests that it relies on the Town Clerk to post notices as it does not have 
acc
ess to the municipal website and states that notice was also posted in the Board’s Community 
Room.  The responsibility for ensuring compliance with the Open Meeting Law lies with the 
Board, not the Town Clerk.  See OML 2016-168.  Even if the Board is unable to post its own 
meeting notices to the municipal website, as is often the case, it nonetheless must ensure that its 
meeting notices are timely posted before proceeding with a scheduled meeting.  Moreover, 
although public bodies are welcome to post notice of their meetings in multiple locations if they 
choose, a local public body has one official notice posting location for purposes of complying

with the Open Meeting Law.  See 940 CMR 29.03(2)(b)(3) & (c); OML 2021-94; OML 2020-20.  
Accordingly, we find that the Board violated the Open Meeting Law by failing to post notice of 
its March 5, 2024, meeting to the municipal website, the official posting location for all public 
bodies in the Town of Yarmouth.   
 
In his request for further review, the complainant alleges that the minutes of the March 5, 
2024, mee
ting are insufficient as they “contain no dialogue.”  We generally decline to review an 
allegation that was not included within the original complaint to the Board because the Board has 
not had an opportunity to respond to it.  See G.L. c. 30A, § 23(b); 940 CMR 29.05(3).  We find 
that this allegation was not clearly raised in the original complaint and thus we do not review its 
merits.  However, we note that the Open Meeting Law requires that a public body “create and 
maintain accurate minutes of all meetings, including executive sessions, setting forth the date, 
time and place, the members present or absent, a summary of the discussions on each subject, a 
list of documents and other exhibits used at the meeting, the decisions made and the actions 
taken at each meeting, including the record of all votes.”  G.L. c. 30A, § 22(a).  When reviewing 
minutes for compliance with the Open Meeting Law, we look for substantial compliance with the 
accuracy requirement.  See OML 2016-105; OML 2013-64.  By substantial compliance, we 
mean that the minutes should contain enough detail and accuracy so that a member of the public 
who did not attend the meeting could read the minutes and have a clear understanding of what 
occurred.  See OML 2012-106.  Minutes must include more than a statement that a discussion 
was held about a specified topic; the minutes must summarize the discussions that were held.  
See OML 2023-16; OML 2019-167; OML 2018-8.  While minutes must include a summary of 
the discussion on each topic, a transcript is not required, and the minutes do not need to include 
every remark or opinion presented.  See OML 2012-29; OML 2011-55.  We remind the Board of 
its obligations under the law.    
 
For t
he reasons stated above, we find that the Board violated the Open Meeting Law.  We 
order immediate and future compliance with the law’s requirements, and we caution that similar 
future violations could be considered evidence of intent to violate the law.  Additionally, we 
order all members of the Board to attend a webinar training on the Open Meeting Law conducted 
by our office within the next 90 days.
