DISCUSSION 
The Open Meeting Law requires a public body to post notice of a meeting at least 48 
hours in advance of a meeting that includes "the date, time and place of such meeting and a 
listing of topics that the chair reasonably anticipates will be discussed at the meeting." G.L. c. 
30A, § 20(b). For local public bodies, the law requires that the municipal clerk post notice of 
meetings in a manner conspicuously visible to the public at all hours in or on the municipal 
building in which the clerk's office is located. G.L. c. 30A, § 20(c). If such notice is not 
conspicuously visible to the public during hours when the clerk's office is closed, such notice 
shall also be made available through one of the alternative methods approved by the Attorney 
General under 940 CMR 29.03(2)(b), such as a website. Topics should be listed in a meeting 
notice with "sufficient specificity to reasonably advise the public of the issues to be discussed 
at the meeting." 940 CMR 29.03(l)(b). We generally consider a topic to be sufficiently 
specific when a reasonable member of the public could read the topic and understand what the 
nature of the public body's discussion will be. See OML 2016-38; OML 2015-74.5 
The complaint alleges that the Committee met privately outside of the public's view. 
There is no dispute that the Committee is a public body subject to the Open Meeting Law. As 
the Committee acknowledged in its response, however, the Committee violated the Open 
Meeting Law by failing to properly post notice of its October 25, 2016 and April 4, 2017 
meetings. The City's official notice posting method is its website. Accordingly, the 
Committee should have filed its notices with the City Clerk to post to the City website. See 
G.L. c. 30A, § 20(a); 940 CMR 29.03. While the Committee could have published additional 
copies of notices to the School District's website, only the notices posted to the City website 
are legally valid for the purpose of the Open Meeting Law. 
Furthermore, the notices posted to the School District's website did not comply with 
the Open Meeting Law's requirement that each notice contain the date, time and location of 
the meeting, along with a list of anticipated discussion topics. See OML 2017-130; OML 
2016-145. The Committee noticed the meeting to the public by publishing a general 
announcement on the School District website's calendar, posted as "CTE Program Advisory 
Committee Meeting 6 PM- CTE Staff." Such a notice is insufficient, in violation of the Open
