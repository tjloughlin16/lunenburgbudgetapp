 RE:  Open Meeting Law Complaints 
 
Dear Attorney Braun: 
 
This office received four complaints from Steven Ballard in October 2022, alleging that 
the Boxborough Finance Committee (the “Committee”) violated the Open Meeting Law, G.L. c. 
30A, §§ 18-25.  The complaints were originally filed with the Committee between August 15, 
2022, and September 22, 2022.
1  Interim Town Administrator Carter Terenzini responded to the 
first complaint, on behalf of the Committee, by letter dated August 22, and you responded to the 
subsequent three complaints, on behalf of the Committee, by letters dated September 21 and 
October 4.  Each complaint alleges that the Committee failed to timely respond to a request for 
meeting minutes.   
 
We resolve these matters by informal action, in accordance with 940 CMR 29.07(2)(a), 
after reviewing the original complaints, the Committee’s responses to the complaints, the 
complaints filed with our office requesting further review, and the minutes of Committee 
meetings held on August 26, September 20, September 27, October 3.  In addition, we spoke 
with you by telephone on November 4.  We find that the Committee violated the Open Meeting 
Law in the ways alleged. 
 
The Open Meeting Law requires that a public body “create and maintain accurate 
minutes of all meetings, including executive sessions.”  G.L. c. 30A, § 22(a).  The minutes of an 
open session, if they exist and whether approved or in draft form, shall be made available upon

request by any person within 10 days.”  G.L. c. 30A, § 22(c).  If such minutes do not yet exist at 
the time of a request, the public body is still required to respond to the request within 10 days 
with an explanation of whether the minutes do or do not exist in either approved or draft form.  
See OML 2018-98; OML 2017-50; OML 2016-71; OML 2015-173.2 
 
A public body must also respond to a request for executive session minutes within 10 
days.  G.L. c. 30A, § 22(g)(2).  If the public body or a designee has determined, prior to the 
request, that certain executive session minutes may be released, it should respond to the requester 
directly, notifying him or her of the availability of those minutes.  See OML 2013-105; OML 
2013-99.  And if, at the time of a request, the public body has not conducted a review of the 
minutes to determine whether continued nondisclosure is warranted, the body must perform such 
a review and release the minutes, if appropriate, no later than its next meeting or within 30 days, 
whichever occurs first.  G.L. c. 30A, § 22(g)(2).  In such circumstances, the body must still 
respond to the requestor within 10 days, notifying him or her that it is conducting this review.  
See OML 2022-123; OML 2015-86; OML 2013-199; OML 2013-99.   
 
Between August 15 and September 19, the complainant sent four emails to Committee 
Chair Maria Neyland and Clerk Gary Kushner requesting meeting minutes.  Specifically, the 
complainant requested copies of the minutes of meetings held on February 1, February 28, April 
26, May 9, May 10, May 24, June 8, July 14, July 19, August 15, and August 16, as well as 
copies of executive session minutes of meetings held on June 8 and August 26.  The Committee 
did not respond to any of the four requests for minutes within the timeframe required by the 
Open Meeting Law.  The complainant thereafter filed the four Open Meeting Law complaints at 
issue here regarding the Committee’s failure to respond to his requests for meeting minutes.  
Because the Committee failed to provide a response within 10 days of each of the four requests 
for minutes, we find that it violated the Open Meeting Law by not timely responding to a request 
for minutes.  We order no further remedial action as the Committee has since posted all 
requested open session minutes to its website, and the Committee has reviewed the requested 
executive session minutes and informed the complainant that continued nondisclosure is 
warranted.
3   
 
For the reasons stated above, we find that the Committee violated the Open Meeting Law 
by failing to timely respond to requests for meeting minutes.  We order immediate and future 
compliance with the law’s requirements, and we caution that similar future violations could be 
considered evidence of intent to violate the law.
