This office received a complaint from David Manjarrez on March 19, 2023, alleging that 
the Minuteman Regional Vocational Technical School Committee (the “Committee”) violated 
the Open Meeting Law, G.L. c. 30A, §§ 18-25.  The complaint was originally filed with the 
Committee on or around March 6, 2023, and the Committee responded to the complaint by letter 
dated March 22, 2023.  The complaint alleges that the Committee failed to announce the results 
of its reviews of executive session minutes at its next meeting following each review and record 
the announcement in the minutes.   
 
Following our review, we find that the Committee did not violate the Open Meeting Law 
as alleged.  In reaching this determination, we reviewed the complaint along with all 
attachments, the Committee’s response to the complainant and additional correspondence from 
the Committee to our office, and the complaint filed with our office requesting further review.  
We also reviewed the notices and minutes for Committee meetings held on December 13 and 20, 
2022, and January 7 and 10 and February 7, 2023. 
 
The Open Meeting Law requires public bodies to create and approve meeting minutes, 
for both open and executive session, in a timely manner.  G.L. c. 30A, § 22(c).  “Timely manner” 
means “within the next three public body meetings or within 30 days, whichever is later, unless 
the public body can show good cause for further delay.”  940 CMR 29.11; see OML 2018-48.

requirement that executive session minutes be periodically reviewed to determine whether they

should be released to the public.  See OML 2019-115.  This initial approval of executive session 
minutes is to approve the minutes as an accurate record of what occurred at a particular executive 
session and does not mean that the minutes are approved for release to the public.  See OML 
2019-163. 
 
Executive session minutes may be withheld from disclosure to the public “as long as 
public
ation may defeat the lawful purposes of the executive session, but no longer.”  G.L. c. 
30A, § 22(1). When the purpose for a valid executive session has been served, the minutes and 
any documents or exhibits used at the session must be disclosed, unless the attorney-client 
privilege or an exemption to the Public Records Law applies.  Id.  Public bodies have an 
obligation to review the minutes of executive sessions at reasonable intervals to determine if 
continued non-disclosure of minutes is warranted, and “such determination shall be announced at 
the body’s next meeting and such announcement shall be included in the minutes of that 
meeting.”  G.L. c. 30A, § 22(g)(1).  Although “reasonable interval” is not defined by the law, our 
office has found a quarterly review or a review every six months to be appropriate.  See OML 
2017-104; OML 2015-166; OML 2013-180. 
 
The complaint alleges that “the com
 mittee has never announced the results of its or of its 
designee’s reviews of the committee’s withheld executive session minutes at the next meeting of 
the committee.”  Complainants must allege violations with a degree of specificity, as our office 
will not conduct broad audits of public bodies based on generalized allegations.  See OML 2021-
133; OML 2019-44; OML Declination 3-20-12 (Wilmington Board of Assessors).  Furthermore, 
we decline to review allegations of violations of the Open Meeting Law that occurred or were 
reasonably discoverable more than 30 days before the instant complaint was filed. To be 
considered timely, an Open Meeting Law complaint must be filed with the public body within 30 
days of the alleged violation, or, if the alleged violation could not reasonably have been known at 
the time it occurred, then within 30 days of the date on which the alleged violation 
could reasonably have been discovered.  See G.L. c. 30A, § 23(b); 940 CMR 29.05(4).  The 
complaint specifies an alleged violation date of June 18, 2013.  The complaint specifically 
alleges that the Committee reviewed executive session minutes during its June 18, 2013, meeting 
but the minutes of the following meeting—held on July 9, 2013—fail to capture any 
announcement about the minutes that were reviewed.  In addition, the complaint alleges that on 
March 23, 2021, the Committee, through its Communications Director, sent to the complainant 
via email minutes of the executive sessions held in 2012 and 2013, with an explanation that the 
minutes had been approved for release with redactions as permitted by the Public Records Law; 
the complaint alleges that open session meeting minutes do not document an announcement 
concerning the review of these minutes.  Therefore, at the latest, allegations concerning the 
procedures the Committee used in reviewing and releasing the 2012 and 2013 executive session 
minutes were discoverable as of March 23, 2021, when the complainant was made aware that the 
executive session minutes had been previously approved for public release.  As the complaint 
was not filed until March 5, 2023, such allegations are untimely and we decline to review them.   
 
We do, how
ever, review allegations in the complaint concerning the minutes of meetings 
held on December 13 and 20, 2022, and January 10, 2023, as minutes of those meetings were 
approved on February 7, 2023, which was less than 30 days prior to the filing of this complaint, 
===PAGE 3===
and the complaint specifically referenced those meeting dates.2  The Committee entered 
executive session on December 13 and 20, 2022, to discuss unrelated matters; the Committee did 
not review executive session meeting minutes on those dates.  Therefore, there was no 
expectation that the minutes of the next meetings thereafter would capture an announcement 
regarding minutes reviewed.  The Committee did enter executive session on January 7, 2023, for 
“review of executive session minutes for release or continued non-disclosure.”  On January 10, 
2023, which was its next meeting following the January 7 review, the Committee’s chair made 
the following announcement, which is captured in its entirety in the minutes of the January 10 
meeting:  
 
As required under the Open Meeting Law, we are announcing that, during 
executive session on January 7, 2023, the School Committee reviewed the 
executive session minutes for all of its meetings held between January 1, 2020 
and December 12, 2022, inclusive, to determine whether the continued non-
disclosure of those minutes is warranted and made the following determinations: 
 
1.) The purposes for which the following executive sessions were called 
have been served and the following executive session minutes, as currently 
approved, are to be released in their entirety, as amended: [meeting dates listed] 
 
2.) The purposes for which the following executive sessions were called 
have been served and the following executive session minutes, as currently 
approved, are to be released subject to redaction by the District’s Records Access 
Officer for all applicable exemptions under the Public Records Law: [meeting 
dates listed] 
 
3.) The purposes for which the following executive sessions were called 
has not been served, but portions of these minutes which no longer serve such 
purposes shall be released with the portions which still serve the purpose of the 
meeting remaining withheld: [meeting dates listed] 
 
4.) The purposes for which the following executive sessions were called 
has not been served and the following executive session minutes continue to be 
withheld in their entirety: [meeting dates listed] 
 
Thus, it is clear that the Committee complied with the Open Meeting Law’s requirement that 
“[s]uch determination shall be announced at the body’s next meeting and such announcement 
shall be included in the minutes of that meeting.”  G.L. c. 30A, § 22(g)(1).
