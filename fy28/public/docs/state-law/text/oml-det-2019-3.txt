RE: Open Meeting Law Complaint
Dear Attorney Riley:
This office received a complaint from Susan Butler on October 18 alleging that the 
Sheffield Board of Selectmen (the “Board”) violated the Open Meeting Law, G.L. c. 30A, §§ 18- 
25.1 The complaint was originally filed with the Board on September 18; and you responded, on 
behalf of the Board, by letter dated October 2. The complaint alleges that the Board failed to 
timely review its executive session meeting minutes and provide those minutes upon request.
Following our review, we find that the Board violated the Open Meeting Law by failing 
to properly respond to a request for executive session minutes and failing to periodically review 
its executive session minutes. In reaching this determination, we reviewed the original 
complaint, the Board’s response to the complaint, and the complaint filed with our office 
requesting further review. We also reviewed open session minutes of Board meetings held 
between August 6 and September 10, as well as the notice and open session minutes of the 
Board’s September 24 meeting. Finally, we spoke with the complainant by telephone on 
December 13.
FACTS
We find the facts as follows. On August 13, the complainant sent a letter to Town Clerk 
Felecie Joyce, as Records Access Officer, and the Board requesting to review all executive 
session minutes of meetings held in 2018; executive session minutes of Board meetings held on 
January 17, 2017; July 18, 2017; August 22, 2017; and September 26, 2017; and “any other 
executive session minutes for 2017 not listed.” The Town Clerk responded on August 27 stating 
that minutes of the Board’s January 17, 2017 meeting do not exist and that “the remaining three 
dates for 2017, and the 2018 minutes have not been submitted to this office.”

On September 3, the complainant sent an identical letter to the Town Clerk and the Board 
again requesting to review specific executive session minutes from 2017 and 2018. Having 
received no further response, this complaint was filed with the Board on September 18. The 
Board responded on October 2 and provided certain of the requested minutes.
The Board held meetings on August 6, August 7, August 20, and August 21 but did not 
review any minutes of earlier executive session during those meetings. The Board also held a 
meeting on September 10 and reviewed but did not release executive session minutes of 
meetings held on June 19, June 25, and July 31.
On September 24, the Board met and discussed the complaint. The Board also reviewed 
and released executive session minutes of meetings held on April 3; May 1; May 21; June 25; 
July 18; and July 31. In addition, the Board reviewed but decided not to release executive 
session minutes of meetings held on August 22, 2017; September 26, 2017; April 17; June 4; 
June 19; and September 10. Minutes that were approved for release have been provided to the 
complainant.
DISCUSSION
The complaint alleges that the Board failed to review executive session minutes from 
2017 and 2018 to determine whether there was a continued purpose for nondisclosure and failed 
to respond to her multiple requests for those minutes. The Open Meeting Law requires that a 
public body “create and maintain accurate minutes of all meetings, including executive 
sessions.” G.L. c. 30A, § 22(a). The meeting minutes must be reviewed and approved in a 
timely manner. G.L. c. 30A, § 22(c). Executive session minutes may be withheld from 
disclosure to the public “as long as publication may defeat the lawful purposes of the executive 
session, but no longer.” G.L. c. 30A, § 22(f). When the purpose for a valid executive session 
has been served, the minutes and any documents or exhibits used at the session must be disclosed 
unless the attorney-client privilege or an exemption to the public records law applies to withhold 
them, in whole or in part, from disclosure. See id. Public bodies have an obligation to review 
the minutes of executive sessions at reasonable intervals to determine if continued non-disclosure 
of minutes is warranted, and to announce that determination at the next meeting following its 
review. G.L. c. 30A, § 22(g)(1); see OML 2013-56; OML 2015-94.2
Here, the complainant requested executive session minutes on August 13. Town Clerk 
Joyce responded on August 27 stating that minutes either did not exist or had not been submitted 
by the Board. The complainant requested the same executive session minutes on September 3 
but received no further response to her request until October 2 when the Board responded to this 
complaint. Upon request by any person to inspect or copy the minutes of an executive session or 
any portion thereof, the body must respond to the request within 10 days following receipt and 
release any such minutes where publication would not defeat the lawful purposes of the 
executive session. See G.L. c. 30A, § 22(g)(2). If the body has not performed a review, it must 
do so and release any non-exempt minutes, or portions thereof, not later than the body’s next

meeting or in 30 days, whichever first occurs. See Id.; OML 2013-180. At the time of the 
August 13 request, the Board had not reviewed the requested executive session minutes. The 
Board’s next meeting following the August 13 request was on August 20 yet it did not conduct a 
review of executive session minutes at that meeting. We find that the Board violated the Open 
Meeting Law where it did not review the minutes at the next available meeting but instead 
waited until September 24. See OML 2016-135.
Moreover, the Board was obligated to respond to the complainant within 10 days of her 
August 13 request. While the complainant received an initial response to her request from the 
Town Clerk, it was not within 10 days as required by law. See G.L. c. 30A, § 22(g)(2). The 
Board did not respond until October 2 when it responded to this complaint. The Board should 
have informed Ms. Butler within 10 days of her request that it was conducting a review of the 
executive session minutes. See OML 2016-135; OML 2013-99. Because the Board did not do 
so, it violated the Open Meeting Law by failing to properly respond to a request for minutes.
CONCLUSION
We find that the Board violated the Open Meeting Law by failing to properly respond to 
a request for executive session minutes and failing to periodically review its executive session 
minutes. We order the Board’s immediate and future compliance with the law’s requirements, 
and we caution that similar future violations could be considered evidence of intent to violate the 
law.
We now consider the complaint addressed by this determination to be resolved. This 
determination does not address any other complaints that may be pending with the Board or with 
our office. Please feel free to contact our office at (617) 963-2540 if you have any questions 
regarding this letter.
