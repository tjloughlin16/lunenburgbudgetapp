DISCUSSION 
 
The Open Meeting Law requires that, except in an emergency, “a public body shall post 
notice of every meeting at least 48 hours prior to such meeting, excluding Saturdays, Sundays 
and legal holidays.”  G.L. c. 30A, § 20(b).  Every notice must include “the date, time and place 
of such meeting and a listing of topics that the chair reasonably anticipates will be discussed at 
the meeting.”  G.L. c. 30A, § 20(b).  Public bodies are required to list topics on a notice with 
“sufficient specificity to reasonably advise the public of the issues to be discussed at the 
meeting.”  940 CMR 29.03(1) (b).  We generally consider a topic sufficiently specific when a 
reasonable member of the public could read the topic and understand the anticipated nature of 
the public body’s discussion.  See OML 2015-35.4 
 
The complaint alleges that the notice for the Board’s April 13 meeting “was false and 
misleading with respect to” site plan applications for the proposed solar panels on Ring Road.  
The April 13 notice included a public hearing for the two solar panel site plan applications.  
However, prior to the April 13 meeting, the Board amended the notice stating that the hearing for 
the Site Plan Review applications for both 126 Ring Rd and 131-137 Ring Road would not be 
heard on April 13 but instead would be continued to the 4th Monday following the Governor’s 
lifting of the State of Emergency.  Yet, on April 27, the Board held a meeting in which it 
discussed and unanimously voted to approve the solar panel site plan applications for both 126 
and 131-137 Ring Road with certain conditions.  Nonetheless, we find that the Board did not 
violate the Open Meeting Law where the Board posted notice of the April 27 meeting 48 hours 
prior to the meeting and sufficiently described the topics to be discussed during the meeting, in 
compliance with the Open Meeting Law. 
 
 While we do not find a violation here, we note our concern that the Board posted notice 
just two days in advance and held a meeting on April 27 where it discussed the Ring Road solar 
panel site plan applications after it had specifically stated, only two weeks earlier, that it would 
not hold a hearing or review the applications until after the State of Emergency is lifted.  We 
commend the Board for holding another public hearing on July 27 where it again discussed the 
Ring Road solar panel site plan applications and re-voted to approve the applications with 
conditions. 
 
CONCLUSION 
 
For the reasons stated above, we find that the Board did not violate the Open Meeting 
Law.  We now consider the complaint addressed by this determination to be resolved.  This 
determination does not address any other complaints that may be pending with our office or the 
Board.  Please feel free to contact our office at (617) 963-2540 if you have any questions 
regarding this letter.
