RE: Open Meeting Law Complaint 
Dear Mr. Valanzola, 
On November 15, 2010, this office received a complaint by Mr. Richard Learned alleging 
violations of the Open Meeting Law, G.L. c. 30A, §§ 18-25, by the Wales Board of Selectmen 
(the "Board"). Specifically, the complaint alleges that the Board violated the Open Meeting Law 
by failing to post a meeting notice 48 hours in advance of the July 6, 2010 meetnig. A copy of 
that complaint is enclosed. The complaint was originally filed with the Board on July 19, 2010. 
The Board's response to the complaint was received on August 12, 2010. 
The complaint alleges that Board failed to provide notice of its July 6, 2010 meeting as 
required by the Open Meeting Law, G.L. c. 30A, § 20(b). That provision of the law states that 
"[e]xcept in an emergency, in addition to any notice otherwise required by law, a public body 
shall post notice of every meeting at least 48 hours prior to such meeting, excluding Saturdays, 
Sundays and legal holidays... Notice shall be printed in a legible, easily understandable format 
and shall contain the date, time and place of such meeting and a listing of topics that the chair 
reasonably anticipates will be discussed at the meeting." In response to Mr. Learned's 
complaint, the Board provided a letter from Ms. Kaye Rose Worth, the Board's Executive 
Secretary, dated August 12, 2010. In that letter, Ms. Worth states that "[a] regularly Scheduled 
meeting was properly posted for July 6, 2010." She also provided a copy of the meeting notice 
for the July 6, 2010, which has a post date of June 30, 2010 (see attached enclosures). 
Ms. Worth attested by letter and over the phone in a conversation on November 16, 2010 
that she posted the meeting notice on June 30, 2010 for the July 6, 2010 Board meeting, well 
before the 48 hour period required for a meeting notice. Our office credits these statements as 
true as we are not in receipt of any clear evidence to the contrary. However, notwithstanding 
that the notice was posted in a timely manner, it was nonetheless deficient in meeting the

requirements of the Open Meeting Law, G.L. c. 30A, § 20(b). While the notice contained the 
date, time, and location of the meeting, it did not contain "a listing of topics that the chair 
reasonably anticipates will be discussed at the meeting," This is a new requirement as of July 1, 
2010. 
The notice was posted on June 30, 2010, however, the meeting occurred on July 6, 2010, 
after the new Open Meeting Law took effect. Therefore the notice was required to contain the 
listing of topics the chair reasonably anticipated would be discussed. Given the timing of the 
violation during the first week that the revisions to the law took place and the conversations our 
office has had with the Board since this complaint was filed, we are satisfied that the Board is 
aware of its obligations under the Open Meeting Law. Accordingly, we order immediate and 
future compliance with the requirements of the Open Meeting Law. We decline to take the 
additional step requested by the complainant of nullifying the July 6, 2010 meeting.
