This office received two complaints from Patrick Higgins on May 23, 2026, alleging that 
the Hopedale Select Board (the “Board”) violated the Open Meeting Law, G.L. c. 30A, §§ 18-25.  
The first complaint was originally filed with the Board on or about May 12, 2026, while the 
second complaint was originally filed with the Board on or about May 13, 2026. Former Town 
Administrator Mitch Ruscitti responded to both complaints, on behalf of the Board, by letter on 
or about May 22, 2026. The first complaint alleges that the Board posted a notice with 
insufficient specificity for a meeting held on April 13, 2026, and failed to verbally announce that 
the meeting was being recorded. The second complaint alleges that the Board posted a notice 
with insufficient specificity for a meeting held on April 27, 2026. 
 
Following our review, we find that the Board violated the Open Meeting Law in the ways 
alleged. In reaching this determination, we reviewed the original complaints, the Board’s 
response to the complaints, and the complaints filed with our office requesting further review. 
We also reviewed the notices, minutes and video recordings of the Board meetings held on April 
13 and April 27, 2026.

FACTS 
We find the facts to be as follows. On April 8, 2026, at 1:51 P.M., the Board posted 
notice of a meeting to be held on April 13, 2026, at 6:30 P.M. The notice included nine topics, 
one of which was “Appointments and Resignations: 1. Treasury/Collections Clerk 
Appointment.” 
The April 13, 2026, meeting was held as planned. During the meeting, the Board heard 
from the Town Administrator who advised that he and three other individuals conducted 
interviews for an entry level clerk position in the Treasurer’s Office. The Town Administrator 
informed the Board that the recommended candidate for the position was Gianna Pedroli. Board 
Member Bernard Stock then made a motion, which was seconded by Board Member Glenda 
Hazard,
2 to appoint Ms. Pedroli to the position. The Board unanimously voted to appoint Ms. 
Pedroli as a clerk in the Treasurer’s Office. 
 On April 23, 2026, at 1:25 P.M., the Board posted notice of a meeting to be held on 
April 27, 2026, at 6:30 P.M. The notice listed nine topics, one of which was “Appointment and 
Resignations: 1. Call Firefighters appointments (2) and resignations (1).” 
 The April 27, 2026, meeting was held as planned. During the meeting, the Board 
announced that it had received a letter of resignation from a named firefighter. Mr. Stock made a 
motion, which was seconded by Ms. Hazard, to accept the resignation, and the Board 
unanimously voted to accept the resignation. The Board then heard from the Fire Chief who 
introduced two individuals, Joseph LeClaire and Corey Roe, and recommended that the Board 
appoint the two individuals as call firefighters. Mr. Stock made a motion, which was seconded 
by Ms. Hazard, to appoint Mr. LeClaire and Mr. Roe as call firefighters in the Town of 
Hopedale. The Board unanimously voted to appoint Mr. LeClaire and Mr. Roe as call 
firefighters. 
DISCUSSION 
 
The Open Meeting Law requires that, except in an emergency, “a public body shall post 
notice of every meeting at least 48 hours prior to such meeting, excluding Saturdays, Sundays 
and legal holidays.” G.L. c. 30A, § 20(b). The notice must be printed in a legible, easily 
understandable format and must contain the date, time and place of the meeting, as well as a 
listing of topics that the chair reasonably anticipates will be discussed. Id. The list of topics shall 
have “sufficient specificity to reasonably advise the public of the issues to be discussed at the 
meeting.” 940 CMR 29.03(l)(b). We generally consider a topic to be sufficiently specific when a 
reasonable member of the public could read the topic and understand the anticipated nature of 
the public body’s discussion. See OML 2015-35; OML 2013-39.

to be appointed. During the April 13, 2026, meeting, the Board appointed an individual as a clerk 
in the Treasurer’s Office. During the April 27, 2026, meeting, the Board appointed two 
individuals as call firefighters. We have consistently stated that the names of applicants for 
appointment or employment, which are known 48 hours in advance of a meeting, should be 
included in a meeting notice. See OML 2022-217; 2019-24; OML 2016-150; OML 2013-163. 
Here, the Board did not assert that it did not know the names of the candidates to be considered 
for appointment during the April 13 and April 27, 2026, meetings. Therefore, we find that the 
Board violated the Open Meeting Law when it did not list in the notices the names of the 
candidates it planned to consider for appointment during the April 13 and April 27, 2026, 
meetings.   
With respect to the allegation in the first complaint that the Chair of the Board4 failed to 
verbally announce that the April 13, 2026, meeting was being recorded, the Board 
acknowledges, and we find, that the Chair failed to make the required announcement and thus 
violated the Open Meeting Law. The Open Meeting Law requires that after “notifying the chair 
of the public body, any person may make a video or audio recording of an open session of a 
meeting of a public body, or may transmit the meeting through any medium . . . . At the 
beginning of the meeting, the chair shall inform other attendees of any recordings.” G.L. c. 30A, 
§ 20(f). The requirement that the chair inform attendees of any recording includes any recording 
made by members of the public body itself, including those made for public broadcasting or 
administrative purposes, such as assisting in the drafting of meeting minutes. See OML 2019-
129; OML 2016-155; OML 2013-136. We note the Board’s efforts to take corrective action and 
comply with the law in the future.  
CONCLUSION 
 
For the reasons stated above, we find that the Board violated the Open Meeting Law by 
failing to list the candidates’ names on the notices of the April 13 and April 27, 2026, meetings. 
We also find that the Board violated the Open Meeting Law by failing to make the required 
verbal announcement that the April 13, 2026, meeting was being recorded. We order the Board’s 
immediate and future compliance with the Open Meeting Law, and we caution that similar future 
violations may be considered evidence of an intent to violate the law. 
 
We now consider the complaints addressed by this determination to be resolved. This 
determination does not address any other complaints that may be pending with the Board or with 
our office. Please feel free to contact our office at (617) 963-2540 if you have any questions 
regarding this letter.
