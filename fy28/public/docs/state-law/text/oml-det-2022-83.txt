 RE: Open Meeting Law Complaints 
 
Dear Attorney Feodoroff: 
 
This office received three complaints from Patrick Higgins on March 29, 2022, alleging 
that the Rehoboth Board of Selectmen (the “Board”) violated the Open Meeting Law, G.L. c. 
30A, §§ 18-25.  The complaints were originally filed with the Board on or about February 27, 
2022, and you responded to all three complaints, on behalf of the Board, by letter dated March 8, 
2022.  The complaints allege that the Board failed to timely approve the minutes of meetings 
held on May 24, 2021, June 14, 2021, July 6, 2021, and September 7, 2021.   
 
We resolve this matter by informal action, in accordance with 940 CMR 29.07(2)(a), 
after reviewing the original complaints, the Board’s response to the complaints, and the 
complaint filed with our office requesting further review.  We find that the Board violated the 
Open Meeting Law in the way alleged.   
 
The Open Meeting Law requires that a public body “create and maintain accurate 
minutes of all meetings, including executive sessions, setting forth the date, time and place, the 
members present or absent, a summary of the discussions on each subject, a list of documents 
and other exhibits used at the meeting, the decisions made and the actions taken at each meeting, 
including the record of all votes.”  G.L. c. 30A, § 22(a).  The meeting minutes must be reviewed 
and approved in a timely manner.  G.L. c. 30A, § 22(c).  “Timely manner” means “within the 
next three public body meetings or within 30 days, whichever is later, unless the public body can

show good cause for further delay.”  See 940 CMR 29.11; OML 2018-48.1  Whenever possible, 
we recommend that minutes of a meeting be approved at the next meeting.  See OML 2018-67; 
OML 2017-133.  
 
The Board was required to approve the minutes of the meetings held on May 24, 2021, 
June 14, 2021, July 6, 2021, and September 7, 2021, within 30 days as that was the later of the 
two time periods for these minutes because the Board met at least three times within 30 days 
following these four meetings.  See 940 CMR 29.11; OML 2018-152.  Since the Board did not 
approve its May 24, 2021, minutes until February 7, 2022, which was 260 days thereafter; did 
not approve its June 14, 2021, minutes until January 31, 2022, which was 232 days thereafter; 
did not approve its July 6, 2021, minutes until January 31, 2022, which was 210 days thereafter; 
and did not approve its September 7, 2021, minutes until February 22, 2022, which was 169 days 
thereafter, we find that the Board violated the Open Meeting Law by failing to approve these 
minutes in a timely manner.2 
 
We remind the Board that the responsibility for complying with the Open Meeting Law 
rests with public bodies and members of such bodies, not with individual public employees, such 
as administrative staff.  See OML 2018-105.  We understand the demands on a public body with 
limited staff; however, the Board is still obligated to timely approve minutes.  See OML 2018-
111; OML 2017-115.  If staff resources are not available to create minutes for the Board to 
approve, then Board members must take on this responsibility to ensure compliance with the law.  
See OML 2017-115.   
 
We must now determine whether these violations are, as the complainant urges, 
intentional ones.  See G.L. c. 30A, § 23(c).  An intentional violation is an “act or omission by a 
public body or a member thereof, in knowing violation of [the Open Meeting Law].”  940 CMR 
29.02.  An intentional violation may be found where the public body acted with deliberate 
ignorance of the law’s requirement or has previously been advised that certain conduct violates 
the Open Meeting Law.  Id.  On December 8, 2021, our office found that the Board violated the 
Open Meeting Law by failing to approve minutes of other meetings in a timely manner.  See 
OML 2021-181.  However, we do not rely on that determination as the basis for a finding of an 
intentional violation, as the minutes at issue here were already untimely prior to the guidance 
issued in that determination.  Even if the Board had approved the minutes immediately following 
the issuance of the determination, the minutes would still have been deemed untimely.  We 
acknowledge that the Board has hired additional staff to assist with the backlog of minutes and 
appears to be making a good faith effort to comply with the law’s requirements.  Therefore, we 
decline to find that this violation is intentional.  See G.L. c. 30A, § 18; 940 CMR 29.02.   
 
For the reasons stated above, we find that the Board violated the Open Meeting Law by 
failing to approve minutes of its May 24, 2021, June 14, 2021, July 6, 2021, and September 7, 
2021, meetings in a timely manner.  Although the minutes of the above-referenced meeting dates 
have since been approved, we understand an additional 15-20 sets of minutes of meetings held 
between September and December 2021 are still not created or approved.  We order the Board to
