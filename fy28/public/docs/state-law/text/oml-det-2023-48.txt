DISCUSSION 
The Open Meeting Law requires that a public body “create and maintain accurate 
minutes of all meetings, including executive sessions, setting forth the date, time and place, the 
members present or absent, a summary of the discussions on each subject, a list of documents 
and other exhibits used at the meeting, the decisions made and the actions taken at each meeting, 
including the record of all votes.” G.L. c. 30A, § 22(a). The meeting minutes must be reviewed 
and approved in a timely manner. G.L. c. 30A, § 22(c). The Open Meeting Law itself does not 
define “timely manner.” However, the Attorney General's Open Meeting Law regulations 
provide that “timely manner” means “within the next three public body meetings or within 30 
days, whichever is later, unless the public body can show good cause for further delay.” 940 
C.M.R. 29.11(2); OML 2018-48. Whenever possible, we recommend that minutes of a meeting 
be approved at the next meeting. See OML 2018-67; OML 2017-133.  
The complainant alleges that the Board did not timely approve minutes from the meetings 
held on September 26, October 3, October 11, October 17, and October 24. The Board asserts 
that pursuant to our determination in OML 2022-236, it has ninety days from the date the 
determination was issued and received, December 28, to approve all meeting minutes that were 
then outstanding, which included minutes for the meetings at issue in this complaint. The Board 
is correct. To be clear, our determination ordered the Board to approve outstanding meeting 
minutes as of December 28 within ninety days. That means the Board has ninety days to approve 
all meeting minutes from September, October, November, and December as of December 28. 
Meetings that took place after December 28, however, are not subject to the ninety day 
timeframe, and those minutes must be approved “within the next three public body meetings or 
within 30 days, whichever is later” pursuant to the Open Meeting Law regulations.

CONCLUSION 
 For the reasons stated above, where the Board approved the meeting minutes at issue in 
accordance with our order issued on December 28, we do not find that the Board violated the 
Open Meeting Law as alleged in the complaint. We now consider the complaint addressed by 
this determination to be resolved. This determination does not address any other complaints that 
may be pending with our office or the Board. Please feel free to contact the Division at (617) 963 
- 2540 if you have any questions.
