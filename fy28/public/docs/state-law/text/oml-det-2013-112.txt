RE: Open Meeting Law Complaint 
Dear Mr. Goldsmith: 
Our office received a complaint from George Harris, dated December 3, 2012, alleging a 
violation of the Open Meeting Law, G.L. c. 30A, §§18-25, by the Wayland Surface Water 
Quality Committee (the "Committee"). Specifically, the complaint alleges that at its September 
5, 2012 meeting, the Committee "(1) failed to follow proper procedures for remote participation 
and (2) deliberated on topics that had not been described in the meeting notice with sufficient 
specificity to reasonably advise the public of issues to be discussed." The complaint was filed 
with the Committee on or about September 24, 2012. The Committee responded by letter dated 
October 24, 2012. 
Following our review, we find that Committee violated the Open Meeting Law by failing 
to list topics with sufficient specificity in the notice for its September 5, 2012 meeting, and by 
failing to follow certain remote participation procedures during that same meeting. In reaching 
this determination, we reviewed the September 24, 2012 complaint; the Committee's October 24, 
2012 response; and the December 3, 2012 complaint filed with our office. We also reviewed the 
minutes and revised minutes of the Committee's September 5, 2012 and October 11, 2012 
meetings. Finally, we reviewed the notices for Committee's July 11, 2012; July 25, 2012; 
August 8, 2012; August 22, 2012; and September 5, 2012 meetings. 
The Committee is a five-member public body. The Committee posted a notice for its 
September 5, 2012 meeting that listed nine topics, including "Dudley Pond," "Heard Pond," 
"Lake Cochituate," "Old Business," "New Business," and "Invoice Approval." On September 5, 
2012, the Committee held its meeting. Three members of the Committee were physically present

at the meeting. A fourth member, Co-Chair Toni Moores, participated by telephone. The 
minutes do not reflect the reason for Ms. Moores' remote participation. During the meeting, the

DISCUSSION 
The complaint alleges that the notice for the September 5, 2012 meeting did not list the 
topics that the Chair anticipated for discussion with enough specificity. The Open Meeting Law 
requires that meetings of a public body be properly noticed, at least 48 hours prior to the 
meeting, in a "legible, easily understandable format and shall contain the date, time and place of 
such meeting and a listing of topics that the chair reasonably anticipates will be discussed at the 
meeting." G.L. c. 30A, § 20(b). The listing of topics "shall have sufficient specificity to 
reasonably advise the public of the issues to be discussed at the meeting." 940 CMR 
29.03(l)(b); OML 2011-15.1 
The topics listed in the notice for the September 5, 2012 meeting include "Dudley Pond," 
"Heard Pond," "Lake Cochituate," "Old Business," "New Business," and "Invoice Approval." 
The name of the Committee is the "Wayland Surface Water Quality Committee" thus, with 
respect to the bodies of water listed in the meeting notice, a member of the public could 
reasonably infer that the discussion about those bodies would relate to their surface water 
quality. We therefore find that, while additional detail may have been helpful if available (such 
as adding the description "plant removal" next to the name of the ponds), these topics were 
sufficiently specific. However, we find that the topics "Old Business," "New Business," and 
"Invoice Approval" were not sufficiently specific. The Committee should have provided 
additional detail about these items "to reasonably advise the public of the issues to be discussed 
at the meeting." 940 CMR 29.03; OML 2013-59; OML 2013-14. 
The complaint also alleges that the Committee failed to follow required procedure when 
one of its members participated remotely during the September 5, 2012 meeting. When 
conducting a meeting where a member is participating remotely, the person chairing the meeting 
must announce "the name of any member who will be participating remotely and the reason 
under 940 CMR 29.10(5) for his or her remote participation. This information shall also be 
recorded in the meeting minutes." 940 CMR 29.10(7)(b). The five permitted reasons for 
participating remotely are personal illness, personal disability, emergency, military service, and 
geographic distance. 940 CMR 29.10(5). All votes taken during any meeting in which a 
member participates remotely must be by roll call vote. 940 CMR 29.10(7)(c).

During the September 5, 2012 meeting, Committee Co-Chair Toni Moores participated 
remotely. The minutes do not reflect which of the five reasons outlined in 940 CMR 29.10(5) 
formed the basis for Ms. Moores' participation. Additionally, the minutes do not note whether 
the vote that was taken was conducted by roll call, noting only that the outcome was unanimous. 
We therefore find that the Committee failed to follow the proper remote participation procedures. 
In its October 24, 2012 response to the complaint, the Committee writes that it "will be vigilant 
in the future about following the remote participation procedures established by the Office of the 
Attorney General. Additionally, the minutes of the September 5, 2012 meeting will be corrected 
to include the names of any members who participated remotely and the reason for his remote 
participation." 
Finally, Mr. Harris alleges that it was not clear from the original minutes who chaired the 
meeting. After this complaint was filed, on October 26, 2012, the Committee revised the 
minutes of the September 5, 2012 meeting to add that Mike Lowery was present and acting chair. 
We therefore find that the Committee took appropriate remedial action. 
For the reasons stated above, we find that Committee violated the Open Meeting Law by 
failing to list topics with sufficient specificity in the notice for its September 5, 2012 meeting, 
and by failing to follow certain remote participation procedures during that same meeting. We 
order the Committee's immediate and future compliance with the Open Meeting Law, and 
caution the Committee that a similar future violation may be considered evidence of an 
intentional violation of the law. 
We now consider this complaint resolved and this matter closed. This letter does not 
resolve any other complaints which may have been filed with our office or the Committee.
