 RE: Open Meeting Law Complaint 
 
 
Dear Mr. Wallace: 
This office received a complaint on May 21, 2020, from Jill Normandin alleging that the 
Westminster Planning Board (the “Board”) violated the Open Meeting Law, G.L. c. 30A, §§ 18-
25. The complaint was originally filed with the Board on or about February 13, and you 
responded on behalf of the Board by letter dated October 26, 2020.1 It is our understanding that 
the complaint alleges that the minutes of the Board’s December 10, 2019, meeting are 
insufficiently detailed and fail to include a list of documents used at the meeting.2 
 
We resolve this complaint by informal action in accordance with 940 CMR 29.07(2)(a), 
and find that the Board violated the Open Meeting Law by failing to include in the minutes for 
its December 10, 2019, meeting a list of the documents used at that meeting. We find the Board 
did not violate the Law in the other way alleged. In reaching this determination, we reviewed the

The Open Meeting Law requires a public body to “create and maintain accurate minutes 
of all meetings, including executive sessions, setting forth the date, time and place, the members 
present or absent, a summary of the discussions on each subject, a list of documents and other 
exhibits used at the meeting, the decisions made and the actions taken at each meeting, including 
the record of all votes.” G.L. c. 30A, § 22(a). When reviewing minutes for compliance with the 
Open Meeting Law, we look for substantial compliance with the accuracy requirement. See 
OML 2013-64; OML 2017-1047.4 By substantial compliance, we mean that the minutes should 
contain enough detail and accuracy so that a member of the public who did not attend the 
meeting could read the minutes and have a clear understanding of what occurred. See OML 
2012-106. Although minutes must include a summary of the discussion on each topic, a 
transcript is not required, and the minutes do not need to include every remark or opinion 
presented. See OML 2012-29; OML 2019-136; 2020-105.  
 
As noted above, meeting minutes must include “a list of documents and other exhibits 
used at the meeting.” G.L. c. 30A, § 22(a). A document is “used” at a meeting when it is, at a 
minimum, physically present, verbally identified, and its contents are discussed by members of 
the public body during an open session. See OML 2014-146; OML 2019-57; OML 2012-42. 
After review of the minutes and audio recording of the December 10 meeting, and discussion 
with the Town Planner, it is clear that several documents were used during the meeting. 
Therefore, we find that the Board violated the Open Meeting Law where it did not include in the 
minutes of the December 10 meeting a list of documents used at that meeting. We order the 
Board to amend the minutes of its December 10, 2019, meeting within sixty days to include a list 
of all documents used at the meeting.  
 
With respect to the allegation that the minutes of the December 10 meeting are 
insufficiently detailed, we find that the Board substantially complied with the requirement that it 
provide an accurate and sufficiently detailed summary of the discussion that occurred. While we 
appreciate that the Complainant feels that particular statements not included are important, we 
find that the level of detail the Complainant requests is not required in order to understand the 
discussion that occurred. Furthermore, the Complainant seems to allege that the minutes are 
insufficient where they do not include the full history of the topic the Complainant is concerned 
with, and because statements made during the meeting were inaccurate. Meeting minutes are a 
summary of what was discussed during a particular meeting. They are not required to contain the 
complete history of a topic over time. Additionally, we review meeting minutes for whether they 
accurately reflect the discussions that took place during a meeting, not whether the statements 
made at the meeting were factually accurate. See OML 2020-62.     
  
For the reasons stated above, we find that the Board violated the Open Meeting Law by 
failing to include in the minutes of the December 10 meeting a list of documents used at that
