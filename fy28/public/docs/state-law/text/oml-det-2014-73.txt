This office received a complaint from Don Warner Saklad dated April 24, 2014, 
alleging that the Cambridge Public Library Board (the Board) violated the Open Meeting 
Law, G.L. c. 30A, §§ 18-25. The complaint was originally filed with the Board on or 
about March 21, 2014, and you responded to the complaint by letter dated April 10, 2014. 
In his complaint, Mr. Saklad alleges that the Board failed to respond to his request for 
minutes from the Board's March 13, 2014 meeting. 
Following our review, we find that the Board did not violate the Open Meeting 
Law by responding as it did to the complainant's request for meeting minutes. In reaching 
this detennination, we reviewed the March 21, 2014 complaint; your April 10, 2014 
response; the April 24, 2014 complaint filed with our office, requesting further review; and 
Mr. Saklad's written request for minutes, dated March 14, 2014; as well as the Cambridge 
Legal Department's responses to his request, dated March 18, 2014 and April 4, 2014. 
We find the facts as follows. The Board held a meeting on the evening of March 
13, 2014. In the early morning hours of March 14, 2014, the complainant submitted a 
request by email to the Board requesting a copy of the minutes from the "most recent" 
Board meeting, including the decisions made, the actions taken and the record of all votes 
taken during the meeting; as well as any and all notes and draft minutes from the meeting; 
any and all documents, exhibits, photographs, and maps that were used during the meeting; 
any and all recordings of the meeting and other materials used in preparation of minutes; a

DISCUSSION 
The Open Meeting Law requires that the "[m]inutes of all open sessions shall be 
created and approved in a timely manner. The minutes of an open session, if they exist and 
whether approved or in draft form, shall be made available upon request by any person 
within 10 days." G.L. c. 30A, § 22(c) (emphasis added). 
The complainant submitted his request for meeting minutes, among other records, 
just hours after the March 13, 2014 meeting concluded. The Board did not violate the 
Open Meeting Law by not providing the minutes within 10 days of the request because the 
meeting minutes had not been drafted as of the date of the request. See OML 2013-31; 
OML 2012-100. The Board timely acknowledged the complainant's request, however, and 
notified him that there were no responsive records. The Law Department followed up with 
a cost estimate once the minutes were created. We therefore find that the Board did not 
violate the Open Meeting Law. 
CONCLUSION 
For the reasons stated above, we find that the Board did not violate the Open 
Meeting Law. We now consider the complaint addressed by this determination to be 
resolved. This determination does not address any other complaints that may be pending
