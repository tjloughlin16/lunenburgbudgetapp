This office received a complaint from Anna Shin on March 13, 2025, alleging that the 
Cambridge School Committee (the “Committee”) violated the Open Meeting Law, G.L. c. 30A, 
§§ 18-25. The complaint was originally filed with the Committee on January 12, 2025, and you 
responded on behalf of the Committee by letter dated January 30, 2025. We understand the 
complaint to allege that, with respect to its December 17, 2024, meeting, the Committee failed to

Following our review, we find that the Committee violated the Open Meeting Law by 
failing to include on the notice for its December 17 meeting the date and time the notice was 
posted. We find that the Committee did not otherwise violate the Law as alleged. In reaching this 
determination, we reviewed the Open Meeting Law complaint, the Committee’s response, and 
the request for further review. We also reviewed postings on the City’s website related to the

FACTS 
 
We find the facts to be as follows. The official notice posting location for public bodies 
in the City of Cambridge is the municipal website. On November 18, 2024, the Committee 
posted a document entitled “Notice” which included the date, time, and place of a Committee 
meeting scheduled for December 17, 2024, at 6:00 p.m. The document did not include a list of 
topics anticipated for discussion at the December 17 meeting. The “notice” did list the date and 
time it was posted.  
 
On December 13, 2024, at 11:10 a.m., the Committee posted a document entitled 
“Agenda” which included the date, time, and place of the Committee’s December 17 meeting as 
well as a list of topics anticipated for discussion at the meeting. The agenda did not record the 
date and time it was posted. The agenda included several topics related to the Interim 
Superintendent’s recommendations regarding closure of the Kennedy-Longfellow School and the 
Committee’s consideration thereof. 
 
At some point on December 16, 2024, the Committee posted a document entitled 
“Supplemental Agenda.” The supplemental agenda added, under the heading “Late Orders,” a 
proposed motion pertaining to the Interim Superintendent’s recommendations regarding the 
closure of the Kennedy-Longfellow School. The supplemental agenda included the date of 
amendment—December 16, 2024—but not the time.
3 
 
The Committee held its meeting on December 17, 2024, at 6:00 p.m. During the meeting, 
the Committee discussed the closure of the Kennedy-Longfellow School and took several votes 
related to this topic, including a vote on the proposed motion that had been added to the agenda 
on December 16, 2024.  
 
DISCUSSION 
 
The Open Meeting Law requires that, except in an emergency, notice be posted for 
“eve
ry meeting at least 48 hours prior to the meeting, excluding Saturdays, Sundays and legal 
holidays.” G.L. c. 30A, § 20(b). The notice must include the date, time, and place of the meeting, 
as well as a list of topics that the chair, and any other individual authorized to create the notice, 
reasonably anticipates, 48 hours in advance, will be discussed at the meeting. See G.L. c. 30A, 
§ 20(b); OML 2022-206; OML 2020-152.4 Additionally, the date and time the notice was posted 
“shall be conspicuously recorded thereon or therewith.” 940 CMR 29.03(1)(d).

We understand the complaint to allege that the C ommittee failed to post proper notice of 
its December 17, 2024, meeting and that the “agenda” for the meeting failed to record the date 
and time it was posted. To begin, we find that the document the Committee posted on November 
18 entitled “notice” did not constitute a notice for purposes of the Open Meeting Law where it 
did not include any list of topics anticipated for discussion. See G.L. c. 30A, § 20(b). That 
document, posted a month prior to the meeting, served as advance advertisement of the date and 
time of the meeting, but not statutory notice of the meeting.   
 
Inst
ead, we review the “agenda” document posted on December 13 to determine whether 
the Committee complied with the Open Meeting Law’s notice requirements. The “agenda” 
posted on December 13 included the date, time, and place of the meeting as well as a list of 
topics anticipated for discussion. We find that the December 13 posting was properly and timely 
posted more than 48 hours prior to the meeting. However, we find that the Committee violated 
the Open Meeting Law where the December 13 posting did not include the date and time it was 
posted.  
 
Next, although not clearly alleged as a specific violation, the Complainant raises general 
conce
rns about the Committee adding items under the topic heading “Late Orders” within 48 
hours of a meeting. To the extent the complaint alleges that the addition of the specific late order 
added to the agenda for the December 17 meeting violated the Open Meeting Law, we find that it 
did not. The late order in this instance related closely to the other topics already included on the 
notice regarding the closure of the Kennedy-Longfellow School and simply made clear that the 
Committee would consider taking a particular vote with respect to that topic. Therefore, the 
amendment did not add a new topic to the notice but rather provided additional clarity regarding 
a previously posted topic. We remind the Committee that a public body may amend a meeting 
notice within 48 hours of a meeting to add a previously unanticipated topic, but not to add a topic 
that was reasonably anticipated but inadvertently omitted from the notice. See 940 CMR 
29.03(1)(d); OML 2020-29; OML 2011-11. Finally, if a meeting notice is amended within 48 
hours of the meeting, the notice must conspicuously record both the date and time the original 
notice was posted and the date and time the notice was amended. 940 CMR 29.03(1)(d). 
 
CONCLUSION 
 
For the reasons stated above, we find that the Committee violated the Open Meeting Law 
by failing to include on the notice for its December 17 meeting the date and time the notice was 
posted. We find that the Committee did not otherwise violate the Law as alleged. We order the 
Committee’s immediate and future compliance with the Open Meeting Law and caution that 
similar future violations may be considered evidence of an intent to violate the Law.
