RE: Open Meeting Law Complaint 
Dear Attorney Waterfall: 
This office received a complaint from Robert Murchison on February 22, 2017, alleging 
that the Sherbom Board of Health (the "Board") violated the Open Meeting Law, G.L. c. 30A, 
§§ 18-25. The complaint was originally filed with the Board on or about January 19, 2017, and 
you responded, on behalf of the Board, by letter dated February 9, 2017. In his complaint, Mr. 
Murchison alleges that the Board failed to timely create and approve minutes for Board meetings 
held from April through December 2016. 
Following our review, we find that the Board violated the Open Meeting Law in the way 
alleged. In reaching a determination, we reviewed the original complaint, the Board's response 
to the complaint, and the complaint filed with our office requesting further review. 
FACTS 
We find the facts as follows. In response to this complaint, the Board has acknowledged 
that, at the time of the filing of this complaint, it had not approved minutes for its meetings held 
between April and December 2016, although it had handwritten draft minutes available during 
that time. Since the filing of the complaint, it has taken steps to approve the backlog of its draft 
minutes. During its February 15, 2017 meeting, the Board approved minutes from the following 
2016 meetings: May 4; May 18; June 1; June 7; June 15; June 27; July 20; and July 28. During 
its March 1, 2017 meeting, the Board approved minutes from meetings held on April 21, 2016 
and August 17, 2016. During its March 15, 2017 meeting, the Board approved minutes from 
meetings held on September 7, 2016; September 21, 2016; and October 5, 2016. Finally, during 
its March 27, 2017 meeting, the Board approved the remaining draft minutes from the following 
2016 meetings: October 13; October 19; November 2; November 16; November 28; December 7 
and December 21. 
DISCUSSION 
The Open Meeting Law requires public bodies to "create and maintain accurate minutes 
of all meetings, including executive sessions." G.L. c. 30A, § 22(a). These meeting minutes

must be reviewed and approved in a timely manner. G.L. c. 30A, § 22(c). Although the Open 
Meeting Law does not define "timely manner," we recommend that minutes of a meeting be 
approved at the next meeting, whenever possible. See OML 2014-15; OML 2012-91.1 For 
bodies that meet regularly—that is, either weekly or bimonthly—we have concluded that a delay 
or two to three months in approving minutes does not comply with the law. See OML 2016-118. 
Lhe complaint alleges that the Board failed to approve minutes from its meetings held 
from April through December 2016. The Board acknowledged that it indeed failed to approve its 
minutes in a timely manner, attributing the delay to a "dramatic" increase in the Board's 
workload during this time. Although we are mindful of the demands of a volunteer public body, 
by waiting at times nearly nine months to approve its minutes, we find that the Board violated 
the Open Meeting Law. See OML 2016-17; OML 2013-37. We commend the Board for its 
remedial efforts in approving the backlog of draft minutes. 
For the reasons stated above, we find that the Board violated the Open Meeting Law by 
failing to approve meeting minutes in a timely manner. We order the Board's immediate and 
future compliance with the law's requirements, and we caution that similar future violations 
could be considered evidence of intent to violate the law. 
We now consider the complaint addressed by this determination to be resolved. This 
determination does not address any other complaints that may be pending with our office or the 
Board. Please feel free to contact our office at (617) 963-2540 if you have any questions 
regarding this letter.
