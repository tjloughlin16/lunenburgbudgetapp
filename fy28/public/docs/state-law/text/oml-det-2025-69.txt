DISCUSSION  
 
The Open Meeting Law was enacted “to eliminate much of the s
 ecrecy surrounding the 
deliberations and decisions on which public policy is based.” Ghiglione v. Sch. Comm. of 
Southbridge, 376 Mass. 70, 72 (1978). To that end, a public body must post notice of every 
meeting at least 48 hours in advance of the meeting, not including Saturdays, Sundays, and legal 
holidays. G.L. c. 30A, § 20(b). Meeting notices must contain the date, time and place of the 
meeting, as well as a listing of topics that the chair reasonably anticipates will be discussed. Id. 
For local public bodies, “notice shall be filed with the municipal clerk and posted in a manner 
conspicuously visible to the public at all hours in or on the municipal building in which the 
clerk’s office is located.” G.L. c. 30A, § 20(c). Alternatively, a municipality may adopt a website 
as its official method of posting meeting notices. 940 CMR 29.03(2)(b). 
 
The 
City of Newburyport has adopted its website, www.cityofnewburyport.com, as the 
official method of notice posting for the City. Notice for the Council’s Tuesday, October 15 
meeting was posted to the City’s website on Friday, October 11 at 11:16 a.m. Because Monday, 
October 14, was a legal holiday in Massachusetts, we find that notice of the Council’s October 
15 meeting was not posted 48 hours, excluding weekends and holidays, in advance of the 
meeting. Therefore, we find that the Council violated the Open Meeting Law by failing to timely 
post notice of its October 15 meeting.  
 
Because we find that the meeting notice was not timely posted in the first instance, we do 
not al
so address the allegation that the notice was improperly amended within 48 hours of the 
meeting to include an anticipated topic. However, we remind the Council that it may only amend 
a meeting notice within 48 hours of the meeting to include a new topic if that topic was not 
reasonably anticipated 48 hours, excluding weekends and holidays, prior to the meeting. See, 
e.g., OML 2021-131 (finding no violation where meeting notice was amended to include a truly 
unanticipated topic); see also, cf. OML 2021-142 (finding a violation where the body discussed 
an anticipated topic that had not been included on the meeting notice and noting, among other 
facts, that the chair himself raised the topic). 
 
CONCLUSION 
 
For the reasons stated above, we find that the Council violated the Open Meeting Law by 
failing to timely post notice of its October 15 meeting. We order the Council’s immediate and
