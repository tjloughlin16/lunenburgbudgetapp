RE: Open Meeting Law Complaint — Rowe Board of Selectmen 
Dear Chair Abbott: 
This office received five complaints from Lisa Danek-Burke, on or about May 17, 2012; 
May 21, 2012; and July 3, 2012; alleging that the Rowe Board of Selectmen (the "Board") 
violated the Open Meeting Law, G.L. c. 30A, §§ 18-25. The complaints allege that the Board 
deliberated on "public business outside of an open public meeting through written 
communication by electronic mail among a quorum of the Rowe Board of Selectmen." The 
complaints were filed with the Board on or about April 5, 2012; April 9, 2012; April 11, 2012; 
and April 30, 2012 (two complaints).1  The Board responded to the complaints by letters dated 
May 1, 2012; May 7, 2012; and June 22, 2012. 
Following our review, we find that the Board violated the Open Meeting Law by 
deliberating over email. We do not believe the violations were intentional, however, and we 
offer this determination as guidance for future conduct. In reaching this determination we 
reviewed the April 5, 2012; April 9, 2012; April 11, 2012; and April 30, 2012 complaints filed 
with the Board. We also reviewed the Board's May 7, 2012 and June 22, 2012 responses to the 
complaints, and the May 17, 2012; May 21, 2012; and July 3, 2012 complaints filed with our 
office. Finally, we reviewed emails sent by members of the Board on March 13, 2012; March 
14, 2012; March 19, 2012; March 28, 2012; and March 29, 2012.

FACTS 
The Board is a three-member public body. On March 13, 2012, Board Chair Noel 
Abbott2  sent an email to several recipients, including the two other Board members. The subject 
was "Phone Booths for tomorrow night's agenda." In the email, Chair Abbott suggested a topic 
for the next Board meeting regarding phone booths. Chair Abbott wrote, in part, "My thinking: 
Perhaps a pay phone can be installed inside the new port-a-potty and changing room building. 
That would be an ideal place and save on a separate phone booth." 
On March 14, 2012, Chair Abbott sent an email to several recipients, including the two 
other Board members. The subject was "Re: Paving Bid Documents and Approval Process." 
Chair Abbott wrote, "Unless anyone on the list above has a strongly different opinion, handicap 
lines only should work well. That will keep our costs down and it will actually make things look 
better. People know where to park." 
On March 19, 2012, Chair Abbott sent an email to several recipients, including the two 
other Board members. The subject was "Outside phone booth and phone for Rowe school." 
Chair Abbott wrote, in part, that "In our Selectman's meeting Paul pointed out that the $400 or 
$500 cost for a pay phone would not repay itself given that there was one call in the past quarter. 
Good point? So, back to plan B — installing an extension that can be used only for local calls and 
911...Given the fact that the town owns the pay booth and has paid the rental on the pay phone 
in the past I would support it coming from the town's general fund and not the school budget but 
as Selectmen we can decide." 
On March 28, 2012, Board member Robert Clancy sent an email to several recipients, 
including the other two Board members. The subject was "Re: Rowe MSBA Statement of 
Interest." In the email, Mr. Clancy provided an update about a phone conversation with an 
official from the Massachusetts School Building Authority (MSBA) regarding a Rowe proposal. 
On March 29, 2012, Chair Abbott replied to the email to provide an update on the Board's 
discussion regarding the same MSBA project. 
DISCUSSION 
The Open Meeting Law requires that "all meetings of a public body shall be open to the 
public," subject to a narrow list of exceptions that can be conducted in executive session. G.L. c. 
30A, § 20(a). The law's purpose is "to eliminate much of the secrecy surrounding deliberations 
and decisions on which public policy is based." Ghiglione v. School Committee of Southbridge, 
376 Mass. 70, 72 (1978). The law defmes deliberation as "an oral or written communication 
through any medium, including electronic mail, between or among a quorum of a public body on 
any public business within its jurisdiction; provided, however, that 'deliberation' shall not 
include the distribution of a meeting agenda, scheduling information or distribution of other 
procedural meeting or the distribution of reports or documents that may be discussed at a 
meeting, provided that no opinion of a member is expressed." G.L. c. 30A, § 18. 
In each of the five emails we reviewed, a Board member sent an email to a quorum of the 
Board discussing matters within the Board's jurisdiction. This constituted deliberation. See

G.L. c. 30A, § 18. If the Board member sending the message wished to convey the information 
therein to the other Board members, he should have presented that information to them during an 
open meeting. In his May 1, 2012 response to the complaints, Chair Abbott wrote that "I 
checked with our town's legal counsel and was advised that my email communications actually 
[were] a violation of Open Meeting Law. The rationale give [sic] by town counsel: Even though 
the communication specifically requested that my co-Selectmen not respond, my offering an 
opinion could be considered a deliberation in violation of Open Meeting Law. In the future I 
will refrain from this kind of communication." We are encouraged by this response, and expect 
that the Board will follow the requirements of the Open Meeting Law in the future. 
CONCLUSION 
We find that Board violated the Open Meeting Law by deliberating over email. The 
Board has acknowledged the violation, and assured our office that its members will refrain from 
deliberating outside of meetings in the future. We therefore order the Board's immediate and 
future compliance with the Open Meeting Law, and caution that future similar violations may be 
considered evidence of intent to violate the law. Furthermore, we order the Board to publicly 
disclose the five emails discussed in this letter at the Board's next meeting.
