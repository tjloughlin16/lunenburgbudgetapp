Re: Open Meeting Law Complaint — Falmouth Board of Selectmen 
Dear Attorney Duffy: 
This office received a complaint from Marc Finneran, dated March 13, 2012, alleging 
that the Falmouth Board of Selectmen (the "Board") violated the Open Meeting Law, G.L. c. 
30A, §§ 18-25. Specifically, the complaint alleges that the Board "met illegally 12-13-11 at 6:30 
p.m. The meeting was illegal because B.O.S. failed to post notice of said meeting." The 
complaint was filed with the Board on or about December 23, 2011. The Board responded to the 
complaint by letter dated January 12, 2012. 
We appreciate the patience of the parties while we reviewed this matter. Following our 
review, we find that the Board did not violate the Open Meeting Law. In reaching our 
determination, we reviewed the December 23, 2011 complaint filed with the Board; the Board's 
January 12, 2012 response; and the March 13, 2012 complaint filed with our office. We also 
reviewed the notice for the Board's December 13, 2011 joint meeting with the Planning Board. 
Finally, we reviewed the minutes of the Board's December 5, 2011 meeting. 
FACTS  
A member of the Falmouth Planning Board resigned in late 2011. Pursuant to G.L. c. 41, 
§ 11, the remaining members of the Planning Board notified the Board of the vacancy. The 
Planning Board then scheduled a joint meeting with the Board to vote to fill the vacancy. A 
notice for a December 13, 2011 joint meeting was prepared by the Planning Board staff and filed 
with the Town Clerk on December 9, 2011. The notice was drafted on Planning Board 
letterhead, and included the title "JOINT MEETING WITH BOARD OF SELECTMEN." The 
notice included a number of topics related to interviewing applicants for the vacant Planning 
Board position.

The two Boards met on December 13, 2011 in open session and interviewed applicants 
for the open seat on the Planning Board. Following the interviews, the two Boards voted to 
appoint one of the applicants to fill the vacancy. 
DISCUSSION 
The Open Meeting Law requires that public bodies post notice of every meeting "at least 
48 hours prior to such meeting, excluding Saturdays, Sundays and legal holidays," and every 
notice include "the date, time and place of such meeting and a listing of topics that the chair 
reasonably anticipates will be discussed at the meeting." G.L. c. 30A, § 20(b). 
Here, two public bodies held a joint meeting on December 13, 2011. The issue raised by 
the complaint is whether a joint notice was sufficient notice for both public bodies, or whether 
the law required separate notices. We find that the joint notice met the requirements of the Open 
Meeting Law.' The joint notice included both Boards' names, the date, time, and location of the 
meeting, and included all of the topics that the two Boards anticipated discussing. We therefore 
find that the notice met the requirements of G.L. c. 30A, § 20(b). 
CONCLUSION 
We find that the Board did not violate the Open Meeting Law by posting a joint notice 
with the Planning Board prior to its December 12, 2011 meeting.

May two or more public bodies hold a joint meeting? 
Yes, two or more public bodies may hold a joint meeting. However, each public body participating in the meeting 
must provide notice pursuant to G.L. c. 30A, § 20. The public bodies must provide independent notice of their 
meetings, or if posting a single notice, must clearly state that each public body will be meeting.
