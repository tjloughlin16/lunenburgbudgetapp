RE: Open Meeting Law Complaint 
Dear Attorney Randazzo: 
This office received a complaint from Ronald Beaty, dated July 29, alleging that the 
Sandwich Board of Selectmen (the "Board") violated the Open Meeting Law, G.L. c. 3OA, §§ 
18-25.' The complaint was originally filed with the Board on July 2, and, after an extension of 
time was granted, the Board responded by letter dated July 27. In his complaint, Mr. Beaty 
alleges that the Board violated the Open Meeting Law by posting an insufficiently specific 
meeting notice. 
Following our review, we resolve this complaint by informal action in accordance with 
940 CMR 29.07(2)(a), and find that the Board did not violate the Open Meeting Law. As we 
have noted in prior determinations resolving complaints filed by Mr. Beaty, a public body does 
not need to list "open session" in its meeting notice, unless an executive session is the only topic 
for discussion. See OML 2015-110; OML 2015-105.2 Here, the notice listed "Pledge of 
Allegiance" prior to the anticipated executive session, thus it was clear that an open session 
meeting would occur. The Board, therefore, did not violate the Open Meeting Law by failing to 
list open session as a distinct topic. 
The complaint also alleges that the description given on the meeting notice—"Town 
Neck Beach"— was not sufficient to reasonably notify members of the public as to what would 
be discussed. We disagree. A piece of property should be described on a meeting notice either 
by address or by some other identifying landmarks. See OML 2012-52; OML 2012-2. Here, the 
notice identified the property to be discussed by name. We find that this was sufficient to satisfy 
the law's requirements. See 940 CMR 29.03(l)(b).
