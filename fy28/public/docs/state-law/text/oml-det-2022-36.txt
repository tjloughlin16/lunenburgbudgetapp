 RE: Open Meeting Law Complaint 
Dear Attorney Austin: 
 
This office received a complaint from Michael Beattie on December 6, 2021, alleging 
that the Winthrop Board of License Commission (the “Board”) violated the Open Meeting Law, 
G.L. c. 30A, §§ 18-25. The complaint was originally filed with the Board on September 21, 
2021, and you responded, on behalf of the Board, by letter dated November 1, 2021, following 
an extension of time granted by this office.1 In his complaint, Mr. Beattie alleges that the Board 
violated the Open Meeting Law by: (1) posting an insufficiently specific notice for a meeting 
held on August 23; (2) failing to provide notice to abutters that the Board planned to discuss a 
specific liquor license application during its August 23 meeting; and (3) failing to consider the 
requirements of G.L. c. 138, § 16, in granting a liquor license near a religious institution.  
 
Following our review, we find that the Board did not violate the Open Meeting Law. We 
resolve this complaint by informal action in accordance with 940 CMR 29.07(1). In reaching 
this determination, we reviewed the original complaint, the Board’s response to the complaint, 
the complainant’s request for further review, and the notice of the Board meeting held on August 
23. 
 
The Open Meeting Law requires that “a public body shall post notice of every meeting at 
least 48 hours prior to such meeting.” G.L. c. 30A, § 22(a). Public bodies are required to post 
notices that “shall be printed in a legible, easily understandable format and shall contain the date, 
time and place of such meeting and a listing of topics.” G.L. c. 30A, § 22(b). A public body must

post notice that include “a listing of topics that the chair reasonably anticipates will be discussed 
at the meeting.” G.L. c. 30A, § 20(b). We generally consider a topic to be sufficiently specific 
when a reasonable member of the public could read the topic and understand the anticipated 
nature of the public body’s discussion. See OML 2016-167, OML 2016-90; OML 2015-196; 
OML 2014-155; OML 2011-11.2  
 
The complaint alleges that the notice for the Board meeting held on August 23 lacked 
sufficient specificity because it failed to list the address of the establishment seeking a liquor 
license. The Board duly posted notice of the August 23 meeting notice, which included the 
following topic: “Continued Public Hearing” “Application for Wine and Malt Liquor License 
Off Premises / DTJ Inc. (D&T Market).” Although including street addresses may be useful to 
the public, such detailed specificity is not necessary if the Board sufficiently identifies the 
establishments with the specific types of license renewals. See OML 2017-33. Here, we find that 
the notice included sufficient specificity where it contained the name of the establishment and 
the type of license under consideration, such that a reasonable member of the public could 
determine the anticipated nature of the public body’s discussion. See OML 2016-90; OML 2011-
11. Therefore, we find that the Board did not violate the Open Meeting Law.    
  
The complaint further alleges that the Board violated the Open Meeting Law by failing to 
provide specific notice to abutters that the Board planned to discuss the liquor license application 
on August 23. The Open Meeting Law does not require public bodies to provide additional 
notice of meetings to specific individuals or groups of individuals for topics discussed during a 
meeting. See OML 2019-17; OML 2013-41. Because the Board duly posted notice of the August 
23 meeting and included, as a topic on the notice, the consideration of an application for a liquor 
license for D&T Market, we find the Board complied with the requirements of the Open Meeting 
Law and find no violation. See OML Declination 1-18-19 (Lanesborough Conservation 
Commission). 
 
Finally, the complaint alleges that the Board “failed to take into consideration” whether 
the establishment requesting the liquor license was 500 feet away from a religious institution as 
required by G.L. c. 138 § 16. The Division of Open Government’s review concerns compliance 
with the Open Meeting Law, G.L. c. 30A, §§ 18-25. Therefore, even if true, this allegation would 
not constitute a violation of the Open Meeting Law. As such, we decline to review this particular 
matter. See OML Declination 1-25-16 (Nahant Housing Authority Board of Commissioners); 
OML Declination 5-21-12 (Hampden-Wilbraham Regional School Committee). We offer no 
opinion on whether any actions taken by the Board could be a violation of some other law or 
regulation outside the scope of the Division’s review.   
 
 For the reasons stated above, we find that the Board did not violate the Open Meeting 
Law. We now consider the complaint addressed by this determination to be resolved. This 
determination does not address any other complaints that may be pending with our office or the 
Board. Please feel free to contact the Division at (617) 963 - 2540 if you have any questions.
