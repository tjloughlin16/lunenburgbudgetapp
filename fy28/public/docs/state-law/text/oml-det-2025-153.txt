Dear Amy Messier: 
 
 This office received a complaint from Patrick Higgins on August 11, 2025, alleging that  
the Somerset Planning Board (the “Board”) violated the Open Meeting Law, G.L. c. 30A, §§ 18-
25. The complaint was originally filed with the Board on July 3, 2025, and you, responded to the 
complaint, on behalf of the Board by letter on July 24, 2025. The complaint alleges that the 
notice for the Board’s June 24, 2025, meeting is insufficiently specific. 
 
 Following our review, we find that the Board violated the Open Meeting Law in the way 
alleged. In reaching this determination, we reviewed the original complaint, the Board’s response 
to the complaint, and the complaint filed with our office requesting further review. We also 
reviewed the notice and minutes of the Board’s June 24, 2025, meeting.  
 
 The Open Meeting Law requires that public bodies post notice of every meeting “at least 
48 hours pr
ior to such meeting, excluding Saturdays, Sundays and legal holidays” and that every 
notice include “the date, time and place of such meeting and a listing of topics that the chair 
reasonably anticipates will be discussed at the meeting.” G.L. c. 30A, § 20(b). A public body is 
required to list topics on a notice with “sufficient specificity to reasonably advise the public of 
the issues to be discussed at the meeting.” 940 CMR 29.03(1)(b). We generally consider a topic 
sufficiently specific when a reasonable member of the public could read the topic and understand 
the anticipated nature of the public body’s discussion. See OML 2015-35. When reviewing a 
meeting notice for sufficiency, unless it is clearly insufficient on its face, we do not review a 
meeting notice standing alone. See OML 2015-109; OML 2014-155. Rather, we review what

was actually discussed at the meeting to determine if the notice was sufficiently specific to make 
the public aware of the discussion topics. 
 
The Board duly posted notice of a meeting to be held on June 24, 2025. One of the topics 
listed on the notice was “Designate a Planning Board representative to be on the B.E.S.S. By-
Law Working Group.” During the June 24, 2025, meeting, the Town Planner asked that one 
member of the Board volunteer to be a member of the Battery Energy Storage System (BESS) 
By-Law Working Group. Two Board members volunteered but the Chair instructed that only one 
member may sit on the working group. 
 
The complaint alleges that the June 24, 2025
 , meeting notice lacked sufficient specificity 
where the public would not comprehend the meaning of the acronym “B.E.S.S.” The Board 
acknowledges, and we find, that the notice was not sufficiently specific because “B.E.S.S.” is an 
acronym not widely understood by members of the public, and thus its use here violated the 
Open Meeting Law. Our office discourages the use of abbreviations or acronyms in notices that 
are not widely understood by members of the public. See OML 2020-137 (finding that “PEC” is 
not a widely understood acronym); OML 2016-5 (finding that “PILOT” is not a widely 
understood acronym); cf. OML 2015-172 (concluding that “FAA” is a widely understood 
acronym). However, we note that, in the June 24, 2025, meeting minutes, the Board has referred 
to the acronym as “Battery Energy Storage System” and we commend the Board for taking such 
remedial action.  
 
For the
 reasons stated above, we find that the Board violated the Open Meeting Law by 
posting a notice with insufficient specificity for its June 24, 2025, meeting. We order immediate 
and future compliance with the law’s requirements, and we caution that similar future violations 
could be considered evidence of intent to violate the law. 
 
 We now consider the complaint addressed by this determination to be resolved. This  
determination does not address any other complaints that may be pending with our office or the  
Board. Please feel free to contact our office at (617) 963-2540 if you have any questions  
regarding this letter.
