 RE: Reconsideration of OML 2020 - 150 
 
Dear Attorney Emerson: 
 Our office received your December 1, 2020, letter in which you request clarification 
concerning the relationship between our November 20, 2020, determination, OML 2020-150, 
and a prior determination, OML 2019-161. Both determinations resolved complaints filed by 
Gerard Mackin against the Weymouth School Committee (the “Committee”). We treat your 
letter as a request for reconsideration of OML 2020-150.  
 
 There is no statutory right to reconsideration by the Office of the Attorney General of an 
Open Meeting Law determination or declination. While the Division of Open Government (the 
“Division”) generally will not reconsider past determinations, the Division may grant 
reconsideration where the request identifies a clerical or mechanical error in the determination or 
a significant legal or factual issue that the Division may have overlooked or misapprehended in 
resolving the complaint.  
 
In OML 2019-161, we previously found the Committee in violation of the Open Meeting 
Law for entering executive session on December 20, 2018, without a proper purpose. In OML 
2020-150, as issued on November 20, 2020, we again concluded that the Commission had 
violated the Open Meeting Law by entering executive session on December 20, 2018, without a 
proper purpose, and we therefore ordered the Committee to release the documents and exhibits 
used during the December 20, 2018, executive session as well as any notes, recordings or other 
materials used in the preparation of the minutes. We acknowledge that OML 2020-150 could be 
construed as finding a separate and additional violation regarding the propriety of the December 
20, 2018, executive session, which was not our intention. We therefore reissue OML 2020-150 to 
clarify that we previously found a violation regarding the December 20, 2018, executive session

DISCUSSION 
1. The Committee has failed to periodically review its executive session minutes for release.  
 The Open Meeting Law requires that a public body “create and maintain accurate 
minutes of all meetings, including executive sessions, setting forth the date, time and place, the 
members present or absent, a summary of the discussions on each subject, a list of documents 
and other exhibits used at the meeting, the decisions made and the actions taken at each meeting, 
including the record of all votes.” G.L. c. 30A, § 22(a). Executive session minutes may be 
withheld from disclosure to the public “as long as publication may defeat the lawful purposes of 
the executive session, but no longer.” G.L. c. 30A, § 22(1). When the purpose for a valid 
executive session has been served, the minutes and any documents or exhibits used at the session 
must be disclosed, unless the attorney-client privilege or an exemption to the Public Records 
Law applies. Id. The burden of justifying continued nondisclosure of executive session minutes 
lies with the public body. Foudy v. Amherst-Pelham Regional School Committee, 402 Mass. 
179, 184 (1988). 
 A public body or its chair or designee must, at reasonable intervals, review the minutes of 
executive sessions to determine if continued nondisclosure of all outstanding executive session 
minutes is warranted. G.L. c. 30A, § 22(g)(1). Although “reasonable interval” is not defined by 
the Law, our office has found a quarterly review or a review every six months to be appropriate. 
See OML 2019-45; OML 2017-104; OML 2015-166; OML 2013-180. At the meeting following 
each periodic review, the public body must announce which executive session minutes will be 
released and which will continue to be withheld at the next meeting following its review, and

 A public body’s obligation to review executive session minutes for possible release is 
ongoing. If a public body reviews executive session minutes and determines that the purpose for 
the executive session remains such that the minutes should continue to be withheld from the 
public, it must continue to review those same executive session minutes at reasonable intervals 
until it determines that the reason for secrecy has expired. See OML 2020-23; OML 2019-133. 
We find no evidence that the Committee periodically reviewed its executive session minutes for 
possible release to the public, and the Committee has provided none. We therefore conclude that 
the Committee has not completed these reviews, and find that the Committee violated the Open 
Meeting Law.  
2. The Committee must release any documents and exhibits used during the December 20, 
2018, executive session as well as any notes, recordings or other materials used in the 
preparation of the December 20, 2018, executive session minutes. 
 The Open Meeting Law provides that “[t]he minutes of any executive session, the notes, 
recordings or other materials used in the preparation of such minutes and all documents and 
exhibits used at the session, may be withheld from disclosure to the public in their entirety” 
under the Public Records Law, “as long as publication may defeat the lawful purposes of the 
executive session, but no longer; provided, however, that the executive session was held in 
compliance with section 21.” G.L. c. 30A, § 22(f) (emphasis added). By contrast, “the minutes of 
any open session, the notes, recordings or other materials used in the preparation of such minutes 
and all documents and exhibits used at the session, shall be public records in their entirety and 
not exempt from disclosure pursuant to any of the exemptions under [the Public Records Law.]” 
G.L. c. 30A, § 22(e).  
 In a prior determination, we found that the Committee entered executive session without 
a proper purpose on December 20, 2018, and therefore we ordered the Committee to release the 
minutes of that executive session. See OML 2019-161. The Committee released the minutes of 
the December 20, 2018, executive session in compliance with our order. See OML 2020-121. 
When Mr. Mackin received the December 20, 2018, executive session minutes, he filed the 
complaint we address today, alleging that the Committee had failed to release documents and 
exhibits used during the December 20, 2018 executive session as well as any notes, recordings or 
other materials used in the preparation of the December 20, 2018, executive session minutes. The 
Committee’s December 20, 2018, executive session meeting minutes list one document which 
was used, “Response draft letter to level III grievance.” Because the Committee lacked a proper 
purpose for meeting in executive session on December 20, 2018, the Committee must release to 
the public any documents and exhibits used during the December 20, 2018, executive session, as 
well as any notes, recordings or other materials used in the preparation of the December 20, 
2018, executive session minutes. The Committee may not redact or withhold from the public any 
portion of these documents. We order the Committee’s compliance within 30 days of receipt of 
this order.

CONCLUSION 
 For the reasons stated above, we find that 1) t he Committee has failed to periodically 
review its executive session minutes for release; and 2) the Committee must release any 
documents and exhibits used during the December 20, 2018, executive session as well as any 
notes, recordings or other materials used in the preparation of the December 20, 2018, executive 
session minutes. We order the Committee’s immediate and future compliance with the Open 
Meeting Law and caution the Committee that a determination by our office of a similar violation 
in the future may be considered evidence of intent to violate the Open Meeting Law. In addition, 
we order the Committee to review all executive session minutes in compliance with the above 
guidance, and release any documents and exhibits used during the December 20, 2018, executive 
session as well as any notes, recordings or other materials used in the preparation of the 
December 20, 2018, executive session minutes, within 30 days of receipt of this order.  
 We now consider the complaint addressed by this determination to be resolved. This 
determination does not address any other complaints that may be pending with our office or the 
Committee. Please feel free to contact the Division at (617) 963 - 2540 if you have any 
questions.
