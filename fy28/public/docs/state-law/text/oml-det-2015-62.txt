RE: Open Meeting Law Complaint 
Dear Ms. Grieco: 
This office received two complaints filed by George Harris, dated February 28, 2014 and 
October 20, 2014, alleging that the Wayland School Committee (the "Committee") violated the 
Open Meeting Law, G.L. c. 30A, §§ 18-25. The first complaint concerns a set of minutes from 
57 executive session meetings held in 2008 and 2009, and alleges that: 1) the Committee failed 
to timely produce these minutes, following a request;1 2) the Committee did not inform the 
public of its determination that continued non-disclosure of certain of these minutes was 
warranted; 3) the redacted portions of these minutes should be disclosed; and 4) the Committee 
failed to take all votes during those executive sessions by roll call vote.2 This complaint was 
originally filed with the Committee on January 8, 2014. The Committee responded by letter 
dated February 4, 2014. The second complaint concerns a set of minutes from 56 executive 
session meetings held between July 27, 2005 and May 5, 2014, and alleges that the Committee 
withheld certain of these minutes longer than was legally permissible. The complaint also 
challenges the sufficiency, detail, and redactions to 41 sets of minutes in this group. The second 
complaint was originally filed with the committee on August 27, 2014. The Committee 
responded by letter dated September 23, 2014.

We appreciate the patience and cooperation of the parties while we reviewed these 
complaints.3 Following our review, we find that the Committee did not violate the Open 
Meeting Law by failing to periodically review executive session minutes from meetings held 
prior to July 1, 2010. However, we find that the Committee may no longer rely upon the Open 
Meeting Law as the basis for continuing to withhold minutes concerning collective bargaining 
discussions held in executive session prior to July 1, 2010 and on January 31, 2011 and 
December 2, 2013. Further, we find that the Committee violated the Open Meeting Law by 
failing to periodically review its executive session minutes, resulting in executive session 
minutes being withheld after the purposes that justified the executive sessions had expired; by 
failing to take a roll call vote before entering executive session on four occasions; by failing to 
list in the minutes of four executive sessions the documents used during those meetings; and by 
failing to provide sufficient detail in 29 sets of executive session minutes. 
In reaching this determination, we reviewed the two complaints filed with the Committee, 
the Committee's responses, and the complaints filed with our office. Additionally, we reviewed, 
in camera, the unredacted executive session minutes referenced throughout this determination. 
We reviewed a chart provided by the Committee detailing the status of its meeting minutes. 
Finally, we spoke with Mr. Harris by telephone on several occasions regarding our review. 
FACTS

Committee's executive session minutes, including all documents and exhibits used at those

portions of 26 sets of minutes from this group. The Committee subsequently voted to publicly 
release, without redaction, 14 of those 26 sets of minutes.4

redacted portions of the discussions involve collective bargaining strategy and non-union 
personnel contract negotiations. None of the discussions involve long-term strategy, rather they 
focus on strategy related to the specific collective bargaining agreements or non-union contracts 
being negotiated at the time.

DISCUSSION 
1. The Committee did not Violate the Open Meeting Law by Failing to Periodically 
Review 57 sets of Executive Session Minutes from Meetings held in 2008 and 2009 
to Determine whether Continued Non-Disclosure was Warranted. 
The Open Meeting Law was revised as part of the Ethics Reform Act of 2009. The 
revised law took effect on July 1, 2010. The revised law added a provision requiring that the 
public body, or its chair or designee, shall, at reasonable intervals, review the minutes of its 
executive sessions to determine whether continued non-disclosure was warranted. G.L. c. 30A, § 
22(g)(1). This determination must be announced at the body's next meeting and included in the 
minutes of that meeting. Id Prior to July 1, 2010, there was no such requirement in the Open 
Meeting Law. See G.L. c. 39, § 23B (repealed by St. 2009, c. 28); OML 2014-105. 
While we encourage public bodies to do so as a best practice, the law does not require 
that they perform a periodic review of executive session minutes of meetings held prior to July 1, 
2010. Thus, the Committee did not violate the law by failing to periodically review the 57 set of

2. Because the Executive Session Purposes have Expired, the Committee mav no 
Longer Withhold or Redact under the Open Meeting Law the Minutes of its 2008. 
2009, January 31. 2011 and December 2. 2013 Executive Sessions. 
The minutes of an executive session, the notes, recordings or other material used in the 
preparation of such minutes and all documents and exhibits used at the session, may be withheld 
from disclosure to the public in their entirety, as long as publication may defeat the lawful 
purposes of the executive session, but no longer. G.L. c. 30A, § 22(f). When the purpose for 
which an executive session was held has been served, the minutes must be disclosed unless the 
attorney-client privilege or one or more of the exemptions under the Public Records Law apply 
to withhold the minutes, or any portion thereof, from disclosure. Id. The burden of justifying 
continued nondisclosure of executive session minutes lies with the public body. Foudy v. 
Amherst-Pelham Regional School Committee. 402 Mass. 179, 184 (1988); OML 2014-138. 
The Committee's first set of released meeting minutes, spanning the time period from 
2008 to 2009, involve discussions held in executive session pursuant to G.L. c. 30A, § 21(a)(2) 
(non-union personnel contracts) or G.L. c. 30A, § 21(a)(3) (collective bargaining agreements). 
These purposes allow a public body to keep information confidential as long as disclosure may 
have a detrimental effect on the public body's negotiating position. G.L. c. 3OA, §§ 21(a)(2), 
21(a)(3). A public body may continue to withhold minutes of this sort of executive session, in 
whole or in part, even after a final collective bargaining agreement has been reached, if the 
public release of those minutes could have a detrimental effect on the public body's collective 
bargaining position in future negotiations. See OML 2012-41. However, such minutes may not 
be withheld indefinitely. At some point, the public interest in transparency will outweigh the 
potential for harm to the public body's future collective bargaining position. After each 
subsequent collective bargaining agreement is reached, therefore, the burden on the public body 
to release the executive session minutes of the older sessions increases. See id. 
None of the discussions during these 2008 and 2009 executive sessions involved long-
term strategy, but rather they appear to have focused on strategy related to the specific collective 
bargaining agreements or non-union contracts being negotiated at the time. Because we have not 
been provided with sufficient evidence to support the Committee's claim that public release of its 
executive session minutes could jeopardize negotiations six or seven years later, we find that the 
executive session purpose has expired, thus the Committee may no longer withhold these 
minutes under the Open Meeting Law. The Committee must publicly release the redacted 
portions of these minutes, unless the attorney-client privilege or one or more of the exemptions 
under the Public Records Law apply. G.L. c. 30A, § 22(f). 
Likewise, the minutes of the Committee's January 31, 2011 and December 2, 2013 
executive session meetings record discussions of collective bargaining strategy that, while part of

3. The Committee Withheld Executive Session Minutes After the Executive Session 
Purpose had Expired. 
A public body, or its chair or designee, must, at reasonable intervals, review the minutes 
of its executive sessions to determine if the stated purpose for that executive session continues to 
justify non-disclosure. G.L. c. 30A, § 22(g)(1). As stated above, the Committee is not required 
to periodically review the minutes of meetings that occurred prior to July 1, 2010.7 However, in 
the second set of minutes produced by the Committee, 32 are from meetings that took place after 
July 1, 2010. The Committee failed to comply with the law by periodically reviewing these 
executive session minutes to determine if the executive session purposes warranted continued 
non-disclosure until it received Mr. Harris' request. 
The Committee acknowledged in its September 23, 2014 response to the complaint that 
"it has not released executive session minutes in a timely manner." As remedial action, the 
Committee reviewed all of its outstanding executive session minutes and has adopted a new 
procedure to review executive session minutes on a regular basis to determine if they may be 
released. We are satisfied that the Committee is now aware of its obligations in this regard. 
4. The Committee Failed to take Roll Call Votes during Four Executive Sessions. 
Any vote taken during an executive session shall be recorded by roll call and entered into 
the minutes. G.L. c. 30A, § 22(c). Mr. Harris identified four meetings during which the 
Committee failed to take or record roll call votes during its executive sessions. The Committee 
acknowledged this error in its September 23, 2014 response to the complaint and has committed 
to recording roll call votes in future executive sessions. Thus, while the Committee violated the 
Open Meeting Law in this respect, we believe it is now aware of its responsibility to record all 
executive session votes as roll call votes. We order the Committee, to the best of its ability, to 
amend the minutes of the four meetings at issue to include information about any roll call votes 
that may have been taken on those dates.

5. The Committee Failed to List in its Minutes the Documents Used during Four 
Executive Sessions. 
Minutes must include a list of documents and other exhibits used during the meeting. 
G.L. c. 30A, § 22(a). Mr. Harris identified five meeting dates for which the executive session 
minutes fail to include such a list.9 The Committee acknowledged this error in its September 23, 
2014 response to the complaint with respect to four of the meeting minutes. However, the 
Committee noted that for its May 30, 2013 meeting, the document referenced in the complaint 
was not used by the Committee during the executive session. While we find a violation with 
respect to these four sets of minutes, we credit the Committee's response with regard to the May 
30, 2013 meeting and find no violation there. 
During its October 20, 2014 meeting, the Committee approved revised minutes that 
included additional listed documents for its July 30, 2012 and December 2, 2013 meetings. We 
therefore order the Committee, within thirty days following receipt of this letter, to review and 
amend the minutes of its January 31, 2011 and January 14, 2013 meetings to include the 
documents used by the Committee during these executive sessions, if it has not already done so. 
6. The Committee Failed to Provide Sufficient Detail in 29 Sets of Minutes. 
A public body must create and maintain accurate minutes of all meetings, including 
executive sessions, setting forth the date, time and place, the members present or absent, a 
summary of the discussions on each subject, a list of documents and other exhibits used at the 
meeting, the decisions made and the actions taken at each meeting, including the record of all 
votes. G.L. c. 30A, § 22(a). A summary need not be a transcript of the discussion, however 
minutes should contain enough detail and accuracy that a member of the public who did not 
attend the meeting could read the minutes and have a clear understanding of what occurred. See 
OML 2013-14; OML 2012-29; OML 2011-55. 
Mr. Harris identified 29 sets of minutes that do not include sufficient detail so that a 
member of the public who did not attend the meeting could read the minutes and have a clear 
understanding of what occurred.10 In its September 23, 2014 response to the complaint, the 
Committee pledged to review its minutes and amend those that were insufficiently detailed. 
During its October 20, 2014 meeting, the Committee reviewed the minutes and approved and 
amended those minutes for which it was able to provide additional detail.11 On November 3,

CONCLUSION 
The Committee did not violate the Open Meeting Law by failing to periodically review 
executive session minutes from meetings held prior to July 1, 2010. However, we find that the 
Committee may no longer rely upon the Open Meeting Law as the basis for withholding portions 
of executive session minutes related to the collective bargaining discussions from meetings that 
occurred prior to July 1, 2010 and on January 31, 2011 and December 2, 2013. Additionally, we 
find that the Committee violated the Open Meeting Law by failing to periodically review 
executive session minutes and release those that no longer needed to be withheld; by failing to 
take a roll call vote before entering executive session on four occasions; by failing to list in the 
minutes of four executive sessions the documents used during those meetings; and by failing to 
provide sufficient detail in 29 sets of executive session minutes. 
We order the Committee to take the following actions: 
1. Immediate and future compliance with the Open Meeting Law; we caution the 
Committee that future similar violations may be considered evidence of an intentional 
violation of the Open Meeting Law; 
2. Publicly release the executive session minutes of its 2008; 2009; January 31, 2011; 
and December 2, 2013 executive session meetings. The Committee may continue to 
withhold or redact portions of these minutes if the attorney-client privilege or one or 
more of the exemptions under the Public Records Law applies. G.L. c. 30A, § 22(f); 
3. Review and amend its January 31, 2011 and January 14, 2013 meeting minutes to 
include the documents used by the Committee during its executive sessions, if it has 
not already done so; 
4. Amend the minutes of the October 12, 2010; January 31, 2011; September 24, 2012; 
and March 10, 2014 meetings to include information about any roll call votes that 
were taken on those dates, to the best of its ability.
