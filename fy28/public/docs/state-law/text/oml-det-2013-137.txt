RE: Open Meeting Law Complaint 
Dear Mr. Scanlan, 
This office received a complaint from Steven Bankert (the complainant) on September 3, 
2013 alleging that the North Attleborough Conservation Commission (the Commission) violated 
the Open Meeting Law, G.L. c. 30A, §§ 18-25. The complaint was originally filed with the 
Commission on July 2, 2013, and the Commission responded to the original complaint by letter 
dated July 17, 2013. The complaint alleges that the notice posted by the Commission in advance 
of its June 4, 2013 meeting did not sufficiently instruct the public "that there would be changes 
to [the] draft" prior to a vote on certain proposed rules and regulations. 
We reviewed the July 2, 2013 complaint filed with the Commission; the Commission's 
July 17 response; the September 1 complaint filed with this office; a letter from the complainant, 
which was also received by this office on September 1 and was not included in the original 
complaint; the notice and agenda of the June 4, 2013 meeting; and the proposed Rules and 
Regulations Governing Structures on Falls and Whiting's Ponds (the Proposed Rules) posted 
prior to the June 4, 2013 meeting.1 Additionally, we conducted telephone interviews in 
September 2013 with Shannon Doyle, Conservation Agent for the Town of North Attleborough, 
and the complainant. 
Following our review, we find that the Commission did not violate the Open Meeting 
Law at its June 4, 2013 meeting.

FACTS 
On May 30, 2013, the Commission filed a notice with the Town clerk for its June 4, 2013 
meeting. This notice was subsequently timely posted. The notice included the following 
information: 
7:15PM CONT. Public Meeting; Revision to Rules and Regulations Governing 
Structures on Falls and Whiting's Ponds (vote on final draft) 
This item was in reference to a draft of a proposed ruleset which was discussed and 
revised at the two previous meetings of the Commission. The discussion was continued to the 
Commission's June 4, 2013 meeting for the purpose of a vote. At the June 4, 2013 meeting, the 
Commission discussed the Proposed Rules and implemented further changes to the previously 
posted draft. The draft was brought to a vote and unanimously approved, as revised, effective 
that day. 
DISCUSSION 
The complainant alleges that the Commission failed to provide sufficient detail in the 
notice posted in advance of its June 4, 2013 meeting. Specifically, he alleges that the notice did 
not clearly state that the Commission would consider revising the draft prior to its final vote on 
the Proposed Rules. 
The Open Meeting Law requires a public body to post a meeting notice 48 hours in 
advance of a meeting and include, a "listing of topics that the chair reasonably anticipates will be 
discussed at the meeting." G.L. c. 30A, § 20(b). Public bodies are required to list topics in a 
meeting notice with "sufficient specificity to reasonably advise the public of issues to be 
discussed at the meeting." 940 CMR 29.03(l)(b). We generally consider a topic to include 
sufficient specificity when a reasonable member of the public could read the topic and 
understand what the nature of the public body's discussion will be. See OML 2013-55, OML 
2011-7.2 
Here, both the Commission's notice for its June 4, 2013 meeting and the accompanying 
Proposed Rules clearly indicated that a draft was at issue for a vote. A draft is "a preliminary or 
tentative sketch, outline, or version (of a document or picture)." WEBSTER'S THIRD NEW 
INTERNATIONAL DICTIONARY OF THE ENGLISH LANGUAGE 683 (unabridged ed. 1961). Thus a 
reasonable member of the public could understand that the Proposed Rules were to be discussed 
and were subject to revision before a vote memorializing their final iteration because nothing in 
the actions of the Commission or the materials distributed by the Commission indicated that the 
nature of the ruleset was permanent or fixed prior to the vote. See OML 2012-75 (no violation to 
omit reasonably inferable information from notice); OML 2011-15 (same). We therefore find

CONCLUSION 
In conclusion, we find that the notice for the Commission's June 4, 2013 meeting was 
sufficiently specific, and the Commission did not violate the Open Meeting Law.
