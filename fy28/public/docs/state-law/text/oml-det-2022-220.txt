This office received a complaint from Margaret Sheehan on March 16, 2022, alleging 
that the Carver Planning Board (the “Board”) violated the Open Meeting Law, G.L. c. 30A, §§ 
18-25.  The complaint was originally filed with the Board on February 14, 2022, and you 
responded to the complaint, on behalf of the Board, by letter dated March 5, 2022.
1  The  
complaint alleges that i) meeting minutes are insufficiently detailed because they do not include 
a list of documents used during the meeting and ii) the Board failed to review prior Open 
Meeting Law complaints.   
 
We appreciate the patience of the parties while we reviewed this matter.  We resolve this 
matter by informal action, in accordance with 940 CMR 29.07(2)(a), after reviewing the 
original complaint, the Board’s response to the complaint, the complaint filed with our office 
requesting further review, and the notices and open session minutes of the Board meetings held 
on January 25 and February 8, as well as video recordings of those meetings.
2  We find that the 
Board did not violate the Open Meeting Law in the ways alleged.   
 
The complaint alleges that minutes of Board meetings held between January 2021 and

whether they contain a list of documents as our office will not conduct broad audits of public 
bodies based on generalized allegations.  See OML 2013-180; OML 2012-106.
3  Nevertheless, 
we did review minutes of Board meetings that occurred in January and February of 2022 
(January 25, February 8, and February 22) and note that these three sets of meeting minutes 
include a list of documents used during each meeting.  We take seriously the allegations raised 
by the complaint, and take this opportunity to remind the Board that the Open Meeting Law 
requires that a public body “create and maintain accurate minutes of all meetings, including 
executive sessions, setting forth the date, time and place, the members present or absent, a 
summary of the discussions on each subject, a list of documents and other exhibits used at the 
meeting, the decisions made and the actions taken at each meeting, including the record of all 
votes.”  G.L. c. 30A, § 22(a).  We note that the absence of a list of documents is not necessarily a 
violation of the Open Meeting Law.  Rather, we look to whether a document is physically 
present, verbally identified, and the contents are discussed by the members of a public body 
during an open meeting, to determine whether it has been “used” for purposes of the Open 
Meeting Law and therefore must be listed in the minutes.  See OML 2014-12; OML 2012-42.   
 
The complaint also alleges that the Board failed to follow proper procedure in responding 
to prior Open Meeting Law complaints.  Specifically, the complaint alleges that the Chair of the 
Board “refused to state publicly in the meetings the substance of the complaints.”  The Open 
Meeting Law requires that, unless an extension has been granted by the Attorney General, within 
14 business days of receipt of an Open Meeting Law complaint, a public body must meet to 
review the complaint’s allegations; take remedial action, if appropriate; and send to the 
complainant a response and a description of any remedial action taken. 940 CMR 29.05(5).  
Upon the filing of an Open Meeting Law complaint with a public body, the chair “shall 
disseminate copies of the complaint to the members of the public body,” and “the public body 
shall review the complaint’s allegations.”  940 CMR 29.05(3), (5).  Thus, a public body must 
meet to review the complaint and formulate a response, or meet to delegate that authority, and 
respond to the complaint within 14 business days.  See G.L. c. 30A, § 23(b); OML 2017-69; 
OML 2012-90.   
 
Here, the Board posted notices for meetings to be held on January 25 and February 8.  
Both notices included the following topic: “Motion to authorize Town Counsel to draft a 
response to the Open Meeting Law Complaint on behalf of the Planning Board.”
4  During the 
January 25 and February 8 meetings, the Board’s Chair announced that the Board had received 
Open Meeting Law complaints, identified the individuals who had filed the complaints, and 
confirmed that the other Board members received the complaints.  The Board then voted by 
majority vote to authorize town legal counsel to draft a response to the complaints.  While we 
acknowledge that the Board did not substantively discuss the allegations raised in the complaints, 
the Open Meeting Law does not require a public body to engage in a substantive discussion of 
the merits of each complaint, nor to read a complaint in full during a meeting.  See OML 2022-
