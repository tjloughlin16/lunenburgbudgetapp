 RE: Open Meeting Law Complaint 
 
Dear Attorney Jenkins: 
This office received a complaint from Patrick Higgins on February 4, 2020, alleging that 
the Westport Board of Selectman (the “Board”) violated the Open Meeting Law, G.L. c. 30A, §§ 
18-25. The complaint was originally filed with the Board on January 4, 2020, and you responded 
on behalf of the Board by letter dated January 22, 2020. In his complaint, Mr. Higgins alleges 
that the Board violated the Open Meeting Law by failing to timely approve minutes of several 
meetings and failing to list the names of the candidates to be interviewed for a position at its 
December 16 meeting.1 The Board concedes that some of the meeting minutes were not timely 
approved. However, the Board asserts that it was not required to list the names of individual 
candidates on its meeting notice, as “Principle Assessor Candidates Interview” was sufficiently 
detailed to advise the public of the topic the Board anticipated discussing. We resolve this 
complaint by informal action in accordance with 940 CMR 29.07(2)(a), and find that the Board 
violated the Open Meeting Law in both respects.  
 First, we find that the Boar d violated the Open Meeting Law by failing to timely 
approved meeting minutes. See G.L. c. 30A, § 22(c); 940 C.M.R. 29.11(2); OML 2020-15 
(“timely manner” means “within the next three public body meetings or within 30 days, 
whichever is later, unless the public body can show good cause for further delay”).2 The Board

concedes that its September 3, 16, 30, and October 15 meeting minutes were not timely 
approved. On the other hand, we agree with the Board that, as it approved its October 28, 
November 12, and December 2 meeting minutes within three meetings, those minutes were 
timely approved. Since the Board has already taken corrective action by approving all 
outstanding minutes, we order no further remedial action in this regard. 
 Second, we find that the Board viol ated the Open Meeting Law by failing to identify 
candidates for the Principal Assessor position on its meeting notice for its December 16, 2019, 
meeting. A public body must post notice of every meeting at least 48 hours in advance, not 
including Saturdays, Sundays, and legal holidays. G.L. c. 30A, § 20(b). Notices must include “a 
listing of topics that the chair reasonably anticipates will be discussed at the meeting.” Id. Where 
the chair delegates to another person the creation of the meeting notice, we look to what that 
person anticipates will be discussed 48 hours before the meeting. See OML 2015-190. The list of 
topics shall have sufficient specificity to reasonably advise the public of the issues to be 
discussed at the meeting. 940 CMR 29.03(l)(b). We have consistently stated that the names of 
interviewees and applicants for appointment which are known 48 hours in advance of a meeting 
should be posted on a meeting notice. See OML 2019-24; OML 2016-150; OML 2013-163. In 
this case, the Board of Selectmen delegated to an Administrative Assistant the task of creating 
the meeting notice. Although the Board chair may not have known the names of the interviewees 
48 hours in advance of the meeting, some, but not all, of the interviewees’ names were known to 
the Administrative Assistant. Therefore, we conclude that all of the names that were known 48 
hours in advance of the meeting should have been included on the meeting notice. For these 
reasons, we find that the Board violated the Open Meeting Law by posting an insufficiently 
detailed meeting notice.  
 We note that in a prior deter mination we found that the Board violated the Open Meeting 
Law by failing to identify in a meeting notice the names and positions of individuals to be 
considered for appointment at a meeting. See OML 2017-177. Although a subsequent, similar 
violation may be considered evidence of an intent to violate the law, we decline to find an 
intentional violation in this case, where the meeting notice was created and posted by a member 
of staff. However, we are concerned about the similarity of the violations and, therefore, we 
order all members of the Board to review the Attorney General’s Open Meeting Law training 
video concerning meeting notices.3 The Board should notify our office in writing that all 
members have complied with this order within sixty (60) days. See 940 CMR 29.07(4).  
 Finally, we order the Board’s immediate and future compliance with the Open Meeting 
Law and caution the Board that a determination by our office of a similar violation in the future 
may be considered evidence of intent to violate the Open Meeting Law. We now consider the 
complaint addressed by this determination to be resolved. This determination does not address 
any other complaints that may be pending with our office or the Board. Please feel free to contact 
the Division at (617) 963 - 2540 if you have any questions.
