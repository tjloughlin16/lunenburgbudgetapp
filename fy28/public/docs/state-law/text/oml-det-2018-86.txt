RE: Open Meeting Law Complaint 
Dear Attorney Feodoroff: 
This office received a complaint from Stephen Morgan, dated May 29, alleging that the 
Ashland Planning Board (the "Board") violated the Open Meeting Law, G.L. c. 30A, §§ 18-25.' 
The complaint was originally filed with the Board on April 30, and, after an extension of time 
was granted, the Board responded by letter dated May 30, 2018. In his complaint, Mr. Morgan 
alleges that the Board violated the Open Meeting Law by: (i) failing to create and approve 
meeting minutes, and (ii) failing to post meeting minutes to a website. 
Following our review, we resolve this complaint by informal action in accordance with 
940 CMR 29.07(2)(a) and find that the Board did not violate the Open Meeting Law. Public 
bodies must create and maintain accurate minutes of all meetings. G.L. c. 30A, § 22(a). Here, 
the complaint points to a vacant section of an Ashland municipal website to support the inference 
that the Board failed to create and approve meeting minutes. As part of its response, the Board 
provided copies of the minutes that were alleged to be missing. It is therefore clear that the 
Board did not violate the Open Meeting Law with respect to this allegation. Further, the 
complaint alleges that the Board failed to post minutes to the municipal website. The Open 
Meeting Law does not require public bodies to post minutes to a website. See OML 2017-157; 
OML 2016-17.2 Because this allegation, even if true, would not violate the Open Meeting Law, 
we decline to review it. Where, as here, it is clear that a public body has not violated the Open 
Meeting Law, complainants should refrain from filing complaints with our office unnecessarily.
