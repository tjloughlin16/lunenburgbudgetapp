RE: Open Meeting Law Complaint — Carver Zoning Board of Appeals 
Dear Attorney Goldberg: 
This office received a complaint from Mr. Robert Belbin, dated July 23, 2011, alleging 
that the Carver Zoning Board of Appeals (the "Board") violated the Open Meeting Law, G.L. c. 
30A, §§ 18-25. Mr. Belbin's complaint alleges that the Board failed to provide draft minutes of 
three 2010 meetings during which the Board considered his zoning request. Specifically, the 
complaint alleges that "[o]n April 20, 2011 [Mr. Belbin] went to the Town Clerk[']s Office to 
inquire about the Zoning Board Minutes[he had] requested on 12-28-2010. The clerk[']s 
assistants notified [him] there are none since [A]ugust of 2010." All other allegations in the 
complaint appear to concern public records matters that fall under the jurisdiction of the 
Secretary of State's Public Records Division, rather than our office.' The complaint was filed 
with the Board on April 21, 2011. The Board responded to the complaint by letter on July 21, 
2011. 
We find that the Board did provide Mr. Belbin with the minutes he sought, but failed to 
do so within 10 days as required by the Open Meeting Law. In reaching this determination, we 
reviewed the April 21, 2011 complaint, the Board's July 21, 2011 response, and the July 23, 
2011 complaint filed with our office. We also reviewed Mr. Belbin's correspondence with the 
Secretary of State's Office and with the Town of Carver regarding his public Records requests. 
I  Mr. Belbin alleges that documents may be missing from his zoning file. This is a public records matter, and is not 
within the jurisdiction of the Division of Open Government. Additionally, Mr. Belbin alleges that the Board 
secretary shredded draft minutes after typing them. The Open Meeting Law states that the "[m]inutes of all open 
sessions shall be created and approved in a timely manner. The minutes of an open session, if they exist and whether 
approved or in draft form, shall be made available upon request by any person within 10 days." G.L. c. 30A, § 
22(c). The law does not address the destruction of documents. These matters should be addressed to the Secretary 
of State's Public Records Division, and not our Division.

Sincerely, 
Mr. Belbin alleges that he went to the Town Clerk's office on April 20, 2011 to request 
minutes from several Board meetings, and was informed that there were no minutes since August 
of 2010. We understand from the Town that Mr. Belbin was provided with the minutes he 
requested in May 2011, with the exception of the minutes from December 4, 2010. On 
December 4, 2010, the Board conducted a site visit at Mr. Belbin's property, but did not hold a 
meeting for purposes of the Open Meeting Law because the definition of "meeting" does not 
include "an on-site inspection of a project or program, so long as the members do not deliberate." 
G.L. c. 30A, § 18. Because the Board conducted a site visit on December 4, 2010, and not a 
meeting, the Board was not required to create minutes. Therefore, the Board did not violate the 
Open Meeting Law by failing to provide Mr. Belbin with minutes from December 4, 2010. 
While it appears Mr. Belbin ultimately received the minutes that were responsive to his 
request, the Board did not comply with the law's requirements regarding the timely provision of 
open session minutes. Mr. Belbin first requested the "minutes from the hearing on my case" in a 
letter to the Board on December 28, 2010. There is some question as to when the Board actually 
received the letter, as it was filed with the Town administrative offices and may not have reached 
the Board. An additional request, filed on the Town's Information Request form, was submitted 
by Mr. Belbin on May 10, 2011. The minutes of the Board's November 15, 2010 and December 
14, 2010 meetings were provided to him by the Board on May 31, 2011. It does appear that Mr. 
Belbin made repeated attempts between December 28, 2010 and May 10, 2011 to obtain copies 
of these meeting minutes. We remind the Board of its obligation to provide the minutes of any 
open meeting, whether in draft or final form, within 10 days of a written request, as required by 
G.L. c. 30A, § 22(c). We order the Board's immediate and future compliance with this 
requirement of the Open Meeting Law. We expect that in the future the Town offices will work 
diligently to respond to such requests within the required time frame. 
We now consider this matter closed. Please contact me if you have any questions 
regarding this letter.
