This office received a complaint from Joseph Graveline on June 28, 2022, and a second 
complaint on August 29, 2022, alleging that the Northfield Planning Board (the “Board”) 
violated the Open Meeting Law, G.L. c. 30A, §§ 18-25.  The first complaint was originally filed 
with the Board on April 25, 2022, and you responded, on behalf of the Board, by letter dated 
May 24, 2022.  The second complaint was originally filed with the Board on June 11, 2022, and 
you responded, on behalf of the Board, by letter dated July 22, 2022, following an extension of 
time granted by our office.  Both complaints allege that the minutes of the Board meeting held on 
February 18, 2021, remain inaccurate even after their revision pursuant to our order in OML 
2022-52.   
 
Following our review, we find that the Board did not violate the Open Meeting Law in 
the way alleged.  In reaching this determination, we reviewed the original complaint, the Board’s 
response to the complaint, and the complaint filed with our office requesting further review.  In 
addition, we reviewed the original and revised open session minutes of the Board meeting held 
on February 18, 2021, the notice and minutes of the Board meeting held on November 18, 2021, 
and April 21, 2022, as well as video recordings of the Board meetings held on February 18, 
2021, and November 18, 2021.

FACTS 
 
We find the facts as follows.  On October 4, 2021, this office issued a determination, 
OML 2021-137, finding that the Board violated the Open Meeting Law by failing to include 
sufficient detail in the minutes of a meeting held on February 18, 2021.  The February 18 
meeting was held remotely via Zoom.  During the February 18, 2021, meeting, the Board held 
three public hearings for three different solar array projects on Pine Meadow Road in the Town 
of Northfield.  The meeting lasted four hours but the Board did not make any decisions or take 
any votes other than a motion to adjourn the meeting.  Rather, the Board heard from the 
proponent of the three projects, asked questions about the projects, and allowed members of the 
public to ask questions.  Four of the five Board members -  Joe Graveline, Meg Riordan, Stephen 
Seredynski, and Homer Stavely - were all visibly and audibly present during the meeting.  
Although the minutes of this February meeting summarized each question that members of the 
Board or the public asked with respect to each solar array project, we found that the minutes did 
not include any summary of the answers that were provided.  Thus, we ordered the Board to 
revise the meeting minutes within 60 days.  During a November 18, 2021, meeting, the Board 
reviewed and approved revised minutes of the February 18, 2021, meeting. 
 
On March 22, 2022, this office issued a determination, OML 2022-52, finding that the 
revised minutes of the meeting held on February 18, 2021, were sufficiently detailed where the 
minutes included responses to the questions asked by members of the Board and the public.  
However, we found that the Board violated the Open Meeting Law by failing to accurately 
record the members present or absent in the minutes of its February 18, 2021, meeting.  
Specifically, we held that the minutes listed Board member Tammy Pelletier as present when she 
was absent from the meeting. 
 
On April 19, 2022, the Board submitted a Request for Reconsideration to our office with 
respect to our finding in determination OML 2022-52 that Ms. Pelletier was absent from the 
meeting.  Attached to the Request for Reconsideration was an affidavit from Ms. Pelletier 
asserting that she was in fact present at the Board meeting held on February 18, 2021.  She 
explained that she joined the February meeting from her phone at about 4:15 P.M., which was 
approximately fifteen minutes after the meeting started.   
 
During a meeting held on April 21, 2022, the Board approved revised minutes of the 
February 18, 2021, meeting.  The minutes continued to list Ms. Pelletier as present for the 
meeting but were revised to indicate that she arrived at the February meeting at 4:15 P.M.  
Moreover, the February 18, 2021, meeting minutes capture the motion to adjourn as follows: 
“Motion by JG [Joe Graveline] to adjourn the meeting at 7:59PM.  SS [Stephen Seredynski] 
seconded.  Motion carried unanimously.”  At the time that the Board adjourned the meeting, the 
only members visibly and audibly present were Chair Stephen Seredynski, Joe Graveline and 
Homer Stavely.  Board member Meg Riordan had stepped away from the meeting approximately 
ten minutes prior to the Board’s adjournment.

DISCUSSION 
 
The Open Meeting Law requires that a public body “create and maintain accurate 
minutes of all meetings, including executive sessions, setting forth the date, time and place, the 
members present or absent, a summary of the discussions on each subject, a list of documents 
and other exhibits used at the meeting, the decisions made and the actions taken at each meeting, 
including the record of all votes.”  G.L. c. 30A, § 22(a).  When reviewing minutes for 
compliance with the Open Meeting Law, we look for substantial compliance with the accuracy 
requirement.  See OML 2016-105; OML 2013-64.
2  By substantial compliance, we mean that the 
minutes should contain enough detail and accuracy so that a member of the public who did not 
attend the meeting could read the minutes and have a clear understanding of what occurred.  See 
OML 2012-106.  While minutes must include a summary of the discussion on each topic, a 
transcript is not required, and the minutes do not need to include every remark or opinion 
presented.  See OML 2012-29; OML 2011-55.   
 
The complaint alleges that the minutes of the February 18, 2021, meeting are still 
insufficient as they continue to list Board member Tammy Pelletier as present when she was not 
present for this meeting.  We acknowledge that Ms. Pelletier did not speak or otherwise 
participate during the February meeting, nor was she visibly present on video at any point during 
this remote meeting.
3  However, Ms. Pelletier asserted by affidavit that she was present at the 
meeting held on February 18, 2021.  Following our review, we are unable to formally find 
whether the minutes, which list Ms. Pelletier as present, are accurate or inaccurate.  Accordingly, 
we are constrained to find that the Board did not violate the Open Meeting Law when it 
approved revised minutes of the February 18, 2021, meeting.

t of members present at the February 18, 2021, meeting, we note our concern with how the 
vote to adjourn the meeting is recorded in the minutes.  The minutes record that vote as “Motion 
carried unanimously.”  However, only three of the five members who were listed as present for 
the meeting actually voted to adjourn the meeting.  To be “accurate,” meeting minutes must 
accurately reflect the discussions that took place during a meeting, including a record of all 
votes.  See G.L. c. 30A, § 22(a); OML 2020-62.  Because the minutes must accurately capture 
the discussions that actually took place, it is inappropriate to include information in meeting 
minutes that was not discussed or did not occur at a meeting.  See OML 2021-128.  
 
Finally, we note that the final vote to adjourn the meeting was not conducted by roll call.
