 RE: Open Meeting Law Complaint 
Dear Attorney Fried:  
 
This Office received two complaints from Walter Tuvell on August 12, 2021, alleging 
that the Reading Select Board (the “Board”) violated the Open Meeting Law, G.L. c. 30A, §§ 18-
25.  The complaints were originally filed with the Board on July 1 and July 3, 2021, and the 
Board responded by letter dated July 21, 2021.1, 2 In his complaints, Mr. Tuvell alleges that the 
Board improperly convened in executive session on June 9 and June 29 to discuss prior Open 
Meeting Law complaints that he had filed with the Board.3 
 
Following our review, we find that the Board did not violate the Open Meeting Law.  In 
reaching this determination, we reviewed the original complaints, the Board’s response to the 
complaints, and the complainant’s requests for further review.  We also reviewed the notices and 
open and executive session minutes of the Board’s June 9 and June 29 meetings.

FACTS 
 
 We find the facts as follows.  On or around May 24, Mr. Tuvell filed an Open Meeting 
Law complaint with the Board.  The Board posted notice for a meeting to be held on June 9; the 
notice included, among other things, the topic “Executive Session to: Respond to Open Meeting 
Law Complaint (filed by Walter Tuvell).”  The Board convened its June 9 meeting as scheduled.  
According to the minutes of the June 9 meeting, one Board member moved to “go into Executive 
Session . . .  pursuant to Executive Session Purpose 1, to discuss an Open Meeting Law 
complaint against the Select Board by Walt Tuvell, and to reconvene in Open Session after the 
executive session.”  The Board voted 3-2 to enter executive session.  During the executive 
session, the Board discussed the Open Meeting Law complaint and remedial action to be taken in 
response to the complaint, and voted by roll call to instruct Town Counsel to respond to the 
complaint. 
 
 On or around June 22, Mr. Tuvell filed a second Open Meeting Law complaint with the 
Board.  The Board posted notice for a meeting to be held on June 29; the notice included, among 
other things, the topic “Executive Session to: Respond to Open Meeting Law Complaint (filed by 
Walter Tuvell on June 22, 2021).”  The Board convened its June 29 meeting as scheduled.  
According to the minutes of the June 29 meeting, one Board member moved to “go into 
Executive Session . . .  pursuant to Executive Session Purpose 1, to discuss an Open Meeting 
Law complaint against the Select Board by Walter Tuvell on June 22nd, 2011, and not reconvene 
in Open Session after the executive session.”  The Board voted 4-1 to enter executive session.  
During the executive session, the Board discussed the Open Meeting Law complaint and whether 
to take remedial action, and voted by roll call to authorize Town Counsel to respond to the 
complaint. 
 
DISCUSSION 
 
The complaints addressed herein allege that the Board improperly discussed Mr. Tuvell’s 
prior Open Meeting Law complaints in executive session on June 9 and June 29.  The Open 
Meeting Law requires that meetings of a public body be properly noticed and open to members 
of the public, unless a proper executive session is convened.  See G.L. c. 30A, §§ 20(a)-(b), 21.  
A public body may enter an executive, or closed, session for any of the ten purposes enumerated 
in the Open Meeting Law. 
 
One permissible reason to convene in executive session is to discuss “the reputation, 
character, physical condition or mental health, rather than professional competence, of an 
individual, or to discuss the discipline or dismissal of, or complaints or charges brought against, 
a public officer, employee, staff member or individual.”  G.L. c. 30A, § 21(a)(1).  Our office has 
unequivocally stated in past determinations that public bodies may meet in executive session 
under Purpose 1 to review Open Meeting Law complaints against the body.  See OML 2021-21; 
OML 2019-170; OML 2019-163; OML 2019-34; ML 2019-31; OML 2015-105; OML 2013-82; 
OML 2012-119; OML 2011-6.  We have determined that an Open Meeting Law complaint is by 
its nature a complaint “brought against[] a public officer,” namely, the members of the public 
body.  See OML 2012-119.  The individual filing a complaint does not have a right to be present 
in a Purpose 1 executive session but may attend at the discretion of the public body.  See OML

2019-64; OML 2013-50.  Accordingly, we find that the Board was permitted to conduct its 
review of the prior Open Meeting Law complaints in executive session during the June 9 and 
June 29 meetings, and therefore did not violate the Open Meeting Law.  
 
CONCLUSION 
 
 For the reasons stated above, we find that the Board did not violate the Open Meeting 
Law.  We now consider the complaints addressed by this determination to be resolved. This 
determination does not address any other complaints that may be pending with our Office or the 
Board.  Please feel free to contact the Division at (617) 963 - 2540 if you have any questions.
