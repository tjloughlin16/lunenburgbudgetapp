FACTS 
 
We find the facts as follows.  During the Citizen’s Open Forum at the beginning of the 
March 29 Board meeting, Ms. Bykofsky stated that several individuals with disabilities who are 
unable to leave their homes have expressed concern about their inability to vote at Town 
Meeting.  She further stated at the meeting that she was told that reasonable accommodations 
need to be made at the municipal level and that individuals with disabilities “must reach out to 
the municipal ADA officer” for equal access and “there is a process they must go through.”  She 
noted that Town Meeting was scheduled to go forward in approximately five weeks.  Aside from 
Ms. Bykofsky’s remarks during Open Forum, there was no discussion at the March 29 meeting 
of how to request a reasonable accommodation or who the ADA officer is.   
 
The minutes of the March 29 meeting note: “Sonya Bykofsky mentioned that many 
people unable to attend town meetings have reached out to her about the ability to vote at the 
town meetings.  She stated that the Town needs to make reasonable accommodations for those 
individuals.”  
 
 
DISCUSSION 
 
Accuracy of minutes 
 
The Open Meeting Law requires that public bodies “create and maintain accurate minutes 
of al
l meetings, including executive sessions, setting forth the date, time and place, the members 
present or absent, a summary of the discussions on each subject, a list of documents and other 
exhibits used at the meeting, the decisions made and the actions taken at each meeting, including 
the record of all votes.”  G.L. c. 30A, § 22(a); 940 CMR 29.11(1).  Although minutes must 
include a summary of the discussion on each topic, a transcript is not required, and the minutes 
do not need to include every remark or opinion presented.  See OML 2023-35; OML 2019-81; 
OML 2012-29; OML 2011-55. 3  When reviewing minutes for compliance with the Open 
Meeting Law, we look for substantial compliance with the accuracy requirement.  For substantial 
compliance, the minutes should contain enough detail and accuracy so that a member of the 
public who did not attend the meeting could read the minutes and have a clear understanding of 
what occurred.  See OML 2023-35; OML 2021-140.

In her complaint, Ms. Bykofsky alleges tha t the minutes of the Board’s March 29 meeting 
omitted details on how reasonable accommodations are obtained, who the ADA officer is, and 
the time-sensitive nature of requesting reasonable accommodations in light of the approaching 
Town Meeting.  However, the Board did not discuss that process at its March 29 meeting.  The 
minutes accurately summarize the complainant’s remarks during the open forum portion of the 
meeting; the Board did not discuss the topic.  The minutes need not, and should not, include 
information beyond that which was discussed.  The Open Meeting Law does not dictate the 
content of a discussion at a meeting; rather, it requires that meeting minutes accurately 
summarize what was actually discussed.  See OML 2023-76; OML 2023-35; OML 2022-189; 
OML 2016-155 (minutes should reflect what was actually discussed at a meeting, not matters 
that were not discussed).  Therefore, we find that the minutes relative to the subject raised by Ms. 
Bykofsky were accurate and sufficiently detailed, and satisfied the Board’s obligations under the 
Open Meeting Law.   
 
 Publ
ic participation 
 
At the core of the Open Meeting Law is the requirement that meetings of public bodies be 
open and a
ccessible to the public.  G.L. c. 30A, § 20(a) (except when meeting in executive 
session, “all meetings of a public body shall be open to the public.”).  Generally, access to a 
meeting must include the opportunity to be physically present as well as to see and hear what is 
being discussed by the members of the public body.  See OML 2020-100; OML 2018-75; OML 
2016-54.  The Open Meeting Law does not require that a public body allow public participation 
during its meetings, but rather provides that “[n]o person shall address a meeting of a public 
body without permission of the chair, and all persons shall, at the request of the chair, be silent.”  
G.L. c. 30A § 20(f).  The Division of Open Government’s review concerns compliance with the 
Open Meeting Law.  Because the allegation that the Board did not allow public comment 
following the Planning Board’s presentation, even if true, would not constitute a violation of the 
Open Meeting Law in any event, we decline to review this allegation.  We offer no opinion on 
whether any actions taken by the Board could be a violation of any other law or regulation 
pertaining to public hearings.  See OML 2014-31; OML Declination 10-30-20 (Billerica Zoning 
Board of Appeals); OML Declination 6-25-18 (Rowley Planning Commission).

CONCLUSION 
 
For the reasons stated above, we find that the Board did not violate the Open Meeting 
Law.  We now consider the complaint addressed by this determination to be resolved.  This 
determination does not address any other complaints that may be pending with our office or the 
Board, Commission and Committee.  Please feel free to contact our office at (617) 963-2540 if 
you have any questions regarding this letter.
