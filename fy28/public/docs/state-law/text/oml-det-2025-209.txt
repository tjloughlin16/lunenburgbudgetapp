RE:  Open Meeting Law Complaint 
 
Dear Mr. Erkkila: 
 
This office received a complaint from Tarah Fitzgerald on August 30, 2025, alleging that 
the Brookfield Planning Board (the “Board”) violated the Open Meeting Law, G.L. c. 30A, §§ 
18-25.  The complaint was originally filed with the Board on or about July 2, 2024, and you 
responded, on behalf of the Board, by letter dated July 24, 2025.  The complaint alleges that the 
Board created insufficient minutes of the meeting held on April 9, 2025.  
 
Following our review, we find that the Board did not violate the Open Meeting law in the 
way alleged.  In reaching this determination, we reviewed the original complaint, the Board’s 
response to the complaint, and the complaint filed with our office requesting further review.  In 
addition, we reviewed the notice and minutes of Board meetings held on April 9, June 4, and 
July 22, 2025, as well as a video recording of the April 9, 2025, meeting.
1   
 
The Open Meeting Law requires that a public body “create and maintain accurate 
minutes of all meetings, including executive sessions, setting forth the date, time and place, the 
members present or absent, a summary of the discussions on each subject, a list of documents 
and other exhibits used at the meeting, the decisions made and the actions taken at each meeting, 
including the record of all votes.”  G.L. c. 30A, § 22(a).  When reviewing minutes for 
compliance with the Open Meeting Law, we look for substantial compliance with the accuracy

During a meeting on April 9, 2025, the Board held a public hearing to consider a special 
permit application filed by Sunfusions, Inc. for a cannabis cultivation facility at 6 Molasses Hill 
Road.  The Board first heard from the applicant’s attorney who provided an overview of the 
project, and the Board questioned the applicant on certain aspects of the project, including light 
pollution, odor control, septic and water use.  The Board then received public comment for over 
one hour and heard from 25 members of the public.
3   
 
The complaint alleges that the minutes of the April 9, 2025, meeting are insufficient as 
they fail to provide an accurate and sufficiently detailed summary of the comments made by 
members of the public during the meeting.  The original version of the minutes of the April 9, 
2025, meeting, which were approved on June 4, 2025, record the public comment period as 
follows: “[Chair] Erkkila opened the floor to the public.  Numerous residents expressed 
opposition to the proposed facility, citing concerns about potential negative impacts on the 
community, property values, quality of life, potential odor issues, increased traffic, water usage 
and potential contamination, and light pollution, while some supporters of the project mentioned 
potential economic benefits for the town.  Some residents also questioned the applicant’s 
experience and raised concerns about past business practices.” 
   
We find that the Board did not violate the Open Meeting Law by creating and approving 
insuf
ficiently detailed minutes.  A public body is not required to capture every comment made in 
order to substantially comply with the accuracy requirement of the Open Meeting Law.  See 
OML 2021-4; OML 2019-136.  Indeed, we have not required the level of detail and precision the 
complainant seeks here.  See OML 2023-143 (finding minutes sufficiently detailed and accurate 
even though, according to complainant, wording in minutes painted a skewed picture of a 
contentious exchange); OML 2019-136 (finding that the public body substantially complied with 
the Law's accuracy requirement for meeting minutes when two members of the public spoke 
during a public comment period and the minutes captured some of the complainant's comments 
but, according to the complainant, failed to capture at least ten points the complainant included 
in her remarks as well as comments made by another speaker).  The Open Meeting Law requires 
that minutes adequately capture the substance of the discussions that occurred so that a member 
of the public who did not attend the meeting could read the minutes and have an understanding 
of what occurred.  See OML 2020-113; OML 2016-40.  After watching a video recording of the

Nonetheless, during a meeting held on July 22, 2025, the Board agreed to include 
additional detail in the April 9, 2025, meeting minutes with respect to public comment.  The 
Board then prepared and approved revised minutes, adding the name of each speaker as well as a 
summary of each individuals’ comments.  In addition, the Board attached to the minutes the 
sign-in sheet, which included the name and address of the individuals who spoke during the 
meeting.  We commend the Board for its remedial efforts.

 reasons stated above, we find that the Board did not violate the Open Meeting
