RE: Open Meeting Law Complaint 
Dear Chair Applegate: 
This office reviewed a complaint dated January 19, 2012, filed by Mr. Michael 
Goodwin, alleging that the Mendon-Upton Regional School Committee (the "Committee") 
violated the Open Meeting Law, G.L. c. 30A, §§ 18-25. Specifically, the complaint alleges that 
the Committee failed to publicly release the minutes of its July 19, 2011; August 2, 2011; August 
15, 2011; and September 12, 2011 executive sessions after the purpose for continued non-
disclosure of these minutes had expired. The complaint was filed with the Committee on 
December 8, 2011. The Committee responded by letter dated January 13, 2012. 
After reviewing the complaint and the Committee's response, we have decided to resolve 
this complaint by informal action,  in accordance with 940 CMR 29.017(2)(a). We find that the 
Committee did not violate the Open Meeting Law, and may continue to withhold these executive 
session minutes until the Committee's need to protect its collective bargaining position has 
expired. 
We reviewed, in camera, the minutes of the Committee's July 19, 2011; August 2, 2011; 
and August 15, 2011 (two meetings) executive sessions. Because these minutes have not been 
publicly released, we do not recount their content in detail here. However, it is clear from our 
review that during these meetings the Committee and its negotiating subcommittee, pursuant to 
===PAGE 2===
executive session Purpose 3, discussed matters related to their strategy for negotiation with 
certain collective bargaining units. See G.L. c. 30A, § 21(a)(3). We understand that the 
Committee has not reached agreement with these units on items for Fiscal Year 2012, and 
expects to continue negotiations into Fiscal Year 2013. Because the Committee is engaged in an 
ongoing collective bargaining negotiation, it does not need to publicly release the minutes from 
these executive sessions at this time. Once this collective bargaining process has concluded, and 
the Committee no longer has a negotiating position to protect with respect to the items discussed 
at these meetings, the Committee must publicly release its minutes, unless the attorney-client 
privilege or one or more of the exemptions under the Public Records law applies to withhold the 
records, or any portion thereof, from disclosure. G.L. c. 30A, § 22(f). The longer the minutes 
remain undisclosed, the heavier the burden on the Committee to prove that the executive session 
purpose remains. See OML 2011-19. 1  
We decline to review additional allegations raised by the complainant in his January 19, 
2012 complaint regarding the Committee's September 12, 2011 agenda and open meeting 
minutes. These allegations were not raised in the initial complaint filed with the public body. 
Potential violations discovered after a complaint has been filed with a public body should be 
alleged in a subsequent complaint filed with that body. This allows the public body an 
opportunity to investigate and take appropriate remedial action with regard to the additional 
allegations. 
With the issuance of this determination, we now consider this matter closed. This letter 
does not resolve any other complaints against the Committee that may be pending with our 
office.
