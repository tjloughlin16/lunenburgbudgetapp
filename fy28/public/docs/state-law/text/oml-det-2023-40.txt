 RE:  Open Meeting Law Complaint 
 
Dear Attorney Braun: 
 
This office received a complaint from Steven Ballard on October 12, 2022, alleging that 
the Boxborough Finance Committee (the “Committee”) violated the Open Meeting Law, G.L. c. 
30A, §§ 18-25.  The complaint was originally filed with the Committee on August 17, 2022, and 
you responded to the complaint, on behalf of the Committee, by letter dated September 6, 2022.
1  
The complaint alleges that the notices of the Committee meetings held on August 15 and August 
16 lacked sufficient specificity by failing to include the date and time the notices were posted.   
 
We resolve this matter by informal action, in accordance with 940 CMR 29.07(2)(a), 
after reviewing the original complaint, the Committee’s response to the complaint, the complaint 
filed with our office requesting further review, the notice of the Committee meeting held on 
August 15, and the original and amended notices of the Committee meeting held on August 16.  
We find that the Committee violated the Open Meeting Law in the way alleged. 
 
The Open Meeting Law requires that public bodies “post notice of every meeting at least 
48 hours prior to such meeting, excluding Saturdays, Sundays and legal holidays.”  G.L. c. 30A, 
§ 20(b).  Every notice must include “the date, time and place of such meeting and a listing of 
topics that the chair reasonably anticipates will be discussed at the meeting.”  G.L. c. 30A, § 
20(b).

The complaint alleges that the Committee posted notices for meetings to be held on 
August 15 and August 16 which did not include the date and time that the notices were posted.2  
The Open Meeting Law requires that “the date and time that the notice is posted shall be 
conspicuously recorded thereon or therewith.”  940 CMR 29.03(1)(d); see OML 2011-45.  
Therefore, the date and time of posting may be listed either on the meeting notice itself or on the 
website next to the link to the meeting notice.  See OML 2019-102; OML 2018-53.  Notices that 
are revised within the 48 hours before the meeting must conspicuously record both the date and 
time that it was posted originally and the date and time that the notice was revised.  See 940 
CMR 29.03(1)(d); OML 2017-183; OML 2013-44.  The Committee admits that when the notices 
were posted to the town website, the official notice posting location, the website failed to list the 
date and time that the notices were posted; generally, the website includes technology which 
allows for the date and time to be conspicuously recorded when a notice is posted.  However, in 
response to the complaint, the Committee worked with town officials to ensure that the website 
included the date and time that notices were posted.  We find that the Committee violated the 
Open Meeting Law by posting notices which did not include the date and time of posting but 
commend the Committee for ensuring future compliance with the law.  Recording the date and 
time enables the public to observe that public bodies are complying with the Open Meeting 
Law’s notice requirements and further provides this office with evidence of compliance with the 
law.  See OML 2013-44.   
 
For the reasons stated above, we find that the Committee violated the Open Meeting Law.  
We order immediate and future compliance with the law’s requirements, and we caution that 
similar future violations could be considered evidence of intent to violate the law.   
 
We now consider the complaint addressed by this determination to be resolved.  This 
determination does not address any other complaints that may be pending with our office or the 
Committee.  Please feel free to contact our office at (617) 963-2540 if you have any questions 
regarding this letter.
