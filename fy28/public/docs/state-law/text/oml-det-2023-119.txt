 RE: Open Meeting Law Complaint 
 
Dear Mr. Haddad: 
 
This office received a complaint from Patrick Higgins on June 28, 2023, alleging that the 
Halifax Finance Committee (the “Committee”) violated the Open Meeting Law, G.L. c. 30A, §§ 
18-25.  The complaint was originally filed with the Committee on or about June 17, 2023, and 
you responded to the complaint, on behalf of the Committee, by letter dated June 27, 2023.
1  The 
complaint alleges that the Committee’s chair failed to announce the names of Committee 
members who participated remotely during the Committee’s May 23 meeting, as required by 940 
CMR 29.10(7)(b) 
 
We resolve this complaint by informal action, in accordance with 940 CMR 29.07(2)(a), 
after reviewing the complaint, the Committee’s response, the request for further review, and a 
video of the Committee’s May 23 meeting.
2  We find that the Committee did not violate the 
Open Meeting Law.  The Open Meeting Law regulations require that “[a]t the start of the 
meeting, the chair shall announce the name of any member who will be participating remotely.  
This information shall also be recorded in the minutes.”  940 CMR 29.10(7)(b).  Video of the 
Committee’s May 23 meeting clearly shows that at the start of the meeting, which was held 
entirely remotely with each Committee member appearing separately on screen by video, the 
Chair announced the name of each Committee member participating remotely.  Nothing more 
was required.  We strongly discourage the filing of Open Meeting Law complaints that include 
allegations contrary to clear facts, and in particular where our office has explained to the
