This office received three complaints from Patrick Higgins ("complainant"), dated 
November 22, 2011, December 8, 2011, and February 17, 2012, alleging that the 
Swansea Board of Assessors ("Assessors") and the Swansea Board of Selectmen ("Select 
Board") violated the Open Meeting Law, G.L. c. 30A, §§ 18-25. Specifically, the 
complaints allege that neither board allowed him to video record its open meetings held 
on November 16, 2011 and November 22, 2011, respectively, and that the Select Board 
did not announce that its meeting was being recorded for local access cable television on 
January 3, 2012. These complaints were originally filed with the boards on or about 
November 16, 2011, December 3, 2011, and January 4, 2012. You responded to the 
complaints, on behalf of the boards, by letters dated November 18, 2011, December 6, 
2011, and January 10, 2012.1  
In making our determination regarding the Assessors' meeting, we reviewed the 
November 16, 2011 complaint filed with the Assessors; the Assessors' November 18, 
2011 response to the complaint, including Board Chair Wayne Gray's written description 
of the events that occurred on the night of November 16, 2011; and the November 22, 
2011 complaint filed with our office. Additionally, we reviewed the complainant's reply 
to the Assessors' response; the notification form, dated November 15, 2011, that the 
complainant claims to have sent to Chair Gray (which the Chair denies ever receiving); 
and a video recording of a portion of the meeting held on November 16, 2011, provided 
by the complainant.

In making our determination regarding the Select Board meetings, we reviewed 
the December 3, 2011 and January 4, 2012 complaints filed with the Select Board; the 
Select Board's responses to the complaints, dated December 6, 2011 and January 10, 
2012; the complainant's replies to the Select Board's responses, dated December 7, 2011 
and February 16, 2012; the December 8, 2011 and February 17, 2012 complaints filed 
with our office; a January 10, 2012 letter written by counsel to Chair Ken Furtado 
notifying him of certain requirements of the Open Meeting Law; and a January 10, 2012 
letter written by counsel to our office confirming that counsel had spoken to Chair 
Furtado about the notification issue. 
Following our review, we find that both the Assessors and the Select Board 
violated the Open Meeting Law by refusing to allow the complainant to record their 
meetings on November 16, 2011 and November 22, 2011, respectively. Additionally, the 
Select Board violated the Open Meeting Law on January 3, 2012 by not publicly 
announcing that its meeting was being recorded for television. 
FACTS  
Board of Assessors 
According to the complainant, on November 15, 2011 he sent notification to 
Chair Wayne Gray that he would be recording all future meetings of the Assessors that he 
may attend. Chair Gray claims that he never received this notification. The Assessors 
met on November 16, 2011, and the complainant was present and recording the meeting. 
Chair Gray stated at the meeting that he did not receive prior notification that the 
complainant would video record the meeting, and asked the complainant to stop. The 
complainant complied. The complainant then immediately walked over to the table and 
presented Chair Gray with the Open Meeting Law Complaint we are now reviewing. 
Board of Selectmen 
According to the complainant, on November 15, 2011, he sent notice to Chair 
Ken Furtado indicating that he may be recording portions of future Select Board 
meetings. The complainant states that at the Select Board's November 22, 2011 meeting, 
Chair Furtado stopped the meeting upon complainant's arrival, and informed him that the 
notice sent on November 15, 2011 was "not good enough notice." Chair Furtado 
therefore did not allow the complainant to record any portion of the meeting. The 
complainant then filed an Open Meeting Law complaint with the Select Board on 
December 3, 2011. 
At a January 3, 2012 Select Board meeting, according to the complainant, Chair 
Furtado stated that he had no intention of making a notification at each meeting that the 
meeting was being recorded and broadcast on local access cable television. After 
receiving the January 3, 2012 complaint (the second against the Select Board), the Select 
Board's counsel sent a letter to Chair Furtado on January 10, 2012 indicating that the 
Attorney General's Open Meeting Law Guide requires the Chair to indicate, prior to the 
start of a meeting, whether the meeting is being recorded. Additionally, counsel

DISCUSSION 
The Open Meeting Law requires that "[a]fter notifying the chair of the public 
body, any person may make a video or audio recording of an open session of a 
meeting...subject to reasonable requirements of the chair as to the number, placement 
and operation of equipment used so as not to interfere with the conduct of the meeting." 
G.L. c. 30A, § 20(e). After reviewing the complaints, it is apparent that at the time the 
alleged events occurred, both boards misunderstood this requirement of the Open 
Meeting Law. 
The video recording of the Assessors meeting shows that members of the board 
expressed differing opinions about what is required by the law. Chair Gray stated that, 
based on counsel's legal advice, they had a choice whether to allow video recording 
during the meetings. According to the December 3, 2011 complaint regarding the Select 
Board meeting on November 22, 2011, Chair Furtado had the same understanding, and 
refused to allow any video recording of that meeting. 
Further, although Chair Gray states he did not receive advance notification from 
the complainant to record the Assessors' meeting, we note that none is required. Any 
member of the public may attend a meeting and notify the chair, at that time, of his or her 
intent to record. The chair only has the authority to set "reasonable requirements . . . as 
to the number, placement and operation of equipment used so as not to interfere with the 
conduct of the meeting." G.L. c. 30A, § 20(e). During the November 16, 2011 Assessors 
meeting, the complainant used one camera and sat at the back of the room. Such conduct 
does not appear to interfere with the conduct of the meeting, and recording should 
therefore have been permitted, following verbal notice to the chair. Moreover, with 
regard to the Select Board's meetings, the complainant gave Chair Furtado notice by 
letter on November 15, 2011 that he may be recording future meetings. Individuals who 
wish to record meetings of a public body should give notice, in writing or verbally, of the 
specific meetings they intend to record. Even if Chair Furtado felt that the blanket notice 
that the complainant may record future meetings was insufficient, however, he still 
should have permitted him to record following verbal notification by the complainant at 
the time of the meeting. 
Finally, we note that Chair Furtado's statement during the January 3, 2012 
meeting that he would not make an announcement at each meeting that the meeting was 
being recorded and broadcast on local access cable television also violated the Open 
Meeting Law. If a public body chair is aware that an audio or video recording is being 
made, he or she must inform other attendees of that fact at the start of each meeting that 
is being recorded. See G.L. c. 30A, § 20(e). 
We note that counsel suggested in both the November 18, 2011 and December 6, 
2011 letters in response to the complaints that both boards will adopt a policy that 
requires a 24-hour prior notification to the chair before any person may record a meeting

of the boards. This policy does not comply with the requirements of the Open Meeting 
Law. As stated above, any member of the public may record a meeting so long as that 
person informs the chair before recording. A public body may request 24-hours' notice 
of intent to record its meetings, but cannot prohibit a person from recording who fails to 
provide the 24-hours' notice, so long as he or she informs the chair before recording. 
CONCLUSION  
We find that both the Assessors and the Select Board violated the Open Meeting 
Law by refusing to allow the complainant to record their meetings on November 16, 2011 
and November 22, 2011, respectively. Additionally, the Select Board violated the Open 
Meeting Law on January 3, 2012 by not publicly announcing that its meeting was being 
recorded for television. We order immediate and future compliance with the Open 
Meeting law, and note that future similar violations may be considered evidence of intent 
to violate the Law. 
We now consider these matters resolved. This determination does not address 
any other complaints that may be pending with our office, the Swansea Board of 
Assessors, or the Swansea Board of Selectmen. Please feel free to contact me if you have 
any questions or believe any facts in this letter to be inaccurate.
