 RE: Open Meeting Law Complaints 
 
Dear Ms. St. Pierre: 
 
This office received a complaint from Patrick Higgins on January 17, 2024, alleging that 
the Dartmouth Board of Library Trustees (the “Board”) violated the Open Meeting Law, G.L. c. 
30A, §§ 18-25.  The complaint was originally filed with the Board on or about December 28, 
2023, and you responded, on behalf of the Board, by letter dated January 17, 2024.  The 
complaint alleges that minutes of the Board’s December 12, 2023, meeting were insufficient in 
various respects.1 
 
After review, we find that the Board did not violate the Open Meeting Law.  The 
complaint alleges that the minutes of the Board’s December 12 meeting were insufficient in the 
following ways: (1) failure to include a list of documents used during the meeting; (2) failure to 
include the location of the meeting; (3) failure to include sufficient detail regarding a motion to 
approve prior meeting minutes; (4) failure to include “a record of how each member voted”; 
(5) inadequate detail regarding a report from the library director; and (6) use of the acronym 
“MOU.”  However, at the time this complaint was filed on December 28, 2023, the minutes of 
the Board’s December 12, 2023, meeting were in draft, unapproved form.  The minutes were 
approved, with revisions, at the Board’s next meeting on January 9, 2024.  Under the Open 
Meeting Law, a public body is responsible for the sufficiency of final, approved minutes as it

Although we do not fully review the approved meeting minutes and make no
determination as to whether the approved minutes are sufficient, we commend the Board for
revising the draft minutes prior to approval to address some of the points identified in the
complaint. Futhermore, we take this opportunity to clarify that meeting minutes are not
required to include a record of how each member voted unless the vote was one that was
required to be conducted by roll call, such as when meeting in executive session or when the
meeting includes remote participants. See G.L. c. 30A, § 22(b); 940 CMR 29.10(7)(c). In
addition, we have explained that using acronyms in meeting minutes does not violate the Open
Meeting Law, especially where the meaning of the acronym is clear or discernible. See OML
2014-138, n. 6; OML 2014-116, n. 4.
For the reasons stated above, we find that the Board did not violate the Open Meeting
Law. We now consider the complaint addressed by this determination to be resolved. This
determination does not address any other complaints that may be pending with our office or the
Board. Please feel free to contact our office at (617) 963-2540 if you have any questions
regarding this letter.
