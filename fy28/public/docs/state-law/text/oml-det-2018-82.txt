RE: Open Meeting Law Complaint 
Dear Mr. McAuliffe: 
This office received a complaint from Patrick Higgins on May 2, alleging that the 
Swansea Board of Selectmen (the "Board") violated the Open Meeting Law, G.L. c. 3OA, §§ 
18-25.1 The complaint was originally filed with the Board on or about April 9, and you 
responded to the complaint, on behalf of the Board, in a letter dated April 23. In his 
complaint, Mr. Higgins alleges that the Board failed to timely approve its February 27 
meeting minutes. 
Following our review, we find that the Board violated the Open Meeting Law in the 
manner alleged. In reaching a determination, we reviewed the original complaint, the Board's 
response to the complaint, and the complaint filed with our office requesting further review. 
FACTS 
We find the facts as follows. The Board usually meets twice per month. However, 
leading up to Town Meeting and busy with budget discussions, the Board met 11 times in 
January and February. The Board approved its February 27 meeting minutes on April 10. 
Between February 27 and April 10, the Board met on the following dates: March 6; March 
20; and March 27. 
DISCUSSION 
The Open Meeting Law requires that a public body "create and maintain accurate

minutes of all meetings, including executive sessions, setting forth the date, time and place, 
the members present or absent, a summary of the discussions on each subject, a list of 
documents and other exhibits used at the meeting, the decisions made and the actions taken at 
each meeting, including the record of all votes." G.L. c. 30A, § 22(a). The meeting minutes 
must be reviewed and approved in a timely manner. G.L. c. 30A, § 22(c). The Attorney 
General's regulations define "timely manner," as approval "within the next three public body 
meetings or within 30 days, whichever is later, unless the public body can show good cause 
for further delay." See 940 CMR 29.11. The Attorney General continues to encourage that 
minutes be approved at the next meeting whenever possible. Id; OML 2017-133.2 
The complaint alleges that the Board failed to approve its February 27 meeting 
minutes in a timely manner. In its response, the Board concedes that it had not timely 
approved them because the Board held "an unusually high number of meetings" in January 
and February. However, because the Board met three times within 30 days following its 
February 27 meeting, it had 30 days, or until March 29, by which to approve those minutes. 
See 940 CMR 29.11; OML 2018-48. An unusually high number of meetings does not 
constitute the basis for finding good cause for the delay. See OML 2018-67. We 
acknowledge that the Board missed the approval deadline by only 12 days. Nevertheless, we 
find that the Board violated the Open Meeting Law by failing to approve its February 27 
meeting minutes in a timely manner. 
For the reasons stated above, we find that the Board violated the Open Meeting Law 
by failing to approve its February 27 meeting minutes in a timely manner. We order the 
Committee's immediate and future compliance with the law's requirements, and we caution 
that similar future violations could be considered evidence of intent to violate the law. 
We now consider the complaint addressed by this determination to be resolved. This 
determination does not address any other complaints that may be pending with our office or 
the Board. Please feel free to contact our office at (617) 963-2540 if you have any questions 
regarding this letter.
