 RE: Open Meeting Law Complaint 
 
Dear Town Administrator Aronstein: 
 
This office received a complaint from Patrick Higgins on October 5, 2022,
1 alleging that 
the Swansea Board of Selectmen (the “Board”) violated the Open Meeting Law, G.L. c. 30A, §§ 
18-25. The complaint was originally filed with the Board on or about September 13, and you 
responded on behalf of the Board by email on October 5. The complaint alleges that the Board 
delegated authority to respond to an Open Meeting Law dated September 2 without first 
reviewing the September 2 complaint.   
 
Following our review, we resolve this complaint by informal action in accordance with 
940 CMR 29.07(2)(a) and find that the Board did not violate the Open Meeting Law. In reaching 
this determination we reviewed the Open Meeting Law complaint, the Board’s response, the 
request for further review, and a video recording of the Board’s September 13 meeting.
2 
 
The Board received an Open Meeting Law complaint from the Complainant on 
September 2. The notice for the Board’s September 13 meeting included the topic “Open 
Meeting Law complaint, received September 2, 2022 at 12:56 p.m.” During the September 13 
meeting the Chair explained that the Board received an Open Meeting Law complaint dated 
September 2 and asked for a motion to delegate the responsibility of responding to the Town 
Administrator.
3 A motion was so made, and the Board voted to delegate the responsibility of 
responding to the Town Administrator.   
 
 
1 All dates are in 2022, unless otherwise noted. 
2 A video recording of the Board’s September 13 meeting may be found at 
https://vimeo.com/channels/1046679/749572069.

We understand the complaint to allege that the Board violated the Open Meeting Law by 
delegating the responsibility of responding to the September 2 complaint to the Town 
Administrator during its September 13 meeting without first holding a substantive discussion 
regarding the September 2 complaint. As we have previously explained to the Complainant on 
several occasions, that a public body may have chosen to refer a complaint rather than discuss its 
substance is the public body’s prerogative. See OML 2017-148; OML 2017-149; OML 2019-40; 
OML 2021-120.4 Accordingly, we find no violation. 
 
For the reasons stated above, we find that the Board did not violate the Open Meeting 
Law as alleged. We now consider the complaint addressed by this determination to be resolved. 
This determination does not address any other complaints that may be pending with our office or 
the Board. Please feel free to contact the Division at (617) 963-2540 if you have any questions.
