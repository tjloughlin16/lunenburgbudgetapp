RE: Open Meeting Law Complaints 
Dear Attorney Emerson: 
This office received three complaints from Joaquin Soares on March 9, 2017 
alleging that the Stoughton School Committee (the "Committee") violated the Open 
Meeting Law, G.L. c. 30A, §§ 18-25. The complaints were originally filed with the 
Committee on February 10, 2017; February 13, 2017; and February 17, 2017; and you 
responded on behalf of the Committee in separate letters dated March 7, 2017. In his first 
complaint, Mr. Soares alleges that the Committee failed to timely approve minutes from 
the November 15, 2016 and November 29, 2016 Committee meetings. In his second 
complaint, Mr. Soares alleges that the Committee's November 15, 2016 open session 
meeting minutes are inaccurate. In his third complaint, Mr. Soares alleges that the 
Committee failed to periodically review its executive session meeting minutes to 
determine whether there is a continued purpose for nondisclosure. 
Following our review, we find that the Committee violated the Open Meeting 
Law by not approving its November 29, 2016 meeting minutes in a timely manner and by 
failing to periodically review its executive session minutes. We find that the Committee 
did not violate the Open Meeting Law in the other ways alleged, however. In reaching 
this determination, we reviewed the original complaints, the Committee's responses to 
the complaints, and the complaints filed with our office requesting further review. We 
also reviewed the notices of and open session minutes from the Committee's November 
15, 2016; November 29, 2016; December 20, 2016; January 10, 2017; January 24, 2017; 
and February 28, 2017 meeting minutes. In addition, we reviewed the three draft 
versions of the Committee's November 15, 2016 meeting minutes, as well as Mr. Soares' 
summary for the minutes. Finally, we spoke with Mr. Soares by telephone on April 3,

FACTS 
We find the facts as follows. At the Committee's November 15, 2016 open 
session meeting, Mr. Scares, who was a Committee member, read a prepared statement 
regarding his displeasure with the Superintendent's contract extension that was approved 
by the Committee during its November 1, 2016 meeting, a meeting he could not attend. 
The Committee met on November 29, 2016 and discussed various school 
improvement projects, the school budget for fiscal year 2018, and approved open session 
minutes from the Committee's November 1, 2016 meeting. It is our understanding that 
the Board's recording secretary stopped attending Board meetings after November 29, 
and did not respond to requests for draft meeting minutes. The minutes for the November 
29 meeting were created from the video of the meeting. 
During the Committee's December 20, 2016 open session meeting, member 
Joseph McDonough moved to accept the minutes from the November 15, 2016 meeting, 
which was seconded by member Anita Hill. Mr. Soares distributed a handout to the 
Committee, entitled "Summary for the minutes: 2016-11-15 Halloween Speech." Mr. 
Soares stated that he would like the comments contained in the summary to be included 
in the November 15 meeting minutes. The Committee unanimously approved a motion 
to table the minutes until the January 10, 2017 meeting to allow members to compare the 
summary to the actual prepared statement read by Mr. Soares at the November 15 
meeting. 
The agenda packet for the Committee's January 10, 2017 meeting included two 
draft versions of the November 15 meeting minutes for the Committee to review. During 
the January 10 open session meeting, the Committee unanimously approved a motion to 
table the November 15 meeting minutes. 
The agenda packet for the Committee's January 24, 2017 meeting included three 
draft versions of the November 15 meeting minutes. During the January 24 open session 
meeting, the Committee reviewed the three versions that had been submitted for 
approval. Version A included a summary of Mr. Soares' prepared statement from 
November 15, version B included Mr. Soares' own summary of his prepared statement, 
and version C included a condensed summary of the statement. Mr. Soares stated that he 
did not accept version A or C because neither version included his reference to data from 
Schooldigger.com. The Committee approved version C. 
During the Committee's February 28, 2017 open session meeting, the Committee 
discussed the three Open Meeting Law complaints filed by Mr. Soares. The Committee 
further discussed which version of the summary of Mr. Soares' statement from 
November 15 should be included in the minutes. The Committee approved a motion to

DISCUSSION 
The Open Meeting Law requires that a public body "create and maintain accurate 
minutes of all meetings, including executive sessions, setting forth the date, time and 
place, the members present or absent, a summary of the discussions on each subject, a list 
of documents and other exhibits used at the meeting, the decisions made and the actions 
taken at each meeting, including the record of all votes." G.L. c. 30A, § 22(a). The 
meeting minutes must be reviewed and approved in a timely manner. G.L. c. 30A, § 
22(c). Although the Open Meeting Law does not define "timely manner," we 
recommend that minutes of a meeting be approved at the next meeting, whenever 
possible. See OML 2014-15; OML 2012-91.2 For bodies that meet weekly or bi-
monthly, we have concluded that a delay of two to three months in approving minutes 
does not comply with the law. See OML 2016-62. 
The first complaint alleges that the minutes from the Committee's November 15, 
2016 and November 29, 2016 meetings were not approved in a timely manner. With 
respect to the November 15 minutes, the Committee moved to accept them during its 
December 20, 2016 meeting but the complainant requested that a summary of his 
prepared statement, which he provided to the Committee that evening, be included in the 
minutes. On January 10, 2017, the Committee tabled the November 15 minutes because 
Chair Katie-Pina Enokian thought they were incomplete. Finally, after reviewing three 
draft versions of the November 15 minutes, the Committee approved version C on 
January 24, 2017. Here, the minutes were ready for approval within two subsequent 
meetings but were tabled when the Committee agreed to review additional material 
provided by the complainant. Considering that the minutes were approved within the 
next two Committee meetings after being tabled, we find that the Committee approved 
the minutes in a timely manner and did not violate the Open Meeting Law. 
With respect to the November 29 minutes, we find that the Committee violated 
the Open Meeting Law by not approving the minutes until February 28, 2017. However, 
we decline to find an intentional violation where the Committee's recording secretary 
stopped attending Committee meetings after November 29, and did not respond to

The second complaint alleges that the Committee's November 15, 2016 meeting 
minutes failed to include an accurate summary of Mr. Soares' statement regarding the 
Superintendent's contract extension. At the November 15 open session meeting, Mr. 
Soares read a prepared statement expressing his displeasure with the Committee's 
renewal of the Superintendent's contract and citing to Stoughton School District's low 
ranking on Schooldigger.com as the reason that the Superintendent did not deserve the 
contract extension. When reviewing minutes for compliance with the Open Meeting 
Law, we look for substantial compliance with the accuracy requirement. See OML 2013-
64. By substantial compliance, we mean that the minutes should contain enough detail 
and accuracy so that a member of the public who did not attend the meeting could read 
the minutes and have a clear understanding of what occurred. See OML 2012-106. 
While the approved meeting minutes do not include reference to Schooldigger.com, they 
otherwise offer a concise summary of Mr. Soares' statements. See OML 2012-29; OML 
2011-55 (while minutes must include a summary of the discussion on each topic, a 
transcript is not required, and the minutes do not need to include every remark or opinion 
presented). Accordingly, we find that the Committee's minutes complied with the 
requirements of the Open Meeting Law. 
The third complaint alleges that the Committee failed to periodically review its 
executive session meeting minutes to determine whether there is a continued purpose for 
nondisclosure. Executive session minutes may be withheld from disclosure to the public 
"as long as publication may defeat the lawful purposes of the executive session, but no 
longer." G.L. c. 30A, § 22(f). When the purpose for a valid executive session has been 
served, the minutes and any documents or exhibits used at the session must be disclosed 
unless the attorney-client privilege or an exemption to the public records law applies to 
withhold them, in whole or in part, from disclosure. See id. Public bodies have an 
obligation to review the minutes of executive sessions at reasonable intervals to 
determine if continued non-disclosure of minutes is warranted, and to announce that 
determination at the next meeting following its review. G.L. c. BOA, § 22(g)(1); see 
OML 2013-56; OML 2015-94. 
In response to the complaint, the Committee acknowledged that it had failed to 
schedule periodic reviews and would "conduct these reviews immediately." The notice 
for the Committee's March 28, 2017 meeting included the heading "Executive Session," 
under which one topic was "Review of Approved Executive Session Minutes per Chapter 
3OA, Sub. Section 22(g)(2)." During the March 28 executive session, the Committee 
reviewed at least fifty sets of executive session meeting minutes from 2014 to 2017. The 
Committee is scheduled to review additional executive session minutes at its June 27, 
2017 meeting. We find that, while the Committee violated the Open Meeting Law by

CONCLUSION 
We find that the Committee violated the Open Meeting Law by not approving its 
November 29, 2016 meeting minutes in a timely manner and by failing to periodically 
review its executive session minutes. We find that the Committee did not violate the 
Open Meeting Law where it included an accurate summary and approved its November 
15, 2016 meeting minutes in a timely manner. We order the Committee's immediate and 
future compliance with the law's requirements, and we caution that similar future 
violations could be considered evidence of intent to violate the law. 
We now consider the complaints addressed by this determination to be resolved.
