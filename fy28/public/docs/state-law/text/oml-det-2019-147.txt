RE: Open Meeting Law Complaint
Dear Attorney Boddy:
This office received a complaint from Harold Rhodes, on April 16, 2019, alleging that the 
Milford School Committee (the “Committee”) violated the Open Meeting Law, G.L. c. 30A, §§ 
18-25. The complaint was originally filed with the Committee on March 7, 2019. The Committee 
has not responded to the complaint.1 In his complaint, Mr. Rhodes alleges that the Committee 
failed to create and approve minutes for its January 15, 2019, meeting in a timely manner.2 Mr. 
Rhodes further alleges that the minutes for the January 15 meeting do not identify the location of 
the meeting and fail to include a summary of the discussion held at the meeting.
We resolve this complaint by informal action in accordance with 940 CMR 29.07(2)(a) 
and find that the Committee violated the Open Meeting Law by failing to approve meeting 
minutes for its January 15 meeting in a timely manner. The Open Meeting Law requires that a 
public body “create and maintain accurate minutes of all meetings.” G.L. c. 30A, § 22(a). The 
meeting minutes must be reviewed and approved in a timely manner. G.L. c. 30A, § 22(c); 940 
CMR 29.11 (2). The Open Meeting Law itself does not define “timely manner.” However, the 
Attorney General's Open Meeting Law regulations provide that “timely manner” means “within 
the next three public body meetings or within 30 days, whichever is later, unless the public body 
can show good cause for further delay.” 940 CMR 29.11 (2). Whenever possible, we recommend 
that minutes of a meeting be approved at the next meeting. After a review of minutes of the 
Committee’s several meetings held after the January 15 meeting, we find that the Committee did

not approve meeting minutes for its January 15, 2019, meeting within the next three meetings or

As for the assertion that the minutes of the January 15 meeting fail to include the location 
of the meeting or an adequate summary of the discussion held at the meeting, we find that the 
minutes remain in draft form and have not yet been approved. Because the minutes may still be 
amended, we do not find a violation of the Open Meeting Law for insufficiency of meeting 
minutes. See OML 2014-13.3
For the reasons stated above, we find that the Committee violated the Open Meeting Law 
by failing to review and approve minutes in a timely manner. We order the Committee’s 
immediate and future compliance with the Open Meeting Law, and caution that similar 
violations in the future may be considered evidence of intent to violate the Law. 940 CMR 29.02. 
In addition, we order the Committee to review and approve the minutes of its January 15 meeting 
within 30 days of receipt of this letter and to certify to our office that it has done so.
We now consider the complaint addressed by this determination to be resolved. This 
determination does not address any other complaints that may be pending with our office or the 
Committee. Please feel free to contact the Division at (617) 963-2540 if you have any questions.
