Dear Attorney Riley: 
This office received two complaints from John Schlichte, on March 5, 2018 and 
March 23, 2018, respectively, alleging that the West Boylston Board of Selectmen (the 
"Board") violated the Open Meeting Law, G.L. c. 3OA, §§ 18-25. Mr. Schlichte's first 
complaint was filed with the Board on December 12, 2017, and alleges that the Board 
discussed an anticipated topic that was not listed in the meeting notice during its November 
15, 2017 meeting.1 The Board responded to this complaint by letter dated January 5, 2018. 
The second complaint was filed with the Board on February 15, 2018, and alleges that the 
Board failed to allow Mr. Schlichte the opportunity to ask questions during a period reserved 
for public comments at the Board's January 17, 2018 meeting. The Board responded to the 
complaint by letter dated April 20, 2018.2 
Following our review, we find that the Board did not violate the Open Meeting Law. 
In reaching this determination, we reviewed the complaints filed with the Board, the Board's 
responses, and the complaints filed with our office requesting our review. We also reviewed 
the minutes of the Board's November 15, 2017 and January 17, 2018 meetings. Finally, we 
spoke with Mr. Schlichte by telephone on May 15, 2018.

FACTS 
We find the facts as follows. The notice for the Board's November 15, 2017 meeting 
included a topic listed as "Consider approving the hiring of Brittany Blaney for the positon 
(sic) of Access Coordinator effective November 16, 2017 at a rate of $17.50 per hour 
(tentative)." 
During the Board's November 15, 2017 meeting, the Board discussed appointing 
Brittany Blaney to the position of Access Coordinator. However, following discussion, a vote 
to appoint her failed, with two in favor and three against. After further discussion, the Board 
considered extending Ms. Blaney's hours with the cable access station from five hours to 
fourteen hours a week at a rate of $17.50 per hour. The motion was amended to provide a 
stipend to Ms. Blaney "in the amount of $4,000 for filling in as Access Coordinator for 
WBPA-TV from July 14, 2017 through November 1, 2017." That motion passed with four in 
favor and one opposed. On January 3, 2018, in response to Mr. Schlichte's December 12, 
2017 complaint, the Board held a new discussion and vote regarding the November 15, 2017 
stipend decision. Once again, the Board approved the stipend with four in favor and one 
opposed. 
During the Board's January 17, 2018 meeting, Mr, Schlichte addressed the Board 
during the Public Comment portion at the beginning of the meeting. Mr. Schlichte spoke 
about his December 12, 2017 Open Meeting Law complaint. When Mr. Schlichte addressed 
questions to members of the Board, the chair informed him that the public comment portion 
of the meeting permits members of the public to address the Board but is not for addressing 
questions to the Board or its members. 
DISCUSSION 
I. The Board's Consideration of a Stipend Naturally Flowed from its Noticed 
Discussion Topic About Hiring Ms. Blaney as Access Coordinator. 
The Open Meeting Law requires public bodies to post notice 48 hours prior to a 
meeting and include a "listing of topics that the chair reasonably anticipates will be discussed 
at the meeting." G.L. c. 30A, § 20(b). Public bodies must list topics for discussion with 
"sufficient specificity to reasonably advise the public of the issues to be discussed at the 
meeting." 940 CMR 29.03(l)(b). We generally consider a topic to be sufficiently specific 
when a reasonable member of the public could read the topic and understand the anticipated 
nature of the public body's discussion. OML 2014-42.3 Where a public body's discussion 
naturally flows from its consideration of the posted agenda topic, the notice was sufficient to 
inform the public of the discussion that occurred. See OML 2018-14; OML 2014-44; OML 
2014-38.

Here, the notice topic informed the public that the Board would be considering Ms. 
Blaney for the position of Access Coordinator at a rate of $17.50 per hour. The Board 
decided not to hire her in that position, but rather to pay her a stipend to reflect interim work 
she had done associated with that position. While that specific action was not reflected in the 
notice, the discussion naturally flowed from the notice topic. 
Although we find no violation of the Open Meeting Law, we commend the Board for 
holding a new vote to ensure that that the public had the benefit of additional notice before 
taking final action. 
II. The Board Did Not Violate the Open Meeting Law bv Limiting Mr. Schlichte 
During Public Comment at its January 17. 2018 Meeting. 
The Open Meeting Law states that "no person shall address a meeting of a public body 
without permission of the chair, and all persons shall, at the request of the chair, be silent." 
G.L. c. 30A, § 20(g), The law therefore gives discretion to the chair of the public body to 
determine who may speak or otherwise participate at a meeting. See OML 2014-23; OML 
2013-64; OML 2012-59. Thus, the chair has the authority to limit public comment to certain 
topics. 
Here, the chair limited the public comment portion of the Board's January 17, 2018 
meeting to prohibit speakers from addressing questions to the Board. The Board still 
permitted Mr. Schlichte to address the Board with his opinions and comments. We conclude 
that the Board acted within the limits of the Open Meeting Law. 
CONCLUSION 
For the reasons stated above, we find that the Board did not violate the Open Meeting 
Law. We now consider the complaints addressed by this determination to be resolved. This 
determination does not address any other complaints that may be pending with our office or 
the Board. Please feel free to contact our office at (617) 963-2540 if you have any questions 
regarding this letter.
