RE: Open Meeting Law Complaint 
Dear Attorney Pollard: 
 
This office received a complaint from Patrick Higgins on January 2, 2020, alleging that 
the Lee Board of Selectmen (the “Board”) violated the Open Meeting Law, G.L. c. 30A, §§ 18-
25.  The complaint was originally filed with the Board on or around December 3, 2019, and you 
responded, on behalf of the Council, by letter dated December 20, 2019.1  The complaint alleges 
that the Council posted an insufficiently detailed meeting notice for its December 3 meeting.   
 
After reviewing the original complaint, the Board’s response to the complaint, the 
complaint filed with our office requesting further review, and the notice and minutes for the 
December 3 meeting, we find that the Board violated the Open Meeting Law as alleged. 
 
FACTS 
 
  We find the facts as follows.  On November 29, the Board posted notice for a meeting to 
be held on December 3.  Under the heading “General Business,” the notice included the topic 
“2020 Liquor License Renewals.”  The notice did not include a list of specific specific 
establishments that were seeking renewal.  The December 3 meeting was held as planned.  At the 
meeting, a list of the establishments seeking liquor license renewal was read aloud, and the 
Board voted unanimously to approve the license renewals. 
 
 
1 Hereafter, all dates in this letter refer to the year 2019.

DISCUSSION 
 
A public body must post notice of every meeting at least 48 hours in advance, not 
including Saturdays, Sundays, and legal holidays.  G.L. c. 30A, § 20(b).  The notice must be 
printed in a legible, easily understandable format and must contain the date, time and place of the 
meeting, as well as a listing of topics that the chair reasonably anticipates will be discussed.  Id.  
The list of topics shall have “sufficient specificity to reasonably advise the public of the issues to 
be discussed at the meeting.”  940 CMR 29.03(l)(b).  We generally consider a topic to be 
sufficiently specific when a reasonable member of the public could read the topic and understand 
the anticipated nature of the public body's discussion.  See OML 2015-35.2   
 
The complaint alleges that the December 3 meeting notice concerning the topic of liquor 
license renewals lacked sufficient detail in that it did not list the “names, addresses and type of 
licenses” to be considered for renewal.  We agree that the Board’s meeting notice should have 
identified the establishments whose license renewals were to be considered.  See OML 2020-20; 
OML 2017-20; OML 2017-33; OML   Although including street addresses may oftentimes be 
useful to the public, such detailed specificity is not necessary if the Board otherwise sufficiently 
identifies the establishments.  See OML 2017-33.  Furthermore, although in a prior 
determination we stated that a meeting notice should include the specific type of license renewals 
to be considered, in that instance the meeting notice simply referred to “license renewals”; here, 
by contrast, the meeting notice specified “Liquor License Renewals.”  See OML 2017-33; OML 
2017-20.   
 
CONCLUSION 
 
For the reasons stated above, we find that the Board violated the Open Meeting Law by 
failing to list on its meeting notice the specific establishments whose liquor licenses were to be 
considered for renewal.  We order the Board’s immediate and future compliance with the Open 
Meeting Law and caution that similar future violations may be considered evidence of intent to 
violate the law.   
 
We now consider the complaint addressed by this determination to be resolved. This 
determination does not address any other complaints that may be pending with our office or the 
Board. Please feel free to contact the Division at (617) 963 - 2540 if you have any questions.
