This office received a complaint from Deborah Nicholson, dated April 30, 2013, alleging 
that the Ashfield Board of Health (the "Board") violated the Open Meeting Law, G.L. c. 30A, §§ 
18-25. Ms. Nicholson's complaint was originally filed with the Board on or about March 24, 
2013, and the Board responded to the original complaint by letter dated April 17, 2013. The 
complaint specifically alleges that 1) the notices for the Board's March 5, 2013 and March 12, 
2013 meetings were not sufficiently specific to inform the public of the topics to be discussed; 2) 
the Board's Chair did not provide sufficient detail regarding the purpose for entering into 
executive session during those two meetings; 3) the Board failed to notify the individuals who 
were the subject of the Board's discussion during its March 12, 2013 meeting; and 4) the Board 
failed to comply with our order in determination OML 2013-14. 
Following our review, we find that the Board did not violate the Open Meeting Law. In 
reaching this determination, we reviewed the March 24, 2013 complaint filed with the Board; the 
Board's April 17, 2013 response; and the April 30, 2013 complaint filed with our office. 
Additionally, we reviewed the open and executive sessions minutes of the Board's March 8, 
2012; March 5, 2013 and March 12, 2013 meetings. Finally, we reviewed an audio recording of 
the Board's March 5, 2013 and March 12, 2013 meetings. 
The Board's March 8, 2012 meeting was the subject of three Open Meeting Law 
complaints, resolved through OML 2013-14, issued by our office on February 11, 2013. In 
OML 2013-14, our office ordered the Board's immediate and future compliance with the Open 
Meeting Law, and ordered the Board to review the Open Meeting Law Guide and certify in 
writing within thirty (30) days of the date of the letter that they reviewed the determination and

DISCUSSION 
1. The Notices for the Board's March 5, 2013 and March 12. 2013 Meetings were 
Sufficiently Specific to Inform the Public of the Topics to be Discussed. 
The Open Meeting Law requires that all meeting notices "shall be printed in a legible, 
easily understandable format and shall contain the date, time, and place of such meeting and a 
listing of topics that the chair reasonably anticipates will be discussed at the meeting." G.L. c. 
3 OA, § 20(b). The list of topics shall have sufficient specificity to reasonably advise the public 
of the issues to be discussed at the meeting. 940 CMR 29.03(l)(b). 
Here, the notice for the March 5, 2013 meeting listed a topic as "attorney General reply to 
O.M.L. complaints." While the Board could have included detail about the specific complaints, 
such as the determination number or name of the complainant, the topic was sufficiently specific 
to inform the public that the Board would be discussing a letter from the Attorney General 
resolving several Open Meeting Law complaints.

The notice for the March 12, 2013 meeting listed two topics: "1. Attorney General reply 
to O.M.L. complaints," and "Executive session - under G.L. C.30a S21(a)(l) to discuss and 
amend the BOH minutes from March 08, 2012, in relation to the mental health condition of an 
employee. Votes may be taken." Again, the Board could have included more detail about the 
specific complaints referenced in the first topic. However, the topic was sufficiently specific to 
inform the public of the nature of the discussion. The second topic was sufficiently specific to 
inform members of the public that the Board would be entering into executive session under G.L. 
c. 30A, § 21(a)(1) to discuss and revise the minutes of its March 8, 2012 executive session. 
2. The Board did not Meet in Executive Session on March 5. 2013. and the Board 
Provided Sufficient Detail about the Purpose for Entering into Executive Session 
During its March 12. 2013 Meeting. 
Before entering into an executive session, the chair shall state the purpose for the 
executive session, stating all subjects that may be revealed without compromising the purpose 
for which the executive session was called. G.L. c. 3OA, § 21(b)(3). A public body may enter 
into executive session to "discuss the reputation, character, physical condition or mental health, 
rather than professional competence, of an individual, or to discuss the discipline or dismissal of, 
or complaints or charges brought against, a public officer, employee, staff member or 
individual." G.L. c. 30A, § 21(a)(1) ("Purpose 1"). 
The Board does not appear to have entered into executive session on March 5, 2013. On 
March 12, 2013, during the open session portion of the meeting, Board member Susan Clark 
explained that the reason for going into executive session would be Purpose 1, to review and 
revise the March 8, 2012 executive session minutes. The Chair then called a vote to enter into 
executive session on that topic. Although the Chair did not himself state the purpose for the 
executive session, the purpose had been stated by Board member Susan Clark prior to the vote to 
enter into executive session. We therefore find that the Board met its obligation in stating the 
purpose for the executive session, and stating all subjects that may be revealed without 
compromising the purpose for which the executive session was called. G.L. c. 30A, § 21(b)(3). 
We remind the Board that the Open Meeting Law specifically states that the Chair should state 
the purpose for the executive session. Id. 
3. The Board Properly Notified the Individual who was the Subject of the Board's 
Executive Session Discussion During its March 12. 2013 Meeting. 
A public body may enter into executive session under Purpose 1, to "discuss the 
reputation, character, physical condition or mental health, rather than professional competence, 
of an individual, or to discuss the discipline or dismissal of, or complaints or charges brought 
against, a public officer, employee, staff member or individual." G.L. c. 30A, § 21(a)(1). The 
individual to be discussed in such executive session shall be notified in writing by the public 
body at least 48 hours prior to the proposed executive session; provided, however, that 
notification may be waived upon written agreement of the parties. Id. 
The complainant alleges that "[djuring the Board of Health meeting [on] March 12, 2013, 
(called to address a decision issued by the Attorney General in response to three Open Meeting 
Law complaints), two of the parties discussed on March 8, 2012 were not notified (this was

confirmed)." The complaint further alleges that the Board "violated executive session by not 
notifying my Advocate (Suzanne Corbett) and myself of the meeting scheduled for March 12,

executive session discussion. We find no evidence that the Board discussed "the reputation,

4. The Board Complied with our Order in Determination OML 2013-14. 
In OML 2013-14, issued on February 11, 2013, our office ordered the Board to review 
the Open Meeting Law Guide and certify in writing within thirty (30) days of the date of the 
letter, that they reviewed the determination and the Guide. Additionally, we ordered the Board 
to draft and approve the revised executive session minutes of its March 8, 2012 meeting within 
thirty (30) days of the date of the letter and publicly release those portions that were not 
appropriate for discussion in executive session. The Board members certified to our office by 
letter dated March 7, 2013 that they reviewed the Open Meeting Law Guide. Additionally, we 
reviewed the revised minutes of the Board's March 8, 2012 executive session and find that the 
Board complied with our order. 
CONCLUSION 
For the reasons stated above, we find that the Board did not violate the Open Meeting 
Law.
