This office received a complaint from Jennifer Barbeau on October 13, 2021, alleging 
that the North Adams City Council Community Development Committee (the “Committee”) 
violated the Open Meeting Law, G.L. c. 30A, §§ 18-25.  The complaint was originally filed with 
the Committee on or about July 30, 2021, and former Committee Chair Benjamin Lamb

complaint alleges that the Committee “has not filed meeting minutes and supportive 
documentation” for meetings held on December 16, 2020, May 5, 2021, June 9, 2021, and July 
20, 2021.1  
 
We resolve this matter by informal action, in accordance with 940 CMR 29.07(2)(a), 
after reviewing the original complaint, the Committee’s response to the complaint, the complaint 
filed with our office requesting further review, the notice of the May 5, 2021, Committee 
meeting, and the notices and minutes of the Committee meetings held on December 16, 2020,

June 9, 2021, and July 20, 2021.  We find that the Committee did not violate the Open Meeting

The Open Meeting Law requires that public bodies post notice 48 hours in advance of a 
meeting and include a “listing of topics that the chair reasonably anticipates will be discussed at 
the meeting.”  G.L. c. 30A, § 20(b).  Public bodies are required to list topics in a meeting notice 
with “sufficient specificity to reasonably advise the public of the issues to be discussed at the 
meeting.”  940 CMR 29.03(l)(b). 
   
The Open Meeting Law further requires that a public body “create and maintain accurate 
minutes of all meetings, including executive sessions, setting forth the date, time and place, the 
members present or absent, a summary of the discussions on each subject, a list of documents 
and other exhibits used at the meeting, the decisions made and the actions taken at each meeting, 
including the record of all votes.”  G.L. c. 30A, § 22(a).  The meeting minutes must be reviewed 
and approved in a timely manner, that is, “within the next three public body meetings or within 
30 days, whichever is later, unless the public body can show good cause for further delay.”  See 
G.L. c. 30A, § 22(c); 940 CMR 29.11; OML 2018-48.2   
 
Here, the complaint alleges that the Committee “has not filed meeting minutes or 
supportive documentation.”  The Open Law Meeting does not require that public bodies post or 
file their minutes to a website or elsewhere, but only that minutes be created, maintained, and 
approved in a timely manner, and made available within 10 days of request.  G.L. c. 30A, §§ 
22(a), (c); OML 2020-78; OML 2017-98.  Nor does the Open Meeting Law require that a public 
body attach supporting documents to a meeting notice or that it publicly post a “meeting packet” 
that contains such documents.  See OML 2022-19; OML 2021-181.  Furthermore, although all 
documents and other exhibits used by a public body during a meeting “shall, along with the 
minutes, be part of the official record of the session” and are considered “public records in their 
entirety,” there is no requirement that the public body affirmatively provide those documents or 
exhibits to persons in attendance at a meeting or attach or include those documents with the 
minutes.  See G. L. c. 30A, §§ 22(d), (e); OML 2016-94.  Nonetheless, we note that the 
Committee attached and posted to the municipal website relevant documents it planned to 
discuss along with the notices for the meetings held on December 16, 2020, and May 5, 2021.  
Although relevant documents were not posted with the notices of the Committee meetings held 
on June 9, 2021, and July 20, 2021, the Committee was not obligated to post such documents and 
therefore we do not find this to be a violation of the Open Meeting Law.  Finally, we find that the 
Committee posted minutes of the meetings held on December 16, 2020, June 9, 2021, and July 
20, 2021, to the municipal website.3  We note that the May 5, 2021, meeting was cancelled and 
therefore the Committee was not required to create minutes of this meeting.
