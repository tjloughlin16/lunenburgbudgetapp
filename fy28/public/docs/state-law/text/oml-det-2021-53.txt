 RE: Open Meeting Law Complaint 
Dear Council Chair: 
This office received a complaint from Nancy Thompson on November 17, 2020, alleging 
that the Berkley Council on Aging (the “Council”) violated the Open Meeting Law, G.L. c. 30A, 
§§ 18-25. The complaint was originally filed with the Council on August 4, and the Council 
responded by letter dated November 9.1, 2 In her complaint, Ms. Thompson, who is a member of 
the Council, alleges that the Council began its July 16 meeting before the posted start time of 11 
A.M., and that a quorum of the Council was not present when the meeting began.3 
We resolve this complaint by informal action in accordance with 940 CMR 29.07(2)(a), 
and find no violation of the Open Meeting Law. In reaching this determination, we reviewed the 
original complaint, the Council’s response to the complaint, and the complainant’s request for 
further review. We also reviewed the Council’s website and corresponded by email with two 
witnesses who attended the July 16 meeting.  
The Open Meeting Law defines a “meeting” as “a deliberation by a public body with 
respect to any matter within the body’s jurisdiction.” G.L. c. 30A, § 18. A “deliberation” is 
defined as “an oral or written communication through any medium, including electronic mail, 
between or among a quorum of a public body on any public business within its jurisdiction; 
provided, however, that ‘deliberation’ shall not include the distribution of a meeting agenda,

or documents that may be discussed at a meeting, provided that no opinion of a member is 
expressed.” Id. A public body may not begin a meeting earlier than the posted start time. See 
OML 2014-35; OML 2013-161. For purposes of the Open Meeting Law, a meeting does not 
begin until a quorum of the public body is present and begins to discuss public body business. A 
“quorum” is a simple majority of the members of a public body. G.L. c. 30A, § 18; see also 
OML 2020-170.4  
Here, the Council has five seats, including one ex officio member. Therefore, three 
members constitute a quorum, regardless of any vacancies. See OML 2013-162.5 On the morning 
of the July 16 meeting, Council Chair Margaret Moroff and Council Member Arlene Medeiros 
convened the Council’s 11 A.M. meeting a few minutes before 11 A.M. A few minutes later, 
Council Member Nancy Thompson arrived to join the meeting. Because the Council has a 
quorum of three members, we find that, for purposes of Open Meeting Law, the meeting did not 
begin until Ms. Thompson arrived and a quorum of the Council began discussing Council 
business. Two witnesses describe Ms. Thompson as arriving between 10:58 A.M. and 11:01 
A.M. Based on the available evidence, we find that a quorum of Council members only 
discussed Council business after 11 A.M. Therefore, as the Council’s July 16 meeting did not 
start until after the posted meeting time, we find no violation of the Open Meeting Law. We 
caution the Council not to start future meetings before the posted start time. We take no position 
as to whether any action purportedly taken by less than a quorum of the Council would constitute 
a valid exercise of the Council’s authority.  
 For the reasons stated above, we find no violation of the Open Meeting Law. We now 
consider the complaint addressed by this determination to be resolved. This determination does 
not address any other complaints that may be pending with our office or the Council. Please feel 
free to contact the Division at (617) 963 - 2540 if you have any questions.
