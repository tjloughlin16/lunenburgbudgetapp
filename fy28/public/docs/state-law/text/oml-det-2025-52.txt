This office received a complaint from Christopher Kelleher on January 7, 2025, alleging 
that the Brookfield Conservation Commission (the “Commission”) violated the Open Meeting 
Law, G.L. c. 30A, §§ 18-25. The complaint was originally filed with the Commission on or 
about October 11, 2024, and you responded on behalf of the Commission via email on October 
24, 2024. The complaint alleges that the Commission has failed to create and timely approve 
several sets of meeting minutes and that the Commission has failed to publish those minutes. 
 
Following our review, we find that the Commission violated the Open Meeting Law by 
failing to create and timely approve six sets of meeting minutes. We find no violation with 
respect to the allegation that the Commission failed to publish meeting minutes. In reaching this 
determination, we reviewed the Open Meeting Law complaint, the Commission’s response, and 
the request for further review. We also reviewed the Town’s site on mytowngovernment.org for 
Commission meeting minutes. 
 
The Open Meeting Law requires that public bodies create and maintain accurate minutes 
of al
l meetings, including executive sessions. G.L. c. 30A, § 22(a). Additionally, minutes of open 
session meetings must be approved in a timely manner. G.L. c. 30A, § 22(c). The Attorney 
General’s Open Meeting Law regulations define “timely manner” as “within the next three 
public body meetings or within 30 days, whichever is later, unless the public body can show 
good cause for further delay.” See 940 CMR 29.11(2). When possible, the Attorney General 
“encourages public bodies to approve minutes at the next meeting.” 940 CMR 29.11(2). Finally, 
the Open Meeting Law requires that “[t]he minutes of an open session, if they exist and whether

approved or in draft form, shall be made available upon request by any person within 10 days.” 
G.L. c. 30A, § 22(c).  
 
The complaint alleges that the Commission violated the Open Meeting Law by failing to 
timely create, approve, and publish minutes for meetings held on October 26, 2023, as well as 
March 28, April 25, May 15, June 17, August 15,
1 and August 28, 2024. To begin, we note that 
the Open Meeting Law does not require public bodies to publish their meeting minutes to a 
website or otherwise disseminate meeting minutes absent a request. See G.L. c. 30A, § 22; OML 
2025-17; OML 2017-25; OML 2016-17.2 Therefore, a failure to “publish” meeting minutes does 
not violate the Open Meeting Law. Nonetheless, we note that the Attorney General encourages 
public bodies to post their meeting minutes to a website as this furthers the Open Meeting Law’s 
goal of government transparency, and we commend the Commission for its commitment to 
posting its meeting minutes online.
3 
 
Next, with respect to the allegation that the Commission failed to create and timely 
approve minutes for its meetings held on October 26, 2023, as well as March 28, April 25, May 
15, June 17, and August 28, 2024, the Commission does not deny that it failed to create and 
timely approve these minutes. Indeed, the Commission’s response suggests that as of the date of 
the response, at least some of the minutes did not exist, even in draft form, and that the 
Commission was working to create them. Therefore, we find that the Commission violated the 
Open Meeting Law by failing to create and timely approve minutes for meetings held on October 
26, 2023, as well as March 28, April 25, May 15, June 17, and August 28, 2024.  
 
We order the Commission’s immediate and future compliance with the Open Meeting 
Law and caution that future findings of a similar violation may be considered evidence of an 
intent to violate the Law. Additionally, we order the Commission to create and approve minutes 
for the above-listed meetings within 30 days of the date of this determination and to certify to 
our office that it has complied with this order.
