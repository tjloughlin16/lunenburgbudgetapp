 RE: Open Meeting Law Complaint 
 
Dear Mr. Minardi: 
 
This office received a complaint from Patrick Higgins on September 25, 2025,1 alleging 
that the Swansea Board of Assessors (the “Board”) violated the Open Meeting Law, G.L. c. 30A, 
§§ 18-25. The complaint was originally filed with the Board on or about September 17, and you 
responded on behalf of the Board by letter dated September 24. The complaint alleges that the 
Board failed to timely approve minutes for meetings held on November 19, 2024; and January 
29, February 12, February 25, March 12, April 16, and April 30, 2025. The complaint also 
alleges that the Board violated the Open Meeting Law by including language on its meeting 
notices regarding G.L. c. 214, § 1B. 
 
Following our review, we find that the Board did not violate the Open Meeting Law by 
failing to timely create and approve meeting minutes. We decline to review the second 
allegation. In reaching this determination we reviewed the original Open Meeting Law 
complaint, the Board’s response, the request for further review, and meeting minutes posted on 
the Board’s page of the Town of Swansea’s website.
2 We also reviewed the notice the Board 
posted of a joint meeting with the Swansea Select Board to be held on November 19, 2024. 
 
The Open Meeting Law requires that public bodies “create and maintain accurate minutes 
of all meetings, including executive sessions . . . .” G.L. c. 30A, § 22(a). Open session minutes 
must be created and approved in a timely manner. G.L. c. 30A, § 22(c). “Timely manner” means 
“within the next three public body meetings or within 30 days, whichever is later, unless the

public body can show good cause for further delay.” 940 CMR 29.11. Whenever possible, we 
recommend that minutes of a meeting be approved at the next meeting. Id.  
 
The complaint alleges that the Board failed to timely approve minutes for meetings held 
on November 19, 2024; and January 29, February 12, February 25, March 12, April 16, and 
April 30, 2025. In its response to the complaint, the Board neither acknowledged nor denied the 
allegation, but instead stated that, “The outstanding meeting minutes have been approved by the 
Board and can be found on the Town of Swansea’s website.”  
 
Based on a review of the Board’s meeting minutes, the Board approved the minutes of its 
meetings held on January 29, February 12, February 25, March 12, April 16, and April 30, 2025, 
at the next meeting following each meeting. Therefore, we find no violation with respect to these 
minutes. Additionally, the Board posted notice of a joint meeting to be held with the Swansea 
Select Board on November 19, 2024. A review of the minutes of that meeting does not show that 
a quorum of the Board attended the Select Board’s November 19, 2024, meeting. Instead, the 
minutes show that the Chair of the Board and the Principal Assessor attended the meeting and 
addressed the Select Board. As such, the November 19 meeting was not a meeting of the Board 
and the Board was not required to create or approve minutes of that meeting.
3   
 
We decline to review the allegation that the Board added to its meeting notices language 
related to G.L. c. 214, § 1B. Our office will not conduct broad audits based on generalized 
allegations. See OML 2014-119; OML 2012-106.
4 The complaint does not identify any 
particular notice alleged to include this language. Regardless, even if we did review this 
allegation, we would not find a violation. The Open Meeting Law requires that meeting notices 
“be printed in a legible, easily understandable format and shall contain the date, time and place 
of the meeting and a listing of topics that the chair reasonably anticipates will be discussed at the 
meeting.” G.L. c. 30A, § 20(b). The Open Meeting Law does not prohibit a public body from 
including additional information on its meeting notices.  
 
For the reasons stated above, we find that the Board did not violate the Open Meeting 
Law by failing to approve open session meeting minutes in a timely manner. We now consider 
the complaint addressed by this determination to be resolved. This determination does not 
address any other complaints that may be pending with our office or the Board. Please feel free 
to contact the Division at (617) 963-2540 if you have any questions.
