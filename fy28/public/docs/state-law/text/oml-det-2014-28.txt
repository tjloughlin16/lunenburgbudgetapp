RE: Open Meeting Law Complaint - Truro Board of Selectmen 
Dear Mr. Cobum: 
This office received a complaint from Frances M. Coco, dated October 1, 2013, alleging 
that the Truro Board of Selectmen (the "Board") violated the Open Meeting Law, G.L. c. 30A, 
§§ 18-25. Specifically, the complaint alleges that the Board improperly denied Ms. Coco's 
request for the executive session minutes from the Board's February 5, 2013 and March 14, 2013 
executive session meetings. The complaint was originally filed with the Board on or about July 
24, 2013, and the Board responded by letter dated August 22, 2013. 
Following our review, we find that the Board did not violate the Open Meeting Law. In 
reaching this determination, we reviewed the July 24, 2013 complaint filed with the Board; the 
Board's August 22, 2013 response; and the October 1, 2013 complaint filed with our office. 
Additionally, we reviewed, in camera, the minutes of the Board's February 5, 2013; February 
12, 2013; and March 14, 2013 executive sessions. 
The Board met in executive session on February 5, 2013 and March 14, 2013. In each 
instance, the Board entered into executive session "to conduct strategy relative to collective 
bargaining" as well as to conduct a grievance hearing regarding Ms. Coco, who serves as the 
Town's Assistant Deputy Assessor. The only topic discussed during the executive sessions was 
the grievance. Ms. Coco was present during portions of both of these executive session 
meetings. On June 10, 2013, Ms. Coco requested copies of the Board's executive session 
minutes from the February 5, 2013 and March 14, 2013 meetings. In a letter dated June 25, 
2013, the Board denied her request, writing in part that the Board "has not yet released the 
minutes of the Executive Sessions."

DISCUSSION 
As an initial matter, Ms. Coco argues that she is entitled to the executive session minutes 
because G.L. c. 30A, § 21(a)(1), or "Purpose 1," guarantees her certain rights. However the 
Board entered into executive session under G.L. c. 30A, § 21(a)(3), or "Purpose 3," and not 
Purpose 1. Purpose 1 allows a public body to enter into executive session "to discuss the 
discipline or dismissal of, or complaints or charges brought against, a public officer, employee, 
staff member or individual." G.L. c. 30A, § 21(a)(1). Purpose 3 allows a public body to enter 
into executive session to "discuss strategy with respect to collective bargaining or litigation." 
G.L. c. 3OA, § 21(a)(3). A public body may choose to enter into executive session under 
Purpose 3, instead of Purpose 1, to discuss a grievance matter because a grievance is part of the 
collective bargaining process. Ghiglione v. School Committee of Southbridge, 376 Mass 70, 73 
(1978); OML 2012-53; OML 2012-27.1 Because the Board was reviewing a grievance matter 
pursuant to a collective bargaining agreement, the Board properly convened in executive session 
under Purpose 3. We therefore decline to review whether Ms. Coco would be entitled to the 
minutes if the executive session had been held under Purpose 1. 
We next consider whether the Board improperly withheld the minutes of its Purpose 3 
executive session. Executive session minutes may be withheld from disclosure to the public "as 
long as publication may defeat the lawful purposes of the executive session, but no longer." G.L. 
c. 30A, § 22(f). When the purpose for a valid executive session has been served, the minutes and 
any documents or exhibits used at the session must be disclosed, unless the attorney-client 
privilege or an exemption to the Public Records Law applies. Id. The burden of justifying 
continued nondisclosure of executive session minutes lies with the public body. Foudy v. 
Amherst-Pelharn Regional School Committee. 402 Mass. 179, 184 (1988). Upon request by any 
person to inspect or copy the minutes of an executive session or any portion thereof, the public 
body must respond to the request within 10 days and provide any minutes that it has determined 
may be released. G.L. c. 3 OA, § 22(g)(2). If the body has not performed a review of the 
requested minutes as of the date of the request, it must do so no later than the body's next 
meeting or 30 days, whichever first occurs. Id. 
The Board responded to Ms. Coco's request in a June 25, 2013 letter and declined to 
release the requested minutes because the purpose for the executive session continues to warrant 
non-disclosure.2 Our office confirmed by telephone with Attorney Brian Riley3 on March 3, 
2014 that the grievance matter discussed during these two meetings is ongoing. The Board 
therefore did not violate the Open Meeting Law by withholding these minutes. We remind the 
Board that once the grievance process has concluded, it should again review the minutes of its 
February 5, 2013 and March 14, 2013 executive session meetings to determine whether the need 
for confidentiality continues, and publicly release those minutes and any documents or exhibits 
used at the executive session, unless the attorney/client privilege or an exemption to the Public
