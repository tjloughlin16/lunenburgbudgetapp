DISCUSSION 
 
I. The Board violated the Open Meeting Law when it failed to review executive 
session minutes at reasonable intervals to determine whether continued 
nondisclosure was warranted. 
 
The Open Meeting Law was enacted “to eliminate much of the secrecy surrounding the 
deliberations and decisions on which public policy is based.”  Ghiglione v. School Board of 
Southbridge, 376 Mass. 70, 72 (1978).  To that end, the Law requires that a public body create 
and maintain accurate minutes of all meetings, including executive sessions.  G.L. c. 30A, § 
22(a).  The Law further states that “[m]inutes of all open sessions shall be created and approved 
in a timely manner”  G.L. c. 30A, § 22(c), while requiring that executive session minutes be 
“review[ed] ... at reasonable intervals to determine if continued non-disclosure of minutes is 
warranted.”  G.L. c. 30A, § 22(g)(1).   
 
In other words, the Open Meeting Law does not require that a public body approve 
executive session minutes in a timely manner, rather that they be “create[d] and maintain[ed]” 
and “review[ed]” “at reasonable intervals” to determine whether continued nondisclosure is 
warranted.  G.L. c. 30A, § 22(a), (g)(1).  See also OML 2024-158;4 OML 2024-29; Swansea 
Board of Selectmen v. Attorney General, Civil Action No. 2173CV00906 (Bristol Sup. Ct. 
December 27, 2023).  Executive session minutes may be withheld for as long as publication may 
defeat the lawful purposes of the executive session, but no longer.  G.L. c. 30A, § 22(f).  
Although “reasonable interval” is not defined by the Law, our office has found a quarterly 
review or a review every six months to be appropriate.  See OML 2020-101; OML 2019-45; 
OML 2017-104.   A public body must announce the result of its review at the next meeting 
following its review.  See G.L. c. 30A, § 22(g)(1); OML 2024-168; OML 2015-94; OML 2013-
56.  A public body’s obligation to review executive session minutes for possible release is 
ongoing.  If a public body reviews executive session minutes and determines that the purpose for 
the executive session remains such that the minutes should continue to be withheld from the 
public, it must continue to review those same executive session minutes at reasonable intervals 
until it determines that the reason for secrecy has expired.  See OML 2023-242; OML 2022- 155; 
OML 2021-129. 
 
The complaint alleges, and the Board concedes, that be tween November 6, 2019, and 
May 6, 2024, the Board did not review executive session minutes to determine whether 
continued nondisclosure was warranted.  The Board asserts that the complaint is not timely as to 
this allegation, however, noting that an Open Meeting Law complaint must be filed within 30 
days of the alleged violation or of the date that the alleged violation reasonably could have been 
discovered.  See G.L. c. 30A, § 23(b); 940 CMR 29.05(3).   We find the complaint timely.  The 
Open Meeting Law imposes a continuing obligation on public bodies to review executive session 
minutes at reasonable intervals.  See G.L. c. 30A, §§ 22 (a) and (g)(1); OML 2020-101; OML

The procedural requirements surrounding executive session, including periodic review of 
executive session minutes, are critical to advancing the transparency aims of the Open Meeting 
Law, because the public has no other way to know what happened at these closed-door sessions. 
See OML 2020-109; OML 2020-15; OML 2019-133.  Similarly, announcing the outcome of the 
review of executive session minutes advances the goal of transparency by informing the public 
that such records are available.  See OML 2024-67; OML 2012-2.  For approximately four and a 
half years, the Board failed to review executive session minutes to determine whether continued 
nondisclosure was warranted.  In failing to perform a review at reasonable intervals, the Board 
violated the Open Meeting Law. 
 
II. The Board violated the Open Meeting Law when it failed to review executive 
session minutes for release in response to a request for executive session minutes. 
 
In addition to the obligation to review executive session minutes at reasonable intervals 
to determine whether continued nondisclosure is warranted, a public body also has certain 
obligations upon receipt of a request for executive session minutes.  Upon receipt of a request for 
executive session minutes, a public body must respond to the requestor within ten days.  G.L. c. 
30A, § 22(g)(2).  If the public body has recently performed a review of the requested executive 
session minutes, then it should provide any minutes it determined should be released and explain 
that the remaining minutes continue to be withheld.  See OML 2024-168; OML 2013-105; OML 
2013-99.  If, however, at the time of a request the public body has not recently conducted the 
periodic review of its executive session minutes, then the body must review and release the non-
exempt minutes, or any portion thereof, not later than the body’s next meeting or 30 days, 
whichever occurs first.  G.L. c. 30A, § 22(g)(2).

CONCLUSION 
 
For the reasons stated above, we find that the Board violated the Open Meeting Law by 
failing to review executive session minutes at reasonable intervals and by failing to review and 
release requested executive session minutes at the next meeting following the complainant’s 
request.  We understand that the Board subsequently reviewed minutes of executive sessions 
held in 2023 and 2024 in response to the request.  We now order the Board to review minutes of 
all executive sessions held from 2019 to present within 90 days of issuance of this letter to 
determine if continued non-disclosure is warranted, to the extent that it has not done so within 
the last three months, consistent with the guidance provided above.  Additionally, we order the 
Board’s immediate and future compliance with the Open Meeting Law and caution the Board 
that a determination by our office of a similar violation in the future may be considered evidence 
of intent to violate the Open Meeting Law.   
 
We now consider the complaint addressed by this determination to be resolved.  This 
determination does not address any other complaints that may be pending with our office or the 
Board.  Please feel free to contact the Division at (617) 963-2540 if you have any questions.
