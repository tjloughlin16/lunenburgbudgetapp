RE: Open Meeting Law Complaint 
Dear Mr. Cayer: 
This office received a complaint from Christopher Loreti, dated April 23, 2013, alleging 
that the Arlington Redevelopment Board (the "Board") violated the Open Meeting Law, G.L. c. 
3OA, §§ 18-25. Specifically, the complaint alleges that the Board refused to release its executive 
session minutes from 12 meetings from 2011 where the purpose for confidentiality no longer 
requires non-disclosure. Additionally, the complaint alleges that the Board failed to create and 
approve in a timely manner the minutes of its open meetings held on November 21, 2011; 
December 5, 2011; and December 12, 2011. The complaint also alleges that the Board failed to 
approve in a timely manner the executive session minutes from its May 2, 2011; June 2, 2011; 
June 13, 2011; and July 7, 2011 meetings. Finally, the complaint alleges that the Board fails to 
review its executive session minutes on regular basis. The complaint was filed with the Board 
on or about February 18, 2013. The Board responded to the complaint by letter dated February 
26, 2013. 
In reaching a determination, we reviewed the February 18, 2013 complaint filed with the 
Board; the Board's February 26, 2013 response; and the April 23, 2013 complaint filed with our 
office. Additionally, we reviewed a June 12, 2013 letter from the Board sent to our office in 
response to our request for documents. Following our review, we find that the Board violated 
the Open Meeting Law by failing to provide minutes in response to a request within the time 
period required by law. However, the Board took appropriate remedial action in response to the 
complaint by reviewing and publicly releasing the requested meeting minutes. We therefore 
order no additional relief.

FACTS 
On or about January 13, 2013, Mr. Loreti sent Board Chair Bruce Fitzsimmons a letter 
requesting copies of the executive session minutes for the following 2011 meetings: April 25, 
June 6, June 13, June 20, June 27, July 26, August 15, September 6, September 26, October 17, 
and November 7. Additionally, Mr. Loreti requested copies of the executive session minutes for 
three meetings that he believed the Board may have held in 2011: November 21, December 5, 
and December 12. In response to the request. Chair Fitzsimmons wrote, in a January 23, 2013 
letter, that the Board "has begun to review Executive Session minutes and recently released 
several sets of minutes." Chair Fitzsimmons noted that the Board did not meet in executive 
session on April 25, 2011 or December 5, 2011. He also wrote that the June 13, 2011 executive 
session minutes were posted to the Town's website1 on January 10, 2013. Finally, Chair 
Fitzsimmons wrote that: 
The Board continues to exercise oversight approval over the [Symmes' redevelopment 
project], including the redevelopers' marketing plans. Since the sales and rentals that 
may result under the marketing plans may trigger certain additional payments to the 
Town under the Land Disposition Agreement, the original purpose of the executive 
session meeting at which Mr. Jacobs presented his report remains vital to the negotiating 
position of the Board, and the Board's position would be compromised if those executive 
session minutes or Mr. Jacob's [sic] report were to be disclosed at the present time. The 
Board will continue to evaluate those minutes and Mr. Jacobs' report to determine 
whether it may be released at a later time. 
Mr. Loreti filed his Open Meeting complaint with the Board on or about February 18, 
2013. In response to the complaint, Chair Fitzsimmons wrote, in a February 26, 2013 letter, that 
the following 2011 executive sessions were under review, and expected to be publicly released 
on March 11, 2013: April 25, June 6, June 20, July 26, August 15, September 6, September 26, 
October 17, November 7, November 21, and December 12. These minutes were published to the 
Town's website on or about March 7, 2013, except for the October 17, 2011 minutes which were 
published on or about April 25, 2013. The Board's November 21, 2011; December 5, 2011; and 
December 12, 2011 open session minutes were approved during the Board's February 25, 2013 
meetings, and posted to the Board's website on February 26, 2013. The Board's May 2, 2011; 
June 2, 2011; June 13, 2011; and July 7, 2011 executive session minutes were approved on 
January 7, 2013 and posted to the Board's website on January 10, 2013. The Board's June 27, 
2011 minutes were approved on January 7, 2013 and posted to the Board's website on February 
25,2013. 
DISCUSSION 
The Open Meeting Law requires a public body to create and maintain accurate minutes of 
all meetings, including executive sessions. G.L. c. 30A, § 22(a). Minutes of all open sessions 
shall be created and approved in a timely manner.2 G.L. c. 30A, § 22(c). Furthermore, upon

request by any person to inspect or copy the minutes of an executive session or any portion 
thereof, the body shall respond to the request within 10 days following receipt and shall release 
any such minutes not covered by an exemption; provided, however, that if the body has not 
performed a review, the public body shall perform the review and release the non-exempt 
minutes, or any portion thereof, not later than the body's next meeting or 30 days, whichever 
first occurs. G.L. c. 30A, § 22(g)(2); OML 2012-106; OML 2012-91; OML 2012-86.3 
The Board failed to provide or review minutes in response to Mr. Loreti's request within 
the time periods described above, and took more than a year and a half to approve the minutes of 
its executive sessions held on May 2, 2011; June 2, 2011; June 13, 2011; June 27, 2011; and July 
7, 2011 and open sessions held on November 21, 2011 and December 12, 2011. However, the 
Board worked diligently following Mr. Loreti's request to approve, review and release its 
minutes, publishing the last of the requested sets on April 25, 2013. We therefore find that the 
Board took appropriate remedial action to resolve the complaint. We remind the Board of its 
obligation to provide non-exempt executive session minutes within 10 days upon request, or if a 
review has not yet been undertaken, then not later than the body's next meeting or 30 days, 
whichever first occurs. G.L. c. 30A, §§ 22(c), (g)(2). 
CONCLUSION 
We find that the Board violated the Open Meeting Law by failing to provide minutes in 
response to a request within the time period required by law. However, the Board took 
appropriate remedial action in response to the complaint by approving, reviewing and publicly 
releasing the requested meeting minutes. We therefore order no additional relief. 
We now consider this matter closed. If you have any questions regarding this letter,
