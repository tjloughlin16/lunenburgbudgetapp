This office received a complaint from Paul Haverty on August 29, 2018, alleging that 
the Boxborough Planning Board (the “Board”) violated the Open Meeting Law, G.L. c. 30A, 
§§ 18-25. The complaint was originally filed with the Board on or about April 10, 2018, and 
you responded to the complaint, on behalf of the Board, in a letter dated August 29, 2018. In 
his complaint, Attorney Haverty alleges that the Board held an improper executive session 
during its March 19, 2018 meeting.1
Following our review, we find that the Board violated the Open Meeting Law in the 
manner alleged. In reaching a determination, we reviewed the original complaint, the Board’s 
response to the complaint, and the complaint filed with our office requesting further review. 
We also reviewed the minutes of the Board’s January 8, 2018 meeting, as well as the notice 
and open and executive session minutes of the Board’s March 19, 2018 meeting.

Massachusetts Avenue that was submitted to the Board on December 23, 2016. The Board 
had previously held numerous hearings on the application. Following the continued hearing 
on the project, the Board voted to convene an executive session “to discuss strategy with

DISCUSSION
The Open Meeting Law requires that all meetings of a public body be conducted in an 
open session, with some exceptions. G.L. c. 30A, §§ 20(a), 21(a). Public bodies may enter a 
closed, executive session for any of ten enumerated purposes, provided that the chair of the 
public body first announces in open session the purpose for the executive session, “stating all 
subjects that may be revealed without compromising the purpose for which the executive 
session was called.” G.L. c. 30A, §§ 21(a), 21(b)(3); see also District Attorney for the N.
Dist. v. Sch. Comm, of Wayland. 455 Mass. 561, 567 (2009) (“[a] precise statement of the 
reason for convening in executive session is necessary ... because that is the only notification 
given the public that a [public body] would conduct business in private, and the only way the 
public would know if the reason for doing so was proper or improper”).
One permissible reason to convene in executive session is “to discuss strategy with 
respect to collective bargaining or litigation if an open meeting may have a detrimental effect 
on the bargaining or litigating position of the public body and the chair so declares.” G.L. c. 
30A, § 21(a)(3) (“Purpose 3”). This purpose offers the narrow opportunity to discuss strategy 
with respect to litigation that is pending or clearly and imminently threatened or otherwise 
demonstrably likely; the mere possibility of litigation is not sufficient to invoke Purpose 3.
See Doherty v. School Committee of Boston, 386 Mass. 643, 648 (1982); Ferryman v. School 
Committee of Boston. 17 Mass. App. Ct. 346, 352 (1983); OML 2012-05.3 When convening 
in executive session pursuant to Purpose 3, a public body should identify the litigation matter 
to be discussed, if doing so will not compromise the lawful purpose for secrecy. See OML 
2016-12; OML 2013-97. While we generally defer to a public body’s assessment of whether 
the inclusion of such information would compromise the purpose for the executive session, a

The complaint alleges that, during its March 19, 2018 meeting, the Board improperly 
convened in executive session pursuant to Purpose 3 regarding the site plan application for 
700, 750, and 800 Massachusetts Avenue even though no litigation was pending or 
imminently threatened concerning that matter. Inherent in any public body’s review of this 
type of application is a potential appeal by a disappointed applicant. We decline to read 
Purpose 3 so broadly as to include a public body’s discussion of a matter pending before it 
anytime it faces the possibility of litigation as a result of its decision on that matter. See OML 
2015-10. While we credit the Board’s position that an appeal of its decision was imminently 
threatened in this instance, we conclude that the executive session discussion did not fit within 
the parameters of Purpose 3 as it primarily concerned the merits of the application rather than 
strategy with respect to any litigative consequences of its decision. See OML 2018-101;
OML 2017-9; OML 2015-6; OML 2012-76. Accordingly, we find that the Board violated the 
Open Meeting Law by convening in executive session under Purpose 3 for this discussion.
We order the Board to publicly release its March 19, 2018 executive session minutes. The 
Board may not redact or withhold from the public any portion of the minutes. See G.L. c.
30A, § 22(f) (“The minutes of any executive session ... may be withheld from disclosure to 
the public in their entirety ... as long as publication may defeat the lawful purposes of the 
executive session, but no longer; provided, however, that the executive session was held in 
compliance with section 21”); OML 2014-42.
CONCLUSION
For the reasons stated above, we find that the Board violated the Open Meeting Law 
by convening in executive session for an improper purpose. We order the Board’s immediate 
and future compliance with the law’s requirements, and we caution that similar future 
violations could be considered evidence of intent to violate the law. Additionally, we order 
the Board to publicly release its March 19, 2018 executive session meeting minutes in 
accordance with the above guidance.
We now consider the complaint addressed by this determination to be resolved. This 
determination does not address any other complaints that may be pending with our office or 
the Board. Please feel free to contact our office at (617) 963-2540 if you have 
any questions regarding this letter.
