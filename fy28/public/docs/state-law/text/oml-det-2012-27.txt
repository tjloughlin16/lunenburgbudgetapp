This office reviewed a complaint filed by the law firm Prince, Lobel, Glovsky & Tye 
LLP, on behalf of its client, the Salem News, dated April 15, 2011, alleging that the Peabody 
School Committee (the "Committee") violated the Open Meeting Law, G.L. c. 30A, §§ 18-25. 
The complaint states that "Staff writer Jesse Roman of the Salem News sent a written request on 
January 11, 2011, to Peabody School Superintendent C. Milton Burnett, seeking unredacted 
copies of all minutes from executive sessions held by the town's school committee during fiscal 
year 2009 that were not previously provided to the newspaper pursuant to an earlier request." 
Superintendent Burnett replied to that request by letter on January 19, 2011, stating that the 
minutes "will remain [u]nreleased due to pending and continuing legal concerns." The 
complaint alleges that the "school committee refuses to specify what 'pending and continuing

determination that the executive session purpose has expired. The complaint was originally filed 
with the Committee on February 17, 2011. The Committee responded to the complaint by letter 
dated March 1, 2011. 
In reaching our determination, we reviewed the February 17, 2011 complaint, the 
Committee's March 1, 2011 response, and the April 15, 2011 complaint. We reviewed the 
unredacted, unreleased executive session minutes, provided to our office by the Committee, for 
the September 22, 2009, October 13, 2009, October 27, 2009, November 24, 2009, December 8,

FACTS 
On January 11, 2011, Salem News Staff Writer Jesse Roman sent the Committee a 
written request for unredacted copies of all minutes from the Committee's executive sessions 
held during fiscal year 2009. The Committee denied this request in a letter dated January 19, 
2011, stating that the requested executive session minutes "will remain unreleased due to 
pending and continuing legal concerns." On February 17, 2011, the Salem News filed a 
complaint with the Committee challenging its reasons for withholding the minutes. 
Based upon our review of ten sets of the Committee's unreleased executive session 
minutes from 2009 and 2010, and Attorney Cocuzzo's January 6, 2012 email providing 
justifications for withholding the minutes from eight of those meetings, we understand the facts 
to be as follows. Because the executive session minutes have not yet been publicly released, we 
do not recount their full content in detail here. 
On October 27, 2009, the Committee convened an executive session prior to its regular 
meeting. The minutes from the meeting do not reflect what purpose was given for entering 
executive session. The minutes reflect discussion of a litigation matter, a collective bargaining 
matter, and several grievance matters. Attorney Cocuzzo's January 6, 2012 email addresses an 
8:00 p.m. executive session on this date, but does not address the earlier executive session that 
was convened at 5:45 p.m. 
On February 23, 2010, the Committee convened an executive session and discussed 
several grievance matters. The minutes from the meeting do not reflect what purpose was given 
for entering executive session. According to Attorney Cocuzzo's January 6, 2012 email, the 
grievances were part of an ongoing legal matter that has not been resolved. 
The Committee convened executive sessions on March 9 and March 23, 2010. The 
minutes do not reflect what purpose was given for either executive session. The committee 
discussed several grievance matters during these sessions. According to Attorney Cocuzzo's 
January 6, 2012 email, the grievances were part of an ongoing legal matter that has not yet been

DISCUSSION 
There are ten purposes for which a public body may enter executive, or closed, session. 
G.L. c. 30A, § 21(a). Executive Session Purpose 2 allows a public body to enter executive 
session to "conduct strategy sessions in preparation for negotiations with nonunion personnel or 
to conduct collective bargaining sessions or contract negotiations with nonunion personnel." 
G.L. c. 30A, § 21(a)(2). Executive session Purpose 3 allows a public body to enter executive 
session to "discuss strategy with respect to collective bargaining or litigation if an open meeting 
may have a detrimental effect on the bargaining or litigating position of the public body and the 
chair so declares." G.L. c. 30A, § 21(a)(3). Grievance matters may also be discussed under 
Purpose 2 or Purpose 3 because they are part of the collective bargaining process. See Ghiglione 
v. School Committee of Southbridge, 376 Mass. 70, 73 (1978) ("[c]ollective bargaining sessions 
encompass not only the negotiations leading to the agreement, but also the resolution of 
grievances pursuant to the collective bargaining agreement"). 
The minutes of an executive session "may be withheld from disclosure to the public in 
their entirety under subclause (a) of clause Twenty-sixth of section 7 of chapter 4, as long as 
publication may defeat the lawful purposes of the executive session, but no longer." G.L. c. 
30A, § 22(f). Public bodies have discretion to enter executive session and to determine when the 
purpose for which the public body has entered executive session has concluded. See G.L. c. 
30A, § 21(a); Foudy v. Amherst-Pelham Regional School Committee, 402 Mass. 179, 182 
(1988). However, the public body also bears the burden of justifying continued nondisclosure of

executive session minutes. Foudy, 402 Mass. at 184. While the Open Meeting Law requires a 
public body to release executive session minutes once the executive session purpose has expired, 
a public body may still redact or withhold minutes subject to the Public Records Law, G.L. c. 4 § 
7, or where discussions may be protected by the attorney/client privilege. See G. L. c. 30A, § 
22(f). 
The Open Meeting Law was revised as part of the Ethics Reform Act of 2009, and the 
new law took effect on July 1, 2010. Prior to the date, the law required a public body to state the 
purpose for an executive session, though there was no clear requirement that the public body 
include that statement of purpose in its minutes. Because the Committee did not list the purpose 
for executive session in any of the pre-July 1, 2010 minutes that we reviewed, we inferred the 
purpose based on our review of the executive sessions minutes. 
Despite several requests from our office, the Committee failed to provide us with 
information about the status of many of the specific grievances and litigation matters discussed 
in the minutes at issue.1  From the information that was provided, however, it is apparent that the 
executive session purpose for several of the meetings at issue has expired, as described below. 
October 27, 2009 Executive Session 
The Committee convened an executive session on October 27, 2009. Attorney Cocuzzo's 
January 6, 2012 email does not address this executive session. From our review, the minutes 
include discussion of a litigation matter, a collective bargaining matter, and several grievance 
matters. These matters are appropriate for executive session under executive session Purposes 2 
and 3. The Committee has not provided us with any information justifying continued non-
disclosure of the minutes from this executive session, therefore we find that the executive session 
purposes have expired. 
February 23, 2010 Executive Session 
The Committee convened an executive session on February 23, 2010 and discussed 
several grievance matters. According to Attorney Cocuzzo's January 6, 2012 email, the 
grievances are part of an ongoing legal matter that has not yet been resolved. Although the 
minutes failed to list which executive session purpose the Committee was entering executive 
session under, it appears from the nature of the discussion reflected in the minutes that the 
Committee intended to enter executive session under Purpose 3. We find that the executive 
session was appropriate under Purpose 3 as part of the collective bargaining process, and agree 
that this purpose has not yet expired because the grievance matter has not yet been resolved.

March 9, 2010 and March 23, 2010 Executive Sessions 
The Committee convened executive sessions on March 9 and March 23, 2010. The 
committee discussed several grievance matters during these sessions. According to Attorney 
Cocuzzo's January 6, 2012 email, the grievances are part of an ongoing legal matter that has not 
yet been resolved. Attorney Cocuzzo also states that portions of the executive session minutes 
cover matters that have since been resolved. Although no purpose was listed in the minutes, we 
find that the executive session was appropriate under Purpose 3 because of the nature of the 
grievance procedure as part of the ongoing collective bargaining process, and agree that, with 
regard to the portions of the discussion concerning ongoing grievance matters, this purpose has 
not yet expired. It has, however, expired with regard to the matters that Attorney Cocuzzo stated 
have been resolved. 
May 11, 2010 Executive Session 
The Committee convened an executive session on May 11, 2010. The committee 
discussed several grievance and litigation matters, as well as a matter related to collective 
bargaining. Because the Committee has not provided us with any information justifying 
continued non-disclosure of the minutes from this executive session, we find that the executive 
session purposes have expired. 
June 8, 2010 Executive Session 
The Committee convened an executive session on June 8, 2010. The committee 
discussed strategy with respect to litigation, which was appropriate under executive session 
Purpose 3. The Committee also discussed a matter with respect to collective bargaining, and a 
matter with respect to general non-union personnel contracts. This executive session was not 
addressed in Attorney Cocuzzo's January 6, 2012 email. The Committee has not provided us 
with any information justifying continued non-disclosure of the minutes from this executive 
session, therefore we find that the executive session purpose has expired. 
September 22, 2009, October 13, 2009, November 24, 2009, 
and December 8, 2009 Executive Sessions  
The minutes from these executive sessions involve discussion of a specific litigation 
matter. In Attorney Cocuzzo's January 6, 2012 email, he states that the executive session 
minutes include discussion of an ongoing legal concern. However, Attorney Cocuzzo also states 
that he will advise the Committee that if the concern has been resolved, then the minutes should 
be released. In light of that pending review, we decline to take a position on whether or not 
continued non-disclosure is warranted. 
CONCLUSION 
We order the Committee to undertake a fresh review of all the executive session minutes
