This office received a complaint from Robert Strayton, dated September 12, alleging that 
the Edgartown Planning Board (the "Board") violated the Open Meeting Law, G.L. c. 3OA, §§ 
18-25.1 The complaint was originally filed with the Board on August 9, and the Board 
responded by letter dated August 24. In his complaint, Mr. Strayton alleges that the Board did 
not allow public comment during a hearing.2 
Following our review, we resolve this matter by informal action in accordance with 940 
CMR 29.07(2)(a), and we find that the Board did not violate the Open Meeting Law. The 
complaint concerns a public hearing that took place on July 11. During this hearing, the Board 
discussed an application for a special permit to erect a cellular tower. The Board chair permitted 
public comment from one member of the audience, but the Chair did not permit other individuals 
to speak. The Open Meeting Law states that "no person shall address a meeting of a public body 
without permission of the chair, and all persons shall, at the request of the chair, be silent." G.L. 
c. 3 OA, § 20(f). The Open Meeting Law therefore gives discretion to the chair of a public body 
to determine who may speak or otherwise participate at a meeting. See OML 2014-23; OML 
2013-64; OML 2012-59.3 Because the Open Meeting Law gives the chair of a public body 
discretion over who may speak or otherwise participate at a meeting, we find no violation with
