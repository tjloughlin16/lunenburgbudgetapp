This office received a complaint from Christopher Kelleher on June 13,1 alleging that the 
Brookfield Board of Health (the “Board”) violated the Open Meeting Law, G.L. c. 30A, §§ 18-
25. The complaint was originally filed with the Board on or about April 17, and you responded
on behalf of the Board by letter dated April 24. The complaint alleges that the Board “refuse[d]
to provide approved meeting minutes from its February 26, 2025 meeting following two formal
written requests.”
Foll
owing review, we resolve this complaint by informal action, in accordance with 940 
CMR 29.07(2)(a).2 We find, and the Board acknowledges, that the Board violated the Open 
Meeting Law by failing to timely respond to a request for meeting minutes. The Open Meeting 
Law requires that a “public body shall create and maintain accurate minutes of all meetings . . . 
.” G.L. c. 30A, § 22(a). “The minutes of an open session, if they exist and whether approved or 
in draft form, shall be made available upon request by any person within 10 days.” G.L. c. 30A, 
§ 22(c). A request for meeting minutes that is directed to a public body will fall under the Open
Meeting Law and requires a response in 10 calendar days. See G.L. c. 30A, §§ 22(c), (g); OML
2019-163. On the other hand, a request for minutes that is directed to a clerk or other municipal
employee as Records Access Officer will likely fall under the Public Records Law and requires a
response in 10 business days. See G.L. c. 66, § 10; OML 2021-6; OML 2019-126; OML 2019-
68.

On March 27, and again on April 8, the Complainant sent an email to the Chair of the 
Board requesting the minutes of the Board’s February 26 meeting. On April 8, 12 calendar days 
after the initial request was received, the Board posted the requested meeting minutes online and 
the Town Administrator responded to the Complainant notifying him that the requested minutes 
had been posted.  
 
For the reasons stated above, we find that the Board violated the OML by failing to 
respond to a request for meeting minutes within 10 calendar days. We order the Board’s 
immediate and future compliance with the Open Meeting Law. The Board has represented that it 
now understands its obligation to respond to a request for meeting minutes made pursuant to the 
Open Meeting Law in 10 calendar days.   
We now consider the complaint addressed by this determination to be resolved. This 
determination does not address any other complaints that may be pending with our office or the 
Board. Please feel free to contact the Division at (617) 963-2540 if you have any questions.
