RE: Open Meeting Law Complaint 
Dear Attorney Howayeck; 
This office received a complaint from Patrick Higgins on October 3, alleging that the Fall 
River Commission on Disability (the "Commission") violated the Open Meeting Law, G.L. c. 
30A, §§ 18-25.1 The complaint alleges that the Commission failed to follow proper procedure to 
enter into executive session during its August 14 meeting and that the Chair made a personal 
attack against Mr. Higgins without providing Mr. Higgins with advance notice. The complaint 
was filed with the Commission on September 5. The Commission responded through counsel by 
letter dated September 18. 
Following our review, we resolve this complaint by informal action in accordance with 
940 CMR 29.07(2)(a), and find that the Commission violated the Open Meeting Law by failing 
to follow the required procedures for entering into executive session during its August 14 
meeting. However, we find that the Commission did not violate the Open Meeting Law by 
referring to Mr. Higgins during that same meeting. 
During the Commission's August 14 meeting, the Chair entertained a motion to go into 
executive session to discuss a complaint. Although a Board member seconded the motion, there 
was no vote, and the Chair did not state whether the Commission would reconvene in open 
session following the executive session. This violated the law. See G.L. c. 3OA, § 21(b); OML 
2011-43; OML 2011-39.2 In response to the complaint, the Commission pledged to follow the 
proper protocol for future executive sessions. We remind the Commission that before entering

into executive session, the Commission must convene in open session; the Chair must state the 
reasons for the executive session, stating all subjects that may be revealed without compromising 
the purpose for which the executive session was called; the Chair must state whether the 
Commission will reconvene in open session; and the Commission must take a roll call vote. See 
G.L. c. 30A, § 21(b); OML 2011-43; OML 2011-39. Furthermore, we order the Commission's 
immediate and future compliance with the Open Meeting Law, and caution that a future similar 
violation may be considered evidence of an intentional violation of the Law. 
With respect to Mr. Higgins' second allegation, we find no violation of the Law. During 
the open session portion of the meeting, the Chair announced that Mr. Higgins had filed a 
separate Open Meeting Law complaint and provided some background to the complaint. A 
public body is not required to provide an individual with notice that he will be discussed during a 
meeting, unless the discussion is part of an executive session under G.L. c. 3OA, § 21(a)(1). See 
OML 2013-41; OML 2013-24; OML 2011-41. Because this discussion took place entirely in 
open session, we find that the Commission did not violate the Law by discussing Mr. Higgins' 
complaint without providing him with individual notice of this discussion.
