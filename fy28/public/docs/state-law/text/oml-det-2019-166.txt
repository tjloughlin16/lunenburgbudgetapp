RE: Open Meeting Law Complaint
Dear Ms. Gill:
This office received a complaint from Michael Capizzi on November 8, 2019, alleging 
that the Concord Historical Commission (the “Commission”) violated the Open Meeting Law, 
G.L. c. 30A, §§ 18-25. The complaint was originally filed with the Commission on October 9, 
2019; and you responded, on behalf of the Commission, by letter dated October 23, 2019. The 
complaint alleges that the Chair of the Commission made insulting remarks toward Mr. Capizzi 
and did not allow an attendant to ask a question during the Commission’s September 16, 2019, 
meeting.
We resolve this matter by informal action, in accordance with 940 CMR 29.07(2)(a), 
after reviewing the original complaint, the Commission’s response to the complaint, the 
complaint filed with our office requesting further review, and the notice and open session 
minutes of the Commission’s September 16, 2019, meeting. We find that the Commission did 
not violate the Open Meeting Law as alleged.
The Open Meeting Law does not require that a public body allow public participation, 
but rather provides that “[n]o person shall address a meeting of a public body without permission 
of the chair, and all persons shall, at the request of the chair, be silent.” G.L. c. 30A § 20(f). The 
law permits the Chair of the Commission to decide who can speak at a meeting. See OML 2017- 
189; OML 2014-23; OML 2012-78.1 Thus, the allegation that the Chair did not allow a question

to be asked during the meeting would not constitute a violation of the Open Meeting Law. We 
do, however, encourage public bodies to allow for as much public participation as time and 
circumstances permit. See OML 2015-12; OML 2014-129; OML 2012-59.
An allegation of unprofessional conduct or incivility towards a member of the 
Commission or the public does not constitute a violation of the Open Meeting Law. See OML 
Declination 6-28-16 (Kingston Board of Selectmen). Thus, we decline to review the allegation 
that a member of the Commission made insulting remarks towards Mr. Capizzi. Nevertheless, 
we encourage civility by all parties during meetings of public bodies.
For the reasons stated above, we find that the Commission did not violate the Open 
Meeting Law. We now consider the complaint addressed by this determination to be resolved. 
This letter does not address any other complaints that may be pending with our office or the 
Commission. Please feel free to contact the Division at (617) 963-2540 if you have any 
questions regarding this determination.
