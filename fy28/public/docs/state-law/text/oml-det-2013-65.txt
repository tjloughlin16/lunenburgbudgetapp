RE: Open Meeting Law Complaint 
Dear Attorney Goldberg: 
This office received an Open Meeting Law complaint from Bernard Brahm dated July 3, 
2012, alleging that the Provincetown Board of Selectmen (the "Board") violated the Open 
Meeting Law, G.L. c. 30A, §§ 18-25. The complaint specifically alleges that the Board held an 
executive session on November 14, 2011 without listing the executive session in the notice, and 
that the minutes of the open session do not state the specific topic that the Board discussed in 
executive session. The complaint was originally filed with the Board on April 30, 2012. The 
Board responded by letter, through counsel, on May 17, 2012. 
After reviewing the complaint and the Board's response, we have decided to resolve this 
complaint by informal action,  in accordance with 940 CMR 29.07(2)(a). We find that the 
Board violated the Open Meeting Law by failing to include the specific executive session 
purpose in the notice for its November 14, 2011 executive session. See G.L. c. 30A, § 20(b); 
OML 2011-56.1  The Board also failed to publicly state prior to the executive session, and failed 
to include in the open session minutes, all of the executive sessions purposes that the Board 
relied upon. G.L. c. 30A, § 21(b)(3); OML 2011-44. The Board acknowledged its error in its 
May 17, 2012 response to the complaint, and we do not find that the violation was intentional. 
The Board has committed to "review the process used for developing meeting notices so as to 
ensure that such notices accurately list, in detail, all topics reasonably anticipated by the Chair. 
Further, the Board and its staff will review the process for voting to enter executive session and 
for accurately recording the same in meeting minutes." We are satisfied that the Board has taken

We order the Board's immediate and future compliance with the Open Meeting Law, and 
caution that future similar violations may be considered evidence of intent to violate the Law. 
Additionally, we order the Board to amend the minutes of its November 14, 2011 open meeting 
to reflect all executive session purposes relied upon, and topics discussed by the Board during 
that meeting. This should occur within thirty (30) days of receipt of this letter. The minutes of 
the Board's November 14, 2011 executive session may be withheld from disclosure to the public 
in their entirety, as long as publication may defeat the lawful purposes of the executive session, 
but no longer. See G.L. c. 30A, § 22(f). 
We appreciate the patience and cooperation of the parties during this investigation, and 
now consider this matter closed. Please contact our office at (617) 963-2540 if you have any 
questions regarding this letter.

This determination was issued pursuant to G.L. c. 30A, § 23(c). A public body or any 
member of a body aggrieved by this order may obtain judicial review through an action 
filed in Superior Court pursuant to G.L. c. 30A, § 23(d). The complaint must be filed in 
Superior Court within twenty-one days of receipt of this order. 
We note that in his July 3, 2012 complaint to our office, Mr. Brahm argued that the discussion held on November 
14, 2011 was not appropriate for executive session. He writes that "not being a litigant in the case Brahm v. HDC, 
the Board of Selectmen do not have a position to protect." We decline to review this allegation, as it was not raised 
in the original complaint filed with the Board. However, we note that our office has said in the past that a Board of 
Selectmen may meet in executive session to discuss strategies with respect to litigation where an entity of the town 
is a party to that litigation. See G.L. c. 30A, § 21(a)(3); OML 9-29-10 (Wayland Board of Selectmen).
