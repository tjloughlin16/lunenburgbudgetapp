RE: Open Meeting Law Complaint 
Dear Attorney Macy: 
This office received a complaint from CJ Ferry on January 20, 2016, alleging that 
the Fall River Licensing Board (the Board) violated the Open Meeting Law, G.L. c. 30A, 
§§ 18-25. The complaint was originally filed with the Board on or about December 26, 
2015, and you responded, on behalf of the Board, by letter dated January 13, 2016. In his 
complaint, Mr. Ferry alleges that the notice for the Board's December 16, 2015 meeting 
failed to sufficiently identify the licenses that the Board would consider for renewal. 
Following our review, we find that the Board violated the Open Meeting Law as 
alleged. In reaching this determination, we reviewed the original complaint, the Board's 
response to the complaint, and the complaint filed with our office requesting further 
review. We also reviewed the notice and minutes of the Board's December 16, 2015 
meeting. 
We find the facts as follows. On December 10, 2015, the Board properly posted 
notice of a meeting for December 16, 2015. The Board intended to consider a variety of 
topics relating to new and existing liquor licenses, including annual renewal of existing 
licenses. The discussion topics listed on this notice specifically identified the type of 
liquor license, the name of the applicant and the name and address of the establishment. 
For example, one of the topics listed as follows: "3. Change of Hours: A. Fernando's 
Place- 92 Alden Street- Fernando Oliveira." The last topic listed, "Approve all submitted 
2016 renewals," fell under the heading, "Old Business/New Business."

During the meeting, the Board discussed and decided on each new/amended license 
application before turning its attention to liquor license renewals for 2016. According to 
the minutes, the Board voted to "approve all submitted 2016 Licenses." 
DISCUSSION 
The Open Meeting Law was enacted "to eliminate much of the secrecy surrounding 
deliberation and decisions on which public policy is based." Ghiglione v. School 
Committee of Southbridge, 376 Mass. 70, 72 (1978). The Open Meeting Law requires a 
public body to post notice 48 hours in advance of a meeting and include a "listing of topics 
that the chair reasonably anticipates will be discussed at the meeting." G.L. c. 30A, 
§ 20(b). Public bodies are required to list topics in a meeting notice with "sufficient 
specificity to reasonably advise the public of the issues to be discussed at the meeting." 
940 CMR 29.03(1 )(b). We generally consider a topic sufficiently specific when a 
reasonable member of the public could read the topic and understand the anticipated nature 
of the public body's discussion. See OML 2015-35.' 
We agree with the complainant that the topic in the Board's December 16, 2015 
meeting notice regarding the 2016 license renewals should have specifically listed each 
license to be renewed. While the notice properly included information about each new 
license application, the topic on license renewals, "Approve all submitted 2016 renewals," 
was insufficiently detailed because it failed to include information about each renewal 
application. See OML 2015-72; OML 2011-11. Accordingly, we find that the Board 
violated the Open Meeting Law. Additionally, we note that the meeting minutes should 
have included information on each and every license renewal that it discussed and 
approved. See G.L. c. 30A, § 22(a). We encourage the Board to update its minutes 
accordingly. 
CONCLUSION 
For the reasons stated above, we find that the Board violated the Open Meeting 
Law by failing to include sufficient detail in its December 16, 2015 meeting notice. We 
order immediate and future compliance with the Open Meeting Law, and caution that 
future similar conduct may be considered evidence of intent to violate the Law. 
We now consider the complaint addressed by this determination to be resolved.
