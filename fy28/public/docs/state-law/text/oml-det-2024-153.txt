 RE: Open Meeting Law Complaint 
Dear Chair Consilvio: 
 
This office received a complaint from Patrick Higgins on January 23, 2024, alleging that 
the Auburn Housing Authority Board of Commissioners (the “Board”) violated the Open 
Meeting Law, G.L. c. 30A, §§ 18-25.  The complaint was originally filed with the Board on or 
around December 29, 2023.
1,2  The complaint alleges that the Board failed to timely approve 
minutes for meetings held on July 6, July 10, and September 7.  The complaint also alleges that 
the Board failed to respond to a request for meeting minutes. 
 
Following our review, we find that the Board violated the Open Meeting Law as alleged.  
In reaching this determination, we reviewed the complaint, the Board’s response, and the 
Complainant’s request for further review filed with our office.  In addition, we reviewed minutes 
of the Board’s meetings held between July and December 2023 as posted on the Auburn Housing 
Authority’s website. 
 
The Open Meeting Law requires that a public body “create and maintain accurate 
minutes of all meetings, including executive sessions, setting forth the date, time and place, the

The Board held meetings on the following dates: July 6, July 10, September 7, September 
21, October 5, November 30, and December 14.  During the Board’s December 14 meeting, the 
Board approved minutes of the July 6, July 10, and September 7 meetings.  As December 14 was 
more than 30 days and more than three meetings after each of the meeting dates at issue, the 
Board failed to timely approve minutes of those three meetings, in violation of the Open Meeting 
Law.   
 
The complaint also alleges that the Board altogether failed to respond to a request for 
meeting minutes that the Complainant sent to the Board’s Chair on September 22.  The Open 
Meeting Law specifies that “the minutes of an open session, if they exist and whether approved 
or in draft form, shall be made available upon request by any person within 10 days.”  G.L. c. 
30A, § 22(c).  If such minutes do not yet exist at the time of a request, the public body is still 
required to respond to the request within 10 days with an explanation of whether the minutes do 
or do not exist in either approved or draft form.  See OML 2023-82; OML 2018-98; OML 2017-
50; OML 2016-71; OML 2015-173.  The Board has not disputed that it failed to respond to the 
Complainant’s September 22 request for minutes, and we find that the Board violated the Open 
Meeting Law. 
 
 For the reasons stated above, we find that the Board violated the Open Meeting Law by 
failing to timely approve meeting minutes and failing to respond to a request for meeting 
minutes.  We order the Board’s immediate and future compliance with the Open Meeting Law 
and caution that similar future violations could be considered evidence of intent to violate the 
law and may result in the imposition of civil penalties of up to $1,000 per violation. 
 
We now consider the complaint addressed by this determination to be resolved.  This 
determination does not address any other complaints that may be pending with our office or the 
Board.  Please feel free to contact the Division at (617) 963-2540 if you have any questions.
