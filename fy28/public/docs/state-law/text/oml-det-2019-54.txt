This office received three complaints from Richard Kennedy, First Assistant Register 
of Deeds in Norfolk County, on October 5, October 31 and November 8, alleging that the 
Norfolk County Commission (the “Commission”) violated the Open Meeting Law, G.L. c. 
30A, §§ 18-25.1 The complaints were originally filed with the Commission on or about 
August 23, September 27 and October 4, and you responded to the complaints, on behalf of 
the Commission, in separate letters dated August 27 and October 10. Mr. Kennedy alleges 
that the Commission failed to timely create and review its minutes from executive sessions 
held on May 2; July 11; and July 18; failed to conduct a review of those minutes to determine 
their confidential status in response to his request; failed to notify him that such a review had 
occurred; and failed to respond to his request for executive session minutes within 10 days.2 
Finally, Mr. Kennedy alleges that the Commission’s August 29 meeting minutes failed to 
reflect the vote of approval of those executive session minutes.3

Following our review, we find that the Commission violated the Open Meeting Law in 
the ways alleged. In reaching a determination, we reviewed the original complaints, the 
Commission’s responses to the complaint, and the complaints filed with our office requesting 
further review. We reviewed the complainant’s request for minutes, the Commission’s 
response, as well as the notices and minutes of the Commission’s meetings held on May 2; 
July 11; July 18; and August 29. Finally, we reviewed a May 10, 2019 letter from 
Commission counsel to our office.
FACTS
We find the facts as follows. On July 19, the complainant submitted a request for 
records to the Commission seeking, in part, executive session minutes of meetings held on 
May 1 and those held between June 1 and July 18.4 On July 26, the Commission responded 
with a cost estimate for providing certain records, but declined to provide any executive 
session minutes, claiming that they are “exempt pursuant to M.G.L. v. 4, § 7 (26), which 
exempts all documents which are ‘specifically or by necessary implication exempted from 
disclosure by statute.’” The Commission cited to the Open Meeting Law, G.L. c. 30A, § 22(f) 
as the statute requiring their exemption from public disclosure. The Commission did not meet 
to review the requested executive session minutes prior to sending its response, but did so in 
response to this complaint, on October 3.
During its August 29 meeting, the Commission approved executive session minutes 
from its executive sessions held on May 2, July 11, and July 18. The original minutes from 
the August 29 meeting do not reflect the Commission’s approval of the minutes. In response 
to this complaint, the Commission acknowledged that the omission was an unintentional 
oversight and thus subsequently revised the minutes to include this detail. Between May 2 
and August 29, the Commission held meetings on the following dates: May 9; May 16; May 
23; May 30; June 6; June 13; June 20; June 27; July 11; July 18; July 25; August 1; August 8; 
August 15; and August 22.
DISCUSSION
The Open Meeting Law requires that minutes of open and executive sessions be 
created and approved in a timely manner. G.L. c. 30A, § 22(c); 940 CMR 29.11. Executive 
session minutes may be withheld from disclosure to the public “as long as publication may 
defeat the lawful purposes of the executive session, but no longer.” G.L. c. 30A, § 22(f). 
When the purpose for a valid executive session has been served, the minutes and any 
documents or exhibits used at the session must be disclosed unless the attorney-client 
privilege or an exemption to the public records law applies to withhold them, in whole or in 
part, from disclosure. See id. Public bodies have an obligation to review the minutes of 
executive sessions at reasonable intervals to determine if continued non-disclosure of minutes

Upon request by any person to inspect or copy the minutes of an executive session or 
any portion thereof, the public body must respond to the request within 10 days following 
receipt and release any such minutes where publication would not defeat the lawful purposes 
of the executive session. See G.L. c. 30A, § 22(g)(2). If the body has not performed a 
review, it must do so and then release any non-exempt minutes, or portions thereof, not later 
than the body’s next meeting or in 30 days, whichever first occurs. See id.; OML 2013-180.
The complainant alleges that the Commission failed to properly respond to his July 19 
request for executive session minutes. Without first conducting a review, the Commission 
responded on July 26 and informed the complainant that the requested minutes were not 
subject to public disclosure. The Commission properly responded to the request within 10 
calendar days. See G.L. c. 30A, § 22(g)(2) (“Upon request by any person to inspect or copy 
the minutes of an executive session or any portion thereof, the public body shall respond to 
the request within 10 days following receipt[.];” OML 2017-50; OML 2016-71; OML 2015- 
173. However, the Commission violated the Open Meeting Law by failing to conduct a 
review prior to sending its response. We agree with the complainant that inherent in sending 
a response to a request for executive session minutes is a notification to the requester that the 
requisite review is scheduled and/or has occurred within 30 days of the request. See G.L. c.
30A, § 22(g)(2). We commend the Commission for taking remedial action on October 3 by 
conducting such a review.
The complainant also alleges that the Commission failed to timely approve the 
requested executive session minutes. The Open Meeting Law requires public bodies to create 
and approve meetings minutes in a timely manner. G.L. c. 30A, § 22(c); 940 CMR 29.11. 
“Timely manner” means “within the next three public body meetings or within 30 days, 
whichever is later, unless the public body can show good cause for further delay.” See 940 
CMR 29.11; OML 2018-48. Whenever possible, we recommend that minutes of a meeting be 
approved at the next meeting. See OML 2014-15; OML 2012-91. The requirement to timely 
create and approve meeting minutes applies fully to executive session minutes, even if release 
of the executive session minutes is not yet appropriate. See OML 2019-35. The Commission 
reviewed and approved the May 2; July 11 and July 18 executive session minutes on August 
29. Between May 2 and August 29, the Commission held 15 meetings. Accordingly, we find 
that the Commission failed to timely approve the minutes because they had not been approved 
within the requisite timeframe of 30 days after each meeting. We commend the Commission 
for pledging to comply with this requirement going forward.
Finally, the complainant alleges that the Commission’s August 29 meeting minutes are 
inaccurate because they fail to include the Commission’s approval of the executive session 
minutes. The Commission acknowledged the oversight and voted to approve an amended 
version on October 3 to include this detail. Here, the minutes omitted the Commission’s vote

omission violated the Open Meeting Law’s requirement that the minutes reflect all actions 
taken at the meeting. G.L. c. 30A, § 22 (a) (minutes must contain “the decisions made and the

CONCLUSION
For the reasons stated above, we find that the Commission violated the Open Meeting 
Law by failing to properly respond to a request for executive session minutes; by failing to 
timely approve its May 9; July 11 and July 18 executive session minutes; and by failing to 
create accurate minutes of its August 29 meeting. We note that the Commission had taken 
remedial action to address the violations before the complaints were filed with this office. We 
order immediate and future compliance with the law’s requirements and we caution that 
similar future violations could be considered evidence of intent to violate the law.
We now consider the complaints addressed by this determination to be resolved. This 
determination does not address any other complaints that may be pending with our office or 
the Commission. Please feel free to contact our office at (617) 963-2540 if you have any
