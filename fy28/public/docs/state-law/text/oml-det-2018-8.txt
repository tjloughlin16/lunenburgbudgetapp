RE: Open Meeting Law Complaint 
Dear Attorney Greenspan: 
This office received a complaint from Peter Manoogian, dated January, 2, 2018, alleging 
that the Saugus School Committee (the "Committee") violated the Open Meeting Law, G.L. c. 
30A, §§ 18-25. The complaint was originally filed with the Committee on September 29, 2017, 
and, after an extension of time was granted, the Committee replied by letter dated November 6, 
2017. In his complaint, Mr. Manoogian alleges that the Committee created and approved 
insufficiently detailed meeting minutes. 
Following our review, we find that the Committee violated the Open Meeting Law as 
alleged. In reaching a determination, we reviewed the original complaint, the Committee's 
response, and the request for further review filed with our office. We also reviewed the minutes 
of an executive session held by the Committee on March 16, 2017. We spoke with the 
Committee's legal counsel by telephone on January 16, 2018, and we spoke with the 
complainant by telephone on January 17, 2018.' 
We find the facts as follows. The Committee entered executive session during a meeting 
on March 16, 2017 to discuss issues relating to a collective bargaining agreement with teachers. 
The minutes indicate the date and place of the meeting, but they do not state the time. The 
minutes of this executive session show a roll call vote to allow a negotiating team to begin 
bargaining with the relevant bargaining unit. The minutes then state, "Discussion followed: 
increased teaching time, health insurance, possible mediation, extended time." The minutes then

DISCUSSION 
The Open Meeting Law requires that a public body "create and maintain accurate 
minutes of all meetings, including executive sessions, setting forth the date, time and place, 
the members present or absent, a summary of the discussions on each subject, a list of 
documents and other exhibits used at the meeting, the decisions made and the actions taken at 
each meeting, including the record of all votes." G.L. c. 30A, § 22(a), When reviewing 
minutes for compliance with the Open Meeting Law, we look for substantial compliance with 
the accuracy requirement. See OML 2013-64.3 By substantial compliance, we mean that the 
minutes should contain enough detail and accuracy so that a member of the public who did 
not attend the meeting could read the minutes and have a clear understanding of what 
occurred. See OML 2012-106. 
The complaint alleges that the March 16, 2017 executive session minutes lack sufficient 
detail. We agree. The minutes list the topics that were discussed— teaching time, insurance, 
etc.—but they do not provide a substantive summary of the Committee's conversations on these 
topics. This lack of detail violated the Open Meeting Law. G.L. c. 30A, § 22(a); OML 2017-
169; OML 2017-144. Further, the minutes do not list the time of the meeting. G.L. c. 30A, § 
22(a). We note a statement from the Committee's legal counsel in response to the complaint that 
more resources will be give committed to meeting minutes in the future, including a designated 
secretary to take the minutes. We commend these steps. 
CONCLUSION 
For the reasons stated above, we find that the Committee violated the Open Meeting Law. 
We order the Committee's immediate and future compliance with the Open Meeting Law, and 
we caution that similar future violations may be considered evidence of intent to violate the law. 
We further order the Committee, within thirty (30) days from receipt of this letter, to amend the 
March 16, 2017 executive session minutes to include the missing detail. Upon completion of 
these amendments, the Committee must notify our office of compliance in writing, pursuant to 
940 CMR 29.07(4). 
We now consider the complaint addressed by this determination to be resolved. This 
determination does not address any other complaints that may be pending with our office or the 
Committee. Please feel free to contact the Division at (617) 963 - 2540 if you have any 
questions.
