RE: Open Meeting Law Complaint 
Dear Ms. Fletcher: 
This office received two complaints filed by George Harris, dated March 24, 2014 and 
April 7, 2014, alleging that the Wayland School Committee (the "Committee") violated the Open 
Meeting Law, G.L. c. 30A, §§ 18-25. The first complaint was originally filed with the 
Committee on or about February 18, 2014, and alleges that the Committee failed to state with 
sufficient specificity the purpose for entering into executive session during its February 10, 2014 
meeting. The Committee responded by letter dated February 24, 2014. The second complaint 
was originally filed with the Committee on or about March 5, 2014, and alleges that, during its 
February 3, 2014 meeting, the Committee failed to publicly state whether it would reconvene in 
open session following its executive session. The Committee responded by letter dated March 
10, 2014. 
Following our review, we find that the Committee violated the Open Meeting Law by 
failing to provide sufficient detail in the notice for its February 10, 2014 meeting; by entering 
into executive session for an improper purpose during its February 10, 2014 meeting; and by 
failing to inform the public that it would reconvene in open session following its February 3, 
2014 executive session. In reaching this determination, we reviewed the February 18, 2014 and 
March 5, 2014 complaints filed with the Committee; the Committee's February 24, 2014 and 
March 10, 2014 responses; and the March 25, 2014 and April 7, 2014 complaints filed with our 
office. We reviewed the notices for the Committee's January 13, 2014; February 10, 2014; and 
February 24, 2014 meetings. We also reviewed the minutes of the Committee's February 10, 
2014 open session meeting and reviewed, in camera, the minutes of the Committee's February 
10, 2014 executive session.

FACTS 
During a February 3, 2014 meeting of the Committee, the Committee entered into 
executive session. Prior to entering into executive session, the Chair failed to publicly announce 
whether the Committee would reconvene in open session at the conclusion of its executive 
session.1 Following its executive session, the Committee reconvened in open session and 
announced that it was publicly releasing all of its executive session minutes from 2009, with 
some redactions. 
The Committee subsequently posted notice for a February 10, 2014 meeting. The notice 
for the February 10, 2014 meeting included the following topic: "Executive Session ... 
Discussion regarding Non-Union Personnel." The minutes record that, during the Committee's 
February 10, 2014 meeting, the Committee voted unanimously to convene in executive session 
"as permitted by M.G.L. 30A, §21(a)(2) to conduct strategy sessions in preparation for 
negotiations with non-union personnel." 
Because the minutes of the February 10, 2014 executive session have not been publicly 
released by the Committee, we do not recount their content in detail here. However, it appears 
that during the executive session the Committee discussed with the Superintendent compensation 
policy for non-union personnel generally, rather than for any specific non-union employees. 
DISCUSSION 
I. The Committee Violated the Open Meeting Law by Failing to Inform the Public that it 
would Reconvene in Open Session Following its February 3. 2014 Executive Session. 
Before entering into executive session, the chair of a public body must publicly announce 
whether the open session will reconvene at the conclusion of the executive session. G.L. c. 30A, 
§ 21(b)(4). The Chair failed to make such an announcement prior to the Committee's February 
3, 2014 executive session. In its March 10, 2014 response to the complaint, the Committee 
writes that it "forgot to indicate whether it would reconvene in open session following the 
Executive Session for adjournment purposes only or to conduct other matters." The Committee 
acknowledges that it "should have stated in its motion that it would reconvene in open session to 
possibly discuss another matter." Furthermore, in response to the complaint the Committee 
adopted a template for motions to enter into executive session in an effort to fully comply with 
this and other requirements of the Open Meeting Law. Accordingly, while we find that the 
Committee's failure to make the required statement did violate the law, we are satisfied that the 
Committee is now aware of its obligations and has pledged to comply going forward.

2. The Committee Violated the Open Meeting Law by Failing to Provide Sufficient Detail 
in the Notice for its February 10, 2013 Meeting, and by Entering into Executive Session 
at that Meeting for an Improper Purpose. 
The Open Meeting Law requires that meetings of a public body be conducted in open 
session, with some exceptions. G.L. c. 30A, §§ 20-21. Public bodies may enter a closed, 
executive session for any of the ten purposes enumerated in the Open Meeting Law, provided 
that the chair of the public body first announces in open session the purpose for the executive 
session, "stating all subjects that may be revealed without compromising the purpose for which 
the executive session was called." G.L. c. 30A, § 21(b)(3). The exceptions to the general rule 
that meetings of a public body shall be open are narrowly construed. See McRea v. Flaherty. 71 
Mass. App. Ct. 637, 641 (2008). The public body bears the burden of demonstrating 
applicability of the relevant basis for the executive session. See District Attorney for the N. Dist. 
v. School Comm. of Wayland, 455 Mass. 561, 566 (2009). 
Additionally, the Open Meeting Law requires that public bodies post notice 48 hours 
prior to a meeting and include a "listing of topics that the chair reasonably anticipates will be 
discussed at the meeting." G.L. c. 30A, § 20(b). Public bodies must list topics for discussion 
with "sufficient specificity to reasonably advise the public of the issues to be discussed at the 
meeting." 940 CMR 29.03(l)(b). We generally consider a topic description sufficiently specific 
when a reasonable member of the public could read the topic and understand the anticipated 'X nature of the public body's discussion. OML 2011 -44. Executive session topics must be 
described in the notice in as much detail as possible without compromising the purpose for 
which the executive session was called. District Attorney for N. Dist., 455 Mass. at 567 ("[a] 
precise statement of the reason for convening in executive session is necessary under the open 
meeting law because that is the only notification given to the public that the school committee 
would conduct business in private, and the only way the public would know if the reason for 
doing so was proper or improper."); see G.L. c. 30A, §§ 20(b), 21(b)(3); 940 CMR 29.03(l)(b). 
One permissible reason for executive session is to "conduct strategy sessions in 
preparation for negotiations with nonunion personnel or to conduct collective bargaining 
sessions or contract negotiations with nonunion personnel." G.L. c. 30A, § 21(a)(2) ("Purpose 
2"). When noticing an executive session under Purpose 2, the public body generally must 
identify the non-union personnel with whom the public body will be negotiating. See OML 
2013-97; OML 2011-56. 
The Committee failed to identify in the notice for its February 10, 2014 meeting the 
specific non-union personnel with whom it intended to negotiate. In its February 24, 2014 
response to the complaint, the Committee explains that the notice for its February 10, 2014 
meeting "was developed in haste and not carefully reviewed, at a time when the Committee had 
met nine times over the course of 5 weeks.. .the statement made about the purpose for entering 
into Executive Session should have included the word 'all' before the words 'non-union

personnel' and should have included the words 'except for the Superintendent' after the words 
'non-union personnel.'" 
While we commend the Committee for acknowledging its error, the response suggests the 
Committee does not entirely understand the proper application of Purpose 2. Purpose 2 allows a 
public body to enter into executive session to "conduct strategy sessions in preparation for 
negotiations with nonunion personnel or ... to conduct contract negotiations with nonunion 
personnel." G.L. c. BOA, § 21(a)(2). While a public body may discuss the contracts of specific 
non-union personnel in executive session, general compensation policy must be discussed in 
open session. OML 2014-30; OML 2013-91. Because our review of the Committee's February 
10, 2014 executive session meeting minutes revealed that the matters discussed concerned 
general compensation policy, rather than preparation for contract negotiations or negotiation with 
specific nonunion personnel, we find that this portion of the meeting should not have occurred 
behind closed doors. 
While we find that the Committee violated the Law, we do not find this to be an 
intentional violation. Lhis office has found, in prior determinations, violations by the Committee 
regarding insufficiently detailed meeting notices and improper executive sessions. OML 2014-
42; OML 2011-9. However, the notice language and executive session purpose cited by the 
Committee in the present matter are sufficiently different from those discussed previously that 
we do not find this to be an intentional violation. See id. For instance, in OML 2014-42 we 
found that the Committee violated the Open Meeting Law by listing only "Personnel Matter" 
without identifying the specific purpose for executive session. Here, the Committee identified an 
executive session purpose in its meeting notice, though it failed to describe that executive session 
purpose with sufficient detail. We caution the Committee that future similar violations may be 
considered evidence of intent to violate the law. 
CONCLUSION 
We find that the Committee violated the Open Meeting Law by failing to provide 
sufficient detail in the notice for its February 10, 2014 meeting, by entering into executive 
session for an improper purpose during its February 10, 2014 meeting, and by failing to inform 
the public that it would reconvene in open session following its February 3, 2014 executive 
session. We order the Committee's immediate and future compliance with the Open Meeting 
Law, and caution the Committee that similar future violations may be considered evidence of an 
intentional violation of the Law. 
Additionally, we order the Committee to publicly release at its next regularly scheduled 
meeting the portion of its February 10, 2014 executive session minutes concerning the discussion 
of compensation policy. Because this portion of the meeting was not a lawful executive session 
held in compliance with G.L. c. 30A, § 21, the minutes may not be redacted under the Public 
Records Law. See G.L. c. 30A, § 22(f) (stating that the minutes of an executive session may be 
withheld from disclosure to the public as long as publication may defeat the lawful purposes of 
an executive session, provided that the executive session was held in compliance with section 
21.)
