RE: Open Meeting Law Complaint 
Dear Attorney Fair: 
This office received a complaint from Lauren Walsh, dated April 26, 2013, alleging that 
the Seekonk Board of Selectmen (the "Board") violated the Open Meeting Law, G.L. c. 3OA, §§ 
18-25. The complaint was originally filed with the Board on or about March 26, 2013, and you 
responded, on behalf of the Board, to the original complaint by letter dated April 12, 2013. In 
her complaint, Ms. Walsh alleges that the Board's February 27, 2013 meeting notice included a 
topic that was not sufficiently specific. 
We reviewed the March 26, 2013 complaint; your April 12, 2013 response on behalf of 
the Board; and the April 26, 2013 complaint filed with our office requesting further review. We 
also reviewed the notice, minutes and recording from the Board's February 27, 2013 meeting, as 
well as the notices from the Board's April 10, 2013 and April 17, 2013 meetings. 
Following our review, we find that the Board did not violate the Open Meeting Law. 
FACTS 
Based upon our review of the material listed above, the facts are as follows. During its 
February 27, 2013 meeting, the Board planned to discuss entering into a lease with ModSpace 
for a location to serve as temporary offices for Fluman Services until the construction of the new 
senior center was finished. The notice listed the discussion topic as follows: "Consider and sign 
contract with ModSpace for the lease of modular unit." During the meeting, the Board did not 
take any action with regard to this topic and continued its discussions until its April 10, 2013 and 
April 17, 2013 meetings. For these meetings, the Board's notice described the topic as follows: 
"Consider and potentially sign contract with ModSpace for the lease of a modular unit(s) for use 
as a temporary Fluman Services office in conjunction with the temporary re-location of the 
Senior Center."

DISCUSSION 
The Open Meeting Law requires that all meeting notices "shall be printed in a legible, 
easily understandable format and shall contain the date, time, and place of such meeting and a 
listing of topics that the chair reasonably anticipates will be discussed at the meeting." G.L. c. 
30A, § 20(b). The "list of topics shall have sufficient specificity to reasonably advise the public 
of the issues to be discussed at the meeting." 940 CMR 29.03(l)(b). We generally consider a 
topic to include sufficient specificity when a reasonable member of the public could read the 
topic and understand the anticipated nature of the public body's discussion. OML 2013-16.1 
The topic, "Consider and sign contract with ModSpace for the lease of modular unit," 
was sufficiently specific to describe the topic to be discussed, though it is clear that additional 
detail concerning the temporary re-location of Human Services may have been helpful to the 
public. The Board recognized this fact, and included this additional detail in its notices for 
subsequent meetings held on April 10, 2013 and April 17, 2013, listing the topic as follows: 
"Consider and potentially sign contract with ModSpace for the lease of a modular unit(s) for use 
as a temporary Human Services office in conjunction with the temporary re-location of the 
Senior Center." We encourage the Board to continue including this level of detail about 
discussion topics in future meeting notices. 
For the reasons stated above, we find that the Board did not violate the Open Meeting 
Law. We now consider the complaint addressed by this determination to be resolved. This 
determination does not address any other complaints which may be pending with our office or 
the Board. Please feel free to contact our office at (617) 963-2540 if you have any questions 
about this letter.
