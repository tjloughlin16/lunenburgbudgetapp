RE: Open Meeting Law Complaint 
Dear Attorney Blue: 
This office received a complaint from Ronald Beaty on August 24, alleging that the 
Massachusetts Gaming Commission (the "Commission") violated the Open Meeting Law, G.L. 
c. 30A, §§ 18-25.1 Specifically, the complaint alleges that the notice for the Commission's July 
23 meeting listed a topic without sufficient detail. The complaint was originally filed with the 
Commission on August 2. The Commission responded by letter dated August 17. 
Following our review, we resolve this complaint by informal action in accordance with 
940 CMR 29.07(2)(a), and find that the Commission did not violate the Open Meeting Law. Mr. 
Beaty alleges the notice was insufficient because it used an abbreviation not generally 
understood by members of the public. The notice for the Commission's July 23 meeting listed a 
topic for discussion as: "205 CMR 139: Licensee Disclosure and Reporting - Final Adoption and 
Approval of Amended SBIS - T. Grossman Deputy General Counsel - VOTE." The acronym 
"SBIS" stands for Small Business Impact Statement. We have generally said that public bodies 
should not use acronyms that are not commonly understood because they may be confusing to 
the public. See OML 2015-116.2 The Commission has conceded that the acronym SBIS was 
not entirely clear to those without specific knowledge of the topic, and we agree. However, we 
find that the general topic—discussion of the Commission's licensee disclosure and reporting 
regulations—was clear. We therefore find no violation of the Open Meeting Law.
