RE: Open Meeting Law Complaint 
Dear Mr. Coburn, 
This office received a complaint from Mr. A. Patrick Hayes dated January 17, 2011, 
alleging that the Natick School Committee (the "Committee") violated the Open Meeting Law, 
G.L. c. 30A, §§ 18-25, by failing to adequately inform the public of a topic for discussion at an 
open meeting on October 18, 2010. The complaint was originally filed with the Committee on or 
about October 19, 2010. We also received the Committee's response to the complaint dated 
November 9, 2010. 
Under the Open Meeting Law, the Attorney General is vested with the responsibility of 
investigating this matter. We find that the School Committee's meeting notice did not provide 
adequate notice as required by the Open Meeting Law, G.L. c. 30A, § 20(b). 
In reaching this determination, we reviewed the two complaints and the Committee's 
response, as well as the meeting notice for the October 18, 2010 meeting and the minutes of the 
October 18, 2010 meeting. 
The complaint specifically alleges that, "Nile Natick School Committee posted an 
agenda for its October 18, 2010 meeting with the following agenda item[l Superintendent's 
Report, Town Meeting Update." The complaint further alleges that, "the School Committee had 
discussion and deliberations and took votes on up to five specific Warrant Articles that are 
coming before Natick Fall Town Meeting." 
===PAGE 2===
According to the meeting notice published ahead of the October 18, 2010 meeting, under 
the category of "Superintendent's Report" appeared an item listed as "1. Town Meeting Update." 
The minutes of the October 18, 2010 Committee meeting list a section as "Town Meeting 
Update." That section states, in part, that "Chairman Coburn reviewed the following town 
meeting warrant articles which are of interest to the School Committee." The minutes then 
summarize the discussion of seven separate warrant articles, including votes on whether the 
School Committee would recommend positive action at Town Meeting. 
The School Committee's response to the complaint states that the Superintendent created 
a task force which undertook a fact-finding process in connection with the warrant articles 
involving the School Committee. The response further states that an October 13, 2010, 
"[d]iscussion among members of the task forced indicated clearly that the topic of Town Meeting 
warrant articles would be on the agenda for October 18, 2010." 
The Open Meeting Law requires a public body to post a meeting notice 48 hours in 
advance of a meeting and include, a "listing of topics that the chair reasonably anticipates will be 
discussed at the meeting." Public bodies are requited to list topics in a meeting notice with 
"sufficient specificity to reasonably advise the public of the issues to be discussed at the 
meeting." 940 CMR 29.03. We generally consider a topic to include sufficient specificity when 
a reasonable member of the public could read the topic and understand what the nature of the 
public body's discussion will be. 
Here, the meeting notice merely listed "Town meeting Update" under "Superintendent's 
Report." It is clear from the response to the complaint that the Committee was aware by October 
13, 2010 of specific Town Meeting Warrant Articles which would be discussed at the 
Committee's October 18, 2010 meeting. Therefore the chair could reasonably have anticipated 
those specific topics more than 48 hours in advance of the October 18, 2010 meeting. "Town 
Meeting Update" does not provide sufficient specificity when the chair of the Committee is 
aware of the specific items to be discussed and voted upon. The meeting notice should have, at a 
minimum, included detail of the nature of discussion; ideally it would also indicate any 
anticipated votes. An appropriate notice topic could have appeared as: 
Discussion of Town Meeting Warrant Articles 1, 9, 10, 18, 32, 33, and 35. The School 
Committee may vote to recommend action on these articles at Town Meeting. 
In the future, the Committee is required to comply with the Open Meeting Law, G.L. c. 
30A, §§ 18-25, and is advised to provide sufficient detail in meeting notices so that members of 
the public know with specificity what issues will be discussed by the Committee. The 
requirement that meeting notices list topics with sufficient specificity is new, and the Committee 
did make an effort to provide notice to the public of the general topic under consideration. 
Therefore, we resolve this complaint by providing additional guidance to the Committee. 
However, the Committee should be aware that evidence of similar violations in the future may be 
considered evidence of an intent to violate the Open Meeting Law. See 940 CMR 29.02. We 
decline to take the additional steps requested by the complainant, which include rescinding the 
action taken by the Committee on this matter.
