RE: Open Meeting Law Complaint 
Dear Attorney Frank; 
This office received a complaint from Patrick Higgins, dated March 20, alleging that the 
Swansea Board of Selectmen (the Board) violated the Open Meeting Law, G.L. c. 30A, §§ 18-
25. The complaint was originally filed with the Board on or about February 27, and the Board 
responded by letter dated March 17.1 In his complaint, Mr. Higgins alleges that it was improper 
for the Board chair to, "resubmit the determination of the Office of Open Government in 2015-
24 without the [Board] first placing this issue on an agenda for a public meeting and deliberating 
as to what steps the [Board] should take." 
Following our review, we find that the Board did not violate the Open Meeting Law. In 
reaching a determination, we reviewed the original complaint filed with the Board; the Board's 
response; complainant's request for further review; and a video of the Board's February 24 
meeting.2 Additionally, we reviewed an April 21 email from Mr. Fliggins to our office, 
responding to our request for certain information. Finally, we spoke via telephone with Attorney 
Arthur Frank on May 1, and reviewed email correspondence from Attorney Frank, dated May 8.

General issued OML 2015-24, in which we found that the Board violated the Open Meeting 
Law. The Board received the written decision on Thursday, February 19. On the morning of

DISCUSSION 
The Open Meeting Law requires that a public body post timely notice for meetings, 
listing all topics the chair reasonably anticipates will be discussed. G.L. c. 30A, § 20(b); 940 
CMR 29.03(l)(b). A public body may discuss a topic that was not included in the meeting 
notice if the chair did not reasonably anticipate the discussion of that topic 48 hours in advance 
of the meeting. See id.; OML 2012-3; OML 2011 -13.4 Here, Chairman Furtado did not 
anticipate discussing the request for reconsideration of OML 2015-24 until he heard back from 
Attorney Frank on the morning of the meeting. We therefore find that the Board was not 
required to list this topic in its meeting notice. See id. 
Furthermore, there is no allegation that the Board discussed this matter outside of a 
meeting. Rather, the allegation is precisely the opposite - that Chairman Furtado took action 
without first discussing the reconsideration issue with his fellow Board members. The Open 
Meeting Law does not require a public body to discuss any topic. OML 2014-98; OML 2013-
64; OML 2012-23. Thus, Chairman Furtado did not violate the Open Meeting Law by 
discussing reconsideration with counsel before raising the issue at a meeting. As our review 
concerns only compliance with the Open Meeting Law, we take no position as to whether any 
other law or Board policy may have required him to do so. See OML 2014-113; OML 2013-
106; OML 2012-47. 
CONCLUSION 
For the reasons stated above, we find that the Board did not violate the Open Meeting 
Law. We now consider the complaint addressed by this determination to be resolved. This 
determination does not address any other complaints that may be pending with our office or with 
the Board.
