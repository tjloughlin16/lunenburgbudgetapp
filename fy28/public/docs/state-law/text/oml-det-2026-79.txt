that the Monson Select Board (the “Board”) violated the Open Meeting Law, G.L. c. 30A, §§ 18-

FACTS 
 
We find the facts as follows. During a meeting held on October 14, 2025, the Board 
reviewed an Open Meeting Law complaint filed by the complainant on September 24, 2025, as 
well as a response to the complaint that had been drafted by town legal counsel.  The Board then 
unanimously voted to authorize town legal counsel to respond to the complaint.  During a 
meeting held on October 28, 2025, the Board reviewed one Open Meeting Law complaint filed 
by Gretchen Neggers on October 14, 2025, and two Open Meeting Law complaints filed by 
Roxanne Gunther on October 15, 2025. The Board voted to forward the complaints to town legal 
counsel for response. 
 
The Board posted notice for a meeting to be held on November 17, 2025, at 7:00 P.M.  
One specific topic included in the notice was listed as follows: 
 
“Old Business” 
 
1. Open Meeting Law Determinations 
• Open Meeting Law Complaint against Select Board dated September 24, 2025, 
filed by Laurent McDonald on file with the Town Clerk  
• Open Meeting Law Complaint against Select Board dated October 14, 2025, filed 
by Gretchen Neggers on file with the Town Clerk 
• Open Meeting Law Complaint against Select Board dated October 15, 2025, filed 
by Roxanne Gunther on file with the Town Clerk 
• Open Meeting Law Complaint against Select Board dated October 15, 2025, filed 
by Roxanne Gunther on file with the Town Clerk 
 
The November 17, 2025, meeting was held as planned. During the meeting, the Board 
briefly reviewed the four Open Meeting Law complaints listed on the notice and which it had 
previously reviewed during its meetings held on October 14 and October 28, 2025. Town 
Administrator Jennifer Wolowicz advised the Board that town legal counsel drafted responses to 
each of the four complaints and asked the Board to review each of the responses. Board member 
Patricia Oney then read town legal counsel’s responses to each of the complaints. 
 
DISCUSSION 
 
The Open Meeting Law requires that, except in an emergency, “a public body shall post 
notice of every meeting at least 48 hours prior to such meeting, excluding Saturdays, Sundays 
and legal holidays.”  G.L. c. 30A, § 20(b). The notice must be printed in a legible, easily 
understandable format and must contain the date, time and place of the meeting, as well as a 
listing of topics that the chair reasonably anticipates will be discussed. Id. The list of topics shall 
have “sufficient specificity to reasonably advise the public of the issues to be discussed at the 
meeting.” 940 CMR 29.03(l)(b). We generally consider a topic to be sufficiently specific when a 
reasonable member of the public could read the topic and understand the anticipated nature of 
the public body’s discussion. See OML 2015-35; OML 2013-39.

The complaint alleges that the Board posted a notice with insufficient specificity for the 
meeting held on November 17, 2025. Specifically, the complaint alleges that the topic “Open 
Meeting Law Determinations” was insufficient because it “makes it appear as if the decision is 
final” and from the Division of Open Government rather than draft responses from town legal 
counsel. Here, during the November 17, 2025, meeting, the Board discussed four Open Meeting 
Law complaints it had received and the responses to the complaints drafted by town legal 
counsel. A public body need not list every detail about a topic to comply with the Law’s 
requirements. See OML 2022-98; OML 2020-132; OML 2019-134. We find that the notice 
contained sufficient specificity to reasonably advise the public of the issues to be discussed at the 
meeting, and thus the Board did not violate the Open Meeting Law. See G.L. c. 30A, § 20(b); 
940 CMR 29.03(l)(b); OML 2015-73; OML 2014-127; OML 2014-58; OML 2014-38.  
 
CONCLUSION 
 
For the reasons stated above, we find that the Board did not violate the Open Meeting 
Law. We now consider the complaint addressed by this determination to be resolved. This 
determination does not address any other complaints that may be pending with the Board or with 
our office. Please feel free to contact our office at (617) 963-2540 if you have any questions 
regarding this letter.
