RE: Open Meeting Law Complaints 
Dear Attorney Murray: 
This office received two complaints from Judith Ahern, dated May 4 and June 5, alleging 
that the Wellfleet Charter Review Committee (the "Committee") violated the Open Meeting 
Law, G.L. c. 30A, §§ 18-25.1 The first complaint was filed with the Committee on March 14 and 
you responded, on behalf of the Committee, by letter dated April 4. In this complaint, Ms. Ahcrn 
alleges that a Committee member violated the Open Meeting Law by expressing his opinions on 
a matter before the Committee in an email to the Town Administrator. The second complaint 
was filed with the Committee on March 16 and you responded by letter dated April 5. Ms. 
Ahem alleges in the second complaint that the Committee failed to produce audio recordings in 
response to a February 23 public records request.2 
We resolve these complaints by informal action in accordance with 940 CMR 
29.07(2)(a), after reviewing the original complaints, and find that the Committee did not violate 
the Open Meeting Law. 
The allegation that a Committee member violated the Open Meeting Law by expressing 
his opinions on a matter before the Committee in an email to the Town Administrator, even if 
taken as true, would not constitute a violation of the Open Meeting Law. "Deliberation" is 
defined, in relevant part, as "an oral or written communication through any medium, including 
electronic mail, between or among a quorum of a public body on any public business within its 
jurisdiction." G.L. c. 30A, § 18. The Town Administrator is not a member of the Committee.

Thus, communications between one member and the Town Administrator that do not otherwise 
reach a quorum of the Committee, even where an opinion is expressed, would not constitute 
deliberation. See OML 2017-199; OML 2017-69; OML 2015-77; OML 2011-52. Therefore, we 
decline to find a violation. See OML 2018-46.3 
The March 16 complaint pertains to Ms. Ahern's February 23 request for audio 
recordings of the Committee's meetings. The Division of Open Government reviews complaints 
to determine compliance with the Open Meeting Law. See OML Declination 1-21-2014. The 
Open Meeting Law states that the minutes of any open session, the notes, recordings or other 
materials used in the preparation of such minutes and all documents and exhibits used at the 
session, shall be public records in their entirety. See G.L. c. 30A, § 22(e). However, the Open 
Meeting Law does not govern a public body's obligation to respond to requests for audio 
recording, which fall under the Public Records Law, G.L. c. 66, § 10. See OML 2014-45; OML 
Declination 7-7-14. Rather, the Supervisor of Records within the Secretary of the 
Commonwealth's Public Records Division is responsible for reviewing appeals for failure to 
provide records public records. Therefore, we decline to find a violation of the Open Meeting 
Law with respect to this allegation. We understand that Ms. Ahern has already contacted the 
Supervisor of Records in the Secretary of the Commonwealth's Public Records Division 
regarding filing an appeal with their office. 
We now consider the complaints addressed by this determination to be resolved. Please 
feel free to contact our office at (617) 963-2540 if you have any questions regarding this letter.
