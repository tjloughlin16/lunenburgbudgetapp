This office received two complaints from Patrick Higgins on July 3, 2025, alleging that 
the Fall River Parks Board (the “Board”) violated the Open Meeting Law, G.L. c. 30A, §§ 18-25.  
The first complaint was originally filed with the Board on or around May 20, and you responded, 
on behalf of the Board, by letter dated June 30.
1  The first complaint alleges that the Board failed 
to provide draft meeting minutes within 10 days of a request.  The second complaint was 
originally filed with the Board on or around June 28, and you responded on behalf of the Board 
by separate letter dated June 30.  The second complaint alleges that the Board failed to timely 
review and respond to the first complaint.  
 
Following our review, we resolve this complaint by informal action, in accordance with 
940 CMR 29.07(2)(a) and find that the Board violated the Open Meeting Law as alleged in the 
complaints.
2

The complainant emailed the Board’s Chair on Saturday, May 10, requesting the minutes 
of all minutes of the Board’s meetings for the years 2022 to 2025, “whether accepted or in draft 
form.”  The complainant specified that the Open Meeting Law required a response within 10 
calendar days and identified a response deadline of May 20.  The Board responded to the 
complainant on May 14 by providing minutes for all meetings held in 2022, 2023, 2024, and the 
first four months of 2025.  The complainant inquired about the draft minutes of the Board’s May 
2025 meeting.  The Board’s Chair responded that the May meeting minutes had not been 
accepted yet and would not be until the Board’s June meeting.  The Complainant countered that 
the Open Meeting Law requires that draft meeting minutes be provided within 10 days of a 
request, and the Chair responded that the request was received on May 12 (the first business day 
following the May 10 request) and that draft minutes would be provided within ten business 
days, by May 23.  The Open Meeting Law complaint was filed on May 20; the minutes of the 
Board’s May meeting were subsequently provided. 
 
The Board has since acknowledged that the Open Meeting Law requires a response to a 
request for meeting minutes within 10 business days, and that it failed to respond in a timely 
manner.  Therefore, we find that the Board violated the Open Meeting Law.
4   
 
Next, the Open Meeting Law requires that withi
 n 14 business days of receiving an Open 
Meeting Law complaint, the public body chair must distribute the complaint to all members of 
the body; the body must meet to review the complaint; and the body must then respond to the 
complainant in writing, providing our office with a copy of the complaint and the response.  See 
G.L. c. 30A, § 23(b); 940 CMR 29.05(3), (5).  Once a public body has reviewed a complaint 
during a meeting, its decision to refer the complaint to another individual to respond rather than 
discuss its substance is the public body’s prerogative.  See OML 2019-40; OML 2017-96.  A 
public body may review a complaint during a meeting and refer the complaint to a designee, 
such as legal counsel, to respond, or a designee may receive the complaint and draft a proposed 
response in advance for the public body to consider and approve during a meeting, but the public 
body may not remove itself entirely from the process.  See OML 2020-164. 
 
Here, the Board does not dispute that it did not respond to the first complaint within 14 
business days of receipt of the complaint.  Therefore, the Board violated the Open Meeting Law 
as alleged in the second complaint by failing to timely respond to the first complaint.  Moreover, 
we note that when legal counsel responded to the first complaint on June 30, the Board still had

For the reasons stated above, we find that the Board violated the Open Meeting Law by
failing to timely provide draft meeting minutes upon request and by failing to timely respond to
an Open Meeting Law complaint. We order the Board's immediate and future compliance with
the Open Meeting Law and caution that similar future violations may be considered evidence of
an intent to violate the Law.
