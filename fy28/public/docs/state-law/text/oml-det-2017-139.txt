Dear Chairman Perry: 
This office received a complaint from Timothy Toomey, dated July 24, alleging that the 
Rowley Board of Selectmen (the "Board") violated the Open Meeting Law, G.L. c. 30A, §§ 18-
25.1 The complaint was originally filed with the Board on June 29, and the Board responded by 
letter dated July 13. In his complaint, Mr. Toomey alleges that he was not allowed to address the 
Board during a June 28 meeting. 
Following our review, we resolve this matter by informal action in accordance with 940 
CMR 29.07(2)(a), and we find that the Board did not violate the Open Meeting Law. We 
reviewed the original complaint, the Board's reply, and the request for further review filed with 
our office. We spoke with the complainant by telephone on August 9 and we spoke with the 
Rowley town administrator by telephone on August 30. 
The Open Meeting Law requires that all meetings of public bodies be conducted in open 
session, with some exceptions. G.L. c. 30A, §§ 20(a), 21(a). The Open Meeting Law states that 
"no person shall address a meeting of a public body without permission of the chair, and all 
persons shall, at the request of the chair, be silent." G.L. c. 30A, § 20(f). The law therefore 
gives discretion to the chair of the public body to determine who may speak or otherwise 
participate at a meeting. See OML 2014-23; OML 2013-64; OML 2012-59.2 
The complaint concerns a June 28 joint meeting between the Board and the Rowley 
Board of Water Commissioners (the "BWC"). During this meeting, the complainant was 
recognized to speak. His comments concerned the minutes of a BWC meeting held in executive 
session on September 11, 2015. The complainant, a former BWC member, alleges that the
