 RE: Open Meeting Law Complaint 
 
Dear Chair Whiteside: 
 
This office received a complaint from Margaret Sheehan, on behalf of Save the Pine 
Barrens, Inc., on July 14, 2021,1 alleging that the Wareham Board of Selectmen (the “Board”), 
and specifically its Chair,2 as well as Town Counsel,3 violated the Open Meeting Law, G.L. 
c. 30A, §§ 18-25. The complaint was originally filed with the Board on or about May 24, and 
you responded on behalf of the Board by letter dated June 2. Specifically, the complaint alleges 
that during the Board’s May 18 meeting, the Chair denied a member of the public the ability to 
participate in the public comment portion of the meeting.  
 
Following our review, we resolve this matter by informal action in accordance with 940 
CMR 29.07(2)(a) and find that the Board did not violate the Open Meeting Law. In reaching this 
determination, we reviewed the Open Meeting Law complaint, the response, the request for 
further review, and additional materials provided to our office.  
 
The Division of Open Government’s jurisdiction concerns compliance with the Open 
Meeting Law. As we explained in a recent determination involving the Board, the Open Meeting 
Law guarantees members of the public a right to access a public body’s meetings, not a right to 
actively participate in those meetings. See OML 2021-71; OML 2020-77; OML 2018-132; OML

2015-12; OML 2012-13.4 The Open Meeting Law states that “no person shall address a meeting 
of a public body without permission of the chair, and all persons shall, at the request of the chair, 
be silent.” G.L. c. 30 A, § 20(f). The Open Meeting Law therefore gives discretion to the chair of 
a public body to determine who may speak or otherwise participate at a meeting. See OML 
2017-154; OML 2014-23; OML 2013-64; OML 2012-59. Because the Open Meeting Law gives 
the chair of a public body discretion over who may speak or otherwise participate at a meeting, 
we find no violation of the Open Meeting Law with respect to this allegation.  
 
Although public comment is within the chair’s discretion, we encourage public bodies 
to allow for as much public participation as time and circumstances permit. See OML 2015-12; 
OML 2014-129; OML 2012-59. Furthermore, we offer no opinion as to whether any actions 
taken by the Board or the Chair, or any town policy that may have been relied on in limiting 
public comment, might violate of the First Amendment or any other law or regulation outside the 
scope of our review. 
  
For the reasons stated above, we find that the Board did not violate the Open Meeting 
Law. We now consider the complaint addressed by this determination to be resolved. This 
determination does not address any other complaints that may be pending with our office or the 
Board. Please feel free to contact the Division at (617) 963-2540 if you have any questions.
