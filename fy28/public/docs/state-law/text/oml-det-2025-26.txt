RE:  Open Meeting Law Complaint 
 
Dear Attorney Mooney: 
 
 This office received a complaint from Bruce Friedman on December 4, 2024, alleging 
that the Malden Licensing Board (the “Board”) violated the Open Meeting Law, G.L. c. 30A, 
§§ 18-25.  The complaint was originally filed with the Board on November 1, 2024, and you 
responded, on behalf of the Board, by letter dated November 18, 2024.  The complaint alleges 
that the Board failed to timely respond to a request for meeting minutes.  
 
We resolve this complaint by informal action, i
 n accordance with 940 CMR 29.07(2)(a), 
after reviewing the original complaint, the Board’s response to the complaint, and the complaint 
filed with our office requesting further review.
1  The Board acknowledges, and we find, that the 
Board violated the Open Meeting Law by failing to timely respond to a request for minutes.2 
While not claiming that it is excused from a finding of a violation, the Board has explained that 
the failure to respond to the complainant’s request for minutes was due to a change of personnel 
and a mistaken belief that the request had already been addressed.

We must determine whether the Board’s violation was, as the complainant urges, 
intentio
nal.  See G.L. c. 30A, § 23(c).  The complainant cites our prior determination, OML 
2024-202, in support of his argument that the Board has been warned previously about the need 
to respond to a request for minutes.  That determination was issued on November 1, 2024, the 
same day that the complainant submitted to the Board his request for minutes.  In light of the 
circumstances set out in the Board’s response and the fact that our previous determination was 
issued on the same day that the complainant submitted his records request such that the Board 
might not have had the opportunity to benefit from our guidance and implement needed changes 
to ensure ongoing compliance, we decline to find that this violation was intentional.   
 
Finally, as previously explained to the complainant in OML 2024-231, OML 2024-202 
and OML
 2024-146, the Open Meeting Law does not govern a public body’s response to a 
request for records other than meeting minutes.  See G.L. c. 30A, §§ 22(c), (e); OML 2024-231; 
OML 2024-202; OML 2024-146.3  Therefore, failure to timely respond to requests for 
documents other than minutes concerns a potential violation of the Public Records Law, rather 
than the Open Meeting Law, and we thus decline to review allegations regarding the failure of 
the Board to respond to requests for documents other than meeting minutes.  
 
We order the Board’s immediate and future compliance with the law’s requirements, and 
we caution the Board that similar future violations could be considered evidence of intent to 
violate the law and may result in a civil penalty of up to $1,000 per violation.  As the Board has 
since provided the requested minutes, we order no further remedial action. 
 
We now consider the complaint addressed by this determination to be resolved.  This 
determination does not address any other complaints that may be pending with our office or the 
Board.  Please feel free to contact our office at (617) 963-2540 if you have any questions 
regarding this letter.
