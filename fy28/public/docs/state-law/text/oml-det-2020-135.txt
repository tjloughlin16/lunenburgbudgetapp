 RE: Open Meeting Law Complaint 
 
Dear Chair Karlson: 
This office received a complaint from George Harris on May 13, 2020, alleging that the 
Wayland Board of Selectmen (the “Board”) violated the Open Meeting Law, G.L. c. 30A, §§ 18-
25. The complaint was originally filed with the Board on March 4, and you responded on behalf 
of the Board by letter dated April 20.1 In his complaint, Mr. Harris alleges that the Board failed 
to timely approve several sets of executive session minutes. 
 We resolve this complaint by informal action in accordance with 940 CMR 29.07(2)(a), 
and find that the Board violated the Open Meeting Law as alleged. The Open Meeting Law 
requires that a public body “create and maintain accurate minutes of all meetings, including 
executive sessions, setting forth the date, time and place, the members present or absent, a 
summary of the discussions on each subject, a list of documents and other exhibits used at the 
meeting, the decisions made and the actions taken at each meeting, including the record of all 
votes.” G.L. c. 30A, § 22(a). The meeting minutes must be reviewed and approved in a timely 
manner. G.L. c. 30A, § 22(c). The Open Meeting Law itself does not define “timely manner.” 
However, the Attorney General’s Open Meeting Law regulations provide that “timely manner” 
means “within the next three public body meetings or within 30 days, whichever is later, unless 
the public body can show good cause for further delay.” 940 C.M.R. 29.1 (2).

 The responsibility for complying with the Open Meeting Law’s requirement that meeting 
minutes be created and approved in a timely manner rests with public bodies and members of 
such bodies, not with individual public employees, such as administrative staff. See OML 2018-
105.2 Therefore, we have generally found that shortages in staff do not provide good cause for 
failing to approve minutes in a timely manner. See OML 2018-105; OML 2015-86. A public 
body may approve executive session minutes in open session if the public body merely wishes to 
approve the minutes, without a substantive discussion of those minutes. See OML 2018-73.3  
 Here, the Board approved its November 25, 2019, December 9, 2019, and December 16, 
2019, executive session minutes at a February 3 meeting. The requirement to approve meeting 
minutes in a timely manner applies equally to minutes of open and executive sessions. See OML 
2020-81. As the Board also met on December 20, 2019, and January 9, 13, 21, 22 and 27, we 
find that the November 25, 2019, December 9, 2019, and December 16, 2019, executive session 
minutes were not approved within three meetings or thirty days. Therefore, the Board violated 
the Open Meeting Law by failing to approve three sets of executive session minutes in a timely 
manner. Whenever possible, we recommend that minutes of a meeting be approved at the next 
meeting. See OML 2019-39; OML 2014-15. 
 The Board has previously been found in violation of the Open Meeting Law for failure to 
timely approve open session meeting minutes. See OML 2016-98. A prior, similar violation of 
the Open Meeting Law may be considered evidence of an intentional violation of the Law. See 
G.L. c. 30A, § 23(c). Here, however, the Board asserts that it strives to approve executive session 
minutes within the next three executive sessions, believing that executive session minutes are 
treated differently than open session minutes. The Open Meeting Law regulations refer to timely 
approval as being within the next three “public body meetings,” not within the next three 
meetings at which a public body chooses to enter executive session.  940 CMR 29.11(2).  
Although we believe this requirement is clear, we note that the Board appears to have approved 
the minutes of the open sessions for each of the meetings in question within the time provided by 
law, and therefore we are persuaded that the Board sincerely, though incorrectly, believed that 
approving executive session minutes within the next three executive sessions complied with the 
Open Meeting Law.4 For these reasons, we do not find the Board’s violation to be intentional. 
We order the Board’s immediate and future compliance with the Open Meeting Law and caution 
the Board that it has now been expressly warned that minutes of both open and executive 
sessions must be approved within the later of the next three meetings or 30 days, and a 
determination by our office of a similar violation in the future may be considered evidence of 
intent to violate the Open Meeting Law and may result in the imposition of a civil penalty.
