 RE: Open Meeting Law Complaint 
 
Dear Mr. Consilvio: 
 
This office received a complaint from Patrick Higgins on October 31, 2024, alleging that 
the Auburn Housing Authority Board of Commissioners (the “Board”) violated the Open 
Meeting Law, G.L. c. 30A, §§ 18-25.  The complaint was originally filed with the Board on or 
about September 11, 2024, and Wayne Page, former Board Chair, responded to the complaint, on 
behalf of the Board, by letter dated September 25, 2024.
1  The complaint alleges that the Board 
failed to approve minutes of the meeting held on April 25 in a timely manner. 
 
After review, we find that the Board did not violate the Open Meeting Law in the way 
alleged.  In reaching this determination, we reviewed the original complaint, the Board’s 
response to the complaint, and the complaint filed with our office requesting further review.  In 
addition, we reviewed the notices and minutes of Board meetings held on September 9 and 
September 18.   
 
The Open Meeting Law requires that a public body “create and maintain accurate 
minutes of all meetings, including executive sessions, setting forth the date, time and place, the 
members present or absent, a summary of the discussions on each subject, a list of documents 
and other exhibits used at the meeting, the decisions made, and the actions taken at each meeting, 
including the record of all votes.”  G.L. c. 30A, § 22(a).  Minutes of all open sessions must be 
reviewed and approved in a “timely manner.”  G.L. c. 30A, § 22(c).  “Timely manner” means

“within the next three public body meetings or within 30 days, whichever is later, unless the 
public body can show good cause for further delay.”  940 CMR 29.11(2).  Whenever possible, 
we recommend that minutes of a meeting be approved at the next meeting.  See OML 2024-114; 
OML 2018-67; OML 2017-133.2 
 
The Board held me etings on April 25, July 11, September 9, and September 18.  The 
Board posted notice for meetings to be held on May 23 and August 22, but both meetings were 
cancelled.  Here, the Board was required to approve the minutes of its April 25 meeting within 
the next three public body meetings as that was the later of the two time periods.  See 940 CMR 
29.11; OML 2018-111.  Since the May 23 and August 22 meetings were cancelled, we find that 
the Board did not meet on those two dates.  See G.L. c. 30A, § 18 (Open Meeting Law defines 
“meeting” as “a deliberation by a public body with respect to any matter within the body’s 
jurisdiction”).  As such, we find that the Board approved the minutes of the April 25 meeting in a 
timely manner when those minutes were approved on September 18, which was the third 
consecutive Board meeting.
3   
 
For the reasons stated above, we find that the Board did not violate the Open Meeting 
Law.  We now consider the complaint addressed by this determination to be resolved.  This 
determination does not address any other complaints that may be pending with the Board or with 
our office.  Please feel free to contact our office at (617) 963-2540 if you have any questions 
regarding this letter.
