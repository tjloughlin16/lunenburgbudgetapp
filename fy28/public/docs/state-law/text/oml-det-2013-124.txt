RE: Open Meeting Law Complaints 
Dear Mr. Doyle: 
This office received two complaints from David MacLean, dated February 8, 2013 and 
March 21, 2013, respectively, alleging that the Franklin Board of Assessors (the "Board") 
violated the Open Meeting Law, G.L. c. 30A, §§ 18-25. The complaints were originally filed 
with the Board on or about January 9, 2013 and February 19, 2013, respectively. Mr. MacLean 
alleges, specifically, that the Board held a meeting or meetings without public notice to discuss 
Open Meeting Law complaints that he had filed with the Board on December 5, 2012 and 
February 8, 2013. 
Following our review, we find that the Board did not meet to discuss Mr. MacLean's 
December 5, 2012 and February 8, 2013 Open Meeting Law complaints. However, we find that 
the Board violated the Open Meeting Law by failing to review the two Open Meeting Law 
complaints before sending its response. 
Mr. MacLean filed Open Meeting Law complaints with the Board on or about December 
5, 2012 and February 8, 2013. Town Attorney Mark Cerel responded to the December 5, 2012 
complaint, on behalf of the Board, by letter dated December 17, 2012. You responded to the 
February 8, 2013 complaint, on behalf of the Board, by letter dated February 13, 2013. The 
Board did not meet to discuss either complaint before the respective responses were sent. 
The Board violated the law by not reviewing both complaints and meeting to issue or 
authorize a response as required by 940 CMR 29.05(5) and G.L. c. 30A, § 23(b). See also OML 
2012-90.1 The Open Meeting Law requires that within 14 business days after receiving an Open

Meeting Law complaint, a public body shall review the complaint's allegations; take remedial 
action, if appropriate; and send to the Attorney General a copy of the complaint and a description 
of any remedial action taken. 940 CMR 29.05(5); G.L. c. 30A, § 23(b). The public body shall 
simultaneously notify the complainant that it has sent such materials to the Attorney General and 
shall provide the complainant with a copy of the description of any remedial action taken. 940 
CMR 29.05(5). 
Additionally, we note that the Board responded to the January 9, 2013 complaint on 
March 4, 2013, 55 days after the complaint was filed. We remind the Board of its obligation to 
respond to an Open Meeting Law complaint within 14 business days. 940 CMR 29.05(5); G.L. 
c. 30A, § 23(b). We appreciate that the Board is comprised of volunteers and that it can be 
challenging to convene a meeting to timely address a complaint. However, the Board is required 
to do so by law. If the public body requires additional time to resolve the complaint, it may 
request an extension from the Attorney General by submitting a written request within 14 
business days after receiving the complaint. 940 CMR 29.05(5)(b). 
For the reasons stated above, we find that the Board violated the Open Meeting Law. 
We order the Board's immediate and future compliance with the Open Meeting Law, and caution 
the Board that a similar future violation may be considered an intentional violation of the law. 
We now consider the complaints addressed by this determination to be resolved. This 
determination does not address any other complaints which may be pending with our office or 
the Board. Please feel free to contact our office at (617) 963-2540 if you have any questions or 
believe any facts in this letter to be inaccurate.
