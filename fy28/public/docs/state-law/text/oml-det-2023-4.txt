This office received a complaint from Patrick Higgins on December 15, 2022, alleging 
that the Fall River School Committee (the “Committee”) violated the Open Meeting Law, G.L. c. 
30A, §§ 18-25.1  The complaint was originally filed with the Committee on November 15, and 
you responded to the complaint, on behalf of the Committee, by letter December 15, following 
an extension of time granted by this office.  The complaint alleges that the Committee failed to 
timely approve 18 sets of minutes of meetings held between March 2022 and September 2022.   
 
Following our review, we find that the Committee violated the Open Meeting Law by 
failing to timely approve six sets of meeting minutes.    We find that the Committee timely 
approved the remaining sets of meeting minutes.  In reaching this determination, we reviewed 
the original complaint, the Committee’s response to the complaint, the complaint filed with our 
office requesting further review, and the notice and minutes of the Committee meeting held on 
October 17, as well as a video recording of that meeting.

FACTS 
 
We find the facts as follows.  The Committee posted notices for, and held meetings on, 
March 9, March 14, March 16, March 23, April 11, May 9, June 13, July 18, August 8, August 
29, September 12, September 29, and October 17.   
 
The Committee is comprised of nine subcommittees – the Evaluation/AIP Subcommittee; 
Facilities and Operations Subcommittee; Finance Subcommittee; Grievance Subcommittee; 
Instructional Subcommittee; Parent & Community Outreach Subcommittee; Policy 
Subcommittee; Special Education, Alternative Education, and Early College Subcommittee; and 
Technology Subcommittee. 
 
The Policy Subcommittee posted a notice for, and held a meeting on, March 28.  The 
Technology Committee posted notices for, and held meetings on, March 29, April 28, and 
October 25.  The Special Education, Alternative Education, and Early College Subcommittee 
posted notices for, and held meetings on, April 4 and June 21.  The Facilities and Operations 
Subcommittee posted notices for, and held meetings, on April 25, July 26, and October 18.  The 
Parent & Community Outreach Subcommittee posted notices for, and held meetings on, May 5, 
September 21, and October 5.  The Instructional Subcommittee posted notices for, and held 
meetings on, June 7 and November 7. 
 
On October 7, 2022, our office issued a determination, OML 2022-184, finding that the 
Committee intentionally violated the Open Meeting Law by failing to approve meeting minutes 
in a timely manner.  Upon receipt of that determination, Superintendent Maria Pontes reviewed 
all outstanding minutes of Committee and subcommittee meetings and identified 18 sets of 
minutes that had not yet been created and approved.   
 
During a meeting held on October 17, the Committee approved 18 sets of meeting 
minutes - nine sets of minutes were from Committee meetings held on March 9, March 16, 
March 23, June 13, July 18, August 8, August 29, September 12, while the other nine sets of 
minutes were from six different subcommittee meetings held on March 28, March 29, April 4, 
April 25, April 28, May 2, June 7, June 21, and July 26.

DISCUSSION 
 
The Open Meeting Law requires that a public body “create and maintain accurate 
minutes of all meetings, including executive sessions, setting forth the date, time and place, the 
members present or absent, a summary of the discussions on each subject, a list of documents 
and other exhibits used at the meeting, the decisions made and the actions taken at each meeting, 
including the record of all votes.”  G.L. c. 30A, § 22(a).  The meeting minutes must be reviewed 
and approved in a timely manner.  G.L. c. 30A, § 22(c).  “Timely manner” means “within the 
next three public body meetings or within 30 days, whichever is later, unless the public body can

show good cause for further delay.”  See 940 CMR 29.11; OML 2018-48.4  Whenever possible, 
we recommend that minutes of a meeting be approved at the next meeting.  See OML 2018-67; 
OML 2017-133.  
 
The complaint alleges that 18 sets of meeting minutes were not approved in a timely 
manner.  Here, nine sets of minutes were meetings of the Committee, while the remaining nine 
sets of minutes were meetings of six different subcommittees of the Committee.  We find that six 
sets of minutes of Committee meetings, which were held on March 9, March 16, March 23, June 
13, July 18, and August 8, were not approved in a timely manner where those minutes were not 
approved within the later of the next three meetings or 30 days following each meeting.  See 
OML 2022-7; OML 2021-155.  As such, we find that the Commission violated the Open 
Meeting Law by failing to approve these minutes in a timely manner.  We find that the remaining 
three sets of minutes of Committee meetings, which were held on August 29, September 12, and 
September 29, were approved in a timely manner as these minutes were approved within the later 
of the next three Committee meetings or 30 days following each Committee meeting. 
 
We next turn to whether the nine sets of minutes of subcommittee meetings were 
approved in a timely manner.  A subcommittee is itself a separate public body for purposes of 
complying with the Open Meeting Law.  Therefore, as we previously explained to the 
Complainant several months before the instant complaint was filed, minutes of a subcommittee 
meeting must be approved within the later of the next 30 days or three meetings of the 
subcommittee.  See OML 2022-85 (minutes of a subcommittee meeting were timely approved by 
the parent public body two months later, as the subcommittee did not hold any meetings in the 
interim); OML 2020-19 (minutes of a subcommittee meeting were timely approved by the parent 
public body over seven months later, as the subcommittee had not held any meetings in the 
interim, even though the parent public body had held numerous meetings). 
 
The Open Meeting Law does not govern the method for approving meeting minutes and 
public bodies may choose the method for approval.  See OML 2020-81; OML 2019-107; OML 
2019-9.  Acceptable methods include, but are not limited to, approval by the public body chair 
alone or by another designated individual, approval by majority vote of the public body, approval 
by consent of the body (approved unless there is an objection), or approval by a parent public 
body on behalf of a subcommittee.  See OML 2019-107; OML 2019-25; OML 2017-34.  The 
method of approval of minutes that a public body chooses does not impact the standard for 
timely approval of minutes, which remains “within the next three public body meetings or within 
30 days, whichever is later.”  940 CMR 29.11.  As the nine sets of minutes of each meeting of 
the subcommittees, which were held on March 28, March 29, April 4, April 25, April 28, May 2, 
June 7, June 21, and July 26, were approved on October 17, and each subcommittee did not hold 
at least three meetings (the later of the two time periods) in the interim, we find that these nine 
sets of minutes were timely approved and neither the Committee nor the subcommittees violated 
the Open Meeting Law with respect to these minutes.  See OML 2022-85.

We remind the Committee that, in general, staffing shortages of the type that all 
governmental bodies with limited resources experience, or periodic staff vacancies, do not 
constitute good cause for failing to approve minutes in a timely manner, as it is the responsibility 
of the public body and its members to take steps to ensure that staff have adequate assistance to 
complete the minutes in a timely manner, including having members of the public body assist in 
the creation of minutes if necessary.  See OML 2021-177; OML 2018-105; OML 2015-86.  
Ultimately, the responsibility for complying with the Open Meeting Law’s requirement that 
meeting minutes be created and approved in a timely manner rests with public bodies and 
members of such bodies, not with individual public employees, such as administrative staff.  See 
OML 2018-105.   
 
We must now determine whether the above violation is, as the complainant urges, an 
intentional one.  See G.L. c. 30A, § 23(c).  An intentional violation is an “act or omission by a 
public body or a member thereof, in knowing violation of [the Open Meeting Law].”  940 CMR 
29.02.  An intentional violation may be found where the public body acted with deliberate 
ignorance of the law’s requirement or has previously been advised that certain conduct violates 
the Open Meeting Law.  Id.  On August 28, 2019, and October 7, 2022, our office found that the 
Committee violated the Open Meeting Law by failing to approve meeting minutes in a timely 
manner.  See OML 2022-184; OML 2019-107.  We specifically explained in those 
determinations that staffing shortages do not constitute good cause for delay, and that public 
body members themselves must take on the responsibility of preparing minutes if needed.  See 
OML 2022-184; OML 2019-107.  However, because the Committee took prompt remedial action  
in response to our finding in determination OML 2022-184 and proactively identified 
outstanding meeting minutes, and further because the Committee had already approved all 18 
sets of meeting minutes at the time the complaint was filed here, we decline to find that this 
violation was intentional.  See OML 2021-124.

CONCLUSION 
 
For the reasons stated above, we find that the Committee violated the Open Meeting Law 
by failing to timely approve six sets of meeting minutes.  We order immediate and future 
compliance with the law’s requirements and we caution that similar future violations could be 
considered evidence of intent to violate the law.  Future intentional violations for failure to 
timely approve meeting minutes may result in a recommendation of a civil penalty. 
 
We now consider the complaint addressed by this determination to be resolved.  This 
determination does not address any other complaints that may be pending with our office or the 
Committee.  Please feel free to contact our office at (617) 963-2540 if you have any questions 
regarding this letter.
