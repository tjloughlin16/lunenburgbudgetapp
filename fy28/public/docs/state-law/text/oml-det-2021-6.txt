This office received a complaint from Nada Milosavljevic-Fabrizio on December 30, 
2020, alleging that the Truro Charter Review Committee (the “Committee”) violated the Open 
Meeting Law, G.L. c. 30A, §§ 18-25.1  The complaint was originally filed with the Committee 
on October 8, and you responded, on behalf of the Committee, by letter dated October 16.  The 
complaint alleges that the Committee failed to timely respond to a request for open session 
meeting minutes. 
 
We resolve this matter by informal action, in accordance with 940 CMR 29.07(2)(a), 
after reviewing the original complaint, the Committee’s response to the complaint, the complaint 
filed with our office requesting further review, and the notice and minutes of the Committee’s 
October 14 meeting.  We find that the Committee did not violate the Open Meeting Law in the 
way alleged.   
 
The Open Meeting Law requires that “[m]inutes of all open sessions shall be created and 
approved in a timely manner.  The minutes of an open session, if they exist and whether 
approved or in draft form, shall be made available upon request by any person within 10 days.”  
G.L. c. 30A, § 22(c).

On September 17, the complainant submitted a request to the Public Records Access 
Officer at rao@truro-ma.org for copies of minutes of the Committee’s November 1, 2019, and 
February 26 meetings.  On September 25, Records Access Officer Noelle Scoullar responded 
advising that the minutes had not yet been approved.  On October 6, the complainant emailed the 
Public Records Access Officer and again requested copies of the Committee’s November 1, 
2019, and February 26 meeting minutes.  This time, Ms. Scoullar forwarded the request to 
Committee Chair Robert Panessiti.  Mr. Panessiti responded later that same day on October 6 
advising the complainant that the minutes of the Committee’s meetings held on November 6, 
2019, and February 26 “will be filed with the town and posted on the website once the draft 
copies have been reviewed and approved by the Committee.”  Responsive meeting minutes were 
provided to the complainant on October 16. 
 
A request for meeting minutes that is directed to a public body will fall under the Open 
Meeting Law and requires a response in 10 calendar days.  See G.L. c. 30A, §§ 22(c), (g); OML 
2019-163.2  On the other hand, a request for minutes that is directed to a clerk or other municipal 
employee as Records Access Officer will likely fall under the Public Records Law and requires a 
response in 10 business days.  See G.L. c. 66, § 10; OML 2019-126; OML 2019-68.  Here, 
although the complainant initially sent her October 6 request for meeting minutes to the Public 
Records Access Officer, the Records Access Officer forwarded the request to the Chair of the 
Committee.  As such, we consider the October 6 request to fall within the Open Meeting Law.3  
See OML 2020-14.  
 
We acknowledge that Chair Panessiti responded to the complainant’s request for minutes 
on the same day it was received, October 6.  However, Chair Panessiti’s response stated that 
draft meeting minutes would not be provided until they had been approved.  The Open Meeting 
Law requires that minutes of an open session, if they exist and whether approved or in draft 
form, must be made available upon request by any person within 10 days.  G.L. c. 30A, § 22(c).  
Nevertheless, the Board approved the November 1, 2019, and February 26 minutes during its 
October 14 meeting and provided copies of the minutes to the complainant on October 16, which 
was within 10 days of the request.  Therefore, we find that the Committee did not violate the 
Open Meeting Law as it timely responded to a request for minutes.   
 
Although not clearly raised in the complaint, we note that the minutes were approved in a 
timely manner.  Meeting minutes must be reviewed and approved in a timely manner.  G.L. c. 
30A, § 22(c).  “Timely manner” means “within the next three public body meetings or within 30 
days, whichever is later, unless the public body can show good cause for further delay.”  See 940 
CMR 29.11; OML 2018-48.  The Committee was required to approve the minutes of its 
November 1, 2019, and February 26 meetings within the next three public body meetings as that 
was the later of the two time periods.  See 940 CMR 29.11; OML 2020-30.  Where the

Committee approved these minutes on October 14, which was its third meeting thereafter, we 
find that the Committee approved these minutes in a timely manner and thus did not violate the 
Open Meeting Law.  See OML 2018-111.  The Open Meeting Law does not require that public 
bodies post their minutes to a website or elsewhere, but only that minutes be approved in a 
timely manner and made available within 10 days of a request.4  G.L. c. 30A, § 22(c); see OML 
2017-98; OML 2017-25.   
 
 For the reasons stated above, we find that the Committee did not violate the Open 
Meeting Law.  We now consider the complaint addressed by this determination to be resolved.  
This determination does not address any other complaints that may be pending with the
