RE: Open Meeting Law Complaint 
Dear Attorney Feodoroff: 
This office received a complaint from Stephen Morgan on February 21, 2017, alleging 
that the Ashland Board of Selectmen (the "Board") violated the Open Meeting Law, G.L. c. 
30A, §§ 18-25. The complaint was originally filed with the Board on or about January 31, 
2017, and you responded, on behalf of the Board, by letter dated February 17, 2017, In his 
complaint, Mr. Morgan alleges that the Board failed to post notice and create minutes of a 
meeting held on December 8, 2016. 
Following our review, we resolve this matter by informal action, in accordance with 
940 CMR 29.07(2)(a). We find that the Board violated the Open Meeting Law as alleged in 
the complaint. However, because the Board took appropriate remedial action in response to 
the complaint, we order no further action. 
The Town has been engaged in a dialogue with five surrounding towns about 
regionalizing resources, which is a topic within the Board's jurisdiction. The towns scheduled 
—armeeting on December ^, 2016 to continue that discussion. -Because only two Board 
members indicated to the Town Manager that they would attend, the Board did not expect a 
quorum and thus did not post notice for the meeting. However, four of the five members, and 
hence a quorum, attended the meeting. 
Because a quorum of the Board attended and actively participated at the December 8 
regionalization meeting, we find that the Board violated the Open Meeting Law by failing to 
notice the event as a meeting of the Board. See G.L. c. 30A, §§18, 20; OML 2017-24; OML
