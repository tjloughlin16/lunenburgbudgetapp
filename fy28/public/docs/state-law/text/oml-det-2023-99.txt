RE:  Open Meeting Law Complaints 
 
Dear Attorney Corridan: 
 
This office received a complaint from Derek Dobosz on March 9, 2023, and a complaint 
from Glenn LaPlante on March 13, 2023, alleging that the Chicopee Planning Board (the 
“Board”) violated the Open Meeting Law, G.L. c. 30A, §§ 18-25.  Mr. Dobosz’s complaint was 
originally filed with the Board on February 7, 2023; Mr. LaPlante’s complaint was originally 
field with the Board on February 6, 2023; and you responded to both complaints, on behalf of the 
Board, by separate letters dated February 24, 2023.  Both complaints allege that the Board 
meeting held on February 2, 2023, was not accessible to the public. 
 
We resolve this matte
 r by informal action, in accordance with 940 CMR 29.07(2)(a), 
after reviewing the original complaints, the Board’s responses to the complaints, the complaints 
filed with our office requesting further review, and the notice and minutes of the Board meeting 
held on February 2, 2023, as well as an audio recording of that meeting.1  Following our review, 
we find that the Board violated the Open Meeting Law in the way alleged.   
 
The Open Meeting Law was enacted “to eliminate much of the secrecy surrounding 
del
iberations and decisions on which public policy is based.”  Ghiglione v. School Committee of 
Southbridge, 376 Mass. 70, 72 (1978).  The law requires that meetings of a public body be 
properly noticed and open to members of the public, unless an executive session is convened.  
See G.L. c. 30A, §§ 20(a)-(b), 21.  Access to a meeting must include the opportunity to be

physically present, as well as to see and hear what is being discussed by the members of the 
public body.  See OML 2017-135; OML 2015-38; OML 2014-3; OML 2013-189.2  In order for a 
meeting to be truly “open to the public,” the meeting venue must be accessible to the public for 
the duration of the meeting.  See G.L. c. 30A, § 20(a); OML 2017-83.   
 
Here, the Board duly posted notice for a meeting to be held on February 2, 2023, at 7:00 
P.M.  T
he notice listed the location of the meeting as Chambers Conference Room, 4th Floor, 
City Hall Annex, 274 Front Street in Chicopee and “via video conference (Zoom).”  However, 
the notice did not provide information necessary to access the virtual meeting, such as a link to 
the Zoom meeting, telephone numbers the public could use to dial into the meeting, the Webinar 
ID number required to log into the meeting, or clear instructions for how to obtain the access 
information. 
 
The February 2, 2023, meeting was held as planned and Board members were present at 
the Cha
mbers Conference Room in the City Hall Annex, the physical meeting location.  Both 
complainants state that when they arrived at the meeting location, the door was locked.  Mr. 
LaPlante waited outside until someone let him in the building; Mr. Dobosz, as a City Councilor, 
used his key to the building to open the door and let himself and others in.  The Board does not 
dispute that the door was locked “intermittently” but suggests that the Board was “not directly 
culpable” because the operation of the front door to City Hall “has been run through the City’s 
Office of Occupational Health & Safety” which “failed to properly schedule a Clerk to staff the 
front door.”  We remind the Board that it is responsible for ensuring compliance with the Open 
Meeting Law and that meetings are accessible and open to members of the public.  Moreover, we 
note our concern that the Board was advised by a member of the public approximately 30 
minutes after the meeting began that the door to the building was locked and individuals were 
unable to access the meeting.  Although both Mr. Dobosz and Mr. LaPlante were eventually able 
to gain access to the building, other individuals were not able to gain access and subsequently 
left the meeting location.  We thus find that the February 2, 2023, meeting was not “open” to the 
public as the door to the City Hall Annex was locked and the Board violated the Open Meeting 
Law where the public was unable to gain access to the meeting location.  See OML 2022-88; 
OML 2014-118; OML 2012-46.   
 
The Board suggests that the meeting was available via Zoom such that individuals could 
have a
ccessed the meeting remotely.  We have previously stated that when all public body 
members participate in a meeting remotely or when a public body meets in person without 
providing the public physical access to the meeting location, the public body must then provide 
the public with adequate, alternative access to the meeting, which requires access to the 
proceedings in real time.  See OML 2020-111; OML 2020-100; Chapter 20 of the Acts of 2021, 
§ 20(b).  However, a public body may hold in-person meetings, as long as access to the meeting 
includes the opportunity to be physically present, as well as to see and hear what is being 
discussed by the members of the public body.  See OML 2021-148; OML 2020-100; OML 2017-
135; OML 2016-54.  Although all members of the Board were present in the physical meeting 
location, Chambers Conference Room at the City Hall Annex, the doors to the City Hall Annex

were locked such that members of the public could not access the meeting. Moreover, we find
that the notice failed to include information necessary to access the Zoom meeting such that
virtual access to the meeting was also inaccessible.
For the reasons stated above, we find that the Board violated the Open Meeting Law by
not ensuring that the meeting held on February 2, 2023, was accessible to the public. We order
the Board's immediate and future compliance with the law's requirements, and caution the Board
that a similar, future violation may be considered evidence of intent to violate the law. Although
we find a violation, we do not order remedial action here where the topic of interest to both Mr.
Dobosz and Mr. LaPlante must ultimately be approved by the City Council after a public hearing
and only received the Board's "recommend/ed] approval" to the City Council during the
February 2, 2023, meeting, Mr. Dobosz and Mr. LaPlante gained access to the building prior to
the Board's discussions of the topic, and the Board has committed to ensuring that the doors to
the building will remain unlocked until meetings have been adjourned.
We now consider the complaints addressed by this determination to be resolved. This
determination does not address any other complaints that may be pending with our office or the
Board. Please feel free to contact our office at (617) 963-2540 if you have any questions
regarding this letter.
