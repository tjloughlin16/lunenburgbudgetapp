RE: Open Meeting Law Complaint
Dear Mr. D’Agostino:
This office received a complaint from Daniel Grayton on August 29, 2018, alleging 
that the Methuen City Council (the “Council”) violated the Open Meeting Law, G.L. c. 
30A, §§ 18-25. The complaint was originally filed with the Council on or about June 14, 
2018, and you responded to the complaint, on behalf of the Council, in a letter dated 
August 27, 2018. In his complaint, Mr. Grayton alleges that the Council failed to post 
notice of a meeting held on June 13, 2018 that had been continued from the previous day’s 
meeting.
Following our review, we find that the Council violated the Open Meeting Law in 
the manner alleged. In reaching a determination, we reviewed the original complaint, the 
Council’s response to the complaint, and the complaint filed with our office requesting 
further review. We also reviewed the notice and minutes of the Council’s June 13, 2018 
meeting.
FACTS
We find the facts as follows. On June 12, 2018, the Council held a meeting during 
which it convened a public hearing concerning the Fiscal Year 2019 Water and Sewer 
Enterprise Fund. Upon the conclusion of the hearing that evening, the Council voted to 
“recess” or continue the hearing until 6:00 P.M. on the following day, June 13, 2018. The 
Council posted notice of this continued meeting on June 13, 2018 at 8:20 A.M.

DISCUSSION
The Open Meeting Law requires that a public body post notice of any meeting at 
least 48 hours prior to such meeting. G.L. c. 30A, § 20(b). A “meeting” is defined as “a 
deliberation by a public body with respect to any matter within the body’s jurisdiction.” 
G.L. c. 30A, § 18. For purposes of the Open Meeting Law, “deliberation” is defined, in 
part, as “an oral or written communication through any medium, including electronic mail, 
between or among a quorum of a public body on any public business within its 
jurisdiction.” Id.
Here, the Council recessed or continued its June 12, 2018 meeting to the following 
evening, on June 13, 2018. The Council posted notice of the continued meeting on the 
morning of the meeting. This late posting violated the Open Meeting Law’s requirement 
that a public body provide the public with at least 48 hours’ notice of a meeting.1 Our 
office has advised public bodies that, where a public body decides to continue a discussion 
to another time, the Open Meeting Law requires that the public body treat the continued 
discussion as though it is a new meeting for purposes of notice posting. See Attorney 
General’s Frequently Asked Questions at https://www.mass.gov/service-details/frequently- 
asked-questions-about-the-open-meeting-law-meetings-notices-and-minutes.2 We find that 
the Council violated the Open Meeting Law by providing the public with roughly nine and 
a half hours’ notice of the continued meeting. See OML 2016-85; OML 2014-6; OML 
2013-149.3 Because the Council acted upon its attorney’s advice by posting notice on the 
morning of the meeting, we decline to find its actions were intentional and take the 
opportunity to remind the Council of this notice posting requirement.
CONCLUSION
For the reasons stated above, we find that the Council violated the Open Meeting 
Law by failing to post notice at least 48 hours in advance of its June 13, 2018 meeting. We 
order immediate and future compliance with the Open Meeting Law’s requirements, and 
we caution that similar future violations may be considered evidence of intent to violate the 
law.
1 We note that the Open Meeting Law does not govern the requirements for conducting public hearings. See 
OML Declination 1-29-15 (Holbrook Planning Board). We therefore offer no opinion on whether the 
posting at issue complies with applicable laws which govern the hearing. Id.

Yes, the public body must treat the meeting as though it is a new meeting for purposes of notice posting. The
