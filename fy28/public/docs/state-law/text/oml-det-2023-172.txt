Dear Attorney Driscoll: 
 
This office received a complaint from Levia Grace on July 21, 2023, alleging that the 
Stoneham Housing Authority Board of Commissioners (the “Board”) violated the Open Meeting 
Law, G.L. c. 30A, §§ 18-25.1  The complaint was originally filed with the Board on June 13, and 
you responded to the complaint, on behalf of the Board, by letter dated June 21.  The complaint 
alleges that the Board failed to properly post notice of meetings. 
 
We resolve this matter by informal action, in acc
 ordance with 940 CMR 29.07(2)(a), 
after reviewing the original complaint, the Board’s response to the complaint, and the complaint 
filed with our office requesting further review.  We find that the Board did not violate the Open 
Meeting Law in the way alleged.   
 
The Open Meeting Law requires that, except in an emergency, “a public body shall post 
notice
 of every meeting at least 48 hours prior to such meeting, excluding Saturdays, Sundays 
and legal holidays.”  G.L. c. 30A, § 20(b).  The notice must be printed in a legible, easily 
understandable format and must contain the date, time and place of the meeting, as well as a 
listing of topics that the chair reasonably anticipates will be discussed.  Id.  The list of topics 
shall have “sufficient specificity to reasonably advise the public of the issues to be discussed at 
the meeting.”  940 CMR 29.03(l)(b).

For local public bodies, notice shall be filed with the municipal clerk and posted in a 
manner
 conspicuously visible to the public at all hours in or on the municipal building in which 
the clerk’s office is located.  G.L. c. 30A, § 20(c).  The governing board of a local housing 
authority “shall be deemed a local public body.”  G.L. c. 30A, § 18.  Alternatively, a 
municipality may designate a website as its official notice posting location for all public bodies 
within the municipality.  940 CMR 29.03(2)(b).  The Town of Stoneham has adopted its website, 
http://www.stoneham-ma.gov, as the official method of posting notice.   
 
The complaint alleges that the Board did not properly post notice of meetings held 
between August 2022 and June 2023.  Complaints must allege violations with a degree of 
specificity, as our office will not conduct broad audits of public bodies based on generalized 
allegations.  See OML 2013-180; OML 2012-106.
2  Nevertheless, we have reviewed the notices 
posted by the Board for meetings held between August 2022 and June 2023 and note that the 
notices were posted to the calendar page on the municipal website, and that the notices included 
the information required by the Open Meeting Law, including the date, time and place of the 
meeting, as well as a list of topics.
 
 
We acknowledge that state regulations require local housing authorities to create and 
main
tain “a website or web page” so that individuals “have a reasonably convenient means to 
communicate with the [local housing authority] and to take steps necessary to access the [local 
housing authority’s] programs and opportunities.”  See 760 CMR 4.02.  Information to be posted 
by the local housing authority shall include: “office hours, the names, addresses, phone numbers, 
email addresses or other means of contact for the [local housing authority’s] Executive Director 
and Assistant Director (if any), all [local housing authority] board members, and senior staff; 
information for tenants on how to report maintenance problems; and copies of its Annual Plan.”  
Id.  However, as a local public body, the Board must also comply with the Open Meeting Law, 
which requires that notices be “posted in a manner conspicuously visible to the public at all 
hours in or on the municipal building in which the clerk’s office is located,” or alternatively, on 
the municipal website if the municipality has adopted the municipal website as the official 
method of posting notice.  G.L. c. 30A, § 20(c); 940 CMR 29.03(2)(b).  Although public bodies 
are welcome to post notice of their meetings in multiple locations if they choose, a local public 
body has one official notice posting location for purposes of complying with the Open Meeting 
Law.  See 940 CMR 29.03(2)(b)(3) & (c); OML 2021-94; OML 2020-20.  We find that the 
Board did not violate the Open Meeting Law where notices were posted to the municipal 
website, the official posting location for all public bodies in the Town of Stoneham.

On June 16, 2021, Governor Charles Baker signed into law An Act Relative to Extending 
Certain COVID-19 Measures Adopted During the State of Emergency, Chapter 20 of the Acts of 
2021 (“Chapter 20”).
 4  Relevant here, Section 20 of Chapter 20 suspended the requirements 
under the Open Meeting Law and the Attorney General’s Open Meeting Law regulations that a 
quorum of a public body, including the chair, be physically present at the meeting location and 
allows all members of a public body to participate in a meeting remotely, provided that the 
public body provide “adequate, alternative means” of public access, such as through telephone, 
internet or satellite enabled audio or video conferencing or any other technology that enables the 
public to clearly follow the proceedings of the public body while those activities are occurring.  
See Chapter 20, § 20(b); OML 2021-135.  Section 20 allows public bodies to provide the public 
with “adequate, alternative” access to meetings instead of providing in-person access but does 
not require public bodies to provide “adequate, alternative” access if in-person access is 
provided. 
 
Here,
 the notices included only one location for the public to access the meetings, 11 
Parker Chase Road in Stoneham, and the physical meeting location was accessible to any 
individual who wished to attend the meetings and Board members were present at this location.  
Because we have consistently stated that the Executive Order and Section 20 of Chapter 20 do 
not require that a public body provide remote access to its meetings if those meetings are instead 
held in-person at a location that is open and physically accessible to the public, we find that the 
Board did not violate the Open Meeting Law here where it provided in-person access.
