 RE: Open Meeting Law Complaint 
 
Dear Ms. Makepeace-O’Neil: 
 
This office received a complaint from Amanda Alix on February 29, 2024, alleging that 
the Shutesbury Select Board (the “Board”) violated the Open Meeting Law, G.L. c. 30A, §§ 18-
25.  The complaint was originally filed with the Board on or about January 25, 2024, and the 
Board responded to the complaint by letter dated February 1, 2024.  The complaint alleges that 
the Board posted a notice with insufficient specificity for a meeting held on January 2, 2024, by 
failing to include the name of the applicant to be appointed as the Assistant Town Clerk. 
 
Following our review, we find that the Board did not violate the Open Meeting Law in 
the way alleged.  In reaching this determination, we reviewed the original complaint, the Board’s 
response to the complaint, the complaint filed with our office requesting further review, and the 
notice, minutes and video recording of the Board meeting held on January 2, 2024.
1 
 
The Open Meeting Law requires that, except in an emergency, “a public body shall post 
notice of every meeting at least 48 hours prior to such meeting, excluding Saturdays, Sundays 
and legal holidays.”  G.L. c. 30A, § 20(b).  The notice must be printed in a legible, easily 
understandable format and must contain the date, time and place of the meeting, as well as a 
listing of topics that the chair reasonably anticipates will be discussed.  Id.  The list of topics 
shall have “sufficient specificity to reasonably advise the public of the issues to be discussed at 
the meeting.”  940 CMR 29.03(l)(b).  We generally consider a topic to be sufficiently specific

when a reasonable member of the public could read the topic and understand the anticipated 
nature of the public body’s discussion.  See OML 2019-70; OML 2015-35; OML 2013-39.2   
 
The Board posted notice for a meeting to be held on January 2, 2024, at 5:30 PM.  The 
notice listed ten topics, one of which was “Review and Appointment of Assistant Town Clerk.”  
The January 2, 2024, meeting was held as planned and the Board heard from Town Clerk Grace 
Bannasch who recommended the appointment of Janet Kiely as Assistant Town Clerk.  The 
Board then unanimously voted to appoint Janet Kiely as Assistant Town Clerk. 
 
We have previously stated that when a public body anticipates making appointments at a 
meeting, the meeting notice should include specific details about what appointments will be 
made and the names of applicants for employment or appointment which are know 48 hours in 
advance of a meeting.  See OML 2020-80; OML 2017-168; OML 2015-196; OML 2014-155.  
Here, the Board asserts that it did not know the name of the candidate to be appointed as 
Assistant Town Clerk at the time the notice was posted but rather learned the name of the 
individual when the Town Clerk specifically recommended that the Board appoint Ms. Kiely 
during the January 2, 2024, meeting.  Therefore, we find that the topic “Review and 
Appointment of Assistant Town Clerk” as listed on the notice included sufficient specificity.  
See OML 2019-128. 
 
For the reasons stated above, we find that the Board did not violate the Open Meeting 
Law.  We now consider the complaint addressed by this determination to be resolved.  This 
determination does not address any other complaints that may be pending with the Board or with 
our office.  Please feel free to contact our office at (617) 963-2540 if you have any questions 
regarding this letter.
