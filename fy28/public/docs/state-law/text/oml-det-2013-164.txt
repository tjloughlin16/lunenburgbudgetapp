RE: Open Meeting Law Complaint 
Dear Mr. Colter: 
This office received a complaint from Deborah Nicholson, dated April 14, 2013, alleging 
that the Ashfield Board of Health (the "Board") violated the Open Meeting Law, G.L. c. 30A, §§ 
18-25. Ms. Nicholson's complaint was originally filed with the Board on or about January 22, 
2013, and the Board responded by letter dated January 29, 2013. In her complaint, Ms. 
Nicholson alleges that the notice of the Board's January 22, 2013 meeting lacked sufficient 
specificity because it failed to include any detail about an executive session topic. 
We reviewed the January 22, 2013 complaint; the Board's January 29, 2013 response; 
and the April 14, 2013 complaint filed with our office, requesting further review. We also 
reviewed the notice and minutes from the Board's January 22, 2013 meeting. 
Following our review, we find that the Board did not violate the Open Meeting Law by 
failing to list a topic with sufficient specificity because no discussion about the topic at issue 
occurred. 
FACTS 
Based upon our review of the material listed above, the facts are as follows. On January 
22, 2013, the Board convened a meeting in open session. The meeting notice, posted on January 
17, 2013, listed among its topics the following: "Executive session if needed." According to the 
meeting minutes, during the meeting Chairman Colter raised the issue of convening a joint 
executive session with the Ashfield Board of Selectmen. At that point, Suzanne Corbett, a 
member of the public, submitted this Open Meeting Law complaint on behalf of the complainant. 
Board member Susan Clark then suggested that the executive session be postponed until another 
meeting. Accordingly, the Board did not convene in executive session that evening.

DISCUSSION 
The Open Meeting Law requires that all meeting notices contain the date, time, and place 
of the meeting and a listing of topics that the chair reasonably anticipates will be discussed. G.L. 
c. 30A, § 20(b). For an executive session topic, the notice must state the purpose for the 
executive session, stating all subjects that may be revealed without compromising the purpose 
for which the executive session was called. See G.L. c. 30A, § 21(b)(3); 940 CMR 29,03(l)(b); 
OML 2012-89. The topic "Executive session if needed" was not sufficiently specific to satisfy 
these requirements. However, in response to the complainant's concerns, the Board ultimately 
chose not to hold an executive session during its January 22, 2013 meeting. We therefore find 
no violation of the Open Meeting Law, but remind the Board of its obligation to list all 
anticipated topics with sufficient specificity in its meeting notices.1 
For the reasons stated above, we find that the Board did not violate the Open Meeting 
Law. We now consider the complaint addressed by this determination to be resolved. This 
determination does not address any other complaints which may be pending with our office or 
the Board. Please feel free to contact our office at (617) 963-2540 if you have any questions 
regarding this letter.
