 RE:        Open Meeting Law Complaint 
 
Dear Attorney Goldberg: 
 
This office received a complaint from David Calhoun on August 19, 2025, alleging that 
the Weston Select Board (the “Board”) violated the Open Meeting Law, G.L. c. 30A, §§ 18-25.  
The complaint was originally filed with the Board on or about July 18, 2025, and you responded 
to the complaint, on behalf of the Board, by letter dated July 30, 2025.  The complaint alleges 
that i) Board members failed to state that no other person was present while participating 
remotely in the June 24 and July 8, 2025, executive sessions, ii) the Board allowed town 
employees to participate in the executive session held on June 24, 2025, and iii) the Chair had a 
conflict of interest and failed to recuse herself during the June 24 and July 8, 2025, executive 
sessions. 
 
We appreciate the patience of the parties while we reviewed this matter.  Following our 
review, we find a de minimis violation of the Open Meeting Law when members failed to state 
that no other person was present when participating remotely in the July 8, 2025, executive 
session.  We find that the Board did not violate the law in the other ways alleged.  In reaching 
this determination, we reviewed the original complaint, the Board’s response to the complaint, 
and the complaint filed with our office requesting further review.  In addition, we reviewed the 
notices and open session minutes of the Board meetings held on June 24 and July 8, 2025, as 
well as video recordings of the open session portions of those two meetings.

FACTS 
 
We find the facts as follows.  The Board is a five-member public body.  On June 20, 
2025, at 12:50 P.M., the Board posted notice of a meeting to be held on June 24, 2025, at 6:00 
P.M.  The notice listed eight open session topics and an executive session topic “to discuss 
strategy with respect to litigation strategy with respect to MBTA Communities Act (3A), and 
City of Cambridge vs. The Housing Appeals Committee, and pursuant to the provisions of 30A, 
§ 21(a)(3), . . . to discuss strategy with respect to collective bargaining with union employees, 
and the Chair so declares that having a discussion in open session would have a detrimental 
effect on the Board’s litigating position.” 
 
The June 24, 2025, meeting was held as planned.  Three of the five Board members were 
present in the physical meeting location in the Select Board meeting room at Town Hall; the 
remaining two Board members were absent from the meeting.  The Board first convened in open 
session and Chair Lise Revers announced that the Board would convene in executive session 
stating the purposes as they were listed on the notice.  Chair Revers further announced that 
having such discussions would have a detrimental impact on the Board’s litigating position.  The 
Board then unanimously voted by roll call to convene in executive session.  The Board returned 
to open session approximately 45 minutes later and discussed the remaining topics listed on the 
notice.  
 
On July 3, 2025, at 10:12 A.M., the Board posted notice of a meeting to be held on July 
8, 2025, at 6:00 P.M.  The notice listed eight open session topics and an executive session topic 
pursuant to Purpose 3 “to discuss strategy with respect to litigation : City of Cambridge vs. The 
Housing Appeals Committee, and the Chair so declares that having a discussion in open session 
would have a detrimental effect on the Board’s litigating position.” 
 
The July 8, 2025, meeting was held as planned.  Three Board members participated 
remotely in the meeting, while the remaining two Board members were absent from the meeting.  
The Board first convened in open session, Chair Revers announced that the Board would 
convene in executive session stating the purpose as it was listed on the notice, and then 
announced that having such discussions would have a detrimental impact on the Board’s 
litigating position.  Chair Revers further advised that the Board would return to open session 
following the conclusion of the executive session.  The Board unanimously voted by roll call to 
convene in executive session.  The Board returned to open session approximately 30 minutes 
later and discussed the remaining topics listed on the notice.  
 
DISCUSSION 
 
The Open Meeting Law was enacted “to eliminate much of the secrecy surrounding 
deliberations and decisions on which public policy is based.”  Ghiglione v. School Comm. of 
Southbridge, 376 Mass. 70, 72 (1978).  The Open Meeting Law requires that all meetings of a 
public body be conducted in an open session, with some exceptions.  G.L. c. 30A, §§ 20(a), 
21(a).  Public bodies may enter a closed, executive session for any of the ten purposes

enumerated in the Open Meeting Law provided that it has first convened in an open session; the 
chair has announced the purpose for the executive session, stating all subjects that may be 
revealed without compromising the purpose for the executive session; a majority of members of 
the body have voted to go into executive session; the vote of each member is recorded by roll 
call and entered into the minutes; and the chair has publicly announced whether the open session 
will reconvene at the conclusion of the executive session.  G.L. c. 30A, §§ 21(a), (b); see also 
OML 2014-94.2   
 
A public body may enter executive session to “discuss strategy with respect to collective 
bargaining or litigation if an open meeting may have a detrimental effect on the bargaining or 
litigating position of the public body and the chair so declares.”  G.L. c. 30A, § 21(a)(3) 
(“Purpose 3”).  When convening in executive session pursuant to Purpose 3, a public body must 
identify the collective bargaining unit with whom it will be negotiating or the litigation matter it 
will discuss, if doing so will not compromise the lawful purpose for secrecy.  See OML 2019-37; 
2018-139; OML 2016-43; OML 2016-12; OML 2015-87. 
 
The Attorney General’s Open Meeting Law regulations require that a public body 
“member participating remotely may participate in an executive session, but shall state at the 
start of any such session that no other person is present and/or able to hear the discussion at the 
remote location.”  940 CMR 29.10(7)(d).  This statement does not have to occur in open session 
but may be made at the beginning of the executive session.  Id.  Finally, although neither the 
Open Meeting Law nor the Attorney General’s regulations expressly require that this declaration 
be documented in the minutes, we encourage public bodies to record such statement in the 
minutes since that is the only way for members of the public to know that the declaration was 
made.  See OML 2020-59. 
 
The complaint alleges that Board members failed to state that no other person was present 
while participating remotely in the June 24 and July 8, 2025, executive sessions.  Here, Board 
members did not participate remotely during the June 24, 2025, meeting but rather were present 
in the physical meeting location at Town Hall.  As such, members were not required to state at 
the start of the executive session that no other person was present or able to hear the discussions.  
However, members participated remotely during the July 8, 2025, meeting.  The Board concedes 
that all three members who participated remotely during the July 8, 2025, executive session did 
not make the required announcement, either in open session prior to entering the executive 
session or at the beginning of the executive session.  Therefore, we find that the Board violated 
the Open Meeting Law when the members failed to make the disclosure, though the Board has 
confirmed that no unauthorized person was present for the executive session, and thus we find 
this violation was de minimis.  See OML 2022-46. 
 
Next, the complaint alleges that the Board allowed town employees to participate in the 
executive session held on June 24, 2025.  The Board states that it invited the Town Manager and 
Assistant Town Manager to the June 24, 2025, executive session, because they are “key 
members of its collective bargaining negotiation team.”  A public body may invite an individual

to attend or participate in an executive session if the public body believes that doing so would 
assist the public body in its work and would not undermine the stated purpose for meeting in 
executive session.  See OML 2020-133, n.2; OML 2021-99.  Therefore, we find that the Board 
did not violate the Open Meeting Law by inviting the Town Manager and Assistant Town 
Manager to attend its June 24, 2025, executive session.  
 
Finally, the complaint alleges that the Chair had a conflict of interest and failed to recuse 
herself during the June 24 and July 8, 2025, meetings when the Board discussed the litigation 
matter involving the Housing Appeals Committee in executive session.  The Division of Open 
Government’s statutory authority concerns compliance only with the Open Meeting Law, G.L. c. 
30A, §§ 18-25.  Even if true, this allegation would not constitute a violation of the Open Meeting 
Law.  See OML Declination 11-3-2023 (Massachusetts Board of Elementary and Secondary 
Education) (Division of Open Government does not review allegations of conflicts of interest); 
OML 2020-36 (allegation of unprofessional conduct does not implicate Open Meeting Law).  
Therefore, we decline to review this allegation and refer the complainant to the State Ethics 
Commission, which enforces the Commonwealth’s Conflict of Interest Law.  See OML 2020-72; 
OML 2013-54. 
 
CONCLUSION 
 
For the reasons stated above, we find a de minimis violation of the Open Meeting Law 
when Board members failed to state that no other person was present when participating 
remotely in the July 8, 2025, executive session.  We order the Board’s immediate and future 
compliance with the law’s requirements, and we caution that similar future violations could be 
considered evidence of intent to violate the law.   
 
We now consider the complaint addressed by this determination to be resolved.  This 
determination does not address any other complaints that may be pending with our office or the 
Board.  Please feel free to contact our office at (617) 963-2540 if you have any questions 
regarding this letter.
