RE: Open Meeting Law Complaint 
Dear Attorney Goldberg: 
Our office received a complaint by Denise Riseborough, dated April 5, 2012, alleging a 
violation of the Open Meeting Law, G.L. c. 30A, §§18-25, by the Nantucket Board of Selectmen 
(the "Select Board") and the Nantucket Planning Board (the "Planning Board"). Specifically, the 
complaint alleges that the Select Board and Planning Board met in a joint executive session on 
February 24, 2012, although no valid executive session purpose applied to the Boards' 
discussion. The complaint was initially filed with the Board on or about March 5, 2012. The 
Boards responded to the complaint by letter dated March 23, 2012. 
Following our review, we find that Boards did not violate the Open Meeting Law by 
discussing a zoning appeal in executive session. In reaching this determination, we reviewed the 
March 5, 2012 complaint; the Boards' March 23, 2012 response; and the April 5, 2012 complaint 
filed with our office. Additionally, we reviewed the notice and open and executive session 
minutes of the Boards' February 24, 2012 joint meeting. 
FACTS 
On January 27, 2012, the Nantucket Building Commissioner issued Ms. Riseborough a 
use permit to operate a home occupation business, namely Nantucket Pizza. On February 22, 
2012, the two Boards posted a joint notice for their meeting on February 24, 2012. One topic in 
the notice was listed as "Poll Members to hold Executive Session, not to return to Open Session, 
to discuss a strategy for potential appeal of the issuance of a Use Permit for a Home Occupation 
at 17 Chuck Hollow Road — Nantucket Pizza." On February 24, 2012, the Select Board and the 
Planning Board held its joint meeting. Following a period of public comment in which residents 
expressed their disapproval of Ms. Riseborough conducting a business in a residential

neighborhood, the Boards entered into executive session. The Planning Board minutes read that 
"A motion was made, and duly seconded, by the Nantucket Planning Board, to move into 
Executive Session to discuss strategy with respect to litigation and the appeal of the issuance of 
Building Permit #94-12, issued on January 27, 2012 to Denise Riseborough for property located 
at 17 Chuck Hollow Road and to not reconvene in Open Session." The Boards then approved 
the motion unanimously. 
We have reviewed the minutes of the executive session, in camera, but because they have 
not yet been publicly released by the Boards, we cannot recount their content here. 
On February 27, 2012, the Nantucket Planning Office served Ms. Riseborough with a 
notice of appeal of her permit. The notice was accompanied by a letter, dated February 24, 2012, 
signed by the Select Board and Planning Board Chairs, that described the reasons for the appeal. 
DISCUSSION 
A public body may enter executive, or closed, session for any of ten enumerated 
purposes. G.L. c. 30A, § 21. One such purpose is Purpose 3, "No discuss strategy with respect 
to collective bargaining or litigation if an open meeting may have a detrimental effect on the 
bargaining or litigating position of the public body and the chair so declares." Id. at § 21(a)(3). 
The public body invoking this executive session purpose must have a bargaining or litigating 
position to protect, and must declare in open session that having this discussion in open session 
may have a detrimental effect on that position. G.L. c. 30A, § 21(a)(3); OML 2011-47.1  The 
mere possibility of litigation is not sufficient to invoke executive session Purpose 3. Litigation 
must be pending or clearly and imminently threatened or otherwise demonstrably likely. See 
Perryman v. School Committee of Boston,  17 Mass. App. Ct. 346, 352 (1983); see also Attorney 
General's Open Meeting Law Guide, February 10, 2012 at page 8. 
The Boards entered into executive session under Purpose 3 during the February 24, 2012 
meeting to consider whether to appeal Ms. Riseborough's permit to the Nantucket Zoning Board 
of Appeals. A decision to pursue an administrative appeal is a litigation decision. See OML 
2012-81. Here, the Boards met in executive session to discuss whether to pursue an appeal of the 
issuance of a building permit to the Nantucket Zoning Board of Appeals. The litigation, 
although not pending, was imminently threatened by the Boards and demonstrably likely. 
Therefore, we find that the Boards appropriately entered into executive session under Purpose 3 
to discuss their litigation strategy. 
CONCLUSION 
For the reasons stated above, we find that the Boards did not violate the Open Meeting 
Law. 
We now consider this complaint resolved and this matter closed. This letter does not 
resolve any other complaints which may have been filed with our office or the Boards. Please 
contact me if you have any questions.
