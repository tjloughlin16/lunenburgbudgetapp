This office received a complaint from Bryan Dumont, dated April 6, 2017, alleging that 
the Shirley Board of Selectmen (the "Board") violated the Open Meeting Law, G.L. c. 3OA, §§ 
18-25. The complaint was originally filed with the Board on March 1, 2017, and the Board 
responded by letter dated March 23, 2017. In his complaint, Mr. Dumont alleges that the Board 
failed to list an anticipated topic on a meeting notice with sufficient detail.1 
Following our review, we find that the Board did not violate the Open Meeting Law. In 
reaching a determination, we reviewed the original complaint, the Board's response, and the 
request for further review filed with our office. We also reviewed all documentation provided 
with these filings, including the notice and minutes of a Board meeting held on February 27, 
2017. We reviewed a video recording of this same meeting,2 Finally, we spoke with the 
complainant by telephone on April 21, 2017, and we spoke with legal counsel for the Board by 
telephone on April 25, 2017.3

FACTS 
We find the facts as follows. The Board posted notice of a meeting to be held on 
February 27, 2017. The notice listed several items under "Old Business" and "New Business." 
One such item listed under "New Business" was, "Safety at Work Report (Heartle), Discussion." 
This item referred to an investigative report concerning allegations made against the police chief. 
This report had been conducted by investigator Jean Haertl of Safety and Respect at Work, LLC, 
The Board convened its February 27, 2017 meeting as planned. During the discussion of 
the investigative report, the chair introduced three motions—(i) to suspend the police chief with 
pay; (ii) to elevate a replacement to temporarily assume the role of police chief; and (iii) to 
conduct further investigation into certain allegations raised in the report. The Board voted in the 
affirmative on all three. 
DISCUSSION 
The Open Meeting Law requires public bodies to post notice of all meetings at least 48 
hours in advance, not including Saturdays, Sundays, and legal holidays. G.L. c. 30A, § 20(b). 
Such notice must include "a listing of topics that the chair reasonably anticipates will be 
discussed at the meeting." Id. Topics must be listed with sufficient detail such that a reasonable 
member of the public could read the notice and understand what the body will discuss. 940 
OMR 29.03(l)(b); OML 2015-35. 
Here, the complaint alleges that the Board's February 27, 2017 meeting notice lack 
sufficient detail to adequately describe the anticipated discussion. Indeed, we agree with the 
complainant that the notice topic was deficient in certain respects. The notice, on its face, failed 
to identify the police chief and did not specify any anticipated action by the Board. Further, the 
investigator's name was misspelled and the company's name was misstated. However, we note 
that the matter of this investigative report had received significant media coverage prior to the 
February 27, 2017 meeting. See, e.g., Joe Atmonavage, Probe report faults Shirley chief on fired 
officer, The Lowell Sun, Dec. 12, 2016.4 Because the notice identified the report to be discussed, 
and because a reasonable member of the public could have read the notice and understood the 
context of the report, we find that the notice satisfied the Board's minimum obligations under the 
Open Meeting Law. See OML 2016-39. While we agree with the complainant that the inclusion 
of additional information about the underlying situation would have been helpful for members of 
the public, we find that the discussion and the ensuing votes were within the scope of the topic as 
listed on the meeting notice. See OML 2016-92. Accordingly, we find no violation of the Open 
Meeting Law. Nevertheless, we advise the Board that the inclusion of additional information on 
future meeting notices will better inform members of the public as to what the Board will discuss 
at its meetings. 
CONCLUSION 
For the reasons stated above, we find that the Board did not violate the Open Meeting 
Law. We now consider the complaint addressed by this determination to be resolved. This
