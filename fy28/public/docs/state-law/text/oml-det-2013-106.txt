This office received an Open Meeting Law complaint from Ms. Sheryl A McMahon on 
behalf of the Dennis Water District, dated September 28, 2012, alleging that the Barnstable 
County Retirement Advisory Council (the "Council") violated the Open Meeting Law, G.L. c. 
3OA, §§ 18-25. The complaint specifically alleges that on September 19, 2012, the Council held 
a meeting that did not include a quorum of its members. The complaint requests that the action 
taken at the meeting be rescinded and considered at a new meeting that includes a quorum of the 
Council. The complaint was originally filed with the Council on or about September 19, 2012. 
The Council responded to the complaint by letter dated September 26, 2012. 
Following our review, we find that the Council did not violate the Open Meeting Law. 
We make no finding with respect to the legal validity of the Council's actions. The Division of 
Open Government only enforces the Open Meeting Law, thus we decline to issue an opinion on 
whether the Council members' actions violated any other laws or Council policies. See OML 
2012-47.1 In reaching this determination, we reviewed the September 19, 2012 complaint filed 
with the Council; the Council's September 26, 2012 response; and the September 28, 2012 
complaint filed with our office. Additionally, we reviewed a July 31, 2012 memorandum from 
Barnstable County Retirement Association Executive Director Debra L. Cohen to the Council, 
and a September 13, 2012 email from Ms. Cohen to the Council. We also reviewed a December 
11, 2012 letter from you to our office. Finally, we reviewed the notice and minutes of the 
Council's September 19, 2012 meeting.

DISCUSSION 
The Open Meeting Law was enacted "to eliminate much of the secrecy surrounding 
deliberations and decisions on which public policy is based." Ghiglione v. School Committee of 
Southbridge, 376 Mass. 70, 72 (1978). The law requires that meetings of a public body be 
properly noticed and open to members of the public, unless an executive session is convened. 
See G.L. c. 30A, §§ 20(a)-(b), 21. A "meeting" is defined, in relevant part, as "a deliberation by 
a public body with respect to any matter within the body's jurisdiction." G.L. c. 30A, § 18. A 
"deliberation" in turn is defined, in relevant part, as "an oral or written communication through 
any medium, including electronic mail, between or among a quorum of a public body on any 
public business within its jurisdiction." Id. A "quorum" is defined as "a simple majority of the 
members of the public body, unless otherwise provided in a general or special law, executive 
order or other authorizing provision." Id. 
The statute that establishes the Council does not provide a quorum requirement for its 
meetings. See G.L. c. 32, § 20. In the absence of a specific statutory requirement, for purposes 
of the Open Meeting Law, the Council's quorum is measured as "a simple majority of the 
members." G.L. c. 30A, § 18. The Council has 54 members, therefore a quorum is 28 members. 
Because a quorum is 28 members, the Council only deliberates when 28 members communicate 
on public business within the Council's jurisdiction. Id. Deliberation may only take place 
during a properly posted meeting. IcL Because only 12 members participated in the September 
19, 2012 meeting, the Council did not deliberate and did not hold a "meeting" for the purposes of 
the Open Meeting Law. A sub-quorum of a body's members may communicate on matters 
within its jurisdiction outside of a meeting, provided that the sub-quorum was not acting as a 
subcommittee. See OML 2012-110, FN 3; OML 2011-16. As there is no evidence that the 
group that met on September 19, 2012 was a subcommittee, and no allegation that serial 
communication between a quorum took place on that date, we find that the Council did not
