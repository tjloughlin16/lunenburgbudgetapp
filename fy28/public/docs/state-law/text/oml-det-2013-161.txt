RE: Open Meeting Law Complaint 
Dear Mr. Kozlowski; 
This office received a complaint from Donna Whitehead, Editor of the Mansfield News, 
dated April 22, 2013, alleging that the Mansfield Board of Selectmen (the "Board") violated the 
Open Meeting Law, G.L. c. 30A, §§ 18-25. Specifically, the complaint alleges that the Board 
convened its January 23, 2013 meeting prior to the posted started time. The complaint was filed 
with the Board on or about February 18, 2013. The Board responded to the complaint by letter 
dated March 14, 2013. 
Following our review, we find that the Board violated the Open Meeting Law by failing 
to provide a precise start time for its January 23, 2013 meeting. In reaching this determination, 
we reviewed the February 18, 2013 complaint filed with the Board; the Board's March 14, 2013 
response; and the April 22, 2013 complaint filed with our office. Additionally, we reviewed the 
notice for the January 23, 2013 meetings of the Board and the Mansfield Municipal Electric 
Department Light Commissioners (the "Light Commission). Finally, we reviewed the minutes 
of the Board's January 23, 2013 meeting. 
The Mansfield Board of Selectmen simultaneously serves as the Mansfield Municipal 
Light Commission. The Light Commission posted notice for a January 23, 2013 meeting for 
7:00 PM, with an 8:30 PM adjournment. The Board posted notice for a January 23, 2013 
meeting to be held at "8:30 PM or [immediately] following Light Comm.'s Meeting." The five 
topics and four subtopics listed in the Board's notice included designated discussion times, with 
the first topic at 8:30 PM and the final topic at 9:45 PM. The Board's notice was posted by the 
Town Clerk on the official Town notice bulletin board. The Town has not designated an 
alternative notice posting location.

The Town also included the notices for the Light Commission and Board meetings on the 
calendar page on its website.1 The website stated that the Light Commission meeting would be 
held from 7:00 PM - 8:30 PM, and the Board meeting from 8:30 PM - 10:00 PM. The listing 
included a link to the Board's meeting agenda, which included the same language as the official 
notice, stating that the meeting would start at "8:30 PM or [immediately] following Light 
Comm.'s Meeting." A disclaimer on the calendar pages reads "The information provided is for 
reference only. You should always contact the Town Hall for the latest information. Not all 
meetings are listed at this time." 
The Light Commission met at 7:00 PM on January 23, 2013, and finished its meeting at 
about 7:45 PM. The Board then opened its meeting at 7:49PM. A reporter for the Mansfield 
News arrived at the Board's meeting at 8:30 PM, believing that the meeting would start at that 
time. 
DISCUSSION 
The Open Meeting Law requires that, except in an emergency, "a public body shall post 
notice of every meeting at least 48 hours prior to such meeting, excluding Saturdays, Sundays 
and legal holidays.... Notice shall be printed in a legible, easily understandable format and shall 
contain the date, time and place of such meeting and a listing of topics that the chair reasonably 
anticipates will be discussed at the meeting." G.L. c. 30A, § 20(b). For local public bodies, 
notice shall be filed with the municipal clerk and posted in a manner conspicuously visible to the 
public at all hours in or on the municipal building in which the clerk's office is located. Id. at § 
20(c). A municipality may designate a website as an alternative notice posting location for all 
local public bodies within the municipality. 940 CMR 29.03(2)(b)(a). 
Here, the Board failed to provide a clear start time for its January 23, 2013 meeting. A 
public body must post notice for a meeting listing the "date, time and place of such meeting." 
G.L. c. 30A, § 20(b). The Board's notice listing the time as "8:30 PM or [immediately] 
following Light Comm.'s Meeting" does not meet this requirement. Members of the public 
should not have to guess when a meeting will start, or attend the entire Light Commission 
meeting to avoid the risk of missing a portion of the Board's meeting. The Board must post 
notice for its meetings with a precise start time. When noticing consecutive meetings of two 
public bodies, the notices may state that the second meeting will start at a precise time, or later if 
the prior meeting runs late. However, a public body's meeting cannot start earlier than the 
posted start time. Thus, the Board could have noticed its start time for "8:30 PM or immediately 
following the Light Commission's meeting if that meeting runs past 8:30 PM." 
Additionally, the municipal website provided a misleading start time, listing the Board's 
meeting as starting at 8:30 PM without the caveat that the start was dependent upon the 
conclusion of an earlier meeting. However, we do not find this to be a violation of the Open 
Meeting Law. Mansfield's official notice posting location is the Town notice bulletin board. 
The notices posted to the Town's website are posted merely as a courtesy, and are not the official 
meeting notices. However, we strongly encourage the Town to ensure that its website and

CONCLUSION 
We find that the Board violated the Open Meeting Law by failing to provide a precise 
start time for its January 23, 2013 meeting. We order the Board's immediate and future 
compliance with the Open Meeting Law, and caution the Board that similar future conduct may 
be considered evidence of an intentional violation of the law.
