RE: Open Meeting Law Complaint 
Dear Attorney Corbo: 
This office received an Open Meeting Law complaint from Jeanne Golrick, dated May 
16, 2013, alleging that the Montague Board of Selectmen (the "Board") violated the Open 
Meeting Law, G.L. c. 30A, §§ 18-25. Specifically, the complaint alleges that 1) on April 1, 
2013, the Board entered into an improperly posted executive session to discuss the release of 
minutes from previously held executive sessions; and 2) that the Board failed to adequately 
respond to Ms. Golrick's February 25, 2013 request for minutes. The complaint was filed with 
the Board on or about April 3, 2013. The Board responded by letter dated April 24, 2013. 
Following our review, we find that, while it made a good faith effort to comply, the 
Board violated the Open Meeting Law by failing to review executive session minutes within 30 
days of a request for those minutes. However, we find that the Board did not violate the Open 
Meeting Law by meeting in Executive Session on April 1, 2013 to review its executive session 
minutes from prior meetings, and that the notice for that meeting was sufficiently specific. In 
reaching this determination, we reviewed the April 3, 2013 complaint filed with the Board; the 
Board's April 24, 2013 response; and the May 16, 2013 complaint filed with our office. 
Additionally, we reviewed the notice for the Board's April 1, 2013 meeting. 
On or about February 25, 2013, Ms. Golrick sent a letter to the Board requesting minutes 
for 22 executive sessions conducted over the course of about three years. The Board responded 
to Ms. Golrick by letter dated March 7, 2013 stating that the Board Chair was working with

DISCUSSION 
1. Despite Making a Good Faith Effort to Comply, the Board Failed to Respond to a 
Request for Executive Session Minutes within 30 Days as Required by Law. 
A public body must respond to a request for executive session minutes within 10 days. 
G.L. c. 3 OA, § 22(g)(2). If the public body has determined, prior to the request, that certain 
executive session minutes may be released, it should respond to the requester directly, notifying 
him or her of the availability of those minutes. See OML 2013-105; OML 2013-99.1 If the body 
previously determined that executive session minutes should remain confidential because 
publication would defeat the lawful purposes of the executive session, it should respond by 
stating the reason why the minutes continue to be withheld. Id. And if, at the time of a request, 
the public body has not conducted a review of the minutes to determine whether continued 
nondisclosure is warranted, the body must perform such a review and release the minutes, if 
appropriate, no later than its next meeting or within 30 days, whichever occurs first. G.L. c. 
30A, § 22(g)(2). In such circumstances, the body should still respond to the requestor within 10 
days, notifying him or her that it is conducting this review. See OML 2013-99. 
The Board met the 10-day deadline for responding to Ms. Golrick's request, but failed to 
conduct the required review within 30 days. Ms. Golrick requested 22 sets of executive session 
meeting minutes on February 25, 2013. The Board responded to her request by letter dated 
March 7, 2013, exactly 10 days later. By issuing this letter, the Board met its first deadline. 
G.L. c. 30A, § 22(g)(2); OML 2013-99. However, the Board met to review and vote on the 
minutes on April 1, 2013, 34 days after Ms. Golrick's request. The Board was required by law to 
meet within 30 days of receiving the request. G.L. c. 30A, § 22(g)(2). The Board then 
announced the results of its review during it April 16, 2013 meeting. Although the Board was 
late in meeting to review and vote on releasing the requested minutes, we note that the request 
encompassed 22 sets of executive sessions minutes, and that the Chair worked with Town

2. The Notice for the Board's April 1. 2013 Meeting was Sufficiently Specific. 
The complaint alleges that the notice for the Board's April 1, 2013 executive session to 
review executive session minutes was improperly posted because it contained insufficient detail 
about the proposed executive session. Meeting notices "shall contain the date, time and place of 
such meeting and a listing of topics that the chair reasonably anticipates will be discussed at the 
meeting." G.L. c. 30A, § 20(b). The list of topics shall have sufficient specificity to reasonably 
advise the public of the issues to be discussed at the meeting. 940 CMR 29.03(l)(b). 
The notice for the Board's April 1, 2013 meeting included the following topic: 
"Consideration of request for release of Executive Session Minutes from previous meetings. 
Executive Session Expected." Although identification of the specific executive session minutes 
the Board planned to review and the corresponding executive session purposes would have been 
useful to the public, we find that this topic was sufficiently specific to inform the public that the 
Board would be reviewing executive session minutes in executive session. G.L. c 3OA, § 20(b); 
940 CMR 29.03(l)(b). Thus, the Board's notice complied with the Open Meeting Law. 
3. The Board's April 1. 2013 Executive Session was for a Lawful Purpose. 
The complaint further alleges that the executive session itself was not proper. In her 
complaint, Ms. Golrick states that "at the [April 1, 2013] meeting the claim was that [the 
executive session] was to comply with the requirements of the Open Meeting Law. This is 
simply preposterous - how can a law exempt itself from itself?" Allow us to explain. The Board 
was required to review and release the non-exempt portions of the 22 executive session minutes 
following Ms. Golrick's February 25, 2013 request. Where publication of minutes may defeat 
the lawful purposes of the executive session, meeting in open session to discuss protected 
minutes may compromise the confidentiality of those minutes.2 Therefore, the Board met in 
executive session under Purpose 7, session "[t]o comply with, or act under the authority of, any 
general or special law or federal grant-in-aid requirements." G.L. c. 3OA, § 21(a)(7). The 
general law the Board met in executive session to comply with was the Open Meeting Law, 
specifically, G.L. c. 30A, § 22(g). The Board reviewed the minutes in executive session and 
voted to publicly release those minutes for which the Board determined publication would no 
longer defeat the lawful purposes for the executive sessions. This was proper.3
