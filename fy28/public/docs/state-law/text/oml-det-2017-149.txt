RE: Open Meeting Law Complaint 
Dear Mr. McAuliffe; 
This office received a complaint from Patrick Higgins on August 4, alleging that the 
Swansea Board of Selectmen (the "Board") violated the Open Meeting Law, G.L. c. 30A, §§ 
18-25.1 Lhe complaint was originally filed with the Board on or about July 11, and you 
responded, on behalf of the Board, by letter dated August 4. In his complaint, Mr. Higgins 
alleges that, during its July 11 meeting, the Board delegated its response to an Open Meeting 
Law complaint without first discussing it. 
Following our review, we resolve this matter by informal action, in accordance with 
940 CMR 29.07(2)(a), and find that the Board did not violate the Open Meeting Law. 
The Board received an Open Meeting Law complaint on June 28. Per its usual 
practice, the Board listed it as a topic on its July 11 meeting notice and, during the meeting, 
agreed to delegate the responsibility of responding to the complaint to the town administrator. 
The complaint alleges that the Board violated the Open Meeting Law by failing to undergo a 
substantive review of the complaint during the meeting. Within 14 business-days' receipt of 
an Open Meeting Law complaint, a public body must review the complaint to ascertain the 
time, date, place and circumstances which constitute the alleged violation. 940 CMR 
29.05(4). Unless an extension has been granted by the Attorney General, the public body 
must review the complaint's allegations; take remedial action, if appropriate; and send to the 
Attorney General a copy of the complaint and a description of any remedial action taken. Id. 
Nothing in the regulations prevent a public body, once it has reviewed a complaint during a 
meeting, from delegating its response to an individual, such as a town administrator. That the
