 RE: Open Meeting Law Complaint 
Dear Attorney Blake: 
This office received a complaint from Michael Baril on February 11, 2022, alleging that 
the Warren Planning Board (the “Board”) violated the Open Meeting Law, G.L. c. 30A, §§ 18-
25. The complaint was originally filed with the Board on December 1, and the Board responded 
by letters dated March 1 and April 5, 2022.1 In his complaint, Mr. Baril alleges that the minutes 
of the Board’s October 25, 2021, meeting are insufficiently detailed.  
Following our review, we find that we find that the Board did not violate the Open 
Meeting Law. In reaching this determination, we reviewed the original complaint including 
attachments, the Board’s responses to the complaint, the request for further review. We reviewed 
the notice and recording of the Board’s October 25, 2021, meeting, as well as the draft and 
approved minutes of that meeting. We communicated by email with you on several occasions in 
March and April of 2022. Finally, we communicated by email with Mr. Baril in February, 
March, and April of 2022, and spoke with him by phone on April 25, 2022.  
 The Open Meeting Law requires that a public body “create and maintain accurate 
minutes of all meetings, including executive sessions, setting forth the date, time and place, the 
members present or absent, a summary of the discussions on each subject, a list of documents 
and other exhibits used at the meeting, the decisions made and the actions taken at each meeting, 
including the record of all votes.” G.L. c. 30A, § 22(a). When reviewing minutes for compliance 
with the Open Meeting Law, we look for substantial compliance with the accuracy requirement.

See OML 2016-105; OML 2013-64.2 By substantial compliance, we mean that the minutes 
should contain enough detail and accuracy so that a member of the public who did not attend the 
meeting could read the minutes and have a clear understanding of what occurred. See OML 
2012-106. While minutes must include a summary of the discussion on each topic, a transcript is 
not required, and the minutes do not need to include every remark or opinion presented. See 
OML 2012-29; OML 2011-55. 
 Here, Mr. Baril alleges that the approved minutes of the Board’s October 25, 2021, 
meeting are inaccurate as the Board removed a paragraph which had been included in the draft 
version of those minutes. The paragraph which was removed described Mr. Baril’s comments 
near the end of the meeting during which Mr. Baril described what he believed was an incident 
of harassment and intimidation of a member of the public which took place in the audience of the 
Board’s meeting earlier in the evening. The Board then briefly asked whether Warren police 
were handling the matter and affirmed that no harassment should take place during Board 
meetings. Mr. Baril’s comments and the Board’s discussion that was prompted by the Mr. Baril’s 
comments lasted approximately three minutes. The Board did not take any action or make any 
decisions. While minutes must include a summary of the discussion on each topic, a transcript is 
not required, and the minutes do not need to include every remark or opinion presented. See 
OML 2021-81 (concluding that minutes were sufficiently detailed though they did not capture 
committee’s three-and-a-half-minute discussion recapping a protocol in response to citizen 
input); OML 2012-29. We find that the minutes of the Board’s October 25, 2021, meeting 
complied with the requirements of the Open Meeting Law. 
CONCLUSION 
 For the reasons stated above, we find that the Board did not violate the Open Meeting 
Law. We now consider the complaint addressed by this determination to be resolved. This 
determination does not address any other complaints that may be pending with our office or the 
Board. Please feel free to contact the Division at (617) 963 - 2540 if you have any questions.
