This office received a complaint from Amy Kates dated February 10, 2014, alleging 
that the Cotuit Fire District Prudential Committee (the Committee) violated the Open Meeting

our order in OML 2013-157.2 
We reviewed the December 18, 2013 complaint; your March 24, 2014 response, on 
behalf of the Committee; and the February 10, 2014 complaint filed with our office, 
requesting further review. In addition, we reviewed the revised minutes from the 
Committee's June 25, 2012; June 28, 2012; and July 10, 2012 meetings. 
Following our review, we find that the Committee did not violate the Open Meeting 
Law in the ways alleged. However, the Committee did violate the Law by failing to respond 
to this Open Meeting Law complaint within fourteen business days of receipt.

FACTS 
We find the facts as follows. On October 28, 2013, our office issued two 
determinations, OML 2013-157 and OML 2013-158, resolving four Open Meeting Law 
complaints that were filed against the Committee. Both determinations contained an order 
that the Committee review the Open Meeting Law Guide and certify in writing to our office 
within thirty days that its members had reviewed both determinations and the Guide. In OML 
2013-158, we also ordered the Committee to draft and approve certain revised meeting 
minutes within thirty days. 
The Committee met three times during the thirty days following the issuance of these 
determinations: on November 4, 2013; November 18, 2013; and November 25, 2013. The 
November 4, 2013 meeting lasted only a few minutes. During that meeting, the Committee 
discussed and voted on whether to appeal the two determinations. The Committee did not 
discuss the two determinations during the November 18, 2013 meeting. During the 
November 25, 2013 meeting, Chair Richard Barry announced that he had copies of the 
determinations to distribute to anyone interested, but the Committee did not discuss them in 
any detail. Immediately prior to this meeting, Mr. Barry drafted a certification letter, which 
all three Committee members signed to certify pursuant to our orders that they had each read 
the determinations and the Open Meeting Law Guide. Mr. Barry does not recall how he 
determined that the other two Committee members were prepared to sign the certification on 
that day. However, the Committee asserts in counsel's written response that no discussion 
occurred outside of a meeting. 
During a meeting on December 5, 2013, the Committee approved certain revised 
meeting minutes.3 This action, taken pursuant to our order in OML 2013-158, followed an 
extension of time to comply granted by our office. The Committee submitted the revised 
meeting minutes to our office on December 9, 2013. 
The present Open Meeting Law complaint was filed with the Committee on December 
18, 2013. The Committee did not respond, and on February 27, 2014 we called Attorney 
Boudreau to inquire about the status of its response. In a letter dated March 24, 2014, 
Attorney Boudreau responded, on behalf of the Committee, to this complaint. 
DISCUSSION 
The Open Meeting Law requires that all meetings of a public body be properly noticed 
and open to members of the public, unless an executive session is convened. See G.L. c. 3OA, 
§§ 20(a)-(b), 21. The Law's purpose is "to eliminate much of the secrecy surrounding 
deliberations and decisions on which public policy is based." Ghiglione v. School Committee 
of Southbridge, 376 Mass. 70, 72 (1978). A "meeting" is defined, in relevant part, as "a 
deliberation by a public body with respect to any matter within the body's jurisdiction." G.L. 
c. 3OA, § 18. The law defines "deliberation," in relevant part, as "an oral or written 
communication through any medium, including electronic mail, between or among a quorum 
of a public body on any public business within its jurisdiction." Id.

The complainant alleges that the Committee members must have discussed their 
compliance with our orders prior to signing the certification of compliance on November 25, 
2013. Because there was no discussion during a public meeting about whether each member 
had reviewed the required documents, she believes the members must have had private 
deliberations on the topic. While we agree it is implausible that the Committee members 
signed the certification form without first confirming that all members had complied, the 
members assert this is the case and, absent evidence to the contrary, we are constrained to find 
that the Committee did not engage in improper deliberation. Additionally, we note that the 
act of signing the certification form outside of a meeting was not itself improper deliberation. 
If a public body makes a decision during a properly posted meeting, it may take related 
administrative action with respect to that decision, such as signing or executing a document, 
outside of a meeting. See OML 2013-160; 2013-84; OML 2013-4. The Committee discussed 
whether or not to appeal the two determinations during its November 4, 2013 meeting. The 
Committee's decision not to appeal these orders signified its intention to comply with them. 
Accordingly, we find that the execution of a certification of compliance outside of a meeting 
was an administrative task and not improper deliberation. See id. 
The complainant also alleges that certain meeting minutes remain inaccurate even 
after their revision pursuant to our order in OML 2013-157. We find that the revised minutes 
from the June 25, 2012, June 28, 2012, and July 10, 2012 meetings are sufficiently accurate 
and detailed. The Open Meeting Law requires that a public body "create and maintain 
accurate minutes of all meetings, including executive sessions, setting forth the date, time and 
place, the members present or absent, a summary of the discussions on each subject, a list of 
documents and other exhibits used at the meeting, the decisions made and the actions taken at 
each meeting, including the record of all votes." G.L. c. 30A, § 22(a). Pursuant to our order 
in OML 2013-157, the Committee revised the meeting minutes to include certain details that 
we noted were missing or insufficiently explained. The revised minutes were approved 
during the Committee's December 5, 2013 meeting. We find, therefore, that the Committee 
complied with our order and do not find a violation of the law. 
Although the Committee did not violate the law in the ways alleged, it violated the 
Open Meeting Law by failing to respond to the present Open Meeting Law complaint in a 
timely manner. The Open Meeting Law requires that within 14 business days after receiving 
a complaint the public body shall review the complaint's allegations; take remedial action, if 
appropriate; and send to the Attorney General a copy of the complaint and a description of 
any remedial action taken. G.L. c. 30A, § 23(b); 940 CMR 29.05(5) (upon the public body's 
written request showing good cause, this office may authorize an extension of time to the 
public body for the purpose of responding and taking remedial action). Attorney Boudreau 
responded to the complaint only after we inquired about the Committee's failure to respond. 
At no point did the Committee request an extension of time to respond to the complaint or 
provide a reason for its failure to respond. Although we found a similar violation in OML 
2014-15, we do not consider that evidence of intentional violation in this matter because that 
decision was issued after this complaint was filed with our office. However, we have serious 
concerns about the Committee's attentiveness to this requirement of the law, and caution that 
future similar violations may be considered evidence of intent to violate the law.

CONCLUSION 
For the reasons stated above, we find that the Committee violated the Open Meeting 
Law by failing to respond within fourteen business days of receipt of an Open Meeting Law 
complaint. We order immediate and future compliance with the Open Meeting Law, and 
caution that future similar conduct may be considered evidence of intent to violate the Open 
Meeting Law. Additionally, we order the Committee to review the Attorney General's Open 
Meeting Law Training Video #6: Public Body Member Certification; Open Meeting Law 
Complaint Process; Resources and Contact Info.,4 and certify in writing to this office, within 
thirty (30) days of the date of this letter, that its members have reviewed this determination 
and the Video. 
We now consider the complaint addressed by this determination to be resolved. This 
determination does not address any other complaints that may be pending with our office or 
the Committee. Please feel free to contact our office at (617) 963-2540 if you have any 
questions.
