 RE: Open Meeting Law Complaint 
Dear Attorney Coughlin: 
This office received a complaint from Patrick Higgins on May 13, 2020, alleging that the 
Fall River Redevelopment Authority (the “Authority”) violated the Open Meeting Law, G.L. c. 
30A, §§ 18-25. The complaint was originally filed with the Authority on April 9, and you 
responded on behalf of the Authority by letter dated May 15.1, 2 In his complaint, Mr. Higgins 
alleges that the Authority failed to produce meeting minutes within 10 days of his request. 
Following our review, we find that the Authority did not violate the Open Meeting Law. 
In reaching this determination, we reviewed the original complaint, the Authority’s response to 
the complaint, and the complainant’s request for further review. We also reviewed an email 
exchange between the complainant and the Authority’s Executive Director. Finally, we emailed 
with you on several occasions in October 2020.  
FACTS 
We find the facts as follows. On April 3, Mr. Higgins made a request “under the Open 
Meeting Law” for copies of “all meeting notices, open session minutes and executive session 
minutes for the Fall River Redevelopment Authority for calendar years, 2019 and 2020.”3 On the 
same day, the Executive Director replied that the Authority had already provided the 2019 
minutes to Mr. Higgins and attached the all then-existent 2020 minutes. The Executive Director

DISCUSSION 
 The Open Meeting Law requires that “[m]inutes of all open sessions shall be created and 
approved in a timely manner. The minutes of an open session, if they exist and whether approved 
or in draft form, shall be made available upon request by any person within 10 days.” G.L. c. 
30A, § 22(c). The complaint alleges that the Authority failed to timely respond to his requests for 
certain meeting minutes. We disagree. Within a few hours of Mr. Higgins’s request, the 
Executive Director provided all the then-existent 2020 meeting minutes, and offered to send the 
2019 minutes as well if Mr. Higgins had not previously received them. When Mr. Higgins 
followed up about the April 1 open session minutes, the Executive Director explained that the 
April 1 meeting minutes were not yet drafted as the meeting was held two days prior. 
Handwritten notes, depending on the condition they are in, may be considered a draft of the 
minutes that need to be produced, and “materials used in the preparation of minutes” are public 
records. See OML 2018-117; OML 2013-131; OML 2013-64; OML 2013-31. However, the 
Authority has since confirmed for us that it possesses no notes of the meeting which were later 
used to create the draft minutes. Because minutes of the April 1 meeting did not exist in either 
approved or draft form, the Authority did not violate the Open Meeting Law by failing to provide 
minutes of that meeting. See OML 2017-50; OML 2016-71; OML 2015-173.4  
Finally, the complaint alleges that the Authority refused to produce “all executive session 
minutes,” but fails to specify any executive session for which meeting minutes were not 
provided. Accordingly, we decline to review this allegation because our office will not conduct 
broad audits of public bodies based on generalized allegations. See OML Declination 4-22-15 
(Natick Economic Development Advisory Committee). 
CONCLUSION 
 For the reasons stated above, we find that the Authority did not violate the Open Meeting 
Law. We now consider the complaint addressed by this determination to be resolved. This 
determination does not address any other complaints that may be pending with our office or the 
Authority. Please feel free to contact the Division at (617) 963 - 2540 if you have any questions.
