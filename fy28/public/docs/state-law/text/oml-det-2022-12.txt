RE: Open Meeting Law Complaint 
Dear Attorney Castro: 
This office received a complaint from Allen Waller on December 16, 2021, alleging that 
the Marblehead Board of Selectmen (the “Board”) violated the Open Meeting Law, G.L. c. 30A, 
§§ 18-25. The complaint was originally filed with the Board on November 10, 2021, and you
responded, on behalf of the Board by letter dated December 10, 2021. In his complaint, Mr.
Waller alleges that “[n]o meeting minutes [were] posted for BOS 5 August 2021 meeting.”
Following our review, we find that the Board did not violate the Open Meeting Law in 
the way alleged. We resolve this complaint by informal action in accordance with 940 CMR 
29.07(1). In reaching this determination, we reviewed the original complaint, the Board’s 
response to the complaint, and the complainant’s request for further review.  
The Open Meeting Law requires that public bodies “create and maintain accurate minutes 
of all meetings.” G.L. c. 30A, §22(a). These minutes must be reviewed and approved by the 
public body in a timely manner. G.L. c. 30A, §22(c). “Timely manner” means “within the next 
three public body meetings or within 30 days, whichever is later, unless the public body can 
show good cause for further delay.” 940 CMR 29.11; see OML 2018-48. Whenever possible, we 
recommend that minutes of a meeting be approved at the next meeting. See OML 2018-67; OML 
2017-133. Moreover, the minutes of an open session, if they exist and whether approved or in 
draft form, shall be made available upon request by any person within ten days. G.L. c. 30A, 
§22(c).
Here, the complaint alleges that the Board violated the Open Meeting Law by failing to 
post minutes of its August 5, 2021, meeting. The Open Law Meeting does not require that public

bodies post their minutes to a website or elsewhere, but only that minutes be created, maintained, 
and approved in a timely manner, and made available within 10 days of request. G.L. c. 30A, 
§§22(a), (c); OML 2020-78; OML 2017-98.1 There is no allegation that the complainant 
requested the minutes and did not receive them. Nonetheless, the Board has provided the minutes 
to the complainant and posted the minutes to the town website.2 Therefore, we find no violation 
of the Open Meeting Law.  
 
 For the reasons stated above, we find that the Board did not violate the Open Meeting 
Law. We now consider the complaint addressed by this determination to be resolved. This 
determination does not address any other complaints that may be pending with our office or the 
Board. Please feel free to contact the Division at (617) 963-2540 if you have any questions.
