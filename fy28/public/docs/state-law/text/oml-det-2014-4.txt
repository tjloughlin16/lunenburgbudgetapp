RE: Open Meeting Law Complaint 
Dear Mr. Rogers; 
Our office received a complaint filed by Paul Logan on behalf of the Steam Engineering 
Institute, dated October 28, 2013, alleging that the Massachusetts Board of Boiler Rules (the 
"Board") violated the Open Meeting Law, G.L. c. 30A, §§ 18-25. Specifically, the complaint 
alleges that Mr. Logan did not receive a sufficiently detailed copy of the Board's July 9, 2013 
meeting minutes following his request. Additionally, the complaint alleges that the Board failed 
to provide minutes of its July 19, 2013 meeting following Mr. Logan's July 29, 2013 request and 
that the Board failed to post notice of its July 19, 2013 meeting. The complaint was originally 
filed with the Board on or about September 16, 2013. The Board responded by letter dated 
October 24, 2013, following an extension of time granted by this office. 
After reviewing the complaint and the Board's response, we have decided to resolve this 
Complaint by informal action, in accordance with 940 CMR 29.07(2)(a). We find that although 
the Board timely provided the July 9, 2013 minutes in response to Mr. Logan's request, the 
minutes were not sufficiently detailed. However, the Board took appropriate remedial action in 
response to the complaint by amending its minutes to include missing information. 
Mr. Logan requested the Board's July 9, 2013 minutes in an email dated August 30, 
2013. The Board responded to the request by letter dated August 30, 2013, and provided written 
minutes. However, for the portion of the minutes detailing the Board's discussion of proposed 
regulations, the minutes stated "Please refer to recording for specific details." This was 
insufficient, as meeting minutes must contain a written "summary of the discussions on each

subject." G.L. c. 30 A, § 22(a); OML 2013-128; OML 2013-14.1 After speaking with our office, 
the Board addressed this omission and amended the minutes to include a summary of the 
discussion of the proposed regulations. 
In a December 10, 2013 letter to our office, Mr. Logan alleges that the amended minutes 
are still insufficient because they fail to include numerous instances in which the Board voted, 
and failed to include the names of Board members who made motions and seconded motions. 
The Open Meeting Law does not require public bodies to include in the minutes the names of 
specific public body members who make and second motions. With respect to recording votes, 
we spoke with Department of Public Safety Assistant General Counsel Caroline Torrisi on 
December 18, 2013 and confirmed that the Board did not take any votes during its July 9, 2013 
discussion of proposed regulations, but rather reached decisions through discussion and 
consensus. The minutes record the instances where the Board agreed upon or adopted language 
in the proposed regulations. We find this to be sufficient to meet the Open Meeting Law's 
requirements that minutes include "decisions made and the actions taken at each meeting, 
including the record of all votes." G.L. c. 30A, § 22(a). 
The remainder of Mr. Logan's allegations are untimely, and therefore we decline to 
review them. The Open Meeting Law states that "prior to the filing of a complaint with the 
attorney general, the complainant shall file a written complaint with the public body, setting forth 
the circumstances which constitute the alleged violation; provided, however, that such complaint 
shall be filed within 30 days of the date of the alleged violation." G.L. c. 30A, § 23(b). If the 
alleged violation could not reasonably have been discovered at the time it occurred, the 
complaint must be filed with the public body within 30 days of when it reasonably could have 
been discovered. 940 CMR 29.05(3). Events that occur during an open session meeting are 
reasonably discoverable at the time they occur. See OML Declination 4-30-13. 
Mr. Logan requested the Board's July 19, 2013 meeting minutes on July 27, 2013. The 
Board responded to that request by letter dated August 8, 2013. Because Mr. Logan filed his 
complaint on September 16, 2013—39 days after the alleged violation—we find the allegation to 
be untimely and decline to review it. We remind the Board of its obligation to provide open 
session minutes within 10 days of a request. G.L. c. 30A, § 22(c). 
Finally, Mr. Logan alleges that the Board failed to post notice of its July 19, 2013 
meeting. Mr. Logan filed his complaint on September 16, 2013—59 days after the open session 
meeting at issue in the complaint. Because the alleged violation was reasonably discoverable at 
the time this meeting occurred, we find this allegation is untimely and decline to review it. 
However, we note that in its October 24, 2013 response to the complaint, the Board 
acknowledged that it failed to post notice to its website for its July 19, 2013 meeting, though 
notice was sent to the Office of the Secretary of the Commonwealth. The Board apologized for 
its mistake and has since updated its website with the notice and minutes from its July 19, 2013 
meeting. The Board pledges that "[m joving forward, we will ensure that there are no additional 
errors with respect to posting of meeting notices." We commend that Board for acknowledging 
its error and taking steps to ensure future compliance.
