This office received a complaint from Attorney Theodore Saulnier dated April 8, 
2014, alleging that the Martha's Vineyard Airport Commission (the Commission) violated 
the Open Meeting Law, G.L. c. 30A, §§ 18-25. The complaint was originally filed with 
the Commission on or about March 3, 2014, and you responded on behalf of the 
Commission to the complaint by letter dated March 17, 2014.' In his complaint, Attorney 
Saulnier alleges that on February 4, 2014; February 5, 2014; and February 7, 2014 
members of the Commission deliberated outside of a properly posted meeting. 
We appreciate the patience and cooperation of the parties during our review of this 
matter. Following our review, we find that the Commission did not violate the Open 
Meeting Law. In reaching this decision, we reviewed the March 3, 2014 complaint; the 
Commission's March 17, 2014 response; and the April 8, 2014 complaint requesting 
further review. We also spoke individually, by telephone, with Commission Chair John 
Alley and with Airport Manager Sean Flynn. 
We find the facts as follows. The Commission is a seven member public body, 
thus four members constitute a quorum. On February 4, 2014, Attorney Susan M. Whalen 
had an unscheduled luncheon with Commission Chair John Alley and Commissioner Peter 
Bettencourt to discuss, in part, the procedure for convening an executive session. 
Following their lunch. Attorney Whalen and Chair Alley met with Airport Manager Sean 
Flynn. The next day. Attorney Whalen and Chair Alley met with Airport management. 
On February 7, 2014, Commissioner Denys Wortman briefly met with Mr. Flynn after

DISCUSSION 
The Open Meeting Law requires that all meetings of a public body be open to the 
public, unless an executive session is convened. See G.L. c. 30A, §§ 20(a); 21. The Law's 
purpose is "to eliminate much of the secrecy surrounding deliberations and decisions on 
which public policy is based," Ghiglione v. School Committee of Southbridge, 376 Mass, 
70, 72 (1978). A "meeting" is defined, in relevant part, as "a deliberation by a public body 
with respect to any matter within the body's jurisdiction." G.L. c. 30A, § 18. The law 
defines "deliberation," in relevant part, as "an oral or written communication through any 
medium, including electronic mail, between or among a quorum of a public body on any 
public business within its jurisdiction." Id 
The complainant alleges that the Commission improperly deliberated outside of a 
meeting on February 4, 2014; February 5, 2014 and February 7, 2014. We find that the 
Commission did not engage in improper deliberation, either in-person, by email, or 
t 2 through a third party on those dates. See G.L. c. 30A, § 18. The individual conversations 
between Chair Alley and Commissioner Bettencourt; Chair Alley and Mr, Flynn; and Mr. 
Flynn and Commissioner Wortman did not constitute deliberation because they were all 
communications between less than a quorum of the Commission's members. See OML 
2013-108. The content of these individual conversations was not shared or otherwise 
discussed with a quorum of the Commission, either in person or by email. See McCrea v. 
Flaherty, 71 Mass. App. Ct. 637, 648-649 (2008) (a public body may not engage in a serial 
communication, whereby a quorum communicates in a non-contemporaneous manner 
outside of a meeting on a matter within the public body's jurisdiction). Accordingly, we 
find that the Commission did not violate the Open Meeting Law,
