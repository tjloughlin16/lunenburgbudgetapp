Re: Open Meeting Law Complaint - Provincetown Board of Selectmen 
Dear Attorney Goldberg; 
This office received a complaint, dated November 21, 2012, from Bernard Brahm, 
alleging that the Provincetown Board of Selectmen (the "Board") violated the Open Meeting 
Law, G.L. c. 30A, §§ 18-25. The complaint specifically alleges that the Board failed to provide 
Mr. Brahm with copies of the minutes from the Board's July 12, 2010 and August 2, 2010 
executive sessions, following his August 14, 2012 request for the minutes. This complaint was 
originally filed with the Board on or around September 18, 2012. We received your response on 
behalf of the Board in a letter dated October 9, 2012. 
Following our review, we find that the Board violated the Open Meeting Law by failing 
to timely respond to a request for executive session minutes. In reaching this determination, we 
reviewed the September 18, 2012 complaint filed with the Board; the Board's October 9, 2012 
response filed by the Board; and the November 21, 2012 complaint filed with our office. 
Additionally, we reviewed the executive session minutes of the Board's July 12, 2010 and 
August 2, 2010 meetings. We also reviewed the notices and open session minutes of the Board's 
July 12, 2010 and August 2, 2010 meetings. Finally, we reviewed the minutes of the Board's 
October 9, 2012 meeting. 
FACTS 
According to the open session meeting minutes, the Board entered into executive session 
on July 12, 2010 "to discuss litigation/bargaining strategy." However, the executive session 
minutes, publicly released on October 9, 2012, state that the Board entered executive session "for 
the purpose of discussing personnel." The executive session lasted 29 minutes. The summary of 
the discussion in the minutes states, in full, "Open discussion pertaining to litigation concerning 
Town Manager Sharon Lynn."

During its August 2, 2010 meeting, the Board entered into executive session to discuss 
"Land" under "Clause 6." The executive session lasted 55 minutes. The summary of the 
discussion states, in full, "Board of Selectmen signed the Walsh agreement. Brief discussion on 
the conflict of interest issues. Open discussion lead by John Giorgio on the purchase and sale of 
Fisherman's Wharf." 
On August 14, 2012, Mr. Brahm submitted a written request to the Board for copies of 
the July 12, 2010 and August 2, 2010 executive session minutes. The Board did not initially 
respond to this request, but after Mr. Brahm filed this complaint, the Board scheduled a review 
of the executive session minutes for its October 9, 2012 meeting. During that meeting, the Board 
entered into executive session to determine whether the requested minutes could be disclosed. 
Following that meeting, the Board publicly released the July 12, 2010 and August 2, 2010 
executive session minutes. 
DISCUSSION 
A public body must respond to a request for executive session minutes within 10 days. 
G.L. c. 3 OA, § 22(g)(2). If the public body has determined, prior to the request, that certain 
executive session minutes should be released, it should respond to the requester directly, 
notifying him or her of the availability of those minutes. OML 2013-99.1 If the body has 
determined that executive session minutes should remain confidential because publication would 
defeat the lawful purposes of the executive session, it should respond by stating the reason why 
the minutes continue to be withheld. Id. And if, at the time of a request, the public body has not 
conducted a review of the minutes to determine whether continued non-disclosure is warranted, 
the body must perform such a review and release the minutes, if appropriate, no later than its 
next meeting or within 30 days, whichever occurs first. G.L. c. 30A, § 22(g)(2). In such 
circumstances, the body should still respond to the requestor within 10 days, notifying him or her 
that it is conducting this review. See OML 2013-99; OML 2012-86. 
Here, the Board failed to respond within 10 days to Mr. Brahm's request for the 
executive session minutes. Mr. Brahm requested the minutes for the Board's July 12, 2010 and 
August 2, 2010 executive session on August 14, 2012. The Board did not respond to Mr. Brahm 
until after he filed an Open Meeting Law complaint on September 18, 2012. At its September 
24, 2012 meeting, the Board acknowledged its "unintentional inadvertent failure to respond fully 
to the request." Following a review of the executive session minutes at its October 9, 2012 
meeting, the Board publicly released the minutes. While we find that the Board violated the law 
by failing to respond to Mr. Brahm's request for minutes within 10 days, because the Board 
acknowledged its error and took appropriate action to address the complaint, we order no 
additional relief. 
As a final matter, although not alleged in the complaint, we note our concern about the 
lack of detail in the minutes of both the July 12, 2010 and August 2, 2010 executive session 
meetings. We remind the Board that minutes must include "a summary of the discussions on 
each subject, a list of documents and other exhibits used at the meeting, the decisions made and 
the actions taken at each meeting, including the record of all votes." G.L. c. 30A, § 22(a). A

CONCLUSION 
We find that the Board violated the Open Meeting Law by failing to respond to Mr. 
Brahm's August 14, 2012 request for executive session minutes within 10 days. Because the
