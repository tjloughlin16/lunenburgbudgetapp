RE: Open Meeting Law Complaint 
Dear Attorney Ouellette: 
This office received a complaint from Frank Condez on May 23, 2014, alleging that the 
Dartmouth Board of Selectmen (the "Board") violated the Open Meeting Law, G.L. c. 3OA, §§ 
18-25. Specifically, the complaint alleges that the Board entered into executive session to 
discuss Mr. Condez without providing him the right to be present during its October 7, 2013; 
October 21, 2013; November 4, 2013; November 18, 2013; December 2, 2013; December 16, 
2013; January 27, 2014; and April 7, 2014 meetings. The complaint was filed with the Board on 
or about April 15, 2014. The Board responded to the complaint by letter dated August 25, 2014.' 
We appreciate the patience and cooperation of the parties while we reviewed this matter. 
Following our review, we find that the Board violated the Open Meeting Law by meeting in 
executive session on numerous occasions without providing Mr. Condez with notice and the 
opportunity to be present. In reaching this determination, we reviewed the original complaint 
filed with the Board; the complaint filed with our office; and the Board's response to both. 
Additionally, we reviewed the notices for, and executive session minutes of, the Board's October 
7, 2013; October 21, 2013; November 4, 2013; November 18, 2013; December 16, 2013; 
January 27, 2014; and April 7, 2014 meetings. Finally, we reviewed a letter from Mr. Condez to 
our office, which we received on September 4, 2014.

FACTS 
We find the facts as follows. The Dartmouth Police Department placed Mr. Condez, a 
sergeant in the Department, on paid administrative leave on October 1, 2013. The Board has 
been considering administrative action against Mr. Condez since that time. On October 7, 2013; 
October 21, 2013; November 4, 2013; November 18, 2013; December 2, 2013; December 16, 
2013; January 27, 2014; and April 7, 2014 (hereafter "the meetings"), the Board met in executive 
session to discuss disciplinary matters related to Mr. Condez. 
For seven of the eight meetings, the notices stated there would be an executive session, 
but did not specify the purpose beyond the statement that it concerned a "Police investigation." 
For example, the notice for the Board's October 7, 2013 meeting listed a topic as "Executive 
Session pursuant to M.G.L. Ch. 30A, Sec. 21. Police Investigation, Proposed Capping of Landfill 
Legal Analysis and Executive Session Meeting Minutes of September 30, 2013." Only for the 
April 7, 2014 meeting did the notice identify the subject of the investigation, stating, "Executive 
Session pursuant to M.G.L. Ch. 30A, Sec. 21.. .Police Investigation Condez." For the Board's 
December 2, 2013 meeting, no executive session topic was listed. The Board did not notify Mr. 
Condez before any of the meetings nor did it provide him with an opportunity to be present 
during the executive sessions. 
Following the Board's April 7, 2014 executive session, during which it considered 
charges against Mr. Condez, the Board sent Mr. Condez a letter informing him that it had issued 
charges against him of violating internal rules and regulations. The letter also advised Mr. 
Condez of his rights under G.L. c. 31, § 41, including notice of a civil service hearing at which 
he could dispute the charges. 
In response to the present complaint, the Board held an executive session meeting on July 
31, 2014 to reconsider the charges issued during its April 7, 2014 meeting. The Board provided 
Mr. Condez with written notice of this executive session on July 29, 2014, and advised Mr. 
Condez of his rights to be present at the executive session, to speak on his own behalf, to have 
counsel present, to cause an independent record to be created of the executive session, and to 
request that the executive session be held in the open. Mr. Condez attended the July 31 
executive session. During the session, the Board voted to withdraw all charges of rules and 
regulations violations issued against Mr. Condez during the Board's April 7, 2014 meeting.2 In 
speaking with the Board's counsel,3 we understand the Board's intent was to repudiate its April 
7, 2014 actions, as well as actions taken during the prior seven meetings identified in the 
complaint during which the Board discussed Mr. Condez. During the executive session, the 
Board issued Mr. Condez a new notice of charges and informed him of his right to hearing 
pursuant to G.L. c. 31, § 41.

DISCUSSION 
A public body may enter executive, or closed, session for any of ten enumerated 
purposes. G.L. c. 30A, § 21(a). Among the ten purposes for executive session is Purpose 1, 
which in pertinent part allows a public body to enter executive session to "discuss 
the.. .discipline or dismissal of, or complaints or charges brought against, a public officer, 
employee, staff member or individual." G.L. c. 3OA, § 21(a)(1). If a public body enters 
executive session under Purpose 1, the individual discussed in the session must be notified in 
writing by the public body at least 48 hours prior to the proposed executive session and must be 
afforded the following rights: to be present at the executive session during deliberations which 
involve that individual; to have counsel or a representative of his own choosing present and 
attending for the purpose of advising the individual and not for the purpose of active 
participation in the executive session; to speak on his own behalf; to cause an independent record 
to be created of the executive session by audio-recording or transcription, at the individual's 
expense; and to request that the session be open. Id. 
The Board failed to afford Mr. Condez any of the rights guaranteed to him by Purpose 1, 
including providing 48 hours written notice of the eight executive sessions during which it 
discussed disciplining him. See G.L. c. 3OA, § 21(a)(1). This was a serious failure to comply 
with the requirements of the Open Meeting Law. Furthermore, although not raised in the 
complaint, we note that the Board also failed to properly notice its executive sessions. The Open 
Meeting Law, G.L. c. 30A, § 20(b), states that a public body shall post notice of every meeting 
and that the notice shall contain "a listing of topics that the chair reasonably anticipates will be 
discussed at the meeting." Public bodies are required to list topics in a meeting notice with 
"sufficient specificity to reasonably advise the public of the issues to be discussed at the 
meeting." 940 CMR 29.03(l)(b). For an executive session topic, the chair must "state the 
purpose for the executive session, stating all subjects that may be revealed without 
compromising the purpose for which the executive session was called." G.L. c. 30A, § 21(b)(3). 
This level of detail must also be included in the meeting notice. See OML 2011-15.4 Here, the 
meeting notices identified the section of the Open Meeting Law dealing with executive sessions, 
G.L. c. 30A, § 21, but failed to identify which of the ten enumerated purposes the Board intended 
to invoke. The Board specified only "Police Investigation" as the nature of the discussion—a 
subject that could potentially fit within several different purposes. At a minimum, the Board 
should have included the statutory citation to the specific executive session purpose it intended to 
invoke. G.L. c. 30A, § 21(b)(3); OML 2014-30. Additional detail, such as the nature of the 
investigation or name of the person whom it concerned, was not required if inclusion would 
defeat the purpose of executive session. G.L. c. 30A, § 21(b)(3).

remedial action during its July 31, 2014 meeting by rescinding its disciplinary action against Mr.

Condez, and holding a new executive session in conformance with the procedures required by 
law. See id. We commend the Board for taking these steps to address the violations that 
occurred. While we find that the Board acted appropriately in response to the complaint, 
because the eight executive sessions were not held in compliance with G.L. c. 30A, § 21, the 
Board may not continue to withhold the minutes of these meetings under the Open Meeting Law. 
See G.L. c. 30A, § 22(f) (minutes of an executive session may be withheld from disclosure to the 
public as long as publication may defeat the lawful purposes of an executive session, provided 
that the executive session was held in compliance with the Open Meeting Law); OML 2014-42; 
OML 2014-30; OML 2014-17. The Board may continue to withhold these minutes in whole or 
in part under any applicable exemptions to the Public Records Law or the attorney/client 
privilege. However, as Mr. Condez had a right to be present during these executive sessions and 
to make his own independent record of those proceedings, but was denied those rights by the 
Board, we order that the unredacted portions of the executive session minutes involving 
discussion of Mr. Condez be provided to Mr. Condez within thirty (30) days of the Board's 
receipt of this determination. 
CONCLUSION 
We find that the Board violated the Open Meeting Law by failing to follow the 
procedures to hold a lawful executive session on eight separate occasions. We commend the 
Board for recognizing its violation and taking some remedial steps. In addition to the action 
already taken by the Board, we order the following: 
1) Immediate and future compliance by the Board with the Open Meeting Law. We caution 
the Board that a similar future violation may be considered evidence of an intentional 
violation of the Law. 
2) Review by each member of the Board of the Attorney General's training video on 
executive sessions, available at the Attorney General's website 
(www.mass.gov/ago/openmeeting). Each member must certify, using the attached form, 
his or her compliance with this order within 30 days following the Board's receipt of this 
determination. 
3) That, within 30 days after receiving this determination, the Board provide to Mr. Condez 
copies of the portions of the minutes describing the Board's discussion of Mr. Condez 
from the following executive session meetings: October 7, 2013; October 21, 2013; 
November 4, 2013; November 18, 2013; December 2, 2013; December 16, 2013; January 
27, 2014; and April 7, 2014.
