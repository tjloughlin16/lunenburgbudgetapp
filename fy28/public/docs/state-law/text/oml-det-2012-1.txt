RE: Open Meeting Law Complaint 
Dear Attorney Gibbons: 
This office received a complaint from John C. Martin, dated August 31, 2011, alleging a 
violation of the Open Meeting Law, G.L. c. 30A §§ 18-25. The complaint alleges that the Town 
of Clinton failed to post notice for meetings of its public bodies in a manner conspicuously 
visible to the public at all hours, as required by G.L. c. 30A, § 20(c). The complainant states, 
generally, that "[o]fficial notices of meeting[s] are only available on the bulletin board located 
inside the Town Clerk's office," and specifically alleges that "[n]otice of the Board of Selectmen 
meeting to be held [on July 20, 2011] was posted in the Town Clerk's office on [July 18, 2011] 
but [was] not visible to the public at all hours." The complaint was originally filed with the 
Board on or around July 20, 2011. We received your response to the complaint, on behalf of the 
Board, on August 26, 2011. 
Following our review of the complaint, we find that the Board violated the Open Meeting 
Law by failing to post notice of its July 20, 2011 meeting in a manner conspicuously visible to 
members of the public at all hours. However, we find that the Board took appropriate action by 
posting all subsequent meeting notices on the Town's website, therefore we order no additional 
remedial action. 
The Open Meeting Law requires that public bodies "shall post notice of every meeting at 
least 48 hours prior to such meeting, excluding Saturdays, Sundays, and legal holidays." G.L. c. 
30A, § 20(b). The Attorney General's Open Meeting Regulations require local public bodies to: 
===PAGE 2===
. . post notice of the meeting in a manner conspicuously visible to the public at all 
hours in or on the municipal building in which the clerk's office is located. Such notice 
shall be accessible to the public in the municipal clerk's office. If such notice is not 
conspicuously visible to the public during hours when the clerk's office is closed, such 
notice shall also be made available through an alternative method prescribed or approved 
by the Attorney General under 940 CMR 29.03(2)(b). A description of such alternative 
method, sufficient to allow members of the public to obtain notice through such method, 
shall be posted in a manner conspicuously visible to the public at all hours on or adjacent 
to the main and handicapped accessible entrances to the municipal building in which the 
clerk's office is located." 
940 CMR 29.03(2)(a). Municipalities are required to notify the Attorney General of their 
adoption of such alternative posting method, including the website address if applicable. 940 
CMR 29.03(1)(c). 
It is our understanding that, in the time since this complaint was filed, the Town of 
Clinton has adopted its municipal website, www.clintonma.gov, as its alternative posting 
method. We remind the Town that both the date and the time that a notice is posted must be 
conspicuously recorded on the meeting notice. See 940 CMR 29.03(1)(b). Further, we note that 
a description of the Town's alternative posting method must be "visible to the public at all hours 
on or adjacent to the main and handicapped accessible entrances to the municipal building in 
which the clerk's office is located." 940 CMR 29.03(2)(a). 
We would also like to take this opportunity to remind the Board of its obligation to 
respond to Open Meeting Law complaints within 14 business days after receipt. The Open 
Meeting Law requires that public bodies send a copy of the complaint, along with a description 
of any remedial action taken, to the Attorney General's Office within 14 business days, unless 
the Office grants a written request for an extension of time for the public body to respond. See 
G.L. c. 30A, § 23(b). Public bodies are also required to send a copy of their response to the 
complainant within 14 business days. See 940 CMR 29.05(5). In this instance, the complaint 
was filed with the Board on July 20, 2011. Our office received notification and a response from 
the Board on August 26, 2011 — 27 business days after the complaint was originally filed. 
We find that the Board failed to post notice of its July 20, 2011 meeting in a manner 
conspicuously visible to the public at all hours. However, after reviewing the Town's website 
and speaking with Michael Ward, Town Administrator, on July 21, 2011 and August 23, 2011, 
we believe that the Board and the Town will comply with the Open Meeting Law's posting 
requirements going forward. Please keep in mind that the Town of Clinton must notify the 
Attorney General in the event that the Town changes its posting method. We now consider this 
matter resolved. Please do not hesitate to contact me at the number below if you have any 
questions.
