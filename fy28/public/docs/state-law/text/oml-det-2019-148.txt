RE: Open Meeting Law Complaint
Dear Attorney Boudreau:
This office received a complaint from Cynthia Gardner on July 19, 2019, alleging that the 
Cotuit Fire District Prudential Committee (the “Prudential Committee”) violated the Open 
Meeting Law, G.L. c. 30A, §§ 18-25. The complaint was originally filed with the Prudential 
Committee on June 5, 2019, and you responded on behalf of the Prudential Committee by letter 
dated June 20, 2019. In her complaint, Ms. Gardner alleges that one member of the Prudential 
Committee attended an unnoticed meeting of the Space Needs Committee and that directions 
were given and agreements were made without authority to do so.
We resolve this complaint by informal action in accordance with 940 CMR 29.07(2)(a) 
and find that the Prudential Committee did not violate the Open Meeting Law. The Prudential 
Committee is a three-member public body; thus, two members constitutes a quorum. G.L. c.
30A, § 18. The Space Needs Committee is a subcommittee of the Prudential Committee, but 
consists of the same three members as the Prudential Committee. On April 10, 2019, Frances 
Parks, a member of the Prudential Committee and the Space Needs Committee, met with Fire 
Chief Rhude and Richard Fenuccio, an architect. Chief Rhude and Mr. Fenuccio are not 
members of the Prudential Committee or the Space Needs Committee. Where only one member 
of the Prudential Committee and Space Needs Committee attended the meeting at issue, there 
was no quorum of any public body present and the meeting was not subject to the Open Meeting 
Law. Thus, we find the Prudential Committee did not violate the Open Meeting Law. See OML 
2014-93.1
To the extent the complaint alleges that a single member of the Prudential and/or Space 
Needs Committee gave direction or took action without the authority to do so, this allegation 
falls outside the scope of the Open Meeting Law and we decline to review it.
