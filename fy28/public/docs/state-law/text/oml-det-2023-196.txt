 RE: Open Meeting Law Complaints 
Dear Ms. Pond: 
This office received a complaint from Paul DeMeo on August 7, 2023, alleging that 
the Gardner Disability Commission (the “Commission”) violated the Open Meeting Law, 
G.L. c. 30A, §§ 18-25.  The complaint was originally sent to the Commission on or about 
June 27, 2023, and you responded by letter dated August 7, 2023.1  The complaint alleges that 
the Commission has failed to post minutes for meetings held on January 14 and March 10, 
2020. 
 
Following our review, we find that the Commission did not violate the Open Meeting 
Law.  In reaching a determination, we reviewed the original complaint, the Commission’s 
response, and the complaint filed with our office requesting further review.   
 
As a preliminary matter, the Open Meeting Law does not require a public body 
to post minutes to a website, but rather requires that minutes be provided within 10 days of a 
request for minutes.  See G.L. c. 30A, § 22(c); OML 2017-157; OML 2016-17.
2  Therefore, a 
failure to post meeting minutes to a website does not violate the Open Meeting Law.  Even 
construing the complaint to allege that the Commission failed to timely approve meeting 
minutes, we find that the Commission did not violate the Open Meeting Law.  The response 
submitted on the Commission’s behalf explains that the Commission’s last meeting was held

on January 14, 2020; the Commission’s March 10, 2020, meeting was canceled due to the 
onset of the Covid-19 pandemic and the Commission has held no meetings since.  The 
complainant does not dispute those facts.  The Open Meeting Law requires that meeting 
minutes be reviewed and approved in a timely manner.  G.L. c. 30A, § 22(c).  “Timely 
manner” means “within the next three public body meetings or within 30 days, whichever is 
later, unless the public body can show good cause for further delay.”  See 940 CMR 29.11; 
OML 2018-48.  Because the Commission has not held three meetings since January 14, 2020, 
approval of minutes from that meeting is not yet untimely.   
 
For the reasons stated above, we find that the Commission did not violate the Open 
Meeting Law.  We now consider the complaint addressed by this determination to be 
resolved.  This determination does not address any other complaints that may be pending with 
our office or the Commission.  Please feel free to contact our office at (617) 963-2540 if you 
have any questions regarding this letter.
