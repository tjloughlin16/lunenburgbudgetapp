RE: Open Meeting Law Complaint 
Dear Ms. Gibson, 
This office received a complaint filed by Catherine Flanagan Stover dated January 18, 
2011 alleging that the Nantucket Board of Selectmen (the "Board") violated the Open Meeting 
Law, G.L. c. 30A, §§ 18-25. The complaint specifically alleges that, "Nile Selectmen 
AMENDED the agenda for the weekly meeting of December 15th, by adding a new item #3: 
'Continued Review or Request for Legal Services Respondents" on the evening of the 13th. The 
agenda was then posted on the town website on Tuesday morning, December 14, 2010 at 10:27 
AM, and with [the Clerk's] office on Tuesday morning, December 14, 2010 at 11:28 AM." 
(emphasis in original). That complaint further alleges that the notice the Board provided for the 
December 15, 2010 meeting was inadequate to meet the requirements of the Open Meeting Law. 
The complaint was originally filed with the Board on or about December 16, 2010. We also 
received the Board's response to the original complaint on or about December 23, 2010. 
We fmd that the Board did not violate the Open Meeting Law. In reaching this 
determination we reviewed the December 16, 2010 and January 18, 2011 complaints, the 
Board's December 23, 2010 response, and the meeting notices and the minutes for the December 
13, 2010 and December 15, 2010 meetings. 
From the information provided by the complaint and the Board's response, we 
understand the following to have occurred. The Board met on December 13, 2010. The sole 
item listed on the agenda was "Legal Services Request for Qualifications (RFQ) Respondents 
Interviews." The minutes of the December 13, 2010 meeting reflect that the Board interviewed 
respondents to a request for qualifications for legal services. The Board heard presentations 
from the firm Deutsch Williams, Attorney Ruth Weil, and the firm Kopelman and Paige, PC.

Following the presentations, the Board discussed the merits of each candidate and decided to put 
off a vote on choosing legal services until the next scheduled meeting, December 15, 2010. A 
meeting notice for December 15, 2010 had already been posted. 
On December 14, 2010, the item "Continued Review of Request for Legal Services 
Respondents" was added to the meeting notice for the December 15, 2010 Board meeting. The 
minutes of the December 15, 2010 Board meeting reflect that the chair of the Board reviewed the 
December 13, 2010 discussion where the Board interviewed the three candidates for legal 
services. The Board then engaged in a substantive discussion on the qualifications and merits of 
each of the candidates. Following discussion, the Board voted 3-2 to award the contract for legal 
services to Kopleman and Paige. 
The Open Meeting Law, G.L. c. 30, §§ 20(b) states in relevant part that, "[e]xcept in an 
emergency, in addition to any notice otherwise required by law, a public body shall post notice 
of every meeting at least 48 hours prior to such meeting, excluding Saturdays, Sundays and legal 
holidays... Notice shall be printed in a legible, easily understandable format and shall contain the 
date, time and place of such meeting and a listing of topics that the chair reasonably anticipates 
will be discussed at the meeting." Any topic discussed at a meeting, which was reasonably 
anticipated by the chair of the public body, must be listed in a meeting notice at least 48 hours in 
advance of the meeting. Public bodies are required to list topics in a meeting notice with 
"sufficient specificity to reasonably advise the public of the issues to be discussed at the 
meeting." 940 CMR 29.03. However, topics which arise within the 48 hours of the meeting, 
which the chair did not reasonably anticipate would be discussed at the meeting, may be added to 
the agenda and considered by the public body. 
Here, the review of candidates for a contract for counsel services was included as a topic 
for the Board's December 13, 2010 meeting. At that meeting, the Board heard from the 
candidates, and decided to table the issue for consideration at the next scheduled meeting, which 
was December 15, 2010. A meeting notice had already been posted for December 15, 2010. 
The Board updated the meeting notice on December 14, 2010, adding the item "Continued 
Review of Request for Legal Services Respondents." The Open Meeting Law requires a meeting 
notice to include a listing of topics the chairs reasonably anticipates will be discussed 48 hours 
before a meeting. However, topics which arise within the 48 hour period before a meeting which 
were not reasonably anticipated by the chair may be considered by a public body if added to the 
meeting notice within the 48 hours before the meeting, or added to the agenda at the meeting 
itself The chair did not reasonably anticipate the topic would be addressed at the December 15,

The Body provided notice to the public that it was reviewing counsel services at its 
December 13th  meeting. Anyone who attended that meeting would have been present for the 
Board's announcement that it would vote on the issue at its December 15th  meeting. This

inform the public of what the Board was considering. We find that the Board did not violate the 
Open Meeting Law because it followed the statutory requirements in providing notice for a 
deliberation on its request for legal services.
