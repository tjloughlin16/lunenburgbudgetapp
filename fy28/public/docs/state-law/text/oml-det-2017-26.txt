RE: Open Meeting Law Complaint 
Dear Attorney Han-is: 
This office received a complaint from Brian Wall, Esq., dated October 13, 2016, alleging 
that the Falmouth Conservation Commission (the "Commission") violated the Open Meeting 
Law, G.L. c. 30A, §§ 18-25. The complaint was originally filed with the Commission on August 
17, 2016, and the Commission responded by letter dated September 1, 2016.' In his complaint, 
Attorney Wall alleges that the Commission posted a meeting notice less than 48 hours prior to 
the meeting. 
Following our review, we find that the Commission violated the Open Meeting Law as 
alleged. In reaching a determination, we reviewed the original complaint, the Commission's 
response, and the request for further review filed with our office. We also reviewed the notice of 
a Commission meeting held on July 18, 2016.

FACTS 
We find the facts as follows. The complaint concerns a special Commission meeting 
held to consider several applications for orders of conditions. On Thursday, July 14, 2016, the 
Commission's administrative assistant sent an e-mail to the town clerk entitled "Public Meeting 
7/18/16 at 3:00 pm" with a meeting notice attached. One of the orders of conditions to be 
discussed at this July 18, 2016 meeting concerned Perch Pond Preservation, Inc., which is 
represented by the complainant. The clerk posted the notice at 3:20 P.M. on July 14, 2016. The 
Commission held the July 18, 2016 meeting at 3:00 P.M. as planned. During this meeting, the 
Commission discussed the Perch Pond Preservation, Inc. application, ultimately voting to deny 
it. No representative of Perch Pond Preservation, Inc. was present at the meeting. 
The Open Meeting Law is designed to eliminate "much of the secrecy surrounding the 
deliberations and decisions on which public policy is based." Ghighlione v. School Committee 
of Southbridge, 376 Mass. 70, 72 (1978). Public bodies must post notice of all meetings at least 
48 hours prior to such meetings, excluding Saturdays, Sundays, and legal holidays. G.L. c. 30A, 
Here, the complaint alleges that the Commission posted notice of its July 18,2016 
meeting less than 48 hours prior to the meeting, excluding Saturdays, Sundays, and legal 
holidays. Indeed, the July 18, 2016 meeting notice was posted at 3:20 P.M. on Thursday, July 
14, 2016—20 minutes shy of the 48 hour deadline. As such, the Commission failed to comply 
with the Open Meeting Law's requirements. See OML 2014-150.2 
For the reasons stated above, we find that the Commission violated the Open Meeting 
Law. We order the Commission's immediate and future compliance and we caution that similar 
future violations may be considered evidence of intent to violate the law. 
We now consider the complaint addressed by this determination to be resolved. This 
determination does not address any other complaints that may be pending with our office or the 
Commission. Please feel free to contact the Division at (617) 963 - 2540 if you have any 
questions.
