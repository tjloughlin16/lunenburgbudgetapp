RE: Open Meeting Law Complaint 
Dear Mr. Carter: 
This office received a complaint from Paullette Leukhardt, dated January 9, 2015, 
alleging that the Ashfield Select Board (the "Board") violated the Open Meeting Law, G.L. c. 
30A, §§ 18-25. The complaint specifically alleges that the Board failed to comply with our order 
in OML 2014-119 that it amend the minutes of its February 26, 2014 meeting to include a 
sufficiently detailed summary of its discussions regarding the minutes of a prior meeting and 
liaison reports. This complaint was originally filed with the Board on November 11, 2014. The 
Board responded by letter dated November 17, 2014. 
Following our review, we resolve this complaint by informal action in accordance with 
940 CMR 29.07(2)(a), and find that the Board did not violate the Open Meeting Law. In 
reaching this determination, we reviewed the complaint filed with the Board, the Board's 
response, and the complaint filed with our office. Additionally, we reviewed the original and 
amended minutes of the Board's February 26, 2014 meeting. Finally, we reviewed a video of the 
Board's February 26, 2014 meeting.1 
In OML 2014-119, we found that the Board violated the Open Meeting Law by failing to 
include in the minutes of its February 26, 2014 meeting an accurate and sufficient summary of its 
discussion about amending its April 8, 2013 meeting minutes.2 We stated that the Board should 
have provided more detail about the nature of its 30-minute discussion, including details of its

discussion about whether to include in those minutes certain documents suggested by Ms.

meeting to amend the February 26, 2014 minutes to include additional detail. The amended

The Open Meeting Law requires that a public body "create and maintain accurate 
minutes of all meetings, including executive sessions, setting forth the date, time and place, the 
members present or absent, a summary of the discussions on each subject, a list of documents 
and other exhibits used at the meeting, the decisions made and the actions taken at each meeting, 
including the record of all votes." G.L. c. 30A, § 22(a). A summary does not need to be a 
transcript of every word spoken at a meeting, but must contain enough detail so that a member of 
the public who did not attend the meeting could review the minutes and understand what the 
body discussed. See OML 2014-67; OML 2013-105. Having reviewed the video of the Board's 
February 26, 2014 meeting as well as the minutes of that meeting as amended on October 22, 
2014, we find that the Board has complied with our order by providing a sufficiently detailed 
summary of the Board's discussion. 
For the reasons stated above, we find that the Board did not violate the Open Meeting 
Law. We now consider the complaint addressed by this determination to be resolved. This 
determination does not address any other complaints that may be pending with our office or the 
Board. Please feel free to contact the Division at (617) 963-2540 if you have any questions.
