 RE:        Open Meeting Law Complaint 
 
Dear Attorney Feodoroff: 
 
This office received a complaint from Patrick Higgins on March 12, 2021, alleging that 
the Easton Cultural Council (the “Council”) violated the Open Meeting Law, G.L. c. 30A, §§ 18-
25.1  The complaint was originally filed with the Council on February 22, and you responded, on 
behalf of the Council, by letter dated March 12, following an extension of time granted by this 
office.  The complaint alleges that the Council violated the Open Meeting Law by failing to 
include on the notice for the February 10 meeting the specific grant applications it planned to 
discuss and the date of the meeting minutes it planned to approve during that meeting.  
 
We resolve this matter by informal action, in accordance with 940 CMR 29.07(2)(a), 
after reviewing the original complaint, the Council’s response to the complaint, the complaint 
filed with our office requesting further review, and the notice and minutes of the Council 
meeting held on February 10.  We find that the Council violated the Open Meeting Law by 
failing to include the specific grant applications it planned to discuss during the February 10 
meeting but did not violate the law in the other way alleged.     
 
The Open Meeting Law requires that public bodies post notice of every meeting “at least 
48 hours prior to such meeting” and that every notice include “the date, time and place of such 
meeting and a listing of topics that the chair reasonably anticipates will be discussed at the 
meeting.”  G.L. c. 30A, § 20(b).  Public bodies are required to list topics on a notice with

“sufficient specificity to reasonably advise the public of the issues to be discussed at the 
meeting.”  940 CMR 29.03(1) (b).  We generally consider a topic sufficiently specific when a 
reasonable member of the public could read the topic and understand the anticipated nature of 
the public body’s discussion.  See OML 2015-35.2   
 
Here, notice for the Council’s February 10 meeting listed five topics, including 
“Review/approval of minutes from last meeting,” “Review & discuss grant modifications & 
status of previously awarded grants,” and “Grant application voting.”  We find that the topic 
listed on the notice with respect to the approval of minutes provided the public with sufficient 
information where it identified the specific minutes that the Council anticipated approving during 
the meeting, that is, the minutes from its last meeting.  During the February 10 meeting, the 
Council approved the minutes of its December 28, 2020, meeting, which was the last meeting 
prior to the February 10 meeting.  As such, we find that the Council did not violate the Open 
Meeting Law.  See OML 2020-34; OML 2017-92; OML 2013-199.  We acknowledge the 
Council’s commitment to listing the specific dates of the meeting minutes it plans to approve on 
all future notices.     
 
However, we find that the Council violated the Open Meeting Law by failing to include 
on the notice a list of the specific grant applicants that the chair anticipated discussing.  See 
OML 2021-36; OML 2018-96; OML 2017-33.  During the February 10 meeting, the Council 
reviewed two grants it had previously awarded and considered 26 new applications.  The notice 
must include details about each grant application, including the identity of the applicant, and/or 
the address that is the subject of an application to be considered by the public body.  This level of 
detail is intended to provide members of the public with sufficient information to understand 
what business is being transacted by the public body and the opportunity to attend and observe 
deliberations in which they are interested.  See OML 2016-167; OML 2011-11.  Furthermore, we 
remind the Council that the specific information must be included on or with the notice itself; it 
is not acceptable for a meeting notice to refer members of the public to another location, such as 
a Town Clerk’s office, to find the required information.  See OML 2019-22; Town of Swansea v. 
Healey, 2018 WL 8692757, at *3 (Suffolk Sup. Ct. October 29, 2018).   
 
For the reasons stated above, we find that the Council violated the Open Meeting Law by 
failing to list on the notice for its February 10 meeting the specific grant applicants it planned to 
discuss.  We order the Council’s immediate and future compliance with the Open Meeting Law, 
and caution that future similar violations may be considered evidence of intent to violate the law. 
 
We now consider the complaint addressed by this determination to be resolved.  This 
determination does not address any other complaints that may be pending with our office or the 
Council.  Please feel free to contact our office at (617) 963-2540 if you have any questions 
regarding this letter.
