 RE: Open Meeting Law Complaint 
 
Dear Mr. Taylor:  
 
 This office received a complaint from Douglas Segrin on June 7, 2022, alleging that the 
Sandisfield Planning Board (the “Board”) violated the Open Meeting Law, G.L. c. 30A, §§ 18-
25.1  The complaint was originally filed with the Board on March 22, and you responded, on 
behalf of the Board, by letter dated April 13.  The complaint alleges that the Board failed to 
provide adequate alternative means of public access to its March 8 and 9 meetings, as the 
meetings were inaccessible by telephone.   
 
We resolve this matter by informal action, in accordance with 940 CMR 29.07(2)(a), 
after reviewing the original complaint, the Board’s response to the complaint, the complaint filed 
with our office requesting further review, and the notices and open session minutes of the Board 
meetings held on March 8 and 9.  We find that the Board did not violate the Open Meeting Law 
in the way alleged.  
 
 The Open Meeting Law requires that meetings of public bodies be open and accessible to 
the public unless an executive session is convened.  See G.L. c. 30A, §§ 20(a), 21.  On February 
15, 2022, Governor Baker signed into law An Act Relative to Extending Certain COVID-19 
Measures Adopted During the State of Emergency, Chapter 22 of the Acts of 2022 (“Chapter 
22”).  Section 20 of Chapter 22 extended, until July 15, 2022, the remote meeting provisions 
established in Governor Baker's March 12, 2020, Order Suspending Certain Provisions of the

Open Meeting Law, G.L. c. 30A, § 20 (“Executive Order”).  Under Governor Baker's Executive 
Order, public bodies were permitted to meet entirely remotely, provided that the public body 
provided adequate, alternative means of public access to the deliberations of the public 
body.  “Adequate, alternative means” may include, without limitation, providing public access 
through telephone, internet, or satellite enabled audio or video conferencing or any other 
technology that enables the public to clearly follow the proceedings of the public body in real 
time.  See OML 2021-135.2 
 
 The Board posted notices for meetings to be held on March 8 and March 9. 3  Both notices 
stated, “Sandisfield Meetings is [sic] inviting you to come in person or attend a scheduled Zoom 
meeting.”  The notices provided information necessary to access the meeting, including a link to 
the Zoom meeting, several telephone numbers the public could use to dial into the meeting, and 
the Webinar ID number required to log into the meeting.  In addition, the notice included the 
physical location of the meeting.  Both the March 8 and March 9 meetings were held as planned 
and certain Board members participated remotely, while others were present in the physical 
location for each meeting.   
 
Mr. Segrin alleges that when he attempted to attend the March 8 meeting by calling in via 
telephone, he was unable to connect.  During the March 9 meeting, the Board tried calling in to 
its own meeting via telephone but was unable to connect and access the meeting using this dial-
up function.  Because the Board held its March 8 and 9 meetings in-person at a location that was 
physically open and accessible to the public, the Board was not required to provide a method of 
alternative remote access to the meeting.  See OML 2021-148; OML 2021-147.  Nevertheless, 
we note that both meetings were accessible through Zoom’s video conferencing function, which 
qualifies as an adequate alternative means for the public to attend remote meetings.  See OML 
2021-10; OML 2020-111.  As the Board is not required to provide more than one means of 
adequate, alternative access, we find that the Board did not violate the Open Meeting Law where 
it provided both in-person access and access through the web-based Zoom platform, without also 
providing access by telephone.  See OML 2021-91. 
 
For the reasons stated above, we find that the Board did not violate the Open Meeting 
Law.  We now consider the complaint addressed by this determination to be resolved.  This 
determination does not address any other complaints that may be pending with the Board or with 
our office.  Please feel free to contact our office at (617) 963-2540 if you have any questions 
regarding this letter.
