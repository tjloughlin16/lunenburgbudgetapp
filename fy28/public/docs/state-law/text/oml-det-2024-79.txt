FACTS 
 
We find the facts as follows.  The Board posted notice for a meeting to be held on July 
12, 2023, at 4:30 P.M.  The notice listed nine topics.  One specific topic was listed as follows:  
 
Why HMP and MVP are important in light of recent events3 
-flooding 
-road damage 
-River bank erosion 
 
The July 12, 2023, meeting was held as planned.  During the meeting, the Board 
discussed the town’s Hazard Mitigation and Municipal Vulnerability Plan.  Specifically, the 
Board discussed the recent floods and the damage caused to town roads.  Two areas of concern 
identified by the Board were Horrigan Road, which had to be closed due to two collapsed 
culverts, and Carson Avenue where bank erosion occurred due to the removal of a dam.  Town 
Administrator Carl McKinney explained that the flooded locations had previously been 
identified in the Municipal Vulnerability Plan and grant money should assist with repairs.  Chair 
Norcross then expressed his concerns with the possible trapping of beavers and the removal of 
two beaver dams at Conte Heights off Horrigan Road.  He mentioned that he had reached out to 
the Environmental Police because he thought the beaver dams might be part of a wildlife 
sanctuary.   
 
DISCUSSION 
 
The Open Meeting Law requires that, except in an emergency, “a public body shall post 
notice of every meeting at least 48 hours prior to such meeting, excluding Saturdays, Sundays 
and legal holidays.”  G.L. c. 30A, § 20(b).  The notice must be printed in a legible, easily 
understandable format and must contain the date, time and place of the meeting, as well as a 
listing of topics that the chair reasonably anticipates will be discussed.  Id.  The list of topics 
shall have “sufficient specificity to reasonably advise the public of the issues to be discussed at 
the meeting.”  940 CMR 29.03(l)(b).  We generally consider a topic to be sufficiently specific 
when a reasonable member of the public could read the topic and understand the anticipated 
nature of the public body’s discussion.  See OML 2015-35; OML 2013-39.
4   
 
The complaint alleges that Chair Norcross improperly discussed the removal of beaver 
dams on Horrigan Road during the July 12, 2023, meeting where that topic was not specifically 
listed on the notice.  The Board maintains that the discussion fell within the topic listed as “Why 
HMP and MVP are important in light of recent events” as the matter related to the flooding that 
had occurred in town.  While we acknowledge that the notice did not specifically indicate that 
the Board would discuss the beaver dams on Horrigan Road, the Open Meeting Law does not

require a public body to list all tangential topics related to a discussion nor does the law require a 
public body to anticipate the course of deliberation or the outcome of a discussion, only that it 
identify the discussion topic with sufficient specificity to reasonably advise the public of the 
issues to be discussed at the meeting.  See G.L. c. 30A, § 20(b); 940 CMR 29.03(l)(b); OML 
2015-73; OML 2014-127; OML 2014-58; OML 2014-38.  Although a close call, we are satisfied  
that the discussion of the beaver dams raised by Chair Norcross related to his concerns with the 
recent flooding that had occurred in town, the damage to specific roads and the causes of the 
flooding, and therefore find that the topic as listed on the notice here was sufficient to reasonably 
advise the public of the issues to be discussed at the meeting.  See G.L. c. 30A, § 20(b); see also 
OML 2023-114; OML 2013-152.   
 
CONCLUSION 
 
For the reasons stated above, we find that the Board did not violate the Open Meeting 
Law.  We now consider the complaint addressed by this determination to be resolved.  This 
determination does not address any other complaints that may be pending with the Board or with 
our office.  Please feel free to contact our office at (617) 963-2540 if you have any questions 
regarding this letter.
