RE: Open Meeting Law Complaint-Amended 
Dear Attorney Mead: 
This office received a complaint from Stephen Morgan on October 27, 2016 alleging 
that the Ashland Board of Health (the "Board") violated the Open Meeting Law, G.L. c. BOA, 
§§ 18-25. The complaint was originally filed with the Board on or about October 14, 2016 
and you responded, on behalf of the Board, by letter dated October 26, 2016. In his 
complaint, Mr. Morgan alleges that the Board deliberated via an outside mediator hired by the 
Board. 
On September 7, 2017 we issued a determination resolving this complaint in which we 
found the Board did not violate the Open Meeting Law based on our finding that no 
communication had occurred between or among a quorum of the Board. The complainant has 
since provided us with emails to support the allegation cited in his complaint. Following a 
further review, we amend the facts in this decision, resolving the matter by informal action, 
in accordance with 940 CMR 29.07(2)(a), and find that the Board did not violate the Open 
Meeting Law. 
The complaint alleges that a third party mediator facilitated deliberation between the 
Board in violation of the Open Meeting Law. In support of the complaint, the complainant 
points to a September 20, 2016 meeting during which the Board decided to engage in 
mediation to resolve conflicts among its members. Hours before that meeting, the mediator 
had sent an email to the Board outlining the mediation process and asking a series of "intake" 
questions to assist the mediator in creating a strategy for the mediation, which would occur in 
open session. Two Board members sent individual emails to the mediator concerning the 
mediation process and three of the five Board members had individual phone conversations 
with the mediator during which they answered the mediator's questions. The mediator did not 
otherwise share those individual communications with other Board members. See OML

2013-1861 citing District Attorney for the Northern District v. School Committee of Wayland. 
451 Mass. 561, 570-571 (2009) ("a public body may not use a non-member, such as a staff 
member, to facilitate communication on matters that the Board would otherwise save for 
discussion at an open meeting"). Because the Board members' individual conversations with 
the mediator did not reach a quorum of the Board, we find that the Board did not violate the 
Open Meeting Law. 
We now consider the complaint addressed by this determination to be resolved.
