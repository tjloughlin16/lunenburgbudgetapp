RE: Open Meeting Law Complaint 
Dear Mr. McCormick: 
This office received a complaint from Ronald Beaty, dated September 14, 2015 alleging 
that the Dennis Board of Selectmen (the "Board") violated the Open Meeting Law, G.L. c. 30A, 
§§ 18-25. Specifically, the complaint alleges that the Board failed to timely respond to a request 
for executive session meeting minutes and failed to periodically review its executive session 
meeting minutes. The complaint was originally filed with the Board on August 17, 2015. The 
Board responded by letter dated September 1, 2015. 
Following our review, we find that the Board did not violate the Open Meeting Law. In 
reaching this determination, we reviewed the complaint filed with the Board, the Board's 
response with attachments, and the complaint filed with our office. Finally, we spoke with 
Board Counsel Michele Randazzo by telephone on November 20, 2015 and January 25, 2016. 
On August 2, 2015, Mr. Beaty sent an email to the Board requesting access to the 
Board's executive session meeting minutes from the years 2010-2014. He also requested all 
documents and exhibits used during those meetings. The Board responded to Mr. Beaty's 
request via letter dated August 10, 2015, explaining that it intended to conduct a review of his 
request at its August 25, 2015 meeting, which was the next regularly scheduled Board meeting. 
On August 25, 2015, the Board met in executive session and voted to release those responsive 
executive session minutes it deemed appropriate for public release. On September 1, 2015, the 
Board forwarded to Mr. Beaty copies of the responsive executive session minutes, along with an 
explanation of the basis for redacting or withholding certain meeting minutes from disclosure. 
FACTS 
===PAGE 2===
The Board explained in its response to the complaint that both the Board and the Chair 
periodically review executive session meeting minutes to determine whether they can be 
released. We confirmed with the Board's Counsel that the Board, in fact, conducted a review of 
its executive session minutes in April 2015 and again in October 2015. 
DISCUSSION 
The Open Meeting Law requires a public body to "create and maintain accurate minutes 
of all meetings, including executive sessions." G.L. c. 30A, § 22(a); OML 2013-47; OML 2013-
16; OML 2012-29.1 A public body, or its chair or designee, must, at reasonable intervals, review 
the minutes of executive sessions to determine if continued nondisclosure of those minutes is 
warranted. G.L. c. 30A, § 22(g)(1). Upon request by any person to inspect or copy the minutes 
of an executive session or any portion thereof, the public body must respond to the request 
within 10 days following receipt and must release any such minutes not covered by an 
exemption. G.L. c. 30A, § 22(g)(2). If the public body has determined, prior to the request, that 
the requested executive session minutes may be released, it should respond to the requester 
directly, notifying him or her whether those minutes are available. See OML 2013-105; OML 
2013-99. If the body previously determined that executive session minutes should remain 
confidential because publication would defeat the lawful purposes of the executive session, it 
should respond by stating the reason why the minutes continue to be withheld. Id And if, at the 
time of a request, the public body has not conducted a review of the minutes to determine 
whether continued nondisclosure is warranted, the body must perform such a review and release 
the minutes, if appropriate, no later than its next meeting or within 30 days, whichever occurs 
first. G.L. c. 3OA, § 22(g)(2). In such circumstances, the body should still respond to the 
requestor within 10 days, notifying him or her that it is conducting this review. See OML 2013-
99. 
The Board followed the procedure as described above. The Board conducted a periodic 
review of all its executive session minutes in April 2015. See G.L. c. 30A, § 22(g)(1). When 
Mr. Beaty requested five years' worth of executive session minutes, the Board sent him a letter 
within 10 days responding to the request. See G.L. c. 30A, § 22(g)(2). At its next scheduled 
meeting, the Board reviewed Mr. Beaty's request, and voted to release those responsive 
executive session minutes it deemed appropriate for public release. Id. Within thirty days of the 
request, the Board forwarded to Mr. Beaty copies of the responsive executive session minutes, 
along with an explanation of the basis for redacting or withholding certain meeting minutes from 
disclosure. Id Because the Board followed the procedure for reviewing executive session 
minutes and responding to a request for executive session minutes, we find that the Board has 
complied with the Open Meeting Law. 
CONCLUSION 
For the reasons stated above, we find that the Board did not violate the Open Meeting 
Law. We now consider this matter closed. Please be advised that this letter does not resolve any
