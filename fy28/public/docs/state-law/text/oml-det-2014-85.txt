RE: Open Meeting Law Complaint 
Dear Attorney Angelini: 
This office received a complaint filed by Peter and Jill E. Mann (the complainants) 
alleging that the Narragansett Regional School Committee (the Committee) violated the Open 
Meeting Law, G.L. c. 30A, §§ 18-25. The complaint was originally filed with the Committee on 
or about March 29, 2014, and the Committee responded by letter dated May 22, 2014. This 
office received a request for further review by email dated May 23, 2014. 
In the complaint, the Manns specifically allege that the Committee failed to discuss a 
topic listed on the agenda for its December 18, 2013 meeting; that the Committee failed to 
approve in a timely manner and disclose the minutes of its January 8, 2014 executive session 
meeting; and that the Committee failed to notify the complainants of a topic listed on the agenda 
for its January 15, 2014 meeting. Following our review, we find that the Committee violated the 
Open Meeting Law by failing to review the requested executive session minutes within 30 days. 
We do not find this violation intentional. Additionally, we find that the purpose for the 
Committee's January 8, 2014 executive session has expired, thus continued non-disclosure of 
those minutes is not warranted. We decline to review the remainder of the complainants' 
allegations due to their untimeliness.1 
In reaching a detennination, we reviewed the March 29, 2014 complaint; the 
Committee's May 22, 2014 response; and the May 23, 2014 request for further review filed with 
this office. We also reviewed the minutes of the Committee's January 8, 2014 executive session, 
in camera, as well as the minutes of its January 15, 2014 open meeting. Additionally, we

FACTS 
We find the facts as follows. The allegations at issue here stem from complaints levied 
against Superintendent Ruth Miller and Principal Shawn Rickan by the complainants. On 
January 8, 2014, the Committee entered into a properly noticed executive session under G.L. 
c. 30A, § 21(a)(1) (Purpose 1) to consider the complaints against Superintendent Miller and 
Principal Rickan. The complainants were present, as were the individuals who were the subjects 
of the executive session. 
On February 19, 2014, the complainants requested the minutes of the January 8, 2014 
executive session. The Committee responded by letter dated February 26, 2014, stating that the 
minutes were unavailable because they were not yet approved. On March 29, 2014, the 
complainants requested that our office review further the Committee's response to their minutes 
request. On May 22, 2014, the Committee met and approved the minutes. At that time, the 
Committee declined to release the minutes, asserting an exemption to the Public Records Law. 
DISCUSSION 
Executive session minutes may be withheld from disclosure to the public "as long as 
publication may defeat the lawful purposes of the executive session, but no longer." G.L. c. 
30A, § 22(f). When the purpose for a valid executive session has been served, the minutes and 
any documents or exhibits used at the session must be disclosed, unless the attorney-client 
privilege or an exemption to the Public Records Law applies. Id. The burden of justifying 
continued nondisclosure of executive session minutes lies with the public body. Foudy v. 
Amherst-Pelham Regional School Committee, 402 Mass. 179, 184 (1988). 
The Open Meeting Law also requires that, upon request by any person to inspect or copy 
the minutes of an executive session or any portion thereof, a public body must respond to the 
request within 10 days and provide any minutes that it has determined may be released. G.L. c. 
30A, § 22(g)(2). If the body has not performed a review of the requested minutes as of the date 
of the request, it must do so no later than the body's next meeting or within 30 days, whichever 
first occurs. Id. 
Here, the Committee responded to the complainant's request for minutes within 10 days. 
However, it failed to conduct a review of the requested executive session minutes within the time 
frame specified by the Law. See G.L. c. 30A, § 22(g)(2). Furthermore, the basis for the 
Committee's initial refusal to provide the minutes - that they had not been approved at the time 
of the request - was improper. Executive session minutes should be timely approved. See OML 
2013-195; OML 2013-180. Failure to approve minutes in a timely manner is not grounds for

withholding them. Minutes may only be withheld if publication may defeat the lawful purposes 
of the executive session. G.L. c. 30A, § 22(f). 
We find that the purpose for the Committee's January 8, 2014 executive session expired 
prior to the date of the complainants' minutes request. The executive session whose minutes are 
at issue here involved complaints against certain public officials. The Committee's January 30, 
2014 letter to the complainants demonstrates that, as of that date, the Committee considered the 
complaints that were the subject of the January 8, 2014 executive session resolved. Therefore, 
we find that the purpose for the executive session had expired at that time, and the January 8, 
2014 executive session minutes relating to the complaints against Superintendent Miller and 
Principal Rickan should be released, unless the attorney/client privilege or an exemption to the 
Public Records Law applies to shield them, in whole or in part, from disclosure. See G.L. 
c. 3OA, § 22(f). 
CONCLUSION 
For the reasons stated above, we find that the Board violated the Open Meeting Law by 
failing to review its January 8, 2014 executive session minutes within 30 days of a request. We 
additionally find that the purpose for the January 8, 2014 executive session has expired. 
Accordingly, we order: 
1. The Committee's immediate and future compliance with the Open Meeting Law, 
and caution that future similar conduct may be considered evidence of intent to violate the Law; 
2. The release of the minutes of the Committee's January 8, 2014 executive session, 
unless the attorney/client privilege or an exemption to the Public Records Law applies to shield 
them, in whole or in part, from disclosure. 
We now consider the complaint addressed by this determination to be resolved. This 
determination does not address any other complaints that may be pending with our office or the 
Committee. Please feel free to contact this office at (617) 963-2540 if you have any questions.
