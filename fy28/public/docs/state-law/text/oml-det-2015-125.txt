RE: Open Meeting Law Complaint 
Dear Attorney Valvo: 
This office received a complaint from Laura Krantz on July 3, alleging that the 
University of Massachusetts Board of Trustees Advancement Committee (the "Committee") 
violated the Open Meeting Law, G.L. c. 30A, §§ 18-25.1 Specifically, the complaint alleges that 
the Committee met on June 3 and held a discussion without a quorum present and failed to 
record minutes of that meeting. The complaint was originally filed with the Committee on June 
8. The Committee responded by letter dated June 23. 
Following our review, we resolve this complaint by informal action in accordance with 
940 CMR 29.07(2)(a), and find that the Committee did not violate the Open Meeting Law. The 
Committee is a standing Committee of the University of Massachusetts Board of Trustees and is 
comprised of eight members. The complaint alleges that the Committee held a meeting on June 
3 and discussed business without a quorum present. The Committee did in fact meet without a 
quorum present, as confirmed by the Committee's response to the complaint. The Open Meeting 
Law defines "deliberation" as, "an oral or written communication through any medium, 
including electronic mail, between or among a quorum of a public body on any public business 
within its jurisdiction." G.L. c. 30A, § 18. The Open Meeting Law defines "meeting" as, "a 
deliberation by a public body with respect to any matter within the body's jurisdiction." Id. 
Finally, the Law defines "quorum" as, "a simple majority of the members of the public body, 
unless otherwise provided in a general or special law, executive order or other authorizing 
provision." Id. Here, because a quorum of the Committee was not present, it did not deliberate, 
thus there was no meeting requiring compliance with the Open Meeting Law. See G.L. c. 30A, §
