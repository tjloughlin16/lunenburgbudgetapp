 RE: Open Meeting Law Complaint 
Dear Attorney Costa: 
This Office received a complaint from Ryan Lapierre on July 17, 2020, alleging that the 
Townsend Board of Water Commissioners (the “Board”) violated the Open Meeting Law, G.L. 
c. 30A, §§ 18-25. The complaint was originally filed with the Board on or about June 23, and the 
Board did not respond.1, 2 In his complaint, Mr. Lapierre alleges that the Board deliberated and 
voted on a topic at the Board’s June 22 meeting that was not included on the meeting notice. 
We resolve this complaint by informal action in accordance with 940 CMR 29.07(2)(a), 
and find that the Board did not violate the Open Meeting Law. In reaching this determination, we 
reviewed the original complaint and the complainant’s request for further review. We also 
emailed with the Board’s counsel on several occasions in November and December of 2020. 
Finally, we emailed Mr. Lapierre on December 3, but did not receive a response.  
The Open Meeting Law requires that all meeting notices “contain the date, time and place 
of such meeting and a listing of topics that the chair reasonably anticipates will be discussed at 
the meeting.” G.L. c. 30A, § 20(b) (emphasis added). A public body may discuss a truly 
unanticipated topic at a meeting, although the Attorney General strongly recommends that public 
bodies, as a matter of best practice, table discussion of topics that might be of significant interest

 At its June 22 meeting, the Board discussed and voted to recommend a finalist, Ken 
Goode, to the Board of Selectmen for appointment as Water Superintendent. This topic was not 
listed on the notice for the June 22 meeting. The Board also voted to increase the salary offer to 
Mr. Goode, as he had received another offer of employment. We understand from the Board’s 
counsel that the Board’s Chair at the time, Nathan Mattila, did not anticipate that the Board 
would discuss recommending Mr. Goode at the June 22 meeting until he learned, at the meeting, 
that the recommendation was time sensitive as Mr. Goode had received another offer. Absent 
sufficient evidence to the contrary, we credit a public body’s account of the facts. See OML 
2019-73; OML 2017-93. Therefore, we find that the Board’s discussion and vote regarding 
recommending Mr. Goode, and increasing the salary offer, were unanticipated by the Chair prior 
to the meeting and find no violation of the Open Meeting Law. Despite declining to find a 
violation, we take this opportunity to remind the Board that the Attorney General strongly 
encourages public bodies, as a best practice for the future, to postpone discussion and action on 
topics that are controversial or may be of particular interest to the public if the topic was not 
listed in the meeting notice and can wait until a subsequent meeting. See OML 2018-119. 
 We now consider the complaint addressed by this determination to be resolved. This 
determination does not address any other complaints that may be pending with our Office or the 
Board. Please feel free to contact the Division at (617) 963 - 2540 if you have any questions.
