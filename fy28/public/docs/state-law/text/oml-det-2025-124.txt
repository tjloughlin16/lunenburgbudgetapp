DISCUSSION 
 
Within 14 business days of receiving an Open Meeting Law complaint, the chair of a 
public
 body must distribute the complaint to all members of the public body; the public body 
must meet to review the complaint; and the public body must respond to the complainant in 
writing, providing a copy of the complaint and response to our office. See G.L. c. 30A, § 23(b); 
940 CMR 29.05(5). A public body may designate an individual, such as legal counsel or the 
chair, to respond to the complaint on the body’s behalf. It is permissible for the complaint to be 
forwarded to legal counsel prior to the public body meeting to review the complaint so that legal 
counsel can review the complaint and draft a proposed response. See OML 2018-134.
5 However, 
before the response is sent to the complainant and our office, the public body must meet to 
review the complaint and authorize the response; the public body may not remove itself from the 
process of reviewing and responding to the complaint. See OML 2021-185; OML 2020-164. 
With respect to the public body’s review of the complaint during the meeting, it is the public 
body’s prerogative as to whether it will discuss the substance of the complaint or simply review 
the complaint and authorize a response; the public body is not required to discuss the substance 
of the complaint in detail. See OML 2021-128; OML 2019-40; OML 2017-96.  
   
 The complaint alleges that the Board violated the Open Meeting Law by failing to meet 
to review the December 20 complaint prior to delegating the responsibility of responding to the 
complaint. Based on the information before us, including the Board’s response and the video 
recording of its January 15, 2025, meeting, we find that the Board did not violate the Open 
Meeting Law where it met to review the complaint and authorize legal counsel to respond prior 
to the response being sent to the Complainant or this office. It does not violate the Open Meeting 
Law for legal counsel to review an Open Meeting Law complaint and propose a response prior to 
the public body reviewing the complaint and authorizing the response. Instead, what is required 
is that the public body review the complaint and authorize the response prior to the response 
being sent to the complainant and our office. 
 
CONCLUSION 
 
For the reasons stated above, we find that the Board did not violate the Open Meeting 
Law as alleged. We now consider the complaint addressed by this determination to be resolved.
