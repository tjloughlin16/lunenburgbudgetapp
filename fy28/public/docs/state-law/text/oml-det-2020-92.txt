 RE: Open Meeting Law Complaint 
 
Dear Attorney Goodman: 
 
This office received a complaint from Richard Bell on February 27, 2020, alleging that 
the Richmond Long Range Sustainability Working Group (the “Group”) violated the Open 
Meeting Law, G.L. c. 30A, §§ 18-25. The complaint was originally filed with the Group on 
December 20, 2019, and, after receiving an extension of time from the Division of Open 
Government, the Group responded by letter dated January 28, 2020. The complaint alleges that 
the Group failed to timely create and approve minutes for four meetings, and that the Group 
failed to post these meeting minutes to the Town of Richmond’s website.  
 
We resolve this matter by informal action, in accordance with 940 CMR 29.07(2)(a), 
after reviewing the original complaint, the Group’s response,1 and the request for further review. 
We also communicated with the Complainant by telephone and with counsel2 by electronic mail.    
 
The Group held meetings on March 1, 2018; June 14, 2018; March 26, 2019; September 
26, 2019; and January 21, 2020. During the Group’s January 21, 2020, meeting, it approved 
minutes for its four previous meetings.3 The complaint alleges that the Group failed to timely 
create and approve minutes for its March 1, 2018; June 14, 2018; March 26, 2019; and 
September 26, 2019, meetings.  
 
The Open Meeting Law requires that public bodies “create and maintain accurate minutes 
of all meetings, including executive sessions . . ..” G.L. c. 30A, § 22(a). These minutes must be

created and approved by the public body in a timely manner. G.L. c. 30A, § 22(c). The Attorney 
General’s Open Meeting Law regulations provide that “timely manner” means within the next 
three public body meetings, or within 30 days, whichever is later. 940 C.M.R. 29.11(2). 
However, we recommend that minutes be approved at the next meeting whenever possible. See 
OML 2019-133; OML 2019-39; OML 2014-15.4 Here, the Group failed to timely approve the 
minutes of its March 1, 2018, meeting where it did not approve those minutes until its January 
21, 2020, meeting, which was the fourth meeting thereafter. With regard to the other meeting 
minutes approved at the Group’s January 21, 2020, meeting, we find that those minutes were 
timely approved. Therefore, we find that the Group violated the Open Meeting Law by failing to 
timely approve the minutes of its March 1, 2018, meeting only. 
   
With respect to the allegation that the Group failed to post meeting minutes to the Town’s 
website, the Open Meeting Law does not require that public bodies post their minutes to a 
website or elsewhere, but only that minutes be approved in a timely manner and made available, 
if they exist, within 10 days of a request. G.L. c. 30A, § 22(c); see OML 2020-78; OML 2017-
98; OML 2017-25. Therefore, we find no violation of the Open Meeting Law with regard to this 
allegation.5   
 
For the reasons stated above, we find that the Group violated the Open Meeting Law 
when it failed to timely create and approve minutes for its March 1, 2018, meeting. We find that 
the Group did not violate the Law in the other ways alleged. We order the Group’s immediate 
and future compliance with the Open Meeting Law’s requirements, and we caution that similar 
future violations may be considered evidence of intent to violate the law.   
 
We now consider the complaint addressed by this determination to be resolved. This 
determination does not address any other complaints that may be pending with our office or the 
Group. Please feel free to contact our office at (617) 963-2540 if you have any questions 
regarding this letter.
