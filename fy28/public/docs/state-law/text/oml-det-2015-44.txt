RE: Open Meeting Law Complaint 
Dear Attorney Goldberg: 
This office received a complaint from Mr. Patrick Higgins, dated December 26, 2014, 
alleging that the Dedham Board of Selectmen (the "Board") violated the Open Meeting Law, 
G.L. c. 30A, §§ 18-25. The complaint was originally filed with the Board on November 23, 
2014, and the Board responded to the original complaint by letter dated December 23, 2014. In 
his complaint, Mr. Higgins alleges that the Board failed to list the name of a particular litigation 
matter discussed in executive session. 
We appreciate the patience of the parties while we reviewed this matter. Following our 
review, we find that the Board did not violate the Open Meeting Law. In reaching a 
determination, we reviewed the original complaint; the Board's response; and the request for 
further review filed with our office. We also reviewed, in camera, unreleased minutes of an 
executive session held by the Board on November 20, 2014. 
We find the facts as follows. The Board is a four-member public body which meets at 
regular intervals to discuss and act on matters of relevance to the management of the Town of 
Dedham ("the Town"). The Board held a meeting on November 20, 2014. The meeting agenda 
listed the following anticipated discussion topic: 
Executive Session: Pursuant to G.L. c. 30A, Section 21(a)(3) Pending Litigation [ ] if an 
open meeting may have a detrimental effect on the bargaining or litigating position of the 
public body and the chair so declares. 
The agenda does not list the name of the litigation matter. During the November 20, 
2014 meeting, the Board entered executive session and discussed the potential settlement of an 
existing lawsuit against the Town.

DISCUSSION 
A public body may enter into executive session for any of ten purposes enumerated in the 
Open Meeting Law. See G.L. c. 30A, § 21(a). Under the law, "a precise statement of the reason 
for convening in executive session is necessary... because that is the only notification given to 
the public that a [public body] would conduct business in private, and the only way the public 
would know if the reason for doing so was proper or improper." District Attorney for the 
Northern District v. School Committee of Wayland, 455 Mass. 561, 567 (2009). One 
permissible reason to hold an executive session is "to discuss strategy with respect to collective 
bargaining or litigation if an open meeting may have a detrimental effect on the collective 
bargaining or litigating position of the public body and the chair so declares." G.L. c. 30A, § 
21(a)(3) ("Purpose 3"). The mere possibility of litigation is not sufficient to invoke executive 
session Purpose 3. See OML 2012-5; OML 2011-30. Litigation must be pending or clearly and 
imminently threatened or otherwise demonstrably likely. See Perryman v. School Committee of 
Boston, 17 Mass. App. Ct. 346, 352 (1983); OML 2012-43. When entering executive session 
pursuant to Purpose 3, a public body should identify the litigation matter or bargaining unit that 
it intends to discuss, if doing so will not compromise the executive session purpose. See OML 
2014-145; OML 2013-91. While we generally defer to a public body's assessment of whether 
the inclusion of such information would compromise the purpose for an executive session, a 
public body must be able to demonstrate a reasonable basis for that claim if challenged. See 
OML 2015-14. 
Because the minutes of the November 20, 2014 executive session have not been publicly 
released by the Board, we do not recount the details of the litigation matter discussed. We note, 
though, that the discussion involved ongoing litigation against the Town. Furthermore, we find 
that naming the party involved in this matter would have compromised the Board's litigating 
position. G.L. c. 30A, § 21(b)(3). We therefore find that the Board did not violate the Open 
Meeting Law by failing to name the litigation matter discussed. See OML 2014-145. 
For the reasons stated above, we find that the Board did not violate the Open Meeting 
Law. We now consider the complaint addressed by this determination to be resolved. This 
determination does not address any other complaints that may be pending with our office or the 
Board. Please feel free to contact the Division at (617) 963 - 2540 if you have any questions.
