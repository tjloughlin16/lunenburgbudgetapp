to timely post notice of the meeting held on December 16, 2024.  Because we find that the 
Council held a meeting for which notice was not properly posted, we need not address the 
remaining allegations.  In reaching this determination, we reviewed the original complaint, the 
Council’s response to the complaint, and the complaint filed with our office requesting further

FACTS 
 
We find the facts as follows.  The City of Newburyport has adopted its website, 
www.cityofnewburyport.com, as the official method of posting notice.  On Thursday, December 
12, 2024, at 5:09 P.M., the Council posted notice for a meeting to be held on Monday, December 
16, 2024, at 6:30 P.M.  However, the website was not functioning properly and there was a delay 
to the posting whereby the notice did not appear on the website until Friday, December 13, 2024.  
The notice listed the location of the meeting as City Council Chambers at City Hall on 60 
Pleasant Street in Newburyport.  The notice also indicated that the meeting would be accessible 
via Zoom and provided information necessary to access the virtual meeting, including a link to 
the Zoom meeting, several telephone numbers the public could use to dial into the meeting, and 
the Webinar ID number required to log into the meeting.   
 
The notice’s header listed an Executive Session at 6:30 P.M. and then “Regular City 
Council Meeting” at 7:00 P.M.  The first topic listed on the notice was an executive session 
under Purpose 3 “To discuss strategy with respect to collective bargaining or litigation if an open 
meeting may have a detrimental effect on the bargaining or litigating position of the public body 
and the chair so declares.”  The notice identified the litigation matter as “General litigation 
update.”  The notice then listed such topics as “Call to Order,” “Late File,” “Public Comment,” 
and “Mayor’s Comment.”  Following these topics, the notice included numerous other topics for 
open session discussion.  
 
The December 16, 2024, meeting was held as planned.  The Council first convened in 
open session at 6:30 P.M. and Council President Edward Cameron announced that the Council 
would convene in executive session under Purpose 3 “to discuss strategy with respect to 
collective bargaining or litigation if an open meeting may have a detrimental effect on the 
bargaining or litigating position of the public body and the chair so declares as there will be a 
general litigation update.”  President Cameron announced that the Council would reconvene in 
open session after the conclusion of the executive session and the Council then voted 
unanimously by roll call to convene in executive session.  
 
The Council met in executive session for approximately 45 minutes and received updates 
from Attorney North on new and resolved litigation matters.  The Council reconvened in open 
session at 7:14 P.M and discussed the open session topics. 
 
DISCUSSION 
 
The Open Meeting Law was enacted “to eliminate much of the secrecy surrounding the 
deli
berations and decisions on which public policy is based.”  Ghiglione v. School Committee of 
Southbridge, 376 Mass. 70, 72 (1978).  The Law states, in relevant part, that except in an

must include “the date, time and place of such meeting and a listing of topics that the chair 
reasonably anticipates will be discussed at the meeting.”  Id.  For local public bodies, notice shall 
be filed with the municipal clerk and posted in a manner conspicuously visible to the public at all

A municipality may designate a website as an alternative notice posting location for all public 
bodies within the municipality.  940 CMR 29.03(2)(b).   
 
The complaint alleges that the Council violated the Open Meeting Law when it held a 
mee
ting on December 16, 2024, without posting notice of the meeting 48 hours in advance.  The 
Council states that “the meeting was timely posted at 5:09pm on 12/12/24 as indicated on the 
backend of the city’s website.  The next morning (Friday) the City realized the website was not 
functioning properly and there was a delay to the posting.”  The Council further suggests that the 
municipal website was simply malfunctioning, and the City Clerk restored the website within six 
hours of the discovery such that the notice of the December 16, 2024, meeting was timely 
posted.  See 940 CMR 29.03(7) (“If a website becomes inaccessible to members of the public 
within 48 hours of a meeting, not including Saturdays, Sundays, and legal holidays, the 
municipal clerk or other individual responsible for posting notice to the website must restore the 
website to accessibility within six hours of the time, during regular business hours, when such 
individual discovers that the website has become inaccessible.”).  We disagree.  A notice is 
properly posted when it is published on the website such that the public can view it.  See OML 
2018-25.3  Here, based on the Council’s explanation, we find that the December 16, 2024, 
meeting notice was not actually posted to the municipal website, the official method of posting 
notice, for the first time until Friday morning, which was not 48 hours prior to the Monday, 
December 16, 2024, meeting.  As such, we find that the Council violated the Open Meeting Law 
by failing to timely post notice to the municipal website for its December 16, 2024, meeting and 
by holding a meeting for which the legally required notice had not been published.

When the only business to be discussed during an open session are the procedural 
requ
irements for entering executive session, public bodies must list “Open Session” on the 
meeting notice, as this is the only means by which members of the public are informed that a 
public body will, in fact, hold an open meeting that they are permitted to attend.  See OML 2013-
9; OML 2012-68.  Without such notice, members of the public are essentially denied the 
opportunity to attend and learn the reasons for the executive session.  See OML 2013-195.  
However, where additional open session topics are included in a meeting notice, we have not 
required that a public body list “Open Session” as a distinct topic.  See OML 2015-157; OML 
2015-110; 2015-105.   
 
One permissible reason to convene in executive session is “to discuss strategy with 
resp
ect to collective bargaining or litigation if an open meeting may have a detrimental effect on 
the bargaining or litigating position of the public body and the chair so declares.”  G.L. c. 30A, § 
21(a)(3) (“Purpose 3”).  Purpose 3 offers the narrow opportunity to discuss strategy with respect 
to litigation that is pending or clearly and imminently threatened or otherwise demonstrably 
likely; the mere possibility of litigation is not sufficient to invoke Purpose 3.  See Doherty v. 
School Committee of Boston, 386 Mass. 643, 648 (1982); Perryman v. School Committee of 
Boston, 17 Mass. App. Ct. 346, 352 (1983); OML 2020-76; OML 2019-164; OML 2012-5.  We 
have previously determined that executive session Purpose 3 does not cover discussions with 
counsel about general areas of law unrelated to any litigation matter nor does receiving legal 
advice from counsel necessarily constitute discussion of litigation strategy.  See OML 2023-182; 
OML 2021-56; OML 2012-55.     
 
When convening in executive session under Purpose 3, a public body must identify the 
litigation matter to be discussed if doing so will not compromise the lawful purpose for secrecy.  
See OML 2016-12; OML 2013-97.  The purpose of the executive session is to protect the public 
body’s litigating position, in general vis-à-vis opposing parties to the litigation, not to keep 
confidential from the public the existence of the litigation matters or to avoid the inconvenience 
of public scrutiny.  See OML 2020-68; OML 2020-158; OML 2023-189.  Therefore, a public 
body must separately identify, on the notice and in the announcement in open session, each 
litigation matter to be discussed, excluding any matters for which identification would 
compromise the purpose for the executive session.  See OML 2024-168.  While we generally 
defer to a public body’s assessment of whether the inclusion of such information would 
compromise the purpose for the executive session, a public body must be able to demonstrate a 
reasonable basis for such a claim if challenged.  See OML 2015-14. 
 
CONCLUSION 
 
For the reasons stated above, we find that the Council violated the Open Meeting Law by 
failing to timely post notice of the meeting held on December 16, 2024.  We order immediate
