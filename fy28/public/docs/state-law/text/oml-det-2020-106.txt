 RE:        Open Meeting Law Complaint 
 
Dear Ms. Vaughn-MacKenzie: 
 
 This office received a complaint from Jessica Packineau on Jun e 10, 2020, alleging that 
the South Lincoln Planning and Implementation Committee (the “Committee”) violated the 
Open Meeting Law, G.L. c. 30A, §§ 18-25. The complaint was originally filed with the 
Committee on January 22, 2020, and Jennifer Burney, Director of Planning and Land Use,  
responded, on behalf of the Committee, by emails dated January 22, 2020 and January 23, 2020.
1 
The complaint alleges that the Committee violated the Open Meeting Law by failing to create 
and “post” minutes of meetings since October 2018.  
 
 After reviewing the complaint, t he request for further review, and minutes of the 
Committee’s February 5, 2020, meeting, we resolve this complaint by informal action in 
accordance with 940 CMR 29.07(2)(a). We find that the Committee violated the Open Meeting 
Law by failing to timely create and approve meeting minutes. However, we find that the 
Committee did not violate the Open Meeting Law by failing to “post” meeting minutes.

 The complaint alleges that the Committee failed to timely create minutes for meetings 
held after October 2, 2018, and then failed to post those minutes to the municipal website. The 
Open Meeting Law requires that public bodies “create and maintain accurate minutes of all 
meetings, including executive sessions.” G.L. c. 30A, § 22(a). There is no specific requirement 
under the law, as the complaint seems to suggest, that minutes be created at or during a meeting. 
However, minutes must be created and approved by the public body in a timely manner, that is, 
within the next three public body meetings or within 30 days, whichever is later. G.L. c. 30A, § 
22(c); 940 C.M.R. 29.11(2). Here, the Committee met 17 times between January 8, 2019, and the 
time the complaint was filed on January 22, 2020.2 During a February 5, 2020, meeting, the 
Committee approved the minutes from those 17 meetings. We find that minutes from 14 of those  
meetings were not created and approved in a timely manner because when these minutes were 
approved on February 5, 2020, it was between 4 and 17 meetings, and 124 and 394 days 
thereafter.3 See OML 2019-21. As such, we find that the Committee violated the Open Meeting 
Law by failing to approve 14 sets of minutes in a timely manner. We find that the minutes from 
meetings held on October 23, 2019, December 18, 2019, and January 28, 2020, were approved in 
a timely manner as they were approved within the next three meetings.  
 
With respect to the Committee’s failure to “post” meeting minutes to the municipal 
website, the Open Meeting Law does not require that public bodies post their minutes to a 
website or elsewhere, but only that minutes be approved in a timely manner and made available 
within 10 days of a request. G.L. c. 30A, § 22(c); see OML 2017-98; OML 2017-25. 4 Therefore, 
we find no violation of the Open Meeting Law where the Committee did not post meeting 
minutes to the municipal website.5 We recognize that the Committee has now posted these 
minutes to the municipal website. 
 
  For the reasons stated above, w e find that the Committee violated the Open Meeting Law. 
We order the Committee’s immediate and future compliance with the law’s requirements, and 
we caution that similar future violations could be considered evidence of intent to violate the 
law. 
 
We now consider the complaint addressed by this determination to be resolved. This 
determination does not address any other complaints that may be pending with our office or the 
Committee. Please feel free to contact our office at (617) 963-2540 if you have any questions 
regarding this letter.
