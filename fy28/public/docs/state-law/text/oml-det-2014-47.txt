RE: Open Meeting Law Complaint 
Dear Attorney Riley: 
This office received a complaint from Gary Allard, dated January 4, 2014, alleging that 
the Sturbridge Conservation Commission (the "Commission") violated the Open Meeting Law, 
G.L. c. 30A, §§ 18-25. The complaint alleges that the Commission failed to properly notice its 
October 3, 2013 meeting.1 The complaint was originally filed with the Commission on October 
31, 2013. The Commission responded by letter dated November 22, 2013, following an 
extension of time granted by our office. 
Following our review, we find that the Commission violated the Open Meeting Law by 
failing to include a topic reasonably anticipated by the Chair in the notice for its October 3, 2013 
meeting. In reaching this determination, we reviewed the October 31, 2013 complaint filed with 
the Commission; the Commission's November 22, 2013 response; and the January 4, 2013 
complaint filed with our office. Additionally, we reviewed the notice and minutes for the 
Commission's September 19, 2013 and October 3, 2013 meetings. 
FACTS 
On September 19, 2013, Sturbridge Conservation Agent Glenn Colbum visited Mr. 
Allard's property and observed a dam located adjacent to a culvert that has been the subject of an 
action with the Sturbridge Board of Health. At the Commission's September 19, 2013 meeting, 
Mr. Colbum reported his findings to the Commission. The Commission discussed the report and 
considered actions it might take to address any wetlands violations. The Commission decided to

The Sturbridge Conservation Agent visited Mr. Allard's property again sometime in the 
days before the October 3, 2013 meeting, and became concerned about rising water levels on the

The notice for the Commission's October 3, 2013 meeting did not include a topic related 
to Mr. Allard's property. There was, however, a topic listed as "Old Business." During the 
Commissions October 3, 2013 meeting, under the heading of "Old Business," the Commission 
discussed Mr. Allard's property. According to the minutes, Linda Cocalis, Chair of the Board of 
Health, supported removing the dam if needed for septic protection and road safety. 
Commissioner David Barnicle identified three issues to resolve with the property: 1) remove the 
dam by hand; 2) take the removed material off-site; and 3) maintain a pipe to allow free flow of 
the stream. The Commission decided that if Mr. Allard does not remove the dam, then they 
would request assistance from the Department of Environmental Protection or start an 
enforcement process. 
DISCUSSION 
The Open Meeting Law requires that public bodies post notice 48 hours in advance of a 
meeting and include, a "listing of topics that the chair reasonably anticipates will be discussed at 
the meeting." G.L. c. 30A, § 20(b). Public bodies are required to list topics in a meeting notice 
with "sufficient specificity to reasonably advise the public of the issues to be discussed at the 
meeting." 940 CMR 29.03; OML 2012-34.2 
The topic of Mr. Allard's property was reasonably anticipated by the Chair 48 hours 
before the Commission's October 3, 2013 meeting, and therefore should have been included in 
the meeting notice. The Commission discussed the property on September 19, 2013 and decided 
not to take action, but rather to place it on the agenda for its next meeting, which was the 
October 3, 2013 meeting. However, the topic was not included in the notice for that meeting. 
The topic "Old Business" is not sufficiently specific to reasonably advise the public that the 
Commission would discuss Mr. Allard's property. A boilerplate notice topic such as "Old 
Business" is vague and does not offer the public an understanding of the anticipated topics to be 
discussed at that particular meeting. See OML 2013-14. 
The Commission contends that it did not anticipate discussing Mr. Allard's property 
because the Conservation Agent visited Mr. Allard's property again just before the October 3, 
2013 meeting, and thus the Chair did not anticipate that the Conservation Agent would raise an 
issue with Mr. Allard's property more than 48 hours' prior to the meeting. However, the Chair 
should have anticipated revisiting this topic given the discussion and action the Commission took 
at its previous meeting on September 19, 2013. The Chair should therefore have listed the topic 
in the meeting notice

Finally, Mr. Allard contends that he should have been provided with individual notice 
that the Commission would be discussing his property at its October 3, 2013 meeting. The Open 
Meeting Law does not require notice to an individual that a public body may discuss a topic 
involving that individual, unless the public body intends to enter executive session under G.L. c. 
3OA, § 21(a)(1). OML 2013-41; OML 2013-24. 
CONCLUSION 
We find that the Commission violated the Open Meeting Law by failing to include a 
topic reasonably anticipated by the Chair in the notice for its October 3, 2013 meeting. We order 
the Commission's immediate and future compliance with the Open Meeting Law, and caution 
that a similar future violation may be considered an intentional violation of the law. 
We now consider this matter closed. If you have any questions regarding this letter,
