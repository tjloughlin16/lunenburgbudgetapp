DISCUSSION  
 
 The Open Meeting Law states in r elevant part that, “a public body shall post notice of 
every meeting at least 48 hours prior to such meeting ... Notice shall be printed in a legible, 
easily understandable format and shall contain the date, time and place of such meeting and a 
listing of topics that the chair reasonably anticipates will be discussed at the meeting.” G.L. c. 
30A, § 20(b). Public bodies must list reasonably anticipated topics in a meeting notice with 
“sufficient specificity to reasonably advise the public of the issues to be discussed at the 
meeting.” G.L. c. 30A, § 20(b); 940 CMR 29.03(l)(b). We generally consider a topic to include 
sufficient specificity when a reasonable member of the public could read the topic and 
understand the anticipated nature of the public body’s discussion. See OML 2019-70.3  
 
A public body need not list each and every detail about a topic in order to comply with 
the law’s requirements.  See OML 2014-122.  Moreover, the Open Meeting Law does not require 
a public body to anticipate the course of deliberation or the outcome of a discussion, only that it 
identify the discussion topic with sufficient specificity to reasonably advise the public of the 
issues to be discussed at the meeting.  See G.L. c. 30A, § 20(b); 940 CMR 29.03(l)(b); OML 
2014-127; OML 2014-58; OML 2014-38.   
 
The complaint alleges that, during its December 18, 2019, meeting, the Committee 
discussed and voted on a topic, the motion to cease operation of the transfer stations, despite this 
topic not being listed on the notice. The December 18, 2019, meeting notice included a topic to 
discuss “the New Draft of CMW Agreement, Dissolution of District, Marion Withdrawal from 
District and other CMW issues.” During the December 18, 2019, meeting, the Committee 
discussed the costs and benefits of continuing to run a transfer station and the revised charter 
agreement drafted by Attorney Crotty. Mr. Partridge’s motion during the meeting was to suspend 
all operations of the District’s transfer stations effective December 31, 2020 and to discuss the 
continuation of the transfer stations with other towns. Ultimately, the Committee voted to 
suspend the operation of the transfer stations in Marion and Rochester effective December 31, 
2020. We find that the motion regarding the suspension of the transfer stations naturally flowed 
from the Committee’s consideration of the posted agenda topic, which notified the public that the 
Committee planned to discuss dissolution of the District.   
 
Where a public body’s discussion, including a vote, naturally flows from its consideration 
of the posted agenda topic, the notice is sufficient to comply with the requirements of the Open 
Meeting Law. See OML 2018-14; OML 2014-44.  Moreover, it is reasonably foreseeable that a 
public body might take action and hold a vote following discussion of a topic listed on a meeting 
notice, even where the noticed topic does not indicate the possibility of a vote. See OML 2018-
14; OML 2015-66. We find that the notice here was sufficient to inform the public of the 
discussions and motion that occurred, and therefore the Committee did not violate the Open 
Meeting Law.
