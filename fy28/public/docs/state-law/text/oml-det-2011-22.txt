RE: Open Meeting Law Complaint 
Dear Attorney Boylan, 
This office received a complaint filed by William Brown, dated March 28, 2011, alleging 
that the West Newbury Housing Authority (the "Authority") violated the Open Meeting Law, 
G.L. c. 30A, §§ 18-25. The complaint specifically alleges that the Authority convened a 
"meeting to consider action or hear complaints and ended in [Mr. Brown's] dismissal on 
12/20/10." The complaint was first filed with the West Newbury Town Clerk on January 10, 
2011, and subsequently with the Authority on or about January 17, 2011. We received the 
Authority's response to the original complaint on or about March 14, 2011. 
We find that the Authority did not violate the Open Meeting Law. In reaching this 
determination, we reviewed the January 17, 2011 and March 28, 2011 complaints, the 
Authority's March 14, 2011 response, and letters from the Authority to Mr. Brown dated 
December 9 and December 20, 2010. We also reviewed the meeting notice and executive 
session minutes from the Authority's December 14, 2010 meeting. Finally, we spoke with Mr. 
Brown by phone on April 5, 2011 regarding his complaint. 
FACTS 
William Brown was an employee of the West Newbury Housing Authority until 
December 20, 2010. Paula Mountain, the Acting Executive Director of the Authority, sent Mr. 
Brown a letter dated, December 9, 2010, informing him of an upcoming meeting of the

Authority. The letter stated in part that "[t]he purpose of this notice is to inform you that the 
West Newbury Housing Authority will be having its Public Board Meeting on Tuesday 
December 14, 2010 at 5:15 p.m. During this meeting we will be entering into Executive 
[Session]. The purpose is as follows: To consider the discipline or dismissal of, or to hear 
complaints or charges brought against William Brown" (emphasis in original). The letter 
proceeded to explain to Mr. Brown his rights under the Open Meeting Law, G.L. c. 30A, § 21(b). 
The meeting notice for the December 14, 2010 meeting contained the agenda item, "Executive 
Session." 
Mr. Brown chose not to attend the Authority's December 14, 2010 meeting. During the 
executive session, the Authority reviewed complaints against Mr. Brown and his job 
performance 2, and voted to terminate his employment. The executive session lasted 
approximately 40 minutes. 
Following the meeting, Acting Executive Director Mountain sent Mr. Brown a letter, 
dated December 20, 2010, terminating his employment with the Authority. The letter laid out 
the reasons for termination as considered by the Authority during the December 14, 2010 
executive session. Mr. Brown subsequently filed his complaint alleging a violation of the Open 
Meeting Law, and stated to this Office by phone that he was not properly informed of his 
dismissal. His understanding from the December 9, 2010 letter was only that the Authority 
would "consider" his dismissal. He believed that there would be a process following the 
Authority's December 14, 2010 meeting where he could argue his case. Mr. Brown stated that 
he did not understand the December 9, 210 letter to be notice that he could be terminated during 
the December 14, 2010 meeting. 
DISCUSSION 
The Open Meeting Law requires that if a public body enters executive session to "discuss 
the discipline or dismissal of, or complaints or charges brought against, a public officer, 
employee, staff member or individual," that the individual "be notified in writing by the public 
body at least 48 hours prior to the proposed executive session." G.L. c. 30A, § 21(a)(1). 
While not raised by the complaint, we note that several topics in the meeting notice for the Authority's December 
14, 2010 meeting failed to provide sufficient detail as required by the Open Meeting Law and the Attorney 
General's Regulations, 940 CMR 29.03(b). The Regulations require that, "[Ole list of topics shall have sufficient 
specificity to reasonably advise the public of the issues to be discussed at the meeting." Id. Topics that appeared on 
the notice such as "Old Business Gatekeeper Report", "New Business" and "Executive Session" are not sufficiently 
descriptive topics. For an executive session, the Authority is required to provide as much detail in the notice as 
possible without compromising the purpose for entering the executive session. Here, the Authority should have 
listed that topic as "Executive Session to consider the discipline or dismissal of, or to hear complaints or charges 
brought against, an employee." 
2  A public body may enter executive session to "discuss the reputation, character, physical condition or mental 
health, rather than professional competence, of an individual, or to discuss the discipline or dismissal of, or 
complaints or charges brought against, a public officer, employee, staff member or individual." G.L. c. 30A, § 
21(a)(1). While a public body generally may not discuss in executive session the professional competence of an 
individual, it is appropriate to do so when considering discipline or dismissal related to a complaint about job 
performance

Here, the Authority fulfilled that obligation. The Authority sent Mr. Brown a letter on 
December 9, 2010, more than 48 hours before the Authority's December 14, 2010 meeting, and 
stated in writing that the purpose of the meeting was to "consider the discipline or dismissal of, 
or to hear complaints or charges brought against William Brown." Mr. Brown chose not to 
attend the executive session, though he had the legal right to do so and was informed of that right 
in the December 9, 2010 letter. Mr. Brown claims that he understood the letter to indicate that 
the Authority would only be considering his dismissal, but not acting on it. 
The Authority complied with the requirements of the Open Meeting Law by sending Mr. 
Brown a letter stating in clear terms that it was considering his dismissal. The language of the 
letter closely followed the language from G.L. c. 30A, § 21(a)(1). It put Mr. Brown on notice 
that his dismissal would be considered and potentially acted upon by the Authority. While it is 
unfortunate that Mr. Brown misunderstood the letter, and we appreciate the frustration he feels in 
not having had the opportunity to present his case, the Authority acted appropriately in following 
the requirements of the Open Meeting Law. 
CONCLUSION 
We find that the Authority complied with the requirements of the Open Meeting Law in 
providing notice to the complainant ahead the Authority's December 14, 2010 executive session.
