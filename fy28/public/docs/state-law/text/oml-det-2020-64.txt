Galvin & Galvin, PC 10 Enterprise Street, Suite 3 Duxbury, MA 02332-3315  rwgalvin@comcast.net    RE: Open Meeting Law Complaint Dear Attorney Galvin:  This office received a complaint from Mary Parsons on December 30, 2019, alleging that the Southfield Redevelopment Authority Board of Directors (the “Board”) violated the Open Meeting Law, G.L. c. 30A, §§ 18-25. The complaint was originally filed with the Board on November 20 and you responded on behalf of the Board by letter dated November 22.1 In her complaint, Ms. Parsons alleges that, at its November 6 meeting, the Board entered executive session without disclosing the parcel of property to be discussed.2   We resolve this complaint by informal action in accordance with 940 CMR 29.07(2)(a), and find that the Board did not violate the Open Meeting Law as alleged. The Open Meeting Law was enacted “to eliminate much of the secrecy surrounding deliberations and decisions on which public policy is based.” Ghiglione v. School Committee of Southbridge, 376 Mass. 70, 72 (1978). The law requires that meetings of a public body be properly noticed and open to members of the public, unless an executive session is convened. See G.L. c. 30A, §§ 20(a)-(b), 21. Among the ten enumerated purposes for executive session is “Purpose 6,” which permits a public body “[t]o consider the purchase, exchange, lease or value of real property if the chair declares that an open meeting may have a detrimental effect on the negotiating position of the public body.” G.L. c. 30A, §21(a)(6). Purpose 6 is intended to preserve confidentiality in negotiating the value of the property to be purchased, exchanged or leased to avoid putting the                                                              1 Unless otherwise specified, all dates refer to 2019. 2 The complaint also alleges the Board’s attorney may have a conflict of interest as he is also “the Land Use Attorney for the Town of Rockland.” We note that, even if true, this allegation would not constitute a violation of G.L. c. 30A, §§ 18-25. public body at a disadvantage in its negotiations for the property. See District Atty. for the 
Plymouth Dist. v. Selectmen of Middleborough, 395 Mass. 629, 631 (1985). When convening in 
executive session under Purpose 6, a public body must identify the property that is the subject of 
the discussion unless such disclosure would compromise the public body’s negotiating position. 
See OML 2016-112; OML 2016-50. 
 
 Here, the Board entered executive session on November 6 without disclosing the parcel 
of property that would be discussed. Ms. Parsons points to a prior determination by our office in 
which we conclude that the Board did not meet its burden of demonstrating that publicly 
disclosing a specific parcel of land that was the subject of a September 11, 2017, executive 
session would have been detrimental to the Board’s negotiating position. The parcel in question 
was owned by the Navy and we concluded that identifying the property would not harm the 
Board’s negotiating position because the universe of parcels left to negotiate with the Navy were 
limited and known to both the Navy and the public. See OML 2018-26.  
 
 The present situation is substantially different from the September 11, 2017, executive 
session in which we found the prior violation. The Southfield Redevelopment Authority’s Land 
Use Administrator acknowledged during the meeting that the parcel in question was not owned 
by the Navy. The executive session minutes for this meeting have not been released to the public, 
and therefore we do not describe the Board’s discussion in detail. However, after questioning the 
Board’s counsel, we are satisfied that the Board has met its burden of demonstrating that publicly 
disclosing the specific parcel that was discussed during the November 6 executive session would 
have been detrimental to the Board’s negotiating position. Therefore, we find that the Board was 
not required to identify the property it planned to discuss during the November 6 executive 
session and did not violate the Open Meeting Law.  
 
 We now consider the complaint addressed by this determination to be resolved. This 
determination does not address any other complaints that may be pending with our office or the 
Board. Please feel free to contact the Division at (617) 963 - 2540 if you have any questions.
