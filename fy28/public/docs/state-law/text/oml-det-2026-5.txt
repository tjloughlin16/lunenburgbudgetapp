FACTS 
 
We find the facts to be as follows.  The Town of Norwood has not adopted an alternative 
notice posting location and as such public bodies in the Town post physical meeting notices.  
However, the Town also posts copies of meeting notices to the calendar page on the Town’s 
website, https://www.norwoodma.gov/.  The Commission posted physical notice of a meeting to 
be held on July 9 at 7:30 PM.  The Commission also posted a copy of the notice of the July 9 
meeting to the Town’s website.  The notice posted to the website was an image-only PDF and 
not a machine-readable PDF. 
 
The meeting notice listed three main topic headings, one of which was “Public 
Hearings.”  Under the heading “Public Hearings,” the notice listed the topic “Proposed changes 
to Norwood Wetlands Protection Bylaw XXV Regulations, including a revised Fee Schedule 
(Section XII), references to Stormwater Management regulation (Section V), and other minor 
alterations. Continued from: 6/18/25.” 
 
The July 9 meeting was held as planned.  During the meeting, the Commission continued 
a discussion begun during its June 18 meeting regarding proposed revisions to the Norwood 
Wetlands Protection Bylaw XXV Regulations.  Carly Rocklen, the Conservation Planner, 
presented to the Commission proposed revisions and edits to the regulations and shared 
recommendations from the Director and Assistant Director of Community Development.  The 
Conservation Planner then recommended that the Commission adopt the revised regulations, 
accepting some proposed edits and rejecting others.  The Commission voted unanimously to 
approve the revised regulations in the manner recommended by the Conservation Planner.  
 
DISCUSSION 
 
I. The Commission did not violate the Open Meeting Law where the notice for its July 
9 meeting was sufficiently specific. 
 
The Open Meeting Law was enacted “to eliminate much of the secrecy surrounding the 
deliberations and decisions on which public policy is based.” Ghiglione v. Sch. Comm. of 
Southbridge, 376 Mass. 70, 72 (1978).  The Open Meeting Law requires that, except in an 
emergency, “a public body shall post notice of every meeting at least 48 hours prior to the 
meeting, excluding Saturdays, Sundays and legal holidays.” G.L. c. 30A, § 20(b).  The notice 
must be printed in a legible, easily understandable format and must contain the date, time and 
place of the meeting, as well as a listing of topics that the chair reasonably anticipates will be 
discussed. Id.  The list of topics shall have “sufficient specificity to reasonably advise the public 
of the issues to be discussed at the meeting.” 940 CMR 29.03(1)(b).  We generally consider a 
topic to be sufficiently specific when a reasonable member of the public could read the topic and 
understand the anticipated nature of the public body’s discussion. See OML 2025-66; OML 
2019-70; OML 2015-35; OML 2013-39.2  A public body need not list each and every detail 
about a topic in order to comply with the law’s requirements. See OML 2025-66; OML 2019-
134.

The complaint alleges that the notice for the Commission’s July 9 meeting lacked 
sufficient specificity because it failed to list the “actual proposed regulation language, fee 
amounts, or summary of changes.”  We disagree.  We find that the topic “Proposed changes to 
Norwood Wetlands Protection Bylaw XXV Regulations, including a revised Fee Schedule 
(Section XII), references to Stormwater Management regulation (Section V), and other minor 
alterations. Continued from: 6/18/25” was sufficient to reasonably advise the public that the 
Commission would discuss proposed changes to the Norwood Wetlands Protection Bylaw XXV 
Regulations. See G.L. c. 30A, § 20(b); 940 CMR 29.03(1)(b).  As such, we find that the 
Commission did not violate the Open Meeting Law with respect to the sufficiency of this topic.  
The level of detail the Complainant seeks is not required by the Open Meeting Law. See OML 
2022-169, n. 4; OML 2020-125. 
 
II. The Commission did not violate the Open Meeting Law when it posted a copy of the 
notice of its July 9 meeting to the Town’s website. 
 
As explained above, a public body must post notice of every meeting at least 48 hours in 
advance of the meeting, not including Saturdays, Sundays and legal holiday. G.L. c. 30A, § 
20(b).  For local public bodies, “notice shall be filed with the municipal clerk and posted in a 
manner conspicuously visible to the public at all hours in or on the municipal building in which 
the clerk’s office is located.” G.L. c. 30A, § 20(c).  Alternatively, a municipality may designate a 
website as its official notice posting location for all public bodies within the municipality. 940 
CMR 29.03(2)(b).  A municipality may only have one official notice posting location for 
purposes of complying with the Open Meeting Law.  Although public bodies are welcome to 
post notice of their meetings in multiple locations if they choose, we look only to the official 
notice posting location for purposes of determining compliance with the Open Meeting Law. See 
OML 2022-18; OML 2021-94; OML 2021-18; OML 2020-20; OML 2017-134.  
 
The complaint alleges that the Commission violated the Open Meeting Law because a 
copy of the notice for its July 9 meeting posted to the Town’s website was inaccessible where it 
was not posted in a machine-readable format.  The complaint does not allege that the 
Commission failed to properly post notice of the July 9 meeting to the Town’s official notice 
posting location, nor does the complaint allege that the Town or Commission refused or failed to 
respond to a request for a reasonable accommodation made under the Americans with 
Disabilities Act.  Where the Town has not adopted its website as its official notice posting 
location, the Commission was not required to post the meeting notice to the Town’s website.  
Therefore, we find no violation with respect to this allegation. See OML 2024-170; OML 2022-
6.  However, we encourage the Town to upload digitally accessible notices when providing them 
on the Town’s website.

CONCLUSION 
 
For the reasons stated above, we find that the Commission did not violate the Open 
Meeting Law as alleged.  We now consider the complaint addressed by this determination to be 
resolved.  This determination does not address any other complaints that may be pending with 
the Commission or with our office.  Please feel free to contact our office at (617) 963-2540 if
