 RE: Open Meeting Law Complaint 
 
Dear Attorney Riley: 
 
This office received a complaint from Manish Sharma on January 11, 2023, alleging that 
the Sudbury Select Board (the “Board”) violated the Open Meeting Law, G.L. c. 30A, §§ 18-25.  
The complaint was originally filed with the Board on November 17, 2022, and you responded, 
on behalf of the Board, by letter dated November 30, 2022.  The complaint alleges that the Board 
did not allow public participation during a meeting held on November 15, 2022.1 
 
We resolve this matter by informal action, in accordance with 940 CMR 29.07(2)(a), 
after reviewing the original complaint, the Board’s response to the complaint, the complaint filed 
with our office requesting further review, and the notice and video recording of the meeting held 
on November 15, 2022.
2  We find that the Board did not violate the Open Meeting Law in the 
way alleged.

The Open Meeting Law does not require that a public body allow public participation in 
its meetings, but rather provides that “[n]o person shall address a meeting of a public body 
without permission of the chair, and all persons shall, at the request of the chair, be silent.”  G.L. 
c. 30A § 20(f); see also OML 2021-27; OML 2020-161; OML 2017-189; OML 2014-23; OML 
2012-78.3  We do, however, encourage public bodies to allow for as much public participation as 
time and circumstances permit.  See OML 2015-12; OML 2014-129; OML 2012-59.  
 
Here, the Board posted notice for a meeting to be held on November 15, 2022.  The 
notice listed 23 topics, including two public comment periods, one at the beginning of the 
meeting for “Public comment on items not on the agenda” and a second “Public Comments” 
towards the end of the meeting before the consent agenda.    
 
The November 15 meeting was held as planned.  After receiving a report from the 
Interim Town Manager and general updates from Board members, the Chair stated that the 
Board would accept comments from the public on items that were not listed on the agenda.  
When the complainant began to speak about the Town Manager candidates, the Chair interrupted 
and stated that “this is on the agenda for later so we’re not going to get into things that are on the 
agenda . . . at this time.”  The Board then discussed the Town Manager candidates and next steps 
for entering into a contract with the new Town Manager; discussed preliminary annual tax rates; 
discussed and voted on the purchase of a vehicle for the Parks & Recreation Department; 
approved signage for the Bruce Freeman Rail Trail; interviewed and appointed candidates to the 
Diversity, Equity and Inclusion Committee; and approved minutes.   
 
The Open Meeting Law permits the Chair of the Board to establish parameters for public 
participation.  See OML 2013-2; OML 2012-23.  Therefore, because it is within the Chair’s 
discretion to limit public participation during a meeting, the Chair did not violate the Open 
Meeting Law by preventing the complainant from addressing the Board during the November 15 
meeting.  See OML 2020-114; OML 2017-189; OML 2014-23; OML 2012-78.  We note that, 
although the Chair limited the complainant from speaking during the first public comment 
period, the complainant was allowed to comment after the Board discussed the annual tax rates 
and at the end of the meeting during the second public comment period.
