RE: Open Meeting Law Complaint 
Dear Attorney Frank: 
This office received a complaint from Patrick Higgins on May 25, alleging that the 
Swansea Board of Health (the "Board") violated the Open Meeting Law, G.L. c. 30A, §§ 18-
25.1 The complaint was originally filed with the Board on or about April 19, and you 
responded to the complaint, on behalf of the Board, in a letter dated May 7. In his complaint, 
Mr. Higgins alleges that the Board failed to timely approve certain meeting minutes. 
Following our review, we find that the Board violated the Open Meeting Law in the 
manner alleged. In reaching a determination, we reviewed the original complaint, the Board's 
response to the complaint, and the complaint filed with our office requesting further review. 
We find the facts as follows. On April 23, the Board approved minutes for meetings 
held on January 22; February 5; and March 19. Between January 22 and April 23, the Board 
met on the following dates: February 5; March 5; March 19; April 2; and April 11. 
Lhe Open Meeting Law requires that a public body "create and maintain accurate 
minutes of all meetings, including executive sessions, setting forth the date, time and place, 
the members present or absent, a summary of the discussions on each subject, a list of 
documents and other exhibits used at the meeting, the decisions made and the actions taken at 
each meeting, including the record of all votes." G.L. c. 30A, § 22(a). The meeting minutes 
must be reviewed and approved in a timely manner, G.L. c. 30A, § 22(c). The Attorney 
General's regulations define "timely manner," as approval "within the next three public body 
meetings or within 30 days, whichever is later, unless the public body can show good cause

The complaint alleges that the Board failed to approve its January 22; February 5; and 
March 19 meeting minutes in a timely manner. In its response, the Board concedes that it had 
not timely approved them "due to staffing and work volume related issues." Because the 
Board met more than three times within 30 days following its January 22 and February 5 
meetings, it had 30 days, or until February 21 and March 7, respectively, by which to approve 
those minutes. See 940 CMR 29.11; OML 2018-48. Staffing and work volume do not 
constitute the basis for finding good cause for the delay. See OML 2018-67. Accordingly, 
we find that the Board violated the Open Meeting Law by failing to approve its January 22 
and February 5 meeting minutes in a timely manner. However, because the Board approved 
its March 19 meeting minutes by the third subsequent meeting, on April 23, the Board timely 
approved them in accordance with 940 CMR 29.11. 
For the reasons stated above, we find that the Board violated the Open Meeting Law 
by failing to approve its January 22 and February 5 meeting minutes in a timely manner. We 
order the Committee's immediate and future compliance with the law's requirements, and we 
caution that similar future violations could be considered evidence of intent to violate the law. 
We now consider the complaint addressed by this determination to be resolved. This 
determination does not address any other complaints that may be pending with our office or 
the Board. Please feel free to contact our office at (617) 963-2540 if you have any questions 
regarding this letter.
