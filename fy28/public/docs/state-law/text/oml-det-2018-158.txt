RE: Open Meeting Law Complaint
Dear Attorney Frank:
This office received a complaint from Patrick Higgins on September 4 alleging that the 
Swansea Recreation Commission (the “Commission”) violated the Open Meeting Law, G.L. c. 
30A, §§ 18-25.1 The complaint was originally filed with the Commission on June 24; and you 
responded, on behalf of the Commission, by letter dated July 11. The complaint alleges that the 
Commission failed to post notice of its June 13 meeting.
We resolve this complaint by informal action in accordance with 940 CMR 29.07(2)(a), 
after reviewing the original complaint, the Commission's response to the complaint, and the 
complaint filed with our office requesting further review. We find that the Commission did not 
violate the Open Meeting Law.
The Open Meeting Law was enacted “to eliminate much of the secrecy surrounding 
deliberation and decisions on which public policy is based.” Ghiglione v. School Board of 
Southbridge, 376 Mass. 70, 72 (1978). Except in an emergency, a public body must post notice 
of every meeting “at least 48 hours prior to such meeting, excluding Saturdays, Sundays and 
legal holidays.” G.L. c. 30A, § 20(b). The notice must be printed in a legible, easily 
understandable format and must contain the date, time and place of the meeting, as well as a 
listing of topics that the chair reasonably anticipates will be discussed. Id.
The Commission posted on Facebook a parent information night to be held on June 13 
from 6 to 7 P.M. The post indicated that it would be “a great opportunity to hear from the 
members of the Commission and meet the Director and Assistant Director,” as well as provide an 
opportunity to ask questions. Here, only two of the five Commission members attended the

informational meeting on June 13. A sub-quorum of a body’s members may communicate on 
matters within its jurisdiction outside of a meeting and such discussion would not constitute a 
deliberation under the Open Meeting Law. See G.L. c. 30A, § 18 (defining deliberation as “an 
oral or written communication through any medium, including electronic mail, between or 
among a quorum of a public body on any public business within its jurisdiction); OML 2013- 
169; OML 2013-160.2 Accordingly, we find no violation of the Open Meeting Law.
We now consider the complaint addressed by this determination to be resolved. This 
determination does not address any other complaints that may be pending with the Commission
