This office received a complaint from Amanda Alix on January 9, 2025, alleging that the 
Shutesbury Select Board (the “Board”) violated the Open Meeting Law, G.L. c. 30A, §§ 18-25.  
The complaint was originally filed with the Board on or about November 20, 2024, and the 
Board responded to the complaint by letter dated November 26, 2024.  The complaint alleges 
that the Board posted a notice with insufficient specificity for a meeting held on October 22, 
2024. 
 
Following our review, we find that the Board violated the Open Meeting Law in the way 
alleged.  In reaching this determination, we reviewed the original complaint, the Board’s 
response to the complaint, and the complaint filed with our office requesting further review.  In 
addition, we reviewed the notice and video recording of the Board meeting held on October 22, 
2024.
1 
 
The Open Meeting Law requires that, except in an emergency, “a public body shall post 
notic
e of every meeting at least 48 hours prior to such meeting, excluding Saturdays, Sundays 
and legal holidays.”  G.L. c. 30A, § 20(b).  The notice must be printed in a legible, easily 
understandable format and must contain the date, time and place of the meeting, as well as a 
listing of topics that the chair reasonably anticipates will be discussed.  Id.  The list of topics 
shall have “sufficient specificity to reasonably advise the public of the issues to be discussed at

Here, the Board posted notice for a meeting to be held on October 22, 2024, at 6:00 PM.  
The notice listed nine topics, two of which were “New Highway Supt Contract” and “New 
Highway Equipment Operator.”   
 
The October 22, 2024, meeting was held as planned.  During the meeting, the Board 
announced that the New Highway Superintendent started working on September 26, 2024, but 
the Town had not yet executed a contract.  Board Co-Chair Rita Farrell stated that she had just 
confirmed with the Town Administrator that typically a member of the Board, a member of the 
Finance Committee, and a member of the Personnel Board would work together to negotiate a 
contract for the Highway Superintendent.  Ms. Farrell nominated Ms. Makepeace-O’Neil to 
represent the Board, and the Board briefly reviewed the contract but ultimately took no action on 
the contract.  The Board then discussed the open position for the Highway Equipment Operator.  
Board Co-Chair Melissa Makepeace-O’Neil stated that the Highway Superintendent 
recommended Michael Reske to the position.  Ms. Farrell then made a motion to appoint 
Michael Reske to the position, which was seconded by Ms. Makepeace-O’Neil, and unanimously 
approved by the Board.   
 
The complaint alleges that the notice of the October 22, 2024, meeting was insufficient 
wher
e it did not include the names of the individuals being considered for Highway 
Superintendent or Highway Equipment Operator.  With respect to the Highway Superintendent 
topic which was listed on the notice as “New Highway Supt Contract,” we do not find a 
violation.  When reviewing a meeting notice for sufficiency, unless it is clearly insufficient on its 
face, we do not review a meeting notice standing alone.  See OML 2015-109; OML 2014-155.  
Rather, we review what was actually discussed at the meeting to determine if the notice was 
sufficiently detailed to make the public aware of the discussion topics.  Here, the Board 
announced that a new Highway Superintendent had begun working in September and briefly 
reviewed a draft contract but took no action on the contract.  As such, we find that the notice 
with respect to this topic reasonably informed members of the public of the anticipated nature of 
the Board’s discussion. 
 
However, we find that the Board should have included on the notice the name of the 
indivi
dual being considered for the position of Highway Equipment Operator.  We have 
consistently stated that the names of applicants for appointment which are known 48 hours in 
advance of a meeting should be included on a meeting notice. See OML 2022-217; 2019-24; 
OML 2016-150; OML 2013-163.  During the meeting, the Board stated that the Highway 
Superintendent recommended Michael Reske to fill the position of Highway Equipment 
Operator, and the Board did not specifically state in its response that the name of this applicant 
was not reasonably anticipated 48 hours in advance of the October 22, 2024, meeting.  As such, 
we find that the notice for the Board meeting held on October 22, 2024, should have included the

name of the individual being considered for appointment as the New Highway Equipment

For the reasons stated above, we find that the Board violated the Open Meeting Law by 
posting an insufficiently specific notice for the October 22, 2024, meeting that failed to identify 
the name of the applicant for Highway Equipment Operator.  We order the Board’s immediate 
and future compliance with the law’s requirements, and we caution the Board that similar future 
violations could be considered evidence of intent to violate the law and may result in a civil 
penalty of up to $1,000 per violation. 
 
We now consider the complaint addressed by this determination to be resolved.  This 
determination does not address any other complaints that may be pending with the Board or with 
our office.  Please feel free to contact our office at (617) 963-2540 if you have any questions 
regarding this letter.
