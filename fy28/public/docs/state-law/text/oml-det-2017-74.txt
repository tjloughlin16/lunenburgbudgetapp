RE: Open Meeting Law Complaint 
Dear Chairman Reynolds: 
This office received a complaint from Julie Barrett, dated April 18, 2017, alleging that 
the North Attleborough Board of Selectmen (the "Board") violated the Open Meeting Law, G.L. 
c. 30A, §§ 18-25. The complaint was originally filed with the Board on January 27, 2017, and 
the Board responded by letter dated February 16, 2017. In her complaint, Ms. Barrett alleges 
that the Board violated the Open Meeting Law by: (i) posting an insufficiently detailed meeting 
notice, and (ii) excluding her from an executive session. 
Following our review, we find that the Board did not violate the Open Meeting Law. In 
reaching a determination, we reviewed the original complaint, the Board's response, and the 
request for further review filed with our office. We also reviewed, in camera, the draft minutes 
of an executive session held by the Board on January 26, 2017. Finally, we spoke with Town 
Manager Michael Gallagher by telephone on April 26, 2017. 
We find the facts as follows. The Board posted notice of a meeting to be held on January 
26, 2017. Among the other topics listed on this notice was an anticipated executive session 
described as follows: 
M.G.L. c. 3OA Section 21(a)(1): The Board of Selectmen will convene in Executive 
Session for the purpose of discussing the discipline or dismissal of or complaints or 
charges brought against, a public employee. 
The Board convened the January 26, 2017 meeting as planned. The complainant was 
present at this meeting. Prior to the Board convening in executive session, the complainant, 
believing that personal information about her would be discussed, requested to attend the 
executive session. The Board denied her request. While the minutes of the executive session

DISCUSSION 
I. The Board's January 26, 2017 Meeting Notice was Sufficiently Detailed. 
A public body must post notice of every meeting at least 48 hours in advance and include 
a listing of topics that the chair reasonably anticipates will be discussed. G.L. c. 30A, § 20(b). 
The list of topics shall have "sufficient specificity to reasonably advise the public of the issues to 
be discussed at the meeting." 940 CMR 29.03(l)(b). This same level of detail is required when 
describing anticipated executive session topics. See OML 2016-162.1 
The complaint alleges that the Board's January 26, 2017 meeting notice lacked sufficient 
detail because it did not identify the individual to be discussed in executive session, A public 
body must disclose as much information as possible about what will be discussed in executive 
session without compromising the lawful purpose for secrecy. See OML 2015-136. When 
discussing an individual public employee in executive session pursuant to G.L. c. 30A, § 
21(a)(1), a public body may omit identifying information about that employee from the meeting 
notice where the body is concerned about protecting the privacy of that individual. See OML 
2013-141. The Town Manager stated to our office that the Board omitted identifying 
information about the individual under discussion because the Board was concerned about 
protecting the privacy of that individual. Accordingly, we find that the meeting notice contained 
sufficient detail. 
II, The Board was Permitted to Exclude the Complainant from the Executive 
Session, 
When a public body convenes in executive session to discuss an individual pursuant to 
G.L. c. 30A, § 21(a)(1), the individual who is the subject of the discussion has certain procedural 
rights, including the right to be present during the executive session. See OML 2015-198; OML 
2011-39. Here, the complainant was not the subject of the January 26, 2017 executive session. 
Based on our review of the draft executive session minutes, the Board did not substantively 
discuss the complainant. Rather, the focus was on another individual. See OML 2014-90 (no 
violation where mention of complainant during executive session is secondary to primary 
discussion of individual). We therefore find that the complainant was not entitled to the 
procedural rights afforded under G,L, c, 30A, § 21(a)(1). See OML 2016-142, Accordingly, we 
find no violation with respect to this allegation, 
CONCLUSION 
For the reasons stated above, we find that the Board did not violate the Open Meeting 
Law, We now consider the complaint addressed by this determination to be resolved. This
