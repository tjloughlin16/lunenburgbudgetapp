FACTS
We find the facts as follows. On December 19, 2018, the Complainant filed two Open 
Meeting Law complaints with the Commission by email. The first complaint was dated 
December 17, 2018, and the second complaint was dated December 19, 2018. On December 20,
2018, the Commission met and discussed the December 17 complaint and on January 10, 2019, 
the Commission met and discussed the December 19 complaint.5 On January 9, 2019, the 
Commission responded through Counsel to the December 17 complaint. The Commission did 
not respond to the December 19 complaint nor did it request an extension of time to respond.
On January 14, 2019, the Complainant sent an email to Counsel informing him that the 
Complainant had not received a response to the December 19 complaint. On or about January 24,
2019, the Complainant filed the instant complaint alleging that the Commission did not respond 
to the December 19 complaint. On February 6, 2019, the Commission requested an extension of 
time to respond to the January 24 complaint. Our office granted the requested extension on that 
same day.6
DISCUSSION
L. The Complainant’s Allegation Regarding the Sufficiency of the Commission’s Review of
her Open Meeting Law Complaints7 at its December 20 Meeting is Untimely and We
Decline to Review it.
To be considered timely, a written Open Meeting Law complaint setting forth the 
circumstances which constitute the alleged violation must, among other things, be filed with the 
public body within 30 days of the date of the alleged violation. G.L. c. 30A, § 23(b). “[I]f the 
alleged violation . . . could not reasonably have been known at the time it occurred, then [the 
complaint must be filed] within 30 days of the date [the violation] should reasonably have been 
discovered.” 940 CMR 29.05(4). Events that occur during an open session meeting are 
reasonably discoverable at the time they occur. See OML 2012-52; OML 2014-85; OML 
Declination 4-3 0-13.8
The Complainant alleges that the Commission failed to properly review her December 17 
and December 19 complaints at its December 20, 2018, meeting because the Commission

2. The Commission Violated the Open Meeting Law by Failing to Respond to the
December 19 Complaint.
The Open Meeting Law requires that within fourteen business days of receiving an Open 
Meeting Law complaint, a public body shall review the complaint's allegations, take any 
appropriate remedial action, and send a response and description of any remedial action taken to 
the complainant. The public body must also notify the Attorney General that it has sent such 
materials to the complainant, providing the Attorney General with a copy of the complaint, the 
response, and a description of any remedial action taken. G.L. c. 30 A, § 23(b); 940 CMR 
29.05(5). Upon the public body's written request showing good cause, this Office may authorize 
an extension of time to respond. 940 CMR 29.05(5)(b).
The Commission claims to have reviewed the December 19 complaint at its January 10, 
2019, meeting, but offers no concrete explanation for why it did not respond to the December 19 
complaint. In its request for an extension of time to respond to the January 24 complaint, the 
Commission states that the Complainant’s “follow-up emails on this question have not actually 
contained or attached a ‘second’ OML complaint... and we have been unable to locate it” and 
suggests that there may have been an error in tracking or logging the complaint. In its February 
20, 2019, response to the December 19 complaint, the Commission states that it provided the 
Complainant with the documents she requested as relief in the December 19 complaint, and 
states that “[s]ince [the Complainant’s] additional questions rely on an assumption that the 
Commission purposefully violates the OML - an assumption with which we disagree - no 
further response is required.” By not responding to the December 19 complaint until February 
20, in the absence of an extension of time granted by this Office, we find that the Commission 
violated the Open Meeting Law.
We must now determine whether the Commission’s failure to respond to the December 
19 complaint was intentional, as the Complainant asserts. An intentional violation is
an act or omission by a public body or a member thereof, in knowing violation of 
M.G.L. c. 30A, §§18 through 25. Evidence of an intentional violation . .. shall 
include, but not be limited to, that the public body or public body member: (a) 
acted with specific intent to violate the law; (b) acted with deliberate ignorance of 
the law's requirements; or (c) was previously informed by receipt of a decision 
from a court of competent jurisdiction or advised by the Attorney General, 
pursuant to 940 CMR 29.07 or 940 CMR 29.08, that the conduct violates [the

Law]. Where a public body or public body member has made a good faith attempt 
at compliance with the law, but was reasonably mistaken about its requirements, 
such conduct will not be considered an intentional violation.
940 CMR 29.02.
Although we find it troubling that the Commission failed to respond to the December 19 
complaint, including after the Complainant’s January 14 email notifying Counsel that she had 
not received a response, we do not find evidence that the Commission intentionally acted to 
violate the Open Meeting Law. The Commission timely responded to seven other Open Meeting 
Law complaints that the Complainant and her husband filed around the same time, thus 
suggesting that the failure to timely respond to the December 19 complaint was an administrative 
oversight. In the absence of evidence that the Commission intended not to respond to the 
December 19 complaint in particular, we decline to find an intentional violation.
CONCLUSION
For the reasons stated above, we find the Commission violated the Open Meeting Law by 
failing to timely respond to the Complainant’s December 19 complaint. We order immediate and 
future compliance with the Open Meeting Law, and caution that similar violations in the future 
may be considered evidence of intent to violate the Law. 940 CMR 29.02.
We now consider the complaint addressed by this determination to be resolved. This 
determination does not address any other complaints which may be pending with our office or 
the Commission. Please feel free to contact our office at (617) 963-2540 if you have any
