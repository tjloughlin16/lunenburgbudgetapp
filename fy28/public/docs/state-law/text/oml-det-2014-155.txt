RE: Open Meeting Law Complaint 
Dear Mr. Heim: 
This office received a complaint from Patrick Higgins, dated October 24, 2014, alleging 
that the Swansea Recreation Commission (the "Commission") violated the Open Meeting Law, 
G.L. c. 30A, §§ 18-25. The complaint specifically alleges that on October 14, the Commission 
Chair posted a notice for an October 21, 2014 Commission meeting that failed to list the specific 
names and addresses of people to be interviewed for the position of recreation coordinator.1 The 
complaint was filed with the Commission on October 14, 2014. The Commission responded by 
letter dated October 21, 2014. 
Following our review, we find that the Commission did not violate the Open Meeting 
Law. In reaching this determination, we reviewed the complaint filed with the Commission, the 
Commission's response to the complaint, and the complaint filed with our office. Additionally, 
wc reviewed the notice for the Commission's October 21, 2014 meeting. 
FACTS 
We find the facts as follows. The Commission posted notice on October 14, 2014 for an 
October 21, 2014 meeting. The notice listed several topics, including: "Interviews/Recreation 
Coordinator." Mr. Higgins filed the present Open Meeting Law complaint on October 14, 2014. 
The Commission met on October 21, 2014 and interviewed candidates for the position of 
recreation coordinator.

DISCUSSION 
A public body must post notice of every meeting at least 48 hours in advance, not 
including Saturdays, Sundays, and legal holidays. G.L. c. 30A, § 20(b). Notices must include 
"a listing of topics that the chair reasonably anticipates will be discussed at the meeting." Id. 
The list of topics shall have sufficient specificity to reasonably advise the public of the issues to 
be discussed at the meeting. 940 CMR 29.03(l)(b). 
Mr. Higgins alleges that the notice for Commission's October 21, 2014 meeting was not 
sufficiently specific. When reviewing a meeting notice for sufficiency, unless it is clearly 
insufficient on its face, we do not review a meeting notice standing alone. Rather, we review 
what was actually discussed at the meeting to determine if the notice was sufficiently detailed to 
make the public aware of the discussion topics. Here, we find that on its face, the notice for the 
October 21, 2014 meeting met the statutory requirements by providing the list of topics the chair 
reasonably anticipated the Commission would discuss. Because the Commission's October 21, 
2014 meeting had not yet occurred when Mr. Higgins filed his complaint on October 14, 2014, 
we find no violation of the Law. We encourage Mr. Higgins, in the future, to reach out to the 
public body if he believes a notice may lack sufficient detail, rather than to file an Open Meeting 
Law complaint prior to the meeting. In this instance, where the notice was posted seven days 
ahead of the meeting, there would have been ample time to for the Commission to revise and 
repost the notice. 
Although we find no violation occurred here, we remind the Commission that, when 
conducting interviews, the meeting notice should identify the individuals whom the Commission 
is considering for appointment, if the Commission knows their identity 48 hours before the 
meeting. See OML 2012-113; OML 2011-11.2 There is no requirement that candidates' 
addresses be listed. 
CONCLUSION 
For the reasons stated above, we find that the Commission did not violate the Open 
Meeting Law. We now consider this matter closed. This determination does not address any 
other complaints that may be pending before this office or the Commission. Please feel free to 
contact our office at (617) 963-2540 if you have any questions.
