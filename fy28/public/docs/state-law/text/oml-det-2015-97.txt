RE: Open Meeting Law Complaint 
Dear Chair Funkhouser: 
This office received two related complaints from Mr. George H. Hanis, alleging that the 
Wayland Finance Committee (the "Committee") violated the Open Meeting Law, G.L. c. 30A, 
§§ 18-25. The first complaint was filed with our office on November 7, 2014, and the second 
was filed with our office on December 23, 2014. The first complaint was originally filed with 
the Committee on September 30, 2014, and the Committee responded by letter dated October 7, 
2014. The second complaint was originally filed with the Committee on October 31, 2014, and 
the Committee responded by letter dated November 14, 2014, In his complaints, Mr. Harris 
alleges that the Committee: (i) failed to conduct a review of executive session minutes in the 
manner required by law; (ii) failed to record roll call votes in its executive session minutes; (iii) 
improperly redacted portions of minutes; (iv) failed to provide certain minutes that were alleged 
to be "missing;" and (v) failed to respond to a request for executive session minutes within the 
time frame required by law. 
Following our review, we find that the Committee violated the Open Meeting Law in 
several of the ways alleged. In reaching a determination, we reviewed the original complaints; 
the Committee's responses; and the requests for further review filed with our office. We also 
reviewed all documents included with these complaints and responses. We also examined 
executive session minutes for twelve meeting dates from the period between January 2007 and 
September 2009.1 We also interviewed former Committee Chair Thomas Greenaway by phone 
on December 31, 2014; January 6, 2015; January 8, 2015; and June 24, 2015.2

FACTS 
We find the facts as follows. The Committee is a seven-member public body that meets 
periodically to discuss matters of relevance to the financial health of the Town of Wayland (the 
"Town"). In May 2014, the Committee began the process of reviewing archived executive 
session minutes from years prior to determine whether they could be released. On July 17,2014, 
Mr. Harris sent a request to the Committee for all executive session minutes from meetings held 
between January 1, 2008 and the date of his request. After that request, Mr. Harris and Chair 
Greenaway reached an agreement whereby Mr. 1 lams would not pursue his request until the 
Committee had a chance to meet in September 2014 to conduct a review of old executive session 
minutes. Because of the large volume of old executive session minutes, and because Chair 
Greenaway asserts that minutes from 2012 to the present relate to the Town's current collective 
bargaining cycle. Chair Greenaway decided in May 2014 to prioritize the review of minutes 
older than 2011. He did not, however, communicate this decision to Mr. Harris. 
The First Complaint 
During the Committee's fall meeting, on September 2, 2014, the Committee reviewed 
and voted to release several sets of older executive session minutes. On September 23, 2014, Mr. 
Harris requested from the Committee copies of all executive session minutes reviewed and 
released by the Committee during its September 2, 2014 meeting. On September 25, 2014, the 
Committee released to Mr. Harris the twelve sets of executive session minutes at issue in Mr. 
Hams' first complaint. The minutes from four sets of minutes in that group contained some 
redactions (November 5, 2007; July 28, 2008; September 15, 2008; and June 22, 2009). When 
Mr. Harris questioned the reasons for these redactions in his first Open Meeting Law complaint. 
Chair Greenaway stated by letter dated October 7, 2014 that, "disclosure of those portions of the 
minutes would have a detrimental effect on the current or future bargaining position of the Town 
of Wayland." Chair Greenaway stated further in a letter dated November 14, 2014 that the 
redactions concerned ongoing negotiations with both union and nonunion employees. 
Also on September 25, 2014, Chair Greenaway informed Mr. Harris that the minutes for 
three executive sessions - March 23, 2009; October 14, 2009; and November 2, 2009 - were 
"missing." On December 29, 2014, after the first Open Meeting Law complaint was filed with 
our office. Chair Greenaway located the three sets of executive session minutes and forwarded 
them to both Mr. Harris and our office. Mr. Hams sent an e-mail, also on December 29, 2014, 
stating, "I have reviewed the executive session minutes below forwarded by Tom Greenaway, 
and they appear to be satisfactory." 
The Second Complaint 
On September 30, 2014, Mr. Harris and Chair Greenaway exchanged e-mails concerning 
the executive session minutes that had been released just days before. Mr. Hams stated, "In 
2010 alone, [the Committee] held 21 executive sessions. In 2007-2009, it held 15 executive 
sessions in total. What's the explanation?" Chair Greenaway responded, "Timing of the 
negotiation cycles. Pretty much a three-year cycle. We held only one [executive session] in 
2011, which we will review the minutes for next time we meet in [executive session]. After that 
we will wait a while, since the Town and School are still negotiating contracts on the cycle we 
started discussing in 2012." Not satisfied with this response, Mr. Harris sent an e-mail to Chair

DISCUSSION 
I. The Committee Violated the Open Meeting Law by Failing to Review its 
Executive Session Minutes at Regular Intervals. 
The Open Meeting Law requires that public bodies periodically review executive session 
minutes to determine whether continued non-disclosure is warranted. See G.L. c. 3OA, § 
22(g)(1). Thus, the Committee had an ongoing obligation, beginning on July 1, 2010, to 
periodically review its executive session minutes to determine whether they could be released. 
See OML 2011-28. As evidenced by Chair Greenaway's statement that the Committee began to 
review executive session minutes in May 2014, which were in some cases seven years old, we 
find that the periodic review of executive session minutes was not an ongoing practice prior to 
May 2014. The Committee's failure to periodically review its executive session minutes violated 
the Open Meeting Law. See G.L. c. 30A, § 22(g)(1). We note that the Committee has since 
initiated a process of review, and we are confident that the Committee is now aware of relevant 
obligations under the Open Meeting Law and will continue to comply. 
II. The Committee Failed to Respond to Mr. Harris' September 30. 2014 Request for 
Executive Minutes as Required by Law. 
Public bodies are required to "create and maintain accurate minutes of all meetings, 
including executive sessions." G.L. c. 30A, § 22(a). A public body must respond to a request for 
executive session minutes within 10 days. See G.L c. 30A, § 22(g)(2). If the public body has 
determined, prior to the request, that certain executive session minutes may be released, it should 
respond to the requestor directly, notifying him or her of the availability of those minutes. See 
OML 2013-105; OML 2013-99. If the body previously determined that executive session 
minutes should remain confidential because publication would defeat the lawful purposes of the 
executive session, it should respond by stating the reason why the minutes continue to be 
withheld. Id And if, at the time of a request, the public body has not conducted a review of the 
minutes to determine whether continued nondisclosure is warranted, the body must perform such 
a review and release the minutes, where appropriate, no later than its next meeting or within 30 
days, whichever occurs first. See G.L. c. 30A, § 22(g)(2). In such circumstances, the body

should still respond to the requestor within 10 days, notifying him or her that it is conducting this 
review. See OML 2013-99. 
Here, Mr. Harris requested all unreleased executive session minutes on September 30, 
2014. While Chair Greenaway had determined in May 2014 that only executive session minutes 
from 2007 to 2011 would be reviewed, he did not respond directly to Mr. Hanis regarding this 
particular request. It was not until Chair Greenaway's November 14, 2014 letter that this 
position was made clear to Mr. Harris. While executive session minutes may be withheld as long 
as the statutory purpose for secrecy still exists, G.L. c. 30A, § 22(f), the Committee's failure to 
respond to Mr. Harris' request for executive session minutes within 10 days violated the law. 
See OML 2013-199. 
III. The Issue of the "Missing" Executive Session Minutes. 
When the Committee provided Mr. Harris with the minutes responsive to his September 
23, 2014 request, the minutes for three executive sessions were missing. Because of this, Mr. 
Harris contends that the Committee has failed to "maintain" records in accordance with the Open 
Meeting Law. See G.L. c. 30A, § 22(a). The Committee's failure to produce these minutes 
within the time period required by law violated the Open Meeting Law. Because the Committee 
subsequently located and provided the minutes to Mr. Harris, we find that the Committee has 
taken appropriate remedial action and order no further relief. 
IV. The Committee was Permitted to Redact Certain Portions of Four Sets of 
Executive Minutes, as a Valid Purpose for Secrecy Still Existed. 
Executive session minutes may be withheld from disclosure to the public, "as long as 
publication may defeat the lawful purposes of the executive session, but no longer." G.L. c. 
3OA, § 22(f). When the puipose for a valid executive session has been served, the minutes and 
any documents or exhibits used at the session must be disclosed, unless the attorney-client 
privilege or an exemption to the Public Records Law applies. Id. 
The first complaint alleges that the Committee improperly redacted certain portions of 
four sets of executive session minutes. The redacted portions of the minutes concerned topics 
related to ongoing negotiations with both union and nonunion employees. Chair Greenaway 
determined at the time that disclosure would have a detrimental effect on the Committee's 
bargaining position, not only with respect to the bargaining units discussed in those sessions but 
also other similarly situated bargaining units in the Town, as well as future negotiations with the 
same units. After reviewing information provided to us by the Committee, we believe that the 
Committee has met its obligation to show that its bargaining position would be burdened. See 
OML 2015-52. The Open Meeting Law permits a public body to withhold executive session 
minutes concerning a present negotiation, if the public release of those minutes could have a 
detrimental effect on the public body's bargaining position in future negotiations. See OML 
2012-41. We find that the Committee did not violate the Open Meeting Law by redacting 
portions of these minutes when it originally produced them to Mr. Hanis. G.L. c. 30A, § 22(f). 
We note, however, that all relevant executive session minutes have now been released in their 
entirety.

V. We Decline to Review the Allegations Regarding the Content of Executive 
Session Minutes for Meetings that Occurred Prior to the Date that our Office 
Assumed Responsibility for Enforcement of the Open Meeting Law. 
The complaint alleges that the Committee's executive session minutes from the period 
between January 2007 and September 2009 fail to record roll call votes, as required by the Open 
Meeting Law. See G.L. c. 30A, § 22(b). Our office assumed authority for the interpretation and 
enforcement of the Open Meeting Law, as it applies to local public bodies, on July 1, 2010. See 
G.L. c. 30A, § 23(a); see also G.L. c. 30A, §§ 18-25, generally. Prior to July 1, 2010, the law 
governing meetings of local public bodies was G.L. c. 39, § 23B (now repealed), and the District 
Attorneys were responsible for its enforcement. One of the practical effects of this change is that 
our office has authority to enforce the Open Meeting Law, G.L. c. 30A, §§ 18-25, only with 
respect to violations that occurred on or after July 1, 2010. See OML Declination 6-3-11 
(Southborough Municipal Facilities Committee); OML 2015-62, Fn. 2. Therefore, we decline to 
review the allegation that the Committee failed to record roll call votes during executive sessions 
held prior to that date, even though facts suggesting such violations may have been discovered 
only recently. See OML 2013-91. 
CONCLUSION 
For the reasons stated above, we find that the Committee violated the Open Meeting Law. 
We order immediate and future compliance with the Open Meeting Law, and we caution that 
similar future violations may be evidence of intent to violate the law. 
We now consider the complaints addressed by this determination to be resolved. This 
determination does not address any other complaints that may be pending with our office or the 
Committee. Please feel free to contact the Division at (617) 963 - 2540 if you have any
