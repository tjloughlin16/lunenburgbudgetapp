Dear Chair Bruno: 
This office received a complaint from Patrick Higgins on January 31, 2023, alleging that 
the Halifax Board of Selectmen (the “Board”) violated the Open Meeting Law, G.L. c. 30A, §§ 
18-25. The complaint was originally filed with the Board on November 21, 2022, and you 
responded on behalf of the Board by letter dated December 1, 2022. The complaint alleges that 
the revised notice for the Board’s November 9, 2022, meeting failed to include the date and time 
that the original notice was posted and that the Board failed to timely approve minutes from its 
meetings held on November 23, 2021, and November 30, 2021. 
Following our review, we find that the Board violated the Open Meeting Law when it 
failed to timely approve meeting minutes. We do not find a violation of the Law in the other 
ways alleged. In reaching this determination, we reviewed the original complaint, the Board’s 
response to the complaint, and the complainant’s request for further review. We also reviewed 
the initial and revised notices of the November 9, 2022, meeting as well as meeting minutes for 
December 14, 2021, and all of 2022.  
FACTS 
We find the facts as follows. The Board held meetings on November 23, 2021, November 
30, 2021, December 14, 2021, January 27, 2022, and March 3, 2022. Either all three Board 
members, or two of the three Board members, were present for all of these meetings. 
Additionally, several other meetings were held between March and July 2022, with a quorum 
present. The former town administrator had resigned in November 2021 and was replaced by an 
interim town administrator. A new town administrator was hired in June 2022 and resigned on 
August 25, 2022. Two members of the Board also resigned on August 25, 2022, leaving only one 
member of the Board.

On November 3, 2022, at 2:47 PM, the Halifax Town Clerk posted notice for the Board’s 
November 9 meeting to be held at 6:00 PM. On November 7, 2022, at 1:00 PM, the Clerk posted 
an amended notice for the same meeting. The November 9, 2022, meeting was held as 
scheduled, with three Board members present. During the November 9 meeting, the Board 
approved minutes from meetings held on November 23, 2021, and November 30, 2021. 
DISCUSSION 
The Open Meeting Law requires that public bodies “post notice of every meeting at least 
48 hours prior to such meeting, excluding Saturdays, Sundays and legal holidays.” G.L. c. 30A, § 
20(b). “The date and time that the notice is posted shall be conspicuously recorded thereon or 
therewith.” 940 CMR 29.03(l)(d); see OML 2011-45.
1 Notices that are revised within the 48 
hours before the meeting must conspicuously record both the date and time that it was posted and 
the date and time that the notice was originally posted. See 940 CMR 29.03(1)(d); OML 2017-
183; OML 2013-44. 
 
Here,
 the Board originally posted notice of its November 9, 2022, meeting, scheduled to 
take place at 6:00 PM, on November 3, 2022, at 2:47 PM. The Board later posted a revised 
notice and included the date and time that it was posted, which was November 7, 2022, at 1:00 
PM. The complainant alleges that the amended notice should have also included the date and 
time that the original notice had been posted. The complainant is incorrect. The amended 
meeting notice was posted 53 hours before the November 9 meeting. A public body is only 
required to indicate the original and revised posting times on a revised notice if it is revised 
within 48 hours of the noticed meeting. Therefore, we do not find that the Board violated the 
Open Meeting Law in this regard. 
 
Next, the complainant alleges that the Board failed to timely approve minutes from its 
Novem
ber 23, 2021, and November 30, 2021, meetings. The Open Meeting Law requires that a 
public body “create and maintain accurate minutes of all meetings, including executive sessions, 
setting forth the date, time and place, the members present or absent, a summary of the 
discussions on each subject, a list of documents and other exhibits used at the meeting, the 
decisions made and the actions taken at each meeting, including the record of all votes.” G.L. c. 
30A, § 22(a). The meeting minutes must be reviewed and approved in a timely manner. G.L. c. 
30A, § 22(c). “Timely manner” means “within the next three public body meetings or within 30 
days, whichever is later, unless the public body can show good cause for further delay.” See 940 
CMR 29.11; OML 2018-48. Whenever possible, we recommend that minutes of a meeting be 
approved at the next meeting. See OML 2018-67; OML 2017-133. 
 
 The Board acknowledges that it approved the minutes from the November 23, 2021, and 
November 30, 2021, meetings nearly a year later, on November 9, 2022, but explains that it had 
good cause for delay because of staffing shortages and therefore approval of the minutes was not 
untimely. Specifically, the Board asserts that the Town Administrator’s resignation in November 
2021, followed by the resignation of two of the three Board members in August 2022, leaving 
only one Board member to handle the workload, constituted good cause for delay in approving

the minutes. The Board also suggests that it was not able to approve meeting minutes until the 
full Board with its new members had convened in November 2022. Although we acknowledge 
the challenges the remaining Board member faced after the resignation of two Board members 
and the town administrator in August 2022, the minutes of meetings held on November 23, 2021, 
and November 30, 2021, were due to be approved no later than January 27, 2022, and March 3, 
2022, respectively, as that was the later of the next 30 days or next three meetings that included a 
quorum of the Board. Therefore, the Board had failed to timely approve the minutes at issue well 
before the resignations in August 2022. 
 
To the extent that the Board argues that the resignation of the Town Administrator in 
Novem
ber 2021 caused the delay, in general, staffing shortages of the type that all governmental 
bodies with limited resources experience, or periodic staff vacancies, do not constitute good 
cause for failing to approve minutes in a timely manner, as it is the responsibility of the public 
body and its members to take steps to ensure that staff have adequate assistance to complete the 
minutes in a timely manner, including having members of the public body assist in the creation 
of minutes if necessary. See OML 2021-177; OML 2018- 105; OML 2015-86. Therefore, we do 
not find that the Board has established good cause for delay in approving the minutes of 
meetings held on November 23, 2021, and November 30, 2021, and the Board violated the Open 
Meeting Law by failing to timely approve those meeting minutes. 
 
Finally, we take this opportunity to inform the Board that the Op
 en Meeting Law does 
not govern the method for approving meeting minutes and public bodies may choose the method 
for approval. See OML 2020-81; OML 2019-107; OML 2019-9. Acceptable methods include, 
but are not limited to, approval by the public body chair alone or by another designated 
individual, approval by majority vote of the public body, approval by consent of the body 
(approved unless there is an objection), or approval by a parent public body on behalf of a 
subcommittee. See OML 2019-107; OML 2019-25; OML 2017-34. The method of approval of 
minutes that a public body chooses does not impact the standard for timely approval of minutes, 
which remains “within the next three public body meetings or within 30 days, whichever is 
later.” 940 CMR 29.11. Accordingly, we find that the Board failed to timely approve the meeting 
minutes for November 23, 2021, and November 30, 2021. 
 
CONCLUSION 
 For the reasons stated above, we find that the Board violated the Open Meeting Law 
when it failed to timely approve meeting minutes. We order the Board’s immediate and future 
compliance with the Open Meeting Law and caution the Board that a determination by our office 
of a similar violation in the future may be considered evidence of intent to violate the Open 
Meeting Law.
