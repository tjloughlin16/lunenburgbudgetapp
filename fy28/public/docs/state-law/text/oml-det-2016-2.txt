RE: Open Meeting Law Complaint 
Dear Mr. Trapani: 
This office received a complaint from Gary Fowler, dated November 20, 2015, alleging 
that the Georgetown Board of Selectmen (the "Board") violated the Open Meeting Law, G.L. c. 
30A, §§ 18-25. Specifically, the complaint alleges that sometime in October of 2014, the Board 
Chair1 communicated outside of a meeting with the Town Administrator on a matter within the 
Board's jurisdiction.2 The complaint was originally filed with the Board on October 14, 2015. 
The Board responded by letter dated November 5, 2015. 
Following our review, we resolve this complaint by informal action in accordance with 
940 CMR 29.07(2)(a), and find that the Board did not violate the Open Meeting Law. A public 
body may only deliberate during a noticed meeting. G.L. c. 30A, §§ 18, 20. A "deliberation" is 
defined as "an oral or written communication through any medium, including electronic mail, 
between or among a quorum of a public body on any public business within its jurisdiction." 
G.L. c. 30A, § 18. A "quorum" is a simple majority of the members of the public body, unless 
otherwise provided in a general or special law, executive order or other authorizing provision. 
Id. The Board is a five-member public body, thus three members constitute a quorum. The 
complaint does not allege that a quorum of the Board communicated outside of a noticed 
meeting. Rather, it alleges that the Board Chair communicated with the Town Administrator
