RE:  Open Meeting Law Complaint 
 
Dear Mr. Waller, 
 
This office received your complaint on May 12, 2022, alleging that the Marblehead 
Board of Health (the “Board”) violated the Open Meeting Law, G.L. c. 30A, §§ 18-25.  The 
complaint was originally filed with the Board on or around March 26, 2022, and Attorney 
Elizabeth Lydon responded, on behalf of the Board, by letter dated May 11, 2022.  The 
complaint alleges that the minutes of Board meetings held on October 12, November 15, 
December 13, December 14, December 17, December 27, and December 29, 2021, and January 
11, 2022, fail to include a list of documents used during each meeting, and that the minutes are 
incomplete insofar as they do not summarize the content of what was said by public participants 
during public comment.  For the reasons stated below, we decline to review this complaint. 
 
Complaints alleging violations of the Open Meeting Law must be filed with the public 
body within 30 days of the alleged violation, or if the alleged violation could not have been 
known at the time it occurred, then within 30 days from reasonable discovery of the alleged 
violation.  G.L. c. 30A, § 23(b); 940 CMR 29.05(4).  Complaints regarding the sufficiency or 
accuracy of open session meeting minutes must be filed within 30 days of the date on which 
those minutes were approved. See OML 2015-26; OML 2011-28; OML Declination 2-13-20 
(Westminster Conservation Commission); OML Declination 5-4-2012 (Sheffield Planning 
Board).1     
 
The Board explains that all the meeting minutes at issue, with the exception of minutes 
for a December 17, 2021, meeting that did not take place, were approved more than 30 days

before the complaint was filed with the Board.2  You do not dispute this point but assert that the 
complaint was timely because it was filed within 30 days of the minutes being posted to the 
Town website.  Although the Open Meeting Law requires that minutes be created, reviewed, and 
approved in a timely manner, it does not require that meeting minutes be posted to a website. See 
OML 2019-147.  Rather, the Open Meeting Law requires that meeting minutes be made 
available upon request within 10 days.  G.L. c. 30A, § 22(c). There is no suggestion that you 
requested the meeting minutes in question and they were not provided to you.  Therefore, we 
find the complaint challenging the sufficiency of the minutes was not timely filed, and we 
decline to review it. 
 
 We now consider this matter closed.  This letter does not address any other complaints 
that may be pending with our office or the Board.  Please feel free to contact the Division at 
(617) 963-2540 if you have any questions regarding this letter.
