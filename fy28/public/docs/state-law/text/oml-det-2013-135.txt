RE: Open Meeting Law Complaint 
Dear Mr. Carter: 
This office received an Open Meeting Law complaint from Ms. Sherrill Jourdan, dated 
December 31,2012, alleging that the Ashfield Board of Selectmen (the "Board") violated the 
Open Meeting Law, G.L. c. 30A, §§ 18-25. Specifically, the complaint alleges that Board Chair 
Douglas Field "violated the open meeting law and constitutional rights of a fellow board member 
when he attempted to silence and remove another sitting member of the Select Board" during the 
Board's September 27, 2012 meeting. The complaint was originally filed with the Board on or 
about October 24, 2012. The Board responded by letter dated November 29, 2012, following an 
extension granted by our office. 
Following our review, we find that Board did not violate the Open Meeting Law. In 
reaching this determination, we reviewed the October 24, 2012 complaint filed with the Board; 
the Board's November 29, 2012 response; and the December 31, 2012 complaint filed with our 
office. Additionally, we reviewed the notice and minutes of the Board's September 27, 2012 
meeting, as well an audio recording of that meeting. 
During the Board's September 27, 2012 meeting, two Board members discussed the 
content of a prior meeting's minutes. The discussion escalated, and the Chair asked Board 
member Paullette Leukhardt to stop speaking. She continued to speak. A police officer asked 
Ms. Leukhardt to stop talking, and then asked her to leave. Ms. Leukhardt did not leave, and 
participated in the remainder of the meeting. 
The Open Meeting Law states that "[n]o person shall address a meeting of a public body 
without permission of the chair, and all persons shall, at the request of the chair, be silent. No

person shall disrupt the proceedings of a meeting of a public body. If, after clear warning from 
the chair, a person continues to disrupt the proceedings, the chair may order the person to 
withdraw from the meeting and if the person does not withdraw, the chair may authorize a 
constable or other officer to remove the person from the meeting." G.L. c. 30A, § 20(f); OML 
2012-95; OML 2012-78; OML 2011-2.1 The term "person" in this section of the law applies to 
both members of the public and members of the public body. Thus, the Chair did not violate the 
Open Meeting Law by asking Ms. Leukhardt to be silent, or by seeking her removal when she 
did not comply.2 
While the Chair acted within his discretion under the law, we strongly discourage the 
removal of properly seated public body members from meetings of public bodies. If, as occurred 
here, tensions escalate to a point where they interfere with the conduct of a meeting, a temporary 
adjournment may be appropriate. Removal should be used only as a last resort. 
We now consider this matter closed. Please contact me if you have any questions 
regarding this letter.
