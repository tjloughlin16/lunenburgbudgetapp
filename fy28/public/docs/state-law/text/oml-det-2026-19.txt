This office received a complaint from Christopher Patzke on November 5, 2025, alleging 
that the Salem City Seal Task Force (the “Task Force”) violated the Open Meeting Law, G.L. c. 
30A, §§ 18-25.  The complaint was originally filed with the Task Force on October 14, 2025, 
and you responded, on behalf of the Task Force, by letter dated November 5, 2025.1  The 
complaint alleges that the Task Force created insufficient minutes of meetings held on June 16, 
July 14, and September 30, 2025. 
 
Following our review, we find that the Task Force created sufficiently detailed minutes of 
the meeting held on September 30, 2025, and therefore did not violate the Open Meeting Law.  
We decline to review the sufficiency of the minutes of the meetings held on June 16 and July 14, 
2025, for the reasons discussed below.  In reaching this determination, we reviewed the original 
complaint, the Task Force’s response to the complaint, and the complaint filed with our office 
requesting further review.  In addition, we reviewed the notices and minutes of the Task Force

DISCUSSION 
 
I. We Find the Allegation Regarding the Sufficiency of the June 16 and July 14, 
2025, Meeting Minutes To Be Untimely. 
 
We decline to review the allegation in the complaint regarding the sufficiency of the 
minutes of the Task Force meetings held on June 16 and July 14, 2025, as we find that allegation 
to be untimely.  Complaints alleging violations of the Open Meeting Law must be filed with the 
public body within 30 days of the alleged violation.  G.L. c. 30A, § 23(b).  If the alleged 
violation could not reasonably have been known at the time it occurred, then the complaint must 
be filed within 30 days of the date it should reasonably have been discovered.  940 CMR 
29.05(3).  When a public body approves meeting minutes in open session, any complaints 
regarding the sufficiency or accuracy of those minutes must be filed within 30 days of the 
meeting during which they were approved.  See OML Declination 9-4-12 (Manchester-by-the-
Sea Zoning Board of Appeals); OML Declination 5-14-12 (Sheffield Planning Board).3   
 
Here, the Task Force approved the minutes of the June 16, 2025, meeting during an open 
meeting held on July 21, 2025; and approved the minutes of the July 14, 2025, meeting during an 
open meeting held on August 18, 2025.  Therefore, any complaint with respect to the sufficiency 
of minutes should have been filed within 30 days of the date that the Task Force approved each 
set of minutes.  See OML Declination 9-4-12 (Manchester-by-the-Sea Zoning Board of 
Appeals); OML Declination 5-14-12 (Sheffield Planning Board).  Where the complaint was filed 
on October 14, 2025, which was more than 30 days after the Task Force approved these two sets 
of minutes in open session, we find this allegation to be untimely and decline to review it.  See 
OML Declination 10-21-20 (Massachusetts Maritime Academy Board of Trustees); OML 
Declination 7-2-18 (Pembroke Planning Board); OML Declination 1-13-14 (Ashfield Select 
Board).

Sufficiently Detailed.

and other exhibits used at the meeting, the decisions made and the actions taken at each meeting, 
including the record of all votes.”  G.L. c. 30A, § 22(a).  When reviewing minutes for 
compliance with the Open Meeting Law, we look for substantial compliance with the accuracy 
requirement.  See OML 2016-105; OML 2013-64.  By substantial compliance, we mean that the 
minutes should contain enough detail and accuracy so that a member of the public who did not 
attend the meeting could read the minutes and have a clear understanding of what occurred.  See 
OML 2012-106.  While minutes must include a summary of the discussion on each topic, a 
transcript is not required, and the minutes do not need to include every remark or opinion 
presented.  See OML 2012-29; OML 2011-55.  Indeed, we acknowledge that it can be difficult to 
record the comments of every speaker especially where there are many speakers whose remarks 
may overlap.  See OML 2023-106; OML 2014-108; OML 2013-47; OML 2012-29. 
 
The complaint alleges that the minutes of the September 30, 2025, meeting are 
insufficient as they include “a highly-censored record of public comments” made during the 
meeting.4  Here, on September 30, 2025, the Task Force held a “public listening session” 
regarding whether the Salem City Seal should be modified.  During the meeting, the Task Force 
heard from six people—three individuals, including the complainant, gave public comment in 
person and three individuals provided comment over Zoom.  Each individual was allowed to 
speak for up to three minutes and the Task Force received public comment for approximately 18 
minutes.  After confirming that there were no other individuals who wished to provide public 
comment, the session was closed.   
 
The minutes of the September 30, 2025, meeting summarize public comment as follows: 
“In total six people spoke during the listening session, four in favor of changing the seal and two 
against.  Five speakers identified as residents of Massachusetts and five specified living in Salem 
and one specified working in Salem.”  The minutes then include the name of each individual 
who spoke and whether they supported or opposed changing the City Seal.  
 
We find that the Task Force did not violate the Open Meeting Law by creating and 
approving insufficiently detailed minutes.

The Open Meeting Law requires that minutes adequately capture the substance of the discussions 
that occurred so that a member of the public who did not attend the meeting could read the 
minutes and have an understanding of what occurred.  See OML 2020-113; OML 2016-40.  
After watching a video recording of the meeting, we find that the minutes capture a concise 
summary of the comments made by members of the public, even if they do not capture every 
remark made.  See OML 2017-104; OML 2012-29; OML 2011-55.  
 
Finally, we take this opportunity to note our concern with the Task Force’s suggestion in 
its response to the complaint about using “Zoom-generated transcripts” as minutes of meetings.  
We remind the Task Force that neither a transcript, nor an audio or video recording of a meeting, 
can constitute minutes for purposes of the Open Meeting Law because the Law requires that 
meeting minutes include a summary of the discussions on each subject, and a transcript or 
recording is not a summary of the meeting.  See G.L. c. 30A, § 22(a) (minutes of all meetings, 
including executive sessions, must include the date, time and place, the members present or 
absent, a summary of the discussions on each subject, a list of documents and other exhibits used 
at the meeting, and the decisions made and the actions taken at each meeting, including the 
record of all votes); see also OML 2024-78; OML 2016-123 fn. 2.  
 
CONCLUSION 
 
For the reasons stated above, we find that the Task Force did not violate the Open 
Meeting Law.  We now consider the complaint addressed by this determination to be resolved.  
This determination does not address any other complaints that may be pending with the Task
