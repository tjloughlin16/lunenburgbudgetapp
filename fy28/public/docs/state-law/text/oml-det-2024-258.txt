 RE: Open Meeting Law Complaint 
 
Dear Attorney D’Ortenzio: 
 
This office received a complaint from Patrick Higgins on September 26, 2024, alleging 
that the Adams Board of Selectmen (the “Board”) violated the Open Meeting Law, G.L. c. 30A, 
§§ 18-25.  The complaint was originally filed with the Board on or about August 24, 2024, and 
you responded, on behalf of the Board, by letter dated August 30, 2024.
1  The complaint alleges 
that the Board failed to approve minutes of meetings held on May 22, June 5, and June 18 in a 
timely manner. 
 
After review, we find that the Board did not violate the Open Meeting Law in the way 
alleged.  In reaching this determination, we reviewed the original complaint, the Board’s 
response to the complaint, and the complainant’s request for review of the complaint.  We also 
viewed a video recording of the Board’s July 17 meeting.
2 
 
The Open Meeting Law requires that a public body “create and maintain accurate 
minutes of all meetings, including executive sessions, setting forth the date, time and place, the 
members present or absent, a summary of the discussions on each subject, a list of documents 
and other exhibits used at the meeting, the decisions made, and the actions taken at each meeting, 
including the record of all votes.”  G.L. c. 30A, § 22(a).  Minutes of all open sessions must be 
reviewed and approved in a “timely manner.”  G.L. c. 30A, § 22(c).  “Timely manner” means

“within the next three public body meetings or within 30 days, whichever is later, unless the 
public body can show good cause for further delay.”  940 CMR 29.11(2).   
 
The Board held meetings on May 22, June 5, June 18, and July 17.  The Board approved 
minutes of its May 22, June 5, and June 18 meetings in open session during its July 17 meeting, 
which was the third, second, and next meeting thereafter, respectively.  Therefore, minutes of all 
three meetings were timely approved within the later of the next three meetings or 30 days.  See 
940 CMR 29.11.    
 
We note that the basis for the subject Open Meeting Law complaint is entirely unclear.  
The complaint implies that minutes of the three meetings remained unapproved as of the date of 
the complaint, August 24.  However, the notice for the Board’s July 17 meeting included 
approval of the May 22, June 5, and June 18 meetings as a topic, and a video recording of the 
July 17 meeting as well as minutes of the July 17 meeting document timely approval of the 
minutes.  We strongly discourage the Complainant from filing Open Meeting Law complaints 
containing meritless allegations, which waste both municipal and Commonwealth resources. 
 
For the reasons stated above, we find that the Board did not violate the Open Meeting 
Law.  We now consider the complaint addressed by this determination to be resolved.  This 
determination does not address any other complaints that may be pending with the Board or with 
our office.  Please feel free to contact our office at (617) 963-2540 if you have any questions 
regarding this letter.
