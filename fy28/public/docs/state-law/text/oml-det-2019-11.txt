This office received a complaint from Nada Milosavljevic-Fabrizio on June 20, 2018, 
alleging that the Westwood Board of Selectmen (the “Board”) violated the Open Meeting 
Law, G.L. c. 30A, §§ 18-25. The complaint was originally filed with the Board on or about 
April 20, 2018, and you responded, on behalf of the Board, in a letter dated May 3, 2018. In 
her complaint, Ms. Milosavljevic-Fabrizio alleges that the Board took action during its March 
23, 2018 meeting that was not listed as a topic on the meeting notice. Ms. Milosavljevic- 
Fabrizio also alleges that the draft minutes from that meeting are inaccurate.
Following our review, we find that the Board did not violate the Open Meeting Law. 
In reaching a determination, we reviewed the original complaint, the Board’s response to the 
complaint, and the complaint filed with our office requesting further review. We also 
reviewed the notice, minutes and video-recording of the Board’s March 23, 2018 meeting, as 
well as the notice and minutes of the Board’s April 9, 2018 meeting.1 Finally, we spoke with 
you by telephone on January 3, 2019.2

30, 2018, the Board had prepared three articles to present to voters that, if approved, would 
have allowed a redevelopment project at Islington Center to proceed. During a presentation

DISCUSSION
The Open Meeting Law requires a public body to post notice 48 hours in advance of a 
meeting. G.L. c. 30A, § 20(b). The notice must include a “listing of topics that the chair 
reasonably anticipates will be discussed at the meeting.” Id. Further, the list of topics must 
have “sufficient specificity to reasonably advise the public of the issues to be discussed at the 
meeting.” 940 CMR 29.03(l)(b). Public bodies may discuss a topic not listed on a meeting 
notice if the topic was not anticipated by the chair 48 hours in advance of the meeting, 
however, we encourage public bodies to postpone discussion of such topics until proper 
notice can be given to the public. See OML 2017-51; OML 2013-87; OML 2013-13.3
The complaint alleges that, during its March 23, 2018 meeting, the Board voted to 
postpone the Annual Town Meeting, although the topic had not been specifically listed on the 
meeting notice. Board member Michael Walsh unexpectedly raised the issue during the 
Board’s discussion of the Islington Center redevelopment project. The Board expressed 
concern that the voters would not have all necessary information to make an informed 
decision at the regularly-scheduled Town Meeting date of May 9, 2018. We find that the 
topic was unanticipated by the chair and thus the Board did not violate the Open Meeting Law 
by discussing and voting on the postponement of Town Meeting during this meeting. See 
OML 2017-126; OML 2013-190 (A public body may consider a topic at a meeting that was 
not listed in the meeting notice if it is a topic that the chair did not reasonably anticipate at 
least 48 hours before the meeting). However, we remind the Board that the Attorney General 
strongly encourages public bodies to postpone discussion and action on topics that may be of 
particular interest to the public if those topics were not listed in the meeting notice. See OML 
2013-61. We commend the Board for revisiting the topic during its April 9, 2018 meeting to 
explain the rationale for its decision to the public.

The complaint also alleges that the draft meeting minutes from the Board’s March 23, 
2018 meeting are insufficient. Because the minutes were in draft form at the time this 
complaint was filed, we decline to review their sufficiency. See OML 2014-62 (“A public 
body may revise draft minutes at any point prior to their approval to correct inaccuracies or 
add detail”). We note, however, that the minutes were not approved until May 1, 2018. 
Meeting minutes must be reviewed and approved in a timely manner. G.L. c. 30A, § 22(c). 
The Open Meeting Law itself does not define “timely manner.” However, the Attorney 
General’s Open Meeting Law regulations provide that “timely manner” means “within the 
next three public body meetings or within 30 days, whichever is later, unless the public body 
can show good cause for further delay.” 940 CMR 29.11(2). Between its March 23 and May 
1, 2018 meetings, the Board met four additional times. The Board should have approved the 
March 23, 2018 meeting minutes by its third subsequent meeting, on April 23, 2018. See 
OML 2018-115; OML 2018-111. We take this opportunity to remind the Board of this 
requirement.
CONCLUSION
For the reasons stated above, we find that the Board did not violate the Open Meeting 
Law. We now consider the complaint addressed by this determination to be resolved. This 
determination does not address any other complaints that may be pending with our office or 
the Board. Please feel free to contact our office at (617) 963-2540 if you have any questions 
regarding this letter.
