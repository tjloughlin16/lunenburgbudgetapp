 RE:        Open Meeting Law Complaint 
 
Dear Mr. Montague: 
 
This office received a complaint from George Harris on May 21, 2020, alleging that the 
Wayland Planning Board (the “Board”) violated the Open Meeting Law, G.L. c. 30A, §§ 18-25. 
The complaint was originally filed with the Board on March 23, 2020, and you responded, on 
behalf of the Board, by letter dated April 13, 2020. The complaint alleges that the Board violated 
the Open Meeting Law by failing to timely approve minutes from its October 17, 2019, 
November 5, 2019, and December 10, 2019, meetings. 
 
After reviewing the complaint, the Board’s response to the complaint, the request for 
further review, and the minutes of the Board’s February 25, 2020, meeting, we resolve this 
complaint by informal action in accordance with 940 CMR 29.07(2)(a). We find that the Board 
violated the Open Meeting Law by failing to timely approve minutes from its October 17, 2019, 
November 5, 2019, and December 10, 2019, meetings. 
 
The Open Meeting Law requires that a public body “create and maintain accurate 
minutes of all meetings, including executive sessions, setting forth the date, time and place, the 
members present or absent, a summary of the discussions on each subject, a list of documents 
and other exhibits used at the meeting, the decisions made and the actions taken at each meeting, 
including the record of all votes.” G.L. c. 30A, § 22(a). The meeting minutes must be reviewed 
and approved in a timely manner. G.L. c. 30A, § 22(c). The Open Meeting Law itself does not 
define “timely manner.” However, the Attorney General’s Open Meeting Law regulations 
provide that “timely manner” means “within the next three public body meetings or within 30

days, whichever is later, unless the public body can show good cause for further delay.” 940 
C.M.R. 29.11 (2). Whenever possible, we recommend that minutes of a meeting be approved at 
the next meeting. See OML 2019-133; OML 2019-39; OML 2014-15.1  
 
The complaint alleges that the Board failed to timely approve minutes from its October 
17, 2019, November 5, 2019, and December 10, 2019, meetings. During the February 25, 2020, 
meeting, the Board approved minutes from its October 17, 2019, meeting, which was 131 days 
and nine meetings thereafter; approved minutes from its November 5, 2019, meeting, which was 
112 days and eight meetings thereafter; and approved minutes from its December 10, 2019, 
meeting, which was 77 days and four meetings thereafter. Although we acknowledge that the 
Board posted draft meeting minutes to the Wayland municipal website, this does not satisfy the 
requirement in the law that the Board approve minutes in a timely manner. See G.L. c. 30A,      § 
22(c). Therefore, we find that the Board failed to approve its October 17, 2019, November 5, 
2019, and December 10, 2019, meeting minutes in a timely manner, and thus violated the Open 
Meeting Law. See OML 2017-155; OML 2015-86.  
 
For the reasons stated above, we find that the Board violated the Open Meeting Law. We 
order the Board’s immediate and future compliance with the law’s requirements, and we caution 
that similar future violations could be considered evidence of intent to violate the law. 
  
We now consider the complaint addressed by this determination to be resolved. This 
determination does not address any other complaints that may be pending with our office or the 
Board. Please feel free to contact our office at (617) 963-2540 if you have any questions 
regarding this letter.
