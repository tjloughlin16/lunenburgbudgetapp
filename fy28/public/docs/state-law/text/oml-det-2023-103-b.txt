This office received a complaint from Patrick Higgins on March 17, 2023, alleging that 
the Fall River Licensing Board (the “Board”) violated the Open Meeting Law, G.L. c. 30A, §§ 
18-25. The complaint was originally filed with the Board on February 25, 2023, and you 
responded to the complaint, on behalf of the Board, by letter dated March 15, 2023.
1 The 
complaint alleges that the Board violated the Open Meeting Law by failing to include sufficient 
specificity on the notice for the Board meeting held on February 23. 
 
We resolve this matter by informal action, in accordance with 940 CMR 29.07(2)(a), 
after reviewing the original complaint, the Board’s response to the complaint, the complaint filed 
with our office requesting further review, and the notices and video recordings of the Board 
meeting held on February 23 and March 14.
2 We find that the Board did not violate the Open 
Meeting Law in the way alleged.

The Open Meeting Law requires that public bodies post notice of every meeting “at least 
48 hours prior to such meeting” and that every notice include “the date, time and place of such 
meeting and a listing of topics that the chair reasonably anticipates will be discussed at the 
meeting.” G.L. c. 30A, § 20(b). Public bodies are required to list topics on a notice with 
“sufficient specificity to reasonably advise the public of the issues to be discussed at the 
meeting.” 940 CMR 29.03(1)(b). We generally consider a topic sufficiently specific when a 
reasonable member of the public could read the topic and understand the anticipated nature of 
the public body’s discussion. See OML 2022-206; OML 2015-35.
3 A public body need not list 
each and every detail about a topic in order to comply with the Law’s requirements. See OML 
2022-98; OML 2020-132; OML 2019-134. When reviewing a meeting notice for sufficiency, 
unless it is clearly insufficient on its face, we do not review a meeting notice standing alone. See 
OML 2015-109; OML 2014-155. Rather, we review what was actually discussed at the meeting 
to determine if the notice was sufficiently detailed to make the public aware of the discussion 
topics. 
 
The complaint alleges that the notice for the Board meeting held on February 23 lacked 
sufficient specificity because the name of the corporation was incorrectly listed as “Sun and Sun 
Convenience” rather than “Sun & Shine Convenience,” and because the notice failed to specify 
Rockland Trust as the receiving entity for the pledges of inventory and collateral. After 
reviewing the notices of both the February 23 and March 14 meetings, as well as the video 
recordings of both meetings, we find that Board did not violate the Open Meeting Law with 
respect to the sufficiency of the February 23 notice. The Open Meeting Law does not require a 
public body to list all tangential topics related to a discussion nor does the law require a public 
body to anticipate the course of deliberation or the outcome of a discussion, only that it identify 
the discussion topic with sufficient specificity to reasonably advise the public of the issues to be 
discussed at the meeting. See G.L. c. 30A, § 20(b); 940 CMR 29.03(l)(b); OML 2015-73; OML 
2014-127; OML 2014-58; OML 2014-38. Although listing additional detail may have been 
helpful to the public, we are satisfied that the topic as listed was sufficient to reasonably advise 
the public of the issue to be discussed at the meeting. See G.L. c. 30A, § 20(b); see also OML 
2013-152. Here, the topic as listed sufficiently identified the topic that was anticipated for

Although we find that the topic as listed on the notice for the Board's February 23
meeting was sufficiently specific, we further note that after receiving the Open Meeting Law
complaint, the Board posted notice for a meeting to be held on March 14, which included the
same topic but correctly identified the business name as "Sun & Shine Convenience." During
that meeting, the Board revoted for clarification and the three motions were approved again.
For the reasons stated above, we find that the Board did not violate the Open Meeting
Law. We now consider the complaint addressed by this determination to be resolved. This
determination does not address any other complaints that may be pending with the Board or with
