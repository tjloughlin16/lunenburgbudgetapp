RE: Open Meeting Law Complaint 
Dear Attorney Hall: 
This office received a complaint from Ms. Karen Merrill dated September 22, alleging 
that the Dracut Finance Committee (the "Committee") violated the Open Meeting Law, G.L. c. 
30A, §§ 18-25.1 The complaint was originally filed with the Committee on August 5, and 
Committee Chair Louise Tremblay and Committee member John Zimini responded in separate 
letters, both dated August 20. In her complaint, Ms. Merrill alleges that the Committee 
discussed a topic not listed on the meeting agenda. 
Following our review, we find that the Committee did not violate the Open Meeting Law. 
In reaching a determination, we reviewed the original complaint; the letters in response from 
Chair Tremblay and Mr. Zimini; the request for further review filed with our office; an agenda 
for a meeting of the Committee held on July 9; and a video recording of the July 9 meeting. 
FACTS 
We find the facts as follows. The Committee is a six-member board that meets regularly 
to discuss matters of financial relevance to the Town of Dracut. The Committee held a meeting 
on July 9. Prior to the meeting, an agenda was posted containing the topics the Committee 
expected to discuss. On it were four topics: 
1) Review, discuss, and act upon a letter of complaint relative to the Finance Committee 
meeting held on Monday June 2, 2014 
2) Review, discuss, and act upon town meeting warrant article #4

3) Review, discuss, and act upon the minutes of the Finance Committee meeting held on 
Monday, June 2, 2014 
4) Executive Session to discuss possible litigation. 
During the discussion of the third agenda item, it became clear that different members of 
the Committee remembered the events of the June 2 meeting differently. To prevent this 
problem from recurring in the future, Mr. Zimini made a motion that all future meetings be 
recorded. The motion passed. Having discussed all business in open session, there was no need 
for the anticipated executive session and the meeting was adjourned. 
DISCUSSION 
The Open Meeting Law requires that public bodies post notice 48 hours in advance of a 
meeting and include a "listing of topics that the chair reasonably anticipates will be discussed at 
the meeting." G.L. c. 30A, § 20(b). A public body may discuss a topic that was not included in 
the meeting notice if the chair did not reasonably anticipate the discussion of that topic 48 hours 
in advance of the meeting. See OML 2012-3; OML 2011-13.2 However, the Attorney General's 
Office strongly encourages public bodies to postpone discussion and action on topics which are 
controversial or may be of particular interest to the public if those topics were not listed on the 
meeting notice. See OML 2012-19. 
At issue is whether the Committee violated the Open Meeting Law by discussing Mr. 
Zimini's motion that future meetings be recorded. While this topic was not listed on the agenda 
for the July 9 meeting, we find that the suggestion to record meetings arose during the course of 
discussion on an anticipated topic. See OML 2014-102; OML 2014-58; OML 2014-44; OML 
2014-38. Accordingly, we find no violation of the Open Meeting Law. See OML 2013-145; 
OML 2012-75. 
As a final matter, we note that the Committee failed to respond to Ms. Merrill's 
complaint in the manner required by law. The Open Meeting Law states that within 14 days of 
receipt of a complaint, the public body must send to the Attorney General a copy of the 
complaint and a description of any remedial action taken. G.L. c. 30A, § 23(b). Before this takes 
place, the Attorney General's regulations require that the chair of a public body "shall 
disseminate copies of the complaint to the members of the public body," and that "the public 
body shall review the complaint's allegations." 940 CMR 29.05(3), (5). It does not appear that 
the Committee reviewed the complaint. Rather, it seems that Chair Tremblay and Mr. Zimini 
responded individually. Proper procedure would have been for the Committee to convene a 
meeting in order to review the complaint before sending a response.3 See OML 2012-90; OML 
2011-6.

CONCLUSION 
For the reasons stated above, we find that the Committee did not violate the Open 
Meeting Law. However, the Committee failed to respond to the complaint in the manner 
required by the Open Meeting Law. We now consider the complaint addressed by this 
determination to be resolved. This determination does not address any other complaints that may 
be pending with our office or the Committee. Please feel free to contact the Division at (617) 
963 - 2540 if you have any questions.
