FACTS 
 
We find the facts as follows.  During an open session meeting held on June 26, 2023, the 
Board discussed abatement requests submitted by Denise Pinardi regarding the valuation of four 
pieces of property on West Chestnut Hill Road.  One of Ms. Pinardi’s sons was present at the 
meeting and presented the Board with additional information.  The Board indicated that it would 
discuss the applications at its next meeting. 
 
The Board posted notice of a meeting to be held on July 10, 2023, at 3:30 P.M.  The 
notice included an executive session topic “to discuss strategy with respect to collective 
bargaining or litigation if an open meeting may have a detrimental effect on the position of the 
public body pursuant to MGL c. 30A§21(a)(3) re: Denise Pinardi.”   
 
The July 10, 2023, meeting was held as planned and the Board convened in open session.  
After the Board approved the minutes of the previous meeting, Director of Assessing Karen 
Tonelli explained that the Board should vote to enter executive session to discuss the Pinardi 
abatement applications.  Board Chair Ann Cenzano announced that the Board would convene in 
executive session, read the reason listed on the notice, and stated that the Board would not return 
to open session after the conclusion of the executive session.  The Board approved a unanimous 
vote by roll call to convene in executive session.  Because the Board has not publicly released 
the minutes of this executive session, we do not recount their content in detail here.  However, 
we understand that the Board discussed the abatement applications submitted by Ms. Pinardi. 
 
The Board posted notice of a meeting to be held on August 21, 2023, at 3:30 P.M.  The 
notice listed several open session topics, including the review of the complainant’s July 27, 
2023, Open Meeting Law complaint and an executive session topic “to comply with or act under 
the authority of any general or special law (MGL Ch 59 § 60) pursuant to MGL c. 30A§21(a)(7) 
re: Denise Pinardi abatement application.”   
 
The August 21, 2023, meeting was held as planned and the Board convened in open 
session.  The Board discussed the July Open Meeting Law complaint filed by the complainant 
and explained that the purpose for convening in executive session that was indicated on the 
notice for the July 10, 2023, meeting “was an unintentional clerical error.”  The Board should 
have listed Purpose 7 not Purpose 3 as the Board anticipated discussing Ms. Pinardi’s abatement 
applications. 
 
Chair Cenzano then announced that the Board would convene in executive session, read 
the reason listed on the notice, and stated the Board would return to open session after the 
conclusion of the executive session.  The Board approved a unanimous vote by roll call to 
convene in executive session.  Because the Board has not publicly released the minutes of this 
executive session, we do not recount their content in detail here.  However, we understand that

DISCUSSION 
 
The Open Meeting Law was enacted “to eliminate much of the secrecy surrounding 
deli
berations and decisions on which public policy is based.”  Ghiglione v. School Committee of 
Southbridge, 376 Mass. 70, 72 (1978).  The Law requires that meetings of a public body be 
properly noticed and open to members of the public, unless an executive session is convened.  
See G.L. c. 30A, §§ 20(a)-(b), 21.  A public body may enter an executive, or closed, session for 
any of the ten purposes enumerated in the Open Meeting Law provided that it has first convened 
in an open session, that a majority of members of the body have voted to go into executive 
session, that the vote of each member is recorded by roll call and entered into the minutes, and 
the chair has publicly announced whether the open session will reconvene at the conclusion of 
the executive session.  G.L. c. 30A, §§ 21(a), (b); see also OML 2014-94.
3   
 
The chair of the public body must announce in open session the purpose for the executive 
ses
sion, “stating all subjects that may be revealed without compromising the purpose for which 
the executive session was called.”  See G.L. c. 30A, §§ 21(a), (b)(3); see also District Attorney 
for the N. Dist. v. School Comm. of Wayland, 455 Mass. 561, 567 (2009) (“[a] precise statement 
of the reason for convening in executive session is necessary ... because that is the only 
notification given the public that a [public body] would conduct business in private, and the only 
way the public would know if the reason for doing so was proper or improper”).  This level of 
detail about the executive session topic must also be included in the meeting notice.  See G.L. c. 
30A, § 20(b); OML 2016-72.   
 
The complaints challenge the propriety of the Board’s executive sessions held on July 10, 
2023, and Augus
t 21, 2023.  One of the ten enumerated purposes for executive session permits a 
public body to enter into executive session “to comply with, or act under the authority of, any 
general or  special law or federal grant-in-aid requirements.”  G.L. c. 30A, § 21(a)(7) (“Purpose 
7”).  Massachusetts General Laws state that applications for tax abatement or exemption may 
only be disclosed to a select group of public officials, though certain information about the 
application must be made public if it is granted.  G.L. c. 59, § 60.  If a Board of Assessors plans 
to discuss the content of an application for tax abatement or exemption, therefore, the Board may 
convene in executive session under Purpose 7, “to comply with, or act under the authority of, any 
general law,” citing G.L. c. 59, § 60 as the statute requiring confidentiality.  See OML 2015-142; 
OML 2013-96.  Accordingly, we find here that the Board’s discussions of abatement 
applications during the meetings held on July 10, 2023, and August 21, 2023, were appropriate 
for executive session under Purpose 7.

While we conclude that the Board had a valid statutory basis for holding the discussions 
in executive session, the Board failed to cite the correct statutory purpose for the executive 
session held on July 10, 2023, both on the meeting notice and in the verbal statement made 
during the meeting.  In addition to relying on executive session Purpose 3 instead of Purpose 7, 
the Board also failed to provide an adequate description of the matter to be discussed in 
executive session, in violation of the Open Meeting Law.  See OML 2021-62.  Although Ms. 
Tonelli explained to the Board that it would review abatement applications during the July 10, 
2023, executive session, when the Chair convened the executive session, she described the 
discussion as one involving a litigation matter rather than describing it as a discussion of an 
abatement application.  See OML 2016-20.  Where a public body has erroneously cited to the 
wrong statutory purpose when entering executive session, we have found no violation of the 
Open Meeting Law if there otherwise was an adequately detailed description of the topic to be 
discussed such that a member of the public could understand the nature of the topic and conclude 
that the topic was appropriate for discussion in executive session.  See, e.g., OML 2023-39 (no 
violation where School Committee incorrectly cited Purpose 3 instead of Purpose 1 for entering 
executive session to discuss Open Meeting Law complaint; chair announced prior to convening 
in executive session that Committee would discuss an Open Meeting Law complaint).  Here, we 
find that the Board did not provide a detailed description adequate to cure reference to the 
incorrect executive session purpose.  Therefore, the notice for the July 10, 2023, meeting did not 
satisfy the requirements of the Open Meeting Law.  See OML 2023-241. 
 
Finally, the complaints allege that the Board does not conduct a periodic review of 
exec
utive minutes.  Public bodies must create and maintain accurate minutes of all meetings.  
G.L. c. 30A, § 22(a).  Executive session minutes may be withheld from disclosure to the public 
“as long as publication may defeat the lawful purposes of the executive session, but no longer.”  
G.L. c. 30A, § 22(f).  When the purpose for a valid executive session has been served, the 
minutes and any documents or exhibits used at the session must be disclosed unless the attorney-
client privilege or an exemption to the public records law applies to withhold them, in whole or 
in part, from disclosure.  See id.   
 
The Open Meeting Law requires that a public body or its chair or designee must, at 
rea
sonable intervals, review the minutes of executive sessions to determine if continued 
nondisclosure of those minutes is warranted.  G.L. c. 30A, § 22(g)(1).  Although “reasonable 
interval” is not defined by the law, our office has found a quarterly review or a review every six 
months to be appropriate.  See OML 2019-45; OML 2017-104; OML 2015-166; OML 2013-180. 
At the meeting following each periodic review, the public body must announce which executive 
session minutes will be released and which will continue to be withheld, and such announcement 
shall be included in the minutes.  G.L. c. 30A, § 22(g)(1); OML 2019-3; OML 2015-94; OML 
2013-56.  The Board acknowledges, and we find, that it did not conduct a periodic review of 
executive session minutes.  However, the Board has committed to regularly reviewing executive 
session minutes to determine whether continued nondisclosure is warranted, and we commend 
the Board for ensuring future compliance with the law.  We remind the Board that it must 
announce the executive session minutes to be released and those to be withheld during the open 
meeting following the review of such minutes, and that such announcement must be included in 
the minutes.

CONCLUSION 
For the reasons stated above, we find that the Board violated the Open Meeting Law by 
posting a notice with insufficient specificity for its July 10, 2023, executive session and failing to 
periodically review executive session minutes.  We order the Board’s immediate and future 
compliance with the law’s requirements, and we caution that similar future violations could be 
considered evidence of intent to violate the law.  Although the Board cited to the incorrect 
statutory purpose for its July 10, 2023, executive session, where we find that discussion in 
executive session was authorized under another purpose, we decline to order further remedial 
action. 
We now consider the complaints addressed by this determination to be resolved.  This 
determination does not address any other complaints that may be pending with our office or the 
Board.  Please feel free to contact our office at (617) 963-2540 if you have any questions 
regarding this letter.
