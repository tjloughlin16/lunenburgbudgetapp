DISCUSSION 
 
The Open Meeting Law requires that a public body “create and maintain accurate 
minutes of all meetings, including executive sessions, setting forth the date, time and place, the 
members present or absent, a summary of the discussions on each subject, a list of documents 
and other exhibits used at the meeting, the decisions made and the actions taken at each meeting, 
including the record of all votes.”  G.L. c. 30A, § 22(a).  When reviewing minutes for 
compliance with the Open Meeting Law, we look for substantial compliance with the accuracy 
requirement.  See OML 2016-105; OML 2013-64.
2  By substantial compliance, we mean that the 
minutes should contain enough detail and accuracy so that a member of the public who did not 
attend the meeting could read the minutes and have a clear understanding of what occurred.  See 
OML 2012-106.   
 
The complaint alleges that the November 21, 2019, meeting minutes include inaccurate 
statements.  First, the complaint maintains that statements made by Board member Brian 
O’Rourke, and captured in the minutes, relative to the inspection of the complainant’s septic 
system and work done by her contractor are inaccurate.  This office reviews meeting minutes to 
ensure that they accurately reflect the discussions that take place during meetings; our office 
does not review minutes to ensure that the statements made during meetings are factually true.  
See OML 2015-133.  The complainant confirmed in a telephone conversation with this office 
that Mr. O’Rourke made the statements reflected in the minutes.  As such, we find that the 
minutes of the Board’s November 21, 2019, meeting are an accurate summary of the discussions 
that occurred during the meeting.   
 
Second, the complainant insists that the minutes inaccurately record that she was yelling 
and swearing during the meeting.  The Board asserts that the complainant was yelling and 
swearing and therefore asserts that the minutes are an accurate summary of what occurred during 
the November 21, 2019, meeting.  Because this meeting was not recorded, there is no definitive 
evidence contradicting either the complainant’s or the Board’s statements.  Following our 
review, we find insufficient evidence to conclude that the meeting minutes are inaccurate in this 
regard.  Accordingly, we find that the Board did not violate the Open Meeting Law.  See OML 
2018-50.

Finally, with respect to the complainant’s concern that the minutes do not include 
questions she asked of the Board, the Open Meeting Law does not require a transcript of the 
discussions, nor that the minutes include every remark made or opinion presented.  See OML 
2017-29; OML 2012-29; OML 2011-55.  With respect to the complainant’s concern that she was 
silenced during the November 21, 2019, meeting, the Open Meeting Law states that “[n]o person 
shall address a meeting of a public body without permission of the chair, and all persons shall, at 
the request of the chair, be silent.”  G.L. c. 30A § 20(f).  The law does not require that a public 
body allow public participation, but rather gives the chair of the public body discretion to decide 
whether to permit public comment during a meeting.  See OML 2017-189; OML 2014-23; OML 
2012-78.  Moreover, the law further states that “[n]o person shall disrupt the proceedings of a 
meeting of a public body.  If, after clear warning from the chair, a person continues to disrupt the 
proceedings, the chair may order the person to withdraw from the meeting and if the person does 
not withdraw, the chair may authorize a constable or other officer to remove the person from the 
meeting.”  Id.  Because it is within the Chair's discretion to limit public participation during a 
meeting, he did not violate the Open Meeting Law by restricting the complainant from speaking 
or asking questions.  We do, however, encourage public bodies to allow for as much public 
participation as time and circumstances permit.  See OML 2019-120; OML 2015-12; OML 
2014-129; OML 2012-59. 
 
CONCLUSION 
 
 For the reasons stated above, we find that the Board did not violate the Open Meeting 
Law.  We now consider the complaint addressed by this determination to be resolved.  This 
determination does not address any other complaints that may be pending with the Board or with 
our office.  Please feel free to contact our office at (617) 963-2540 if you have any questions 
regarding this letter.
