This office received a complaint from Peter Krawczyk, dated June 2, 2012, alleging that 
the Warren Board of Selectmen (the "Board") violated the Open Meeting Law, G.L. c. 30, §§ 18-
25. The complaint alleges that the Board's March 3, 2012 and March 10, 2012 meeting notices 
failed to contain topics with sufficient specificity to reasonably advise members of the public of 
the topics for discussion, and that the meetings held on those dates where held on days in which 
Town Hall was closed. The complaint also alleges that on March 3, 2012 the Board 1) held its 
meeting at a location for which the handicapped accessible entrance was locked; 2) failed to 
clear the municipal building's parking lot of snow which made it difficult for an individual with 
a wheelchair to attend the meeting; and 3) failed to record and broadcast its meeting. This 
complaint was originally filed with the Board on March 15, 2012. We understand that the Board 
responded to the complainant in a letter dated March 20, 2012, but failed to send a copy of its 
response to the Attorney General's Office as required by the Open Meeting Law.' See G.L. c. 
30A, § 23(b). 
Following our review we find that the Board did not violate the Open Meeting Law by 
failing to post meeting notice topics with sufficient specificity; by failing to make video 
recordings and broadcast its meetings; or by holding its March 3, 2012 meeting at a location for 
which the handicapped accessible entrance was locked and the parking lot was not plowed. 
However, we find that the Board violated the Open Meeting Law by not providing reasonable

FACTS 
March 3, 2012 Meeting 
A notice for the Board's Saturday, March 3, 2012 meeting to be held at the Shepard 
Municipal Building (the "Municipal Building") was posted on Wednesday, February 29, 2012, 
and contained two topics: "Annual Town Meeting Articles" and "FY13 Budgets." The 
Municipal Building was closed for normal business that day. We understand that there was a 
dusting of snow during the morning of March 3, 2012. According to the Board, the amount of 
snow did not require snow plowing of the parking lot. The Board states that the main entrances 
to the Municipal Building, including the handicapped accessible entrance, were open throughout 
the meeting and salted by a member of the Board prior to the start of the meeting. 
The meeting convened at 8:00 A.M. The minutes state that: 
The Board reviewed all Town Budgets for FY2013. The Board approved all budgets 
except the following until further information is furnished: Building Department, Fire 
Department, and Sewer Department. The Board is waiting for the following budgets: 
Cable, Landfill, Parks, and Warren Library. 
The Board tabled discussion of the Annual Town Meeting Articles until its next meeting. The 
meeting was adjourned at 10:30 A.M. The Board did not make a video recording of the meeting. 
March 10, 2012 Meeting 
A notice for the Board's Saturday, March 10, 2012 meeting, to be held at the Municipal 
Building, was posted on Tuesday, March 6, 2012. The notice contained one topic: 
"Special/Annual Town Meeting Articles." The meeting was convened at 8:00 A.M. The 
Municipal Building was closed for regular business on that day. According to the Board, both 
main entrances to the Municipal Building, including the handicapped accessible entrance, were 
open throughout the meeting, however. 
The minutes of the meeting list the articles that the Board reviewed and record the 
Board's vote with respect to each article. The meeting was adjourned at 9:00 A.M. The Board 
did not make a video recording of the meeting.

DISCUSSION 
I. The Board's March 3, 2012 and March 10, 2012 Meeting Notices Listed Topics that 
were Sufficiently Specific.  
The Open Meeting Law requires that public bodies post notice of each meeting 48 hours 
in advance of the meeting, excluding Saturdays, Sundays, and legal holidays. G.L. c. 30A, § 
20(b). Such notice must contain the date, time, and location of the meeting, along with a listing 
of topics that the chair reasonably anticipates will be discussed at the meeting. Id. The listing of 
topics must contain enough detail to give members of the public a clear understanding of what 
will be discussed during the meeting. 940 CMR. 29.03(1)(b). We generally consider a topic to 
include sufficient specificity when a reasonable member of the public could read the topic and 
understand what the nature of the public body's discussion will be. See OML 2012-34. 2  
The complainant states that the topics listed on Board's March 3, 2012 and March 10, 
2012 meeting notices were "vague" and did not contain a "breakdown of what [the Board was] 
going to discuss." The March 3, 2012 meeting notice contained the topic: "FY13 Budgets." 3  
According to the minutes, the Board met with various department heads and reviewed "all Town 
budgets" for the upcoming year. The March 10, 2012 meeting notice contained the topic: 
"Special/Annual Town Meeting Articles." During that meeting, the Board reviewed and made 
recommendations on all articles for the upcoming special and annual Town Meetings. 
Where a public body anticipates discussing specific budget items or Town Meeting 
warrant articles, it must specifically list them in its meeting notice. See OML 2011-7. However, 
where a public body is reviewing the entire Town budget or all articles for a Town Meeting 
warrant, it is not required to identify all budget items or warrant articles on its meeting notice. 
See OML 2012-83. The notices for the Board's March 3, 2012 and March 10, 2012 meetings 
were sufficiently specific because they informed the public that the Board would be generally 
reviewing the Town budget for the upcoming year and the warrant for the upcoming Special and 
Annual Town Meeting. 
II. The Board did not Violate the Open Meeting Law by Failing to Make a Video 
Recording or Broadcast its March 3, 2012 and March 10, 2012 Meetings.  
The complainant alleges that the Board violated the Open Meeting Law by failing to 
record and broadcast its meetings. The Open Meeting Law states that "after notifying the chair 
of the public body, any person may make a video or audio recording of an open session of a 
meeting of a public body, or may transmit the meeting through any medium." G.L. c. 30A, § 
20(e). The complainant does not allege that anyone was denied the right to record or broadcast 
the March 3, 2012 or March 10, 2012 meetings, nor have we been presented with any evidence 
that suggests so. In addition, a public body is not required to make an audio or video recording 
of its meetings. See OML 2010-3. Therefore, we find that the Board did not violate the Open 
Meeting Law by failing to record and broadcast its March 3, 2012 and March 10, 2012 meetings.

III. The Board did not Violate the Open Meeting Law because its March 13, 2012  
Meeting was held at a Location where the Door was Open and the Entrance was not 
Obstructed by Snow.  
The Open Meeting Law states that all meetings of a public body must be open to the 
public. G.L. c. 30A, § 20(a). For a meeting to be truly "open to the public," it must be held at a 
location that is accessible to persons with disabilities. See id.. see also OML 2012-49 (stating 
that meetings of public bodies must be held in locations that are compliant with the Americans 
with Disabilities Act). Furthermore, Title II of the Americans with Disability Act requires that 
municipal services, including public meetings, be made accessible to people with disabilities. 42 
U.S.C. §§ 12131-12165 (1990). 
There is disagreement between parties as to the level of accessibility of the March 3, 
2012 meeting. The complainant claims that on March 3, 2012, he "went to the [Municipal 
Building], and the only door open was the one to get to the selectmen's room. The handicapped 
accessible entrance was locked." Furthermore, the complainant claims that "the parking lot was 
not plowed so a person in a wheel chair could not enter the building even if they wanted to." 
The Board claims that both main entrances of the Municipal Building, including the handicapped 
accessible entrance, were unlocked during its March 3, 2012 meeting. Additionally, the Board 
claims that the amount of snow that accumulated on the morning of March 3, 2012, did not 
require snow plowing of the parking lot, and both entrances to the building were treated by one 
of its members prior to the start of the meeting. We credit the Board's account, and do not find 
that the Board held a meeting at a location for which the handicapped entrance was locked, or 
failed to clear the parking lot of snow. 
IV. The Board did not Violate the Open Meeting Law by Holding its March 3, 2012 and 
March 10, 2012 Meetings on a Day in which Town Hall was Closed, but did Violate 
the Open Meeting Law by Failing to Provide Reasonable Accommodations to  
Individuals who Require Mobility Aids.  
The Board's webpage, located on the Town of Warren's website, 4  contains the following 
disclaimer: 
The Shepard Building is accessible by means of a stair lift to those unable to climb the 
steps and to those using conventional wheelchairs. The lift does not accommodate 
scooter type chairs due to the weight limit Persons wishing to meet with the Selectmen 
who are unable to use the lift may contact the office 24 hours before a posted meeting to 
request an accommodation. 
The complainant states that the meetings held on March 3, 2012 and March 10, 2012 
were not handicapped accessible because the Municipal Building is closed on Fridays. 
Therefore, individuals requiring use of the wheel chair lift were not able to request 
accommodations 24 hours before the meetings. 
Public bodies must assure independent physical access to their meetings for people who 
use mobility aids. See 28 CFR §§ 35.149-.150; G.L. c. 93 § 103; G.L. c. 30A § 20(a); Dep't of 
Justice, Americans with Disabilities Act: Title II Technical Assistance Manual, at 5.1000 (1993).

While the Open Meeting Law does not prohibit a public body from holding a meeting on a day 
for which public buildings are closed,5  requiring 24 hours' notice for people who use scooter 
type mobility aids does not conform to this requirement. Therefore, the Board violated the Open 
Meeting Law on March 3, 2012 and March 10, 2012 by failing to provide reasonable 
accommodations to individuals who require mobility aids.6  
V. 
	
	 The Board Failed to Create Adequate Minutes of its March 3, 2012 and March 10, 
2012 Meetings.  
While not specifically alleged in this complaint, we would be remiss in our obligations to 
point out that the Board failed to create sufficiently detailed minutes of its March 3, 2012 and 
March 10, 2012 meetings. The Open Meeting Law requires public bodies to "create and 
maintain accurate minutes of all meetings . . . setting forth the date, time and place, the members 
present or absent, a summary of the discussions on each subject, a list of documents and other 
exhibits used at the meeting." G.L. c. 30A, § 22(a) (emphasis added). Meeting minutes do not 
have to contain every remark or statement made during the meeting, but must be detailed enough 
so that a member of the public who did not attend the meeting could read the minutes and have a 
clear understanding of what occurred. See OML 2012-29; OML 2011-55. 
The Board's March 3, 2012 meeting lasted two and a half hours, however the minutes 
only contain four brief sentences. A member of the public who was not in attendance at the 
meeting would not have a clear understanding of what occurred during the meeting. The March 
10, 2012 meeting minutes simply lists the warrant articles that the Board reviewed and action it 
took with regards to the articles. The minutes do not contain a summary of the discussion that 
took place with regards to each article. 
CONCLUSION 
We find that the Board did not violate the Open Meeting Law because the Board's March 
3, 2012 and March 10, 2012 meeting notices listed topics with sufficient specificity. The Board 
also did not violate the Open Meeting Law by failing to make a video recording and broadcast its 
March 3, 2012 and March 10, 2012 meetings because it was not required to do so. Finally, the 
Board's March 3, 2012 meeting did not violate the Open Meeting Law because it was held at a 
location for which the handicapped accessible entrance was open and the parking lot was 
unobstructed by snow. However, we do find that the Board violated the Open Meeting Law by 
not providing reasonable accommodations during its March 3, 2012 and March 10, 2012 
meetings to individuals who require mobility aids. Additionally, we find that the Board's March 
3, 2012 and March 10, 2012 meeting minutes failed to contain an adequate summary of the 
discussions on each topic.
