RE: Open Meeting Law Complaint 
Dear Mr. Yunits, 
This office received a complaint from Katherine Connolly, dated September 28, 2010, 
alleging that the Holbrook Board of Selectmen (the "Board") violated the Open Meeting Law, 
G.L. c. 30A, §§ 18-25, by failing to adequately inform the public of a topic for discussion at an 
open meeting on August 24, 2010. A copy of that complaint is enclosed. 
Under the Open Meeting Law, the Attorney General is vested with the responsibility of 
investigating this matter. We reviewed the complaint, the Board of Selectmen's response, dated 
September 24, 2010, the notice for the August 24, 2010 meeting of the Board of Selectmen, and 
the minutes from the April 29, 2010 and August 24, 2010 Board of Selectmen meetings. 
The complaint specifically alleges that the Board discussed a study to extend Hanison 
Boulevard into Avon, and that the "[t]he agenda item did not clearly defme the location or 
address of this proposed 'complex' during its August 24, 2010 meeting. Our review of the 
August 24, 2010 notice for the meeting of the Board finds that that Board lists "F. Proposed 
Holbrook Industrial Development Complex." under "New Business" on the notice. 
Public bodies are required to list topics in a meeting notice with "sufficient specificity to 
reasonably advise the public of the issues to be discussed at the meeting." 940 CMR. 29.03. 
When planning to discuss a specific piece of property, public bodies should include sufficient 
information in the meeting notice to identify the property. A specific address is always 
preferable; however, if the Board is unable to list a specific address then it may list bordering 
streets, intersections, landniarks or other identifying information. 
===PAGE 2===
Here, the minutes of the April 29, 2010 meeting indicate that the Board heard a 
presentation on a specific proposed location for the Holbrook Industrial Development Complex.' 
The August 24, 2010 meeting notice did not provide stIfficient information because it did not 
identify the specific property to be discussed, either by address or by identifying landmarks. 
Rather, the notice simply referred to the proposed project by name. Thus, the Board failed to 
fully comply with the Open Meeting Law. 
• In the future, the Board of Selectmen is required to comply with the Open Meeting Law, 
G.L. c. 30A, §§ 18-25, and is advised to provide sufficient detail in meeting notices so that 
members of the public know with specificity what issues and locations will be discussed by the 
Board. The requirement that meeting notices list topics with sufficient specificity is new, and the 
Board partially complied with it. Therefore, we resolve this complaint by providing additional 
guidance to the Board. However, the Board should be aware that evidence of similar violations 
in the future will be considered evidence of an intent to violate the Open Meeting Law. See 940 
CMR 29.02. We decline to take the additional stcp requested by the complainant, which is to 
rescind action taken by the Board of Selectmen on this matter.
