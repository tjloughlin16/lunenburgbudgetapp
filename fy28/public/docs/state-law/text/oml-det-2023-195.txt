 RE: Open Meeting Law Complaint 
 
Dear Attorney Mackey: 
This office received a complaint from Patrick Higgins on August 7, 2023, alleging that 
the Massachusetts Gaming Commission (the “Commission”) violated the Open Meeting Law, 
G.L. c. 30A, §§ 18-25.  The complaint was originally filed with the Commission on or around 
July 10, and you responded, on behalf of the Commission, by letter dated July 31.1  The 
complaint alleges that the Commission failed to list on the notice for its July 10 meeting the 
names of candidates for the Interim Executive Director position that it planned to interview 
during the meeting. 
We resolve this matter by informal action, in accordance with 940 CMR 29.07(2)(a), 
after reviewing the original complaint, the Commisison’s response to the complaint, the 
complaint filed with our office requesting further review, and the notice for the Commission’s 
July 10 meeting.  We find that the Commission violated the Open Meeting Law by failing to list 
on the notice for its July 10 meeting the names of candidates it planned to interview during the 
meeting.   
 The Open Meeting Law requires that a public body post notice of every meeting at least 
48 hours in advance, not including Saturdays, Sundays, and legal holidays.  G.L. c. 30A, § 20(b).  
Notices must include “a listing of topics that the chair reasonably anticipates will be discussed at 
the meeting.”  Id.  The list of topics shall have sufficient specificity to reasonably advise the 
public of the issues to be discussed at the meeting.  940 CMR 29.03(l)(b).  We have consistently 
stated that to comply with the specificity requirement, the names of interview candidates and 
applicants for employment or appointment which are known 48 hours in advance of a meeting

should be listed on a meeting notice.  See, e.g., OML 2023-15; OML 2022-219; OML 2020-80; 
OML 2019-24; OML 2018-94; OML 2016-150; OML 2013-163; OML 2011-23.  There is no 
suggestion that the Commission did not know the names of the candidates it intended to 
interview during its July 10 meeting at least 48 hours in advance of the meeting; therefore, the 
names of the candidates should have been included on the meeting notice.  By identifying the 
candidates for interview only as “Candidate 1” and “Candidate 2” on the meeting notice, the 
Commission violated the Open Meeting Law.  
For the reasons stated above, we find that the Commission violated the Open Meeting 
Law by failing to list on the notice for its July 10 meeting the names of candidates it planned to 
interview during the meeting.  We order the Commission’s immediate and future compliance 
with the Open Meeting Law and caution the Commission that a determination by our office of a 
similar violation in the future may be considered evidence of intent to violate the Open Meeting 
Law.   
We now consider the complaint addressed by this determination to be resolved.  This 
determination does not address any other complaints that may be pending with our office or the 
Commission.  Please feel free to contact the Division at (617) 963 - 2540 if you have any 
questions.
