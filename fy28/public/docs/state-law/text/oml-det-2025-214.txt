FACTS 
 
We find the facts  to be as follows. The Board posted notice for a meeting to be held on 
July 28. The notice included several topics, one of which was “Dave Graziano, Tree Warden and 
Ryan Kress, National Grid: Continuation of April 17, 2025[,] Tree Hearing.” During the Board’s 
July 28, meeting, Dave Graziano, the Town’s Tree Warden, called to order the continuation of a 
public hearing that Mr. Graziano had held on April 17 regarding National Grid’s request to 
remove public shade trees under an extended vegetation management program. Town Counsel 
had recommended that the continued hearing be held during the Board’s July 28 meeting because 
the Town had received from the Complainant a written objection to National Grid’s proposed 
plan and the Board would ultimately be required to consider and make a determination with 
respect to the objection. Mr. Graziano opened the public hearing, explaining that the purpose of 
National Grid’s proposed plan is to ensure that the electrical grid is safe and protected from 
forces of nature. The Complainant and Ryan Kress, representative for National Grid, both 
commented on the specifics of the plan and how it would impact the Town and homeowners. 
Town Coun
sel and Town Administrator spoke as well, providing advice regarding the hearing 
and the Board’s role in the process.  
 
 All t
hree members of the Board also discussed the proposed removal of trees and 
National Grid’s extended vegetation management program, sharing their opinions and asking 
questions. After these discussions, Mr. Graziano closed the public hearing. Thereafter, the Board 
voted unanimously to support Mr. Graziano’s recommendation that the Town participate in 
National Grid’s extended vegetation management program.  
 
DISCUSSION 
 
  The  Ope
 n Meeting Law was enacted “to eliminate much of the secrecy surrounding the 
deliberations and decisions on which public policy is based.” Ghiglione v. Sch. Comm. of 
Southbridge, 376 Mass. 70, 72 (1978). Meeting notices must contain the date, time, and place of 
the meeting, as well as a listing of topics that the chair reasonably anticipates will be discussed. 
Id. The list of topics shall have “sufficient specificity to reasonably advise the public of the 
issues to be discussed at the meeting.” 940 CMR 29.03(l)(b). We generally consider a topic to be 
sufficiently specific when a reasonable member of the public could read the topic and understand 
the anticipated nature of the public body’s discussion. See OML 2015-35. When reviewing a 
meeting notice for sufficiency, unless it is clearly insufficient on its face, we do not review a 
meeting notice standing alone. See OML 2016-149; OML 2015-109; OML 2014-155. Rather, we 
review what was actually discussed at the meeting to determine if the notice was sufficiently 
specific to make the public aware of the discussion topics. See OML 2016-149. Although it is 
good practice for a notice to specify that a vote will be taken when a vote is indeed anticipated, 
the Open Meeting Law does not require this. See OML 2024-192; OML 2024-156; OML 2023-
186; OML 2021-153; OML 2019-157; OML 2019-36; OML 2015-66; OML 2012-75. It is

2012-75. 
 
 The com
 plaint specifically alleges that the Board violated the Open Meeting Law where 
the notice topic “Dave Graziano, Tree Warden and Ryan Kress, National Grid: Continuation of 
April 17, 2025[,] Tree Hearing” did not indicate that the Board might take a vote. We do not find 
the notice insufficient because it failed to indicate that a vote may be taken. As explained above, 
it is reasonably foreseeable that a public body may take action following discussion of a topic 
and the Open Meeting Law does not require that a public body indicate on its meeting notice that 
a vote may be taken. However, we find the notice insufficiently specific where the topic “Dave 
Graziano, Tree Warden and Ryan Kress, National Grid: Continuation of April 17, 2025[,] Tree 
Hearing” did not reasonably inform the public that the Board would discuss whether to approve 
the removal of trees as proposed by National Grid under its extended vegetation management 
program. See OML 2024-218; OML 2024-43. Although the topic, as listed, might have been 
meaningful to Board members and individuals already aware of the Tree Warden’s previous 
public hearing on the matter, we find it was insufficient to reasonably inform members of the 
public unfamiliar with the issue of the anticipated nature of the discussion. See OML 2021-121; 
OML 2020-164; OML 2013-55. Therefore, we find that the Board violated the Open Meeting 
Law by including an insufficiently specific topic on the notice for its July 28 meeting. 
 
CONCLUSION 
 
For the reasons stated above, we find that the Board violated the Open Meeting Law by 
including an insufficiently specific topic on the notice for its July 28 meeting. We order the 
Board’s immediate and future compliance with the Open Meeting Law and caution that similar 
future violations may be considered evidence of an intent to violate the Open Meeting Law. 
Because the minutes and video recording of the meeting are available online and because the 
Complainant did in fact attend the July 28 meeting despite the insufficiently specific notice, we 
do not order additional remedial action. 
 
We now consider the complaint addressed by this determination to be resolved. This 
determination does not address any other complaints that may be pending with the Board or with 
our office. Please feel free to contact our office at (617) 963-2540 if you have any questions 
regarding this letter.
