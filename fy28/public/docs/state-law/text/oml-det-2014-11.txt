RE; Open Meeting Law Complaint 
Dear Attorney Riley: 
This office received a complaint from Kimberly Wetherell, dated November 29, 2013, 
alleging that the Peru Board of Selectmen (the Board) violated the Open Meeting Law, G.L. c. 
30A, §§ 18-25. The complaint was originally filed with the Board on or about August 26, 2013, 
and you responded, on behalf of the Board, to the complaint by letter dated October 8, 2013. In 
her complaint, Ms. Wetherell alleges that during its August 19, 2013 meeting, the Board's 
meeting location could not accommodate all members of the public in attendance. 
We reviewed the August 26, 2013 complaint; your October 8, 2013 response to the 
complaint, on behalf of the Board; and the November 29, 2013 complaint filed with our office 
requesting further review. We also reviewed the minutes and portions of an audio recording 
from the August 19, 2013 meeting. Finally, we reviewed photographs of the meeting room taken 
during the Board's August 19, 2013 meeting. 
Following our review, we find that the Board did not violate the Open Meeting Law. 
We find the facts to be as follows. During its August 19, 2013 meeting, the Board 
convened at its regular meeting location, but had a larger than usual group of individuals in 
attendance. According to the minutes from the August 19, 2013 meeting, in addition to the two 
Board members, eleven residents, two town officials and one Town employee attended the 
meeting. While everyone in attendance was able to see and hear the proceedings, there was not 
adequate seating and some attendees had to stand on the other side of a half wall. Based on the 
composition and the numerous obstacles in the room, such as tables and a half wall, additional 
seating was unfeasible or, at the very least, would have impeded attendees' view of the meeting.

In response to this complaint, beginning with its meeting on September 9, 2013, the 
Board relocated its regular meeting location to a gymnasium space in Town Hall in order to 
accommodate a larger number of attendees. 
DISCUSSION 
The Open Meeting Law states that all meetings of a public body must be open to the 
public. G.L. c. 30A, § 20(a). Access must include the opportunity to be physically present as 
well as to see and hear what is being discussed by the members of the public body. OML 2014-
3; OML 2013-189; OML 2012-66. In addition, for a meeting to be truly "open to the public," it 
must be held at a location that is accessible to persons with disabilities. See G.L. c. 30A, § 20(a); 
see also OML 2012-49 (stating that meetings of public bodies must be held in locations that are 
compliant with the Americans with Disabilities Act). 
We find that the Board did not violate the Open Meeting Law because individuals in 
attendance had sufficient access to the meeting. See OML 2014-3. The photographs show a 
crowded room with most of the attendees standing around the edges, but do not demonstrate that 
the meeting was inaccessible to members of the public. Individuals could hear and see the 
Board, but were not provided with adequate seating. Although the Board did not violate the law, 
it nevertheless took appropriate action in response to this complaint by relocating its regular 
meeting location to a larger space. We commend the Board's efforts to be responsive to the 
complainant's concerns. 
CONCLUSION 
For the reasons stated above, we find that the Board did not violate the Open Meeting 
Law. We now consider the complaint addressed by this determination to be resolved. This 
determination does not address any other complaints which may be pending with our office or 
the Board. Please feel free to contact our office at (617) 963-2540 if you have any questions 
regarding this letter.
