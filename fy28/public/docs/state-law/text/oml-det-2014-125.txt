RE: Open Meeting Law Complaint 
Dear Attorney Dupere: 
This office received a complaint filed by Richard Cohen, dated September 15, alleging 
that the Lanesborough Elementary School Committee (the "Committee") violated the Open 
Meeting Law, G.L. c. 30A, §§ 18-25.' Specifically, the complaint alleges that the Committee 
discussed a topic during its afternoon meeting on April 30 that was not described in sufficient 
detail in the meeting notice.2 The complaint was originally filed with the Committee on May 28. 
i 3 The Committee responded by letter dated August 1. 
Following our review, we find that the Committee's notice did not violate the Open 
Meeting Law. In reaching this determination, we reviewed the complaint filed with the 
Committee, the Committee's response, and the complaint filed with our office. Additionally, we 
reviewed the notice for and minutes of the Committee's two April 30 meetings.

The Committee posted notice for two April 30 meetings, one at 3:30 PM and one at 7:00

Budget/Public Flearing," "2014-15 School Calendar," and "FY 15 lunch pricing decisions." 
On the morning of April 30, Mr. Cohen emailed Chair Barton to protest the 3:30 PM 
meeting, writing that the meeting would violate the Open Meeting Law because the notice did 
not provide sufficient detail about the anticipated topic for discussion. Mr. Cohen asked that 
both meetings be rescheduled. The Committee did not reschedule the meetings. 
The Committee met on April 30 at 3:30 PM. The Committee discussed the proposed 
FY15 school budget. Chair Barton led a review of several budget items that he felt might be 
reduced, including school lunch, sick leave, sidewalk repairs, a new hire, and special education 
transportation. No votes were taken at the meeting. 
The Committee met again at 7:00 PM. After introducing the proposed FY15 school 
budget, the Committee held a hearing to receive input from members of the public. The 
Committee then reviewed the budget and voted on a number of changes, including approving a 
price increase for school lunches. 
DISCUSSION 
The Open Meeting Law requires that public bodies post notice of every meeting at least 
48 hours prior to such meeting, excluding Saturdays, Sundays and legal holidays, and include in 
the notice "the date, time, place of such meeting and a listing of topics that the chair reasonable 
anticipates will be discussing at the meeting." G.L. c. 30A, § 20(b). Public bodies are required 
to list topics in a meeting notice with "sufficient specificity to reasonably advise the public of the 
issues to be discussed at the meeting." 940 CMR 29.03(l)(b). We generally consider a topic to

include sufficient specificity when a reasonable member of the public could read the topic and 
understand the anticipated nature of the public body's discussion. See OML 2012-10. 
Here, the Committee posted notice for a 3:30 PM April 30 meeting and included the topic 
"FY 15 Budget discussion." During the meeting, the Committee discussed different portions of 
the budget as part of a review of potential reductions ahead of a formal public hearing at 7:00 
PM that night. While it may have been helpful for the Chair to include additional details in the 
notice about specific budget items he anticipated discussing, as written the topic was sufficient to 
inform the public that the Committee would be discussing the entire FY 15 school budget. We 
therefore find that the notice complied with the Open Meeting Law. 
As a final matter, although not raised by the complainant, we note our concern about 
certain emails that we reviewed during the course of our investigation. We remind the 
Committee that communications over email regarding Committee business between and among 
members of the Committee must be limited to the distribution of a meeting agenda, scheduling 
information or the distribution of reports or documents to be discussed at a meeting. Id. Any 
further discussion must be reserved for a properly noticed open meeting. G.L. c. 30A, §§ 18, 
20(b). 
CONCLUSION 
We find that the notice for the Committee's April 30 afternoon meeting did not violate 
the Open Meeting Law. With the issuance of this determination, we now consider this matter 
closed. Please contact our office at (617) 963-2540 if you have any questions.
