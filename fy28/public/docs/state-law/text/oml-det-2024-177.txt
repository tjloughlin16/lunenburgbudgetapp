RE: Open Meeting Law Complaint 
 
Dear Attorney Assad: 
 
This office received a complaint from Patrick Higgins on July 23, 2024, alleging that the 
Fall River Superintendent of Schools Search Committee (the “Committee”) violated the Open 
Meeting Law, G.L. c. 30A, §§ 18-25.  The complaint was originally filed with the Committee on 
June 19, 2024; and you responded to the complaint, on behalf of the Committee, by letter dated 
August 22, 2024.
1  The complaint alleges that the Committee failed to timely respond to a 
request for meeting minutes.   
 
Following our review, we find that the Committee did not violate the Open Meeting Law 
in the way alleged.  In reaching this determination, we reviewed the original complaint, the 
Committee’s response to the complaint, and the complaint filed with our office requesting further 
review.   
 
The Open Meeting Law requires public bodies to create and maintain accurate minutes of 
all meetings.  G.L. c. 30A, § 22(a).  The “minutes of an open session, if they exist and whether 
approved or in draft form, shall be made available upon request by any person within 10 days.”  
G.L. c. 30A, § 22(c).

On June 6, 2024, the complainant sent an email to Committee Chair Mimi Larrivee 
requesting “meeting minutes, both open and executive session for all meetings of the” 
Committee.  On June 19, 2024, having received no response from the Committee, the 
complainant filed the instant Open Meeting Law complaint regarding the Committee’s failure to 
respond to his request for meeting minutes.    
 
The Committee maintains that Ms. Larrivee did not receive the June 6, 2024, request, 
explaining that the School Department’s computer security system identified the complainant’s 
email as “spam” and deleted it.  The Committee provided a copy of the computer notification 
indicating that the complainant’s email was deleted.  We credit the Committee’s explanation that 
Ms. Larrivee did not receive the June 6, 2024, request for meeting minutes.
2  Therefore, we do 
not find a violation of the Open Meeting Law based on the allegation in the complaint.   
 
However, we note that the Committee learned of the request for minutes on July 23, 
2024, when our
 office provided Ms. Larrivee with a copy of the complaint.  We note our concern 
that the Committee did not respond to the request for minutes or the complaint until August 22, 
2024, well beyond the timeline required by the Open Meeting Law for both a response to 
meeting minutes and a response to a complaint.  See G.L. c. 30A, § 22(c) (requiring response to 
request for minutes within 10 days); G.L. c. 30A, § 23(b) (requiring response to complaint within 
14 business days); see also OML 2021-134 (meeting minutes must be made available within 10 
calendar days of request); OML 2013-120 (same).3  We remind the Committee of its obligations 
under the law.  Where the Committee has provided copies of minutes, we order no further 
remedial action. 
 
In his request for further review, the complainant suggests that minutes were approved by 
the S
chool Committee, not the Superintendent of Schools Search Committee, and were 
improperly redacted.  We generally decline to review an allegation that was not included within 
the original complaint to the public body because the public body has not had an opportunity to 
respond to it.  See G.L. c. 30A, § 23(b); 940 CMR 29.05(3).  We find that these allegations were 
not clearly raised in the original complaint and thus we do not review their merits.  Nonetheless,  
we note that the Open Meeting Law does not specify the manner in which minutes need to be 
approved and public bodies may choose the method for approval.  See OML 2023-4; OML 2020-
81; OML 2015-26; OML 2014-126.  We have stated that where a parent public body has 
sufficient connection to or control over a subordinate public body, such as a subcommittee or a 
preliminary screening committee, it is a permissible practice for one public body to approve the 
minutes of another public body.  See OML 2017-34.

publication under the Open Meeting Law.  See G.L. c. 30A, § 22(f).  However, when the purpose 
for the executive session has concluded, the Open Meeting Law no longer authorizes the
