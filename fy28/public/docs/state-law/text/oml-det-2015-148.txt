RE: Open Meeting Law Complaint 
Dear Mr. Butler: 
This office received a complaint from Ronald Beaty, dated September 4, alleging that the 
Barnstable School Committee (the "Committee") violated the Open Meeting Law, G.L. c. 30A, 
§§ 18-25.1 The complaint was originally filed with the Committee on July 26, and, after an 
extension of time was granted, the Committee responded by letter dated September 4. In his 
complaint, Mr. Beaty alleges that the Committee posted an insufficiently specific meeting notice. 
Following our review, we resolve this complaint by informal action in accordance with 
940 CMR 29.07(2)(a), and find that the Committee did not violate the Open Meeting Law. As 
we have noted in prior determinations resolving complaints filed by Mr. Beaty, a public body 
does not need to list "open session" in its meeting notice, unless an executive session is the only 
topic for discussion. See OML 2015-110; OML 2015-105.2 Here, the notice listed several other 
topics besides the anticipated executive session, thus it was clear that an open session meeting 
would occur. The Board, therefore, did not violate the Open Meeting Law by failing to list open 
session as a distinct topic. 
Next, the complaint alleges that an agenda item—an anticipated executive session "to 
discuss strategy regarding collective bargaining with the AFSCME contracts"—is insufficiently 
specific. While our office general discourages public bodies from using acronyms on meeting 
notices, see OML 2014-138, "AFSCME" is a widely understood abbreviation for the American 
Federation of State, County & Municipal Employees. It is therefore unlikely that the use of this 
acronym led to confusion among interested members of the public. The use of this abbreviation 
on the meeting notice did not violate the Open Meeting Law. See OML 2014-116.
