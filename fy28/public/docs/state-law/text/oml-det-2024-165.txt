FACTS 
 
We find the facts as follows.  On May 13, 2024, this office issued a letter of 
determination, OML 2024-104, in response to a complaint filed by Elvio Rodrigues, finding that 
the Board had violated the Open Meeting Law.  The Board posted notice of a meeting to go 
forward on May 21.  However, that meeting was cancelled.  The Board then posted notice of a 
meeting for May 28, which included as a topic of discussion “Correspondence.”  The letter of 
determination, OML 2024-104, which had found that the Board violated the Open Meeting Law, 
was not referenced on the notice.  The May 28 meeting went forward.  According to draft 
minutes produced by the Board, there was no discussion of OML 2024-104 at the May 28 
meeting.  When the Board reached the topic of correspondence, the Board voted unanimously to 
“accept and place on file correspondence received.”   
 
DISCUSSION 
 
The Open Meeting Law requires that, except in an emergency, “a public body shall post 
notic
e of every meeting at least 48 hours prior to such meeting, excluding Saturdays, Sundays 
and legal holidays.”  G.L. c. 30A, § 20(b).  The notice must be printed in a legible, easily 
understandable format and must contain the date, time and place of the meeting, as well as a 
listing of topics that the chair reasonably anticipates will be discussed.  Id.  The list of topics 
shall have “sufficient specificity to reasonably advise the public of the issues to be discussed at 
the meeting.”  940 CMR 29.03(l)(b).  When a public body does not actually engage in any 
deliberation on a particular topic, it cannot be found to be in violation of the Open Meeting Law 
with respect to the sufficiency of the meeting notice relative to that topic.  See OML 2023-131; 
OML 2016-76.   
 
The B
oard did not discuss at its May 28 meeting the letter of determination issued in the 
complainant’s favor.  Although some public bodies choose to discuss Open Meeting Law 
complaint determinations during their public meetings, there is nothing within the Open Meeting 
Law which requires them to do so.  It is not clear whether the correspondence that was 
“accept[ed] and place[d] on file” included OML 2024-104.  However, even if it did, the Board 
was not required to reference it in the notice for the May 28 meeting, as it was not discussed.

CONCLUSION 
 
For the reasons stated above, we find that the Board did not violate the Open Meeting 
Law.  We now consider the complaint addressed by this determination to be resolved.  This 
determination does not address any other complaints that may be pending with our office or the 
Board.  Please feel free to contact our office at (617) 963-2540 if you have any questions 
regarding this letter.
