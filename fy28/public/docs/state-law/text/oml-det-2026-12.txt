RE:  Open Meeting Law Complaint 
 
Dear Mr. Mendes, 
 
This office received a complaint from Patrick Higgins on September 18, 2025, alleging 
that the Somerset Planning Board (the “Board”) violated the Open Meeting Law, G.L. c. 30A, §§ 
18-25.  The complaint was originally filed with the Board on or about August 21, 2025, and you 
responded, on behalf of the Board, by email dated September 17, 2025.
1  The complaint alleges 
that the Board failed to timely approve minutes of a meeting held on May 13, 2025. 
 
We resolve this matter by informal action, in accordance with 940 CMR 29.07(2)(a), and 
find that the Board violated the Open Meeting Law in the way alleged.  In reaching this 
determination, we reviewed the original complaint, the Board’s response to the complaint, the 
complaint filed with our office requesting review, and the minutes of the Board meeting held on 
August 26, 2025. 
 
The Open Meeting Law requires that a public body “create and maintain accurate 
minutes of all meetings, including executive sessions, setting forth the date, time and place, the 
members present or absent, a summary of the discussions on each subject, a list of documents

and other exhibits used at the meeting, the decisions made and the actions taken at each meeting, 
including the record of all votes.”  G.L. c. 30A, § 22(a).  “Minutes of all open sessions shall be 
created and approved in a timely manner.”  G.L. c. 30A § 22(c).  “Timely manner” means 
“within the next three public body meetings or within 30 days, whichever is later, unless the 
public body can show good cause for further delay.”  940 CMR 29.11(2).  Whenever possible, 
we recommend that minutes of a meeting be approved at the following meeting.  See OML 
2023-33; OML 2018-67; OML 2017-133.2  
 
The Board acknowledges, and we agree, that it failed to timely approve the minutes of 
the May 13, 2025, meeting where the minutes were approved during the meeting held on August 
26, 2025, which was the fourth meeting thereafter.  As such, we find that the Board violated the 
Open Meeting Law by failing to approve these minutes in a timely manner.  See OML 2023-187; 
OML 2022-144. 
 
We note that at the time the complaint was filed with our office on September 18, 2025, 
the Board had approved the May 13, 2025, meeting minutes.  In instances where a public body 
acknowledges a violation and all appropriate corrective action has been taken, we urge 
complainants not to consume scarce state resources by requesting review where the transparency 
purpose of the complaint has already been addressed by the corrective action taken.  See OML 
2024-224; OML 2024-20; OML 2023-113; OML 2017-191; OML 2012-46.  Indeed, the Open 
Meeting Law complaint process is designed to allow public bodies to take corrective action and 
address concerns raised in a complaint without this office’s involvement.  See G.L. c. 30A, § 
23(b) (“At least 30 days prior to the filing of a complaint with the attorney general, the 
complainant shall file a written complaint with the public body, setting forth the circumstances 
which constitute the alleged violation and giving the body an opportunity to remedy the alleged 
violation.”). 
 
For the reasons stated above, we find that the Board violated the Open Meeting Law.  We 
order the Board’s immediate and future compliance with the law’s requirements, and we caution 
that similar future violations could be considered evidence of intent to violate the law.   
 
We now consider the complaint addressed by this determination to be resolved.  This 
determination does not address any other complaints that may be pending with our office or the 
Board.  Please feel free to contact our office at (617) 963-2540 if you have any questions 
regarding this letter.
