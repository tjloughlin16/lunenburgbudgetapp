Dear Attorney Blake: 
 
This office received a complaint from Lisa McLoughlin on April 12, 2021, alleging that 
the Northfield Planning Board (the “Board”) violated the Open Meeting Law, G.L. c. 30A, §§ 
18-25.1  The complaint was originally filed with the Board on or about March 9, and you 
responded, on behalf of the Board, by letter dated March 30, following an extension of time 
granted by this office.  The complaint alleges that the Board’s February 18 meeting minutes are 
insufficiently detailed.   
 
We resolve this matter by informal action, in accordance with 940 CMR 29.07(2)(a), 
after reviewing the original complaint, the Board’s response to the complaint, the complaint filed 
with our office requesting further review, and the notice and open session minutes of the Board’s 
February 18 meeting, as well as a video recording of that meeting.2  We find that the Board 
violated the Open Meeting Law in the way alleged.   
 
The Open Meeting Law requires that a public body “create and maintain accurate 
minutes of all meetings, including executive sessions, setting forth the date, time and place, the 
members present or absent, a summary of the discussions on each subject, a list of documents

and other exhibits used at the meeting, the decisions made and the actions taken at each meeting, 
including the record of all votes.”  G.L. c. 30A, § 22(a).  When reviewing minutes for 
compliance with the Open Meeting Law, we look for substantial compliance with the accuracy 
requirement.  See OML 2016-105; OML 2013-64.3  By substantial compliance, we mean that the 
minutes should contain enough detail and accuracy so that a member of the public who did not 
attend the meeting could read the minutes and have a clear understanding of what occurred.  See 
OML 2012-106.  While minutes must include a summary of the discussion on each topic, a 
transcript is not required, and the minutes do not need to include every remark or opinion 
presented.  See OML 2012-29; OML 2011-55.   
 
The complaint alleges that the February 18 meeting minutes failed to include an adequate 
summary of the discussions that took place during the meeting.  During the February 18 meeting, 
which lasted four hours, the Board held three public hearings for three different solar array 
projects on Pine Meadow Road in the Town of Northfield.  Although the minutes summarize 
each question that members of the Board or the public asked with respect to each project, the 
minutes do not include any summary of the answers that were provided.  Rather, the minutes 
simply state that a specific individual “responded.”  This statement appears in the minutes 42 
times.  The Open Meeting Law requires that meeting minutes summarize the discussions that 
were held.  See OML 2019-167; OML 2018-8.  We find that the Board violated the Open 
Meeting Law by failing to include a sufficient summary of the discussions that took place for 
each public hearing during the February 18 meeting.  We acknowledge that it is not necessary to 
record the comments of every speaker, and that it can be difficult to do so where there are many 
speakers whose remarks may overlap.  See OML 2014-108; OML 2013-47; OML 2012-29.  
Here, only one individual spoke at a time and the minutes sufficiently summarized each question 
posed to the applicants of the solar array projects.  However, by failing to include any of the 
responses to the questions, members of the public reading the minutes would not have a clear 
understanding of the discussions that occurred as to each project.  See OML 2016-105.   
 
 For the reasons stated above, we find that the Board violated the Open Meeting Law by 
failing to include sufficient detail in the minutes of its February 18 meeting.  We order the Board 
to revise those meeting minutes and to certify to this office within sixty (60) days of the date of 
this letter that it has done so.  See 940 C.M.R. 29.07(4).  We also order the Board’s immediate 
and future compliance with the law’s requirements, and we caution that similar future violations 
could be considered evidence of intent to violate the law.   
 
We now consider the complaint addressed by this determination to be resolved.  This 
determination does not address any other complaints that may be pending with the Board or with 
our office.  Please feel free to contact our office at (617) 963-2540 if you have any questions 
regarding this letter.
