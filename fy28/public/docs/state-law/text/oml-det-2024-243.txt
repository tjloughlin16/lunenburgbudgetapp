 RE: Open Meeting Law Complaint 
 
Dear Ms. Marchione: 
 
This office received a complaint from David Travis on August 8, 2024, alleging that the 
New Marlborough Highway Planning Working Group (“Group”) violated the Open Meeting 
Law, G.L. c. 30A, §§ 18-25.  The complaint was originally filed with the Group on June 14, 
2024, and you responded, on behalf of the Group, by letter dated July 8, 2024.  The complaint 
alleges that the Group failed to post minutes for eight meetings held between February 9, 2021, 
and September 27, 2023. 
 
Following our review, we find that the Group did not violate the Open Meeting Law in 
the way alleged.  In reaching this determination, we reviewed the original complaint, the 
Commission’s responses to the complaint, and the complaint filed with our office requesting 
further review. 
 
The Open Meeting Law requires that a public body “create and maintain accurate 
minutes of all meetings, including executive sessions, setting forth the date, time and place, the 
members present or absent, a summary of the discussions on each subject, a list of documents 
and other exhibits used at the meeting, the decisions made, and the actions taken at each meeting, 
including the record of all votes.”  G.L. c. 30A, § 22(a).  Minutes of all open sessions must be 
created and approved in a timely manner.  G.L. c. 30A, § 22(c).  “Timely manner” means 
“within the next three public body meetings or within 30 days, whichever is later, unless the

public body can show good cause for further delay.”  940 CMR 29.11.  Whenever possible, we 
recommend that minutes of a meeting be approved at the next meeting.  See OML 2024-114; 
OML 2018-67; OML 2017-133.1   
 
Here, the complaint states that no “minutes or any documentation have been posted from 
the” Group with respect to eight meetings held between February 9, 2021, and September 27, 
2023.
2  The Open Meeting Law does not require public bodies to “post” meeting minutes 
anywhere.  See OML 2023-179; OML 2015-173; OML 2013-117.  Rather, the Law requires that 
“[t]he minutes of an open session, if they exist and whether approved or in draft form, shall be 
made available upon request by any person within 10 days.”  G.L. c. 30A, § 22(c).3  Therefore, 
the Group did not violate the Open Meeting Law by failing to post its meeting minutes to a 
website. 
 
The complaint does not assert that minutes were requested, an
 d the Group failed to 
provide such minutes.  Thus, we do not review, and offer no opinion on, whether the Group may 
have violated the Open Meeting Law with respect to responding to a request for minutes.  
Nonetheless, we remind the Group that a request for meeting minutes that is directed to a public 
body will fall under the Open Meeting Law and the body must make minutes of an open session 
available within 10 days of a request, if they exist, whether approved or in draft form.  See G.L. 
c. 30A, § 22(c).  Directing a requester to a website where the requested minutes are posted and 
accessible complies with the requirement to make the minutes “available.”  See OML 2024-24; 
OML 2022-205.  We note that the Open Meeting Law does not govern a public body’s response 
to a request for records other than meeting minutes.  See G.L. c. 30A, §§ 22(c), (e); OML 2024-
146; OML 2023-145; OML Declination 8-19-13 (Southwick Board of Selectmen).  Therefore, 
failure to timely respond to requests for documents other than minutes concerns a potential 
violation of the Public Records Law, rather than the Open Meeting Law.
