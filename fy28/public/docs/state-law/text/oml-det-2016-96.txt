DISCUSSION 
In general, meetings of a public body must be noticed and open to the public. G.L. 
c. 3OA, § 20(a), (b). A meeting is "a deliberation by a public body with respect to any matter 
within the body's jurisdiction." G.L. c. 30A, § 18. A deliberation is "an oral or written 
communication through any medium, including electronic mail, between or among a quorum 
of a public body on any public business within its jurisdiction." Icl A "quorum" is, generally, 
a simple majority of the members of a public body. Id 
Public bodies have an obligation to "create and maintain accurate minutes of all 
meetings." G.L. c. 30A, § 22(a). These meeting minutes must be reviewed and approved in a 
timely manner. G.L. c. 3OA, § 22(c). Although the Open Meeting Law does not define 
"timely manner," we recommend that minutes of a meeting be approved at the next meeting, 
whenever possible. See OML 2014-15; OML 2012-91.3 
The complainant alleges that the Working Group has failed to comply with the Open 
Meeting Law and failed to create minutes of its meetings. We find that the Working Group 
met only twice, on November 14, 2012 and May 29, 2013, since our office assumed 
enforcement authority of the Open Meeting Law. Since those meetings, two members of the 
Working Group, on their own initiative, have continued to work on projects concerning the 
site. Because these two individuals constitute less than a quorum of the Working Group and 
do not otherwise constitute a subcommittee, we find that they were not a "public body" 
subject to the law and, therefore, did not violate it. See OML 2014 93. 
The Working Group itself did violate the law by failing to timely approve its meeting 
minutes. Although the Working Group created minutes of its meetings, the May 29, 2013 
minutes remain in draft form. Therefore, we find that the Working Group violated the Open 
Meeting Law by failing to approve these minutes in a timely manner. Because the Working 
Group has been disbanded, we order the Board, as the parent body who created the Working

CONCLUSION 
For the reasons stated above, we find that the Working Group violated the Open 
Meeting Law by failing to approve minutes in a timely manner. We order immediate and 
future compliance with the Open Meeting Law, and caution that future similar conduct may 
be considered evidence of intent to violate the Law.
