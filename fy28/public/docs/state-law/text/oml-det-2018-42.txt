RE: Open Meeting Law Complaint 
Dear Attorney Feodoroff: 
This office received a complaint from Stephen Morgan on December 13, alleging that 
the Ashland Board of Selectmen (the "Board") violated the Open Meeting Law, G.L. c. 3OA, 
§§ 18-25.1 The complaint was originally filed with the Board on or about December 4, and 
you responded to the complaint, on behalf of the Board, in a letter dated December 13. In his 
complaint, Mr. Morgan alleges that the Board's November 14 meeting minutes are 
incomplete and do not include a list of documents used during the meeting. 
Following our review, we resolve this complaint by informal action in accordance 
with 940 CMR 29.07(2)(a), and find that the Board did not violate the Open Meeting Law. 
The complaint alleges that the Board's November 14 meeting minutes are incomplete. 
However, at the time this complaint was filed, the minutes were in draft, rather than final, 
form.2 Under the Open Meeting Law, a public body is responsible for the sufficiency of final, 
approved minutes as it may revise draft minutes at any point prior to their approval to correct 
inaccuracies or add detail. See OML 2018-20.3 Accordingly, because the minutes were in 
draft form at the time of this complaint, we find that the Board did not violate the Open 
Meeting Law by creating inaccurate or insufficiently detailed minutes. See OML 2014-13; 
OML 2014-7. We also note that the Board did not actually discuss the topic alleged to have 
been omitted from the draft minutes. Thus, the complainant's request for our review of this 
complaint appears to have been unnecessary.
