This office received a complaint from Michael Goodwin, dated May 22, 2014, alleging 
that the Hopkinton Board of Selectmen (the "Board") violated the Open Meeting Law, G.L. c. 
30A, §§ 18-25. Mr. Goodwin originally filed his complaint with the Board on March 3, 2014, 
and the Board responded by letter dated March 26, 2014.' In his complaint, Mr. Goodwin 
alleges that the Board entered into executive session for an improper purpose during its February 
25, 2014 meeting. Additionally, the complaint alleges that the notice for the Board's February 
25, 2014 meeting lacked sufficient detail about a topic the chair reasonably anticipated 
discussing.2 
Following our review, we find that the Board did not violate the Open Meeting Law. In 
reaching this determination, we reviewed the March 3, 2014 complaint filed with the Board; the 
Board's March 26, 2014 response; and the May 22, 2014 complaint filed with our office. We 
also reviewed the notice and open and executive session minutes from the Board's February 25, 
2014 meeting. Finally, we reviewed the minutes of the Board's May 20, 2014 meeting and a 
May 22, 2014 email from Mr. Goodwin, addressed to our office.

FACTS 
On February 21. 2014, the Board posted notice of its February 25, 2014 meeting. The 
meeting notice listed five purposes that the Board intended to invoke in order to enter into 
executive session. One of these purposes was listed as, "to consider or [interview] applicants for 
employment or appointment as part of a preliminary screening process, because an open meeting 
may have a detrimental effect in obtaining qualified applicants." The notice also included the 
following topic under Town Manager's Report: "a. Finance Director staff hire." 
The Board held its meeting on February 25, 2014. After the chair called the meeting to 
order, the chair motioned to go into executive session pursuant to G.L. c. 20A, §§ 21(a)(3), 
21(a)(4), 21(a)(6), and 21(a)(8) (Purposes 3, 4, 6 and 8). The Board then voted to enter into 
executive session. In its response to the complaint, the Board confirms that it did not discuss any 
hiring matters while in executive session. Following its executive session discussion, the Board 
exited executive session and reconvened in open session. Once in open session, the Town 
Manager presented his report, during which he stated that the Town was advertising a Finance 
Director position and sought to complete the hiring process by the second week in April. 
DISCUSSION 
A public body may enter into executive session for any of ten enumerated purposes. G.L. 
c. 30A, § 21(a). Under Purpose 8, a Board may enter into executive session "to consider or 
interview applicants for employment or appointment by a preliminary screening committee if the 
chair declares that an open meeting will have a detrimental effect in obtaining qualified 
applicants." G.L. c. 30A, § 21(a)(8). A "preliminary screening" is defined as "the initial stage 
of screening applicants conducted by a committee or subcommittee of a public body solely for 
the purpose of providing to the public body a list of those applicants qualified for further 
consideration or interview." G.L. c. 30A § 18. 
The notice and minutes from the Board's February 25, 2014 meeting indicate that the 
Board intended to enter into executive session under Purposes 3, 4, 6 and 8. Because a 
preliminary screening committee must be a committee of a public body, rather than the public 
body itself, it would not have been appropriate for the Board to discuss matters in executive 
session pursuant to Purpose 8. See OML 2013-155. However, the Board did not actually 
discuss any topics related to a hiring process during its February 5, 2014 executive session. We 
therefore find that the Board did not violate the Open Meeting Law.
