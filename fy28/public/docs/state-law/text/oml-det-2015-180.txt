This office received a complaint from Ronald Beaty, dated July 29, alleging that the 
Falmouth Board of Selectmen (the "Board") violated the Open Meeting Law, G.L. c. 30A, §§ 
18-25.1 Specifically, the complaint alleges that the Board failed to give notice that it would 
convene in open session prior to its June 29 executive session meeting and that the notice for the 
June 29 meeting was not sufficiently specific. The complaint was originally filed with the Board 
on July 8. The Board responded by letter dated July 23. 
Following our review, we find that the Board did not violate the Open Meeting Law. We 
note that the allegations raised by this complaint are substantially similar to a complaint we 
resolved in OML 2015-110, concerning this same body.2 The present complaint first alleges that 
the Board failed to list "open session" prior to executive session in the notice for its June 29 
meeting. This was addressed in OML 2015-110, where we wrote that where additional open 
session topics are included in a meeting notice, we have not required a public body list "open 
session" as a distinct topic. See OML 2015-105; OML 2013-155, FN 5; OML 2013-141, FN 7. 
The format of the notice at issue here is substantially similar to that reviewed in OML 2015-110, 
thus, for the same reason, we find that the Board likewise did not violate the Open Meeting Law 
by omitting "Open Session" from this notice. See id. 
The complaint also alleges that the executive session topic in the notice for the Board's 
June 29 meeting was insufficiently detailed. One of the executive session subtopics, concerning 
wind turbine litigation, was also considered in OML 2015-110, where we found the topic to be 
sufficiently detailed. For the reason discussed in that decision, we reach the same conclusion

here. The additional executive session subtopics in the June 29 meeting notice, listed under the 
general heading "G.L. c. 30A, s.21(a)(3)," were: "Labor Settlement- Pending Arbitration Case; 
Labor Settlement - AFSCME Unit B; Workers Compensation Settlement." 
A public body may enter into executive session for any of ten enumerated purposes. G.L. 
c. 30A, § 21(a). Before entering into executive session, the chair must state the purpose for the 
executive session, stating all subjects that may be revealed without compromising the purpose 
for which the executive session was called. G.L. c. 30A, § 21(b)(3). In general, this level of 
detail must also be listed on a meeting notice. See G.L. c. 30A, § 20(b); OML 2011-56; OML 
2011-15. One purpose for executive session is for public bodies to discuss, "strategy with 
respect to collective bargaining or litigation if an open meeting may have a detrimental effect on 
the bargaining or litigating position of the public body and the chair so declares." G.L. c. 30A, § 
21(a)(3) ("Purpose 3"). When entering executive session pursuant to Purpose 3, a public body 
must identify the litigation matter or bargaining unit that it intends to discuss, if doing so will not 
compromise the executive session purpose. See G.L. c. 30A, § 21(b)(3); OML 2014-145; OML 
2013-91. While we generally defer to a public body's assessment of whether the inclusion of 
such information would compromise the puipose for an executive session, a public body must be 
able to demonstrate a reasonable basis for that claim if challenged. See OML 2015-14. 
Here, the notice for the June 29 executive session clearly identified both the statutory 
purpose, "G.L. c. 30A, s.21(a)(3)," and the subjects of the discussion. The first subject involved 
a grievance matter that was headed to arbitration, and the Board's consideration of a settlement. 
The Board chose not to identify the relevant employee's name out of concern for the privacy of 
other employees involved in the situation that gave rise to the grievance. The second subtopic 
involved a discussion of settlement with AFSCME Unit B, the collective barging unit of the 
American Federation of State, County, and Municipal Employees. For that subject, the Board 
chose to identify the unit with which it was bargaining. The third subtopic concerned a 
compensation settlement with a worker who suffered a serious injury. The Board chose not to 
identify the worker out of respect for the worker's privacy following the injury. We find that the 
Board's decisions regarding the level of information to include in the executive topic were 
reasonable, and therefore find that the executive session topic was sufficiently detailed to inform 
members of the public of the subjects to be discussed the June 29 meeting. See G.L. c. 30A, §§ 
20(b), 21(b)(3). 
We now consider this matter closed. Please be advised that this letter does not resolve 
any other complaints that may be pending with this office or with the Board. Please feel free to 
contact our office at 617-963-2540 if you have any questions regarding this letter.
