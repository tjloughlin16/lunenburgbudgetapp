RE: Open Meeting Law Complaint
Dear Mr. Saulino:
On August 261 this office received a complaint filed by Collin Dias, alleging that the Fall 
River Licensing Board (the “Board”) violated the Open Meeting Law, G.L. c. 30A, §§ 18-25.
The complaint was originally filed with the Board on July 26. The Board did not respond.
We resolve this complaint by informal action in accordance with 940 CMR 29.07(2)(a). 
Mr. Dias alleges that the Board failed to timely approve minutes for its April 17 and May 15 
meetings. After review of the Board’s July 9 meeting minutes and the Board’s meeting notices 
posted to the City’s website, we find that the Board violated the Open Meeting Law with regard 
to approval of its April 17 meeting minutes only.
The Open Meeting Law requires that a public body review and approve its meeting 
minutes in a timely manner. G.L. c. 30A, § 22(c); 940 CMR 29.11 (2). The Attorney General's 
Open Meeting Law regulations provide that “timely manner” means “within the next three public 
body meetings or within 30 days, whichever is later, unless the public body can show good cause 
for further delay.” 940 CMR 29.11 (2). Whenever possible, we recommend that minutes of a 
meeting be approved at the next meeting. See OML 2019-20.2 The Board held meetings on the 
following dates: April 17, May 15, June 11, June 19, and July 9. At its July 9 meeting, the Board 
approved the minutes of its April 17 and May 15 meetings. The April 17 meeting minutes were 
not reviewed and approved until the fourth meeting and 83 days after the April 17 meeting, and 
were therefore not timely approved. However, the Board timely approved its May 15 meeting 
minutes at the July 9 meeting, the third meeting following its May 15 meeting. Therefore, we 
find that the Board violated the Open Meeting Law by failing to timely approve the minutes of

its April 17 meeting only. We order immediate and future compliance with the Open Meeting 
Law, and caution that similar violations in the future may be considered evidence of intent to 
violate the Law. 940 CMR 29.02.
Finally, we independently note that the Board did not respond to the current complaint as 
required by the Open Meeting Law. After receiving an Open Meeting Law complaint, a public 
body must, within 14 business days, meet to review the complaint's allegations; take remedial 
action, if appropriate; and send a response to the complainant. See with 940 CMR 29.05(5). The 
public body must also provide the Attorney General with a copy of the complaint, the response, 
and a description of any remedial action taken. Id; G.L. c. 30A, § 23(b). We remind the Board of 
its obligation to comply with all provisions of the Open Meeting Law, including those related to 
responding to Open Meeting Law complaints.
We now consider the complaint addressed by this determination to be resolved. This 
determination does not address any other complaints that may be pending with our office or the 
Board. Please feel free to contact the Division at (617) 963-2540 if you have any questions.
