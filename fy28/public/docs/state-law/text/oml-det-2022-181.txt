This office received a complaint from Katherine Connolly on August 23, 2022, alleging 
that the Holbrook Housing Authority (the “Authority”) violated the Open Meeting Law, G.L. c. 
30A, §§ 18-25.
1  The complaint was originally filed with the Authority on April 9.  Although the 
Authority initially requested an extension of time to respond to the complaint on May 2, and this 
office granted an extension until May 16, the Authority did not respond to the complaint.  The 
complaint alleges that the Authority held an improper emergency meeting on March 29.  
 
We resolve this matter by informal action, in ac
 cordance with 940 CMR 29.07(2)(a), 
after reviewing the original complaint, the complaint filed with our office requesting further 
review, and the notice of the Authority meeting held on March 29.  We find that the Authority 
violated the Open Meeting Law in the way alleged.   
 
On M
arch 29 at 8:29 A.M., the Authority posted notice for a meeting to be held later that 
same day at 4:00 P.M.  The notice listed eight topics, which included “DHCH Contract of 
Financial Assistance,” “Certificate of Substantial Completion Parking and Paving Project.” 
“Section 8 Payment Standards,” “Section 8 Dedham Housing Authority Consolidation,” and 
“Department of Labor Standards Minimum Base Rate.”

The Open Meeting Law requires that, except in an emergency, “a public body shall post 
notice of every meeting at least 48 hours prior to such meeting, excluding Saturdays, Sundays 
and legal holidays.”  G.L. c. 30A, § 20(b).  In an emergency, a public body shall post notice as 
soon as reasonably possible prior to such meeting.  G.L. c. 30A, § 20(b).  An “emergency” is 
defined as “a sudden, generally unexpected occurrence or set of circumstances demanding 
immediate action.”  G.L. c. 30A, § 18.  The burden of justifying the need for an emergency 
meeting lies with the public body.  See OML 2012-7; OML 2010-6.
2   
 
Despite numerous requests from our office, we did not receive any response from the 
Authority.3  The Open Meeting Law requires that within 14 business days of receipt of a 
complaint, the public body must meet to review the complaint’s allegations; take remedial 
action, if appropriate; send to the complainant a response and a description of any remedial 
action taken; and send to the Attorney General a copy of the complaint and a description of any 
remedial action taken.  G.L. c. 30A, § 23(b); 940 CMR 29.05(5).  Moreover, the Open Meeting 
Law requires that, “[u]pon notification of an investigation, any person, public body or any other 
... official or entity who is the subject of an investigation, shall make all information necessary to 
conduct such investigation available to the attorney general.”  G.L. c. 30A, § 24(a).  We caution 
the Authority that we may find a violation of the Open Meeting Law for a future failure to 
respond to our request for information and/or materials in the course of an investigation.  See 
OML 2016-127. 
 
Where the Authority did not respond to the complaint and did not deny any of the 
alleg
ations raised in the complaint, we find that it failed to justify the need for the emergency 
meeting held on March 29.  As such, we find that there was no valid emergency basis for the 
March 29 meeting and therefore the Authority violated the Open Meeting Law by convening this 
meeting without posting notice 48 hours in advance of the meeting.  See OML 2021-9; OML 
2014-150.  
 
For t
he reasons stated above, we find that the Authority violated the Open Meeting Law. 
We order the Authority’s immediate and future compliance with the Open Meeting Law, and we 
caution that similar future violations may be considered evidence of intent to violate the law.  In     
addition, we order all members of the Authority to attend an Open Meeting Law webinar training 
provided by our office within 90 days of the Authority’s receipt of this letter.  Finally, we order 
the Authority to release the minutes of the March 29 meeting to the complainant, if it has not 
already done so.
