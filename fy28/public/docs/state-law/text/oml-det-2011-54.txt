Dear Attorney McCarron: 
Our office has reviewed a complaint filed by Mr. Kevin Bowe, dated July 18, 2011, 
alleging violations of the Open Meeting Law, G.L. c. 30A, §§ 18-25, by the West Newbury 
Board of Selectmen (the "Board"). The complaint alleges that at the Board's May 18, 2011 
meeting, the Board "cited — and the agenda stated — 'legal matters' as the sole reason to meet in 
executive session...nowhere does the [Open Meeting Law] mention 'legal matters' as a 
purpose." The complaint was originally filed with the Board on June 14, 2011. We received the 
Board's response to that complaint dated June 30, 2011. 
We find that the Board did not violate the Open Meeting Law by meeting in executive 
session on May 18, 2011, though the Board failed to provide sufficient detail of its reasons for 
entering executive session. In making our determination, we reviewed the June 14, 2011 and 
July 18, 2011 complaints and the Board's June 30, 2011 response. We also reviewed the notice 
and the open and executive session minutes for the Board's May 18, 2011 meeting. Finally, we 
reviewed a letter from you as Town Counsel dated August 11, 2011, which provided background 
information on the May 18, 2011 executive session. 
FACTS 
The West Newbury Board of Selectmen posted a notice on Monday, May 16, 2011 at 
2:51 PM for a 7:00 PM meeting scheduled for Wednesday, May 18, 2011. One topic for 
discussion that was listed in the notice read "Executive Session, MGL Chapter 30A §21(a): 
Executive Session minutes of May 4, 2011, Legal Matter." At the May 18, 2011 meeting, the 
Board entered executive session at 7:50 PM, and adjourned at 8:30 PM. According to the 
Board's June 30, 2011 response to the complaint, the Board entered executive session to "receive 
and discuss written communications from Town Counsel relating to legal matters affecting the 
===PAGE 2===
Town." 
We reviewed the executive session minutes from the Board's May 18, 2011 meeting. 
Because the purpose for the executive session has not yet expired, we do not include the specific 
content of those minutes here, though we note that the discussion covered topics related to 
litigation and confidential documents. 
DISCUSSION 
The Open Meeting Law was enacted "to eliminate much of the secrecy surrounding 
deliberations and decisions on which public policy is based." Ghiglione v. School Committee of 
Southbridge, 376 Mass. 70, 72 (1978). The law requires that meetings of a public body be 
properly noticed and open to members of the public, unless an executive session is convened. 
See G.L. c. 30A, §§ 20(a)—(b), 21. Notice of every meeting must be posted "at least 48 hours 
prior to such meeting, excluding Saturdays, Sundays and legal holidays," and every notice shall 
include "the date, time and place of such meeting and a listing of topics that the chair reasonably 
anticipates will be discussed at the meeting." G.L. c. 30A, § 20(b). 
A public body may enter executive session for one of ten enumerated purposes, provided 
that it has first convened in an open session, that a majority of members of the body have voted 
to go into executive session and the vote of each member is recorded by roll call and entered into 
the minutes. G.L. c. 30A, §§ 21(a)—(b). Before entering the executive session, the chair must 
state the purpose for the executive session, stating all subjects that may be revealed without 
compromising the purpose for which the executive session was called. Id. Among the ten 
purposes are Purpose 3, "[t]o discuss strategy with respect to collective bargaining or litigation if 
an open meeting may have a detrimental effect on the bargaining or litigating position of the 
public body and the chair so declares"; and Purpose 7, "[t]o comply with, or act under the 
authority of, any general or special law or federal grant-in-aid requirements." G.L. c. 30A, §§ 
21(a)(7), (8). 
After reviewing the executive session minutes of the Board's May 18, 2011 meeting, we 
find that the Board's discussion covered topics that were appropriate under executive session 
Purposes 3 and 7. The Board discussed strategies with respect to imminently threatened or 
pending litigation after reviewing a communication from counsel. This was appropriate under 
Purpose 3. The Board also discussed documents protected from disclosure under Massachusetts 
General Law. This was appropriate for executive session under Purpose 7. 
Although the Board had appropriate reasons to enter executive session, the Board failed 
to provide sufficient notice to the public about its reasons for entering executive session. As the 
complainant points out, there is no executive session purpose for "legal matter[s]." The Board 
should have stated in its notice "G.L. c. 30A, §21(a)(3) — to discuss strategy with respect to 
litigation", even if unable to name the specific litigation because of its early stages. 
Additionally, the chair of the Board should have publicly stated prior to entering executive 
session that "an open meeting may have a detrimental effect on the litigating position of the 
Board" as required by Purpose 3. The Board should have also listed "G.L. c. 30A, §21(a)(7) — to 
comply with, or act under the authority of, a Massachusetts General Law by reviewing

documents protected from disclosure under Massachusetts General Law." The Board could have 
made a determination whether listing the specific Massachusetts General Law would have 
compromised the purpose for entering executive session. 
Finally, we note the requirement that minutes shall include "a list of documents and other 
exhibits used at the meeting." G.L. c. 30A, § 22(a). The executive session minutes we reviewed 
did not include a list of any documents or exhibits used by the Board, though the Board clearly 
discussed specific documents, including "written communications from Town Counsel relating 
to legal matters affecting the Town," as the Board described in its June 30, 2011 response to the 
complaint. 
CONCLUSION 
We find that the Board did not violate the Open Meeting Law by meeting in executive 
session on May 18, 2011, though the Board failed to provide sufficient detail about its reasons 
for entering executive session. We order immediate and future compliance with the 
requirements of the Open Meeting Law, and further order the Board to attach "a list of 
documents and other exhibits used" at the Board's May 18, 2011 meeting to the final, approved 
executive session minutes once they are released to the public. 
We now consider this complaint resolved and this matter closed. Please contact me if 
you have any questions or believe any of the facts presented to be inaccurate.
