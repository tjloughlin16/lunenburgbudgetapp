Dear Chair Norton: 
This office received a complaint from Brian Fruzzetti, dated December 21, 2015, alleging 
that the Hanson Capital Improvement Committee (the "Committee") violated the Open Meeting 
Law, G.L, c. 30A, §§ 18-25. The complaint was originally filed with the Committee on 
November 13, 2015, and the Committee responded by letter dated December 3, 2015. In his 
complaint, Mr. Fruzzetti alleges that the Committee failed to maintain meeting minutes. 
We appreciate the patience of the parties while we reviewed this matter. Following our 
review, we find that the Committee violated the Open Meeting Law as alleged in the complaint. 
In reaching a determination, we reviewed the original complaint, the Committee's response, and 
the request for further review filed with our office. We spoke with Committee Chair John 
Norton by telephone on March 8 and 15, 2016.1 Finally, we spoke with the Hanson Town Clerk 
by telephone on June 24, 2016. 
FACTS 
We find the facts as follows. The Committee is a six-member public body. On October 
19, 2015, the complainant requested from the Hanson Town Clerk copies of all minutes of 
Committee meetings from 2011 to the date of the request. This request also included, under the 
Public Records Law, copies of meeting notices.2 On this same date, the Town Clerk provided 
the complainant with minutes of the following meetings: January 10 and 31, 2011; January 23, 
February 6, and February 13, 2012; July 28, August 19, September 15, and December 15, 2014; 
February 23 and August 31, 2015. No minutes were provided for 2013. The Town Clerk also

DISCUSSION 
Under the Open Meeting Law, a public body must create and maintain accurate minutes 
of all meetings, including executive sessions, setting forth the date, time and place, the members 
present or absent, a summary of the discussions on each subject, a list of documents and other 
exhibits used at the meeting, the decisions made and the actions taken at each meeting, including 
the record of all votes. G.L. c. 30A, §22(a). Minutes of all meetings must be created and 
approved in a timely manner. G.L. c. 30A, § 22(c). If requested, minutes of open session 
meeting must be made available within ten days. Id. 
Here, as the complaint alleges, the Committee has failed to maintain minutes for many of 
its meetings. Indeed, the Committee concedes that there are many meeting dates for which 
minutes do not exist. For example, no minutes exist for any Committee meetings that took place 
during calendar year 2013.5 We credit the Committee's statement that these minutes were in the 
custody of an individual, who, unbeknownst to the Committee, discarded them. However, the 
responsibility for complying with the Open Meeting Law rests with public bodies and members 
of such bodies, not with individual public employees such as clerks or other administrative 
assistants. See OML 2012-79. The Committee is therefore responsible for ensuring compliance 
with the law's requirements. We find that the Committee violated the Open Meeting Law by 
failing to maintain its meeting minutes. See OML 2012-30. 
CONCLUSION 
For the reasons stated above, we find that the Committee violated the Open Meeting Law. 
We order the Committee's immediate and future compliance with the Open Meeting Law, and 
we caution that similar future violations may be considered evidence of intent to violate the law. 
We further order the Committee to re-create minutes for all meetings, using any materials 
available, including personal notes or recordings. These minutes shall be re-created shall take 
place no later than sixty (60) days from receipt of this letter. Finally, we order the Committee to 
review the Attorney General's Open Meeting Law Guide.6 All Committee members must certify
