 RE: Open Meeting Law Complaint 
Dear Attorney Riley: 
This office received a complaint from Jill Normandin on February 21, 2021, alleging that 
the Westminster Planning Board (the “Board”) violated the Open Meeting Law, G.L. c. 30A, §§ 
18-25. The complaint was originally filed with the Board on January 22, 2021, and you 
responded on behalf of the Board by letter dated February 10, 2021. In her complaint, Ms. 
Normandin alleges that the Board’s meeting minutes and meeting notice for a December 8, 2020, 
meeting were insufficient detailed.1  
Following our review, we find that the Board violated the Open Meeting Law by 
approving meeting minutes for the December 8 meeting which were insufficiently detailed. 
However, we find that the notice for the December 8 meeting was sufficiently specific. In 
reaching this determination, we reviewed the original complaint, the Board’s response to the 
complaint, and the complainant’s request for further review. We also reviewed the meeting 
notice, meeting minutes and a recording of the Board’s December 8 meeting.2 We emailed with 
the Town Planner and Town Counsel on several occasions in September 2021 to request further 
information. Finally, we spoke with Ms. Normandin by phone on August 5, 2021 and emailed 
with her on several occasions in August and September of 2021.

FACTS 
On December 2 around 9:38 a.m., the Board posted a meeting notice for a meeting to be 
held at 7:00 p.m. on December 8. Under the heading “other business,” the notice contained the 
topic, “Planning Board discussion on possible zoning amendments for 2021 Annual Town 
Meeting in May.”  
The recording of the December 8 meeting demonstrates that the Town Planner presented 
to the Board a summary of his research about driveway by-laws in light of a recent court case 
which had raised the issue, although he noted that such issues were rare. Town Counsel had 
recommended a clarification be made to the earth removal by-law to specify that residential 
driveways were exempt from the current by-law. The Town Planner commented that offering 
any changes to the by-laws would provide an opportunity for opponents to argue against the 
proposed changes at Town Meeting. One member stated that he would prefer not to make any 
changes if it would create an opportunity for opponents to discuss the earth removal by-law at 
Town Meeting. The Board agreed that it was uncommon for contractors to improperly install 
driveways under the Town’s current by-laws, and felt that Town Counsel’s clarification was not 
needed. At the end of the discussion, which lasted approximately six minutes, the Board voted 
unanimously not to propose any changes to the zoning by-laws.  
DISCUSSION 
1. The Board approved meeting minutes for the December 8 meeting which were 
insufficiently detailed.  
 The Open Meeting Law requires that a public body create and maintain accurate minutes 
of all meetings “setting forth the date, time and place, the members present or absent, a summary 
of the discussions on each subject, a list of documents and other exhibits used at the meeting, the 
decisions made and the actions taken at each meeting, including the record of all votes.” G.L. c. 
30A, § 22(a). When reviewing minutes for compliance with the Open Meeting Law, we look for 
substantial compliance with the accuracy requirement. See OML 2017-104; OML 2013-64.3 By 
substantial compliance, we mean that the minutes should contain enough detail and accuracy so 
that a member of the public who did not attend the meeting could read the minutes and have a 
clear understanding of what occurred. See OML 2019-145; OML 2012-106. Although a 
transcript of the discussions is not required, and the minutes do not need to include every remark 
or opinion presented, minutes must include a substantive summary of the discussion on each 
topic. See OML 2018-8; OML 2012-29; OML 2011-55. 
 The Board approved meeting minutes for the Board’s December 8 meeting which are 
insufficiently detailed. Under “Planning Board discussion on possible zoning amendments for 
2021 Annual Town Meeting in May,” the meeting minutes merely state that “D. Bartkus made a 
motion to leave the bylaws as is with no additional amendments for the May 2021 Annual Town 
Meeting, M. Smith seconded, all in favor, motion carried (4-0).” During the meeting, the Town 
Planner shared with the Board his research about possible changes to the Town zoning by-laws 
regarding driveways, recommending that no change was needed. The Board then discussed

whether to propose a change to the by-laws at the upcoming Town Meeting regarding driveways, 
eventually agreeing not to propose any changes. Because the meeting minutes do not capture the 
Town Planner’s presentation, nor any of the Board’s discussion leading up to the vote, we find 
that the minutes of the December 8 meeting are insufficiently detailed. We therefore find that the 
Board violated the Open Meeting Law by approving insufficiently detailed minutes for the 
December 8, 2020, meeting. However, we find that the minutes for the December 8, 2020, 
meeting were otherwise sufficiently detailed.  
 Next, we must determine whether the Board’s violation was, as the complainant urges, 
intentional. See G.L. c. 30A, § 23(c). An intentional violation is an “act or omission by a public 
body or a member thereof, in knowing violation of [the Open Meeting Law].” 940 CMR 29.02. 
An intentional violation may be found where the public body acted with deliberate ignorance of 
the Law’s requirement or has previously been advised that certain conduct violates the Open 
Meeting Law. Id.  
 In a prior determination which was issued on December 4, 2020, we found that the Board 
violated the Open Meeting Law by failing to include in the minutes for its December 10, 2019, 
meeting a list of the documents used at that meeting. See OML 2020-155. Although that 
determination also related to the adequacy of meeting minutes, we found that the Board had 
summarized the Board’s discussion during the December 10, 2019, meeting with sufficient 
detail. We find no other evidence of an intentional violation. Therefore, as the violation that we 
found in OML 2020-155 related to a different issue, we decline to find an intentional violation in 
this case. 
2. The notice for the Board’s December 8 meeting was sufficiently specific. 
 A public body must post notice of every meeting at least 48 hours in advance, not 
including Saturdays, Sundays, and legal holidays. G.L. c. 30A, § 20(b). The notice must be 
printed in a legible, easily understandable format and must contain the date, time, and place of 
the meeting, as well as a listing of topics that the chair reasonably anticipates will be discussed. 
G.L. c. 30A, § 20(b). The list of topics shall have “sufficient specificity to reasonably advise the 
public of the issues to be discussed at the meeting.” 940 CMR 29.03(l)(b). We generally consider 
a topic to be sufficiently specific when a reasonable member of the public could read the topic 
and understand the anticipated nature of the public body’s discussion. See OML 2015-35. Where 
a public body anticipates discussing specific Town Meeting warrant articles, it must specifically 
list them in its meeting notice. See OML 2020-17; OML 2011-7. However, where a public body 
is reviewing all articles for a Town Meeting warrant, it is not required to list all of 
the warrant articles on its meeting notice. See OML 2020-17; OML 2012-83. 
 The Board’s counsel explains that the Chair added the “possible zoning amendments” 
item as a placeholder on the notice, in case the Town Planner or a Board member wanted to 
discuss any particular aspect of the Town’s zoning by-law for amendment at the upcoming Town 
Meeting. Absent evidence to the contrary, we credit the public body’s account of the facts. See 
OML 2020-172; OML 2019-73; OML 2017-93. As the Board’s Chair did not anticipate 48 hours 
in advance of the meeting any specific topics that would be discussed under the “possible zoning 
amendments” item, we find that the notice included a listing of topics that the chair reasonably 
anticipated would be discussed. See OML 2020-44; OML 2019-121. Therefore, we find that the 
notice for the Board’s December 8 meeting was sufficiently detailed.

CONCLUSION 
 For the reasons stated above, we find that the Board violated the Open Meeting Law by 
approving meeting minutes for the December 8, 2020, meeting which were insufficiently 
detailed. We order the Board’s immediate and future compliance with the Open Meeting Law 
and caution the Board that a determination by our office of a similar violation in the future may 
be considered evidence of intent to violate the Open Meeting Law.   
 We also order the Board to amend the minutes of its December 8, 2020, meeting within 
30 days of receipt of this determination to include a summary of the discussion of possible 
zoning amendments that took place during the December 8 meeting.  
 We now consider the complaint addressed by this determination to be resolved. This 
determination does not address any other complaints that may be pending with our office or the 
Board. Please feel free to contact the Division at (617) 963 - 2540 if you have any questions.
