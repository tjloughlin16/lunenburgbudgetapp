RE: Open Meeting Law Complaint  
 
Dear Town Manager Duncan:  
 
This office received a complaint from Patrick Higgins on June 28, 20241 alleging the 
Foxborough Select Board (the “Board”) violated the Open Meeting Law, G.L. c. 30A, §§ 18-25.1 
The complaint was originally filed with the Board on June 4, and you responded, on behalf of the 
Board, by letter dated June 24.  The complaint alleges that the notice of the Board’s May 21 
meeting was not sufficiently specific because it did not include votes to reconsider and rescind a 
prior vote taken at the Board’s April 9 meeting, that created the position of Director of 
Operations. 
  
Following our review, we find that the Board did not violate the Open Meeting Law as 
alleged.  In reaching this determination, we reviewed the original complaint, the Board’s 
response to the complaint, and the complainant’s request for further review.  We also reviewed 
the minutes of the April 9 meeting, and the notice and minutes of the May 21 meeting.  Finally, 
we viewed recordings of the April 9 and May 21 meetings.2

FACTS 
 
We find the facts as follows.  The B oard held a duly posted meeting on April 9.  At that 
meeting, Town Manager Paige Duncan addressed the Board relative to the FY 24 and FY 25 
Managerial Non-Union Pay Plan (“the Pay Plan”).3  She explained that approval of the Pay Plan 
would impact some positions and would also create a position of Director of Operations.  She 
pledged that the latter would not be a new, stand-alone position that would add to the budget.  
Instead, she planned to have an existing staff member take on the role of Director of Operations 
in addition to their current duties.  The Pay Plan was approved by roll call vote, 4-0. 
 
At town elections held on May 6, two Board members who had voted in favor of the Pay 
Plan lost their re-election bids.  The Board next met on May 7 and then on May 21.  The notice 
of the Board’s May 21 meeting included the topic “Discuss Director of Operations Role.”  The 
notice did not list an anticipated vote relative to the Director of Operations position or to the Pay 
Plan.  At the May 21 meeting, Chair Stephanie McGowan introduced the topic of the Director of 
Operations and then recognized one of the newly elected members, Bill Yukna, to speak to the 
subject.  Mr. Yukna moved to reconsider the vote of April 9 authorizing the creation of the 
position of Director of Operations as part of the Pay Plan.  He expressed concern that the 
Director of Operations over time would become an Assistant Town Manager.  The Board 
previously had denied the prior Town Manager’s request for an Assistant Town Manager.  Chair 
McGowan, who had not attended the April 9 meeting, echoed Mr. Yukna’s concerns.  The 
motion to reconsider was approved by a vote of 3-1, and was followed shortly by a motion to 
rescind the April 9 vote creating the position of Director of Operations.  That motion was 
approved by unanimous vote.   
 
DISCUSSION 
 
The Open Meeting Law was enacted “to eliminate much of the secrecy surrounding the 
deliberations and decisions on which public policy is based.”  Ghiglione v. Sch. Co mm. of 
Southbridge, 376 Mass. 70, 72 (1978).  A public body must post notice of every meeting at least 
48 hours in advance, not including Saturdays, Sundays, and legal holidays.  G.L. c. 30A, § 20(b). 
Notices must include the date, time and place of the meeting, as well as a “a listing of topics that 
the chair reasonably anticipates will be discussed at the meeting.”  Id.  The list of topics shall 
have sufficient specificity to reasonably advise the public of the issues to be discussed at the 
meeting.  940 CMR 29.03(l)(b).  We generally consider a topic to be sufficiently specific when a 
reasonable member of the public could read the topic and understand the anticipated nature of 
the public body’s discussion.  See OML 2022-98; OML 2019-134; OML 2012-34.4  A notice 
need not include every detail about an anticipated discussion; rather, topics for discussion must 
be listed on the notice with “sufficient specificity to reasonably advise the public of the issues to 
be discussed at the meeting,” 940 CMR 29.03(1)(b), so that the public can make an informed 
decision about whether to attend the meeting to observe an anticipated discussion.  See OML 
2023-86; OML 2019-95; OML 2015-74.

Here, the notice of the May 21 meeting was sufficiently specific.  Notice of the meeting 
included the topic “Discuss Director of Operations Role.”  Reading this notice, a reasonable 
member of the public would understand that the Board planned to discuss the position of 
Director of Operations.  Moreover, the Board was not required to specify on the meeting notice 
that it might vote to reconsider and rescind the April 9 vote, which had created the position of 
Director of Operations.  We have consistently explained that it is reasonably foreseeable that a 
public body may take action and hold a vote following discussion of a topic listed on a meeting 
notice; the Open Meeting Law does not require that the notice separately indicate the possibility 
of a vote.   See, e.g., OML 2024-156; OML 2023-186; OML 2021-153; OML 2019-157; OML 
2019-36; OML 2015-66; OML 2012-75.  In some cases, a vote is not known or reasonably 
anticipated prior to the meeting discussion.  See, e.g., OML 2023-145 (course of discussion 
underscored that exact language of vote on mask policy was not reasonably anticipated in 
advance of meeting).  We acknowledge that in this instance, Chair McGowan appeared to 
anticipate the motion.  Nonetheless, although it is good practice for a notice to specify that a vote 
will be taken when a vote is indeed anticipated in advance, we have consistently explained that 
only the topic need be listed on the notice, not the course of action.  See OML 2019-36; OML 
2015-66.  We are satisfied that the topic as listed on the notice of the May 21 meeting was 
sufficient to reasonably advise the public of the issue to be discussed at the meeting, and that a 
vote or votes might follow.  Therefore, notice of the May 21 meeting was sufficiently specific. 
 
CONCLUSION 
  
For the reasons stated above, we find that the Board did not violate the Open Meeting 
Law. We now consider the complaint addressed by this determination to be resolved. This 
determination does not address any other complaints that may be pending with the Board or with 
our office. Please feel free to contact our office at (617) 963-2540 if you have any questions 
regarding this letter.
