RE: Open Meeting Law Complaint 
Dear Attorney Cipriano: 
This office received a complaint from Donald O'Neil, Esq., dated May 17, 2018, alleging 
that the Southborough Zoning Board of Appeals (the "Board") violated the Open Meeting Law, 
G.L. c. 30A, §§ 18-25. The complaint was originally filed with the Board on February 26, 2018, 
and the Board responded by letter dated April 5, 2018.1 In his complaint, Attorney O'Neil 
alleges that the Board failed to maintain and approve in a timely manner several sets of meeting 
minutes from 2014. 
Following our review, we find that the Board violated the Open Meeting Law as alleged 
in the complaint. In reaching a determination, we reviewed the original complaint, the Board's 
response to the complaint, and the request for further review. We also reviewed handwritten 
notes that were taken during Board meetings held in 2014 as well as the agendas for those 
meetings. Finally, on June 19, 2018 we spoke to the Board's legal counsel, who provided us 
with meeting notes and minutes.2 
FACTS 
We find the facts as follows. The Board held five meetings between February and July of 
2014.3 The Board maintained notes of these meetings, but due to changes in administrative 
personnel, many of those notes were never converted into draft minutes, and the Board never 
approved minutes for any of those meetings. On December 22, 2017, in response to a discovery 
request relating to ongoing litigation, the Board produced several boxes of documents to the

DISCUSSION 
The Open Meeting Law requires that a public body "create and maintain accurate 
minutes of all meetings, including executive sessions, setting forth the date, time and place, the 
members present or absent, a summary of the discussions on each subject, a list of documents 
and other exhibits used at the meeting, the decisions made and the actions taken at each meeting, 
including the record of all votes." G.L. c. 30A, § 22(a). The meeting minutes must be reviewed 
and approved in a timely manner. G.L. c. 30A, § 22(c). The Open Meeting Law does not define 
"timely manner." According to current Open Meeting Law regulations, effective October 6, 
2017, "timely manner" means "within the next three public body meetings or within 30 days, 
whichever is later, unless the public body can show good cause for further delay." 940 CMR 
29.11. Prior to the adoption of this regulation, our office had stated that, for a body that met 
regularly, a delay of two to three months in approving minutes did not comply with the law. See 
OML 2017-33; OML 2016-62; OML 2013-31. 
The complaint alleges that the Board failed to create and approve meeting minutes in a 
timely manner. In its response, the Board argued that, because the notes had previously been 
furnished to the complainant's client, the present complaint is untimely. See G.L. c. 3OA, 
§23(b); 940 CMR 29.05(4). The Open Meeting Law imposes a continuing obligation to maintain 
minutes of all meetings, see G.L. c. 30A, § 22(a); OML 2013-45, and the Board's timeliness 
argument is therefore unavailing. Approximately four years have passed since the meetings at 
issue occurred. In this time, the minutes of the first three meetings have been neither drafted nor 
approved. The handwritten notes which the Board has maintained are not up to the standard of 
draft minutes, insofar as they lack sufficient detail and clarity such that a member of the public 
who did not attend the meeting could read the minutes and have a clear understanding of what 
occurred. See OML 2016-156. We therefore find the Board violated the Open Meeting law by 
failing to create and maintain minutes of the February 2, March 26, and April 7 meetings. See 
id. In addition, the June 25 and July 15, 2014, draft minutes were never approved. No matter 
which regulatory standard we apply, the Board has failed to approve the minutes of all five 
meetings in a timely manner. See G.L. c. 3OA, § 23(b). This, too, violated the Open Meeting 
Law. 
CONCLUSION 
For the reasons stated above, we find that the Board violated the Open Meeting Law by 
failing to create minutes from meetings conducted on February 26, March 26, and April 1, 2014. 
In addition, we find that the Board violated the Open Meeting Law by failing to timely approve 
minutes of all the meetings at issue. We order the Board's immediate and future compliance with 
the law, and caution the Board that a future similar violation may be considered evidence of an 
intentional violation of the law. We also order that the Board, to the best of its ability and using 
the notes that it has provided us, create and approve minutes for the February 26, March 26, and
