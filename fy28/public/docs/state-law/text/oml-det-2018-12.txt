RE: Open Meeting Law Complaint 
Dear Mr. Gagner, Jr.: 
This office received a complaint from William Witaszek, dated December 8, alleging that 
the Warren Board of Selectmen (the "Board") violated the Open Meeting Law, G.L. c. 30A, §§ 
18-25.' The complaint was originally filed with the Board on October 17, and the Board 
responded by letter dated November 6. The complaint alleges that the Board did not allow 
members of the public to address the Board during a September 26 meeting. 
Following our review, we resolve this complaint by informal action in accordance with 
940 CMR 29.07(2)(a), and find that the Board did not violate the Open Meeting Law. In 
reaching this determination, we reviewed the complaint filed with the Board, the Board's 
response, and the complaint filed with our office. We spoke with Mr. Witaszek by telephone on 
January 8, 2018. 
The Open Meeting Law does not require that a public body allow public participation I a 
meeting. See OML 2014-23; OML 2012-23.2 The Open Meeting Law provides that, "[n]o 
person shall address a meeting of a public body without permission of the chair, and all persons 
shall, at the request of the chair, be silent." G.L. c. 30A, § 20(g). Thus, the Board was not 
required by the Open Meeting Law to permit members of the public to address the Board during 
its September 26 meeting. To the extent that the complaint alleges that the Board violated 
certain public hearing requirements, other statutes, or the Board's own guidelines, we decline to 
address those issues as the Division is tasked with enforcing the Open Meeting Law.
