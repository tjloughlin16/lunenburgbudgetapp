RE: Open Meeting Law Complaint 
Dear Mr. Rourke: 
Our office received a complaint from John Memory on September 22, 2010, alleging that 
a member of the Somerville Retirement Board (the "Board") violated the Open Meeting Law, 
G.L. c. 30A, §§ 18-25. Specifically, the complaint alleges that Board member Edward Bean 
improperly interfered with the posting of notice for an emergency meeting on June 30, 2010, and 
that the emergency meeting should have been allowed to take place. The complaint was 
originally filed with the Board on or about July 29, 2010, and the Board responded to the original 
complaint by letter dated September 15, 2010. We appreciate the patience of the parties as we 
reviewed this matter. 
Following our review, we find that Bean's actions did not violate the Open Meeting Law 
because no meeting occurred on June 30, 2010 that required notice, and because there was no 
lawful reason to hold an emergency meeting on that date. In reaching this determination, we 
reviewed the complaint filed on July 29, 2010; the Board's September 15, 2010 response; the 
Board's minutes for meetings held between January 21, 2010 and August 17, 2010; the notice for 
the Board's June 30, 2010 emergency meeting that was emailed to the Board on June 30, 2011; 
and two letters from Mayor Joseph A. Curtatone, dated June 30, 2011, regarding appointees to 
the Board. We also reviewed email exchanges between Modified Accelerated Cost Recovery 
System Legislative Chairman Ralph White and the Board, and we reviewed an email and a letter 
from City Solicitor Francis X. Wright, Jr. to Bean and to the Board, respectively. 
FACTS 
Based upon our review of the material listed above, the facts are as follows. The Board 
has five members, therefore three constitutes a quorum. The complainant was a member of the 
Board at the time of the alleged violation.

The Board is responsible for making determinations regarding Cost of Living 
Adjustments ("COLA") for Somerville's retirees and survivors. Between January 21, 2010 and 
June 14, 2010, the Board discussed the COLA for Fiscal Year 2011 ("FY11"). During its March 
18, 2010 meeting, the Board noted that it was required to provide 30 days' notice to the Mayor 
and Board of Aldermen before it could vote on the COLA for a given fiscal year. The Board 
planned to vote on the FY11 COLA during its April 15, 2010 meeting. However, the vote did 
not take place on that date because the Board had not provided the required 30 days notice to the 
Mayor and Board of Aldermen. The vote on the FY11 COLA was then placed on the agenda for 
the Board's June 14, 2010 meeting; however Bean requested that it be "tabled until July." The 
Board agreed, and scheduled a vote on the FY11 COLA for July. 
On June 29, 2010, the Board received an email from White stating that, to pass a COLA 
for FY11, the Board must take a vote by June 30, 2010. The email stated, in relevant part, "The 
Somerville Retirement Board has not taken a vote  on a July COLA, which effectively kills any 
opportunity for Somerville's retirees and survivors to receive a new COLA. PERAC's t  earlier 
ruling does not allow a July COLA, unless a vote was taken prior to that month." (emphasis in 
original) 
The complainant and two other Board members, Mary Phinney and Lawrence Murphy, 
subsequently decided that, in order to comply with the June 30, 2010 deadline, an emergency 
meeting was necessary. On June 30, 2010 at 11:19 a.m., Principal Clerk Marie Vetrano sent an 
email, as directed by the complainant, to all five Board members indicating that a meeting would 
take place at 3:30 p.m. that afternoon. The email stated, "Please be advised the Somerville 
Retirement Board has called an emergency Retirement Board Meeting today June 30, 2010 at 
3:30 PM, at which time the Board will discuss a Cost of Living Adjustment for eligible 
retirees/survivors." However, the complaint alleges that when Phinney attempted to post notice 
for the meeting in City Hall, Bean brought her into his office, yelled at her, and threatened that 
changes would be made to her office if she posted the notice. In addition, Board member 
Murphy received a letter that day from the Mayor notifying him that, effective at 1:00 p.m., he 
had been removed as a member of the Board. A separate letter from the Mayor was sent to the 
Board the same day, stating that Janice Delory had been appointed to replace Murphy on the 
Board. Later that day, the complainant and Phinney attempted to hold the emergency meeting at 
the proposed time, however none of the other Board members attended. 
Sometime after June 30, 2010, Board Chairman Rourke2  requested the City Solicitor's 
legal opinion as to the deadline for voting on an annual COLA. In a letter dated July 28, 2010, 
the City Solicitor stated that, "PERAC's position that the deadline for voting on a [COLA] for 
each fiscal year is June 30th  of the prior fiscal year is a reasonable interpretation of the statute." 
In his letter, Wright refers to G.L. c. 32, § 103(d), which provides that "[i]n any case where such 
revised retirement allowance, pension or annuity cannot be administratively determined in time 
for adjusting the monthly payment for July pursuant to section 13, such cost of living adjustment 
shall be added to the monthly retirement allowance, pension or annuity in August or September, 
as the case may be." (emphasis in original) The City Solicitor concludes that "this section 
implie[s] that the revised retirement allowance, pension or annuity must be voted upon before 
The Public Employee Retirement Administration Commission.

DISCUSSION 
At the time the alleged violation occurred, the revised Open Meeting Law, G.L. c. 30A, 
§§ 18-25, was not yet in effect. Therefore we apply the version of the Open Meeting Law in 
effect at the time of the alleged violation, G.L. c. 39, § 23B. Under the old Municipal Open 
Meeting Law, "meeting" was defined, in relevant part, as "any corporal convening and 
deliberation of a governmental body for which a quorum is required." G.L. c. 39, § 23A 
(repealed). The current version of the law also requires communication between or among a 
quorum in order for a meeting to occur. See G.L. c. 30A, § 18. Both versions of the law define 
"quorum" as a simple majority of the body, unless otherwise defined by certain legal sources. 
See id.; G.L. c. 39, § 23A (repealed). 
Although the complainant and fellow Board member Phinney attempted to meet on June 
30, 2010, the Board's remaining three members did not attend. Three members constitute a 
quorum of the Board, thus no meeting occurred on that date because a quorum was not present. 
We therefore find that Board member Bean did not violate the Open Meeting Law by preventing 
notice from being posted for this meeting, since no meeting was held on that date requiring 
notice. 
Furthermore, we find that it would have been improper for the Board to hold an 
emergency meeting on June 30, 2010. The old Municipal Open Meeting Law required that 
"[e]xcept in an emergency, a notice of every meeting of any governmental body shall be filed 
with the clerk of the city or town in which the body acts, and the notice or a copy thereof shall, at 
least forty-eight hours, including Saturdays but not Sundays and legal holidays, prior to such 
meeting, be publicly posted." G.L. c. 39, § 23B (repealed). The meaning of the term 
"emergency" did not change when the law was revised in 2009, and continues to be defined as "a 
sudden, generally unexpected occurrence or set of circumstances demanding immediate action." 
G.L. c. 30A, § 18. The burden of justifying the need for an emergency meeting falls on the

public body. See OML 2012-7; 2011-45; 2010-6. 4  If the event precipitating the need for a 
meeting was anticipated, it may not be considered an emergency as that term is defined in the 
statute. See OML 2011-45 ("Where a public body has advanced notice of circumstances that are 
likely to require deliberation, it is not an emergency. Emergency meetings are reserved for 
circumstances that are unanticipated and require an immediate response to, for example, protect 
public safety or health"). 
The complainant contends that an emergency existed because the Board realized on June 
29, 2010 that it had to take a vote by June 30, 2010 to pass a COLA for FY11. While some 
Board members may have believed that the circumstances demanded immediate action, there 
was no "sudden, generally unexpected occurrence or set of circumstances" that required an 
emergency meeting on that date. See G.L. c. 30A, § 18; G.L. c. 39, § 23A (repealed). The June 
30, 2010 deadline did not arise suddenly or unexpectedly. It appears that the deadline for voting 
on a COLA for each fiscal year may be inferred from G.L. c. 32, § 103(d). Moreover, the Board 
acknowledged in March 2010 that it understood it must provide 30 days notice to the Mayor and 
Board of Alderman before voting on the COLA for FY11. Accordingly, the Board had the 
necessary information to vote in a timely fashion well before the June 30, 2010 deadline. For 
these reasons, we fmd that the Board did not have a lawful reason to hold an emergency meeting 
on June 30, 2010. 
CONCLUSION 
Following our review, we find that Board member Bean's actions did not violate the 
Open Meeting Law because no meeting occurred on June 30, 2010 that required notice, and 
because there was no lawful reason to hold an emergency meeting on that date.
