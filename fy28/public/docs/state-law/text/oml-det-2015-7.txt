RE: Open Meeting Law Complaint 
Dear Ms. Bonner: 
This office received a complaint from Richard Tgettis on October 14, 2014, 
alleging that the Nahant Housing Authority Board (the Board) violated the Open Meeting 
Law, G.L. c. 30A, §§ 18-25. The complaint was originally filed with the Board on or 
about October 10, 2014, and you responded to the complaint by letter dated October 13, 
2014. In his complaint, Mr. Tgettis alleges that the Board failed to respond to his request 
for meeting minutes. 
Following our review, we find that the Board did not violate the Open Meeting 
Law. In reaching this determination, we reviewed the October 10, 2014 complaint; your 
October 13, 2014 response to the complaint;1 the October 14, 2014 complaint filed with 
our office requesting further review; Authority Executive Director Donna McDonald's 
November 1, 2014 letter to our office; and the December 2, 2014 and December 11, 2014 
letters from Mr. Tgettis to our office. We also reviewed the notice and open and executive 
session minutes from the Board's August 5, 2014 meeting. Finally, we spoke by telephone 
on December 8, 2014 with Chair Bonner and with Ms. McDonald. 
FACTS 
We find the facts as follows. On September 4, 2014 the complainant, who is an 
employee of the Authority, submitted a handwritten request to his supervisor, Ms. 
McDonald, requesting to "see the minutes and reports that were voted on last Tues. (sic)." 
According to the complainant, he was referring to a Board meeting held on Tuesday, 
September 2, 2014, during which the Board voted to approve and release the minutes from 
an executive session held on August 5, 2014. Ms. McDonald did not forward his request

DISCUSSION 
The Open Meeting Law requires that the "[mjinutes of all open sessions shall be 
created and approved in a timely manner. The minutes of an open session, if they exist and 
whether approved or in draft form, shall be made available upon request by any person 
within 10 days." G.L. c. 30A, § 22(c). Likewise, a public body must respond to a request 
for executive session minutes within 10 days. Id. at § 22(g)(2). If the public body has not 
yet reviewed the minutes to determine whether continued confidentiality is warranted, 
however, it must do so by its next meeting or within 30 days, whichever is sooner. Id-
It does not appear that the Board knew about the complainant's request for minutes 
until this complaint was filed. The complainant's request was submitted to his supervisor, 
Ms. McDonald, who is not a member of the Board and therefore not subject to the Open 
Meeting Law. Because the Board did not receive the complainant's request for minutes, 
we find that it did not violate the Law by not responding to it within the statutory time 
period. 
However, we take this opportunity to note that we have concerns about the Board's 
attentiveness to the Open Meeting Law complaint process. As we reminded the Board in a 
prior determination, OML 2014-55, the Open Meeting Law requires that within 14 
business days after receiving a complaint the public body shall review the complaint's 
allegations; take remedial action, if appropriate; and send to the Attorney General a copy 
of the complaint and a description of any remedial action taken. G.L. c. 30A, § 23(b); 940 
CMR 29.05(5). While a public body may authorize an individual such as the Chair or 
Town Counsel to respond on the public body's behalf, the Open Meeting Law and the 
Attorney General's regulations require that the entire public body first review the Open 
Meeting Law complaint. See G.L. c. 30A, § 23(b); 940 CMR 29.05(5). Because Chair 
Bonner sent a response to this complaint prior to a review by the full Board, the Board 
failed to follow the requisite steps for responding to this complaint. While we do not find 
a violation here, the Board is advised that failure to follow the complaint process may, in 
some circumstances, constitute a violation of the Law. 
CONCLUSION 
For the reasons stated above, we find that the Board did not violate the Open 
Meeting Law. We now consider the complaint addressed by this determination to be 
resolved. This determination does not address any other complaints that may be pending
