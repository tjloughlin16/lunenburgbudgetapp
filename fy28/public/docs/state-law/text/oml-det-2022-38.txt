 RE: Open Meeting Law Complaint 
Dear Mr. Snyder: 
This letter addresses two complaints from John Weingold, dated December 6 and 27, 
2021, alleging that the Monterey Lake Garfield Working Group (the “Group”) violated the Open 
Meeting Law, G.L. c. 30A, §§ 18-25. The complaints were filed with this office for review on 
January 13 and 25, 2022, respectively. After communications with this office regarding the 
complaints, the Group sent a response to our office on February 22, 2022.1 In his first complaint, 
Mr. Weingold alleges that the Group failed to create and approve meeting minutes for numerous 
meetings in 2019, 2020, and 2021. In his second complaint, Mr. Weingold alleges that the Group 
failed to respond to his first complaint. 
Following our review, we find that the Group violated the Open Meeting Law when the 
Group failed to timely approve meeting minutes and failed to properly respond to two Open 
Meeting Law complaints. In reaching this determination, we reviewed the original complaints, 
the Group’s response and the complainant’s requests for further review. We also reviewed the 
Group’s webpage on the Monterey town website. Finally, we communicated by email with you 
on several occasions in December 2021 and January 2022.  
DISCUSSION 
1. The Group failed to approve meeting minutes in a timely manner.  
 The Open Meeting Law requires that a public body “create and maintain accurate 
minutes of all meetings, including executive sessions, setting forth the date, time and place, the 
members present or absent, a summary of the discussions on each subject, a list of documents

and other exhibits used at the meeting, the decisions made and the actions taken at each meeting, 
including the record of all votes.” G.L. c. 30A, § 22(a). The meeting minutes must be reviewed 
and approved in a timely manner. G.L. c. 30A, § 22(c). The Open Meeting Law itself does not 
define “timely manner.” However, the Attorney General’s Open Meeting Law regulations 
provide that “timely manner” means “within the next three public body meetings or within 30 
days, whichever is later, unless the public body can show good cause for further delay.” 940 
C.M.R. 29.1 (2); OML 2022-13.2  
 Here, the Group concedes that the Group has not created and approved meeting minutes 
for multiple meetings which took place in 2019, 2020 and 2021 due to limited staff resources to 
create meeting minutes. We therefore find that the Group violated the Open Meeting Law. We 
remind the Group that the responsibility for complying with the Open Meeting Law rests with 
public bodies and members of such bodies, not with individual public employees, such as 
administrative staff. See OML 2018-105. We understand the demands on a public body with 
limited staff; however, the Group is still obligated to timely approve minutes. See OML 2018-
111; OML 2017-115. If staff resources are not available to create minutes for the Group to 
approve, then Group members must take on this responsibility to ensure compliance with the 
Open Meeting Law. See OML 2017-115. 
2. The Group failed to properly respond to two Open Meeting Law complaints.  
 The Open Meeting Law requires that within 14 business days of receiving an Open 
Meeting Law complaint, a public body shall meet to review the complaint’s allegations, take any 
appropriate remedial action, and send a response and description of any remedial action taken to 
the complainant. The public body must also notify the Attorney General that it has sent such 
materials to the complainant, providing the Attorney General with a copy of the complaint, the 
response, and a description of any remedial action taken. G.L. c. 30 A, § 23(b); 940 CMR 
29.05(5). Upon the public body’s written request showing good cause, this office may authorize 
an extension of time to respond. 940 CMR 29.05(5)(b). Here, we find no evidence that the Group 
met to review the complaints or sent a response to Mr. Weingold. Therefore, we find that the 
Group violated the Law by failing to properly respond to two Open Meeting Law complaints. 
CONCLUSION 
 For the reasons stated above, we find that the Group violated the Open Meeting Law 
when the Group failed to timely approve meeting minutes and failed to properly respond to two 
Open Meeting Law complaints. We order the Group’s immediate and future compliance with the 
Open Meeting Law and caution the Group that a determination by our office of a similar 
violation in the future may be considered evidence of intent to violate the Open Meeting Law.  
 We order the Group to create and approve, within ninety (90) days of receipt of this 
letter, minutes for all of its 2019, 2020 and 2021 meetings for which the Group has not already 
approved minutes. The Group should create these minutes to the best of its ability, through 
whatever means are available, including the memories and individual notations of the attendees.
