This office received a complaint from Jill Normandin on February 13, 2023, alleging that 
the Westminster Planning Board (the “Board”) violated the Open Meeting Law, G.L. c. 30A, §§ 
18-25. The complaint was originally filed with the Board on November 8, 2022, and you 
responded on behalf of the Board by letter dated December 12, 2022.1 The complaint alleges that 
the Board failed to timely respond to a request for meeting minutes and that the Board does not 
record its meetings. 
 
Following our review, we find that Board violated the Open Meeting Law by failing to 
timely respond to a request for meeting minutes. In reaching this determination, we reviewed the 
original complaint, the Board’s response to the complaint, and the complainant’s request for 
further review. We also reviewed additional email correspondence provided by the complainant 
and Board. 
 
FACTS 
 
We find the facts as follows. On October 27, the complainant emailed Town Planner 
Tracy Murphy and requested an electronic recording of the Board’s October 25 meeting. Ms. 
Murphy responded that no recording was available and that minutes were being prepared. The 
complainant states that she also emailed the Chair on October 28 and October 29 to request 
minutes of the October 25 meeting; the complainant provided this office with a copy of an email 
she sent to the Board’s then-Chair on October 28. On November 8, the complainant filed this

DISCUSSION 
The Open Meeting Law requires public bodies to create and approve meeting minutes, 
whether for open or executive session, in a timely manner. G.L. c. 30A, § 22(c). “Timely 
manner” means “within the next three public body meetings or within 30 days, whichever is 
later, unless the public body can show good cause for further delay.” 940 CMR 29.11; see OML 
2018-48. Whenever possible, we recommend that minutes of a meeting be approved at the next 
meeting. See OML 2018-67; OML 2017-133.
2 Minutes of an open session, if they exist and 
whether approved or in draft form, shall be made available upon request by any person within 10 
days. G.L. c. 30A, § 22 (c). If such minutes do not yet exist at the time of a request, the public 
body is still required to respond to the request within 10 days with an explanation of whether the 
minutes do or do not exist in either approved or draft form. See OML 2018-98; OML 2017-50; 
OML 2016-71; OML 2015-173. 
 
The c
omplainant asserts that the Board failed to timely respond to her request for minutes 
of the Board’s October 25 meeting. We find that the complainant requested the minutes from the 
Board’s then-Chair on October 28 but did not receive any response; she later received the 
minutes on November 21, after she filed this complaint. Because the Board failed to respond to 
the request for meeting minutes within 10 days of the request, we find that the Board violated the 
Open Meeting Law.  
 
 Next, the
  complainant alleges that the Board does not record its meetings. The Open 
Meeting Law states that “after notifying the chair of the public body, any person may make a 
video or audio recording of an open session of a meeting of a public body, or may transmit the 
meeting through any medium.” G.L. c. 30A, § 20(e). The Open Meeting Law does not, however, 
require public bodies to record or transcribe, or otherwise broadcast, their public meetings. See 
OML 2019-153; OML 2013-8; OML 2010-3. Because there is no requirement in the Open 
Meeting Law that the Board record its meetings, we find no violation in this regard. 
 
CONCLUSION 
 For the reasons stated above, we find that the Board violated the Open Meeting Law by 
failing to timely respond to a request for meeting minutes. We order immediate and future 
compliance with the law’s requirements, and we caution that similar future violations could be 
considered evidence of intent to violate the law. Because the Board provided the complainant 
with the requested meeting minutes shortly after this complaint was filed, and the Board’s 
makeup has substantially changed since the events at issue, we order no further remedial action.
