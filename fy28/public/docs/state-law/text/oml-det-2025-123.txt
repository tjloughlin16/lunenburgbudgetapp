FACTS 
 
On November 1, 2024, the complainant sent a written request to the Board for the 
minutes of the Board’s executive sessions held on June 25, 2018, September 17, 2018, October 
28, 2019, and December 16, 2019.  The request specified that it was made pursuant to the Open 
Meeting Law and noted that the requested minutes must be reviewed at the Board’s next 
meeting, scheduled for November 4, 2024. 
 
During the Board’s November 4, 2024, meeting, the Board reviewed the minutes of 
December 16, 2019, as those minutes had been the subject of a prior request.  The Board did not 
address or review the minutes of June 25, 2018, September 17, 2018, or October 28, 2019.  On 
December 11, 2024, then-Town Manager Mark Pruhenski responded to the complainant 
regarding the three sets of executive session minutes that “the town is withholding these for the 
time being as they remain privileged under a multi-party confidentiality agreement.  Until a legal 
review is completed, we cannot fulfill your request.”  On February 10, 2025, the Board reviewed 
the three sets of executive session minutes and voted to release the minutes subject to redactions 
to be made by legal counsel.  On February 24, 2025, the Board provided the complainant with 
the June 25, 2018, September 17, 2018, and October 28, 2019, executive session minutes with 
very limited redactions. 
 
DISCUSSION 
 
The Open Meeting Law requires that a public body “create and maintain accurate 
minut
es of all meetings, including executive sessions.”  G.L. c. 30A, § 22(a).  Executive session 
minutes may be withheld from disclosure to the public “as long as publication may defeat the 
lawful purposes of the executive session, but no longer.”  G.L. c. 30A, § 22(f).  When the 
purpose for a valid executive session has been served, the minutes and any documents or exhibits 
used at the session must be disclosed unless the attorney-client privilege or an exemption to the 
public records law applies to withhold them, in whole or in part, from disclosure.  See id.  Public 
bodies have an obligation to review the minutes of executive sessions at reasonable intervals to 
determine if continued non-disclosure of minutes is warranted, and to announce that 
determination at the next meeting following its review.  G.L. c. 30A, § 22(g)(1); see OML 2019- 
35; OML 2015-94; OML 2013-56.   
 
In addition, upon request by any person to inspect or copy the minutes of an executive 
ses
sion or any portion thereof, the body must respond to the request within 10 days following 
receipt and release any such minutes where publication would not defeat the lawful purposes of 
the executive session; provided, however, that if the body has not performed a review, the public 
body shall perform the review and release the non-exempt minutes, or any portion thereof, not 
later than the body’s next meeting or 30 days, whichever occurs first.  G.L. c. 30A, § 22(g)(2); 
OML 2024-97; OML 2012-91; OML 2012-86.  In such circumstances, the public body must still 
respond to the requester within ten days, notifying them that the body is conducting a review of 
the requested executive session minutes.  See OML 2013-99. 
 
The Board does not dispute that it received the request for executive session meeting 
minutes on November 1, 2024, and only reviewed one of the four sets of requested minutes at its

next meeting, on November 4, 2024.  We acknowledge that the request was made on a Friday, 
only one business day before the Board’s next meeting, thus providing very little time for the 
Board to compile the minutes, let alone prepare them for review.  However, we note that the 
Board did not review the requested minutes by its following meeting, or even the meeting after 
that.  The Board did not respond in any way until the Town Manager responded to the 
complainant on December 11, 2024, by which time the Board had held five meetings; 
furthermore, it is not clear if the Board or anyone acting on its behalf reviewed the minutes until 
February 10, 2025.  Therefore, we find that the Board violated the Open Meeting Law.   
 
The Board ultimately provided the minutes to the complainant on February 24, 2025, 
wit
h limited redactions to two of the sets of minutes.  The complainant asks our office to review 
and opine on the appropriateness of the redactions.  We decline to do so.  First, our review is 
limited to allegations of the Open Meeting Law contained in a complaint filed first with the 
public body; no Open Meeting Law complaint has been filed challenging the redactions.  
Second, and more importantly, the Board voted on February 10, 2025, to release the minutes to 
the public subject only to limited redactions deemed necessary by legal counsel.  In so doing, the 
Board was no longer withholding the executive session minutes based on a claim that publication 
would defeat the lawful purpose for the executive session; instead, the redactions were made 
pursuant to applicable exemption(s) to the public records law and/or the attorney-client privilege.  
See G.L. c. 30A, § 22(f).  The Division of Open Government’s review concerns compliance with 
the Open Meeting Law, G.L. c. 30A, §§ 18-25.  The Open Meeting Law authorizes the Attorney 
General to investigate a complaint alleging a violation of the Open Meeting Law but does not 
give the Division the authority to determine whether the assertion of the attorney-client privilege 
is justified or whether an exemption to the Public Records Law applies to allow a public body to 
redact portions of executive session minutes. See OML 2022-87; OML 2016-129; OML 2014-
22; OML 2013-7 
 
CONCLUSION 
 
For the reasons stated above, we find that the Board violated the Open Meeting Law by 
failing to properly respond to a request for executive session meeting minutes.  We order the 
Board’s immediate and future compliance with the law’s requirements.  As the requested 
minutes have since been provided to the complainant, we order no further remedial action. 
 
We now consider the complaint addressed by this determination to be resolved.  This 
determination does not address any other complaints that may be pending with our office or the 
Board.  Please feel free to contact our office at (617) 963-2540 if you have any questions 
regarding this letter.
