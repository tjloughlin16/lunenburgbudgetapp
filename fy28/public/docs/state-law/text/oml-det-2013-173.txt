RE: Open Meeting Law Complaint - Wenham Audit Committee 
Dear Mr. Andrews: 
This office received an Open Meeting Law complaint from Michelle Bailey, dated May 
8, 2013, alleging that the Wenham Audit Committee (the "Committee") violated the Open 
Meeting Law, G.L. c. 30A, §§ 18-25. Specifically, the complaint alleges that the Committee 
failed to review Ms. Bailey's January 29, 2013 Open Meeting Law complaint; that the 
Committee failed to approve the minutes of its November 27, 2012 meeting in a timely manner; 
and that the draft minutes of the Committee's November 27, 2012 meeting are inaccurate. The 
complaint was filed with the Committee on or about March 11, 2013. Town Administrator Mark 
Andrews' responded to the complaint on behalf of the Committee in a letter dated May 2, 2013, 
following an extension of time granted by our office. 
Following our review, we find that the Committee did approve its November 27, 2012 
meeting minutes in a timely manner, and that those minutes are accurate. However, we find that 
the Committee failed to review Ms. Bailey's January 29, 2013 Open Meeting Law complaint 
prior to the Town Administrator's response. 
The Committee meets once a year to review the Independent Auditor's Report at the 
close of the fiscal year. The Committee met on November 27, 2012. Ms. Bailey filed an Open 
Meeting Law complaint on January 29, 2013, alleging that the Committee violated the Open 
Meeting Law. Town Administrator Andrews responded to that complaint by letter dated 
February 15, 2013. On March 11, 2013, Ms. Bailey filed the present Open Meeting Law 
complaint. Upon receiving the March 11, 2013 complaint, the Committee held a special meeting

DISCUSSION 
1. The Committee Approved the Minutes of its November 27. 2012 Meeting in a 
Timely Manner. 
A public body must create and maintain accurate minutes of all meetings. G.L. c. 3 OA, § 
22(a). Minutes of all open sessions shall be approved in a timely manner. G.L. c. 30A, § 22(c). 
The Open Meeting Law does not provide a definition of "timely manner," but we have 
recommended that minutes be approved at the next meeting whenever possible. See OML 2013-
37; OML 2012-91.2 
The Committee meets only once a year. The Committee met on November 27, 2012, and 
approved the minutes of that meeting during a special meeting on May 1, 2013—some five 
months later. Because the Committee meets only annually, the Committee may approve minutes 
at its next regularly scheduled meeting, even where that meeting is not for another year. 
However, as a best practice, the Committee should create draft minutes of its meetings shortly 
thereafter, so that anyone seeking an unofficial record of what occurred at a meeting may obtain 
it without having to wait a year. See G.L. c. 30A, § 22(c). 
2. The Committee's November 27. 2013 Meeting Minutes are Accurate. 
As noted above, the Open Meeting Law requires a public body to "create and maintain 
accurate minutes of all meetings." G.L. c. 30A, § 22(a). When reviewing minutes for 
compliance with the Open Meeting Law, we look for substantial compliance with the accuracy 
requirement. See OML 2012-29; OML 2011-55. For substantial compliance, the minutes should 
contain enough detail and accuracy so that a member of the public who did not attend the 
meeting could read the minutes and have a clear understanding of what occurred. See id. 
We find that the Committee substantially complied with the accuracy requirement, and 
that the minutes contain enough detail and accuracy so that a member of the public who did not 
attend the meeting could read the minutes and have a clear understanding of what occurred. The 
Committee made corrections to the minutes before approving the final version, addressing the 
specific issues raised in Ms. Bailey's March 11, 2013 complaint filed with the Board. In her 
May 8, 2013 complaint filed with our office, she does not allege any specific inaccuracies in the 
final, approved minutes for the Committee's November 27, 2012 meeting, and notes that she did 
not attend the original November 27, 2012 meeting. 
3. The Committee Failed to Review the January 29. 2013 Open Meeting Law 
Complaint. 
Within 14 business days after receiving an Open Meeting Law complaint, unless an 
extension has been granted by the Attorney General as provided in 940 CMR 29.05(5)(a) and

(b), a public body must review the complaint's allegations; take remedial action, if appropriate; 
and send to the Attorney General a copy of the complaint and a description of any remedial 
action taken. 940 CMR 29.05(5). The public body shall simultaneously notify the complainant 
that it has sent such materials to the Attorney General and shall provide the complainant with a 
copy of the description of any remedial action taken. Id. 
Here, the Town Administrator responded to the January 29, 2013 complaint on behalf of 
the Committee. The Committee did not first review the complaint and authorize the Town 
Administrator to respond. The Committee acted appropriately by convening to address the 
substance of the complaint, but should also have reviewed the complaint for the purpose of 
providing a response. See G.L. c. 30A, § 23(b); 940 CMR 29.05(5); OML 2013-130; OML 
2012-90. 
CONCLUSION 
We find that the Committee approved its November 27, 2012 meeting minutes in a timely 
manner, and that those minutes are accurate. However, we find that the Committee failed to 
review Ms. Bailey's January 29, 2013 Open Meeting Law complaint prior to the Town 
Administrator's response. 
We order the Committee's immediate and future compliance with the Open Meeting 
Law, and caution that a similar future violation may be considered evidence of an intentional 
violation of the Law. We appreciate the patience and cooperation of the parties during this 
review, and now consider this matter closed. Please contact our office at (617) 963-2540 if you 
have any questions regarding this letter.
