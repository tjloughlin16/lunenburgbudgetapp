RE:  Open Meeting Law Complaint 
 
Dear Ms. Casagrande: 
 
This office received a complaint from Barbara Dewsnap on November 3, 2025, alleging 
that the Saugus Board of Selectmen (the “Board”) violated the Open Meeting Law, G.L. c. 30A, 
§§ 18-25.  The complaint was originally filed with the Board on or about October 17, 2025, and 
Board Chair Debra Panetta responded, on behalf of the Board, by email on October 20, 2025.1  
Specifically, the complaint alleges that the Board failed to properly respond to a request for 
minutes of a meeting held on September 30, 2025. 
 
Following our review, we find that the Board violated the Open Meeting Law in the way 
alleged.  In reaching this determination, we reviewed the original complaint, the Board’s 
response to the complaint, the complaint filed with our office requesting further review, and the 
minutes of the Board meeting held on September 30, 2025.   
 
 
1 We remind the Board that the Open Meeting Law requires that within 14 days of receipt of a complaint, the public

complaint to the members of the public body,” and “the public body shall review the complaint’s allegations.”  940 
CMR 29.05(3), (5).  Thus, a public body must meet to review the complaint and formulate a response, or meet to 
delegate that authority, and respond to the complaint within 14 business days.  See G.L. c. 30A, § 23(b); OML 2017-
69; OML 2012-90.

The Open Meeting Law requires that a public body “create and maintain accurate 
minutes of all meetings, including executive sessions.”  G.L. c. 30A, § 22(a).  The minutes of an 
open session, if they exist and whether approved or in draft form, shall be made available upon 
request by any person within 10 days.  G.L. c. 30A, § 22(c).  If minutes do not yet exist at the 
time of a request, the public body is still required to respond to the request within 10 days with 
an explanation of whether the minutes do or do not exist in either approved or draft form.  See 
OML 2017-50; OML 2016-71; OML 2015-173.2 
 
On October 6, 2025, the complainant hand delivered a letter to the Board requesting a 
copy of the draft minutes of the Board meeting held on September 30, 2025.  Board Clerk 
Meredith Casagrande responded by letter dated October 14, 2025, stating that “the minutes have 
not yet been approved by the Board.  Once this document is available I will contact you . . . .”   
 
Requests for meeting minutes may fall under the Public Records Law or the Open 
Meeting Law, depending on how the request is made, and the requirements for responding differ. 
A request for minutes that is directed to a clerk or other municipal employee as Records Access 
Officer will likely fall under the Public Records Law and requires a response in 10 business 
days.  See G.L. c. 66, § 10; OML 2019-126; OML 2019-68.  A request for meeting minutes 
made directly to the public body falls under the Open Meeting Law and requires a response 
within 10 calendar days.  See G.L. c. 30A, § 22(g)(2); OML 2019-125.   
 
Here, we find that the request fell under the Open Meeting Law where the request was 
sent to the Board.  See OML 2019-145 (finding that public body failed to timely respond to a 
request for meeting minutes that was sent to body’s administrative assistant); OML 2018-126 
(same).  Although Ms. Casagrande responded to the request within 10 days, the response 
explained that minutes of the September 30, 2025, Board meeting could not be provided because 
they were “not yet approved by the Board.”  The Open Meeting Law requires that draft minutes, 
if they exist, be provided if requested from the Board.  See G.L. c. 30A, § 22(c); OML 2022-42.  
The Board does not suggest that draft minutes did not exist at the time of the request.  As such, 
we find that the Board violated the Open Meeting Law by failing to properly respond to a request 
for minutes.  See OML 2017-115.   
 
For the reasons stated above, we find that the Board violated the Open Meeting Law.  We 
order the Board’s immediate and future compliance with the law’s requirements, and we caution 
that similar future violations could be considered evidence of intent to violate the law.  We order 
no further remedial action where the Board has posted the minutes of the September 30, 2025, 
meeting to the municipal website.
