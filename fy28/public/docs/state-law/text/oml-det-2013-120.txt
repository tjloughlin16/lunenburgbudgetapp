Re: Open Meeting Law Complaint 
Dear Attorney Coyle; 
This office received a complaint from Paul Cesan, dated December 3, 2012, alleging that 
the Southwick Board of Selectmen (the "Board") violated the Open Meeting Law, G.L. c. 3 OA, 
§§ 18-25. This complaint was originally received by the Board on November 6, 2012. We 
received your response on behalf of the Board in a letter dated November 28, 2012. The 
complaint alleges that the Board failed to create and approve meeting minutes in a timely 
manner, and that the Board failed to provide open session meeting minutes within 10 days of 
request. 
Following our review, we find that the Board violated the Open Meeting Law by failing 
to provide meeting minutes within 10 days of request and by failing to approve the requested 
meeting minutes in a timely manner. However, we find that the Board took appropriate remedial 
action in response to the complaint and order no additional relief. In reaching this determination, 
we reviewed the November 6, 2012 complaint filed with the Board; your November 28, 2012 
response on behalf of the Board; and the December 3, 2012 complaint filed with our office. 
On October 23, 2012, the complainant visited the Town Clerk's office and submitted a 
written request for minutes of Board meetings that were held on October 17, 2010; December 6, 
2010; July 18, 2011; August 1, 2011; August 15, 2011; August 29, 2011; September 12, 2011; 
September 19, 2011; October 17, 2011; December 12, 2011; December 19, 2011; and January 3, 
2012. According to the complainant, the meeting minutes were not available in the Town 
Clerk's office on the date of his request.

During its November 5, 2012 meeting, the Board voted to approve the requested meeting 
minutes. On November 6, 2012, the complainant filed an Open Meeting Law complaint with the 
Board. We understand that on November 6, 2012, the complainant received the requested 
meeting minutes. 
DISCUSSION 
A public body is required to create and maintain minutes of all meetings and must make 
its open session minutes available within 10 days of request. G.L. c. 30A, §§ 22(a), (c). The 
complainant made his request for meeting minutes on October 23, 2012. The Board provided the 
minutes on November 6, 2012 - 14 days after the complainant's original request.1 Therefore, the 
Board violated the Open Meeting Law by failing to provide its open session minutes within 10 
days of request. 
Additionally, minutes of all open sessions must be created and approved in a timely 
manner. G.L. c. 30A, § 22(c). While the Open Meeting Law does not define "timely manner," 
we recommend that a public body create and approve its minutes no later than the next meeting, 
if possible. See OML 2012-91 2 The Board acknowledged that many of the requested minutes 
were not "accepted" at the time of the complainant's request. Because the minutes at issue were 
approved between 11 and 23 months after the meetings they record, we find that the minutes 
were not approved in a timely manner, and therefore that the Board violated the Open Meeting 
Law. 
The Board approved and provided the complainant with the minutes at issue in response 
to the original complaint filed with that body. Additionally, in its written response, the Board 
states that it "will institute a procedure whereby the Minutes will be created and accepted in a 
timely manner in accordance with [the Open Meeting Law] and will provide an agenda item on 
each agenda for the acceptance of prior meeting minutes." We find that this was an appropriate 
response to the complaint, and order no additional relief. 
CONCLUSION 
For the reasons stated above, we find that the Board violated the Open Meeting Law by 
failing to approve its meeting minutes in a timely manner and by failing to provide meeting 
minutes within 10 days of request. Because the Board took appropriate remedial action, we do 
not order any additional relief.
