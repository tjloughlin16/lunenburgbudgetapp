RE: Open Meeting Law Complaints 
Dear Dr. Evans: 
This office received two complaints from Claire Barden on April 24, alleging that the 
Winchester School Committee (the "Committee") violated the Open Meeting Law, GL. c. 
30A, §§ 18-25.1 The complaints were originally filed with the Committee on or about March 
23 and March 24, and you responded, on behalf of the Committee, by letters dated March 23 
and March 27, respectively.2 In her complaints, Ms. Barden alleges that the notice for the 
Committee's March 27 meeting was not properly posted at least 48 hours before the meeting 
and failed to conspicuously record the date and time that it was posted. Additionally, Ms. 
Barden alleges that the cancellation notice for the Committee's March 28 meeting failed to 
conspicuously record the date and time that it was posted. 
Following our review, we resolve this matter by informal action, in accordance with 
940 CMR 29.07(2)(a), and find that the Committee did not violate the Open Meeting Law. 
Both complaints were filed with the Committee days prior to the scheduled meetings 
and both meetings were subsequently canceled. The Open Meeting Law's notice posting 
procedures are not implicated when a meeting does not go forward. See OML 2016-145, 
citing OML 2014-155 (unless it is clearly insufficient on its face, we do not review the 
sufficiency of a meeting notice standing alone, but rather we review what was actually 
discussed at the meeting to determine if the notice was sufficiently detailed to make the public 
aware of the discussion topics).3 Because neither meeting actually convened, we find no

violation. Although we find no violation, we remind the Committee of the requirement to 
conspicuously record the date and time that a meeting notice is posted, rather than received, 
by the town clerk. See 940 CMR 29.03(l)(b); OML 2013-44. 
We also find no violation with respect the manner in which the Committee's March 28 
meeting was canceled. The Open Meeting Law does not impose any requirements on the 
cancellation of a meeting. See G.L. c. BOA, § 20(b). Our office encourages public bodies to 
notify the public in some way of a cancellation, which is just what the Committee did when it 
posted a cancellation notice on the Town website six weeks prior to the scheduled meeting 
date. 
We now consider the complaint addressed by this determination to be resolved.
