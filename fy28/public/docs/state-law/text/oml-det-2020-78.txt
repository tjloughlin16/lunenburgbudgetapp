RE:        Open Meeting Law Complaint 
 
Dear Attorney Roche: 
 
This office received a complaint from Craig Swinson on April 1, 2020, alleging that the 
Richmond School Committee (the “Committee”) violated the Open Meeting Law, G.L. c. 30A, 
§§ 18-25.1 The complaint was originally filed with the Committee on February 20, and you 
responded, on behalf of the Committee, by letter dated March 11. In his complaint, Mr. Swinson 
alleges that the Committee violated the Open Meeting Law by not “publishing” its meeting 
minutes in a timely manner.  
 
After reviewing the complaint; the Committee’s response; the request for further review 
filed with our office; and minutes from Committee meetings held between January 14 and 
February 11, we resolve this complaint by informal action in accordance with 940 CMR 
29.07(2)(a). We find that the Committee did not violate the Open Meeting Law.  
 
The Open Meeting Law requires that public bodies “create and maintain accurate minutes 
of all meetings, including executive sessions.” G.L. c. 30A, § 22(a). These minutes must be 
reviewed and approved by the public body in a timely manner. G.L. c. 30A, § 22(c). The 
Attorney General’s Open Meeting Law regulations consider “timely manner” to be within the 
next three public body meetings, or within 30 days, whichever is later. 940 C.M.R. 29.11(2). 
However, we recommend that minutes be approved at the next meeting whenever possible. See 
OML 2019-133; OML 2019-39; OML 2014-15.2

Specifically, the complaint alleges that the Committee failed to timely approve and 
“post” minutes of its January 14 meeting.3 The Committee states that its practice is to approve 
minutes at its next meeting or at the following meeting.  Here, the Committee approved the 
January 14 minutes at its February 11 meeting, which was the Committee’s second meeting 
following the January 14 meeting. As such, we find that the January 14 minutes were approved 
in a timely manner and the Committee did not violate the Open Meeting Law.  See OML 2019-
20. 
 
With respect to the complainant’s allegations that certain approved minutes have not been 
published, the Open Meeting Law does not require that public bodies post their minutes to a 
website or elsewhere, but only that minutes be approved in a timely manner and made available 
within 10 days of a request. G.L. c. 30A, § 22(c); see OML 2017-98; OML 2017-25. Therefore, 
we find no violation of the Open Meeting Law.4 Nonetheless, the Committee has chosen to post 
its meeting minutes on its website and has notified the complainant that minutes from its 
meetings are available at that location. 
 
For the reasons stated above, we find that the Committee did not violate the Open 
Meeting Law. We now consider the complaint addressed by this determination to be resolved. 
This determination does not address any other complaints that may be pending with our office or 
the Committee. Please feel free to contact our office at (617) 963-2540 if you have any questions 
regarding this letter.
