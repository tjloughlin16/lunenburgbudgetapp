RE: Open Meetmg Law Complaint 
Dear Attorney Feodoroff: 
This office received a complaint from Stephen Morgan, dated June 1, 2018, alleging that 
the Ashland Board of Assessors (the "Board") violated the Open Meeting Law, G.L. c. 3 OA, §§ 
18-25. The complaint was originally filed with the Board on April 30, 2018, and, after an 
extension of time was granted by our office, the Board responded by letter dated May 31, 2018. 
In his complaint, Mr. Morgan alleges that the Board violated the Open Meeting Law by: (i) 
approving minutes for eleven meetings that fail to list documents used in the meetings; and (ii) 
failing to approve the minutes of two meetings.1 
Following our review, we resolve this complaint by informal action in accordance with 
940 CMR 29.07(2)(a), and find that the Board violated the Open Meeting Law by failing to list 
documents used during the meeting. The Open Meeting Law requires, in relevant part, that a 
public body "shall create and maintain accurate minutes of all meetings, including ... a list of 
documents used at the meeting." G.L. c. 30A, §22(a). A document is "used" at a meeting when, 
at a minimum, it is physically present, verbally identified, and its contents are discussed. See 
OML 2017-169.2 
The complaint alleges that the Board approved meeting minutes that lacked the required 
list of documents used during each meeting.3 In response to this complaint, the Board concedes 
that certain minutes lacked the required list of documents. The Board voted to amend the minutes

to add the missing list of discussed documents during its May 31, 2018 meeting. The goal of this 
office in enforcing the Open Meeting Law is achieving compliance with that law. Where a body 
acknowledges it failed to fully comply and takes steps to ensure compliance going forward, we 
will generally find that to be sufficient remedial action in response to a complaint. See QML 
2017-30. Accordingly, we find that the action taken by the Board was sufficient to address the 
violations that occurred. 
The complaint further alleges that the Board did not create and approve minutes of its 
August 23, 2017 and February 8, 2018 meetings. In support of this allegation, the complaint 
points to a vacant section of the Board page on the Ashland municipal website to support the 
inference that the Board failed to create and approve these meeting minutes. As part of its 
response, the Board provided copies of the minutes that were alleged to be missing. It is 
therefore clear that the Board did not violate the Open Meeting Law with respect to this 
allegation. Where, as here, it is clear upon receiving a response that a public body has not 
violated the Open Meeting Law, complainants should refrain from filing complaints with our 
office unnecessarily. See OML 2018-86. 
We now consider the complaint addressed by this determination to be resolved. This 
determination does not address any other complaints that may be pending with our office or the 
Board. Please feel free to contact the Division at (617) 963 - 2540 if you have any questions.
