 RE:        Open Meeting Law Complaint 
 
Dear Ms. Farrell: 
 
This office received a complaint from Amanda Alix on August 14, 2024, alleging that the 
Shutesbury Select Board (the “Board”) violated the Open Meeting Law, G.L. c. 30A, §§ 18-25.  
The complaint was originally filed with the Board on or about July 12, 2024, and the Board 
responded to the complaint by letter dated August 15, 2024.
1  The complaint alleges that the 
Board posted a notice with insufficient specificity for a meeting held on June 17, 2024, by failing 
to list the candidates for appointment. 
 
Following our review, we find that the Board violated the Open Meeting Law in the way 
alleged.  In reaching this determination, we reviewed the original complaint, the Board’s 
response to the complaint, and the complaint filed with our office requesting further review.  In 
addition, we reviewed the notice, minutes and video recording of the Board meeting held on June 
17, 2024.
2

The Open Meeting Law requires that, except in an emergency, “a public body shall post 
notic
e of every meeting at least 48 hours prior to such meeting, excluding Saturdays, Sundays 
and legal holidays.”  G.L. c. 30A, § 20(b).  The notice must be printed in a legible, easily 
understandable format and must contain the date, time and place of the meeting, as well as a 
listing of topics that the chair reasonably anticipates will be discussed.  Id.  The list of topics 
shall have “sufficient specificity to reasonably advise the public of the issues to be discussed at 
the meeting.”  940 CMR 29.03(l)(b).  We generally consider a topic to be sufficiently specific 
when a reasonable member of the public could read the topic and understand the anticipated 
nature of the public body’s discussion.  See OML 2019-70; OML 2015-35; OML 2013-39.
3   
 
Here, the notice of the June 17, 2024, meeting included the following topic: “Review for 
Vote, FY25 Annual Appointment List.”  The notice did not include the names of any individuals 
to be appointed.  During the meeting, the Board discussed and approved the appointments and 
reappointments of numerous town employees and public body members that the Board is 
required to approve on an annual basis.    
 
We have consistently stated that the names of applicants for appointment or e
 mployment 
which are known 48 hours in advance of a meeting should be included on a meeting notice. See 
OML 2022-217; 2019-24; OML 2016-150; OML 2013-163.  The Board has not claimed that the 
specific appointments or reappointments were not reasonably anticipated 48 hours in advance of 
the June 17, 2024, meeting.  As such, we find that the notice for the Board meeting held on June 
17, 2024, should have included the names of the individuals to be considered by the Board.  
Accordingly, the Board violated the Open Meeting Law by failing to include this information on 
the notice.  
 
We acknowledge that the list of appointments considered during the meeting consisted of 
ten pa
ges of individuals.  However, we take this opportunity to clarify that the list of candidates 
for appointment must be included as part of the notice document itself, even if it is several pages 
long; it is not sufficient to reference a separate list that is available elsewhere.  See OML 2022-
68; OML 2020-21; OML 2019-22.  In the event of a similar situation, the Board may include as 
part of the notice document a list of each public body or town department for which an 
appointment would be made, along with the names of the candidates for consideration. 
 
For t
he reasons stated above, we find that the Board violated the Open Meeting Law by 
posting an insufficiently specific notice for the June 17, 2024, meeting that failed to identify the 
individual candidates being considered for appointment.  We order the Board’s immediate and 
future compliance with the law’s requirements, and we caution that similar future violations 
could be considered evidence of intent to violate the law.
