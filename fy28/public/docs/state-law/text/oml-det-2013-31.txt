Following our review, we find that the Board did not violate the Open Meeting 
Law by responding as it did to the complainant's request for meeting minutes, but did fail 
to create and approve meeting minutes in a timely manner. 
FACTS 
Based upon our review of the material listed above, the facts are as follows. In a 
written request to the Board, dated June 19, 2012, the complainant requested the minutes 
from the Board's June 13, 2012 meeting. The complainant subsequently sent another 
written request to the Board, dated June 21, 2012, requesting minutes from its June 20, 
2012 meeting. At the time the requests were made, draft minutes from these two meetings 
did not exist. On June 20 and June 25, 2012, respectively, Ms. Fitz-Gibbon responded to 
the requests by letter, acknowledging receipt of the complainant's requests and notifying 
her that she would be contacted when the minutes were available. 
Once the minutes were drafted, the Board provided copies of the draft minutes to 
the complainant. On July 30, 2012, the Board provided the June 13, 2012 meeting minutes 
and on August 20, 2012, the Board provided the June 20, 2012 meeting minutes. 
DISCUSSION 
The Open Meeting Law requires that the "[m]inutes of all open sessions shall be 
created and approved in a timely manner. The minutes of an open session, if they exist and 
whether approved or in draft form, shall be made available upon request by any person 
within 10 days." G.L. c. 30A, § 22(c) (emphasis added). 
A public body is required to provide minutes within ten (10) days of a request for 
minutes, provided that they exist. See OML 2012-100. The complainant submitted her 
first request for minutes six days after the June 13, 2012 meeting, and her second request 
for minutes just one day after the June 20, 2012 meeting. The Board did not violate the 
Open Meeting Law by not providing the minutes within 10 days of these requests because 
the meeting minutes had not been drafted as of the date of those requests. The Board 
timely acknowledged the complainant's requests, however, and provided a copy of the 
draft minutes as soon as they were created. 
While the Board provided the complainant with the minutes once they were 
drafted, we find that the Board did not comply with the Law's requirements regarding the 
timely creation and approval of minutes. The Open Meeting Law does not provide a 
definition of "timely manner," but we recommend that minutes be approved at the next

CONCLUSION 
For the reasons stated above, we find that the Board violated the Open Meeting 
Law by failing to create and approve meeting minutes in a timely manner. We order 
immediate and future compliance with the Open Meeting Law, and caution that future 
similar conduct may be considered evidence of intent to violate the Open Meeting Law.
