This office received a complaint from E. Lyn Lemaire, dated January 28, 2016, alleging 
that the Carlisle Affordable Housing Trust (the "Trust") violated the Open Meeting Law, G.L. c. 
30A, §§ 18-25. The complaint was originally filed with the Trust on December 4, 2015, and the 
Trust responded by letter dated December 21, 2015. In her complaint, Ms. Lemaire alleges that 
the Trust violated the Open Meeting Law by: (i) discussing a topic during a meeting that had not 
been listed on the meeting notice; and (ii) deliberating outside of a noticed meeting.1 
Following our review, we find that the Trust did not violate the Open Meeting Law. In 
reaching a determination, we reviewed the original complaint; the Trust's response; and the 
request for further review filed with our office. We spoke with the complainant by telephone on 
March 2, 2016. Finally, we spoke with the Trust's legal counsel on March 8 and 22, 2016.2

complainant circulated a memorandum to the members of the Trust outlining her concerns over a 
proposed development project planned for 338 Bedford Road. The Trust is the owner of the

DISCUSSION 
I. The Allegation Concerning the September 9, 2015 Meeting is Untimely. 
The Open Meeting Law requires that complaints be filed with the public body within 30 
days of the alleged violation. G.L. c. 30A, § 23(b); 940 CMR 29.05(1). If the alleged violation 
could not have been known at the time it occurred, then the complaint must be filed with the 
public body within 30 days of the date on which the alleged violation could reasonably have 
been discovered. 940 CMR 29.05(3). Complaints alleging violations that take place during an 
open session can reasonably be discovered at the time they occur. See OML 2012-52; OML 
Declination 1-26-11 (Charlemont Board of Health).

Here, the complaint alleges that the Trust discussed a topic during its September 9, 2015 
meeting that had not been listed on the meeting notice—that is, the consideration of Site Plan 
Review for 338 Bedford Road. This meeting took place entirely in open session for which the 
complainant was present. Therefore, in order to be timely, any complaint concerning the 
September 9, 2015 meeting should have been filed with the Trust within 30 days of that meeting. 
Because the present complaint was filed with the Trust on December 4, 2015—71 days after the 
meeting—we find that the allegation is untimely and we decline to review it. G.L. c. 3OA, § 
23(b); 940 CMR 29.05(1),(3). 
II. The Trust Members did not Deliberate Outside of an Open Meeting. 
The Open Meeting Law is designed to eliminate "much of the secrecy surrounding the 
deliberations and decisions on which public policy is based." Ghighlione v. School Committee 
of Southbridge. 376 Mass. 70, 72 (1978). The Law requires that meetings of a public body be 
properly noticed and open to members of the public, unless an executive session is convened. 
See G.L, c. 30A, §§ 20(a)-(b), 21. The law defines a "meeting" as, "a deliberation by a public 
body with respect to any matter within the body's jurisdiction." G.L. c. 30A, § 18. A 
"deliberation" is defined as "an oral or written communication through any medium, including 
electronic mail, between or among a quorum of a public body on any public business within its 
jurisdiction." Id. For the purposes of the Open Meeting Law, a "quorum" is a simple majority of 
the member of the public body. Id. 
Here, the complaint alleges that the Trust members deliberated outside of a noticed 
meeting. As evidence, the complaint points to the differences between the discussion during the 
September 9, 2015 meeting and the application signed by the chair on September 24, 2015. 
However, the complaint points to nothing demonstrating communication between or among a 
quorum of the Trust members prior to the chair signing the application, and the Trust's response 
stated that the chair signed the application without consulting with other Trust members. 
Moreover, the application was prepared by a third party. Till, Inc. Absent evidence to the 
contrary, we credit the chair's version of events. See OML 2016-27. We find that the Trust 
members did not deliberate outside of a meeting and therefore did not violate the Open Meeting 
Law. See G.L. c. 30A, § 18; OML 2013-133; OML 2011-40; OML 2011-52. 
CONCLUSION 
For the reasons stated above, we find that the Trust did not violate the Open Meeting 
Law. We now consider the complaint addressed by this determination to be resolved. This 
determination does not address any other complaints that may be pending with our office or the 
Trust. Please feel free to contact the Division at (617) 963 - 2540 if you have any questions.
