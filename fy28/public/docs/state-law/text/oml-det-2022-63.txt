 RE: Open Meeting Law Complaints 
 
Dear Attorney Feodoroff: 
 
This office received two complaints from Patrick Higgins on December 8, 2021, alleging 
that the Rehoboth Board of Selectmen (the “Board”) violated the Open Meeting Law, G.L. c. 
30A, §§ 18-25.  Both complaints were originally filed with the Board on or about November 29, 
2021, and you responded to both complaints, on behalf of the Board, by letter December 7, 2021.  
Both complaints allege that the Board failed to timely approve the minutes of a meeting held on 
July 26, 2021.   
 
We resolve this matter by informal action, in accordance with 940 CMR 29.07(2)(a), 
after reviewing the original complaint, the Board’s response to the complaint, and the complaint 
filed with our office requesting further review.  We find that the Board violated the Open 
Meeting Law in the way alleged.   
 
The Open Meeting Law requires that a public body “create and maintain accurate 
minutes of all meetings, including executive sessions, setting forth the date, time and place, the 
members present or absent, a summary of the discussions on each subject, a list of documents 
and other exhibits used at the meeting, the decisions made and the actions taken at each meeting, 
including the record of all votes.”  G.L. c. 30A, § 22(a).  The meeting minutes must be reviewed 
and approved in a timely manner.  G.L. c. 30A, § 22(c).  “Timely manner” means “within the 
next three public body meetings or within 30 days, whichever is later, unless the public body can 
show good cause for further delay.”  See 940 CMR 29.11; OML 2018-48.1  Whenever possible, 
we recommend that minutes of a meeting be approved at the next meeting.  See OML 2018-67; 
OML 2017-133.

The Board approved the minutes of its July 26, 2021, meeting during its November 29, 
2021, meeting, which was the eighteenth meeting and 127 days thereafter.  As such, we find that 
the Board violated the Open Meeting Law by failing to approve these minutes in a timely 
manner.   
 
We remind the Board that the responsibility for complying with the Open Meeting Law 
rests with public bodies and members of such bodies, not with individual public employees, such 
as administrative staff.  See OML 2018-105.  We understand the demands on a public body with 
limited staff; however, the Board is still obligated to timely approve minutes.  See OML 2018-
111; OML 2017-115.  If staff resources are not available to create minutes for the Board to 
approve, then Board members must take on this responsibility to ensure compliance with the law.  
See OML 2017-115.   
 
For the reasons stated above, we find that the Board violated the Open Meeting Law by 
failing to approve minutes of its July 26, 2021, meeting in a timely manner.  We order immediate 
and future compliance with the law’s requirements, and we caution that similar future violations 
could be considered evidence of intent to violate the law.  Although our office found that the 
Board violated the Open Meeting Law for similar conduct in a prior determination, OML 2021-
181, we decline to find this violation to be intentional as the Board was not aware of that 
decision at the time it approved the minutes.  However, we caution the Board that future 
intentional violations for failure to timely approve meeting minutes may result in a 
recommendation of a civil penalty. 
 
We now consider the complaints addressed by this determination to be resolved.  This 
determination does not address any other complaints that may be pending with our office or the 
Board.  Please feel free to contact our office at (617) 963-2540 if you have any questions 
regarding this letter.
