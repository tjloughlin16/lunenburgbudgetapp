Mission
The Mission of the Lunenburg Public Schools is to provide all students with the skills, 
confidence, and passion for life-long learning so that they may find their own paths to 
successful careers, active citizenship, and rewarding lives.
Vision
The Lunenburg Public Schools is committed to becoming a leader in education by 
focusing on its values of attending to the personal well-being of its students, providing 
high-quality curricula and student-centered instruction for deeper learning at all levels, 
and promoting the development of attributes and skills necessary for continuous 
learning for all.
===PAGE 3===
July 2018 Superintendent’s Entry Plan (SY2018-2019)
November 2019 Superintendent’s Report of Findings and development of the Strategy 
for Continuous Improvement with four objectives. (SC approved November 2019)
● To modernize teaching and learning by establishing effective teams of educational leaders to accomplish the 
essential work for transforming practice, and hiring and developing excellent educators who are continually 
learning and adapting practice to meet the needs of all students. (TT&L)
● To create a culture that values data use and continuous learning in a climate that is safe, caring, and 
inclusive and reflective of our core values. (CC&C)
● To improve operational efficiencies, including communication within the district and with the community, 
in order to decrease time spent on management and increase time spent on instructional leadership. (CO)
● To ensure all students are provided equal access to a quality education, providing them the appropriate 
support and challenge so that they can become skilled life-long learners. (EAAO)
March 2020 COVID Pandemic School Shutdown 
FY20-24 COVID GRANTS: ESSER 1, 2, and 3 funded: teachers, counselors, tutors, stipends 
(Behavioral Health Coordinator), COVID supplies, and Chromebooks
FY24 Action Plans for the Strategy for Continuous Improvement focused on Cultivating Culture & 
Climate.
FY25 Schools received an override to fund the budget; there were six teacher positions, two counselor 
positions and one administrator position cut due to sunsetting COVID grants.

FY25 School Department Budget ProcessAugust
District Leadership Team participated in data summits; reviewed the Strategy for Continuous Improvement; revised Strategy Priority Action Plans; aligned School Improvement Plan goals and administrator/teacher evaluation goals to the Strategy Priority Action Plans. 
October/November
Department heads and building principals began working on budgets which were submitted to the Director of Finance & Administration in November.December/January
Central Office Leadership began meeting with building administration and department heads to discuss the impact of a projected 12% increase to health insurance, coupled with CBA and contractual service increases, on the FY26 budget.  Each expense line was scrutinized to ensure that every necessary expense was accounted for.  Discussions began regarding potential personnel cuts pending the Interim Town Manager’s target budget. A level services budget was presented on January 22, 2025.
February
The proposed budget will be adjusted when the actual health insurance percentage is known.  There will be Budget Q&A session at the first School Committee meeting in February.
March
The Superintendent will present a recommended budget. There will be Budget Q&A session at the first School Committee meeting in March.  The School Committee will hold a budget hearing and  approve a budget; and the school department budget will be presented to the Finance Committee (March 6, 2025).
