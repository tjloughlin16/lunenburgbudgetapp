INCLUSION SUPPORT 
Lunenburg High School
6
===PAGE 7===
Q1. What inclusion support do you currently 
provide?
Inclusion Support is provided at the high school in a multitude of  ways.  
Essential Classes are classes that are the same as College Prep classes but 
operate at a slower pace.  These classes have moved into a co-teaching model 
across curriculum areas.   Paras are also working in some general education 
classrooms to support students that need additional assistance.  These paras 
also assist as needed in Academic Support Center classes. 
7
===PAGE 8===
Q2. What is going well with your delivery of  
inclusion services to students?
Staff  indicated the following as going well:
• Special education team members work well together
• Para support
• Essential classes moved into a co-teaching model across curriculum areas
• Staff  accessibility to students
8
===PAGE 9===
Q2. What is going well with your delivery of  
inclusion services to students?
Staff  indicated the following as going well:
• Special education staff  have input into the master schedule each school year 
in order to facilitate successful student outcomes
• Academic Support Center Classes provide additional support to 12-15 
students between 2 blocks with a para
9
===PAGE 10===
Q3. What is not?
Staff  indicated the following as not going well:
• There is currently not a large sub pool.  This affects paras placed in specific 
classrooms, who are pulled to cover a class.  This impacts student 
achievement overall, with specific impact on executive functioning issues.  
This issue, along with the rotating schedule impacts communication about  
long term assignments.
10
===PAGE 11===
Q4.   If  you could change only one thing about proving 
inclusionary support, what would that one thing be?
The ONE THING noted by staff  is as follows:
• To have the Special Education Teachers at the High School function in a true 
co-teaching capacity, rather than feeling at times like a para in an inclusive 
classroom
11
===PAGE 12===
Q5. With the new IEP format on the horizon, what 
specific support if  any would be helpful with the successful 
implementation in your building or district-wide?
Staff  indicated that there has been training in the District on the new IEP format 
which will be implemented next school year.  Special education staff, general education 
staff, parents and the school committee have been trained on this new format.  The 
specific support that staff  feel would greatly assist with the successful implementation 
of  the new IEP is:
• A full day professional development day with all special education staff  – perhaps 
end of  September- to discuss and resolve any technical problems, etc., with the new 
IEP

RECOMMENDATIONS
1. Share this report with applicable stakeholders
2. Review the ability to provide support to the current Co-Teaching Model.  Such 
support could include training of  both Special Education and General 
Education Teachers,  with ongoing observations/coaching. 
13
===PAGE 14===
RECOMMENDATIONS
3. Determine how to better track assignments – both short term and long term.    
Develop/revise google form to be used consistently by staff/students. This will    
assist with providing targeted assistance to students in the Academic Support 
Center, specifically with students having executive functioning issues.
4. Review the 3 Year Schedule with the Parent Advisory Council.  This will allow 
the PAC to have input into the evaluation process, as required under DESE Special 
Education Criteria 32.
