During the month of May, educators, specialists and coaches utilize three different academic 
screeners in mathematics and literacy - Universal Screener for Number Sense (USNS), Dynamic 
Indicators of Basic Early Literacy Skills (DIBELS), and iReady - to monitor students' growth. 
The first section of this report describes the end-of-year assessment progress in aggregate; the 
second section by our District goal for students with disabilities. 
 
Section I: Assessment Aggregate Data 
 
Universal Screener for Number Sense Aggregate  
 
In the Universal Screener for Number Sense (USNS), student achievement is categorized into 
four categories:  Proficient (green), Basic (yellow), Below Basic (orange), and Well Below Basic 
(red) based on the number of items students answer correctly in both verbal and written tasks. 
Students are expected to improve one category by mid-year and two category levels by the end 
of the school year.

The aggregate data above shows a significant decrease in both the Well Below Basic and Below 
Basic categories from fall to spring, decreasing from a total of 30 students to 9 in grade 1, and 
decreasing from 53 students to 6 in grade 2. This significant decrease is the result of stronger 
Tier 1 instruction supported by coaching and professional learning provided by a consultant. In 
addition, this decrease is also the result of targeted Tier II interventions specific to the number 
sense skills identified in the screener, and coaching of teachers during PLC meetings on the types 
of interventions that lead to the greatest impact on student achievement along with the materials 
to use.  
 
In addition, the data shows an increase in the Proficient category from fall to spring for both 
grades, with grade one increasing from a total of 24 to 73 students, and grade 2 increasing from 
31 to 74 students. 
 
Both grades did show a mild increase in Well Below Basic and Below Basic categories from 
winter to spring, which would be impacted by the end-of-year content increasing in difficulty, as 
well as the differences in the complexity of the questions asked within the screener itself.

MTSS % Ranges for Support 
A Multi-Tiered System of Support (MTSS) is a way of doing things within the schools that 
impacts every student. Teams of educators regularly come together to plan for and check the 
academic growth and behavioral and social-emotional needs of each student.    
Within the MTSS framework, every student receives core instruction in their classroom. This is 
called Tier One. Some students need additional strategic or targeted instruction, which is called 

===PAGE 8===

Tier Two.  In a typical class, approximately 15-20 % of students will require strategic/targeted 
support (Tier II) at any given point in the year in addition to general classroom instruction.  
Tier III or intensive support is very focused and takes place individually or in very small groups.  
In a typical class, approximately 1-5 % of students will require intensive support at any given 
point in the year in addition to general classroom instruction. It is important to note that Tier III 
is not the same as special education. Students with or without a disability may or may not need 
Tier III support. The visual below depicts the percentages.

Section II: District Goal 
 
The District student learning goal for 2024-2025 is stated below for the two grade spans: 
Grades K-5: students with disabilities who are substantially below grade level benchmark will 
grow their foundational skills as measured by at least above average growth in iReady, the 
Zones of Progress in DIBELS and/or Forefront Universal Screener for Number Sense. 
 
Grades 6-12: students with disabilities who are substantially below grade level benchmark will 
grow their skills as measured by at least above average growth in iReady and/or classroom-
based measures.

During the 2024-2025 school year, students in grades 1-2 are being screened three times during 
the school year to track progress. Based on the success of this screener in identifying targeted 
interventions for students, all students in grades K-5 will be screened three times during the 
2025-2026 school year.  
 
In grades 1-2 mathematics there were 29 students with disabilities included in this data set. The 
data showed encouraging trends in the winter; however, there has been a backslide in results for 
both grades from winter to spring. There are several factors that could account for this reversed 
trend. First, there is an increase in the total number of students with disabilities being screened, 
and without their fall or winter data being included in the prior data sets, it’s difficult to know 
how much growth each of those students actually made vs. where they fall on the chart overall. 
Second, the complexity of the screener  toward more critical thinking in the spring may cause 
some students with disabilities to  struggle with these more challenging tasks, which could bring 
their composite score down in certain categories of math, specifically place value and addition & 
subtraction. The focus for the 2025-2026 school year will be on using this screener in a more 
thorough manner to focus on more targeted Tier II and Tier III supports to move the students in 
the Well Below Basic and Below Basic categories into the Basic and Proficient categories.  
 
 
DIBELS Universal Screener for Early Literacy 
 
The Early Literacy Universal Screener, DIBELS, is an important tool to help identify students 
who may be struggling with reading/reading readiness.  Typically, the screener is given three 
times a year. Kindergarten and Third grade will be screened twice this year. Students' composite 
scores are categorized into four categories; At Risk, intensive (red), Some Risk, strategic 
(yellow), Minimal Risk, at benchmark (green), Negligible Risk, above benchmark (blue). While 
these categories are important for understanding a student's overall literacy skills, they do not 
accurately show student growth. For example, each assessment period, the benchmark points 
increase significantly from fall to winter. Therefore, a student may be categorized as still At Risk 
(red) but make significant progress toward increasing their overall composite score. A first grade 
student may have scored 304 (red) in the fall and made growth,  increasing their score 78 points 
to still have a red composite score of 372. That is why the growth data is represented in the 
different graphs below.
