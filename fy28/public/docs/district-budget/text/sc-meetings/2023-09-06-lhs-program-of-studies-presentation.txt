The Medieval Experience - Honors (Did Not Run) (SS/HIST)           0.5 credit
Grades 10-12
Prerequisite: Recommendation from Social Studies teacher
 
In this Honors-designated course, students will explore the politics, theology, territorial reach, and socio-economic dynamics of both 
Western Christendom and Dar-al Islam between the fall of the Roman Empire and La Reconquista. The semester will entail a vigorous 
research component, where students will sharpen their skills analyzing texts, art, and architecture from cultural milieus far removed 
from their own. 
Completion of additional summer reading is required for the Honors level.

Nuclear Age (SS/HIST)(Did Not Run)(SS/HIST) 0.5 credit
Grades 11-12 
 
Prerequisite: United States History
In this College Prep-designated course, students will chart the evolution of post-World War II American culture, 
politics, and economic phenomena in tandem with Cold War international policy and conflict through the Reagan 
administration. The semester will include a research component where students will strengthen their critical reading 
abilities across modern media – texts, film, television, and advertising.

(Unified Art) Art HistoryNew Course (replacing Video Production I)0.5 credit
Grades 10-12
Prerequisite: none
Art History is the academic study and analysis of works of art, those who created the work and the culture 
and times in which those works were produced. This course will serve as an introduction to various art 
movements and art making techniques around the world dating from Global Prehistory to the present. 
Focus will be placed on analysis of content, form, function, and context to uncover symbolism, technique, 
cultural influence, and the artistic intent of works in media such as drawing, painting, printmaking, 
photography, sculpture, and installation.
Please note: Art History is not a studio class. We study and analyze work through class discussion, 
research, writing, presentations, and exams.

(Unified Art) Choice Based Art      NEW COURSE 0.5 credits
9-12, no prerequisite
Choice Based Art will be utilized in a TAB (Teaching for Artistic Behaviors) classroom as a method of teaching for 
artistic behaviors like planning, problem-solving and generating ideas. Students will be introduced to numerous 
materials and techniques in 2D and 3D art and design inclusive of drawing, ceramics, printmaking, painting and others,  
and be able to create their own artistic compositions based on a theme coupled with  their own expression, 
interpretation and innovation. It involves the Studio Habits of Mind:   Develop Craft:Learning to use tools, materials, 
and artistic conventions . Studio Practice: Taking care of tools, materials, works, and space. Engage & Persist: Finding 
personally meaningful projects and sticking to them . Envision: Imagining new artworks and steps to bring them to life. 
Express: Making works that convey personal meaning and interpreting meaning in the works of others. This class is 
designed for individuals who want to challenge themselves in a variety of materials and techniques, generation of 
original ideas and personal expression.

(English) Creative WritingNew Course (replacing Sports Literature) 0.5 credit
Grades 11-12
Prerequisite: None
This course is meant to spark your literary interests, talents, and inclinations, so that you can walk away with 
a clearer image of who you are – or rather, who you might be – as a writer. This applies to everyone, from 
those who might have never written before, to those who are fairly decided on their preferred forms of 
writing, and everyone in between. You will read a fair amount of short stories and poems, and then write 
your own. You will critique the works of your classmates, and help one another on this path of self-discovery 
and skill improvement.

Statistics – College Prep (New Course) 1 credit 
 Grades:  11-12
 Prerequisites: Geometry and Algebra II 
● This course is for students continuing their study in mathematics after the completion of Algebra II. 
● Many academic programs at the college level include statistics as a requirement, and the ability to 
interpret data is critical in the modern world. 
● The focus of this course will cover the concepts and methods of collecting, analyzing, and drawing 
conclusions from data. 
● Big idea topics include interpreting categorical and quantitative data, making inferences and justifying 
conclusions asked on such data, distributions and hypothesis testing, and using probability to make 
decisions about real-world applications. 
● The use of technology is integrated throughout the course.
