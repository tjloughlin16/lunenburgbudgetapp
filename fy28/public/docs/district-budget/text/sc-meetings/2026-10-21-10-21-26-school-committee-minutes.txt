10. Old Business 
a. Update on Current Capital Projects 
 
Mr. Stevens reported that preliminary work was underway on the Primary School driveway and parking study. 
Initial concepts had recently been received and would be reviewed before presentation to the Committee. 
Members discussed traffic circulation, parking capacity, emergency vehicle access, and the relationship between 
the driveway design and playground location. Potential changes could affect the playground or adjacent playing 
fields. The Committee emphasized the need to coordinate the projects so that playground improvements would 
not conflict with future driveway construction. 
Previously appropriated planning funds were discussed as $30,000 for the driveway and $30,000 for the 
playground. Members sought designs and cost estimates in advance of Spring Town Meeting. The discussion also 
addressed the sequence of design, appropriation, and construction bidding. 
The Committee emphasized the importance of an age-appropriate and accessible Primary School playground and 
thanked staff for advancing the projects. 
Technology staff reported that access-control improvements at Turkey Hill had been completed before the school 
year. Equipment for the Primary School had been obtained, but installation remained dependent on additional 
cabling work. 
 
b. FY28 Capital Plan 
 
Dr. Fortuna presented the administration’s recommended capital projects for FY28 through FY33, with additional 
longer-term replacement planning. She explained that the Committee would determine its priorities at a subsequent 
meeting.

Turf, Track, and Retaining Walls 
 
Dr. Fortuna recommended the field and track project as the first priority. She reported that a structural engineer had 
examined the retaining walls before Homecoming and determined that the lower wall was in a state of failure. 
The resulting safety restrictions required areas to be blocked off above and below the lower wall, making track lanes 
seven and eight unavailable. The engineer also advised that the current fall season should be the final season of use 
for the bleachers and press box because of concerns involving continued freeze-thaw deterioration. 
Dr. Fortuna recommended treating the retaining walls, drainage, track, and field as a coordinated construction project. 
She noted that drainage work would be necessary regardless of whether the replacement playing surface was artificial 
turf or natural grass. 
The November warrant request was intended to fund design work and preparation for construction bidding. The goal 
was to develop a more complete project cost for consideration at Spring Town Meeting. Members discussed potential 
interim arrangements, including use of other districts’ facilities, additional away games, and the resulting 
transportation or rental expenses. 
The Committee discussed the need for public, coach, and player input regarding the future playing surface. No 
decision was made between artificial turf and natural grass. 
An earlier project estimate of approximately $3 million was included in the capital plan. Dr. Fortuna explained that 
the project would likely require a debt exclusion and further guidance regarding financing. 
 
Primary School Playground, Driveway, and Parking 
Dr. Fortuna recommended the Primary School playground and driveway improvements as the second priority. 
Members emphasized accessibility and suitability for the ages of children attending the school. Mr. Stevens was asked 
to explore whether usable existing playground equipment could be relocated for other municipal use. 
An estimate of $309,205 was discussed for replacing the playground in its existing location. The final design, 
accessibility features, and any relocation would require further review. Members suggested seeking input from 
knowledgeable community members and students. The driveway estimate remained preliminary because the scope 
had not been established. Mr. Stevens cautioned that drainage, lighting, and parking expansion could affect the final 
cost. 
 
Auditorium Audio-Visual Improvements 
The proposed auditorium project was estimated at $98,489 and included replacement or improvement of projection, 
sound, and related equipment. Staff explained that equipment failures had resulted in revisions to an existing upgrade 
proposal.Members discussed the auditorium’s use for school productions and community meetings. Technology staff 
described interim arrangements and backup equipment for the upcoming Town Meeting. 
 
Turkey Hill Clocks and Bells 
Technology staff described an aging bell system that was no longer supported by its vendor and could only be 
configured through an alternate method following a hardware failure. Some clocks were also failing. 
The proposed replacement would combine clock and bell functions through networked equipment and provide 
opportunities for future integration with paging and telephone systems. 
 
District Network Switches 
Technology staff proposed replacing core network switches in FY28 and the remaining distribution and access 
switches in FY29. Staff identified April 2030 as the deadline for replacing the existing equipment and emphasized the 
need to complete the work before that date. 
Members asked staff to continue exploring grants while retaining the projects in the capital plan because replacement 
could not depend on grant availability. 
 
Facilities Truck 
Mr. Stevens explained that reliable trucks were necessary for district snow removal. Two older vehicles were in poor 
condition, and the department needed three reliable vehicles to maintain operations. 
A replacement truck and plow were discussed at an estimated cost of $61,600. Members considered the effect of 
equipment failure on school openings, snow-removal responsibilities, and cooperation with the Department of Public 
Works.

FY29 Projects 
Proposed FY29 work included remaining exterior door replacements at Turkey Hill, district security camera 
replacement, and completion of network switch replacement. 
Mr. Stevens described recurring repair and security concerns with approximately ten remaining Turkey Hill doors. 
Separate earmarked funding was discussed for an accessible entrance serving the central office. 
Technology staff reported an updated estimate of approximately $160,000 for district security cameras, distinguishing 
that work from the camera servers replaced previously. 
 
FY30 and Longer-Term Projects 
The Committee reviewed proposed classroom display replacements, bell systems, and district telephone and paging 
improvements. Technology staff recommended moving toward coordinated systems to simplify management and 
improve communication across buildings. 
Later projects included wireless access points, Turkey Hill planning and design, classroom displays, an accessibility 
lift, and roof replacement. Turkey Hill scheduling remained dependent in part on the Massachusetts School Building 
Authority process and the future of the building. 
Members requested that the Primary School roof be added to the plan and that the building’s exterior condition 
continue to be monitored. They supported maintaining a working schedule of anticipated equipment and building 
replacements. 
 
Items Below the Capital Threshold 
The Committee reviewed facilities needs below the stated $20,000 capital threshold, including a Turkey Hill hot water 
tank, a portable personnel lift, and design work for Primary School boiler replacement. 
Mr. Stevens explained that a personnel lift would allow staff to reach lighting and other equipment without repeatedly 
paying rental charges. An approximate purchase cost of $17,000 was discussed. 
Members requested that Primary School boiler planning be moved forward in the schedule. Mr. Stevens was asked to 
provide available replacement estimates and explore potential grant assistance. 
No vote was taken on the capital plan. Members were asked to provide feedback on priorities and scheduling. 
 
c. Turf Field and Retaining Walls
