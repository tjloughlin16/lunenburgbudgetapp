On behalf of the Lunenburg Public Schools and the families we serve, the Lunenburg School 
Committee is expressing our growing concern over the significant financial challenges that our 
district, and many others across Massachusetts, are facing. Despite our commitment to providing 
the highest quality education to our students, this year’s budget season has forced us to make 
painful decisions that will have lasting impacts on our schools, staff, and community. 
Lunenburg, like many municipalities, is grappling with state funding shortfalls that have led to 
unsustainable budget gaps. As a result, our district has had to make difficult cuts, including: 
 Reduction in 28.5 FTE, approximately 10% of staff. 
 Increase in class sizes in grade 5-12. 
 Reductions in administrative staff, including the restructuring of the Director of Finance 
& Administration, the elimination of an instructional specialist coach, and the removal of 
an assistant principal at LMHS. 
 Increased fees for families, including a newly implemented bus transportation fee and a 
significant rise in athletic fees.  
 Limited ability to maintain class sizes and instructional support, which may negatively 
impact student learning and teacher workload. 
We also share a broader concern about the long-term sustainability of our schools under current 
funding models. While we appreciate the state's investment in education, the increasing reliance 
on local overrides and user fees is not a viable long-term solution. We need a funding model that 
adequately supports essential student services without overburdening families or forcing school 
districts to make impossible choices. 
Additionally, the increasing costs of special education services, mandates without corresponding 
funding, and the unpredictability of state aid make it exceedingly difficult to plan responsibly for 
the future. We urge the state to consider: 
1. Increased Chapter 70 funding that reflects the true cost of educating students, particularly 
in communities that do not have a large commercial tax base. 
2. Special education funding reform to ensure districts receive adequate reimbursements for 
mandated services. 
3. A commitment to reimbursement for regional and non-regional transportation to alleviate 
the burden on towns with limited revenue sources. 
As public officials of our community’s schools, we are deeply troubled by the inequities and 
financial burdens that are being placed on students, families, and staff. We ask for your urgent 
attention to these matters. 
Thank you for your time and consideration. We look forward to working together to ensure the 
future success of all students.
