Lunenburg Public Schools is committed to providing a safe, positive, and productive educational 
environment where students can achieve the highest academic standards. No student shall be subjected to 
harassment, intimidation, bullying, or cyberbullying. 
Lunenburg Public Schools recognizes that certain students may be more vulnerable to become targets of 
bullying, harassment, or teasing based on actual or perceived characteristics, including race, color, 
religion, ancestry, national origin, sex, socioeconomic, status, homelessness, academic status, gender 
identity or expression, physical appearance, or sensory, disability, or by association with a person who has 
or is perceived to have one or more of these characteristics. The district will identify specific steps it will 
take to create a safe, supportive environment for vulnerable populations in the school community, and 
provide all students with the skills, knowledge, and strategies to prevent or respond to bullying, 
harassment, or teasing. 
This plan applies to students and members of a school staff, including, but not limited to educators, 
administrators, school nurses, cafeteria workers, custodians, bus drivers, athletic coaches, advisors to an 
extracurricular activity and paraprofessionals. 
I. Prevention And Intervention Plan 
A. History 
On May 3, 2010 Governor Patrick signed an Act Relative to Bullying in Schools. The law prohibits bullying 
and retaliation in all public and private schools, and requires schools and school districts to take certain 
steps to address bullying incidents. Chapter 86 of the Acts of 2014, amended G.L. c. 71, §37O. 
The Lunenburg Public Schools Bullying Prevention & Intervention Plan was developed in consultation 
with teachers, school staff, professional support personnel, school volunteers, administrators, community 
representatives, local law enforcement agencies, students, parents, and guardians and adopted by the 
Lunenburg School Committee in public session on December 15, 2010. Biennially, the District will involve 
representatives from each of these constituencies in aspects of Plan review. 
B. Leadership 
The Superintendent and/or his/her designee will, in consultation with district stakeholders, review and 
update the Plan at least biennially. 
The district’s School Principals and Assistant School Principals are responsible for the implementation and 
oversight of the Bullying Prevention and Intervention Plan. 
The Superintendent or designee will maintain accurate reporting contacts, links, forms, and SaferWatch 
information on the district website. Principals will communicate current procedures to students, families, 
and staff.

II. Definitions 
Aggressor is a student or a member of a school staff who engages in bullying, cyberbullying, or retaliation 
towards a student. 
Bullying is the repeated use by one or more students or school staff members of a written, verbal, or 
electronic expression, or a physical act or gesture, or any combination thereof, directed at a target that: 
• causes physical or emotional harm to the target or damage to the target’s property; 
• places the target in reasonable fear of harm to him/herself, or of damage to his/her property; 
• creates a hostile environment at school for the target; 
• infringes on the rights of the target at school; or 
• materially and substantially disrupts the education process or the orderly operation of a school.  
Cyber-bullying is bullying through the use of technology or any electronic communication, including 
writing, images, sounds, or data transmitted by electronic means, such as email, internet communications, 
and instant messages. 
Cyber-bullying also includes electronic impersonation and distribution or posting of messages where the 
statutory conditions of bullying are met. 
Bullying and cyberbullying may occur in and out of school, during and after school hours, at home and in 
locations outside of the home. 
When bullying and cyberbullying are alleged, the full cooperation and assistance of parents and families 
are expected. 
For the purposes of this policy, the term bullying will include cyberbullying. 
Hostile environment is bullying that creates sufficiently severe or pervasive intimidation, ridicule, or insult 
to alter a student’s education. 
Retaliation is intimidation, reprisal, or harassment against a student for reporting, witnessing, or 
providing information about bullying. 
School Staff includes educators, administrators, counselors, nurses, cafeteria workers, custodians, drivers, 
coaches, advisors, support staff, and paraprofessionals. 
Target is a student against whom bullying, cyberbullying, or retaliation has been perpetrated. 
Plain-language clarification: Not every disagreement or unkind action meets the legal definition of 
bullying. Schools will still address hurtful, unsafe, discriminatory, or disruptive conduct. 
III. Prohibition Against Bullying and Retaliation 
Bullying and retaliation are prohibited: 
• On school grounds; 
• On property immediately adjacent to school grounds; 
• At school-sponsored or school-related activities; 
• At functions or programs whether on or off school grounds; 
===PAGE 3===
• At school bus stops; 
• On school buses or other vehicles owned, leased or used by the school district; or 
• Through use of technology or electronic devices owned, leased or used by Lunenburg Public Schools. 
The prohibitions also apply to off-campus conduct or personal technology if it creates a hostile 
environment at school, infringes on the target’s school rights, or materially and substantially disrupts 
education or school operations. 
IV. Reporting Bullying or Retaliation 
Reports of bullying or retaliation may be made by staff, students, parents or guardians, or others,  and may 
be oral or written. Oral reports made by or to a staff member shall be recorded in writing. 
Reports by students, parents, guardians, and others who are not district staff may be anonymous. No 
formal disciplinary action shall be based solely on an anonymous report. 
The district reporting form is available on the district website. Use of a form is not a condition of making a 
report. 
The form will be distributed at the beginning of the school year, available in school offices, counseling and 
nurse offices, posted online, and offered in prevalent home languages. 
Reports may also be made by postal mail, dedicated email, or anonymous tip reporting app.

An anonymous report should, where known, identify the target, alleged aggressor, dates, times, and

Reports made in person, by phone, email, or other informal means must be documented by the receiving 
employee and sent promptly to the appropriate administrator. Families are not required to create an app 
account to make a report. 
A. Reporting by Staff 
A staff member will immediately report suspected bullying or retaliation to the principal or designee; to 
the Superintendent if the principal or assistant principal is the alleged aggressor; or to the School 
Committee if the Superintendent is the alleged aggressor. 
Staff retain their authority to respond to behavioral or disciplinary incidents consistent with applicable 
school policies and procedures. 
B. Reporting by Students, Parents or Guardians, and Others 
Students, parents, guardians, and other individuals who become aware of bullying or retaliation are 
expected to report concerns. Anonymous reporting is permitted, but disciplinary action will not be based 
solely on an anonymous report.

Students and families may seek help from staff to complete a report. Students will have safe, private, age -
appropriate options to speak with a staff member or administrator. 
If the principal or assistant principal is the alleged aggressor, the Superintendent investigates. If the 
Superintendent is the alleged aggressor, the School Committee Chair investigates. 
After investigation, appropriate notices, safety measures, disciplinary responses, and law enforcement 
referrals will be made as required. 
V. Responding to a Report of Bullying or Retaliation 
A. Safety 
Before fully investigating alleged bullying or retaliation, the principal or designee assesses the need to 
restore safety and protect the alleged target from further incidents. 
Safety measures may include an individualized safety plan, seating arrangements, identifying a safe staff 
contact, or adjusting the alleged aggressor’s schedule or access to the target. 
Additional safeguards may be implemented during and after investigation. Students who report, witness, 
or provide information about bullying must be protected against retaliation. 
The principal or designee will document the initial safety assessment and protective steps, when 
indicated. Safety measures may be revised as new information emerges and need not await a 
substantiated bullying determination. 
B. Obligations to Notify Others 
a. Notice to parents or guardians. Upon determining bullying or retaliation has occurred, the principal or 
designee will promptly notify parents or guardians of the target and aggressor and explain response 
procedures, consistent with 603 CMR 49.00. 
Parents or guardians may be contacted before the investigation when appropriate. 
When contact information is available, the administrator will ordinarily acknowledge receipt within two 
school days. This proposed timeline does not delay immediate safety actions. Families will receive updates 
on significant delays, consistent with student privacy. 
b. Notice to another school or district. When an incident involves students from multiple schools or 
districts, the first informed principal or designee will promptly notify the other school principal by 
telephone, consistent with privacy laws and 603 CMR 49.00. 
c. Notice to law enforcement. The principal or designee will notify local law enforcement when there is a 
reasonable basis to believe criminal charges may be pursued, consistent with the law, regulations, and 
local agreements. 
The same provisions concerning former students younger than 21 will apply as set out in the district plan. 
The principal may consult a school resource officer and other appropriate persons. 
VI. Investigation 
The School Principal and/or Assistant School Principal shall promptly investigate reports of bullying. 
Investigation may include interviewing the alleged target, alleged aggressor, staff members, students, and 
witnesses. 
===PAGE 5===
Investigations must be consistent with applicable school procedures. Confidentiality will be maintained to 
the extent possible for persons reporting or sharing information. 
The principal or assistant principal shall provide status updates to involved students and/or their parents 
or guardians. 
The administrator will document the report date, evidence considered, interviews, safety measures, 
determination, family contacts, and planned follow-up. Families will receive reasonable updates when an 
investigation takes longer than anticipated, consistent with privacy requirements. 
VII. Determination 
The principal or designee shall make a determination based on all facts and circumstances. If bullying or 
retaliation is substantiated, the principal or designee will take steps reasonably calculated to prevent 
recurrence and prevent restriction of the target’s participation in school. 
The principal or designee will determine necessary remedial, responsive, and disciplinary action 
consistent with district disciplinary policies. 
Knowingly false allegations may result in student disciplinary action consistent with school policy. 
The parent or guardian of the target shall receive information about the Department of Elementary and 
Secondary Education Problem Resolution System regardless of the determination. 
Administrators, counselors, nurses, and teachers will continue to develop support plans for targets, 
aggressors, and appropriate family members, including behavior plans, social-skills instruction, and 
counseling where appropriate. 
Families will receive an explanation of the finding and any safety or support measures relevant to their 
own child, without disclosing confidential information about another student. 
When reported conduct does not meet the statutory bullying definition, school staff will still address 
substantiated harmful or disruptive behavior under applicable district rules. 
Ordinarily within ten school days of the determination, the school will complete a documented check-in 
with affected students regarding safety, recurrence, retaliation, and need for further support. This is a 
proposed operational timeline. 
VIII. Resources 
Lunenburg Public Schools shall provide counseling or referrals to appropriate services, including 
guidance, academic intervention, and protection to students affected by bullying, including targets and 
aggressors, as necessary. 
Each year the District shall inform parents or guardians of the anti-bullying curricula in use, including 
information about bullying dynamics, cyberbullying, and online safety. The Plan and related information 
shall be posted online. 
Available support may also include individualized check-ins, behavior interventions, social-skills 
instruction, and support to witnesses or affected families where appropriate. Staff will review whether 
supports are effective.

IX. Training And Assessment 
Annual training will be provided to employees and volunteers with significant student contact on 
preventing, identifying, responding to, and reporting bullying. 
A. Notice and Training 
Annual written notice of the Plan will be provided to staff through school or district handbooks and codes 
of conduct, including staff duties and bullying of students by staff members. 
Age-appropriate, evidence-based bullying prevention instruction will be incorporated into student 
curricula. 
Professional development informed by research will address: 
• age-appropriate strategies to prevent bullying; 
• immediate and effective interventions to stop bullying; 
• the complex interaction and power differential among aggressors, targets, and witnesses; 
• research about bullying and students at heightened risk; 
• the incidence and nature of cyberbullying; 
• internet safety related to cyberbullying. 
School or district leaders may identify other relevant professional development topics. 
Annual communications and training will include plain-language examples distinguishing bullying, 
retaliation, peer conflict, and other harmful behavior, together with clear reporting steps and the duty to 
address all concerning conduct. 
B. Assessment 
Lunenburg Public Schools will collect and report to DESE required data, including allegations, 
substantiated incidents, students disciplined, and other information required by the Department. 
Periodic student surveys will assess school climate and the prevalence and nature of bullying. Reporting 
and survey data will inform revisions to the Plan. 
During each biennial review, leadership will consider available student, family, and staff feedback and 
aggregate reporting and follow-up data while protecting student confidentiality. 
X. Problem Resolution System 
Any parent wishing to file a claim or concern or seeking assistance outside of the district may contact the 
Massachusetts Department of Elementary and Secondary Education Problem Resolution System (PRS). 
Website: https://www.doe.mass.edu/prs/ | Email: compliance@doe.mass.edu | Telephone: 781-338-
3700. Hard copies are available at the Superintendent’s Office. 
XI. Relationship To Other Laws 
Consistent with state and federal law, no person shall be discriminated against in public school admission, 
privileges, or courses of study because of protected characteristics, including race, color, religion, ancestry, 
national origin, sex, socioeconomic status, academic status, gender identity or expression, physical 
appearance, sexual orientation, or disability. 
===PAGE 7===
Nothing in this Plan restricts the school or district from taking action to remedy discrimination or 
harassment under local, state, or federal laws or district policies. All students shall receive the same 
protection regardless of status under law. 
Nothing in the Plan limits discipline or other action under M.G.L. c. 71 §§37H, 37H½, 41 or 42; M.G.L. c. 76 
§5; other applicable laws; local policies; or collective bargaining agreements for harmful, violent, or 
disruptive conduct.
