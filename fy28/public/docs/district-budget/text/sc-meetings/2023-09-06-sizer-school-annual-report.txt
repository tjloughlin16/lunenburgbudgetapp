School Performance and Program Implementation 
 
 
 
 
 
Mission and Key Design Elements 
  
The mission of Sizer School is to be a school where students are known personally, challenged 
intellectually, and participate actively in their learning. Guided by its commitment to diversity and 
inclusiveness, the school seeks to send graduates into the world who THINK for themselves, CARE about 
others, and ACT creatively and responsibly. The key design elements of the school are the Common 
Principles of The Coalition of Essential Schools: Learning to use one’s mind well; Less is more: depth over 
coverage; Goals apply to all students; Personalization; Student-as-worker, teacher-as-coach; 
Demonstration of mastery; A tone of decency and trust; Commitment to the entire school; Resources 
dedicated to teaching and learning; and Democracy and equity (see Appendix E). Below are details of 
how Sizer implemented each of these design elements during the 2020-21 school year. 
  
Learning to use one’s mind well 
Sizer leveraged the opportunities presented by the necessary blend of remote, hybrid and in-person 
learning to focus on this personal growth design element. Beginning, middle, and end-of-year Advisory 
Meetings focused on student-identified goals related to being a learner in this unique environment. 
During these meetings, students presented their goals to advisors and parents/guardians, and 
developed individual plans to meet those goals throughout the school year. The end-of-year meetings 
focused on reflection and setting learning goals for the coming school year.   
  
Less is more: depth over coverage 
Ongoing development and editing of Sizer’s curriculum was a significant focus during the 2020-21 school 
year. Given the abrupt move to digital learning and shift to entirely Google Classroom-based instruction, 
all teachers had to adapt their curriculum to be delivered virtually. This began with each teacher 
identifying the most critical content. In essence, the entire year was an exercise in narrowing down what 
was most important to learn and going deep on this knowledge and these skills. 
  
Goals apply to all students 
As part of the professional development at the beginning of the school year, all teachers established 
universal learning goals and routines for their Google Classrooms. Many conversations were had during 
Common Planning Time and academic team meetings about setting dynamic goals for all students that 
integrated the challenges of learning through the pandemic and also held expectations for students that 
honored the established learning goals. All Seniors completed and presented their Senior Projects, and 
students in grades 7-11 worked toward the successful completion their learning goals. 
  
Personalization 
Advisory Meetings continued to be tailored to each student’s progress and circumstances. Senior 
Projects were modified to students’ strengths and challenges with those students most in need due to 
the pandemic given choice in how to complete and present their projects. Teachers worked with 
administrators to identify pathways to Just Beginning (JB), Sizer’s minimum passing grade, in courses 
students were struggling to pass, selecting a highly-focused set of core goals to be completed. Tutors 
were hired to support students in completing their individual pathways.

Student-as-worker, teacher-as-coach 
Given that much of the student learning took place remotely during the 2020-21 school year, it was 
necessary that students took up more self-direction as learners. Teachers developed and deepened their 
skills as digital learning coaches. Depending on the level of student need and engagement, teachers 
offered Google Classroom breakout rooms, individual Google Meets help sessions, and other 
communication formats such as Google Voice calls and texting. 
  
Demonstration of mastery 
As has been the case in previous years, mastery was demonstrated by Sizer students through diligent 
work completion, consistent attendance, and end-of-year exhibitions. Those exhibitions included both 
Senior Projects and end-of-year Advisory presentations.  
  
A tone of decency and trust 
Sizer prioritized the delivery of COVID-19 relevant training, support, and supervision. Teachers were 
empowered to make decisions related to classroom content and the structuring of their Google 
Classrooms. Students, families, teachers, and staff had access to a wide range of individualized 
pandemic-related supports, including tutors, one-to-one support, Return-to-Work Plans and Return-to- 
Building Plans. Several family and staff forums were conducted to afford all groups an opportunity to ask 
questions and voice concerns. 
  
Commitment to the entire school 
All decisions regarding transition from hybrid to remote learning as well as the eventual return to full-
time, in-person instruction were made with a commitment to the entire school. Family and staff forums, 
as referenced above, allowed all voices a chance to be heard. Feedback was always carefully combined 
with best practices recommended by the Fitchburg Board of Health, the Centers for Disease Control and 
Prevention (CDC), and the Department of Elementary and Secondary Education (DESE). Proudly, these 
safety measures resulted in zero cases of in-school COVID-19 transmission. While health and safety was 
certainly paramount during the 2020-21 school year, Sizer balanced those needs with the necessity of 
consistent teaching and learning, and supporting our community.  
  
Resources dedicated to teaching and learning 
This principle took on much greater significance during the pandemic as teachers pivoted curriculum 
and instruction to meet the needs of a hybrid learning environment with both remote and in-person 
students simultaneously learning. The professional development schedule was crafted to focus primarily 
on teaching and learning with other planned topics minimized. Sizer developed a process, led by the 
Director of Development and Communication, which succinctly leveraged federal and state funding to 
deliver teaching resources and supplies to educators striving to meet new and different learning needs. 
  
Democracy and equity 
As detailed below in the Access and Equity section, Sizer formed a team dedicated to leading the 
diversity, belonging, inclusion, and equity (DBIE) work at the school. It is the intention of this group to 
thoughtfully implement systemic changes within the school community. Throughout the year, Sizer 
demonstrated a commitment to democracy and equity by surveying and responding to diverse school 
community needs, including but not limited to scheduling, technology, food, and transportation. The 
Student Services Team addressed the diversity of Sizer Students by continuing to deliberately build a 
sense of belonging through regular check-ins with all students, including personalized visits for remote 
learners and their families as they supported learning environments at home.

Access and Equity: Discipline Data 
 
Sizer School is committed to the goal of reducing disciplinary rates overall and to have gaps between 
subgroups reduced by at least half by June 2024 as outlined in our strategic plan overview (see Appendix 
G). Though the pandemic created obstacles throughout the 2020-21 school year, Sizer School continued 
to make progress on reducing the use of out-of-school suspensions overall, in particular for 
Hispanic/Latino students. Sizer staff have participated in the Rethinking Discipline initiative led by DESE 
and completed a number of professional development opportunities, including but not limited 
to: Cultivating a Culture of Caring; From Safe Space to Brave Space: Facilitating Classroom Discussions on 
Bias, Injustice, and Oppression; Relationship Mapping to Support Social-Emotional Well-Being; and 
Understanding Trauma and Trauma-Sensitive Schools in the midst of COVID-19. 
 
Building on the professional development work done throughout the 2020-21 school year, Sizer plans to 
implement systemic and systematic changes and respond to disciplinary issues “in the moment.” For the 
2021-22 school year, Sizer has hired a new Assistant Principal. The addition of the Assistant Principal will 
allow for a more dedicated focus on student services, such as leading and directly supervising the 
Student Services Team (SST), Dean of School Culture, and other staff as appropriate and ensuring 
continued positive growth. The Assistant Principal role will also continue to develop school-wide policies 
and procedures related to the operation of a safe and welcoming school, including the maintenance of a 
comprehensive Student and Families Handbook. This position will oversee the creation and ongoing 
support of a Student Council, which will be utilized to create an avenue for student voice to make 
meaningful changes to our disciplinary processes, policies, and procedures. Sizer continues to 
implement its system of restorative justice for more serious behavioral offenses. Examples of these 
practices include restorative conferencing, mediation, and restitution.  
 
Sizer has moved its peer mediation program entirely in-house with support from an outside provider. 
This was the third year of this program living solely in our school. This has allowed frequent access to 
mediation services and more consistency in the use of mediation. Through our partnership with 
Mediation Services of North Central Mass, Inc. (MSI), we were also able to provide an 18-hour training 
during the 2020-2021 school year for 12 students on in-depth conflict resolution and communication 
skills to help resolve peer-to-peer conflicts within our school. Though the pandemic caused 
complications with holding effective mediations, Sizer will continue to use peer meditation as a tool for 
addressing disciplinary issues and conflict resolution. 
Going into the 2021-22 school year, Sizer has built a comprehensive plan to continue to reduce 
disparities among subgroups for better equity at our school. We will continue to conduct classroom 
observations to gather more data about any potential disproportionality in the frequency and severity of 
classroom-based disciplinary practices; to identify key levers and areas for continued professional 
development and growth around equity and cultural competence; to monitor and respond to discipline 
data and review discipline practice as it relates to overall disparities; and to increase efficiency of Tier 1, 
2, and 3 interventions by clarifying processes and tools for progress monitoring, including use of internal

Sizer School supports the achievement of all students and is committed to educating students who are 
academically skilled and prepared for the ever-changing demands of postsecondary education and the 
21st-century workplace. During the 2020-21 school year, Sizer continued to utilize iReady assessments 
for diagnostic purposes and analyzed the data and program trends to support individual growth and 
assess areas of need that may have arisen due to the COVID-19 pandemic. The Math department used 
iExcel, both for instructional and diagnostic assessment purposes. Results were utilized to identify 
learning gaps, tutoring possibilities, course placements, and intervention entry points for each student. 
 
In addition to these two diagnostic tools, teachers implemented a range of formative and summative 
assessments to measure student learning and academic progress. These included entry and exits tickets, 
Socratic seminars, assignments rubrics, journal entries, portfolios, focused classroom literacy practices 
(e.g., close reading, guided text annotation and analysis), math labs, student debates, short essay 
assignments, research papers, and classroom observations. Teachers continuously monitored student 
learning through Google Classroom and in-person and virtual classes and meetings. Student academic 
progress was measured through the completion of assignments as well as the engaged presence of 
students. As students signed into Google Classroom each day, they demonstrated their learning through 
activities like the effective checking of primary, secondary, and tertiary source credibility, creating and 
performing yoga and dance routines, contributing to productive class discussions, applying their 
knowledge across topics and content areas, presenting their work to a range of audiences, correctly 
solving math problems, using data as evidence in making claims, and persuasively writing to assert an 
argument through extended essay projects. While the pandemic presented extensive challenges, Sizer 
adapted its academic program to meet the needs of students and to ensure their academic progress.

Academic Program    
 
The primary focus of the academic program for the 2020-2021 school year was to design and deliver 
robust and dynamic digital curriculum and instruction. The philosophy of the curriculum creation 
process was consistent with Sizer’s essential school common principle of depth over breadth. Teachers 
worked on identifying the most essential content of their courses and building their remote instruction 
and assessments around that content while also building their online and blended teaching and learning 
capacity. In anticipation that the school might need to move between learning models (in-person, 
remote, and hybrid), teachers prepared for each of the three models from the beginning of the school 
year. All students were given Chromebooks, hotspots, and other technologies as needed to ensure 
access to the academic program through all of the changes in learning models throughout the year. 
 
Sizer School began the 2020-21 school year in a full-time, remote learning model. After being remote for 
the beginning of the school year, Sizer brought students back to the school utilizing a hybrid model with 
students learning both in person and at home in the late fall. Due to a regional COVID-19 outbreak, the 
school shifted back to all remote in late winter and stayed remote until the beginning of April 2021 with 
a return to a hybrid model. Parents were surveyed, and students had the choice of returning to in-
person learning or remaining fully remote learners. Schedules were designed so in-person and remote 
learners could synchronously access their classes. Sizer School moved to full, in-person instruction (5-
day model) at the end of April. Families were again surveyed and given the choice to select in-person 
instruction or to remain in full remote learning status for the remainder of the school year.   
 
Advanced Placement and Extended Coursework  
Sizer students continued to take advantage of advanced coursework during the 2020-21 school year, 
participating in early college high school, dual enrollment, and AP classes. Schedules were coordinated 
with Fitchburg State University to ensure that students could fit their coursework into their schedule 
without conflict. AP courses were offered in US History, English Literature & Composition, and Biology.  
 
Access, Equity, and COVID-19 Protocols 
The Sizer School leadership team met continuously and collaboratively in the summer prior to the 
opening of the 2020-21 school year to generate a comprehensive safety plan for the community. The 
plan included several versions to address safety needs in a fully remote, blended, and fully in-person 
learning model. Staff analyzed the building, measuring each space to determine both the 3-foot and 6-
foot distance maximum number of students. New procedures were created for entering and exiting the 
building, eating lunch, transitioning to and from classes, cleaning of spaces, and accessing the Nurse. 
Sizer established a Nurse’s annex space and implemented hand hygiene, physical distancing, and 
masking for everybody in the building. School leadership established highly-structured transition 
routines with increased staff supervision to ensure order and distancing. Lunch service was moved to 
larger spaces to ensure 6-foot social distancing, and we coordinated food orders with Fitchburg Public 
Schools, providing prepackaged food and drink items for students. Administration carefully followed the 
COVID- 19 data, as provided by the Commonwealth, to monitor positivity rates in the region to inform 
decisions about school safety.

Supports for Diverse Learners 
Fostering a sense of belonging and partnership among students and families, Sizer utilizes a multifaceted 
approach and a holistic understanding of the unique learning profiles of students to identify and 
implement instructional strategies that are explicitly constructed and highly-scaffolded. These strategies 
support students who benefit from specially-designed instruction, expertly crafted accommodations to 
increase access to instruction, increased access to focused academic coaching, targeted skill 
development in organization and executive functioning, opportunities for peer collaboration, mindful 
attention to the development of self-advocacy and increasing independence, and compassionate 
responses to those who struggle with emotional and behavioral challenges. Learning specialists partner 
with general education teachers to inform the development of learning goals and instruction with a 
clear understanding of unique student needs based on data and research and integrate and implement 
best practices for all learning needs in an inclusion model. Paraprofessionals form the foundation of the 
learning specialist-general education teacher bridge to provide specific academic and behavioral support 
and coaching. Within the inclusion setting, Sizer utilizes academic support and coaching classes, math 
and reading interventions, targeted accommodations, careful attention to building effective 
relationships with students and families, and clear communication and collaboration with related service 
providers to provide successful access to challenging curriculum and set high expectations for every 
student.  
 
During the past year, Sizer staff approached the challenges of mastering a virtual instructional platform 
with careful attention to the impact of remote learning on all learners. The robust partnership between 
instructional staff, special education staff, and student services staff established a crucial structure for 
quick identification of student needs unique to the pandemic. Weekly student concerns meetings for 
each grade level included a holistic team of general education teachers, learning specialists, 
administrators, clinicians, and the school nurse. Problems that were quickly identified and addressed 
included access to technology, skill development in utilizing a remote learning platform, emerging 
mental health issues, basic life needs, and how to provide meaningful accommodations. Multi-pronged 
strategies with ongoing follow-up communication and documentation of what was attempted and what 
worked became the established practice. 
 
Teachers, learning specialists, and paraprofessionals developed innovative breakout rooms that 
provided inclusive peer groups the opportunity to focus on specific academic tasks or concepts. Google 
Classrooms were customized to meet the needs of individual students. All staff were cross-trained in 
both the virtual instructional platform and in recognizing the social emotional impact of the pandemic. 
High-needs students were identified and provided with a range of intensive and strategic opportunities 
to connect and access instruction, including one-to-one mentoring and in-person instructional support. 
Clinicians provided a key component in outreach and support to families and in supporting instructional 
staff in knowing the most effective way to remain connected to students and families. Thoughtful 
planning of building-wide tasks that became exponentially more detailed due to the demands of social 
distancing and safety protocols created an opportunity for administrative and clinical staff to connect

with students during lunch or morning check-ins. The relationships established and connections made 
were invaluable in building partnerships between staff and families and in understanding more fully the

emotion paved the way for creative problem solving focused on the whole student.  
 
From the recognition of the need for an effective remote learning platform, Sizer intentionally informed 
its pedagogical practices with an increasing awareness of the holistic needs of students and the 
importance of honoring the impact of pandemic related events and situations on students and families. 
The lessons of this year have opened the door to deepening the practice of inclusivity and developing 
the skill sets of all staff to recognize and address unique student needs and target the acceleration of 
learning effectively.  Throughout this past year, collaboration, communication, and compassion have 
been the hallmarks for working together in service to students and have had a subtle but substantial 
transformative impact on continuing the mission to see, understand, and engage every student.

Organizational Structure of the School 
 
During the 2020-21 school year, the Executive Director/Principal position returned to being two 
separate positions. Dr. Michelle Paranto started in her role as Executive Director on July 1st, and 
Frederick Randall served as Principal. The rest of the organizational structure of the school did not

School Name: Sizer School, a North Central Charter Essential School 
 
2020-21 Implementation Summary 
1. The COVID-19 pandemic dramatically affected Sizer School’s Recruitment and Communication plan 
for the 2020-21 school year. Federal, state, and local guidelines for dealing with the COVID-19 
pandemic limited some communication strategies and in many cases prohibited them altogether 
(e.g., attending/holding public events, in-person tours/meetings and community networking). These 
limitations impacted 7th grade enrollment numbers. By the March 1, 2021 enrollment deadline, only 
38 students confirmed seats in the 7th grade for the 2021-22 school year. Typical enrollment years 
result in between 60-75 7th grade applicants. There was not a marked increase or decrease in 8th and 
9th grade applicants. 
2. Throughout January and February, Recruitment and Communication Coordinator, Lindsay 
Sauvageau, networked with local charters through the Massachusetts Charter School Association 
(MCPSA) via Google Meets and Zoom, sharing best practices. Most of the recruitment strategies 
shared during those meetings proved to positively impact recruitment. A 7th grade Backfill Lottery was 
reopened on 4/1/21. Applications were accepted until 6/1/21, and a lottery was held on 6/3/21; 27 
7th grade applicants accepted for fall 2021. 
Of the 65 total accepted 7th grade students, 13 families identified their students as English Language 
Learners with a majority speaking Spanish as a first language. Other ELL students identified as 
speaking Portuguese or Haitian Creole. Between the Primary Lottery in March and the Backfill Lottery 
in June, Sizer accepted 16 9th grade students and 4 families identified their students as English 
Language Learners, all Spanish-speaking. 
3. Sizer’s efforts to stay connected with Sizer families proved successful this year despite the COVID-
19 pandemic. Of the 86 total accepted students for the 2021-22 school year in grades 7, 8, and 9, 
twenty-three had siblings attending Sizer School. Demographic data provided by families in the 
enrollment process indicate that our incoming class of students as captured in the October 1, 2020 
SIMS reports will meet the comparison index or school’s gap narrowing targets. Further discussion 
with the Department is not needed at this time.

General Recruitment Activities for 2020-21 
 
Advertisement Runs 
Sizer School put two advertising banners on MART public transportation buses from December-
March. This bus traveled throughout the tri-city area covering Leominster, Fitchburg, and Gardner. 
An advertisement for Sizer School was featured in all Coffee News publications distributed online and 
in local businesses throughout the North Central Mass area. 
NEW – Sizer School began running advertisements on Facebook and Instagram. Locally targeted ads 
(to reach eligible families within all sending districts) were run on both platforms from January 
through March. 
 
Public Access Cable 
Sizer School will utilize the Fitchburg Public Access Channel and the Leominster Public Access Channel 
to promote Sizer School events and other Sizer School announcements and enrollment deadlines. 
 
Networking 
Sizer School was not able to hold or participate in any public events (none available due to COVID-19), 
nor any in-person networking opportunities (none available due to COVID-19). In January and 
February, through Google Meets and Zoom, Lindsay Sauvageau met with local charters through the 
Massachusetts Charter School Association (MCPSA) to share best practices and recruitment 
strategies.  
 
Information Sessions 
Sauvageau held one information session through Google Meets every month from January through 
July. These information sessions were also recorded and made available on YouTube and Facebook. 
All information provided was made available in English and Spanish.  
NEW - Sizer School: LIVE! 
In addition to online and recorded information sessions, Sizer School launched Sizer School: LIVE!, a 
series of 10 LIVE online Facebook events that featured staff interviews (Principal, Executive Director, 
School Nurse, Facilities Director, Athletics Director, etc.), COVID-19 updates, building tours and more. 
These videos were aired LIVE on Facebook to all followers and then shared with local community 
Facebook pages like Discussing Fitchburg, Leominsterites United, as well as local pages that serve 
Townsend, Ashby, Ashburnham, Westminster, Gardner, Lunenburg, etc. 
  
Promotional Materials & Signage 
Sizer School continued the use of lawn signs as a marketing tool. Sizer School signs were placed in 
high-traffic areas and in front of family homes throughout Lunenburg, Fitchburg, Leominster, 
Lancaster, Gardner, Ashburnham and Westminster. 
  
News Media Presence 
Sizer School utilized local newspapers in order to stay connected with the community and keep the 
Sizer community informed. Sizer School press releases were featured in local newspapers at least 
twice a month (often more) in at least one of Sizer’s sending districts as well as the Sentinel & 
Enterprise and Worcester Telegram, both of which provide news coverage to all sending districts.

Social Media Presence 
Social Media has been Sizer’s greatest asset for reaching Sizer families and our sending districts 
during the COVID-19 pandemic. Sizer increased use of its Facebook, Instagram, and Twitter pages to 
reach the public and share school updates.  
As many students and their families use Instagram to stay connected, this platform was used to 
promote student achievements, community involvement, and educational opportunities. Virtual and 
hybrid learning, inside and outside of the classrooms, were especially highlighted.  
Any information that could be recorded and shared via YouTube was transferred to this easily 
sharable format. Teacher introductions for new students were recorded, as well as Nurse’s Corner 
videos with COVID-19 updates, and all COVID-19 policies and procedures were explained in video.  
This year, Sizer School continued to use its TikTok account to highlight student achievements and 
moments of success inside and outside of the classrooms; it was also used to make engaging and 
informative videos to share on all other social media platforms. 
  
School Website 
Sizer School’s newly redesigned website was heavily utilized this year as a direct link for all families to 
get access to all of the information shared above as well as all COVID-19 communications and 
updates.  
NEW – Community Engagement Enrichment Opportunities 
Despite the limitations that COVID-19 put on in-person learning and community engagement, three 
new Sizer Clubs were started, two of which directly worked with outside community groups. 
Sound & Vision was started as Sizer’s first Audio Visual Club. It was run in partnership with Fitchburg 
Access Television. Students were able to virtually meet weekly to learn audio/visual basics and ended 
the year by helping FATV set up the AV portion of the Class of 2021 Graduation Ceremony in June. 
Lindsay Sauvageau and FATV’s Education Director, Jarrod Roberts, led the club. 
An environmental club was established mid-year named Reclaiming Sunshine. Its project for the year 
was working with a local nonprofit group, Growing Places, to learn basic gardening and grow 
seedlings for sale during local farmer’s markets in the area. Growing Places is a nonprofit dedicated to 
expanding food access and supporting sustainable farming throughout the area. Growing Places 
Education Director, Kyle Anderson, worked with students to show them how to grow and care for 
plants and then how to connect with other local farmers to share products and practices.

2021-2022 
  
Please provide a brief narrative report on the successes and challenges of implementing strategies from the 
2020-21 Retention Plan.  
Sizer School examines retention data on an ongoing basis utilizing various teams and departments within 
the school, including student services, Executive Director’s office, recruitment office, and instructional 
teams. From our 2020-21 retention plan, Sizer has successfully maintained the following: 
o Small class size and overall student load: Most teachers at Sizer teach between 60 and 85 students 
per day with most class sizes ranging from 13-16. This allows for differentiation and knowing 
students’ needs. Additionally, Sizer is focusing on strategies to increase student engagement, 
diversifying the ways students learn, discuss, and demonstrate an understanding of content and 
skills. 
o Early warning indicators: Sizer tracks student course performance, attendance, tardiness, and 
behavior and identifies students who may be at risk. The Student Services Team (SST) guides 
teacher teams in identifying successful strategies, following student progress, and coordinating or 
providing additional services as needed. 
o Knowing each student well: Sizer has a strong advisory system as a building block of culture, 
communication, and relationships. Advisors are the first and strongest conduit for families and 
students to connect with Sizer, monitor progress, and address needs. Advisors meet with students 
and family/guardians/caregivers three times per year. These meetings are used to establish 
relationships, share and develop student goals, and provide opportunities for greater 
communication. 
o A tiered system of support: Sizer offers differentiated instructional approaches for the whole class, 
small group, and individualized instruction. The support network includes learning specialists, a 
literacy specialist, a math specialist, an academic tutoring class, and other courses designed to meet 
student needs. 
===PAGE 32===
32 | P a g e  
 
o Collaborative team effort: Sizer works as a team to make sure every student is getting the best 
resources that we can offer them. Teachers, counselors, administrators, and support staff come 
together and work collectively to create plans for students to make sure their academic, social, 
emotional, and behavioral needs are being met consistently. Student services staff reach out to 
families when concerns arise and work with them to provide services. Staff collaborate with outside 
resources to include You, Inc., Community HealthLink, Department of Children and Families, MOC, 
Gandara Center, United Way, Our Father's House, Ginny's Food Pantry, Youth Mobile Crisis, the 
SHINE Initiative, Sandy Hook Promise, Seven Hills, Department of Transitional Assistance, 
Community Health Connections, and the Spanish-American Center. Sizer staff’s commitment to 
teamwork and sharing best practices helps us ensure that all students are achieving success 
throughout their day. 
o Expanding programs to meet student needs: Sizer has seen promising support for our Tier 3 
students. Sizer administration, counseling, and teaching staff focused on building the skills essential 
to facilitating successful participation in general education classrooms for students through the 
Bridges resource. Bridges staff worked closely with general education teachers to support students 
in the classroom with creative scheduling, targeted skill development, managing classroom 
expectations, and coaching students in the general education classroom. During the pandemic, 
Bridges staff members were able to offer additional support to a wider range of students, offering 
additional resources that typically would not happen within a different school year. In 2021-22, Sizer 
will continue the evolving conversation on the Bridges resource on how to expand upon the success 
with the program and will focus on targeting naturally occurring social groups and collaborative 
learning opportunities that can be built into the design of the school day. 
Moving into the 2021-22 school year, Sizer anticipates an increased focus on enhancing and improving 
what Sizer does well to continue to make progress on retention within our school. After improvements 
in retention for the 2017-18 and the 2018-19 school years, Sizer saw an increase of 6 percent for the 
2019-20 school year. Most of this increase came from our 8th and 9th grades. For the 2020-21 school 
year, Sizer saw a decrease of 6.4 percent with an overall attrition rate of 11.7 compared to 18.1 percent 
from the year prior. The 2020-21 attrition rate will be the lowest it has been in the last 5 years. Students 
identified as High Needs decreased from 16.1 to 9.6 over the course of this school year, and Low Income 
from 17.6 to 7.9. Students with disabilities also saw a minor decrease from 11.8 to 10.4.  
Though these numbers are still above state average, and the pandemic has created complications to 
what would reflect an accurate school year, Sizer will use data to inform decisions and practices for the 
2021-22 school year to continue attrition rates meeting state averages.

Conditions 
 
On February 16, 2017, the MA Department of Secondary and Elementary Education (DESE) 
Commissioner renewed the Sizer School charter with one condition:   
 
Condition: 
As a result of enrolling more than 20 percent of its total enrollment from school districts not included in 
its original charter for 2 consecutive years, Sizer shall submit the following: 
 By June 1, 2017, Sizer must send the Department an action plan regarding its enrollment pattern 
from school districts not included in its original charter. 
o Should the school determine to maintain its current region, by August 2017, Sizer must 
submit for Department approval a plan that ensures that enrollment from districts 
outside of the school’s region does not exceed 20 percent of its total population by June 
30, 2021, and corresponding charter amendment requests for the school’s maximum 
enrollment, enrollment policy, and any other relevant material terms. 
o Should the school wish to add districts to its current region, the school must submit an 
amendment request by August 1, 2021.

 The recruitment and retention plans that the school submits on August 1 in 2017 through 2021 
must address how the school intends to increase recruitment efforts within the region specified 
in the school’s charter. 
 
Progress Update 
Since 2018, Sizer has not seen a recurrence of two years in a row of students attending Sizer at a rate 
above 20% out of chartered districts. Sizer will continue to monitor outside attendance percentages 
each year of the charter term, and if two consecutive years occur again, Sizer will consider adding to its 
list of chartered towns.
