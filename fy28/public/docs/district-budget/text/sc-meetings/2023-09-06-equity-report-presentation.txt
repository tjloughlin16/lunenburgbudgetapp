Bullying is identified in the report. However - the authors’ main 
focus was on the LGBTQ+ community. Does this take away from 
the bullying that takes place daily regarding students who do not 
fall into one of the authors’ special categories. 
No, it does not.
Can we have the raw data?
Response: No. TEP does not share the raw data. The student 
group populations for students who identified as BIPOC have very 
low numbers. So low that TEP did not feel safe sharing race 
identifiable information. One might be able to assume (simple 
assumption based on skin color) which students represent which 
data points. TEP’s goal is to help all students feel safe as 
a community and as individuals, and as a race.

The author referenced negative impact caused by 
traditions. Would that be kids that don’t feel welcome during the 
float building? Something like that? 
Response: Yes. The consensus, especially based on open 
feedback, is that because you are a small district, floats are 
sometimes difficult for the newly incoming students/families 
regardless of race, to connect to. However, it was not just the 
newly incoming students and families, but also those that may 
have no connections to sports or after school activities.
Were the auditors privy to free lunch info by student? In general?
Free/reduced lunch info was never provided to the equity 
auditors.

Were the auditors CORI’d?
The auditors were not CORI’d.  They did not have 
unmonitored access to students. They were accompanied by 
building and district administrators.
When the auditors spoke to students/staff about discipline and 
bullying came up, do we know if the legal definition of bullying 
was used?
Response: Bullying came up from your community. TEP 
didn’t have questions centered on bullying. Therefore it would 
be fair to say that it was NOT the legal definition. Chances are it 
is the community’s interpretation of bullying (aka repeated 
unkindness or insensitivity).

Does the consultant know of a high performing high school 
that creates a sense of belonging and warmth that we could 
visit?
Response: I have yet to see a school or district that has it 
down to a science; one that is operating with fidelity. 
Several MA Districts are working hard to do this heart-
work. They are consistently trying and have policies and 
consequences to support their efforts. They are a work in 
progress: Lynnfield Public Schools, Sudbury Public Schools, 
Watertown Public Schools 
Outside of Massachusetts there is a private Catholic school, 
Ursuline Academy in Wilmington, DE.

Does the consultant have suggestions for modifying our PD Committee to add 
more teacher voice?
Response: Put out a survey to teachers and ask them for their input 
directly. (We already do this survey annually)
Are there any specifics from families regarding communication issues and at 
what level, teacher/school/district/School Committee?
Response: Some of the communication statements came as a result of 
digging deeper into the website translation issue and understanding the lack of 
access that EL families may face. Handbooks are not readily available in hard 
copies in school offices.  
Some staff reported on the need for improved communication. Some of 
it is due to other’s perceptions and their own interpretations of what they want 
rather than what was said by leadership. The leadership team can state and be 
clear on what communication will look like in the District. Then offer the 
opportunity for staff to offer feedback.
