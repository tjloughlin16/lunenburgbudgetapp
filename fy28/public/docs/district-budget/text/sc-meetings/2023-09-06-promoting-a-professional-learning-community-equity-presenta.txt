History of Course Work in District  
12 years ago Karen Martin and Superintendent Calmes hired me to develop professional 
development courses focusing on: Student Driven Learning, Community Service 
Learning, Social Emotional Learning, and Executive Functioning. This laid the 
groundwork for a Professional Learning Community that became committed to 
integrating impactful projects.
2018 - Dr. Burnham’s brought her commitment and enthusiasm for professional 
development to our school community and her vision of access and equity for all 
created a focused direction.  
The overarching intention of the courses since Dr. Burnham’s arrival is Building 
Cultural Competence and a Culture of Care.
First Professional Development Day was an event that included speakers that 
represented diverse cultures and experiences that was enthusiastically attended by 
educators. 
Since that time, Dr. Burnham has supported the continuation of building a 
Professional Learning Community to support the integration of neurodiverse and 
culturally responsive practice supporting the development of courses to address 
district needs.

Cultural Competence Related to Education 
Culturally Relevant Pedagogy (CRP) is a philosophical 
outlook towards one’s approach to teaching that informs 
the what, the how, and the why. CRP focuses on the 
academic and personal success of students as 
individuals and as a collective. It ensures students 
engage in academically rigorous curriculum and 
learning, feel affirmed in their identities and 
experiences, and develop the knowledge and skills to 
engage the world and others critically. 
Culturally Relevant Pedagogy was coined by Gloria 
Ladson Billings in 1993.

The goal of this course is to learn how to best create transformative PLC’s that have 
the power to enhance educational systems and cultural competence. Through the use of a 
staff empowerment model, participants will learn how to create and implement impact 
projects that create change in cultural mindset and equitable practice.
The session will demonstrate a Mindful Integrative Process that requires each 
participant to pause and reflect upon their thoughts, feelings and ideas related to a 
culture of care and equity for all stakeholders.
Through this reflective practice, participants will glean insights with the intention 
of integrating those insights into meaningful, purposeful tasks and transformative 
projects. We will utilize constructive conversations and dynamic dialogues to deepen 
our understanding and to strengthen our practice.
The creation and integration of participant-driven impact projects related to specific 
populations served within the district has the intention of transforming cultural 
mindsets that ultimately lead to the progression of equitable practice. Participants 
will leave the workshop with an innovative process and unique product that will 
generate immediate systemic change related to equity and a culture of care.

We know how it feels when 
administrators, colleagues or 
students do not “own” their mistakes.
Remember that feeling when rolls are 
reversed.  When we make a mistake 
with our students, how should we 
respond?  
Is it different if it is whole class 
vs. one on one?  Is it different if 
other adults are in the room?  
What if your mistake occurs in front 
of administration?
How do we navigate this? 
Owning Our Mistakes SCENARIOS:
- You misgender a student in 
class or in a meeting
- You share something about a colleague 
that you were not supposed to share
- Someone overheard you saying 
something that upset them
- You gave incorrect information and a 
student pointed that out
- Someone points out that a 
phrase you used is offensive 
to them
- You used a tone of voice with an 
administrator that wasn’t professional
- You made an assumption that 
was incorrect in front of 
others

Navigating Uncomfortable Feelings
           WHAT MAKES US FEEL UNCOMFORTABLE?
Communicating with adults or students who:
- Remind us of a difficult person/time in our lives
- Have opinions that are very different from our own
- Don’t approach conversations in the same way we do
- More bold
- More reserved
- Look for an argument
- Not being sure of what to do next, caught between two choices
- Learning that you were wrong, that you hurt someone’s feelings, made 
a mistake
