Recommendations for School Committee consideration | November 2026  
Purpose of these recommendations 
Tonight, I am sharing my recommended priorities for the FY2028 budget and asking the School 
Committee to consider the direction they establish. I am not presenting a proposed budget, asking for 
funding decisions, or seeking approval of specific positions or programs. I believe it is important to 
agree on what we value before we begin making decisions about what we can afford. Over the past 
year, we have asked a great deal of our educators: to deepen their use of high-quality instructional 
materials, sharpen the focus on grade-level standards, examine evidence of student learning, and 
strengthen the conditions that allow all students to succeed. Our next budget should give that work the 
consistency and support it needs to take hold. 
I also recognize the fiscal realities facing Lunenburg. We will not be able to fund every worthwhile 
request, and we should not confuse a longer list of initiatives with better outcomes. For now, I am 
recommending a framework for the budget-development process, not a predetermined set of 
expenditures. As we develop the numbers, our challenge will be to protect the classroom experience, 
meet our obligations, invest where the evidence is strongest, and be transparent about the tradeoffs. 
Stability, transparency, and accountability are not separate from our educational mission; they make it 
possible to deliver on it. 
Our instructional commitments 
1. Strong core instruction and higher achievement 
Our first priority is to ensure that every student encounters rigorous, standards-aligned instruction 
every day. We have invested in high-quality instructional materials. FY2028 must protect the full and 
thoughtful implementation of those materials rather than send educators in another direction before 
this work has matured. Collaborative planning and calibrated learning walks should help us identify 
what is working, where support is needed, and whether students are doing the thinking that grade-
level work requires. 
2. Excellent educators and sustainable working conditions 
Students benefit from skilled educators who know their community and have the time and support to 
do their jobs well. Competitive compensation, thoughtful staffing, reasonable class sizes, mentorship, 
and job-embedded professional learning should guide our choices. We must also pay attention to the 
cumulative weight of initiatives and expectations. Professional growth is not simply another workshop; 
it requires time to plan, practice, reflect, and improve. 
3. Inclusive instruction and special education 
Meeting the needs of students with disabilities is both a legal responsibility and an educational 
commitment. We must adequately fund required services, related supports, specialized staffing, 
transportation, and tuition while strengthening the capacity of our schools to serve students effectively.

4. Safe, supportive, accountable learning environments 
Academic expectations cannot be separated from the learning conditions in our buildings. Students 
need belonging, predictable routines, appropriate intervention, and clear accountability. Staff need 
training, access to expertise, and dependable responses when behavior disrupts learning or 
compromises safety. In FY2028, we should examine behavioral and mental health supports with 
particular attention to the elementary grades.  
5. Evidence-informed improvement 
Our assessments, literacy screening, student work, and classroom observations should help us adjust 
instruction—not simply fill reports. We should protect the tools and collaborative time needed to 
identify needs early, monitor response to intervention, and review progress. An important measure will 
be our stated goal of increasing average ELA student growth percentile in grades 4–8 to 55, alongside 
grade-level literacy benchmarks and other indicators of student learning. 
6. Broad opportunity and preparation for life beyond school 
A strong education includes the arts, physical education, advanced study, electives, career 
exploration, and opportunities for leadership and civic engagement. We should be cautious about 
balancing the budget by narrowing the experiences available to students. Our graduates need to be 
literate, thoughtful consumers of information, ethical users of technology, and capable of contributing 
to their communities. Those aspirations should be evident in what we preserve. 
The systems that make this possible 
7. Financial stewardship and operational reliability 
Families, staff, and taxpayers deserve budgets that are understandable and accurate. FY2028 should 
build on stronger financial leadership, clear purchasing and payroll procedures, timely reconciliation, 
realistic special education and transportation projections, and regular reporting. We should use 
multiyear forecasts to distinguish one-time relief from ongoing capacity. Efficiencies are important, but 
the test of an efficiency is whether it preserves or strengthens our ability to serve students. 
8. Safe facilities and responsible capital planning 
Our facilities are part of the educational experience. Preventive maintenance, life safety, reliable 
technology, and security must remain part of the operating conversation. At the same time, major 
improvements—including the McCarron & Mulligan Track and Field facility, Primary School site 
improvements, and long-range planning for Turkey Hill Elementary School—should advance through 
appropriate capital planning and funding channels. We should not allow essential infrastructure needs 
to disappear, nor should we treat capital projects as interchangeable with instructional staffing. 
How these recommendations would guide budget development 
If the School Committee agrees with this general direction, I recommend using these priorities to 
evaluate the proposals that come forward later in the budget process. We can ask:  
1. Does a request meet a legal, contractual, or safety obligation?  
2. Does it protect core classroom instruction?  
3. Is there evidence of student need and a plausible path to improved outcomes?

Outcomes these priorities should help us advance 
We should report on a limited number of meaningful measures: literacy benchmarks and student 
growth; consistent use of standards-aligned instructional materials; student access to timely academic 
and behavioral supports; staff retention and vacancies; and financial forecast accuracy and reporting 
timeliness. Not every measure will move in one year, and the budget alone cannot guarantee the 
results. It can, however, show whether we have put our resources behind the conditions most likely to 
produce them. 
Closing 
I love the ambition we have for Lunenburg’s students. I also know that ambition without focus can 
leave our educators trying to do too many things at once. My recommendation is that FY2028 be a 
year of strengthening our foundation: protecting great teaching, supporting students and staff, 
following through on the instructional work already underway, and restoring confidence in how we 
manage public resources. I welcome the School Committee’s feedback on these recommendations, 
including whether something important is missing. My hope is that we leave this discussion with a 
shared sense of direction. The decisions about dollars, staffing, and specific requests will follow as the 
budget takes shape.
