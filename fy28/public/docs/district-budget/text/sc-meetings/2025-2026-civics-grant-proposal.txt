FY2027 Civics Teaching and Learning Grant Proposal 
Fund Code: 0589 
Applicant: Lunenburg Public Schools 
Schools Served: Lunenburg Middle School (Grade 8); Lunenburg High School (Grades 9–12) 
Project Period: July 1, 2026 – June 30, 2027 
Total Funds Requested: $25,000 
Program Unit: Office of Literacy and Humanities, Center for Instructional Support 
I. Purpose and Project Overview 
Lunenburg Public Schools submits this proposal for the FY2027 Civics Teaching and Learning 
Grant to expand equitable, high-quality civic learning opportunities for students in Grade 8 and 
Grades 9–12, in alignment with Chapter 296 of the Acts of 2018 and the 2018 Massachusetts 
History and Social Science Framework. This proposal is grounded in a clear reality: while civic 
education is a statutory and moral imperative, Lunenburg lacks the local fiscal capacity to 
provide equitable, experiential civics programming without state support. As a result, access to 
meaningful civic learning has historically depended on course placement, teacher discretion, or 
family resources rather than student need. Grant funding will allow the district to move from 
uneven access to a coherent, inclusive Grade 8–12 civic learning continuum, ensuring that all 
students—regardless of background—can engage in sustained civic inquiry, authentic civic 
experiences, and meaningful civic action embedded within core instruction. 
II. Statement of Need 
Lunenburg Public Schools is a small district with very limited discretionary funding for 
enrichment programming, instructional materials, and specialized professional development. 
After meeting core instructional, staffing, and operational obligations, there are no remaining 
local funds dedicated to civics-specific curriculum development, experiential learning, or 
professional learning. As a result, experiential civic learning opportunities have been limited in 
scope and reach; applied civic experiences such as Mock Congress, civic simulations, and 
institutional visits have primarily been available to students enrolled in advanced coursework or 
students whose families can afford enrichment opportunities; and Grade 8 students, in particular, 
have had minimal access to authentic civic learning experiences connected to democratic 
institutions. This inequity is not a matter of will or instructional commitment; it is a matter of 
resources. Without grant funding, the district must continue to rely on static instructional 
approaches and limited, course-based opportunities, perpetuating disparities in civic learning 
access. This grant represents a critical opportunity to ensure that civic education in Lunenburg is 
not a privilege, but a guarantee for all students. 
III. Project Goals and Objectives 
The goals of this project are to eliminate inequities in access to civic learning by providing all 
Grade 8 students and a broader cohort of Grades 9–12 students with meaningful, experiential 
civic education; build educator capacity to implement student-centered, inquiry-based civics 
instruction regardless of course or program; engage students in authentic civic learning 
connected to democratic institutions, civic processes, and real-world issues; and establish 
sustainable instructional structures that ensure civic learning remains embedded in core 
instruction beyond the grant period.

IV. Project Activities and Implementation 
Civics-Aligned Instructional Materials 
Grant funds will support the acquisition of civics-aligned U.S. and Massachusetts instructional 
texts and resources to be used across Grade 8 and Grades 9–12. These materials will serve as 
shared, equitable instructional anchors supporting civic inquiry and issue analysis, source 
evaluation and evidence-based reasoning, and preparation for civic simulations and projects. 
Without grant support, the district cannot purchase updated, civics-specific resources, resulting 
in reliance on outdated or teacher-created materials that vary significantly across classrooms. 
Professional Development 
Professional development is the primary equity lever in this proposal. Grant-funded professional 
learning will ensure that high-quality civic instruction does not depend on individual teacher 
background, course assignment, or access to external opportunities. Educators across Grade 8–12 
will develop shared expertise in facilitating student-led civic inquiry and discourse, designing 
inclusive civic action projects, and aligning instruction to the civic knowledge, skills, and 
dispositions outlined in state standards. By investing in educator capacity rather than isolated 
programs, this project ensures that all students across courses and grade levels experience high-
quality civics instruction, even in a district with limited local resources. 
Student-Led Civic Learning Experiences 
All Grade 8 students will participate in experiential civic learning opportunities connected to 
democratic institutions and civic processes. These experiences will include structured 
preparation, active engagement, and reflection tied to civic inquiry projects. At the high school 
level, students in Grades 9–12 will engage in applied civic learning experiences that deepen civic 
reasoning, analysis, and participation. These experiences will expand access beyond advanced 
coursework and provide meaningful civic engagement opportunities for a broader student 
population. Students will demonstrate their learning through student-led civic action, such as 
developing policy proposals, presenting civic recommendations, or engaging in structured civic 
discourse connected to issues relevant to their communities. Without grant funding, these 
experiences are not financially feasible at scale. 
V. Alignment to Grant Priorities 
 
Equity 
This proposal directly addresses equity by confronting the resource-based barriers that have 
historically limited access to high-quality civic learning in Lunenburg. Civic learning 
experiences will include language supports and instructional scaffolds aligned with the 2020 
WIDA English Language Development Framework to ensure multilingual learners can fully 
participate in civic inquiry, discussion, and action. Grant funding will ensure universal access to 
experiential civic learning at Grade 8, expand applied civic opportunities to a broader, more 
representative group of high school students, remove reliance on family resources or course 
placement for civic engagement, and provide shared instructional materials and professional 
learning across classrooms. This grant is the mechanism that allows Lunenburg to fulfill the 
intent of Chapter 296 for all students, not just some.

Sustainability 
Grant-funded investments are structured for long-term impact. Instructional materials will be 
used across multiple years, and professional development will build internal expertise that 
supports sustained implementation. Shared civic inquiry frameworks and expectations will be 
embedded within the Grade 8–12 curriculum, ensuring that the benefits of this initiative extend 
well beyond the grant period. 
Civic Deeper Learning 
Students will actively do civics through inquiry, discussion, collaboration, and action. Civic 
learning will be integrated into core instruction and connected to real-world civic issues and 
democratic institutions, fostering meaningful engagement rather than surface-level exposure. 
VI. Outcomes and Evaluation 
As a result of this project, all Grade 8 students will participate in sustained, experiential civic 
learning; more students in Grades 9–12 will engage in applied civic inquiry and action; educators 
will demonstrate increased capacity to facilitate inclusive, student-centered civic learning; and 
civic education will become more equitable, coherent, and sustainable across Grade 8–12. 
VII. Two-Year Continuation Vision 
Year 1 will focus on expanding equitable access and building instructional capacity. Year 2, 
contingent upon funding, will deepen implementation, refine civic inquiry projects, and 
formalize shared Grade 8–12 expectations. In Year 2, the district will explore hosting a local 
civic project showcase to provide students with an authentic audience for their civic work and to 
strengthen school-community connections. 
VIII. Budget Summary 
Instructional Materials: $3,500 
Professional Development: $9,000 
Student Civic Learning Experiences: $10,500 
Civic Project Implementation and Supplies: $2,000 
Total Requested: $25,000 
IX. Eligibility and Compliance 
Lunenburg Public Schools is an eligible Massachusetts Local Education Agency. All required 
Curriculum Data Collection has been completed. All proposed activities align with allowable 
uses under the FY2027 Civics Teaching and Learning Grant and comply with applicable 
Massachusetts General Laws. The district is prepared to implement this project upon approval 
and will comply with all reporting, fiscal, and programmatic requirements associated with the 
grant. 
X. Conclusion 
For Lunenburg Public Schools, this grant is not a supplemental enhancement; it is a critical 
equity investment. It provides the means to ensure that civic education is accessible, meaningful, 
and sustained for all students, regardless of background or circumstance. With this support, 
Lunenburg is prepared to deliver a model of civic teaching and learning that reflects the intent of 
state law and the values of democratic education.
