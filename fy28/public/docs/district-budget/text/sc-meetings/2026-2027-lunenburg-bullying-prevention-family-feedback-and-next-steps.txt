What does Massachusetts law call bullying?
Lunenburg, Massachusetts  •  Understanding Bullying 02
Bullying is repeated conduct by one or more students or school staff members, directed at a 
student, that causes at least one of these outcomes:
Harm
Physical or emotional harm, or 
damage to property
Fear
Reasonable fear of harm or 
property damage
School impact
A hostile school environment, 
interference with rights, or 
substantial disruption of 
education
Includes cyberbullying. A power imbalance is a common feature, but is not a separate element of the 
Massachusetts statutory definition.

What is not necessarily bullying?
Lunenburg, Massachusetts  •  Understanding Bullying 04
A disagreement
Students have different opinions or argue with one 
another.
A one-time unkind act
An isolated hurtful remark or incident, without a 
pattern.
Mutual conflict
Students engage in a back-and-forth dispute.
An unwanted outcome
Not being invited, being corrected or facing a fair 
consequence is not automatically bullying.
Important: “Not bullying” never means “not serious.” Unsafe, discriminatory, threatening or 
harmful conduct still requires action.

Family feedback: what we heard
Lunenburg, Massachusetts  •  Understanding Bullying 08
October–November 2026 feedback highlighted both strengths and opportunities to improve.
Keep what works
Families valued the definitions and multiple ways to 
report concerns.
Make the process clearer
Explain who receives reports, what happens next 
and when families hear back.
Follow through
Clarify student safety plans, follow-up, retaliation 
protections and accountability.
Make it easier to use
Add a simple flowchart, current contacts, working 
links and student-friendly language.
Feedback reflects respondents’ experiences; it is not a districtwide prevalence measure.
