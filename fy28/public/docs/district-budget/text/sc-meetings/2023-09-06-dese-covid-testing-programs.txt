Our Schools Are Safe
• Last fall, DESE initiated Test & Stay to keep close contacts in school 
using repeated daily testing. 
• This program put a strain on school health staff as they try to keep up 
with contact tracing and repeated daily testing.
• Four and a half months later, with hundreds of thousands of results 
from across the state, more than 98% of those tests have been 
negative. We know it is overwhelmingly safe to keep students in 
school. 
3
===PAGE 4===
Secondary Transmission is Low
• Nationwide data related to Test and Stay was examined to determine the 
extent to which secondary transmission (i.e., transmission to close contacts) 
is occurring in schools. 
• The evidence from California and Illinois cited by the CDC in their Test to 
Stay guidance noted secondary transmission rates of only 0.7-1.5%.1
• Test positivity rates in Test and Stay indicate that individuals 
identified as close contacts in school are very unlikely to contract or 
spread COVID-19. 
4
1 https://www.cdc.gov/media/releases/2021/s1217-Test-To-Stay.html

Changes in Other New England States
• Other states, like Connecticut and Vermont, have recently transitioned from 
individualized contact tracing to the use of at-home tests and focusing 
school health efforts on symptomatic testing.

New COVID Testing Option to Optimize 
In-Person Learning
• Districts and schools participating in symptomatic and/or pooled 
testing may choose to continue those testing strategies and 
discontinue contact tracing and Test & Stay.
• Using their professional discretion, school health professionals may 
determine if specific situations warrant contact tracing. 
• Districts and schools that elect to make this change will be provided 
with rapid antigen at-home tests for all participating staff and 
students that can be used on a weekly basis.
6
===PAGE 7===
The New Option
• DESE and DPH recommend that districts/schools select this new 
option, and if they choose to implement it, they must notify 
their local boards of health. 
• This new option is only available to districts/schools that 
continue or start symptomatic and/or pooled testing. 
• Districts/schools that choose to maintain Test & Stay will 
continue contact tracing and will not be eligible to receive rapid 
antigen at-home tests.

===PAGE 9===
Our Recommendation
After consultation with Dr. Michael Lyons, LPS Medical Director, 
and the LPS Nursing staff, we recommend that the district: 
• suspend the Test & Stay program 
• implement symptomatic/diagnostic testing of students 
and staff following Mass DPH and DESE guidance. 
Access to symptomatic testing does not replace the need for a 
parent to consult their child's Primary Care Provider.
===PAGE 10===
Symptomatic Testing
Symptomatic Testing:
• does require a signed consent from the parent/guardian/staff 
member.  
• will provide the nursing staff with another tool to assess students 
presenting with mild symptoms. 
• a negative test will enable students presenting with mild symptoms 
while at school, to remain in school. 
===PAGE 11===
Symptomatic Testing
Participation in the Symptomatic Testing program will allow us to 
distribute home tests to staff and students.  
• Home test distribution requires staff/parents/guardians to opt-in to the 
program and agree to reporting requirements.  No signed consent is 
necessary.
As per DESE, when at-home test distribution and symptomatic testing 
are fully in place, the district will discontinue Test & Stay and routine 
contact tracing. 
LPS Nurses may choose to contact trace for certain situations of 
possible exposure.
===PAGE 12===
Dr. Lyons and the LPS Nursing staff strongly 
state that:
• Students and staff should not come to school if ill. 
• Parents/Guardians and Staff will continue to conduct a daily symptoms 
check prior to coming to school.
• The nursing staff will continue to advocate for parents/guardians 
to consult with a child's Primary Care Provider (PCP). 
• The option of providing an antigen test when students present with 
symptoms does not replace the need for consultation with the 
PCP. 
• Nurses will assess students, refer to their PCP, and request a return to 
school note when appropriate.
