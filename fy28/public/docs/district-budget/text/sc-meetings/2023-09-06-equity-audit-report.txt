ACKNOWLEDGEMENTS 
 
It is important that we remain diligent in our efforts to create, maintain, and provide 
exceptional learning opportunities for all students, especially in the 21st Century. This Equity 
Audit is a core reflection of the efforts needed to enhance and broaden the lens of work in the 
Lunenburg Public Schools (LPS), while unpacking the core of, “What does it mean to have an 
exceptional equitable education system”? Together, The Equity Process, Superintendent Kate 
Burnham, her leadership team, and many others who engaged in the creation of this Equity 
Audit demonstrated LPS’ efforts towards their continued growth to enhance and sustain an 
equitable education system for its community. 
 
The Equity Process had the ongoing pleasure to work alongside some members of the 
Lunenburg School Committee, students, parents, staff, and the leadership team to determine 
ways to elevate the culture of learning in LPS. This Audit reflects the needs stated by the 
community and shown by data. We would like to thank the community humbly and sincerely 
for the opportunity to conduct meaningful work with and for you. Without your courage, 
candor, support, and hospitality this Audit would not have been possible. 
 
EXECUTIVE SUMMARY 
Purpose   
“Education breeds confidence, confidence breeds hope, and hope breeds peace. – Confucius”  
 
Lunenburg Public Schools (LPS) worked alongside The Equity Process also known as TEP, to 
objectively understand, establish, and make decisions about the hard truths that come with 
discovering and defining exceptional learning environments. Together, we set forth a set of 
processes that would translate into what exceptional learning looked and felt like to the 
community while centering the needs of social emotional learning (SEL) for students and adults. 
The data-driven Audit was conducted through a research and evidence-based approach. Data 
was gathered through numerous steps to collect information on LPS and its stakeholders. 
Review of polices, survey responses, facilitated discussions, equity walks on all campuses, and 
more, represent pivotal data points used to conduct this Audit. Through a call for transparency 
and truth, LPS teachers, staff, students, families and leaders supported this process by sharing 
their personal and professional experiences. One of TEP’s main outcomes, was to ensure others 
understood the need for equitable practices during students’ learning experiences. The 
collected data provided evidence, information and patterns that allowed TEP to analyze District 
polices, processes, procedures, and the human experience through the lens of exceptional 
inclusive learning for all. 
 
The Audit itself is broken into sections structured in a thought-provoking way. It is composed of 
the inquiries relating to social emotional equity, racial equity, diversity, inclusion, and 
belonging.  As you review each section you will be able to recognize the District’s dedication to

THE EQUITY AUDIT+ 
What Should You Know About the Equity Audit? 
The Equity Process is dedicated to incorporating structures that allow our clients to foster 
positive, safe, brave, courageous, and intellectual relationships with people of all races, 
cultures, ethnicities, ages, socioeconomic backgrounds, (dis)abilities, genders and sexual 
orientations. It is our objective to co-establish environments where all people feel valued and 
welcomed to benefit all stakeholders with an emphasis on students’ needs.  
 
An equity audit is informed using research and evidence-based tools used specifically to gather 
data on the School District’s community at large. It can be a partial (department, school, 
policies, etc.) or complete District Audit. The time it takes to complete the Audit ranges from 96 
to 180 days. Through our work, we will be able to discover and uncover information regarding 
both equitable and inequitable practices within our clients’ school system. We do so by working 
through a series of Phases and by using vetted tools. 
 
Phase I includes but is not limited to looking deeply at the scope of the project through vetted 
protocols that allows us to closely examine all written materials such as policies and 
procedures. Phase II includes but is not limited to a series of processes in which we conduct 
surveys and facilitate discussions with various stakeholders/community members. Phase III 
sums up the process by creating a report that includes findings and recommendations offered 
to the District. All our Phases are conducted through the lens of our RECIII Philosophy©. 
 
The RECIII Philosophy©, focused on relationships, engagement, challenges, impact, instruction, 
and inclusion, is equity in compassionate, mindful, and humble action for K-20/K20 education 
systems. It is one of the frameworks used to conduct our Equity Audits, Equity Walks, Equity 
Surveys, and Facilitated Discussions. The framework is student and community centered and 
measures transferable skills across datasets, human behaviors, observations and interactions, 
as well as it helps to maintain a core objective and global lens. We believe that building 
sustainable and authentic relationships with students foster trust that leads to their 
engagement. That engagement and trust allow students to own their confidence. It also creates 
the desire for them to rise to the challenging lessons of their classrooms. It helps to build 
students as leaders. It offers the adults data that leads to awareness, intentionality and best 
impactful instructional and socioemotional practices. Thus, lending to an environment for 
authentic and culturally inclusive student success for each student in the community. 
 
During an Audit, The Equity Process collects data and information by working closely with the 
District's superintendent and designees. In summary, the Equity Audit is a comprehensive 
benchmarking tool that assesses, remarks, and provides recommendations to support a more 
inclusive school environment for ALL including adults. It has a strong focus on compassion, 
mindful awareness, justice, equity, diversity, and inclusion for school communities. While we 
recognize that many outcomes of strong Belonging, Justice, Equity, Diversity and Inclusion (B-
JEDI) work can’t necessarily be measured or quantified through an antiquated way of looking at

Key Equity Driven Vocabulary    
All – 100% of Lunenburg’s stakeholders or students depending upon context. 
Belonging - The ongoing culture created to have all community members feel welcome across 
differences by honoring, celebrating, and accepting those differences.  
BIPOC – Black, Indigenous, and People of Color.  
Cultural Inclusivity – A pedagogy grounded in mutual awareness, acceptance, and actions of 
bringing together people of all ages and levels of society both in and out of the classroom. 
Culturally Responsive Practices – Formulated practices derived from asset-based pedagogies 
that include, own, honor and celebrate students’ cultural identities and lived experiences into 
the classroom as tools for effective instruction. They center the knowledge of marginalized 
communities in classroom instruction. As a result of CRP, all students are empowered to 
become lifelong learners and critical thinkers. 
DEI – Diversity, Equity, and Inclusion 
Diverse Learning Communities - Learning communities that support collaborative partnerships 
between students and staff to restructure curriculum to address structural barriers to 
educational excellence. They are inclusive of all races, ethnicities, genders, creeds, sexual 
orientations, and abilities. 
Diversity – The practice or quality of including or involving people from a range of different 
social, racial, and ethnic backgrounds, genders, sexual orientations, abilities, etc. 
Diversity Hiring – Hiring based on merit with exceptional care taken to ensure procedures have 
reduced biases related to a candidate's age, race, gender, religion, sexual orientation, and other 
personal characteristics that are unrelated to their job performance. 
Equity – The quality of being fair and impartial to all while having the wherewithal needed to 
gain and or offer access and entry to opportunities without barriers against abilities, races, 
genders, ages, sexual orientations, religions, etc. 
Equality – The state of being equal, especially in status, rights, and opportunities. 
Global Lens – The practices, understanding and outlook of belonging, diversity, equity and 
inclusion that fosters a mutual respect across cultures locally, nationally, and internationally to 
focus on the unique authenticity of deepening our connections to the current and future world.

Inclusion – The practice or policy of providing equal access to opportunities and resources for

JEDI – Justice, Equity, Diversity, and Inclusion 
Microaggression - Indirect, subtle, or unintentional discrimination against members of a

Racial Equity – A process and outcome. As an outcome, we achieve racial equity when race no 
longer determines one's socioeconomic outcomes; when everyone has what they need to 
thrive, no matter the color of their skin. As a process, we apply racial equity when those most 
impacted by structural racial inequities are meaningfully involved in the creation and 
implementation of the institutional policies and practices that impact their lives. 
RECIII©

inclusive student success for each student in the community.”

empathy for others, establish and maintain supportive relationships, and make responsible and 
caring decisions.  Please see their website for more information and deeper understanding of

LUNENBURG, MA COMMUNITY OVERVIEW 
 
Lunenburg, Massachusetts is in the northern Worcester County in central Massachusetts. 
According to the 2020 US census there are 11,816 people living in Lunenburg. 94.2% white, 
1.6% African American, 6.1% Hispanic/Latino, 1.0% Asian, 2.1% two or more races. 82.8% of 
residents own their homes, and the median value of those homes is $326,400. 96% of the adult 
population are high school graduates, 38% have a bachelor’s degree or higher. The median 
household income is $99,601 and 7.8% of the population is identified as living in poverty.  
Source: US Census Bureau

LPS STRUCTURES AND POLICIES 
Purpose 
The Equity Process examined various structures, policies, and procedures through a RECIII© 
lens. Our goal was to investigate, interrogate, and capture evidence of alignment and 
misalignment of systems that support the District’s mission statement, core verbalized values 
and practices that support learning and teaching. Areas examined included the school 
committee, employee evaluations, employee manual, and student handbooks.  
Summary 
A short summary represents some notices made during our review of the included documents 
listed in this section. Bullets will offer our suggested recommendations for each category.  
Student and Parent Handbooks and Policies 
The student and parent handbooks did not appear to be welcoming, as evidenced by the 
following three points. 1. They did not include a message from the school principal.  2. They 
were written only in English. 3. They focused on technical information and rules rather than the 
social emotional well-being of all students.  
 
The district website held loads of information including an opportunity to translate the website 
into several different languages. However, at the time of our review, the translation option did

Recommendations: 
• Include SEL language in all school level communication including, but not limited to 
handbooks. 
• Each school should review the student and parent handbook through the lens of

as EL, BIPOC and students, staff, and families who identify as LQBTQIA+.

OBSERVATION DAY 
Purpose 
The Equity Process spent a day observing classrooms and common spaces within Lunenburg 
Public Schools. We used an observation tool, which we developed called RECIII©. TEP believes 
The Framework is necessary to promote and document equitable practices observed in 
classrooms with a powerful sense of effectiveness. Each member of the observation team has 
been trained on RECIII’s tools prior to the day of observations. They were also trained on how 
to calibrate observations with relevant feedback to the school community which was observed. 
Summary 
Three core teams were assigned to specific schools to visit. They visited Lunenburg Primary 
School, Turkey Hill Elementary Schools, and Lunenburg Middle and High Schools, on November 
9th, 2022. They observed practices in classrooms, common spaces (hallways, cafeteria, 
playground, etc.), and offices. Using the RECIII© protocol, they recorded observations for each 
school and then met and conferenced to calibrate the observations and offer feedback to 
school based leaders.  
Lunenburg Primary School and Turkey Hill Elementary School 
At the Primary and Turkey Hill Elementary Schools, we observed a total of 11 classrooms from 
kindergarten through the fifth grade. While observing the classrooms, we saw that teachers 
sufficiently engaged students by compassionately demonstrating the expectations of the 
classroom. This made for a kind, non-judgmental classroom environment. Students often 
mirrored the demeanor of their instructors, which was mainly kind, bubbly, and appropriate for 
the subject being taught. Teachers commended and praised students for their efforts in their 
learning. This was shown by assessing student knowledge in a student friendly manner. 
Students demonstrated their understanding through collaboratively and independent work 
efforts. Teamwork appeared to be a major theme at all grade level.  
 
Teachers in every classroom were highly engaged in their lessons, students’ work and 
happiness. Visually, it looked like the teachers were highly engaged. Evidence of this was shown 
in the way that most of them sat on the rug with their students or sat at students’ desks. 
===PAGE 27===
26 | P a g e  
For more information, please contact The Equity Process, LLC at our website 
Several students were observed asking for support from their teachers in this way, “Can you sit 
with me while I try and solve this problem”? Each time a teacher was requested to sit with a 
student, it was clear that the teacher was aware of the student’s skill(s) in their response.  
Either they sat with the student and encouraged the student along, quietly conferenced with 
them in a very confidential way or share that they can request help from another student who 
might be able to further support. It appeared that teachers made sure that students were 
comfortable with their own learning. All teachers observed in the noted student to teacher “I 
need help” interaction, checked back in with the student to ensure their understanding. 
Overall, the classrooms and community spaces visited were very inclusive and welcoming of all 
likenesses and differences. Students appeared comfortable expressing themselves in class 
when they were struggling and showed signs of joy during learning. 
 
Despite there being a new curriculum, students and teachers championed through any 
noticeable challenges. They appeared to be navigating those challenges together. Students 
remained on task even throughout the most difficult lessons. Paraprofessionals and support 
teachers who were present in observed classrooms supported and engaged students 
appropriately and with fidelity. 
Lunenburg Middle School and High School 
At the Lunenburg’s Middle and High Schools, we observed 12 classrooms in grades six through 
twelve. Overall, students exhibited exemplar studious behavior and teachers showed various 
ways of compassionate teaching. The Middle and High Schools also had some challenges. It was 
noticed that while there was previous mention to support its students who are LGBTQIA+, we 
did not notice visual displays that represented their LGBTQIA+ student population through book 
choice and or posters, wall art, and other visual representation. We would like to note that 
brave and safe spaces were outfitted and identified with a sticker or another visual. In addition, 
the majority of classrooms, walls, and community areas did not show vast representations of 
diverse people and cultures expected in a learning community that values global learning for 
21st Century learners. In a subset of classes efforts were noticed that individual teachers made 
an effort to support all students through book choices, wall art, quotes by Dr Martin Luther King 
and other notable change agents for equity and equality. 
 
It was noticed that teaching styles varied at this level of education. Some classrooms were 
structured through a traditional lecture where the instructor’s voice carried the lesson. In 
classrooms where there was more adult discourse over student discourse, it was also observed 
that the instructors were inconsistent with offering student encouragement and/or praise. 
Other classrooms were structured collaboratively through teacher and student interaction. In 
these classrooms student voice and choice were noticeably exhibited. There was a prominent 
level of engagement and student discourse in classrooms that were student centered and 
collaborative, and students received words of encouragement and praise. 
 
In the spaces visited on Observation Day, we sensed a ‘business as usual’ attitude rather than 
an attitude focused on belonging. When TEP had a chance to speak with students at the Middle

DESE TIERED-FOCUSED MONITORING REVIEW 
Purpose And Summary 
Please note that during the 2021-2022 school year, Lunenburg Public Schools participated in a 
Tiered Focused Monitoring Review (TFM) conducted by the Massachusetts Department of 
Elementary and Secondary Education (DESE) Office of Public-School Monitoring (PSM). The 
purpose of the Tiered Focused Monitoring Review is to monitor compliance with regulatory 
requirements focusing on special education and civil rights. DESE conducted a TFM site visit in 
Lunenburg Public Schools during the week of May 16, 2022, to evaluate the implementation of 
Group B Universal Standards in the program areas of special education, civil rights, and other 
related general education requirements. The team interviewed staff and parents, observed 
classroom facilities, and reviewed the programs underway in the District. The final report 
identified two items in Civil Rights in need of improvement: CR24 and CR25.  
 
A description of the DESE TFM final report along with TEP’s questions and recommendations 
were discussed with the Senior Leadership team and will not be shared in this section.  Many of 
the items discussed are currently in progress and the team will need to meet on a scheduled 
basis to determine the final priorities, benchmark the priorities, and implement them with 
fidelity.  TEP leaves this section for the Senior Leadership team to further prioritize, interrogate, 
evaluate, and create by DESE driven deadlines through a compassionate and equity driven lens.

THE NORTH STAR 
 
The Equity Process has assembled recommendations based on the results of the Equity Audit.  
The audit was conducted through a detailed process to gather information and evidence 
including 1:1 interviews, surveys, and a review of current policies, processes, and procedures, 
including a visit to District schools and ongoing meetings with District leaders. TEP reviewed 
publicly available data on student demographics, staffing, MCAS/Accountability, and discipline. 
TEP held facilitated discussions with stakeholders to understand how equitable and inequitable 
practices present themselves and affect students. During research, observations, meetings, 
interviews, and conversations, stakeholders were actively engaged in discussions around the 
changes that needed to occur within LPS As a result, we noticed opportunities to sustain best 
practices.  
 
The Equity Process Makes The Following Recommendations:  
• Continue your efforts on creating a welcoming and inclusive school climate and culture 
for ALL, especially those who do not fit into the idea of “Lunenburg’s Traditions”. 
• Explore the psychologically harmful impact that traditions can cause in an effort to 
create safety nets for those that lose their sense of belonging during times that LPS 
celebrate traditions. 
• Provide all teachers, administrators, and staff with professional development and 
training on LGBTQIA+ awareness and intentionally creating safe spaces within all District 
schools, as well as celebrating the LGBTQIA+ community in ways that matter to them.

AN ANCHOR AND LEVER 
 
Essential statements were shared during all Phases of the Equity Audit by stakeholders. They 
allowed us to understand the District’s needs and purpose for the Audit. The statements came 
from various stakeholders of Lunenburg Public Schools. They were an essential anchor, lever 
and driver of our work. The statements were: 
 
“I’d like to know where our District is with our equity work”, said a senior leader. 
“I want students to have a voice in what we do here”, said a staff member. 
“Everyone needs SEL, even adults need it”, said a School Committee member. 
 
Navigating through difficult conversations such as SEL, equity, diversity, inclusion, and 
belonging are never easy.  It is often most challenging when objectively working to interrogate, 
uncover, and remove implicit biases that live within a heavily homogenous population. LPS 
must ensure that there is an active and equitable system that supports all students in accessing 
an exceptional 21st Century global education even after TEP exits the District.  
 
The essential statements kept us grounded during our time in Lunenburg. Through the 
statements we learned a lot about the needs of LPS from the stakeholders’ perspectives. The 
Audit’s platform allowed us to reciprocate our knowledge in the most mindful, compassionate, 
and human-centered ways. As you set the priorities for the District we hope that you too can 
use the essential statements as an anchor and lever.
