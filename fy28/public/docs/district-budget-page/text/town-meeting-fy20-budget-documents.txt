Athletics
Unified Basketball Coach: Unified Sports joins people with and without intellectual
disabilities on the same team. It was inspired by a simple principle: training together and
playing together is a quick path to friendship and understanding. In Unified Sports,
teams are made up of people of similar age and ability. That makes practices more fun
and games more challenging and exciting for all. Having sport in common is just one
more way that preconceptions and false ideas are swept away.
Lunenburg High School was a pioneer in the implementation of one of the first unified
programs in the state several years ago when the unitied track was offered. That sport
which has now grown in the Midland Wachusett League and through the state was only
the beginning. Other districts throughout the state and in our own league have added
additional unified offerings such as basketball.
The season is a 3 to a 4-week season during the last week of October till the 2 or 3~ week
of November. The teams play 4-5 games and participate in a jamboree with our league
schools at the end of the season. The cost to operate the program is minimal, essentially
needing to fund a coach, and transportation. Another coach or I will officiate for no
charge, we can either use older basketball uniforms or purchase t-shirts. The school
already has everything else needed for games and practices.

Teachers
• Literacy Coach: This position was included in the budget with alternative funding
source. The position will be funded with federal grant monies, if the annual allocation
can support it. This new Literacy Coach position is needed to provide expertise in
literacy instruction as the district begins to design a strong literacy program districtwide,
but particularly in K-5. Teachers are going to need in-district expert support as they
make this transition. Literacy development is vitally important, particularly in K-3. The
Literacy Coach will be able to assist teachers in design of effective whole class lessons, as
well as interventions for struggling readers and writers.
• Teachers and Paraprofessional: A determination will be made in July and August 2019 if

• Instructional Technology Specialist Teacher/Coach: This position would provide job-
embedded technology professional development and technical support for teachers in
the classroom. This support may be in the form of researching instructional technology
tools and applications, co-planning with teachers, providing technical assistance during
a lesson, and designing and facilitating technology workshops for teachers after school.
Athletics
• Coaches, Middle School: These positions are associated with an anticipated expansion of
the Middle School athletic program, should the recommendation for expansion be

• Associate Coaches: Ten (10) Associate Coaches were not funded at this time.
• Athletic Trainer: Athletic Trainers benefit the following:
Coaches- Full-time athletic trainers are uniquely qualified to facilitate 360-degree
communication between the coach, the athlete (and/or their parents) and if applicable
the athlete's physician. In the morning the AT can communicate with the physician on
the athlete's progress, get feedback from the athlete during their lunch break and email
their status to the coach before an afternoon practice. This circle of communication not
only provides full transparency during the student's healing process but also gives the
coach the time necessary to plan a day's game or practice based on the student's
participation status. Another advantage enjoyed by coaches with full-time athletic
trainers on staff is the ability to focus solely on coaching. In some situations, the coach's
role expands to overseeing athletic injuries after completing first aid training, placing
===PAGE 5===
but also their injury rehabilitation services. With a full time, athletic trainer at their
child's school, parents do not need to take off work to for their child's physical
rehabilitation appointment and save money on those associated costs. Of course, if the
parent has a preferred rehabilitation provider, the athletic trainer can work with them
when rehabilitation is complete to make sure the student is effectively transitioning back
into participation.
Students- Athletic trainers have the student athlete's best interest mind. Athletic trainers
want to see students excel on the field, but never at the cost of putting themselves at an
unnecessary risk of injury. It is the athletic trainer's job to ensure that the student-
athlete has the best possible high school athletic career while keeping them healthy for
their future, in sports or otherwise.
Budget Reductions, Impact Statements
Teacher Reductions
THES Enrollment Bubble Teacher to LMS: A grade 7 teacher will be switched to grade 6
to accommodate the larger class size (138). This will make six grade 6 teachers. The past
several years we have had 6- 10 new sixth graders register over the summer.
Grade 7 will function with 5 full-time teachers. Student enrollment is currently 131,
making class sizes approximately 26 per class (1 with 27).
Foreign Language Teacher: This would be an elimination of an LMS program developed
over the past two years in which students are exposed to three languages and different
cultures in order to make a more informed decision on which foreign language they want
to take in grade 8.
This is a true reduction of educational programming for students. LMS will run 6th and
7th grade specials and enrichment with one less offering, making enrichment class sizes
larger (average: 28-29).
Instructional Support Staff Reductions
Paraprofessional, Study Hall: This will impact the LHS Advisory program. We will not
be able to mandate the Thursday 24-minute activity which is the heart of the advisory
program. Staff will simply be supervising students in study halls. This impacts teacher
planning and making connections with students.

Non-Personnel Reductions
