UNDERSTANDING LUNENBURG’S FY27 BUDGET: 
How We Got Here, What Each Option Means, and What Comes Next 
 
(LUNENBURG, MA)-  Each winter work begins in earnest to develop the next fiscal year’s budget.  
However, with budgetary pressures showing in recent years, including an override in FY25 to fund the 
Lunenburg Public Schools (LPS) and a one-time transfer of $500,000 of free cash to LPS in FY26, Town and 
School leadership aligned early to take a coordinated, transparent, and collaborative approach to the FY 2027 
budget.   
 
Developing the Budget 
 
The Select Board, School Committee, and Finance Committee formed the Tri-Board Committee in the 
summer of 2025 to tackle budget challenges facing Lunenburg. The Tri-Board examined revenues, 
expenditures, and service-level priorities across Town and School departments, in collaboration as opposed 
to segregation.  Departments shared details on the services they provide, state and federal mandates, their 
future goals, and their operational difficulties.   
 
The Tri-Board, as well as individual Boards and Committees, developed three budgets now before 
Lunenburg in an open and transparent manner.  These budgets were built collaboratively, debated openly, 
and will now be considered by voters at the May 2, 2026, Annual Town Meeting and the May 16, 2026, 
Annual Town Election.  This release is intended to give voters insight into how the budget is developed and 
what each option will mean for Lunenburg. 
 
The Town Manager presented a preliminary budget to the Finance Committee on February 19, 2026, that 
reflected level services, but also incorporated targeted investments, such as the addition of a fourth shift in 
the Fire Department. That budget totaled approximately $53.1 million, roughly $3.36 million more than 
available revenues. This was not a final budget proposal, but was a starting point to quantify the gap between 
available revenues and budgetary requests.   
 
Throughout February and March, department heads - from Fire Chief to the Library Director to the 
Superintendent - appeared before the Finance Committee to explain their needs, justify their requests, and 
answer questions from elected officials and the public. Those presentations are posted in full, with video 
recordings on the Town Budget Hub site on www.lunenburgma.gov/835.   
 
Over several weeks, the budget was revised to reach a balance. Expense lines were reexamined to budget 
these lines closer to actual expenditures.  This is a deviation from conservative budgeting and is not without 
consequence.  Additionally, some service-level reductions were made to reflect current operational needs,

The Revenue Reality 
 
Understanding the FY27 budget begins with understanding revenue. Lunenburg’s total revenue available for 
appropriation in FY27 is $49,963,990.19.   
 
Under Proposition 2½, the Town’s property tax levy may grow by no more than 2.5% per year, plus new 
growth from new construction and development.  
 
For FY27, that means: 
 
• The FY26 levy limit of $32,910,811 increases by 2.5%, adding $822,770, an increase of $175.88 to 
the average single-family home valued at $517,296.  (Voters interested in understanding their 
estimated tax increase without an override should visit the Division of Local Services (DLS) Property 
Tax Impact Calculator, select “Lunenburg” from the drop-down menu and insert $822,770 into the 
field.) 
 
• Estimated new growth of $400,000 is added 
 
• Excluded debt of $2,199,352.52 is added, reflecting prior voter-approved debt obligations 
 
• Maximum allowable property tax levy: $36,332,933.80 
 
State aid of $11,404,917 is budgeted on the Governor’s Local Aid proposal, as the state budget is not 
finalized until summer. Local receipts of $3,508,024 are estimated conservatively, as directed by DLS.  
These include modest increases in Motor Vehicle Excise and Investment Income based on recent actuals. 
 
After deducting non-appropriated expenses including Cherry Sheet assessments and the Assessors’ Overlay, 
and the levy amount directed to the Capital Plan ($244,576), $49,963,990.19 remains for town and school 
operations.   
 
However, Lunenburg faces a structural deficit when fixed costs grow faster than the 2.5% cap. These include 
items outside the Town’s control: the Worcester Regional Retirement System assessment rose 9.95% - 
$238,178 over FY26; and active and retiree health insurance saw a substantial increase of $682,718.29 - 
town and school - over FY26. Meanwhile, the costs of services, fuel, utilities, and supplies continue to climb. 
 
Three Budgets: What Voters Will Decide 
 
The Town is presenting three budget options at Annual Town Meeting.  
 
• The first is the balanced budget, which aligns spending with available revenue. This budget includes 
targeted investments in priority areas, preservation of critical services, but also reduces or eliminates 
funding in other areas to close the gap. 
• The Tier 1, or “core services,” budget builds off the balanced budget by restoring many of the 
reductions made to reach balance, while also making targeted investments in certain priority areas. 
• The Tier 2, or “restorative services,” budget reflects a return to the service levels the Town and 
School have been working toward.

The Balanced Budget: $49,963,909.19 
 
The FY27 balanced budget does not replicate FY26 services.  It also reflects deliberate investments, 
including a dedicated Assistant Town Manager, an Assistant School Business Manager, and a fourth Fire 
Department shift as these investments were determined critical to the Town's long-term operational needs. 
Those investments are funded within the balanced budget by reductions elsewhere. 
 
The result is a budget that is balanced to available revenue of $49,963,990.19, but that looks and operates 
differently than FY26, with some services reduced, and others strengthened.   
 
To fund those investments within available revenue, the balanced budget makes reductions across nearly 
every Department.  Some reductions are restored in the various override scenarios, while others are not.    
 
Voters should understand these reductions clearly: 
 
• Public Safety: Per diem firefighter coverage reduced from 16 to 10 hours per day, limiting the ability 
to staff a second ambulance or maintain two firefighters on an initial fire call during evening hours.  
Expense and training lines are also reduced. The Police Department’s administrative clerk position 
(16 hours/week) is eliminated, and public lobby access is reduced after 3:00 p.m. 
• DPW / Roads: The Pavement Management Plan is reduced from $282,000 to $50,000. Drainage, 
stormwater compliance, and road supply budgets are also reduced. 
• Library: The Young Adult Services Librarian position (30 hours/week) is eliminated and library 
materials and digital resources are also reduced. 
===PAGE 4===
• Council on Aging: The two Dietary Aide positions (9 hours/week) that support the senior meal 
program are eliminated (one position vacant). Supply funding and senior programming are reduced. 
• Recreation: The part-time Recreation Director (19 hours/week) position is eliminated, ending town-
run recreation programming. Beach programming is funded for summer 2026 only; the Town Beach 
would not open in summer 2027 if no override passes.  Summer band concerts are also eliminated.   
• IT / Communications: The IT and Communications Manager position (32 hours/week) is eliminated 
to partially fund the Assistant Town Manager position. 
• Schools: The balanced budget results in substantial staff cuts in the Lunenburg Public Schools: two 
fewer teachers at the Primary School, two fewer teachers at the Turkey Hill, an occupational therapy 
assistant, an interventionist at the Primary School, an academic interventionist, and a custodian.    
Athletics and extracurricular activities are also impacted by the balanced budget with several sports 
and all athletic transportation being cut.  The balanced budget includes the proposed additions of the 
Assistant Business Manager, an English Language Learning teacher, and a Special Education teacher 
at the Primary School.   
 
The funding to operate the TC Passios building has been removed The building will be decommissioned 
during FY27 due to its declining condition, annual operating costs, and required repairs.  Additionally, 
approximately $220,000 in non-attrition related cuts have been made in the municipal balanced budget and 
$163,835 in the school’s balanced budget that are not restored in any version.  
 
Tier 1 Override: $52,321,641.87 - Requires a $2,400,000 Override 
 
If voters approve the Tier 1 override at Town Meeting on May 2 and at the ballot on May 16, an additional 
$2,357,651.68 becomes available above the balanced budget: $1,600,000 for Lunenburg Public Schools and 
$757,651.68 for municipal departments. 
 
Under Tier 1, many of the reductions in the balanced budget are reversed: 
 
• Fire: Per diem coverage restored to 16 hours per day. Fire training, EMS supply lines, protective 
equipment, and capital replacement budgets are restored to support operations, including the newly 
expanded fourth-shift. 
• Police: Public lobby hours restored through the Salary Reserve/FF line. Uniforms and firearms 
qualifications budget restored. 
• DPW: Pavement Management Plan restored to $282,000. Drainage, stormwater compliance, and 
supply budgets restored. A new junior engineer/project manager position ($86,000) is added to 
support MS4 (stormwater) compliance, contractor oversight, and capital project management.  
Currently the DPW Director is the only professional/engineering staff in the department. 
• Library: Young Adult Services Librarian restored. Library materials, digital resources, and 
programming restored. 
• Council on Aging: Dietary Aide funding restored. Senior programming and kitchen supplies restored. 
• Recreation: Recreation Director position restored at current salary, allowing summer camps, 
programming, and beach operations to continue in 2027. 
• IT / Communications Manager: Position partially restored at approximately 19 hours per week. 
• Schools: $1,600,000 restores most eliminated positions and allows the district to maintain current 
class sizes and specialized services. 
 
What remains reduced under Tier 1: The Police administrative clerk (16 hours/week) is not restored. One 
Academic interventionist specialist position at the high school is not funded. These items are addressed in 
Tier 2.

The Tier 2 override provides $3,248,545.83 above the balanced budget: $1,948,527 for Lunenburg Public

In addition to everything restored in Tier 1, Tier 2 adds:

• Schools: $1,948,527 restores all eliminated positions and supports additional program investments 
including an academic interventionist at the middle school and expanded school supplies, technology 
and maintenance. 
 
How Does a “Tiered” Override Work 
 
Under Massachusetts law, a community may present multiple override options with each tier representing a 
different level of funding.  
 
Operationally:  
 
• Town Meeting may adopt contingent budget appropriations tied to override outcomes;  
• At the ballot, voters consider each override question separately;  
• If multiple tiers are presented, the outcome is implemented based on the highest level of override 
approved by voters.

Free Cash 
 
Free cash is created (or reduced) by any variances to budgeted revenues and expenditures, including local 
estimated receipts, such as motor vehicle excise tax, meals and rooms excise tax, and licenses and permits, as 
well as real and personal property taxes and amounts remaining in unspent budget lines.  Certified free cash 
will also include prior year free cash amounts that were certified but unexpended from the prior year. 
 
Per DLS, “As a nonrecurring revenue source, free cash should be restricted to paying one-time expenditures, 
funding capital projects, or replenishing other reserves.”  DLS does not recommend that free cash be 
budgeted for ongoing operational purposes.  The Town has its own policies regarding how Free Cash should 
be spent and prioritized.    
 
DLS also recommends that communities understand the role free cash plays in sustaining a strong credit 
rating and strive to generate free cash in an amount equal to 5-7% of its annual budget.  This year, Lunenburg 
certified a record $3.354 million in free cash - 6.65% of the operating budget - well within DLS 
recommendations.  In the last 10 years, Lunenburg has been below DLS free cash recommendations for 
seven years, only meeting this recommendation in 2022, 2023, and 2026. 
 
At the end of FY25, the LPS District experienced a larger-than-normal budget surplus (more than double the 
historical average of $200,000). The surplus was primarily caused by two factors: significant turnover and 
unfilled positions in the facilities department resulted in unspent salaries and stalled maintenance projects 
and several paraprofessional salaries were ultimately covered by newly identified grants. However, the 
previous administration did not adjust the general operating budget to reflect these savings.  These unspent 
funds become part of the town's certified "free cash".
