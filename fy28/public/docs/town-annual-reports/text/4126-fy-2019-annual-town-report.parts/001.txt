Lunenburg was settled in 1718 and incorporated in 1728. It occupies a land area of
approximately 26.4 square miles. Lunenburg is located in north central Massachusetts
approximately 43 miles from Boston. It is bordered on the west by the Town of Ashby and
the City of Fitchburg, on the south by the Town of Lancaster and the City of Leominster, on
the east by the Town of Shirley, and on the north by the Town of Townsend.
Local legislative decisions are made by an open town meeting consisting of all registered
voters in the town. Subject to the legislative decisions made by town meeting, the affairs of
the town are generally administered by a five member Board of Selectmen and a Town
Manager. Local school affairs are administered by an elected seven member School
Committee while local taxes are assessed by a three member Board of Assessors, all are
appointed on an at-large basis for staggered three-year terms.
Lunenburg is a semi-rural community located in northern Worcester County. Three water
›odies account for somewhat of a clustered approach to the town's historical development
Lake Whalom and the former Whalom Amusement Park delineated the Whalom District
from the remainder of Lunenburg. This district was developed as a housing base for many
factory workers working in the mills and factories of Leominster and Fitchburg. In many
ways the characteristics of the Whalom District more closely reflect characteristics of these
cities than of the rest of Lunenburg. The Hickory Hills area of town, around Hickory Hills
Lake, was developed as a private community comprised largely of vacation cottages. The
third body of water is Shirley Reservoir, a public water body also developed with mainly
vacation cottages that have now been modified to year-round residences. Commercial and
industrial development is permitted in several areas of town, primarily along the two major
arteries (Routes 2A and 13), and the town's peripheries, due largely to the close proximity of
Leominster's and Fitchburg's sewer and water systems. Although many farms and orchards
had slowly developed into single tamily housing, farming continues on in Lunenburg.

During 2019 there were many topics addressed and accomplishments to highlight:
• As part of the annual goal setting process, the Selectmen reviewed their goals from FY19
and formalized their goals for FY20. The goals the Board identified for FY2019 included
identifying, defining, discussing, and finalizing the criteria to be used in determining the
use and/or re-use of the Town Hall, Ritter, old Primary, Brooks House, and TC Passios and
to propose at least one project to the 2020 Annual Town Meeting regarding the use and/or
reuse of the old Primary School. The Board identified the criteria used in determining the
use and/or re-use of the aforementioned buildings in the fall of 2019 and discussed the
future use of the old Primary throughout the year and continued to narrow down the focus
for the future use of this building into 2020.
• The Town Manager's goals included developing and implementing steps towards the
redevelopment/reuse of 925 Massachusetts Avenue, implementation of a recycling
contamination enforcement program, and planning/implementing accessibility
improvements.
• There were multiple projects that addressed accessibility at various town facilities, town
property, and public rights of way throughout the year. In January the Town Manager
announced receipt of an ADA Project Grant from MA Office on Disability for $7,500 for
accessibility improvements to the Lane, Cowdrey, and Small Town Forest properties. Also
in January the Veterans Service Office was relocated from the Eagle House to the Ritter
Memorial Building to be more accessible for veterans as the previous office was located on
a second floor without a handicap lift so the Veterans Officer was relocated to the Ritter
Memorial Building lower level that has a lift. The Town Manager coordinated the use of
Montachusett Technical students to renovate the second floor Town Hall restroom to
become a handicapped accessible bathroom and this was started in February and completed
by May. In March the Town Manager applied for and ultimately received a District Local
Technical Assistance grant to update the Town's ADA Self-Evaluation and Transition
Plan. The Town was one of three communities that participated in a Public Rights of Way
study through our regional planning agency that used a consultant to review the town's
sidewalks, ramps, and other ADA requirements. Around June the handicapped parking
spot at Town Hall was moved from an uneven brick sidewalk to a concrete level spot, the
chairlift door at Town Hall was fixed, and the uneven granite steps at the Ritter Memorial
Building were repaired.

• The redevelopment and/or reuse of 925 Massachusetts Avenue, an old vacant gas station
with potential environmental hazards, has been a property the Town has sought to clean-
up for over a decade but due to the unknown environmental hazards have not taken the
property for back taxes so that the onus of the potential clean-up costs was not borne by
the Town. After much research and consultation with the State and legal counsel, we were
able to gain an Administrative Search Warrant that gave the Town the ability to apply for
grant funding to perform an environmental assessment and right to access the property for
the inspection and sampling needed. In September the Court granted the Administrative
Search Warrant and the Town Manager submitted a Brownfields Site Assessment Grant
through Montachusett Regional Planning Agency for the costs of the environmental site
assessment, which was approved. The environmental site assessment is expected to be
complete by the summer of 2020 and the project will be eligible for clean-up costs
through MassDevelopment.
• In the fall of 2019 the Town Manager worked with a consultant to apply for grant funding
through the Municipal Vulnerability Preparedness (MVP) Program, which provides
support for cities and towns to plan for climate change and implement key adaptation
actions for resiliency. The planning grant applied for is a necessary step in order to apply
for construction costs to implement projects that are identified during the planning phase.
In 2020, the Town was notified we received this grant.
• After successfully applying for District Local Technical Assistance from our regional
planning agency to create a business brochure and create a permitting guidebook, both
were completed in January. The Business Brochure was created with assistance from the
Lunenburg Business Association and Land Use Director in an effort to highlight the
town's natural resources and increase awareness of these locations to residents who many
not know where public access to trails and conservation lands are located. The Permitting
Guidebooks was created to assist customers with projects of any type and scale to simplify
how they can file permits. Another project that was applied for and received through our
regional planning agency was for a Walkability and Parking Study. This study focused on
traffic data, crash data, an inventory of bike and pedestrian infrastructure for the Main
Street area and Whalom Lakefront area. The report was finalized in November and
identified a number of recommendations to address improving the walkability and parking
in both the Main Street and Whalom area.

DEPARTMENT PHILOSOPHY
Honesty and integrity are absolutes. They characterize the only forum in which anything
worthwhile can be accomplished.
Efficiency: Make every effort to proceed correctly the first time. It may take a bit longer but
will not require repeated attempts.
Loyalty up and down the chain of command is essential to success.
Every employee is essential. Those at the top are support people. The real work is done by
the men and women who drive the trucks, operate the equipment, turn the wrenches, and provide a
seamless flow of paperwork.

Public Works staff is either working or on call twenty-four hours a day, three hundred and

ensure others are aware of our contribution. This is not for an expectation of praise, but rather to
make sure efforts are not dismissed as they merge into the fabric of our everyday expectation of
service.

Winter Operations
Snow and ice present a major challenge. The safety of the public depends on the ability of the DPW to
naintain our roadways at a high level of safety despite the severity or the duration of snow and icıng
events. To that end, we have developed a winter operations procedure based upon a detailed plan with a
flexibility to accommodate the unpredictability of each storm. The expectation of immediate service is
high and must be delivered to every part of town in a timely manner.
Departmental preparedness begins with successful implementation of the capital plan.
Financial support is necessary to provide effective fleet rotation insuring minimal interruption of
services due to breakdowns and repairs. In the fall, salt is delivered to the Chase Rd. facility.
Lunenburg is in a consortium with 14 other communities in an effort to procure the lowest possible
price. From late summer to early fall vehicle maintenance staff inspects and prepares the trucks,
spreaders and plows for winter operations. During the construction season road crews address right-of-
way issues, overhanging branches, and pavement defects in order to eliminate obstacles to snow
removal. Each division within the DPW is involved in winter operations. Typically, DPW staff
combines with up to 14 subcontractors to provide service.
Generally, it is not the amount of snow but the duration of the event, that determines the
overall cost. For example, a fast moving snow storm of 6-8 inches will cost significantly less than a
long duration freezing rain event even though the accumulation is much less. Historically, the
lepartment operates in a deficit due to a yearly allocation that is below the cost of an average winter.
It is a dedicated group of individuals who successfully challenge the worst Nature has to offer
regardless of severity or duration.

The Lunenburg Property Tax Work-Off Program offers income eligible senior citizens age 60+ an
opportunity to have an abatement applied to their property tax bill. Participants worked in various
town departments to earn the tax abatement doing general office, custodial, library shelving, Meals
on Wheels, and kitchen assistance duties.
From January 1, 2019 to June 30, 2019 participants received $10 for each hour worked to earn the
$1,000 abatement. At this time income eligibility for participants was $40,000 for a one-person
household and $60,000 for a two-person household.
The Lunenburg Property Tax Work-Off committee voted at the March 27, 2019 meeting to increase
income eligibility for participants of this program to $50,000 for one person households and $75,000
for two-person households.
Also voted at that meeting was an increase in the hourly wage from $10 to $12 per hour along with
an increase to the abatement cap up to $1,500. These increases became effective at the beginning of
FY20 (July 1, 2019).
There were seven Lunenburg residents who participated from January 1, 2019 - November 1, 2019.
These individuals as a whole, accumulated 837.25 hours which amounted to $8,761.00 in abatements.
Eligibility Requirements:
* Applicants must be 60 years of age or older
*Own and occupy a home in Lunenburg as their primary residence for at least one year
*Provide proof of income by submitting prior year income tax return information
*Receive a property tax bill in their name or their spouse's name who is also 60 years of age or older
* Meet the income guidelines of: one person household - $50,000 / two person household - $75,000
*All new applicants are required to have a CORI background check and must re- apply and be
