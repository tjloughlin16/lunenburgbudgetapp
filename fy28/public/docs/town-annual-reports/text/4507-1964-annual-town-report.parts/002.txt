PARKS
Richard F. Mulligan, Chairman
Clarence E. May, Jr.
James F. Plunkett
The Park Department has under its jurisdiction, and is responsible for, the maintenance of six parks and playground
areas, three baseball and softball diamonds, three tennis courts, one ice skating pond, one outdoor basketball court,
and a running track and jumping pits. Additionally, it is responsible for providing organized and supervised activities
for the benefit of the residents of Lunenburg. The cost of maintaining these areas during 1964 was $1,833.50. In
addition to the usual playground maintenance the figure includes the cost of reforestation of the Marshall Park and
Marshall Pondpine groves, the clearing of brush along the shoreline of Marshall Pond and the repair of field stonewalls
at Marshall Park.
Adult programs which were sponsored by the Park Department included a softball league, horseshoes, tennis,
tennis instruction, a winter volleyball program and an open gym period.
The school age programs, both summer and winter, had greater attendance than ever before and were received
with great enthusiasm. Mr. Emory Merrill served as Director of the program. Miss Susan Crowell supervised the Center
playground and Miss Gail Rouleau, the Wallis Park playground. Miss Debbie McDonald and Miss Delores Bourgeois
conducted the arts and crafts program both in the Center and Wallis Parks. In addition to the general playground act-
ivities the summer schedule included and crafts, basketball leagues for boys and girls, boys baseball, girls softball,
tennis instruction, track, and several special events such as a Soap Box Derby, a trip to Pleasure Island and outings
at Willard Brook. The winter program included ice skating, hockey and a Biddy Basketball League.
Special projects carried out with monies appropriated under special articles included the repair and resurfacing
of the Marshall Park tennis courts for the sum of $1,875, the purchase of some additional playground apparatus for
use in the Center and Wallis Park playgrounds at a cost of $500, and a chain link fence and gates at Marshall Park
at a cost of $625.
Other projects which were completed during 1964 included the purchase of snow removal equipment to facilitate
the maintenance of the ice skating pond, the repair of the quonset hut at Wallis Park, the installation of additional
lighting at Marshall Pond and the construction of additional jumping pits at Marshall Park.
Plans for the 1965 season include the continuation of the construction of a softball field at Marshall Park, the
reconstruction of the Little League Field at Lesure Avenue, the expansion of the tennis program to Wallis Park, and
study to determine if a more suitable area is available in which to conduct the band concerts.
The $2,000 provided by a special article for the preliminary development of the "Cowdrey Lot" as a swimming
and recreation area has not been used as the Park Commission is awaiting professional aid which has been sought to
lay out this project, with the coordination of the Conservation Commission. Preliminary plans have been made and
more detailed plans are forthcoming. Both the Park and Conservation Commissions are looking forward with enthusiasm
to the realization of this project as it is felt that the development of this area will provide enjoyment to a large segment
of our population.
Articles included in the 1965 warrant call for an appropriation of $740 for a new flagpole in the center of
Town, $1,215 for the purchase and installation of a chain link fence at Wallis Park and Lesure Avenue Park, and
$380 for dugouts at Marshall Park Little League Field.
In 1964 the total cost of carrying out the various Park Department programs and maintaining the several park and
playground areas was $8,491.84.

limited to borrowings and loans, investments, leases, licenses, purchases and conveyances, and contracts, and also including the

SOME LUNENBURG PEOPLE WHO HAVE CONTRIBUTED TO CONSERVATION --
CLAYTON STONE (1871-1944)
A true naturlist who taught for years the wonders of nature, not only to the
people of Lunenburg, old and young, but to the Boy Scouts and many other groups in
the surrounding areas.
BENJAMIN WHITING (1804-1900)
He spent much of his life as a sailor. He collected stones and minerals from
all parts of the world and presented the collection to the Town.
JULIA BAILEY (1874-1959) WILLA J. HARLEY (1890-1963)
They served as Girl Scout leaders for many years for the Lunenburg troops, were
nature study experts and instilled in many Lunenburg girls the love of birds, flowers
and trees.
MRS. WILLIAM R. PEABODY
For nearly 20 years a member of the Town Forest Committee, a lecturer for the
State Women's Club Conservation Committee, the oldest in years of today's conservation
workers and the youngest in spirit.
IRVING KIMBALL
A true disciple of conservation and a great inspiration and advisor to all of
our present day conservation workers.
JOHN FITCH should have believed more in Conservation:
In 1747 John Fitch, for whom Fitchburg was named, and his entire family were
captured by the Indians and taken to Montreal where a year later he and his family
were ransomed. The attack by the Indians was provoked because John Fitch felled a
tree which crushed an Indian wigwam. The Fitch family lived in the northwest part
of Lunenburg which is now a part of Ashby.
Note: As HAROLD D. HARLEY, who organized Lunenburg's Conservation Commission in
1961, who has served as its Chairman ever since and who has also served as a director
of the Massachusetts Association of Conservation Commissions and is presently 1st
Vice-President of the State Association, has requested that he not be listed as one
of the persons who have contributed to Conservation, his name has not been included
above. We have not, therefore, acknowledged the fact that Harold is regarded through-
out the State as a champion of the Conservation Cause.

Chartrand; Vice-Chairman, Mr. C. Edward Stillman; Treasurer, Mrs. John J. Kerrigan; Secretary, Mrs. Einard Warpula; Publicity,
Mrs. Richard Smith; Cookie Chairman, Mrs. George Mason. Other members of the Town Committee are Mr. and Mrs. Warren Ward,
Mr. Robert Logan and Mr. Thomas Passios.

Eighty young people were enrolled in various projects throughout the year. The annual sliding party rally was held at Hill-
croft Farm. Mrs. Stillman's 4-H girls served refreshments to a hungry lot. The Boots and Bits Horse Club and the Conservation Club
came through with very interesting displays in the Elementary School Show Case. The Dairy Club won the Blue Seal Feed Trophy for
Herdsmanship and two members of this club won top judging awards. A Senior Dairy Judging award was won by Suzanne Stillman
and the Junior Dairy Judging by Gereth Stillman, the latter award being a calf donated by the Worcester County Farm Bureau.
William Donaldson made a fine showing with heifer COW at the fair in Spencer. The Poultry Club made a good showing in the Broiler
Contest which was won by Maurice and James Hugg and in the Egg Laying Contest in which Glenn Stillman's birds took top honors.
Two of our club members attended the State Judging School at the University of Massachusetts at Amherst. Christine DeBetten-
court, Gregg and Gereth Stillman received awards at the Garden Tour sponsored by the Worcester Horticultural Society. Other
activities included Senior Girls' Day in Worcester, 4-H Teen Forum in Boston, Horse Camp at Spencer, a Cookie Sale and several
rallies. Cookies were baked for Community Christmas Baskets.
The 4-H Conservation Club has been involved in a multiplicity of activities ranging from bridge building to constructing
bird houses. A "Half-Campership", was awarded to Jack Shollwho attended the 4-H Camp in Spencer and scholarships were awarded
to Dick Daoust and Bill Donaldson. These boys were selected to attend a six day conference at the University of Massachusetts with
a portion of their expenses being paid by the State. This summer the Conservation Club captured third place at the 4-H Fair in
Spencer for their exhibit on fire prevention.
The boys labored diligently to repair a bridge spanning Mulpus Brook on the "Cowdrey Lot". The bridge is now safe, functional
and temporary. Plans are under way for rebuilding the bridge next summer strong enough to take a herd of wild horses (or at least
strong enough to support any person onhorseback as future plans for the "Cowdrey Lot" include a system of bridle paths). Onregular
meeting nights the boys were divided into teams and each team constructed some type of bird house. Chickadee and Wood Duck
houses and feeders were featured in hopes that during the harsh winter the birds wouldseek food and shelter in them. The Conservation
Club members have excellent attendance records and attack each project with vigor.

SCHOOL BUILDING COMMITTEE
Charles H. Hillman, Chairman
Russell H. Damon
Mrs. C. Edward Stillman
Donald Johnson
Charles C. Burnap, Jr.
Louis V. Olivet, Jr.
Carl W. Proctor
This Committee was appointed by the Selectmen, as per Article 5, and has met
continuously throughout the year on, at least, a weekly basis. We met with most of the
Town Committees, as well as Selectmen and School Administration officials, so that all
ideas available could be considered. In the Fall, there were two combined meetings with
this Committee and all the members of the various Town Committees to discuss the tentative recommendations being
proposed by this Committee and also to receive suggestions from those present. The Committee studied the four sug-
gestions as put forth in the Town Meeting. These were: 1) Additions to the existing Junior-Senior High School; 2)
Additions to the existing Elementary School; 3) New Elementary construction on the central site and 4) New Elementary
construction on Town-owned land in the Whalom area.
The Committee also studied the construction of an intermediate-ultimate Junior High School in the central complex.
In order to properly consider these possibilities, the services of Mr. Korslund, of the architectural firm of Korslund,
Normand and Quann, were retained to assist US.
The Committee felt that the solution to the problem should definitely solve the problem at hand, definitely fit into
the overall long-range plan, be flexible, be timely, and the cost should be consistent with the solution of the problem.
After much study of the data presented by the School Housing Study Committee, and after two or three joint meet-
ings with this Committee, the data appeared to definitely indicate an Elementary problem. Therefore, this Committee
unanimously felt that an Elementary school was required to not only solve our immediate problem but also to prepare an
adequate foundation for a continuing fine educational program. It was also felt that the central area of the Town
should be reserved for future Secondary school construction and that the Elementary school should be located in an
area where there is a large concentration of elementary students present.
In order to gain additional ideas, the School Building Assistance Commission, located in Boston, was contacted.
This Commission approves our requests for State Aid and they work on the following principles: a) The Town must
define the Educational Program, b) We must build to support this Program and c) We must build adequately. These
must be met before approval for this assistance will be given. The SBAC confirmed our recommendations and gave
preliminary approval to our proposal.
The recommendation of this Committee is that we build a 16-room Elementary School on the Town-owned land,
located off West and Pratt Streets in the Whalom district.
A report prepared in brochure form will be distributed to the Town in the week before a Special Town Meeting
to be called for February 8, 1965. A preliminary meeting will be held in the Cushing Auditorium on Monday evening,
February 1, 1965, to which everyone in Town is invited to hear the report. This is being done so that anyone wishing
can get all the facts pertaining to this study in sufficient time to evaluate them prior to action on the warrants at the
Special Town Meeting. If the Town, at this Special Town Meeting, votes to appropriate the sum of $38,000 the Com-
mittee will continue in the preparation of detailed plans and specifications so that they may secure construction bids
and bring this information to the Town for further action.
The estimated cost of this proposed Elementary School including site development, buildings, kitchen equip-
ment, furnishings, Architectural and Engineering fees and Contingencies is $828,600.

Superintendent's Annual Report
In the course of the past year I had the good fortune to receive, through the courtesy of Mrs.
Laurence Ebersole, a copy of the first Annual Report for the school year 1891-92 prepared by the
first Lunenburg Superintendent of Schools, Miss Flora E. Kendall. As one might imagine, the Report
is interesting not only because of the cost figures it reports, but also because it brings into
focus the tremendous advancement and change public education has experienced over the 70 year span
of a lifetime. Schools at that time operated for six months only with regular attendance on the
part of pupils apparently not too great a concern to parents, There was no High School and although
the report does not say it, it recalled to mind the fact that during that era about six (6) of
every ten (10) pupils did not get beyond a third grade education, Interestingly enough, the total
cost for the Operation of Schools was $3050. Classes were ungraded, including a wide range of ages,
excessively large, so that no more than ten minutes could be allotted to a recitation in a given
subject area,
Such conditions, inadequate and unsatisfactory as they were then in the words of Miss Kendall,
would in today's world be practically impossible if youth were to attain any future success. Our
society has become so much more complex that the opportunity for one to make his way in the world
is almost in direct proportion with the amount and type of education he has. Doors of opportunity
are closed for the untrained, the unskilled, the uneducated if one is to attain a satisfactory
station in life.
Such tremendous changes and demands to educate all pupils places on the Community, the State
and the Nation an ever increasing financial burden to keep pace with the times. In America, the
development of minds to their capacity is important to everyone whether they be located in the most
sophisticated, wealthy community or the smallest and poorest hamlet. The ability of the Community
to pay should not restrict the quality of education he receives for every pupil must compete with
others as he goes out into the world, and the development of a good mind in a student wherever he
may live, may one day be of great importance to the future survival of our society. Lunenburg
recognizes this need and makes valiant efforts to fulfill it.
Recognition of the need for quality Education is evident not only by the citizens of Lunenburg
but also by the State and Federal Governments.
We must at this point reflect briefly upon the recent actions of the State and Federal Govern-
ments concerning education. They have recognized that "Education has come to be regarded as one of
the two chief domest issues of this decade, that its aim is to develop every pupil's ability to
live usefully in society and to meet successfully the demands which society has a right to make on
him." The year 1964 was the greatest year of progress in history for financial aid at the Federal
level for education. The provisions of the National Defense Education Act were extended and
broadened to give Federal support and help with Reading, Language Arts, Geography and Social Studies
and great impetus and encouragement were given to the Vocational School movement, the effects of
which will be more readily felt as our Regional Vocational School progresses.
Statewide, after a two-year period of study of the status of education in Massachusetts, the
so-called Willis Report was issued on December 30, 1964. The report reflects continuously the need
for improvement; "improvement in educational opportunity, improvement in quality, improvement in
management and improvement in coordination required at all levels of organization and instruction
from kindergarten through higher education", All of these facts the local community has recognized
and has made remarkable progress within its financial limitations. This report recognizes that the
great need for educational improvement is additional money; $123,000,000. of it alone for 1965-66
"to help restore Massachusetts to the pre-eminence envisioned by Horace Mann and demanded even more
by today's society". The implementation of certain recommendations of this report can provide
greater stimulus for effective education in each and every community and can do much to ease the
local tax burden.

- EXPLANATION OF 1965 BUDGET INCREASE -
Obvious to all is the fact that as enrollments continue to increase at an average of somewhat
over 100 pupils each year that the Budget must go up since it requires more of everything--
materials, supplies, books, teachers, etc. to fulfill their needs, We will this September be add-
ing three additional staff members at the Junior-Senior High School level; had sufficient Elemen-
tary space been available, at least one or two teachers would have been added there in order to
maintain the standard of class size we feel is necessary to good education.
Of the total increase of $67, 137., $66,280. is accounted for in salaries alone for all School
Department personnel, Because of State increase of Teacher minimum salaries, because of the large
number of teaching personnel involved and because of a built-in policy characteristic of most
systems of increments for all personnel, this increase is unavoidable if we are to compete in
acquiring the services of the personnel who largely determine the effectiveness of the School
System. We cannot afford the luxury of being in the position of having fine facilities but third-
rate instructional personnel. We are proud of what we are today, grateful for the quality teacher
and non-teaching personnel we have and are determined to remain competitive enough that all will
want to continue in producing a School System to which all of us can point with pride, We cannot
afford second-best in our teaching personnel for I am convinced that the good teacher will educate
children effectively in spite of potential obstacles.

- "HILLS TO BE CLIMBED" -
Ahead of us in the Lunenburg Public Schools remain many challenges. We must continue to be
alert, big enough to recognize genuine needs and willing enough to give of ourselves for the future
progress of our schools.
American educational philosophy stresses the fact that we must try to educate all--this we cam
not do adequately under our comprehensive school organization. We must continue to work toward
bringing a Regional Trade School to fruition to meet the needs of that almost 50% of our youth who

- A WORD OF APPRECIATION -
The longer I work in this field of Public Education, the more impressed I am with the effort
and interest the various members of the School Committee give so unselfishly to further the welfare
of education. I am sure most people do not, nor cannot comprehend the kind of devotion which must
go into serving as a School Committee member; the Community owes them a debt of gratitude.
- OUR THANKS -
The School Committee and the Superintendent of Schools once again want to express their appre-
ciation to all of those who have combined their efforts to make our schools as meaningful as
possible.
Only through the cooperative efforts of many groups and individuals can we fulfill the goals
we hope to attain. Noteworthy among such individuals is Miss Signe Schultz who retired after 37
years of selfless service to the Town of Lunenburg and its schools.
To the parents and taxpayers who provide the financial support, and the encouragement to their
children to work to capacity; to the students who develop as we try to mold and direct them; to
Principals, Department Heads, Supervisors, Teachers and Coaches, who are the ones who give of them-
selves to better prepare our young people for their place as citizens; to the ever valuable Secre-
taries who fulfill so many important responsibilities; to the Custodians and their Supervisor, who
continue to show pride in the care of our facilities; to the School Lunch personnel and their
Supervisor, for keeping us properly fed; to the Bus Owners and Drivers, who fulfill their daily
assignments so faithfully; and to our cooperative School Nurses, who look after our health; to all
of these, we are grateful that you are part of the Lunenburg Public Schools.
For the continued cooperation of the Parent-Teacher's Association, Mrs. Mary Padula and the
Town Office personnel, the Finance Committee, and all Lunenburg citizens, especially the unselfish
one who has no children in school, we express our thanks. Our school system is as effective as the
combined efforts of all of US make it.
RALPH W. BROWN
- Superintendent of Schools

What we believe we cannot do we can not do. What we believe we can do we can do."
