Board of Selectmen - left to right: Normand J. Thompson, Chairman Sherwood G. Winchester and Rudolph Masella.
Town government is becoming more complex each year. New laws passed by the General Court coupled with
demands from the State's many regulatory agencies, require constant change in the method by which we run our Town.
Especially difficult are changes which require new expense monies, but contain no method with which to fund them.
With the economy of the State in financial trouble, speculation is for less financial reimbursement and the possibility
of an increased tax rate. Holding the line on local budget spending and capital investment, seems more necessary
now than ever before.
In January of 1975, we were required to have a Building Inspector. This was accomplished by Lunenburg joining
together with the Towns of Shirley and Townsend and forming the Tri-Town Building Inspector Board. This Board con-
sists of one Selectman from each community. Subsequently, Mr. Aust in R. Fitzsimmons was chosen as the Building
Inspector. This joint effort reduced the cost to the three towns as each share an equal amount of the expenses. This
first year the Building Inspector's salary was funded through the Comprehensive Employment & Training Act, and must
now be included in the local budgets of each community. The formation of the Tri-Town Building Inspector Board at
first presented some problems which have since beensmoothedout and is working out quite satisfactorily at thistime.
The Selectmen, acting in the capacity of Licensing Authority, issued many routine licenses and renewals as
required throughout the year. Eight hearings were held this year mostly pertaining to alcoholic beverage licenses.
The Refuse Disposal Planning Committee is representing Lunenburg as a member of the Central Massachusetts
Solid Waste Committee. The formation of the Solid Waste Committee took place late in 1975 and brings together
most communities in Worcester County in a effort to operate a resource recovery system. This Committee offers a
promising solution to our future solid waste problems.

Board of Assessors
On December 31, 1974 the Commissioner of Corporations and Taxation sent a lettertoall "Boards of Assessors
and Executive Officials in all Cities and Towns of the Commonwealth".
It contained a copy of the Town of Sudbury & others vs. Commissioner of Corporations and Taxation & others,
a decision of the Massachusetts Supreme Judicial Court handed down on December 24, 1974. In effect the Court
unequivocally declared:
.that (1) the commissioner (of corporations and taxation) has the power and the duty to direct
local assessors to take such action as will tend to produce uniformity throughout the Commonwealth
in valuations and assessments; (2) the (state tax) commission has the power and the duty to direct city
and town officers to furnish such returns and statements relative to the amount and value of taxable
property in the city or town as it deems necessary to enable it to determine and establish for each
city and town an equalized valuation which shall be the fair cash value of all property in such city
or town subject to local taxation as of January 1 in each even-numbered year; and (3) the functions
of the commissioner and the commission in these respects are to command and not merely to advise
or educate, and it is the legal duty of the assessors to obey their lawful commands.'
Pursuant to the interlocutory decree to be entered, the Commissioner and Commission are required to make a
report of progress to the Court within six months and periodically thereafter.
As a result of this letter, the local Board of Assessors had to submit a plan to the tax commissioner by April 1,
1975 for bringing property assessments to full cash value and effecting uniformity of assessments among all property
within each class and among all classes. Our plan to the Commissioner consisted of the following information:
1. The Town had been revalued in 1969 and 1970 by the firm of Whipple, Magane and Darcy.
2. The Assessors and staff were thoroughly instructed in the operation and use of the Whipple, Magane and
Darcy evaluation and assessment system. This included fieldwork and record keeping. We were left with the
firm's manuals, guides and forms to maintain the system in equity.
3. All property is viewed on a yearly basis to insure up-to-date detailed drawingson property record cards.
4. All assessing courses offered by the Commonwealth of Massachusetts are attended byall Assessors and clerks
to maintain knowledge of the law.
5. Statistical study of all salessince 1970has been maintained andwill be usedas a base year for established
values.
We have sales broken down to establish the direction of sale prices and the average percentage increase over-
all for a given year plus the same information on specific types of houses. The last information is necessary to deter-
mine value movement within a type house.
A second study was set up consisting of a selection of homes in each specific type and revaluation of these
homes using the new 1974 Department Property Assessment Manual system and the manual figures.
Upon completion of this project the Board of Assessorshave hard data to determine exact percentage increases
required to modify existing assessed valuation of real estate. (It might be brought out that a new valuation at this
time could cost between $50,000 and $60,000. Updating as the Town is doing, by an outside firm could cost over
$19,000.)
Plans for personal property and land were also included in our initial presentation to the State. After several
minor adjustments with the State to our original plan, it was felt that Lunenburg's total valuation averaged 28% less
than full-market value. To arriveat 100% valuation within three years and in order to comply with the Sudbury de-
cision, the Board of Assessors are taking the following actions:
1. On January 1, 1976 increase all residential real estate valuation by 10%.
2. On January 1, 1976 increase all land valuation by 10%.
3. On January 1, 1976 increase all commercial property valuation by 3%.
4. Continue evaluation of all sales to maintain up-to-date market values.
5. On January 1, 1977 and January 1, 1978 make further adjustments as required to update the town's total
valuation in order to comply with the law.

I know just what you're going to say, Zeke - "Why don't you bring in a lot of industry, like they have in the
city I moved from? That will surely reduce our taxes." Besides having a short memory - remember all those taxes
in the city? - you're wrong again, Zekel Although industry would increase the tax base in Lunenburg, there is very
little chance that a small amount of industry would reduce Lunenburg's net tax burden, whereas it's a surebet that any
appreciable amount of industry would considerably increase it. I know this argument will be challenged, Zeke, but
all you have to do - and I've done it - is study and compare adjusted-value tax rates of towns, for instance along
Route 128, which have acquired considerable amounts of industry. Based on the present apparent ratio of assessed
value to market value of commercial property in Lunenburg, a $2,000,000 industry would only offset $1.00 on our tax
rate. I cannot conceive of any $2,000,000 industry which in our Town would not bring with it considerably more than
one tax dollar's worth of increased annual expense - i.e., more homes, more schooling, more policing, more street
maintenance, etc. No, Zeke, if your sole purpose in attracting industry is to reduce our net tax burden, forget it.
We all know, Zeke, that growth and change never stop, nor do we want them to. But we must keep in mind that
although uncontrolled growth can increase tax bases, it almost always creates a net tax burden. We must therefore
make sure that Lunenburg's growth and change truly reflect the needs and desires of the majority of its citizens, not,
as is often the case, the needs of special interest groups. There are many growth-guiding techniques which can be
used. Some of the techniques which have controlled Lunenburg's growth and tax rates are Zoning, Subdivision Con-
trol, Conservation of Open Space, and Wetlands Protection. You will find, Zeke, that along with responsible admin-
istration and good financial management, these have been andwill continue to be some of the best tools for improving
the quality of our lives and keeping the small-town character of our Town, while keeping the tax rate within reason.
We sometimes forget, Zeke, that desired changes may have tremendous secondary impact. An example would be
the installation of a public sewer system in a town such as Lunenburg, which with its ample developable area and its
fine school system is already growing at a slightly more than comfortable rate. Besides costing every resident an arm
and a leg, and costing the persons who live in the particular area to be sewered two arms and two legs, it spawns the
risk of runaway growth, as previously unbuildable lots become buildable. Our State legislators are continually faced
with proposals to outlaw large-lot zoning in cities and towns that have sewerage. If any such legislation should be
passed, which might result in the reduction of Lunenburg's lot sizes from an acre to 1/4 that size, our growth would in-
deed be uncontrollable. Therefore, Zeke, this fall when the matter of sewerage comes up, you'd better hot-foot it to
the Town Meeting, and if by any chance the Meeting votes to embark on this project, you'd better run for the hills!

Building Inspector
The State Building Code and the establishment of a building regulatory system became effective January 1,
1975 in accordance with M.G.L.A. Chapter 802, Acts of 1972. Accordingly, the respective Boards of Selectmen
of the Towns of Lunenburg, Shirley and Townsend held several joint meetings to discuss the feasibility of forming a
Tri-Town Building Inspector Board not only to comply with the State Building Code, but to minimize the expense to
each community. Subsequently on February 27, 1975, I was appointed Tri-Town Building Inspector.
Basically, the State Building Coderequires that the Building Inspectorbe responsible for the examination of all
applications and the issuance of permits as prescribed by the Code for the construction, reconstruction, alteration,
renovation, repair, demolition, removal, signs, use and occupancy ofall buildings includinga Certificate of Inspec-
tion annually to all buildings that accommodate more than twenty persons. The required permit stated above is in
addition to any other permit required by statute, by-law or regulations, such as plumbing and gas permits, sanitary
permits, etc., all of which may or may not be applicable to a respective application.
Ordinary repairs to buildings and structures may be made without application or notice to the building offi-
cial; but such repairs shall not include the cutting away of any wall, partition or portion thereof, the removal or
cutting of any structural beam or bearing support, or the removal or change of any required means of egress, or re-
arrangement of parts of a structure affecting the exitway requirements; nor shall ordinary repairs include addition to,
alteration of, replacement or relocation of any standpipe, water supply, sewer, drainage, drain leader, gas, soil,
waste, vent or similar piping, electric wiring or mechanical or other work affecting public health or general safety.
Applications and information relative to building permits may be obtained at and completed applications de-
livered to the Selectmen's Office, Town Hall. Any questions relative to the State Building Code may be answered
by contacting me any weekday preferably during the hours 8:00 - 10:00 A.M. at the Memorial Building (Legion House)
on Memorial Drive (Telephone: 582-4613).

Highway Department
Keep your eyes on that speedometer as you travel
along the highways these days, for to travel beyond the
55-mile per hour limit could help prevent Massachusetts
from getting approval for Federal-aid highway projects
after January 1, 1976. This is the word from the U.S.
Department of Transportati which has amended its regu-
lationon state certification of speed limit enforcement to
list quite precisely just what information each state must
submit to obtain necessary certification for this approval.
The Department of Transportat requirement is that each
state describe its enforcement policies, list the number of
citations issued for speeding each month, and describe the
program for monitoring highway speed. Summed up
in brief, it means that after January 1, 1976, there will
be no certification approval for those Federal-aid projects
which can go a long way toward helping US have better
highways in our state. That is, unless we adhere to the
rules.

Veterans' Services
During Fiscal Year 1976the budgets for this Department were keptat a minimumbecause all recipients 65 years

Through the process of elimination, a veteran cannot
remain on the rolls if he is qualified to seek gainful em-
ployment. We make an investigation of same and request
a medical report from his family physician. Veterans are

Tree Warden
Bacillus thuringiensis (Thuricide) is a microbial insecti-
cide that is non-toxic and non-pathogenic to man and warm-
blooded animals, fish and honeybees, but is capable of induc-
ing fatal disease in certain insects, mostly caterpillars. Also,
on the basis of evidence to date, this bacillus is harmless to
parasitic and predatory insects and other forms of life, but is
useful against such insects as cankerworm, fall webworm, gypsy
moth, elmspanworm, linden looper and some control of the tent
caterpillar.
Chlordane is not permitted for use on foliage pests but may
be used against Japanese beetle and other turf insects, carpenter
ants, black vine weevil and other principally boring-type in-
sects. Endosulfan (Thiodan) is labeled to prohibit drift in resi-
dential areas and may be used in nurseries, etc., or applied by
paint brush in residential areas, and isone of the longest lasting
aphicides available. It is also effective against many borers,
i.e. dogwood, rhododenron, lilac and peach, and will control
many leaf defoliators, beetle larvae and eriophyid mites. It is

Dog Officer
Dog Officer, August N. Misner, feels that our most perplexing problem last year was the nuisances caused
by the dog population in and about the Center of Town-specifically the disturbances on the grounds of the Elementary
School. Generally, these dogs follow the walking school population causing disturbances before, during and after
school hours, and most recently causing traffic hazards on public ways as well as at the entrance and exit to the school
property. In most cases, the owners of these animals have been most cooperative, but I feel that it will be neces-
sary in the future to order individual dogs restrained permanently if these nuisances and safety hazards continue as in
the past. (By authority of Chapter 140, Section 147 M.G.L.A.)

animals the most usual call dealt with complaints relative to a neighbor's dog and the most unusual calls dealt
with skunks in cellars and muskrats caught in fishing lines at Lake Whalom. One small deer was struck by a motor
vehicle on Summer Street, ran off into the woods where it was too severely injured to fight off an attack by dogs in
the area. Numerous accounts of animals molesting livestock were reported and the matter subsequently resolved.

200 licenses were issued through the Town Hall. I wish at this time to thank Mary Padula, Town Clerk, and her

Article 8. To see if the Town will vote to insure future compliance with the National Flood Insurance Program by adopting
the following resolve: WHEREAS, certain areas of Lunenburg are subject to periodic flooding causing serious damages to properties
within these areas; and WHEREAS, relief isavailable in the formof Federally subsidized flood insurance authorized by the National
Flood Insurance Act; and WHEREAS, it is the intent of this Town meeting to require the recognition and evaluation of flood hazards
in all official actions relating to land use in the flood plain areashaving special flood hazards; and WHEREAS, this body has the legal
authority to adopt land use and control measures to reduce future flood losses pursuant to the constitution and laws of the Common-
wealth of Massachusetts: NOW, THEREFORE BE IT VOTED, that this Town Meeting hereby: 1. Assures the Federal Insurance Ad-
ministration that it will act upon, as necessary, adequate land use and control measures with effective enforcement provisions con-
sistent with the criteria set forth in Section 1910 of the National Flood Insurance Program Regulations; and 2. Vests the Board of
Selectmen with the responsibility, authority and means to (a) Delineate or assist the Administrator, at his request, in delineating
the limits of the areas having special flood hazards on available local maps of sufficient scale to identify the location of building
sites. (b) Providesuch information as the Administrator may request concerning present uses and occupancy of the flood plain. (c)
Cooperate with federal, state, and local agencies and private firms which undertake to study, survey, map, and identify flood plain
or mudslide areas, and cooperate with neighboring communities with respect to management of adjoining flood plain areas in order
to prevent aggravation of existing hazards. (d) Submit on the anniversary date of the community's initial eligibility an annual report
to the Administrator on the progress made during the past year within the community in the developmentand implementation of flood
plain management measures. 3. Appoints a Town Agent to maintain for public inspection and to furnish upon request a record of
elevations (in relation to mean sea level) of the lowest floor (including basement) of all new or substantially improved structures
located in the special flood hazard areas, If the lowest floor isbelow grade on one or more sides, the elevation of the floor imme-
diately above must also be recorded 4. Agrees to take such other official action as may be reasonably necessary to carry out the
objectives of the program, or act anything thereon. VOTED TO PASS OVER ARTICLE 8.
Article 9. To see if the Town will vote to insure future compliance with the National Flood Insurance Program by adopting
the following resolve: WHEREAS, the Town of Lunenburg has adopted and is enforcing the Commonwealth of Massachusetts State
Building Code, and WHEREAS, Lunenburg the aforesaid prohibits any person, firm or corporation from erecting, constructing, en-
larging, altering, repairing, improving, moving or demolishing anybuilding, or structure without first obtaininga separate building

construction or substantial improvements to determine whether proposed building sites will be reasonably safe from flooding. If a
proposed building site is in a location that has a flood hazard, any proposed new construction or substantial improvement (including
prefabricated and mobile homes) must (i) be designed (or modified) and anchored to prevent flotation, collapse, or lateral movement
of the structure, and (ii) use construction materials and of equipment that are resistant to flood damage, and (iii) use construct
methods and practices that will minimize flood damage; and 2. That the Town Agent shall review subdivision proposals and other pro-
posed new developments to assure that (i)all such proposals consistent with the need to minimize flood damage, (ii)all publicutil-

Article 20. To see if the Town will vote to establish a Water Study Committee consisting of seven members to be chosen by
the Board of Selectmen, provided, however, that three shall be voters-at-large of Lunenburg and four shall be members of the Dev-
elopment and Industrial Commission, Lunenburg Planning Board, Board of Water Commissioners of the Lunenburg Water District and
Lunenburg Conservation Commission, respectively, any vacancies which mayoccurto be filled by the Board of Selectmen, said Com-
mittee to study the present and future water needs of the Town; develop a master planwith timetables and cost estimates where pos-
sible, including in said plan, but not limited to, future and domestic and industrial water needs, ecological and environmental con-
siderations, and the advisability of the Town assuming the responsibility for water supply and distribution, said Committee to report
to the Town not later than thenext Annual Town Meeting, said report to contain the Committee's findings with specific recommenda-
tions regarding water needs over the next five year period and general recommendations concerning the water needs for the five year
period thereafter; the Board of Selectmen to have the right in its discretion to extend the life of the Committee but not beyond the
Annual Town Meeting of 1978, or act anything thereon. VOTED THAT THE TOWN ESTABLISH A WATER STUDY COMMITTEE TO
STUDY THE PRESENT AND FUTURE DOMESTIC AND INDUSTRIAL WATER NEEDS OF THE TOWN, SUCH COMMITTEE TO CON-
SIST OF FIVE MEMBERS, ONE VOTER AT LARGE WHO RESIDES IN THE WATER DISTRICT AND ONE VOTER AT LARGE WHO
RESIDES OUTSIDE THE WATER DISTRICT TO BE APPOINTED BY THE SELECTMEN AND MODERATOR, AND THREE ADDITIONAL
MEMBERS, ONE TO BE APPOINTED THE DEVELOPMENT AND INDUSTRIAL COMMISSION, ONE TO APPOINTED BY THE
PLANNING BOARD AND ONE TO BEAPPOINTED BYTHE BOARD OF COMMISSIONERS OF THE LUNENBURG WATER DISTRICT
FROM THEIR RESPECTIVE MEMBERSHIPS, SAID COMMITTEE TO REPORT ITS FINDINGS TO THE TOWN NOT LATER THAN THE
NEXT ANNUAL TOWN MEETING, SAID REPORT TO INCLUDE SPECIFICRECOMMENDATIONS CONCERNING THE NEED FOR
EITHER A PROFESSIONAL ENGINEERING WATER SURVEY TO PROVIDE THE TOWN WITH A MASTER PLAN WITH TIMETABLE
AND COST ESTIMATES WHERE POSSIBLE, OR A CONTINUING WATER NEEDS STUDY INDEPENDENT OF A PROFESSIONAL
SURVEY, AND IF A PROFESSIONAL SURVEY IS RECOMMENDED THE REPORT SHALL INCLUDE THE APPROXIMATE COST OF
SUCH PROFESSIONAL SURVEY AND A SUMMARY OF ITEMS TO BE INCLUDED THEREIN.
