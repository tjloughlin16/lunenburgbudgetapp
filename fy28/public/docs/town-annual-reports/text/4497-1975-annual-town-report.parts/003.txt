OPEN HOUSE:
Again we conducted Open House on two different days this fall. The response from the commun-
ity was most gratifying. We estimated that approximately 300 parents, grandparents, and friends
toured the building and watched classes in action.
Mr. Starr showed a video-tape segment of the Inside-Out series which is used by some of our
science teachers the second half of the school year. The Inside-Out video films deal with values
and is part of our Health Education Program.
We wish to thank the Parent-Teacher Organization mothers who served coffee during the Open
House.
GUIDANCE AND SPECIAL SERVICES:
Mrs. Miriam Light is the new Guidance Counselor in the Middle School, replacing Mrs. Willow
Shire who accepted a position in the Walpole School System.
Mrs. Light is a very capable person with a very good background in education and an expertise
in Guidance.
With the advent of Chapter 766, approximately 45% of her time is taken up with referrals by
teachers and parents. Most children that are referred must go through an evaluation process that
-108-
===PAGE 111===
requires a great deal of time. After the evaluation process, children found in need of special
services must have an educational prescription written up and are then referred to special resource
teachers who follow up on the educational prescription.
The rest of Mrs. Light's time is devoted to individual guidance, group guidance, career occu-
pations, and a host of other areas in need of service.
A new position was created this year in Guidance and was filled by Mr. William McDonald. Mr.
McDonald divides his time between the T. C. Passios School and the Middle School. With so much
time devoted to Chapter 766 and other special services it was imperative to take some of the load
away from the Elementary and Middle School counselors. It was also felt that we needed the male
image in counseling on both the Elementary and Middle School levels. Thus far, it has worked out
very well and more children are receiving services than ever before.
Mrs. Marybarbara Hughes is our new resource teacher this year replacing Mrs. Sharon Migliozzi
who requested and received a transfer to Grade 5. Mrs. Hughes is a special resource teacher and
devotes her time to children who are in need of special services. Mrs. Hughes works closely with
the classroom teachers and other special resource teachers to see that children in need of special
services are given as much help as possible.
CURRICULUM:
There have not been any great changes in curriculum this year. Most of our Math classes are
following the I. M. S. program (Individualized Math System) that was initiated in the Elementary
School two years ago. Our science program in grades 5 and 6 is called S. C. I. S. (Science Cur-
riculum Improvement Study). This program has been modified by the Middle School teachers to suit
the needs of the student and also to save expenses. In the area of Social Studies we are using the
Data Bank approach in grades 5 and 6. The Reading program, under the very capable direction of
Mrs. Lillian Griffiths, has become highly individualized which we feel will serve the needs of more
students. Mrs. Griffiths has also initiated a remedial reading program this year for some students
in grades 5, 6 and 7. The remedial reading teacher, Mrs. Gail Boucher, was employed through the
C. E. T. A. program (a federally-funded program). At this time, there is some question as to
whether the C. E. T. A. program will be funded again. We hope the federal government will see fit
to continue this worthwhile program.

MIDDLE SCHOOL PHILOSOPHY:
I feel that the children in the Middle School are very fortunate to have a faculty that phil-
osophically is more student-orientated than subject-orientated. The staff certainly strives for
academic success for all students, but they also realize that each child has different strengths
and weaknesses and must be viewed as an individual. The staff capitalizes on each child's
-109-
===PAGE 112===
strengths and tries very hard to help each child in areas that need improvement. All faculty mem-
bers set academic standards that are realistic and attainable for each child. Of course some stu-
dents need more "prodding" than others which can create problems from time to time. But, I liken
this to a coach who at times gets after his players, not because he doesn't like them but because
he wants them to live up to their potential.
We also try to instill self-discipline rather than impose a great deal of discipline. If
students respect parents, teachers, and their fellow students, problems such as vandalism are kept
to a minimum. Fortunately, most parents in the town believe in this approach and are very cooper-
ative with the school. This cooperation between 95% of the parents and the teachers is reflected
in the condition of our building and the good rapport between most students and teachers.
Middle School students are also very fortunate to have facilities in the areas of Homemaking,
Industrial Arts, Music, Physical Education and Art. Being exposed to these personal development
subjects gives a child a broader resource base for life and makes for a happier child.

tive Assistants; the faculty, secretaries, custodians, Math Aides, Library Aides, and everyone else
for their dedication and effort. I would also like to thank everyone in the Superintendent's
office and the School Committee members for their continued cooperation and support.

A total of 132 visits were made to parents of school children concerning physical problems,
school absences, vision and hearing failures, and Chapter 766 follow-ups. Many contacts are also
made by telephone; and the nurses are always available for conferences with any parent, either at
school or home.
The Health Program in the schools includes vision and hearing screening done annually on all
children. Parents are notified of any failures; with referral forms to be taken to an eye or ear
doctor and returned to the school SO we can follow the doctor's recommendation. The vision and
hearing screening is also offered to pre-school children. In the spring all children entering
kindergarten in the fall are asked to come in for a screening program; 3 and 4 year old youngsters
will also be tested if the parents request it. In the testing of these young children it is possi-
ble to pick up the "lazy eye" while it may still be easily correctible under doctor's care. Be-
cause of a change in State Law, routine physical examinations are no longer required every three
years. In place of this, a complete physical is required on entering school; transferring students
must have a recent physical; any child with frequent complaints or absences will be examined; stu-
dents being evaluated under Chapter 766 will have a physical; and comprehensive examinations will
be given all students involved in active sports programs.

an equally rewarding opportunity to serve the health needs of the children of Lunenburg in 1976.

School Lunch
In the cafeterias "We're Movin' On". As
you can see we began September with a healthy
cash balance in spite of the common factors you
Report
all see and experience with increased wages and
food costs. Our new equipment has made for
greater efficiency, better prepared foods,
larger storage and less waste. Our walk-in
freezers (remember how we obtained these for the
cost of dismantling and installing only) have
enabled me to buy and plan more advantageously.
With the freezer space we now have, waste is nil.
Our a la carte program in the High School
has taken root and is growing. Someday I even wonder if it will overtake the Type "A" tray-meal.
Following the belief that teenagers appreciate variety and should be able to make choices, we offer
wholesome and nutritional choices: Each day we make a soup or a chowder. Our sandwiches are
hearty--club, B. L. T. Submarines and the like. We offer a variety of salads and every day there
are apples and oranges. Now and then we surprise the students with cookies or pastry and of course
you never run a cafeteria without little packages of peanut butter and crackers!
Our efforts have gained us two "A's"--"A" for atmosphere and "A" for attitude. I can sense a
tremendous change here both from within and without toward us and from us. We also have "Happy
Days". Our little folks get "special day fancies" on holidays. Did you hear we had the
"Horribles" served our Hallowe'en meal at the Middle School and Santa's Helpers in the tasseled
hats and white beards served our Christmas dinner at the High School. Our weight guessing contest
was really fun. 365 high schoolers tried to guess the weight of our Giant Pumpkin. 101 lbs. was
the winning guess with eight other teenagers winning free lunches. This contest generated a lot
of interest and fun.
A long needed bakers oven has been installed in our Middle School through participation in
the Non-Food Assistance Program of the Bureau of Food and Nutrition. A heavy baking program with
only two convection ovens often created problems at the Middle School especially with the Annex
satellite feeding program also being prepared there.
All in all I feel we're really "cooking" now and we're not stopping there, "We're Movin' On".
Senior, Kenneth Chester,
contemplates a la carte
offerings.
