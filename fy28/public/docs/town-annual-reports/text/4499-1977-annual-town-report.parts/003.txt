HAROLD K. CARRIER -
August 26, 1968 - August 19, 1977
Harold began as a junior custodian in the
Passios Elementary School. Effective January
1, 1971 he was appointed by the School Commit-
tee to assume the responsibility of senior
custodian at the Turkey Hill Middle School.
In accepting his intention to retire, the
School Committee expressed their appreciation
for his contributions to the school during his
nine years of service. His pride and care of
the Middle School, as well as his cooperative,
understanding leadership as President of the
Lunenburg Custodial Union, were two points of
appreciation stated by the Committee.
A retirement party was held by school
staff on September 15 for Harold at which time
they wished him "bon voyage" on his pending
trip to Hawaii and many happy years of retire-
ment.
In Memory of
ALBERT H. BALCOM
On February 13, 1977, A1 passed away in
Naples, Florida at the age of 77. For the past
few years he had been making his home in Tahini
Mobile Village, Estoro, Florida.
A1 began as custodian on October 12, 1953
in the then brand new Lunenburg Elementary
School. He remained at the Elementary School
until March 25, 1957 at which time he trans-
ferred to the Lunenburg Jr.-Sr. High School
and assumed the duties of Head Custodian. He
remained in this position until his retirement
On July 21, 1967, having completed fourteen
years of "devoted and conscientious service.' 11
During his tenure he also served as School
Attendance Officer.
He was survived by his wife, one daughter,
three grandchildren and five great-grandchil-
ren, and several nieces and nephews.

School Nursing Report
Lunenburg now has its own staff of nurses directly under the control of the school depart-
ment, effective this September, as a result of a change in providing community nurse coverage made
by the Board of Health.
Headed by Jean E. Hobbs, R. N. ,as full-
time coordinating nurse and nurse at the
Elementary School, the other two staff mem-
bers are Marie B. (Ginger) Richards, R. N.,
part-time at the Middle School, and Jean M.
Larkin, R. N., part-time at the High School.
Dr. Joel Neuschatz is the school physician,
with Dr. Gary Asher assisting with special
needs assessment physicals and as a member
of the Health Education Curriculum Committee.
To continue the focus of the school
health program on preventative health
measures and early detection of possible
health problems, the school nurses function
in many areas. In order to comply with state
regulations for immunizations required to
attend school, a complete check was made of
all health records, and parents were con-
(L-R) "Ginger" Richards, Jean
tacted in an effort to determine immuniza-
Larkin, Jean Hobbs
tion status. At clinics held in conjunction
with Nashoba Associated Boards of Health,
the following immunizations were given:
169 M-M-R (Measles-Mumps-Rubella) to grades K-6 and boys, grades 7-12; 59 Measles to girls, grades
7-12; 98 Tetanus-Diphtheria to grades one and eleven; and 47 Polio to grade one. The end result
of this activity was that all in-coming kindergarten students were immunized and many older stu-
dents brought up-to-date on their immunization series.
Also, through Nashoba and the school nursing staff, 63 Tine tests were done on first-grade
students, and 121 Mantoux tests were done on school personnel to certify them as being free from
tuberculosis. Students in grades two, five, and eight are able to participate in a dental flouride
program, and students in kindergarten and grade three may have their teeth checked by the dental
hygienist.
Vision and hearing screening is done annually on all students, as required by state regula-
tions. Parents are notified of failures, with referral forms to doctors returned to the school.
Physical examinations are required for all students entering school for the first time, for trans-
fer students, for all students involved in junior-senior high school competitive sports, for stu-
dents evaluated under Chapter 766, and for students with frequent absences and complaints. Hear-
ing and vision screening is done on all entering kindergarten students, and on any three or four
year old upon parent request. When necessary, conferences with parents are held in school or over
the telephone, with the nurses available at anytime to talk to parents who have questions or con-
cerns.
The postural screening program for students in grades five through eight, developed in 1977,
is continuing under the guidance of the Department of Public Health. The initial screening is
done in gym class, with re-screening by the school nurse. Parents are contacted if there is need
for further evaluation by a physician.
Both Mrs Hobbs and Mrs. Larkin are members of the Health Education Curriculum Committee, and
it is anticipated that in the future the school nurse will be more involved in health education on
a formal basis. However, every time a school nurse sees a child in the health office, there is an
informal session of health education. A Red Cross Multi-Media First Aid class was also taught to
grade-ten students by Mrs. Hobbs and Ms. Claire Friedrich, science teacher at the high school.

The nurse's role in Chapter 766 is increasing. as are the number of students involved. Lunen-
burg nurses make home assessment visits to gather birth and develop mental history, and serve as
liaison with private physicians and as medical resource person in core evaluation team meetings.
To keep current with changes in the medical field and new programs available for the school
health programs, the nurses are members of the American School Health Association and the Massa-
chusetts School Nurses' Association. All three nurses attended a five-session school nurse seminar
in addition to a workshop on scoliosis screening, conducted by the Department of Public Health,
Central District, and have participated in various programs offered by the Massachusetts School
Nurses' Association, Fitchburg State College, and Nashoba Associated Boards of Health. Mrs. Hobbs
participated in a three-day workshop for "Mandated Reporters of Child Abuse and Neglect," pre-
sented by the Office for Children. In turn, a program was conducted for local school administra-
tors, guidance counselors, and nursing staff, involving representatives from the Department of
Welfare and police, on problems of child abuse and neglect, and procedures to be used in reporting
suspected instances.
In conclusion, the school nursing staff is pleased to be able to continue the excellent school
health program conducted in the past by the Lunenburg Public Health Nursing Service and is looking
forward to expanding the health services offered students in the Lunenburg school system.
