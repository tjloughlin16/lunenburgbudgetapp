It goes without saying that without good tax collections, the Cash Management program discussed in the Treas-
urer's report would not amount to much. In the very near future, all persons with past due property bills will receive
the following schedule of Tax Takings, As these dates will be strictly adhered to, delinquent taxpayers are urged to
contact their mortgaging banks immediately or take such other steps as will assure compliance.
Tax Taking for failure to pay all Fiscal 1977 taxes:
May 10, 1978
Tax Taking for failure to pay all Fiscal 1978 taxes:
October 3, 1978
Tax Taking for failure to pay all Fiscal 1979 taxes:
June 23, 1979
The State has also speeded up the processing of Motor Vehicle Excise, which means that many individuals who were
heretofore accustomed to receiving bills for vehicles registered on January 1 some time in the fall or even later, will
already have received their bills for 1978 by the time this report is distributed. Judy Anderson and Posey Carey are
pictured below commencing to process a voluminous box of excise bills received from the State, while Shirley McBride
and Sharon Guertin receive and process payments.

Since the Lunenburg School Committee has recently approved this grade reorganization plan
with implementation scheduled for next September, it is important to present the following detailed
grade reorganization report as part of this annual message.
GRADE REORGANIZATION
I. BACKGROUND RATIONALE
In the spring of 1976 the Lunenburg School Committee approved the following recommendation:
"Effective with the 1978-79 school year, the 8th grade will be relocated in the
Middle School and the 5th grade will be relocated in the Passios Elementary School. The
kindergartens and first grades will be relocated in the Elementary Annex. The 3rd grade
would move from the Annex to the Passios School. The position of Annex supervisor will
be eliminated with these duties to be assumed by the Elementary Assistant Principal.
"A plan of implementation which would include any revisions in the program of stud-
ies for the 5th and 8th grades, schedules, the recommended building reassignment of
staff, and recommendations for needed building modifications le. 9., removal of walls,
carpeting, folding partition) should be drawn up and presented to the school committee
prior to January 15, 1978.
"In 1978-79, the High School and Middle School students should ride the same buses
and be on similar time schedules."
The plan to reorganize grades in Lunenburg schools has been made feasible by the steady and
gradual decline of pupil enrollment since 1972. The factors contributing to this decline have
been well documented and discussed in past studies both locally, regionally, and nationally. It
is estimated that by September 1978, Lunenburg pupil enrollment will have dropped by 222 students
- 89
===PAGE 92===
over the 1972 level. With fairly stable conditions predicted, future enrollment projections as
calculated in 1977 by Dr. John Marshall, educational consultant, call for a further decline of
another 200 students between 1978 and 1982. By 1982, it is expected that our pupil enrollment
will be approximately 1800 students. Through the 1980's pupil enrollment should begin to level
off and then gradually start to increase.
The major reasons for grade reorganization are as follows:
1. We need to remove 8th graders from the high school. This move is long overdue.
Certain academic and social problems are created in a grade 8 to 12 building.
With 8th graders in the middle school, many of their present group identity and
social pressure problems will be eliminated. Academic scheduling and academic
credit problems will also be alleviated.
2. From a growth and development standpoint, a K-5, 6-8, 9-12 grade reorganization
represents a more effective mixture and arrangement of students. The research
indicates strong support for this type of organization. Also, such a grade
organization serves as a good base-line for the future if pupil enrollment
should begin a sharp incline. Building additions or new schools could then be
planned around the K-5, 6-8, 9-12 sequence. The new grade organization also
provides flexibility, in that, depending upon future enrollments and available
space, grade 5 can-either continue in the Passios School or be shifted to the
middle school as part of a grade 5 through 8 organization.
3. Long-range, the housing of the kindergartens and first grades at the Annex has
certain educational advantages. The smaller, more intimate school building
should be a positive factor in making the initial school experience for children
a successful one. Also, much can be said for creating an early childhood educa-
tion center in one building where first grade and kindergarten teachers can work
cooperatively and strive for greater individualization and personalization of
instruction. If enrollment at this level should surprisingly increase in future
years (it is expected to sharply decline), the Annex can easily be expanded and
modified to handle this contingency.

VI. CURRICULUM IMPLICATIONS
There are several changes created by reorganization that will have a significant effect upon
our present curriculum. These changes are necessitated by either (1) declining course enrollment;
(2) lack of facilities; (3) conflicts in scheduling; (4) the emergence of other needs and priori-
ties; (5) a sounder philosophical rationale; or (6) new opportunities to make program improvements.
The changes are as follows:
1. A formal program in Industrial Arts and Home Economics will no longer be offered
5th graders. If feasible, we plan to incorporate many of the home and industrial
arts skills and concepts in a new Friday activity program for 4th and 5th
graders.
- 94
===PAGE 97===
2. Instrumental Music will continue to be offered 5th graders; however, because of
scheduling conflicts, the format will be different. A double period of instruc-
tion will be provided once a week; however, additional practice time will have
to be scheduled after school at least one afternoon per week. If our music
staff is unable to be scheduled for this after-school responsibility under their
contractual obligations (i. e., 7 hour and 5 minute school day), the after-
school session will be treated as a personal development activity (similar to
the Middle School proposal) and instructors will be paid an hourly rate. The
after-school session would also be contingent upon the availability of late
buses.
3. The formal foreign language program (Spanish and French) currently in 7th grade
will be dropped. It is recommended that this program be shifted to 8th grade
and that four years of foreign language be offered in grades 9 through 12.
Declining enrollments and relatively small class sizes in foreign language
classes during the past few years make this move feasible. With a total of five
years of foreign language still available for students, it will still be possible
for accelerated language students to complete the 5th level of our language pro-
gram. In recent years with six years of language experience available for all
students, there have been an insufficient number of students electing a Spanish
V and French V. (According to our policy, in order to justify an approved
course at least twelve (12) students must elect it.) Although it is anticipated
that there may be enough students to justify a Spanish V class next year, future
prospects do not look good since the High School, similar to the Elementary
level, is shortly about to feel the effects of declining enrollment.
Because teaching loads appear to be relatively light among foreign language
teachers in the High School, it is also recommended that thought be given next
year to scheduling available High School language teacher(s) to 7th grade
communications classes. Working cooperatively with Dr. Feinberg and 7th grade
teachers, the foreign language teacher could teach a unit in foreign language
readiness and an orientation to a foreign language and culture. Such an orienta-
tion would provide 7th-graders with a brief introduction to the study of another
language and provide some basis for their selection of French or Spanish in 8th
grade.
4. Eighth grade athletic programs (girl's and boy's basketball) as currently organ-
ized will be eliminated. Budget money for these programs will be transferred to
the new after-school personal development program at the Middle School. Eighth
graders will be allowed to try out for ninth-grade teams only where there are

WHO ARE THE CHILDREN AND YOUTH RECEIVING SPECIAL SERVICES?
In general, the special needs population in Lunenburg is like that of other communities
though we have no children identified as blind or profoundly deaf at the present time. We have
young children in the age range of 3 years to 8 years unable to walk and talk. In addition to
their nursery activities they receive speech therapy and physical therapy. Children with repaired
cleft palates require speech correction. Some of these children have been left with problems as a
result of birth injury, childhood diseases or accident.
Physical differences in children's bodies must be dealt with as we teach but must not be a
basis for not providing educational opportunity. Other children have problems from causes as yet
unclear to medical science, and many of our learning disabilities youngsters continue within that

HOW ARE STUDENTS IDENTIFIED FOR SPECIAL SERVICES?
Children's problems may initially be discovered by teachers, parents, therapists or
physicians. A Core Evaluation Team (c. E. T.) referral is to be made on any child whose physical
or mental condition may substantially affect school performance.
Generally, the C. E. T. is comprised of an administrator, classroom teacher, school nurse,
psychologist, and learning specialist. Assessments are made of a student's strengths and problem
areas. The team includes the parent at the time of sharing information and developing an educa-
tional plan.
HOW ARE CHILDREN REGISTERED AS HAVING SPECIAL NEEDS?
The determination of special need results from the decision of a Core Evaluation Team. It
is not surprising that most children who are presenting problems as a reason for being referred
are, subsequently, found to have special needs for services. The study of the child is an indi-
vidual matter and the Core Team may conclude that some children have no special needs or need
monitoring as a minimum of service. Some mild special needs may be substantial in the view of the
team while other needs are not.
A student-centered educational plan is developed by the Core Evaluation Team and the parent.
The child's name is entered on the special needs register when the child receives services. The

Project Greenthumb is an exciting new program with a "split" personality. Students alternate
study in two different areas: greenhouse management and forestry. During any given marking
period, one-half of the students enrolled in Project Greenthumb study conservation and applied
forestry techniques with Mr. Douglas Fleming; the other one-half study horticulture and green-
house management with Mr. Charles Tarleton, teacher, and Mrs. Linde Sacksen, aide. At the end of
each marking period, the students switch sections. The program is made possible through Title
IV-C, which provides Federal funds for innovative programs in education.
Project Greenthumb provides an alternative to general biology for high school sophomores, and
allows an individualized plan for selected students from other grades. During the morning students
are enrolled in courses of a traditional length, including English, Record-keeping, and an elective
subject. During the afternoon, three class periods are spent in Project Greenthumb.
Some of the highlights of the Greenthumb plan include:
(1) Our own mini-bus for field trips and transportation to work experiences.
(2) Training and use of student leaders to assist in supervising work experiences
and demonstrating proper techniques.

Retirements
NELDE K. DRUMM - September 17, 1955 - June 30, 1977
In 1955, Mrs. Drumm was hired as an art teacher in
the high school on a one day per week basis. Her responsi-
bility was increased to three days per week beginning
September 1, 1957. On September 1, 1958, Mrs. Drumm was
appointed Art Supervisor for the system on a full-time
basis.
The current high quality of the school system's art
program can be contributed to Mrs. Drumm's diligent
efforts, dedication and leadership over the years. The
exceptional level of excellence attained by many of Mrs.
Drumm's past students also is a tribute to her and the
outstanding program she gave shape and direction to from
1955 to 1977.
LILLIAN S. GRIFFITHS
- September 1, 1941 - June 30, 1944
- February 6, 1950 - June 30, 1977
Mrs. Lillian (Szocik) Griffiths began her teaching
career in Lunenburg as a junior high teacher in the old
high school building (now the Annex) on September 1, 1941,
resigning in June 1944 to be married. She returned in
February 1950 as a grade two teacher in the old Center
School to complete the 1949-50 school year. On September
1, 1957 she was appointed as Supervisor of Reading and
Allied Subjects, the position she held until her retire-
ment.
During her thirty years of service as a teacher and
supervisor, Lil's qualities of human warmth and dedicated
interest in children and her continued deep professional
commitment, loyalty, and conscientiousness contributed to
her uniqueness as a professional. The current quality and
success of Lunenburg's elementary reading program can be
directly attributed to her outstanding efforts and skills.
Lil was honored last June by the entire Lunenburg school
staff at a testimonial held at the Old Mill. Current and
past faculty members and many of her friends were present
to pay tribute to this very special person.
BARBARA R. PEASE - October 24, 1960 - June 30, 1968
- September 1, 1968 - June 30, 1977
Mrs. Pease began her tenure in Lunenburg as a guidance
secretary in the high school. She resigned from this
position in June of 1968.
In September of 1968, Mrs. Pease was elected as a
home economics teacher at the high school, retiring in
June 1977 after nine years of service.
In addition to her duties as a home economics teacher,
Barbara served as an understanding confidante and counselor
to many of her students. Her pleasant and friendly
personality and cooperative professional attitude will be
missed.
