QUESTION NO. 2 and LAW PROPOSED BY INITIATIVE PETITION
Do you approve of a law summarized below, which was disapproved by the House of Representatives on May 6, 1980, by a
vote of 5-146, and on which no vote was taken by the Senate before May 7, 1980?
SUMMARY - The proposed law would limit certain taxes, and change laws relating to school budgets and compulsory binding
arbitration. It would impose a limit on state and local taxes on real estate and personal property equal to 2½% of the full and fair
cash value of the property being taxed. If a locality currently imposes a tax greater than 2½% of that cash value, the tax would
- 86 -
===PAGE 89===
have io be decreased by 15% each year until the 2½ level is reached. If a locality currently imposes a tax of less than 2½, It
would not be allowed to increase the tax rate. In either situation, a city or town could raise its limits by a 2/3 local vote at a
general election.
The proposed law would provide that the total taxes on real estate and personal property imposed by the state or by localities
could never be increased by more than 2½% of the total taxes imposed for the preceding year, unless two thirds of the voters agreed
to the increase at a general election.
It would further provide that no law or regulation which imposes additional costs on a city or town, or a law granting or in-
creasing tax exemptions, would be effective unless the state agrees to assume the added cost. A division of the State Auditor's De-
partment would determine the financial effect of laws and regulations on the various localities.
The proposal would limit the amount of money required to be appropriated for public schools to that amount voted upon by the
local appropriating authority. It would also repeal the law which provides for compulsory binding arbitration when labor negotiations
concerning police and fire personnel come to an impasse. In addition, the petition would provide that no county, district, or au-
thority could Impose any annual increase in costs on a locality of greater than 4% of the total of the year before.
The proposed law would also reduce the maximum excise tax rate on motor vehicles from $60 per thousand to $25 per thousand,
and it would allow a state income tax deduction equal to one half of the rent paid for the taxpayer's principal place of residence.

QUESTION NO. 3 LAW PROPOSED BY INITIATIVE PETITION
Do you approve of a law summarized below, which was disapproved by the House of Representatives on May 6, 1980, by a
vote of 2-147, and on which no vote was taken by the Senate before May 7, 1980?
SUMMARY - The proposed law would limit local property taxes and state taxes and would provide for increased state aid for
local educational purposes.
The act would limit local property taxes in the years 1981 through 1984 to the amount levied In the previous year increased
by the percentage increase in personal income of the residents of the Commonwealth during the previous year. The local property
tax limit could be exceeded to offset decreases in local aid, to cover shortages for prior years and to pay court judgments. Pension
and retirement allowances, payments to other governmental units, principal and interest on any indebtedness, unemployment com-
pensation, amounts required to be raised as a condition of a state or federal grant, and costs for special education programs would
be excluded from the property tax limit.
These local limits would be reduced by any excess taxes actually collected over the tax limit for the preceding year. The
limit would not apply to any municipality having a general tax rate of less than $35 per thousand of equalized valuation. The tax
limit could be exceeded by a two-thirds vote of the local appropriating body.
The cost of regional and Independent vocational schools would be subject to the same limitations.
The proposed law would also limit state taxes imposed in the years 1981 through 1984 to an amount no greater than that im-
posed the previous year, increased by the percentage increase in the personal income of Massachusetts residents in the previous
year. This state tax limit could be exceeded only to increase local aid or to assume other costs approved by a two-thirds vote of
the state legislature. The amounts necessary to pay principal and interest on state indebtedness, pensions, retirement allowances,
unemployment compensation, and court judgments, and money required to be raised as a condition of a federal grant would not be
subject to the state tax limit. The total amount of local aid for any year which would be subject to legislative appropriation could
not be less than the total amount of aid for the preceding year increased by half the increase in collected state taxes during that
preceding year. The state tax limit would be reduced by any excess taxes actually collected over the tax limit for the preceding
year.
The proposed law also would require, subject to legislative appropriation, a gradual increase in the percentage of local ed-
ucational costs paid by the Commonwealth to a level of 50% in 1984. The proposal would also require, again subject to legislative
appropriation, that school aid paid by the Commonwealth in any year between 1981 through 1984 must be at least 15% greater than
that provided in 1980.

QUESTION NO. 4 - REFERENDUM ON AN EXISTING LAW
Do you approve of a law summarized below, which was approved by the House of Representatives on November 1, 1979, by
a vote of 83-62, and which was approved by the Senate on November 1, 1979?
SUMMARY - The law provides for increases in the salaries of members of the legislature and the constitutional officers of the
Commonwealth. The law increases salaries of members of the legislature by an annual amount varying from $1,853 to $17,923. The size of the
raise conferred on a particular Individual depends upon his position within the legislature. The law has the effect of settingthe
base salary for a legislator at $20,335, but under the law legislative salaries range as high as the approximately $55,920 paid to the
President of the Senate and the Speaker of the House of Representatives.
The salaries of the constitutional officers are increased either by $20,000, in the case of the Governor, or $10,000 in all
other cases. The law raises the annual salary of the Governor to $60,000, that of the Attorney General to $47,500, and the salaries
of the Lieutenant Governor, Secretary of the Commonwealth, Treasurer and Receiver General, and the Auditor to $40,000.
The law also amends the statutes pertaining to the organization of the offices of the Secretaries of Administration and Finance
and of Human Services and to the compensation of senior officials within those offices. It gives the Secretaries of Administration
and Finance and of Human Services greater flexibility in establishing positions and setting salaries for those under their supervision.

THE PAST YEAR:
This past year brought us many surprises and wonderful experiences. In January and February of
this past year, we worked very hard on completing one of the goals that we had established the
previous year. That goal was to establish a five-year plan to update, renovate and improve our
buildings. On May 19th, we presented to a special town meeting a proposal for a Two Million dollar
revision of our present buildings and septic systems. The townspeople overwhelmingly voted against
the proposal but what was accomplished, I feel, is that we were able to present to the town future
concerns that would have to be dealt with. Such problems as roofing problems, failed septic
systems, poor energy efficient buildings and specific lack of facilities for handicap students just
do not go away by voting against them. They are problems that will have to be dealt with in a
different manner, but they will have to be dealt with.
I feel we ended the last year in June financially solvable and having accomplished some major
goals. We completed and submitted our Basic Skills Program Plan to the State Department of Educa-
tion. This plan was approved and through the efforts of John McCarthy, Department Head in English
at the High School, the plan was disseminated and published in the local papers. I must congratu-
late John and his very large committee for their fine efforts to produce this plan and to further
continue to be involved for its implementation.

Advisory Committee that was made up of parents, staff and students. These committees are in their
second year of involvement with our principals and have been a source of excellent information,

a "classroom family" where sharing, mutual respect, and caring is evidenced. It has been our ob-

the learning process throughout the year.

MAINTAINING THE BASICS:
The Lunenburg Public Schools have developed a
standardized testing program which is administered in
selected grades on an annual basis. The grade levels
identified for complete mathematics and reading evalua-
tions in the elementary school are grades two and four.
All students in these grades are required to take eight
subtests which comprise the Stanford Achievement Test.
Last spring there were 149 second grade students
tested. The combined average grade level score for the
five subtests in reading for all second graders was 3.8.
This translates to third grade eighth month, or one full
year above the national average of students who were
tested. The reading average for fourth grade students
at the Passios School was 6.5 or just under two years
above the national average.
In the area of mathematics the combined subtest average score in grade two was 3.3 or approx-
imately one-half year over the national average, while in grade four the average was 5.7 or
approximately one year above the national average.
The Stanford Achievement Tests are not intended to be a specific determinant of student
achievement. They do, however, provide us with an annual objective indication of how our children
compare with their peers throughout the country. Over the past four years in which we have used
the Stanford Test our scores have maintained the same consistent high average.

MIDDLE SCHOOL READING PROGRAM VALIDATED
BY DEPARTMENT OF EDUCATION:
In the spring of 1980, school systems were
invited to submit descriptions of their reading
programs to the Massachusetts Department of Edu-
cation. Validation criteria (79 items) were
established by the Massachusetts Right to Read
Effort. The purpose of validation was to recog-
nize reading programs which were exemplary, pro-
viding a model for other schools by meeting
standards in categories of: Community and School
Climate; Organizing and Managing a Reading Pro-
gram; Staffing a Reading Program; Selecting and
Utilizing Materials; Fostering Reading Interests.
We submitted our Middle School Reading Pro-
gram for validation because the validation
ROSE M. FEINBERG, Ed. D.

At a Validated Reading Programs Conference in October, Lunenburg and Framingham were the two
systems recognized as having met the 79 criteria supported by written evidence and by a visiting
committee which met with teachers, specialists and administrators. Lynn Fletcher (Middle School
reading specialist) and Dr. Rose Feinberg (Director of Reading) discussed the unique features of
the Turkey Hill Middle School Reading Program and distributed materials describing the program to
the two hundred educators selected to attend from school systems across the state. Mr. Floyd
Barney, Turkey Hill Middle School Principal, and Dr. William C. Allard, Superintendent, also repre-

PROJECT LITE IN GRADES 5 AND 6:
The English programs in grades 5 and 6 have been enriched by PROJECT LITE (Learning Inte-
grated through Esthetics), an approach integrating arts with basic skills.

In grade 5, PROJECT LITE teaches nouns, pronouns, verbs, adjectives and adverbs using art,
music and movement. In grade 6, PROJECT LITE teaches creative writing, poetry and research skills
using music and art.
The classes used the program on a trial basis Winter/Spring 1980. We then applied for and
were granted a Title IV-C adoption grant. These funds enabled us to obtain the excellent materials
of PROJECT LITE for our system on a permanent basis. PROJECT LITE is used in addition to our
English textbooks and has had positive learning results.

JUNIOR GREAT BOOKS PROGRAM:
In the fall of 1980, the reading department became interested in Junior Great Books, a pro-
gram of interpretive reading and discussion for students in grades 2-12. In this program, stu-
dents "develop the habit of reading critically, interpreting what they read, and supporting their
interpretations with ideas and facts from the readings.' Dr. Rose Feinberg and Lynn Fletcher
received the necessary leadership training in the fall of 1980 and will be training teachers and
volunteer parents to lead Junior Great Books groups in grades four, six and eight. Funding for
the books is supported by a Title IV-C federal grant written by Dr. Goodrich.

earned in the reading/language arts area, for they are the ones who play the most important part
