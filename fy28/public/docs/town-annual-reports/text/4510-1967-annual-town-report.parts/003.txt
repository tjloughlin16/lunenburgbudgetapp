LIBRARY SERVICES
For the first time in the history of the
Lunenburg Elementary Schools, we have had the
services of a librarian this year for three
days a week. We are fortunate in having the
services of a most qualified librarian, Mrs.
Eleanor Whitcomb. In the past, the work has
been done by the Reading Supervisor and volun-
teer mothers whose services have been greatly
appreciated.
It is the purpose of the Elementary School
Library to furnish educational material in ad-
dition to the regular textbooks available, and
books that are worthwhile and fun to read. The
emphasis for the younger children is on the
pleasure reading can bring. There are two
types of books for this age group - picture
books which are self-explanatory, and the be-
ginning readers to give the younger child the
pleasure of a book he can read by himself. The
upper elementary students need books to aid

A Man's Mind may be likened to a garden, which may be
intelligently cultivated or allowed to run wild; but whether cul- -
tivated or neglected, it must, and will, bring forth. If no useful
seeds are put into it then an abundance of useless weed-seeds,
will fall therein, and will continue to produce their kind.
Just as a gardener cultivates his plot, keeping it free from
weeds, and growing the flowers and fruits which he requires, SO
may a man tend the garden of his mind, weeding out all the wrong,
useless, and impure thoughts, and cultivating toward perfection
the flowers and fruits of right, useful, and pure thoughts. By pur-
suing this process, a man sooner or later discovers that he is the
master-gardener of his soul, the director of his life. He also re-
veals, within himself, the laws of thought, and understands, with
ever-increasing accuracy, how the thought-forces and mind-elements
operate in the shaping of his character, circumstances, and destiny.
- JAMES ALLEN

School Nurse
The Town of Lunenburg employs two full time registered Nurses, Mrs. Doris M. Lahti and Mrs.
Gladys V. Barney, who do Public Health Nursing as well as the School Nursing.
During 1967, 244 visits were made to parents of school children for: follow up on some physi-
cal defects found by the School Doctor, vision and hearing failures, health supervision and absences
from school. If absolutely necessary, transportation was arranged to doctors, dentists and special
clinics.
The State requires all students, in competitive sports and Grades 4, 7 and 10, have annual physi-
cal examinations. Many of these physicals are done by students own family doctor; the rest are done
by School Physician, Dr. John DeLeo.
The State of Massachusetts set up a measles immunization program this year in an attempt to im-
munize all children who had not had the disease. One dose is presumably life long protection.
Through the Nashoba Associated Board of Health, the vaccine was offered to school children in Grades
1 through 6. A total of 213 children received the measles vaccine, most of the others who had not
had measles have previously been protected by their own doctor or in the Lunenburg Well Child Clinic.
In December of 1967, a law became effective in the Commonwealth of Massachusetts which requires
every school child - Kindergarten through Grade 8 - to be immunized against Small Pox, Diptheria,
Tetanus, Pertussis (Whooping Cough), Measles and Polio, unless exempted for Medical or religious rea-
sons.
If a school child has not had above immunizations, the parents will be asked to have family
physician sign a slip saying immunizations will be given. It is the feeling of the Nursing Staff
that, with few exceptions, our school children have received all of these valuable immunizations.
Our school system has been most fortunate in being able to keep immunizations at a very high
level through the programs offered each year by "The Nashoba Board of Health" as follows:
Diptheria and Tetanus Boosters and re-vaccination against Smallpox - Grades 1, 6 and 11.
Grades 1 only - Trivalent Oral Polio to children who have not previously had vaccine and a
Booster dose to those who have already completed series.
Tine Testing for Tuberculosis is administered to Grades 1 and 9 with follow up on any posi-
tive reactors and their families.
Dental Fluoride Treatments to Grades 2, 5 and 8 given by Mrs. Clarence May, Dental
Hygienist.
All the above programs require signed permission from parent and are certainly well accepted
with few refusals.
Vision and hearing testing are done on all students annually. Notices of failures are mailed
home to parents with a doctor's form attached. This form is to be returned to the nurse, so that the
school may best follow your doctor's recommendations in helping your child.
We are grateful for the cooperation of the School Superintendent, Principals and all other
school personnel, without whose cooperation it would be impossible to have our health programs so
successfully carried out.
Respectfully submitted,
DORIS M. LAHTI, R. N.

to high school students as well as 3,025 free

REPORT OF TITLE III ESEA - P.L. 89-10 PROGRAM
I hereby submit the first annual report
on Title III ESEA for the year 1966-67.
At a time when education is commanding
National concern, Lunenburg applied for and
received a federal grant $27,570.81 to pursue
an idea to aid preschool children prepare for
the first grade. The importance and value of
such an idea is emphasized by the knowledge
that the Lunenburg project was one of only
three-hundred awarded the grant out of some
2,000 applicants from across the nation.
Many people contributed to the success of
the project. Among them were PTA mothers, stu-
"See What I Can Do"
dent teachers from the State College at Fitch-
Late morning activity, using his hands to
burg, and well-known consultants. Sixteen mem-
create something before the class.
bers of the Lunenburg Elementary staff contri-
buted to better education by helping to pre-
pare these children for school. Rounding out
the staff of professionals were eight social
workers from the Mental Health Clinic in Fitchburg. Much of the project's success came from the
willingness of the parents of some 100 children, who allowed their child to participate in this pro-
ject. It speaks well for the parents of Lunenburg and their dedication and interest in quality edu-
cation for their children.
Saturday morning Intervention Sessions were held from January to June for a number of children
who for one reason or another were considered unready for the first grade. Having had this addition-
al boost helped many of these children to acquire the necessary strength so vital to a successful
start in school.
It is as yet too early to determine the
full effect the project will have on these
children. However, there have been some
healthy signs emerging that indicate that the
program is having some positive effects.
We are very fortunate here in Lunenburg to
have had Federal financing for an educational
idea that was conceived locally and to be able
to nurture it into a strong trunk from which
better educational excellence will blossom and
grow. We are confident that with the continued
cooperation from the parents the project will
have its desired effect on all children both
in Lunenburg and across the nation.
