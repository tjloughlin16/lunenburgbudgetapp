MEMORIAM
ROBERT VOWLES
1931 - 1994
Bob was born in Lunenburg on January 1931. He graduated from St. Bernard's
Central Catholic High School in Fitchburg in 1949 and Fitchburg State College in
1966. While a student at Fitchburg State College, he was also a Teacher at
Lunenburg High School. Bob also worked as a Driving Instructor in the City of
Fitchburg. He came to work in Lunenburg n 1971 as a Police/Fire Dispatcher. On
July 1, 1987 he was elevated to Head Dispatcher. During his 23 years of service to
the Town, Bob developed may forms and codes still used by the Police/Fire today.
He reluctantly retired on July 31, 1994 because of a prolonged illness. Bob was a
highly motivated, intelligent individual who excelled in what he did. His devotion to
his job and the Town of Lunenburg were second to none. Mr. "V" will be sadly
missed by all of us and especially those who worked with him.

BOARD OF SELECTMEN
1994 has shown the Town a number of changes, many of which would not have been
accomplished without the cooperation and hard work of various Town officers, boards,
committees, departments and commissions.
Construction of approximately 5,600 ft. of ten inch sewerline along Route 2A was
recently completed and we anticipate the Proponents will be gifting this sewerline to the
Town in Early 1995. The Board of Selectmen/Sewer Commissioners have also
experienced a great property owner interest in Sewering areas of the Town. They expect
to allow abutters along the Route 2A stretch, who were given the opportunity to connect
from their property boundry to the pipe to tie on to this line in mid 1995.
The Sanitary Landfill ceased operations as of April 30, 1994. At this time the Board of
Health began licensing private haulers for trash removal in the Town. The closure plans
have been submitted to the D.E.P., and capping is expected to take place in the Summer
of 1995.
The Town has also realized modernization by the re-construction of the Center and the
installation of the first set of traffic lights. Shortly thereafter the second set was
installed at the recently completed retail development at the junction of Routes 2A and
Route 13. After a few minor adjustments all seems to be running smoothly and the Board
has received many positive comments regarding these improvements.
At the Town Hall efforts have continued towards installing and networking a new
computer system among the various departments located here. Other improvements to
the building have continued throughout the year, of particular note is the repairing of
the Tower Clock which has not run for a number of years. The Board at this time wishes
to acknowledge the volunteer efforts of three citizens who gave of themselves and worked
a number of nights and weekends to repair the 100 year old clock which was gifted to the
Town by Jacob H. Fairbanks in 1894. It is with deep appreciation that the Board
recognizes Robert N. Fickett, Edward P. Dextraze and Jon E. Jameson for all their
expertise and hard work in restoring this treasure from the past.
In conclusion the Board would like to thank many of the individuals who work for the
Town or who volunteer their time and commitment on behalf of the Town by serving on
many boards, committees and commissions. Without their hard work and dedication the
successes we have had during 1994 would not have been possible.
AMERICANS WITH DISABILITIES ACT COMMISSION
The $15,000 voted at the May 1994 Annual Town Meeting will be used to install an
elevator chair lift for a handicapped access to the newly renovated second floor at the
Town Hall.
Most major improvements in access to public buildings in Town, with the exception of
the schools, have been accomplished. There are still some minor modifications to be
made and hopefully these will be accomplished this year.
A list is being compiled of modifications necessary in the schools, with major
improvements needed if elevators were installed in all two story school buildings. The
schools, when necessary, reassign class rooms, and there is a stair rail lift at the High
School, to accommodate students unable to access certain areas.
There is still a need to provide a canopy over the ramp at the Town Hall, as ice and snow
falling from the roof presents a problem. The best way to do this is being investigated.
Work will be done in the spring to more clearly define handicapped parking in front of the
Town Hall, not done as part of the recent road and sidewalk construction in the area.

BOARD OF ASSESSORS
1994 was a year of change in the Assessors' Office with the retirement of Board Member
Paul Gagliardi. Assistant Assessor, Judy Holden and Clerk Lisa Normandin resigned from
their positions in 1994. Lisa has accepted a position in the Building Department for the
Town of Lunenburg. The Board wishes to express their appreciation for their service to
the Board and the Community.
The Board welcomes our new elected Assessor Christopher Comeau, a long time
Lunenburg resident, who will bring management skills and a strong knowledge of our
community.
Due to the structural change in the Assessors' Office, a study was made of the assessing
needs in the region. It was found that similar communities in the area also required the
expertise of a professional Assessor. Those communities most in need were Townsend,
Ashby and Lunenburg. It was determined that it was most cost effective if these
communities share the expense of a professional Assessor. With the approval of the State
Department of Revenue and the three community bodies, the Regional Board was
created. A search was conducted in August to fill the newly created position of Regional
Assessor.
Thru extensive interviewing, the Regional Board selected Mr. Harald Scheid, who
previously held the position of Town Assessor in the community of Lexington for 6 years.
Harald brings to Lunenburg not only his expertise in assessing but a broad knowledge in
computer skills, enabling the Assessors office to interact with other departments.
Part of this transition year included a new Assessors' staff. Dorothy Musgrove joined the
staff in June as part-time Clerk. Her previous experience includes 10 years as Sr. Clerk
in the Fitchburg Assessors Office. Norma Frost joined the staff in August as Principle
Clerk. She offers to the community an extensive knowledge of real estate and
management skills. Both Norma and Dorothy took on the challenge with great
enthusiasm.
Due to the staff changes and reduced hours, a public access computer was installed in the
foyer of the Town Hall. This computer enables the public to access property record
information during Town Hall Hours.
The Board of Assessors' wish to thank the community for their patience thru this
transition year. We will continue to work with Lunenburg taxpayers to promote a clearer
understanding of assessment functions and procedures regarding property valuations.
The Board of Assessors' and its staff look forward to continuing its relationship with
taxpayers in resolving questions they have regarding their assessments. Complete
information regarding the various tax exemptions are available to qualifying taxpayers
and may be obtained at the Assessors' Office or by calling 582-4145.

CABLE ADVISORY COMMITTEE
This is my second opportunity as Chairperson of the Lunenburg Cable Advisory
Committee (CAC) to apprise the citizens of Lunenburg of our committee's activities.
The CAC is a standing committee of the Board of Selectmen. The current members of
the committee include; former CAC Chairperson Edward Dextraze, Lance May and
Patrick O'Malley, Secretary.
Our mission, as always, is to make recommendations to the Board of Selectmen to help
ensure that the current cable television license holder, Cablevision Inc., complies with
their duties outlined in the existing license. To this end, the CAC meets on a monthly
basis with Cablevision, Inc. During those public meetings the CAC and Cablevision, Inc.
are available to answer questions associated with cable television service to the citizens
of Lunenburg. It is important to note, that the meetings are designed to address cable
television issues raised by any citizen of the Town, not just cable television subscribers.
For example, issues discussed may include whether Cablevision, Inc., plans to cable
certain areas of the community that are not currently being services by the license
holder. In addition, other subscriber related issues are also addressed. The CAC also
researches and makes recommendations to the Board of Selectmen on other cable
television issues that can have an impact on the citizens of Lunenburg.
Lunenburg's current cable television license expires in 1996. As a result, during 1994,
the majority of the CAC's activity involved contract negotiations in pursuit of the new
cable television license agreement. It is important to remember, and somewhat sobering
to realize that a new agreement, whoever the vendor, will secure the right to deliver cable
television service to the Town of Lunenburg into the next millennium. To that end, the
CAC during 1994 sought bids from other contractors. It is our belief that given the
constantly changing contour of the telecommunications industry, we would be remiss in
our duties not to investigate alternative ways of delivering cable service to the citizens of
Lunenburg.
Regrettable, during the course of 1994 the CAC, through the Board of Selectmen, was
forced to convene a public hearing to determine whether Cablevision, Inc. was in breach
of their agreement with the Town. A public hearing should not be confused with the CAC
monthly public meetings. A public hearing is full evidentiary hearing that allows for the
swearing of witnesses and the admission of evidence to determine if a party is in breach
of contract. This dispute, sprang from Cablevision, Inc., reneging on a 1993 written
promise to cable the Mulpus Road area of Lunenburg. At the public hearing, after some
spirited debate, Cablevision, Inc. chose to reaffirm their 1993 written commitment, and
begin construction as soon as possible. Cable installation of the area should be
completed by the Spring of 1995.
It is this committee's profound belief, as exemplified by the citizens of the Mulpus Road
area, that the best way to ensure the continued quality of service and to shape
Lunenburg's new agreement is for the citizens of Lunenburg to clearly articulate the
types of services they would like to see from a license holder. Therefore, I would urge all
citizens of Lunenburg (both subscribers and non-subscribers) to make an effort to attend
our scheduled monthly meeting and let their views be known. This process enables the
CAC, Cablevision, Ind., or other future license holders to clearly understand the cable
television needs of the citizens of the Town of Lunenburg. The date and time of the
CAC's monthly meetings are posted in the foyer of the Town Hall.
Prepared by Leon Weaver, Chairman, Lunenburg Cable Advisory Committee.

COUNCIL ON AGING
This year, the Council on Aging amended their Mission Statement to more accurately
reflect its role in the community and its services for elders.
"The Mission of the Council on Aging is to advocate for elder issues and promote the
physical, emotional, and economic well-being of older adults by responding to their
diverse needs and interests, enhancing their dignity, supporting their independence, and
to offer programs beneficial to all members of the community without regard to economic
status, race, color, creed, or disability."
One way this Mission is carried out is by the operation of the Eagle House Senior Center,
through the employment of a professional director, staff and from volunteers. In Fiscal
Year 1994, 26,274 units of service were provided for 42 percent of residents over the age
60, plus 258 units of service for non-elders. (Non-elder services include Commodity Food
distribution, Flu Clinic, information and referral services and intergenerational
programs). Since Fiscal Year 1991, the first year statistics were compiled, the number of
elders served has increased by 24.5 percent, while the services to non-elders has almost
tripled.
The increase is due in part to participation by more elders but also to new programs and
activities. This year Formula Grant funds from the Executive Office of Elder Affairs paid
for a part-time Outreach Worker, to visit elders in their homes to assess needs and make
referrals if warranted. The Serving Health Information Needs of Elders (SHINE) program is
conducted by two trained volunteer counselors who dispense information on health
insurance options and help solve problems with coverage. Periodic meetings of the
"Breakfast Club" offered educational programs on a variety of topics. Intergenerational
activities in conjunction with the Lunenburg Public Schools, hearing and podiatry
screening, and a 10 week nutrition program are examples of program expansion.
Long-time volunteer Elvira May Kennedy, former Council Member and Chairman and Trip
Coordinator, was honored at the annual Volunteer Recognition Reception held to show
appreciation of the over 3,700 hours of assistance contributed by a most dedicated group
of individuals. If the Council on Aging had to pay for the time given by volunteers, the
COA budget would need to be increased by some $36,000. The formation of an active
support group, "Friends of the Eagle House, Inc.", with a membership drive and plans for
further fund raising, will lead to continued financial support for the Eagle House, beyond
that which is provided by the Town.
The opening of the Eagle House Senior Center in 1990 gave Lunenburg elders a place to
call their own, and provided the Council on Aging a base for operation of elder services.
Even before the doors opened, it was felt the facility would be too small to meet the
needs of the elder population. Four years later, we have strong evidence that more space,
both for staff and programs and activities is necessary now, and to prepare for the future.
The Board of Selectmen have formed a Building Committee composed of representatives
of the Council on Aging, Town boards, elder groups, and citizens at large to oversee a
grant application to the Executive Office of Communities and Development Small Cities
Program to fund an addition to the center. Despite the degree of competition and the
comprehensive process, the Council on Aging looks forward to a successful outcome of
this endeavor.
Members of the Council on Aging in 1994 were Jean Hobbs, Chairman; Nicholas Rimpas,
Vice-Chairman; and Raymond DeLisle, Paul Doherty, Jane Doyle, Eleanor Gay, Frieda
Jacobs, Judith Jennison, Beatrice Stepton, Frances Therrien, and Agnes Withington. Staff
included Jeanne DeBruin, Director; Nancy Rimpas, Secretary; Nancy Sullivan, Outreach
Worker; Gerald Beloin, Raymond Stone, and Harry Hager, Van Drivers; Norman
Winchester, Custodian; Sandy Cole, Meal Site Manager; and Virginia Blair, Elder Service
Corp Worker. Sandy Hartenstein is Volunteer Meals on Wheels Coordinator and Bea
Stepton, Volunteer Tour Coordinator. The Meal Site Manager position, formerly funded by
the Green Thumb Program, is presently vacant.

BOARD OF HEALTH
Rabies reports continued at a high level during the year, though not as high as 1993. A
total of 110 raccoons, 5 possums and 1 skunk were destroyed for suspected rabies. Only
those destroyed animals which have bitten or scratched someone are sent to the State
Laboratory for testing. The good news is that none of the 3 specimens sent to the State
Laboratory tested positive for the disease.
The Town landfill on Young's Road closed in the Spring. Wanting to ensure a smooth
transition, the Board instituted a licensing procedure for refuse haulers in Town. In order
to obtain a permit, haulers must offer recycling. A total of 8 haulers were granted
residential permits for 1994. The transition from landfill to private haulers has been
relatively smooth as there were only a few incidents of roadside trash disposal. The
landfill has not been capped. This will be an expensive procedure over the next several
years.
The Sewer Commissioners completed their negotiations with the City of Fitchburg for a
tie in to their sewer system for a portion of Massachusetts Avenue. The Lunenburg
Crossing shopping mall, which opened in October, sends its sanitary waste to Fitchburg.
The Board supports efforts for future tie-ins to the Fitchburg Sewer System.
Perhaps the most important event of the year was the promulgation of the final version of
the Massachusetts Title 5 Regulations for the siting, construction, inspection, upgrade
and expansion of on-site sewage treatment and disposal systems and for the transport
and disposal of septage. With some exceptions, these regulations will take effect on
March 31, 1995. These regulations represent the first change in State subsurface sewage
disposal requirements since 1978, 1995 promises to be an interesting year as we
implement the new regulations.

CENTRAL MASSACHUSETTS MOSQUITO CONTROL PROJECT
The Central Massachusetts Mosquito Control Project (CMMCP) now provides its services
to twenty seven cities and towns throughout Middlesex and Worcester Counties. The
project is pleased to announce that the towns of Stow and Blackstone became members of
the CMMCP as of July 1, 1994. Several other towns will be considering joining the
project at their spring town meetings. This growth helps us achieve regional mosquito
control.
The project is headquartered in a modern facility located at 111 Otis Street, Northboro
MA. Tours of the headquarters or visits to field work sites can be arranged by calling the
office in advance.
The project utilizes an Integrated Pest Management approach to mosquito control. This
type of a program blends several methods and techniques with expertise, experience, and
scientific research to provide the member communities with modern environmentally
sound, cost effective mosquito control.
Our public education efforts again this year were highlighted by the Mosquito Awareness
Program offered to all elementary schools within the project. Project staff meet with
students and teachers to discuss mosquito biology, mosquito habitat, and what they and
their families can do to prevent mosquitoes from breeding around their homes. The
program includes a slide talk show, handouts and coloring books, samples of live
mosquito larvae, and the opportunity for students and teachers to ask questions.
The project's Water Management Programs continue to show positive results. By
cleaning clogged and overgrown waterways, mosquito breeding is reduced, wetlands are
restored, and water quality is improved. This form of mosquito control although labor
intensive yields great dividends and helps to reduce the dependence on pesticides.
Areas where mosquito larvae are found are treated with BTi mosquito larvicide to prevent
their emergence. We encourage the public to notify us as to any areas they suspect could
breed mosquitoes. Field crews will investigate all such sites and treat as needed.
The project strives to handle all mosquito problems with water management or
larviciding but recognizes that there are times when adult mosquito spraying is the only
viable solution. In such cases residential and recreational areas are treated with either
handhold or pick-up truck mounted sprayers.
The project's surveillance program monitors adult mosquito and larval population density
and is the backbone for prescribing various control techniques.
The project's video "Working for You" is available to anyone interested in learning more
about mosquito control and the services provided by the Central Massachusetts Mosquito
Control Project.

FIRE DEPARTMENT
The Fire Department responded to the following calls in 1994: Chimney-4, Structure-26,
Standby MVA-13, Service-19, Alarm Activation-57, Outside Electrical-14, Investigations-
34, Brush-24, Medical-5, Extrication-2, Illegal Burning-12, Animal Ice Rescue-3, Mutual
Aid-32, Motor Vehicle-12, Hazardous Materials-13, False-8, Good Intent-3, Search-1,
Aircraft Standby-2, Detail-1, Rescue/Ambulance-382. Total calls for 1994 were 667. This
represents an increase of 98 calls from the previous year.
The Town was very fortunate in that only one major structure fire occurred last year.
Although our calls for service increased by close to 100 runs, there are a number of
reasons that our major fire incidents are low. One is that we do a lot of work with the
children in the school system and pre-school. We work directly with the children and
also with the teachers to teach then about fire safety and fire prevention. It has paid off
in less fires. The other area that we work hard in is code enforcement and inspections.
This process brings us in contact with potential fire problems early and allows us to
prevent them. Also, proper enforcement to insure installation of sprinkler and alarm
systems at the time of construction saves us problems and money later on.
We will receive a new fire truck in March 1995. This will complete the replacement of
our older apparatus and put us in good shape for the present time with the exception of
not having a ladder truck. This is a continuing problem along with the need for a new
station. These needs have been talked about for a number of years and are still pressing.
Of more immediate need is that we address the constant underfunding of the operating
budget. We have the second largest department in Town and operate with the smallest
budget of all major departments i.e. Schools, Police, Highway and Fire. For years we have
been robbing Peter to pay Paul and it is now catching up to us. We need to seriously look
at starting to fund the Fire Department budget adequately so we can keep up with the
growth of the Town. If we continue the path of always trying to play catch up, someone
is definitely going to get hurt, or worse yet, killed. I urge you to study our needs and
inform your elected and appointed officials that you would like to see your Fire
Department adequately funded so that we can adequately protect you and your property.
Our firefighters place huge demands on themselves and their families so that they can
put in the time required to protect the citizens of Lunenburg from fires and also in the
emergency medical field. As their Chief, I commend them for their efforts and I extend
my sincere thanks to them for their service.

MONTACHUSETT REGIONAL VOCATIONAL TECHNICAL SCHOOL
Superintendent-Director Stratos G. Dukakis is pleased to report that during 1994, Monty
Tech administrators and School Committee members focused many of their efforts and
resources on bringing the school into compliance with the recently adopted state
Education Reform Act. To this end, a School Advisory Council and Parent Advisory
Committee were established in an effort to provide administrators with parent,
community and business input and direction in formulating future policy and goals.
20
===PAGE 23===
Mandated changes in professional development and recertification requirements for staff
are also being addressed.
Monty Tech is proud to be a local leader in the federally funded School-To-Work
movement, known as "Communities and Schools for Career Success" or CS2. Along with
our neighboring school districts in Leominster and Fitchburg, Monty Tech is
participating in this exciting new initiative, established to better prepare students to
make a smooth transition from school to a work environment.
School Choice also went into effect at Monty Tech this past year, allowing students
residing in other voc-tech school districts in the state to attend Monty Tech.
Monty Tech's 23rd Annual Commencement Exercises were held Friday, June 3, in the
Vietnam Veterans' Memorial Physical Education Complex at Mount Wachusett
Community College, Gardner. A total of 178 members of the Class of 1994 were
presented with their diplomas and trade certificates. Guest speaker was the Honorable
John W. Olver, U.S. House of Representatives, 1st Congressional District.
At the annual Awards Night program, held here at Monty Tech on Tuesday, May 31,
members of the graduating class were recognized and honored for their many
accomplishments in the areas of academics, vocational-technical skills, athletics and
civic contributions.
In keeping with an ongoing trend, many of Monty Tech's recent graduates continue to
postpone full-time employment until after completing college and technical school.
Some of the institutions members of the Class of 1994 were accepted to include:
University of Massachusetts at Lowell and Amherst, University of Maine, Massachusetts
Maritime Academy, Johnson & Wales University, Franklin Pierce College, Bradford
College, Liberty University, Endicott College, Daniel Webster College, Mount Wachusett
Community College, Hesser College, Becker College, and Fitchburg, Framingham,
Worcester, Salem, Bridgewater and North Adams state colleges.
Those choosing to go directly into the work force were able to find work in their trade
areas. A few of the 73 area companies hiring Monty Tech grads this past year include:
Baker Main Street Motors, Banner Mold & Die Co., Victorian House Restaurant, Dr. Daria
Karos, Simplex Time Recorder Co., Ranor Inc., Dupuis Power Equipment Corporation,
Bisson Tool & Die, Athol Plumbing & Heating Supply, United Plastics, New Wing Designs,
Courier Printing and Nypromold Inc.--to name just a few.
Some of the class opted to join the Armed Forces, where they were selected for training
in the following specialties: nuclear electrician, intelligence, security forces, aircraft
mechanics, military police, bio-medical equipment repair, construction equipment
operator and artillery\ computers.
As it commenced its 24th year of operation on August 31, Monty Tech continued to
further its primary goal of implementing programs and updating equipment to ensure its
students are able to successfully meet the challenges of a highly technical and
competitive work force of the 21st century. Some of the improvements for the new
school year include: the hiring of a full-time social worker; expansion of the Tech Prep
program--which allows eligible graduates to attend Mount Wachusett and Quinsigamond
community colleges and Worcester Technical Institute with advanced standing--to
include more programs and more institutions of higher learning; establishment of a Peer
Mediation program; inclusion of Drama and multi-cultural Literature into the English
curriculum, and full implementation of a fine Arts curriculum, Also, the staff is
continuing its two-year evaluation process for re-accreditation by the New England
Association of Schools and Colleges, Inc.
A long-range goal was realized this past year with the establishment of the Monty Tech
Athletic Hall of Fame. Twenty outstanding athletes, along with Yvonne St. Jean and her
late husband, Roger, founders of Monty Tech's first athletic booster clubs, were inducted

Twice a year, the public is invited to visit Monty Tech and see firsthand what vocational-
technical education in the 90's is all about, and what it has to offer students and
taxpayers. Each year, these two events--Open House, held October 26, and Career
Awareness program, held March 8--consistently attract many prospective students and
interested members of the community.
On the first Sunday in October, a record number of visitors attended an Open House at
this year's house built by students and staff in the Monty Tech House Building Program.
Located off Bennett Road in Ashby, the spacious, eight room, contemporary-styled house

The Continuing Education Program has been providing low-cost, high-quality training
through courses in practical arts, enrichment, trade preparation and extension, and
apprenticeship training since 1971. This past year, the program reached its highest

to provide job opportunities after graduation.

SUPERINTENDENT OF SCHOOLS REPORT
It is my pleasure to submit to you my second annual report concerning the Lunenburg
Public Schools. With the passage of the Education Reform Act of 1993 school districts
have been working on compliance with the new regulations governing schools from both
programmatic and financial points. School Councils are actively working in the schools
establishing school improvement plans for the upcoming school year.
Lunenburg recognizes that the highest priority is to continue to provide an appropriate
education for every student in the schools. The mission of the Lunenburg Public Schools
is, "To develop responsible, self-motivated students through a learning environment that
is exciting, challenging and innovative." This is the foundation of our schools and
everyone on the staff strives toward this mission. To support this mission, the school
committee established the following goals for the 1994-1995 school year:
Develop a district-wide, multi-year, unified technology plan that addresses all areas of
technology utilization within the Lunenburg School System.
Continue to expand and improve the quality of communication with the community to
ensure acceptance and support of the Lunenburg School's mission, goals, and policies.
Continue to support and emphasize facilities improvement and expansion through the
school building committee, capital planning committee, and implementation of a five-
year facilities maintenance plan.
Support the school councils in their development of school improvement plans
through regular interaction, communication, and review through the administration and
direct meetings.
Continue the comprehensive review of all policies as they are affected by educational
reform but also those in need of improvement, clarification, and modernization.
Progress is being made on each of these goals as this annual report is submitted. A
school building committee is meeting and are reviewing the short and long range school
space needs to meet the demands of the growing school population. The roof on the
Turkey Hill Middle School was completed this summer as well as a new gym floor as
approved at last town meeting.
In September through the efforts of many individuals including the parents and residents
of Lunenburg that support the various fundraising activities in the schools, fifteen
computers and several pieces of software were purchased. With this purchase we continue
our efforts of bringing much needed technology to our schools. A special thank you is
extended to the Lunenburg PTO for their support of this effort and generous donation. I
am pleased to report that in January, 1995, the Lunenburg Public Schools was awarded a
grant from the state that will allow the purchase of fifteen additional computers for use
by students in the schools.
Our work on reviewing and revising the curriculum in grades K to 1 has been going as
planned. By February, 1995, the revised mathematics curriculum will be ready for review
by the school committee for adoption for the 1995-1996 school year. At the present time
teams are working on the science, industrial technology and health curriculums. All the

job well done.
Words cannot express my appreciation to all those involved in our schools. From the
staff, administration, volunteers, support organizations and individuals that provide so

on behalf of the community and students to ensure quality education for all students. I
would like to thank the committee for their support of all those involved with the

Lunenburg continues to provide a strong education that prepares students with a wide

TURKEY HILL MIDDLE SCHOOL
The student enrollment at Turkey Hill Middle School increased from 549 students during
the 1993-1994 school year to 585 for school year 1994-1995. Principal, Mary Cringan
and Assistant Principal, Jeffrey Mulqueen along with the entire staff recognize their
responsibility in preparing young adolescents to be successful, productive and
contributing members of a changing global society.
The THMS School Advisory Council developed their first annual School Improvement
Plan. Council members Pam Trainque, Irene Masciarelli, Maryann Kane, Sally Ahokas,
Betty Burnap, Mariam Cronin, Sharon Kimball, Carol McManus and Mary Cringan wrote
the components of the plan after seeking input from parents, staff and community
members from the results of surveys, questionnaires and informational meetings.
Academic excellence remains a high priority at THMS. The entire staff works very hard
to provide the maximum number of opportunities for all students to learn. It becomes
the responsibility of the student and family to do the rest. It is our expectation that
parents, students and teachers will work together in making the middle school years a
most positive, rewarding and academically challenging experience for all. The four basic
curricular areas in grades five through eight are: language arts, science, social studies
and mathematics. In grade eight, all students have the opportunity to study either
French, Spanish or Latin. Special Area subjects (art, music, home economics, technology
education, health and physical education) offer a unique element to the middle school
curriculum. These special subjects provide valuable experiences for students.

PALMS, Partnerships to Advance the Learning of Math and Science. Noreen Berry,
Lunenburg's PALMS Specialist, along with teachers Susan Petrowicz and Deloris Gudaitis
are providing programs for parents and training for teachers in new ways to teach and
integrate math, science and technology. Eric Beers, grade eight and Mariam Cronin,
grade seven Language Arts teachers were selected by the Massachusetts Department of
Education to participate in the New Standards Project. The New Standards Project (NSP)
is adapting a set of very high national education standards and developing a new kind of
assessment system designed to gauge student progress toward those standards. Mr.
Beers and Mrs. Cronin are just two of thousands of classroom teachers across the country
who are playing an important role in building practical new assessments, with the help
from leading experts in assessment, learning and curriculum. LEAF, The Foxboro
Company and the Polaroid Corporation have joined with us to help with this project. This
partnership is pioneering ways for schools and businesses to collaborate to further the
academic successes of students.

HIGH SCHOOL ANNUAL REPORT 1994
Frank A. Sambuceti
Lunenburg High School offers a comprehensive program of studies for students in grades
9-12. Since over 75 percent of the graduates of Lunenburg High School typically pursue
higher education, our curriculum provides a rigorous program of college preparatory
courses in English, mathematics, science, social studies and foreign language.
Complementing this academic core are quality offerings in business education, art,
music, industrial technology and physical education.
Lunenburg High School also provides an extensive, high quality co-curricular and extra
curricular program. Our students are encouraged to participate in the many clubs,
organizations, events and/or interscholastic athletic teams which are available. Among
our numerous student clubs and activities are: Debate Club, Cooking Club, Youth and
Government Club, Yearbook, Student Council, International Club, Drama Club, Math
Team, National Honor Society, Nature/Environment Club, S.A.D.D., Literary Magazine,
School Newspaper, Academic Decathlon Team, Latin Club, Ski Club, National Art Honor
Society, Future Business Leaders of America, and the Amnesty International Club.
Additionally Lunenburg High School sponsors a wide variety of interscholastic sports at
the various levels of boys' and girls' competition. Our athletic program includes:
baseball, basketball, cross country, football, golf, soccer, field hockey, tennis, track and
softball.
Lunenburg High School has a long and proud tradition of academic excellence and
scholastic achievement. This was much in evidence during the past school year. In
November of 1993 we hosted, for the second consecutive year, the prestigious, statewide
Massachusetts Academic Decathlon Competition. Additionally, LHS student teams
competed successfully in numerous regional. state and even national events including:
the Mt. Wachusett "Head to Head" contest (which we won!), both the state and national
"Envirothon" and the Massachusetts Future Business Leaders of America Competitions.
Our Math Team placed high in District events as did our Latin students who
distinguished themselves at the state "Certamen" competition, which LHS once again
hosted last spring. Similar results were achieved by Lunenburg High School students in
many other scholastic competitions and national examinations. Also deserving note was
the naming of Mrs. Kathleen McGuigan of our Language Department as the Massachusetts
Classical League's Latin Teacher of the Year for 94/95.
A major focus for the high school during the past year was to further enhance building
level and system wide curriculum coordination and revision. Very considerable progress
has been realized in meeting this goal. LHS staff and administration played a central role
in the development of a comprehensive five year cycle of system wide curriculum review.
Additionally, a model for content area curriculum development and revision, including a
systematic process for curriculum approval, integration and implementation has been
established. The first of the major academic areas to be examined, mathematics, has
recently been completed. The results of these professional endeavors will be reflected in
a revamped and revitalized K-12 math curriculum in 1995/1996 which will incorporate
the highly regarded and widely accepted principles of the National Council of Teachers of
Mathematics. Similar initiatives are presently underway in Science, Health and
Industrial Technology and we confidently anticipate implementing the products of these
successful efforts the following school year.

THOMAS C. PASSIOS ELEMENTARY SCHOOL
The Thomas C. Passios School has as its goal the education of all children. It strives
toward promoting growth in a variety of areas with as many means and methods as
possible. To this end, the School seeks regularly to improve its curriculum, to meet the
needs of a diverse student population, to provide for the emotional, social, and physical
needs of students, to insure that teachers are current with educational trends, to teach
students basic technological skills, and to keep parents abreast of what is happening in
their school.
Quality schools continually assess their existing curricula in order to identify areas of
strength and weakness. This aspect of education has become more important because of
the accelerated pace of new knowledge brought about by an ever-changing world dictated
in part by sophisticated technology. Our school has remained educationally competitive
by recognizing the significance of collaborating with other schools, colleges, businesses,
parents and agencies in sharing expertise and resources.
One such program which has provided this collaboration has been the Partnerships
Advancing the Learning of Mathematics and Science (PALMS). Now in its third year,
PALMS has dramatically changed the approach to teaching and learning Math and Science
at the Elementary School. This inquiry-based (hands-on) method of instruction has
transformed a routinely teacher-directed Math and Science program into a stimulating,
challenging, and integrated student-engaged program.
During the development of PALMS, the Elementary School staff has had the opportunity
to work closely with Math and Science specialists from Fitchburg State College, the New
England Science Center, and the Activities for the Integration of Math and Science (AIMS)
program. Funding and resources for these programs have been provided by the National
Endowment of the Sciences, the Lunenburg Educational Advancement Foundation, the
Eisenhower Grant, Block Grants, the Foxboro Corporation, as well as the Massachusetts
Department of Education.
Another example of successful collaboration has resulted in the advancement of
technology in many of our classrooms. Through the combined efforts of the Lunenburg
Parent Teachers Organization, the Elementary School Fundraising Committee, and the
Lunenburg School budget, twelve IBM CD ROM personal computers have been purchased
for our third and fourth grade classrooms. Two individuals have provided training and
guidance in establishing an effective and successful classroom computer program. Lela
Male, Computer Specialist for the Lunenburg School system, serves as our technical
adviser, trouble shooter, and computer mentor. Leslie Perrault, Elementary School
Parent and Computer Consultant, provides on-going instruction/training for the staff in
the educational uses of the computer.
The Professional Development program has had to keep pace with an ever-changing
society, as well. The school considers the continuing education of its staff to be critical
to the basic education of its students. The education of our children must reflect
changing, up-to-date curricula, and teachers are the cornerstone of this philosophy. The
Professional Development program at the Elementary School encompasses a wide variety
of current educational theories and methodologies. Staff members this year have taken
part in such diverse workshops as Running Records, Personal Nutrition, Discipline with
Dignity, Science Process Skills, Developing Science Kits, Multiple Intelligences, Kids'
Kits, Computer Training, Recertification Guidelines, Special Area Teacher Roles in
Special Education, Project Read, and Alternative Assessment.

outstanding balances.

purchases under the Capital Plan for FY95. Town Meeting also approved the Capital
Plan and we will be doing a bond issue for this and last years borrowing authorizations
by July 1995. Our Auditors and the Bond Rating Agencies frowned upon the fact that in
