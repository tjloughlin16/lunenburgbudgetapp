JOHN A. LOVE (March 17, 1969 - September 26, 1970)
(July 12, 1971 - September 1, 1978)
Mr. Love first began as a custodian at the Elementary
School replacing Harold Carrier who had transferred to the
newly opened middle school in 1969. John remained in this
position until September 26, 1970 at which time he resigned to
accept another position. However, when an opening occurred at
the elementary school in 1971, he was rehired and began his
duties on July 12. After serving almost nine years, for personal
reasons, John found it necessary to retire effective September
1, 1978. His pride and care as an elementary custodian was
very much appreciated and will certainly be missed by staff
members. A quiet man, he is respected by all.
ROLAND J. SEGUIN, SR. (December 7, 1970 - July 5, 1978)
On December 7, 1970, Mr. Seguin began his duties as a custodian in the Turkey Hill Middle
School. He remained at the middle school until he applied for and was appointed as Annex custodian
effective November 1, 1973. In February 1978, Roland indicated his intention to retire effective
July 5, 1978, having completed 7½ years of service. He was commended "for his particular pride and
care of the Elementary Annex building which had greatly aided in making the school an attractive
place and pleasant surrounding for both students and teachers.'
In Memory of
ANATOLE LIMOGES (1902 - 1978)
"Nat", as he was known to all of us, passed away at the
Marshall Mall in Leominster where he was employed as a part-time
custodian, on November 21, 1978. He is survived by his wife,
two daughters, six grandchildren and several nieces and nephews.
He and his wife, Evelyn, had celebrated their Golden Wedding
Anniversary on September 17th.
"Nat" first began his service with the Lunenburg Public
Schools as a substitute custodian and on April 6, 1964 he was
appointed as our part-time groundsman. On January 1, 1965, this
position became full-time. His service consisted of duties as
a custodian, groundsman, maintenance man and general handyman.
On May 4, 1972 (his 70th birthday) he retired after eight years
of outstanding, conscientious, cooperative and dependable
service. He was respected by all and will be sorely missed.

1952. Prior to coming to Lunenburg in 1946 he had taught a half-year in Natick. ON

#3 - VIRGINIA F. WELCH, Executive Secretary to the Superintendent of Schools and Clerk to the
School Committee, COMPLETING 35 YEARS OF SERVICE ON DECEMBER 26, 1978.
"Ginny," as she is known systemwide, is a native daughter of Lunenburg, graduating from
Lunenburg High School with the Class of 1943. When she began her service back in 1943,
Lunenburg was a member of Union #24 consisting of Ashby-Lunenburg-Townsend, with the
superintendent's office located in the Spaulding Memorial School in Townsend. In 1950,
more office space was needed, and the office was relocated in its present quarters
(Brooks House) in Lunenburg. On July 1, 1954, Lunenburg withdrew from the union and
continued as a single school system. In her 35 years, Ginny has worked for five super-
intendents--Charles M. Robinson (retired, living in New Hampshire) from 1943-1949;
Maurice P. Billings (retired, living in Townsend, MA) from 1949-1962; Ralph W. Brown
(retired, living in Florida) from 1962-1970; Dr. William R. Holland (Superintendent of
Schools in Narragansett, Rhode Island) from 1970-1978; and presently, Dr. William C.
Allard since August 1, 1978. With only five superintendents in a thirty-five year
period it certainly speaks well for the high quality school system that Lunenburg has.
Ginny has experienced all phases of the central office operation as receptionist, payroll
clerk, bookkeeper, consultant, and executive secretary.

School Nursing Report
by JEAN E. HOBBS, R. N.
Coordinating Nurse
This year, a concentrated immuniza-
tion program was conducted by the school
nursing staff. A complete check was made
of all student health records to identify
students in need of immunization, using
the criteria set by the Massachusetts
Department of Public Health. Considerable
time was spent in interpreting immuniza-
tion needs to parents, initiating contacts
to private physicians, and organizing
clinics, held with the assistance of the
nursing staff from Nashoba Associated
Boards of Health. Total immunizations ad-
ministered were: 12 DPT; 236 Td;48 Polio;
101 Measles; and 137 MMR. Many parents
Mrs. Hobbs tests for student hearing.

immunized by their own physician.
The end result was that all Lunenburg students were certified as having complete immunizations,
and the administration was not forced to exclude any student, as would have been required had not
the program been successful. Credit is due, not only to the nursing staff, but to classroom
teachers and administrators who cooperated in the efforts to contact students and in the conduct-
ing of the clinics.
Other health programs carried out in conjunction with Nashoba Associated Boards of Health are
the tuberculosis testing and dental programs. Mantoux tests for tuberculosis were done on 84 of
the school personnel, and tine tests were done on 92 children in first grade. Dental Hygienists
from Nashoba offer fluoride treatments to students in grades two, five, and eight, and students in
kindergarten and grade three may have their teeth checked.
In addition to required vision and
hearing screening, postural screening is
done on students in grades five through
eight. Participating students are screened
initially in gym class by the physical
education instructor, and re-screening and
necessary referral is completed by the
school nurse. A blood pressure screening
for students in grade eight is being in-
stituted this year.
Immunization monitoring and screen-
ing programs are an important part of a
school health program, but they are by no
means all the functions of the nursing
staff. School health services include
early identification of pupil health prob-
lems, pupil health counseling, counseling
parents in regard to health care re-
sources, and then motivating parents to
obtain whatever health care is needed for
their children, providing adequate care

School Lunch Program Report
by MARJORIE H. STILLMAN, School Lunch Supervisor
Our present high school student body does not know what it is
like to have only a Type "A" lunch or what I refer to as a "tray
lunch." In 1974 I began a simple a la carte service. Like
"Topsy" it grew. With it came problems. A cafeteria designed to
prepare and serve one-type meal must change in many ways to
accommodate an a la carte service--a service offering many types
of foods. I began by making the center of our line into this
service. The cash registers went outside the kitchen on mobile
units and a display and service case installed in their place.
A la carte really "took off." For over a year there was crowding,
pushing, pilfering. Something had to be done. I certainly wouldn't have battled the crowd as the
student had to participate. Mr. Carey permitted me to try a new scheme. One end of our line was
designated "tray meals," the other "a la carte." This eliminated the center scramble, but on the
days we served a very popular meal, the tray line was long and slow.
In the spring of 1978 I began planning to establish our present a la carte service. The
promise of delivery for September opening never materialized. At last on January 2, we opened our
new a la carte line which consists of three mobile units and a milk cooler. A previously purchased
refrigerator for a la carte was moved from the kitchen. We now have a separate unit operating
under, but outside our present set up. Sue Cioffi has been appointed to oversee its operation
working with Mrs. Beaulac, Cafeteria Manager. We are in full operation offering soup, salads,
sandwiches, a hot entree, fresh fruit, milk shakes, hot chocolate, ice cream and yogurt. It is
working beautifully.

===PAGE 170===
It's new! A-la-carte!
Of interest to the taxpayer, I would like to make note that this equipment purchased for my
program comes from my revolving account and not from your school budget. The revolving account is
monies from sales and state and federal reimbursements based on meal and milk counts. I have been
able in many cases to get help from non-foods assistance,a federal program paying 75% and I paying
25% from my revolving account.
AT THE MIDDLE SCHOOL: "A la carte is great!" The word got to the Middle School. In April,
Mr. Barney asked me if I could do something in a la carte for his school. We ran a trial program
for the month of May. It was a huge success-the children were excited; the faculty supportive.
We offer a simple service here--soup, salads, sandwiches, milk, and a sweet.
I'm really proud of my program. I have a wonderful group of women carrying out my program.
The pupils seem happy, the faculty complimentary. How great it makes me feel when a faculty member
comes back from visiting a school somewhere and commends our program--that is when I grow a little
taller.
