QUESTION 1 - PROPOSED AMENDMENT TO THE CONSTITUTION
Do you approve of the adoption of on amendment to the Constitution summarized below, which was approved by the General
Court in joint sessions of the House of Representatives and Senate on May 28, 1975, by a vote of 220-53, and on September 7,
1977, by a vote of 243-20?
SUMMARY - The proposed constitutional amendment would permit the legislature to establish as many as four different classes
of real property for tax purposes. Property in any one class would be required to be assessed, rated and taxed proportionately but
property in different classes could be assessed, rated and taxed differently. The legislature could grant reasonable exemptions.
The constitution presently requires all property (other than wild lands, forest lands, and certain agricultural and horticultural lands)
to be assessed and rated equally at full value for tax purposes.

YES - 1025

HAPPY BIRTHDAY, DEAR LUNENBURG, HAPPY BIRTHDAY TO YOU!!
These words and a gigantic birthday cake were the closing scenes of the Pageant, and the final unit of the
Parade. In both instances, everyone joined in the singing and the cheers in tribute to our Town -- Lunenburg --
250 years old. What a year -- from the opening ceremony to the Ball -- and a good time was had by all !!
On January 1st, at noon on the Center Common, Rudolph Masella, Chairman of the Board of Selectmen, read
the Proclamation designating 1978 as the 250th Anniversary year. A Winter Carnival on February 4th and 5th was
complete with snow sculptures, snowmobile races, and a dance. With an appropriate ceremony on Arbor Day, April
28th, a buttonwood free was planted on the Center Common, and the Boston Post Cane was presented to the Town's
oldest resident, Mrs. Mabel Barrett.
A Woodsmen's Day at the Sportsman's Club on June 17th, was followed by the July 3rd Bonfire and 18th Army
Band Concert, and by the Firemen's Muster on July 9th. Throughout the year, many organizations conducted events
in conjunction with the 250th Anniversary contributing to the observance.
On August 1st, the actual date of Incorporation as a Town in 1728, Alumni Park was dedicated, cannons were
fired and church bells rung to mark the formal opening of Old Home Week. In a program heldon the Lower Common,
the original Article of Incorporation was read by the Chairman of the Boardof Selectmen, Sherwood G. Winchester,
and an old-time band concert was presented by the Town Band. Tour Day on August 2nd, with narrated bus tours
and a luncheon, featured visits to the three oldest houses in the Community, as well as to the Upper Town Hall and
the Historical Society. Whalom Park saluted the 250th Anniversary with a Lunenburg Day on August 3rd, with that
day's events concluding with a Block Dance and dance exhibit in Lunenburg Center. Recreation Day was observed
on Saturday, August 5th. During Old Home Week, a Hospitality Booth was in operation in front of the Ritter Mem-
orial Library, manned by volunteers, A guest book for visitors was maintained and souvenir items were for sale.

The highlight of Old Home Week was the pageant, "Under the Buttonwood Tree", performed on August 4th and
5th in the High School Auditorium, with a cast and crew of over 150. Church services on Sunday, August 6th, were
followed by family picnics, a concert by the Westminster Brass Ensemble, a parachute jump, and climaxed with the
"best fireworks ever" on the grounds at the High School.
The 250th Anniversary Parade on October 1st with its many bands and floats, was one of the biggest and best
organized of any community in the area. The Grand Ball on October 8th at the Wallace Civic Center, complete
with Phyllis Diller and Skitch Henderson, was a fitting closing to the year's activities.
Noneof these eventswould have been possible without the work of many people, and it is not possible to name
them all. Special tribute must be paid to the talents of Marjorie Lucier, author of "Under the Buttonwood Tree", and
to Janet Cragin, its Director, togetherwith the many other people who contributed to the production; to Joseph Cro-
nin, Chairman, and Margaret Bachmann of the Parade Committee; and to Francis McCarthy, President, and Donald
Critchley of the Rotary Club, sponsors of the Ball.
One of the major accomplishments of the 250th Anniversary observance was the publication of the Town his-
tory "Lunenburg - The Heritage of Turkey Hills", co-authored by Nelde K. Drumm and Margaret P. Harley. An-
other permanent remembrance is the 250th Anniversary Quilt, with squares contributed by Lunenburg needlewomen,
and constructed under the direction of Dondi Larue, assisted by Barbara Pease.
Other accomplishments of the 250th Anniversary Committee since being appointed in 1973 include the mount-
ing of date plaques on houses built in the 1700's; placing granite markers at homesite of Samuel Page, the buttonwood
tree on Buttonwood Place, and the new buttonwood tree on the Center Common; erection of the Commemorative Arch
and Town Seal on the Center Common; and reviving of the July 3rd Bonfire and Independence Day observance.
A scrapbook is being compiled of pictures, programs, newspaper articles and other information pertaining to
the 250th Anniversary celebration. With permission of the Townspeople, this, together with the 250th Anniversary
Quilt, will be placed in the collection of the Historical Society. A movie of the pageant has been donated to the
Historical Society by Dr. Franklin Holzer, and a video tape of the parade produced and placed at the Ritter Memor-
ial Library. Both are available on loan to Town organizations.
To finalize 250th projects, a T. G. I. O. Dance is scheduled for February 3rd at the Lunenburg Sportsman's
Club. The T. G. 1. O. stands for "Thank God It's Over", an honest feeling on the part of the Committee, for five
years is a long time to be planning and preparing for a Townwide celebration. On the other hand, missed will be the
togetherness and the sense of Town pride that was generated by the activities. Hopefully, the 250th Anniversary
Committee has kindled a spark that will last at leastuntil 2028, when Lunenburg is 300 years old, and another Com-
mittee is directing the celebration. The following is the 250th Anniversary Committee Treasurer's report submitted

HEALTH EDUCATION
Through the Curriculum Advisory Committee, established by the Superintendent of Schools, a
sub-committee was selected in 1976 to study the health education opportunities offered in the
Lunenburg Public Schools.
The findings indicated a greater need for formal and specific health education instruction
on the secondary level. Although the subject has been taught for many years on the secondary
level, there had been no special syllabus, but merely a "hit or miss" program through the regular
science and social studies courses.
In 1977 a teacher was contracted to coordinate and implement a health education program with
the help and counsel of a Health Education Committee made up of administrators, teachers, nurses,
physicians, and a clergyman.
- 110 -
===PAGE 113===
From this input a pilot instructional program was established last year resulting in a formal
required program being implemented in 1978-79. Presently, there are one hundred eighty-five grade
nine students being taught this course twice a week for the full year. The course of study in-
cludes such topics as values, nutrition, physiology, smoking, alcohol and drugs, communicable
diseases, family living, etc.
It has been recommended that in the future all students graduating from Lunenburg High School
will have been required to pursue this important subject.
The syllabus is available at the school for any interested parent or other citizen.
FACILITY REORGANIZATION
All who read the Superintendent's Annual Report of 1977 are aware of the great amount of
study, work, and effort which went into grade reorganization as a result of declining enrollment
in K - 7 and overcrowding in the high school.
The major effect of this reorganization in the high school has been the removal of the
eighth grade from the student body, and action long overdue because of social, motivational, and
behavioral discrepancies between high school and middle school students. By my observations the
high school student body has been much better behaved and the climate of the school has greatly
improved because of the change. I am sure that the same can be reported by those responsible for
the instructional program of grade eight students.
A minor effect of the reorganization plan has been a needed restructure of the academic
facility locations of the high school which was not possible during overcrowded conditions of the
past. Each subject area department has now been located in its own designated and separate region
within the school.
This restructuring has resulted in the better sharing within departments of common instruc-
tional materials and resources, the sharing of instructional assistance from colleagues with
common academic interests which leads to camaraderie and improved morale, and more effective
supervision and evaluation.

REPORT OF DIRECTOR OF READING (K-12)
AND LANGUAGE ARTS (K-8)
Since Director of Language Arts is a new
title in this school system, people frequently
ask for a definition of "language arts."
The language arts are usually considered
all the skills needed for sending and receiving
communication. Speaking and writing are the
major divisions under "expressing" or sending
communication. Listening and reading are the
major divisions under receiving communication.
LANGUAGE ARTS
Sending Communication Receiving Communication
ROSE M. FEINBERG, Ed. D.
Speaking
Listening
Writing
Reading
At the middle school and high school, most language arts components are taught during the
"English" period, with greater emphasis on writing, grammar and literature.
Under the general headings of (1) listening, (2) speaking, (3) writing, and (4) reading, many
other skills are developed.
(1) Concentration on listening includes such skills as following directions, meaningful par-
ticipation in discussions and comprehension of oral information.
(2) Concentration on speaking includes such skills as group discussion, appropriate language
usage, clear expression of ideas and other oral communication.
Listening and speaking skills are frequently taught and/or reinforced at the same time
since they are basically dependent on each other in activities.
(3) Concentration on writing includes such skills as written expression of ideas, spelling,
punctuation, vocabulary usage, sentence development, handwriting and other areas of
written communication.
(4) Concentration on reading includes a multitude of skills ranging from recognizing the
alphabet to using reference materials effectively. Since reading involves so many skills,
it is generally treated as a subject with its own heading as well as being considered one
of the language arts.
The emphasis on specific language arts components may vary at different grade levels, but the
relationship between listening, speaking, writing and reading crosses all grades. An experience
with language in one area helps enrich the other areas. For example, better understanding of what
is read takes place when a child has experienced the words in some way previously--seeing the
objects, discussing the idea or even hearing words spoken by others. Through reading, children
broaden their experiences, vocabulary and understandings. Reading can, thus, help improve written
and oral communication.
Although the "language arts" are taught in specific time blocks, they are necessarily part of
all subjects and are reinforced throughout the school day as students speak, listen, read or write
in any subject.

PARENTS AND READING/LANGUAGE ARTS PROGRAMS
Efforts have been made to keep parents informed about the language arts and reading programs
and to encourage parental involvement at the elementary school.

and issues. Some of the titles of articles in IT'S ELEMENTARY were: "Reading At Home, What's On
The Bulletin Board?, Creative Dramatics In The Language Arts Program, Parent coffees Focus On
Home Activities, using Television For Learning, Summertime Vacation Learning, Books and Magazines
Make Excellent Holiday Gifts."
The school-wide Newsletters have included articles about the definition of language arts, a
description of the seventh-grade reading program, and essays written for a state-wide writing
contest.
Parent Coffees were conducted at both the elementary and middle schools.
The series of coffees at the elementary school were directed to parents of children from pre-
school through grade four. The purposes of this series were: (1) To give parents a better under-
standing of the reading/language development process; (2) To provide specific suggestions for the

skills.

taken home. These are available to any parent upon request. Other meetings of this type are
planned.

Passios School. The volunteer program continues, with parents collecting and donating material,
making learning activities at home, and helping in some classrooms. The school language arts pro-
gram has been enriched by parental involvement.
CONCLUSION
The skills a student has in the language arts (reading, writing, speaking, listening) will
contribute to the student's performance in most areas of learning. Why do some children progress
further and faster than others in the language arts areas? There are many influences on a child's
development of language arts skills, including: physical readiness, student ability, background
experiences, personal attitude and school experiences.
There are no guarantees that following any one plan will mean success for a child in school.
The school does much through instruction to build the skills children need in the language arts.
What should not be overlooked, however, is the importance of the home in the development of the
child's abilities.

Each school has a Resource Room for special needs students. In some respects, the Resource
Room is like the one room school of earlier years. At any given class period, the students may

MARILYN (MAcLEAN) KIBLING (Sept. 1, 1968 - July 20, 1978)
Marilyn, a native of Lunenburg and a graduate of Lunenburg
High School in 1940 and Fitchburg State College in 1944,
receiving her BS degree in Elementary Education, was hired by
the Lunenburg School Committee as a kindergarten teacher for
the 1968-69 school year. This was the first year when kinder-
garten in Lunenburg came under the jurisdiction of the Lunen-
burg Public Schools. Marilyn had had 10½ years of previous
teaching experience in Townsend, Leominster and Fitchburg prior
to her appointment. Over her years of service she took
additional graduate courses, participated in workshops and as
indicated in the elementary principal's report, she played a
major role in the development of Lunenburg's kindergarten curr-
iculum. Mrs. Kibling had requested and received school commit-
tee approval for a year's leave-of-absence for the 1978-79
school year, but on August 10, 1978, she informed school
officials that she had officially retired effective July 20,
1978. All of us wish her many years of retirement and thank
her again for a job well done.
GLORIA (VALITON) MAcPHADDEN (Sept.1, 1957 - June 30,1978)
Gloria is a native daughter of Lunenburg and graduated
from Lunenburg High School in 1933. She received her BSE degree
from Framingham State Teachers' College in June 1937, majoring
in home economics with a minor in clothing. Prior to her
appointment effective September 1957 as a home economics teacher
in the new Lunenburg Jr.-Sr. High School, she had taught in
Northampton and Fitchburg, MA vocational schools. In acknowl-

her retirement years ahead and thanked her for her conscientious
service to Lunenburg students during her twenty-one years of
service and her efforts and leadership in serving as home
economics department chairperson."
