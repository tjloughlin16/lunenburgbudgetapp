BOARD OF SELECTMEN
A major personnel change occurred in the Board of Selectmen's office this year with the retirement of
Francelina Vaughan. Laura Dunbar was hired from the payroll department to fill Frans position and has
done quite well in acclimating herself to the array of duties associated with the department.
The American with Disabilities Advisory Committee was developed to advise the Board on the implementation
of the Americans with Disabilities Act of 1992. The Selectmen also authorized the Towns involvement with
CONCEWM. CONCEWM is made up of a group of approximately 14 communities working together on the disposal
of recyclable materials.
As mentioned in last years town report, Lunenburg is facing major changes in the disposal of our solid
waste. While a repermit was successful in extending the use of the existing landfill for the remainder
of the capacity within cell 3, Lunenburg will not be permitted to dispose of solid waste in cells 4 or 5
without an extensive lining and leachate collection program.
The skyrocketing cost of health insurance is a primary concern for the Town. Discussions have been
ongoing for some time on the various options to contain these costs. Other projects that have progressed
through 1992 include the signalization of 2A and Main Street, the negotiations of a new cable TV
contract, and the initiation of discussions with Fitchburg on the purchase of sewer and water services.
Repair work continues on our historic Town Hall preventing further deterioration of the building while
working to renovate the office spaces within the structure. 1992 brought handicap access to Town Hall
via a ramp on the side of the building.
Once again, this Board appreciates the opportunity to service you, the citizens of Lunenburg, as best we
can for the good of the Town.
BOARD OF ASSESSORS
Lunenburg adopted a quarterly tax billing system for the first time in fiscal year 1993. The Assessors
made a preliminary tax commitment to the Collector on June 15, 1992. Estimated quarterly tax bills were
based on the prior year's tax and were due in two (2) equal installments on August 1st and November 1st.
Actual tax bills were mailed on December 31, and the last two quarterly payments were due on February 1st
and May 1st. It was a new experience for the townspeople and for all departments involved, requiring
additional work with no increase in staff. The inter-departmental cooperation helped make the transition
easier for everyone.
The Assessors office of the 90's is not the same as the Assessors office of the 80's. As the state cuts
its budget and trims its staff, increasing responsibilities and requirements are handed down to the
municipal level. The Registry of Motor Vehicles no longer prints motor vehicle excise bills.
Certification requirements have become more technical and detailed.
Values of property are no longer held for a 3 year period. The market has declined since fiscal year
1990. The State has encouraged all Assessors to review values annually in response to the market. It is
possible to do this analysis because of the computer valuation system. It's hard to believe that this
computer system is now more than 5 years old. We have put it to good use. During the year, we will be
upgrading the hardware and software, still using the Department of Revenue's CAMA valuation package. The
projected efficiency and speed of the new system should improve the process.
The Board of Assessors and its staff look forward to working with taxpayers in resolving questions they
may have about the valuation process.

BUILDING OFFICIAL
The Building Department has experienced an increase in permit activity from declining construction.
Building of new single family dwellings has nearly doubled from each of the previous two years. Requests
for information regarding properties being refinanced have resulted in much time spent in this office
supplying information on Building/Zoning to lending institutions, engineering companies doing property
surveys, and others that are trying to determine the correct status of such.
Home owners should be made aware of Mass General Law C.142A. This law requires that all home
improvement work commenced on or after July 1, 1992 that is contracted for and exceeds $1000.00 requires
a contractor who must supply proof of registration at the time of a building permit application with this
office. The law is not extremely complicated but does have exemptions, therefore you are encouraged to
call for further information on this or any other building or zoning information.
The following is a tabulation showing building permit activity from the past year. New homes, 34,
garages, 91, residential renovations, 58 and wood stove inspections, 36, commercial renovation, 1.

FIRE DEPARTMENT
The Fire Department responded to the following calls in 1992: Service - 12; Mutual Aid - 21; Hazardous
Materials - 23; Structure - 35; Motor Vehicle - 16; Alarm Activation - 63; Motor Vehicle Accidents - 13;
Investigations - 15; Rubbish - 2; Brush/Grass - 18; Medical - 2; Ice Rescue - 3; Outside Electrical - 7;
Good Intent - 2; Illegal Burning - 2; Miscellaneous - 4; Rescue/Ambulance - 317.
The Fire Department is quickly getting to the point where we are not going to be able to continue to meet
the needs of the public if we don't invest some money in capital items. These items include a larger and
more modern fire station to adequately house our present and future apparatus and give our workers a
safer environment to work in. The conditions at our station now are totally inadequate in every aspect.
The station is also unsafe to work in due to a lack of ventilation of diesel fumes. This affects the
health of firefighters police officers, dispatchers and office personnel. The lack of an adequate sewage
disposal system is another concern, as we can't decontaminate protective clothing and personnel the way
we should. Even if we had the septic facilities, we don't have the room for decontamination areas and
washers and dryers. These are problems that didn't exist when the station was originally built, but they
must be addressed as soon as possible.
Another major item we must address is the purchase of a ladder truck. New construction and the size of
buildings has made it almost foolhardy to place a firefighter on a roof without a ladder or platform to
stand on. The cost of a ladder truck or platform cannot be compared to the loss of one life. Both the
station replacement and the ladder truck needs have been outlined in my annual reports every year since
1985. I think we have to seriously take a look at these two items this year.
The recent fire at MacMillans cost us our 1961 reserve pumper. Major damage was done to the engine and
the cost to repair or replace the engine is more than the truck is worth. This is another problem that
needs to be addressed.
Our budget is extremely small to run a 48 man, 24 hours per day department on. It has been mostly level
funded for the last 3-4 years and before that, we saw only minimal increases. These actions have
seriously eroded our capabilities to supply you with adequate services. It's only because the people in
this department are truly dedicated to your protection and -well-being that we have been able to function
at the level we have been. We cannot expect the present circumstances to remain the same and still
expect to receive the same service. Something has to give eventually, or someone is going to end up
getting hurt. There are also numerous other problems that we are starting to see develop, due to lack of
adequate funding of the budget.
I would like to thank the officers and men and women of my department for their dedication to their
duties as firefighters and EMT's. Their efforts make Lunenburg a safer place to live.

HOUSING AUTHORITY
This past year the Lunenburg Housing Authority completed the retrofit of the long awaited fire alarm
upgrade. The Commissioners extend their thanks to Lunenburg's Fire Chief Dennis Carrier for the
assistance he offered to the Authority's engineering firm. The elderly apartments in the Pearl Brook
development now have an up to date fire and smoke alarm detection system.
We continue to replace the refrigerators and ranges in the Pearl Brook Apartments, and will do SO as our
budget permits.
During the course of 1992 the Authority received 19 new applications for housing assistance, consisting
of 9 elderly/handicapped applications and 9 for family housing. We leased seven new tenants into Pearl
Brook Apartments, and one into the family housing units. Our waiting list for the Pearl Brook Apartments
have four applicants awaiting placement. The family waiting lists have 27 applicants awaiting placement.
The year Ella Rae DeNault tendered her resignation from the Board of Commissioners. It was accepted with
regret. Ella served the Town of Lunenburg, and the tenants of the Lunenburg Housing Authority for 14
years. The Authority welcomed James McKenna as a new member. Our commitment remains to provide decent,
safe, affordable housing to our tenants, and continue to seek new housing opportunities for those still
in need.
HOUSING PARTNERSHIP
The Lunenburg Housing Partnership is a group of Lunenburg residents appointed by the Selectmen to
coordinate strategies for expansion of affordable housing in Lunenburg. This affordable housing includes
rentals as well as for-sale housing, existing housing stock as well as new construction.

PLANNING BOARD
The Planning Board accepted with regret, the resignation of William Allen in 1992 and David Couhig, 8
Country Road was appointed to replace Mr. Allen. Eleanor Gay's resignation as secretary was also
accepted. The Planning office benefited by her years of experience.
25 ANR plans were processed, an increase of 10 plans over the previous year. A residential project with
an affordable housing component on Flat Hill Road will be presented this Spring. Building permits have
been issued for Goodrichville Common. Villages at Blueberry Hills has not started construction. There is
no formal status change on the Woodlands at Hickory Hills.
In the Industrial area, the Board is working with the Gitto-Global Corporation, Pioneer Park, to complete
the construction under the Development Plan Review. Occupancy is targeted for April 15, 1993 with full
operation by May 1, 1993. Dietrich Industries, 198 Summer Street, is also in reconstruction under a
Development Plan Review. Their process is a cold steel process. The occupancy date is slated for March
1, 1993.
Under the Commercial ByLaw, the Board is working with a Development Plan Review for CBL & Associates,
Boston, MA for a Shopping Mall at 301 Massachusetts Avenue. The project will present two anchor stores
and several specialty stores. This project is undergoing intense review in the areas of drainage,

POLICE DEPARTMENT
"Lunenburg - Support it or lose it"
Anyone who reads a newspaper or watches the news has to know what a serious problem our country is facing
in regards to public safety. Look at the two major cities that partially surround us, Fitchburg and
Leominster. We have seen these communities deteriorate over the past 5 - 10 years into a deplorable
state. The main reason for this is constant underfunding for public safety. You cannot continue to do
police work with a "band-aid: approach. Once you lose control of your community, it is virtually
impossible to get it back. What would have cost a few thousand dollars at the time, now costs hundreds
of thousands of dollars to correct. Many people think it couldn't happen to Lunenburg, but the spillage
has to go somewhere. Already we are experiencing problems with gangs in our community. Don't become a
victim to crime in your community by denying public safety the resources to do their jobs.
In 1992, domestic crimes rose drastically in the Town of Lunenburg, as well as other communities within
the Commonwealth. The poor economy coupled with high unemployment was one of the major causes for this.
These are highly volatile crime situations that are extremely dangerous to the citizens of our community,
and also our officers. These crimes are up 22% from 1992. Also of major concern is the increase on
alcohol/substance abuse crimes in our community, which are up 50% from 1991. These increases alone, with
===PAGE 12===
the increased complaint/investigations handled by our department make it increasingly hard for us to keep
up with calls and provide you with the type of service needed. Keep in mind, there are only two police
officers working at any one time, and with new growth in our community (commercial and residential, as
well as spillage form Fitchburg and Leominster), it is going to get worse. Even now we find ourselves
prioritizing our calls and leaving a call we are on to go to a call of a more serious nature and
returning to our fist call 30 - 45 minutes later. If this trend continues, we are going to have to
increase the size of our police force to maintain an effective and efficient police department.
You will also note that our motor vehicle violations are up 120% - 1706 for 1992 VS. 781 for 1991. This
is because we were able to obtain a traffic safety grant for $10,000. This money was put in a revolving
fund established at our last annual Town Meeting to develop a comprehensive traffic safety program and
heighten awareness of traffic safety issues within the community. This program has been very successful;
and I thank you all for your support in this worthwhile endeavor.
Once again, we were successful in obtaining grant monies for our drug task force with the City of
Fitchburg. This year they cut our allotment to $39,000 jointly. We were able to get an extension on
$4,423 of unspent funds, but we need an additional $4,280 to keep our drug officer for one more year. We
ask for your support in this endeavor.

RITTER MEMORIAL LIBRARY
It seems the library is the ultimate budget stretcher in these financially strained times. Only 1% of
the Town budget goes to funding Ritter and with that small sum a lot was accomplished in 1992.
We checked out over 60,000 items, including books, magazines, videos, books-on-tape, Day Care Kits,
passes to area museums, and prints form the lending picture collection. The library was open 35 hours
per week (31 hours in July and August).
This fast pace of checking materials out, a 119% per hour increase since 1981, has solidified the
library's commitment to automating operations using the same barcode system that grocery stores and auto
supply stores, etc. have been using since the 1970's. We hope to automate on the same equipment as the
Lunenburg High School so that eventually students can access both catalogs from computers at Ritter, the
school, and perhaps even their own homes.
Volunteers contributed over 25 hours per week to Ritter in 1992. Two High School students, Mary Jane
Hall and Christine Hrul, volunteered on a regular ten hour per week schedule at Ritter during the summer
and helped move practically every book in the library as we completed a major reorganization and
consolidated our technical services in one area upstairs. Many other young people and adults donate time
on a weekly or monthly basis to help with library tasks appropriate for volunteers. There are several
areas where special training or financial accountability preclude the use of volunteers, but the help
services. they have SO generously given has allowed the staff to keep up with the ever greater demand for library
The Friends of Ritter Library, our non-profit support group, granted Ritter $500 toward the purchase of a
computer with a CD-ROM drive for computerized reference service. A matching grant from the Lunenburg
Education Advancement Foundation (LEAF) and a trustee fund raiser netted sufficient funds to purchase the
equipment. It has been a great tool for students and adults alike. We plan to expand CD-ROM reference
services as finances allow.
Activities at Ritter during 1992 included Library Day at Fenway, the popular "Down to Earth" gardening
series, two book sales, a quilt raffle & booth at the Lunenburg Country Fair, Make a Difference Day
during which volunteers did yard work, washed windows, and recovered seat cushions, the hugely popular
Summer Reading Olympics, American Girl Week and Renaissance Week.
During the school year Ritter is the library for the Primary School and services classes there about 3
hours per week.
Ritter's Summer Reading Program involved over 400 children from preschool age through teens providing
incentives and activities to keep them excited about reading. There were events scheduled every day
including our unique book writing program in which children write, illustrate and bind books which then
become part of the library's permanent lending collection. Other Summer Olympic highlights included a
tour of the Sentinel & Enterprise, an Irish storyteller, an orienteering program, the annual chalk art
show, and Debbie O'Carroll's mime and magic production to name a few. The grand finale was an evening
Ice Cream Social and Band concert to honor retiring library director Elizabeth Burnap.
Betty Burnap retired in August after eleven years as director. Her reception was attended by many
admirers from age 2 to over 80. Patience, dignity, and intelligent leadership were the hallmarks of her
directorship. We all miss her.
Susan Tallman, assistant director for five years, replaced Betty as director in September. Barbara
Friedman, a professional librarian with over twenty years of experience, came in as assistant director.
Long time volunteer Sandy Mailloux was hired as a part time library page. Budget cuts required cutting
our custodian's hours from 15 to 10. One technical typist position and the assistant director were cut
by 2 1/2 hours each. These latest personnel cuts come after a 10% cut in staff hours last year and
underscore our need to automate for efficiency.

Title II of the Americans with Disability Act (ADA) which applies to municipalities, became effective on
January 26, 1992. It is the intent of the Town of Lunenburg to fully comply with the provisions of the
ADA. An interim Advisory Committee to the Board of Selectmen on Disability has been appointed, consisting
of Selectman Michael J. Sauvageau, Building Inspector Roger Williams, Council on Aging Chairman Jean E.
Hobbs, and John Romano. A Commission on Disability will be established by vote at a subsequent town
meeting.

services. Facilities have been placed in order of priority as to accessibility for handicapped and a
timetable established for addressing any necessary changes. Based on this timetable, funding for
modification renovations will be requested at Town Meeting over a three year period. Details of the work
done to date are on file at the Town Hall.
BOARD OF HEALTH
Health and environmental concerns continued to be in the forefront of the community during calendar 1992.
Unfortunately, all of these issues whether large or small place an increased financial burden on the
Town. The first documented cases of rabies in raccoons in quite some time resulted in the need of
additional funds for both the testing and disposal of these rabid animals. It is reasonable to assume
that a similar expenditure will be required next year.
===PAGE 16===
A much more significant issue was the mutual decision made by both the Board of Selectmen and the Board
of Health to hire an engineer to prepare and submit a closure plan for the Town's Landfill. At this time
it is anticipated that all refuse disposal at the Landfill will cease on or before January 1, 1994.
Decisions regarding where the rubbish will go and how it will get there after this date have yet to be
made. However, the one certainty is that it will represent a significant increase in cost to all members
of the community.
Upcoming revision to the existing State Sanitary Code will probably result in changes regarding the
installations, repair and possibly the inspections of subsurface disposal systems. In a community such
as ours which is almost 100% dependent upon individual septic systems, these revisions will have
significant financial impacts.
Finally, some plans for commercial and residential development within the Town called for the creation of
a Town Sewer Department and a public sewer connection to the City of Fitchburg. While the Board of
Health believes that the Town needs to be careful and cautious as it proceeds along this track, it is
confident that the development of a public sewer system is far superior to the continued use of
individual septic systems for all new development.

COUNCIL ON AGING
All programs and services at the Eagle House Senior Center at 25 Memorial Drive are open to people 60
years of age and older, regardless of residence and income.
Information on all elder services, referrals, and counseling for elders and their families is available
at the center. The center offers a noontime meal, a transportation service for local and area trips for
shopping, medical and other appointments, and medical trips to the Boston and Worcester corridors. The
center also serves as the pickup site for Meals on Wheels delivery. Programs include speakers on medical
and other topics, health clinics, and a lending library of large print books. Craft classes, cribbage
tournaments, bridge and other card games, Bingo, intergenerational and special programs are among the
activities conducted. A newsletter is published 11 times a year and mailed three of those times.
Ridership on the senior van has increased some 13.5 percent when comparing September to December 1992
with the same months last year. The five day a week service was instituted by the COA and Montachusett
Area Regional Transit (MART) in September 1991.
Currently, benefits amounting to $219,000 a year are provided for elders in Lunenburg. Of this amount,
$33,672 or 15.5 percent is paid by the town, and $136,988 or 62.3 percent provided by others such as
Montachusett Home Care, MART, Project Green Thumb, Nashoba Nursing Service, Montachusett Opportunity
Council, the
Bureau of Elder Affairs, Fitchburg Gas and Light Company, client donations, local businesses advertising
in the COA newsletter, and donations made to the Eagle House Senior Center Committee, Inc. The remaining
22.2 percent comes from the value of volunteer help, with 196 volunteers in FY92 giving 5,015 hours,
worth $48,000.
For FY92, the Council on Aging provided over 21,281 units of service to 42 percent of Lunenburg residents
over the age of 65. In addition, information booklets were sent to the 90 residents who turned 60 in
1992, and those age 60 to 64 were included when the newsletter was mailed.
The COA budget provides for office and meeting space for the American Legion and Lunenburg's Veterans'
Agent, and for meetings of other town committees as a backup to the Town Hall. The use of the Eagle House
was donated to the PTO for their annual house tour fund raiser. The congregate meal site is one of the
training stations for students from FLLAC special needs class in Lunenburg schools. Elders contribute
food year round to area food banks, and helped feed hungry families by assisting Project Bread in their
spoon decorating endeavor. COA staff and volunteers conduct the periodic Surplus Food distribution to
qualified Lunenburg residents.

THOMAS C. PASSIOS ELEMENTARY SCHOOL
This year a new reading program was adopted K-6 both at the elementary and middle schools. The program
was selected by the staff in the spring of 1992 after many months of discussion and examination of the
most current published programs in reading. The new reading program is published by the Houghton Mifflin
Company and is called The Literature Experience. It was selected as the program which fit best with the
current reading curriculum as well as with the teachers' criteria for a quality reading program.
The Houghton Mifflin program is a literature-based reading program. This means that the student
selections in the anthologies are whole pieces or excerpts from award winning children's literature. The
selections are arranged around themes designed to stimulate students' interest in reading. There are
genre themes such as mystery, science fiction, adventure, traditional tales, and fantasy; author themes,
nonfiction themes and topic themes. In addition to the reading that students do in the anthologies,
there are also class sets of paperback books which accompany the program and which relate to the themes
in the anthologies, so that students are required to do a great deal of reading beyond the selections in
the anthologies. Every skill in the Houghton Mifflin program-from phonics and decoding to comprehension
and critical thinking-is taught across an entire theme SO that students have the time they need to learn
each skill. In addition, the Houghton Mifflin program places a great deal of emphasis on writing and
critical thinking skills, subjects that the staff has identified as essential areas of curriculum.
In addition to the adoption of a new reading program, the elementary school has chosen to teach reading
within homerooms rather than group between classrooms homogeneously for reading. This decision was made
because teachers felt the classroom teacher, who best knows a student's strengths and weaknesses, is in
the best position to teach that student reading as well. The staff has also been influenced by the
current research on ability grouping which points to the many disadvantages of grouping and labeling
students by ability.
A full-year staff development course in the teaching of reading and writing using the most current models
of reading instruction was offered at the elementary school last year as well as two courses this year on
the reading and writing process. A majority of the staff have participated in these courses as
preparation for the implementation of the new reading program. Further staff development options are
planned for next year as well, with an emphasis on the integration of reading and the other language arts
and the teaching of reading and writing across the curriculum.
TURKEY HILL MIDDLE SCHOOL
Turkey Hill Middle School focuses on an educational process that recognizes and provides for the
uniqueness of the individual. This is accompanied by a program that is intellectually challenging and
recognizes the students academic, emotional, physical, aesthetic, and social needs. In a time of
transition between elementary and high school, our students will be encouraged and guided to discover not
only self-worth and potential, but sensitivity toward human relationships, respect for the abilities,
values and attitudes of others, and a sense of responsibility to the entire community.
Turkey Hill Middle School is home to 522 students in grades five through eight. Our curriculum in grades
five, six and seven include reading, english, math, science and social studies. In grade eight, all
students take a foreign language of their choice; Spanish, French or Latin and English, science geography
and either algebra, pre-algebra or general math. Our unified arts program consists of home economics,
industrial arts, instrumental music, art, health and physical education.
HIGH SCHOOL
Lunenburg High School offers a comprehensive program of studies for students in grades 9-12. Since
almost 75 percent of the graduates of Lunenburg High School typically pursue higher education our
curriculum provides a strong program of college preparatory courses in English, Mathematics, science,

education, art, music, industrial technology and physical education.
Lunenburg High School also provides an extensive, high quality co-curricular and extra curricular program
and our students are encouraged to participate in the many clubs, organizations, events and/or
interscholastic athletic teams which are available. Among our numerous student clubs and activities are:
Debate Club, Youth and Government Club, Yearbook, Student Council, International Club, Math Team,
===PAGE 21===
National Honor Society, Nature/Environment Club, S.A.D.D., School Newspaper, Academic Decathlon Team,
Latin Club, Ski Club, National Art Honor Society, Future Business Leaders of America, and the Amnesty
International Club. Additionally Lunenburg High School sponsors a wide variety of interscholastic sports
at the various levels of boys' and girls' competition. Our athletic program includes: baseball,
basketball, cross country, football, golf, soccer, field hockey, tennis, track and softball.
Lunenburg High School has a long tradition of academic excellence and scholastic achievement. 1992 was a
year which saw our students distinguish themselves in academic competitions at both local and state wide
levels. In April a team of LHS pupils competed against high schools from across Northern Worcester
County in a scholastic contest held at Mt. Wachusett Community College called "Head to Head". Only a
narrow overtime loss to Leominster High School in the dramatic final round denied Lunenburg a first place
finish. As runner up school we were awarded a full scholarship to Mr. Wachusett which was subsequently
presented to a deserving member of the graduating class.
In November of 1992 Lunenburg High School was proud and privileged to host the Small School Division of
the prestigious Massachusetts Academic Decathlon. This well known Scholastic Competition matches high
school teams from across Massachusetts in a series of religious academic events which cover the
curriculum. Of the 22 high schools which competed the LHS team finished 7th, with a number of our
students earning medals for individual excellence.
LHS' proud tradition of athletic achievement was again demonstrated in 1992. Our Basketball team enjoyed
what was perhaps its most outstanding season in the school's history. Undefeated during regular season
play (17-0) the team won the Small School Division of the Clark Tournament, the St. Bernard;s Christmas
Tournament, was Mid-Wach Division C Champion and ended post season play with a remarkable 10-1 record.
The trend toward increased student enrollment at the high school first noted in 1991 has not only
continued by has in fact accelerated. Our present enrollment of 475 students represents an increase of
almost 20% over last year and projections strongly suggest that this steady growth in our pupil
population will likely continue.
In the Fall of 1992 the High School submitted to the New England Association of Schools and Colleges its
"Two Year Progress Report". This important document represents the first of the major, comprehensive
Reports which must be submitted to NEASC detailing the implementation status of the Recommendations
contained in LHS' 1990 Evaluation. Copies of the complete Report are available at the Ritter Memorial
and High School Libraries, Town Hall, the Superintendent's office and from members of the School
Committee and High School Parent Advisory Council. The Report documents that over 70% of the
Recommendations contained in our 1990 NEASC Evaluation have either been completed or are currently in
progress. This is by any standard a commendable implementation rate and a clear and powerful
demonstration of Lunenburg High School's commitment to the pursuit of excellence. While much still
remains to be done, significant deficiencies in our educational facilities and physical plant remain as
yet unresolved, we are confident that with the continued support and good will of our community no
obstacle is too great to overcome.
Lunenburg High School remains committed to providing its students with every possible opportunity to
learn and grow as well as to contributing to the overall quality of life in the community.

TOWN CLERK TAX COLLECTOR TREASURER
1992 was an especially busy year for these three departments. The Treasurer's
office lost Laura Dunbar to the Board of Selectmen, Laura replaces Fran Vaughan
who retired this year; We wish Laura the best in her new position. Gayle Padula
came on board to do payroll, however her real area of expertise is with computers
and she has already proven to be a valuable addition to my staff. In the Tax
Collector's office, quarterly tax bills were issued for the first time. In an earlier
report I said that the Treasurer in me said "YES, YES, YES" to quarterly tax bills
and that the Collector in me said "NO, NO, NO" well, the refrain now is "OH, OH,
OH" that's a lot of work. The workload in the Collectors office has almost doubled
with the quarterlies and there has been no increase in staff, (in fact the budget was
cut!) This is causing backlogs in other areas of the three departments. We will be
looking for either additional help or a lock box service to process the payments in
the near future. The good news is that we haven't had to borrow in anticipation of
revenue so far. There is still a chance that we will need to borrow as we near the
end of the Fiscal Year.
We were able once again to obtain a 100% collection rate of FY 92 Real Estate
Taxes. This means that taxes were either collected or a lien was placed on the
property at the Registry of Deeds thus protecting the Town's interest.
Collections of motor vehicle excise taxes have increased, particularly prior years'
taxes, thanks to the Registry of Motor Vehicles new "Tagging" system and the
extra efforts made by our Deputy Tax Collector; many thanks to her!
The Town Clerk's office was humming almost constantly with elections (seven in
all) and town Meetings that were held this year. I was re-elected to a second term
this year and extend my sincere thanks to the voters and look forward to serving
another three years. We also held our first recall election since the recall provision
was adopted ten years ago. While the recall provision is a valuable tool for the
voters in any community, it must be used responsibly. With frustration levels ever
on the rise, we are seeing more and more recall provisions being adopted by our
neighbors and recall elections being held - most with the same results as those of
Lunenburg. In many instances these petitions are being brought as a result of
disagreements with, rather than any wrongdoing on the elected official's part. But,
again the recall is a valuable tool and it is up to the voter to justify and validate the
purpose of the recall through his vote at the polls.
As a result of the 1990 Federal Census, a small portion of Precinct A is now in the
Fifth Congressional District while the rest remains in the First Congressional
District. We were required to redistrict, which was effective for our Federal
Elections this year. We also have to re-precinct. This means that we will now
have four precincts and this change will become effective with the 1994 State
Elections.
The Board of Registrars is grateful to Bud Coutu for the new Precinct signs that he
SO generously donated. These are the signs displayed at the polls at each election
showing the Precinct each street is in.
