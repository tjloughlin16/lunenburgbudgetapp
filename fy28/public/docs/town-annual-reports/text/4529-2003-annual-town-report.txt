MEMORIAM
RONALD LAMOTHE
1941-2003
L
Every once in a while, an individual comes into our lives who inspires us through his
words, deeds, commitment and simple presence. When this occurs, we know
immediately how fortunate we are. Ron Lamothe was one such individuall Known as
the architect of the Lunenburg baseball program, Ron has been on the minds of the
community, past and present players and coaches ever since his passing from
cancer on January 10, 2003. He touched the lives of many through his years of
teaching, coaching and civic involvement.
Ron taught social studies for 34 years, was active in local politics, coached baseball
for 28 years and football for 32 years. Was named to the Massachusetts Assistant
Football Coaches Hall of Fame and Massachusetts Baseball Coaches Association Hall of Fame. He was a
leader in town government as an active member of the Board of Selectmen, Charter Commission, Library
trustees and Community Council. Plain and simple Ron Lamothe was what Community service and High
School athletics are all about-dedication, teamwork, passion, respect and love.
RICHARD F. MULLIGAN
1928-2003
Dick was a life long resident of Lunenburg and the Athletic Director at the High
School for over 25 years. He distinguished himself as a coach in track and
basketball. During his long tenure from 1958 to 1976 as track coach he had a 12-
year winning streak. He was instrumental in starting the indoor track program in
Central Massachusetts and was inducted into the Massachusetts State Coaches
Hall of Fame.
Over the years, Dick coached teams that accumulated records close to rivaling
those compiled by any coach in Massachusetts. He had a profound effect as a
caring individual with a genuine interest for youngsters involved in the athletic
program. He was a man that was totally involved and he made everyone feel important. He was an excellent
example for all to emulate.

BOARD OF SELECTMEN / SEWER COMMISSIONERS
The Board of Selectmen / Sewer Commissioners wish to regretfully acknowledge the passing of Selectman Ronald
Lamothe who lost his fight to cancer during his second term in office. We will long remember the diligent campaign
that he waged for his position as Selectman and the numerous hours he spent waving to passerby's from the town
common. We cannot fail to mention his service to the youth of Lunenburg both as a teacher and coach who spent
countless hours in nurturing the independent minds and bodies of our next generation. Ron was instrumental in the
Town Hall suggestion box and brought forward the concerns of the citizens every week. He will be deeply missed by
this Board and the Community.
The search for CAFO continued and culminated with the hiring of David DeManche from Uxbridge. Unfortunately, Mr.
DeManche tenure was not very extensive with his resignation just three months after filing the position. This
resulted in the Town resuming its search to fill the vacancy in accordance with the Town Charter. During this
process Laura Williams was appointed Interim CAFO and worked mutually with Town Accountant Karen Brochu in
fulfilling the additional responsibilities of this position.
The Phase 1, Contract If and Electric Avenue sewer Installation projects continued throughout the year and are not
expected to be completed until the end of 2004. This has caused quite a navigational challenge to residents and
business owners and the Board has been sympathetic to the Towns plight and will continue to work with the
Contractors and property owners to accomplish the completion of these main lines and spurs with the least impact
as possible.
Representatives from the Board are also serving on the Building Committees for the Primary School, Public Safety
Facility and the Library. Construction commenced on both the Primary School and the Public Safety Facility and
completion is expected sometime next year for these two buildings and as of this writing; the Library is just
beginning the design phase.
The Board extends a special "thank you" again this year to the Lunenburg / Townsend Rotary Club for decorating the
Gazebo at Christmas and the Turkey Hill Gardeners for the seasonal Town Hall decorations and flowers in the upper
common. We would also acknowledge the generous donations that were received and utilized so that the Town
could continue with the traditional Band Concerts on the Common and any donations to this cause would be greatly
appreciated.
Your Board has enjoyed working In service to the Town, working with each other and with you the citizens. The
Selectmen would also like to acknowledge all of the Individuals who work so hard for the Town and those who
volunteer their time and commitment by serving on the various Boards, Commissions and Committees within the
Town.
Please feel free to direct any questions to our office staff Laura Williams or Linda Douglas @978-582-4144. If they
cannot assist you, they will forward your call to the appropriate authorities. Our office hours are Monday,
Wednesday and Thursday 8:00 AM to 4:00 PM, Tuesday 8:00 AM to 6:30 PM and Friday closed.

BUILDING INSPECTOR/ZONING OFFICIAL
It has been an extremely busy year in the Building Department. The office issued 390 building permits in 2003. Sixty-one permits
were for new homes, the remaining 329 permits were for additions, swimming pools, sheds, decks and other general
maintenance permits.
The new Public Safety Building is underway, as well as a new Primary School and the Chester Mossman Teen Center, it is an
exciting year for Lunenburg.
Zoning Enforcement is ongoing. Zoning complaints must be submitted in writing to receive prompt attention.
The Building Department recommends that street numbers be posted on the home and on mailboxes using 4-inch Arabic
numerals. This is helpful in an emergency situation and for locating homes for Inspection purposes. We also recommend that
smoke detector batteries be changed every six months.
Residents should be aware that Massachusetts Law requires that all persons, partnerships and corporations who bid or perform
residential contracting (reconstruction, alterations, additions, etc.) for work exceeding $1000.00 be registered with the
Commonwealth of Massachusetts. This office will enforce this provision, however, you should ask your contractor about his/her
insurance and registration.
The Building Department is located on the second floor of the Town Hall. Office hours for the Building Department are Mondays
7:00-3:30, Tuesday 8:00-6:30, Wednesday 8:00-3:30, Thursday 7:00-3:30 and closed Friday. Michael J. Sauvageau is the Building
Inspector/Zoning Official and Lisa Normandin serves as the Administrative Assistant to the Building Department.
CAPITAL PLANNING
Criteria: Mandates, Safety, Need, Crisis, Cost Effectiveness and Ability to Fund
The Capital Planning Committee has been working on the FY Plan based again on the above criteria. This year, the CPC has
been working with the Finance Committee to better assess the Capital Budget vs. the Operating Budget for the Town. This
procedure does two things; one, it gives the citizens of the community a truer picture of the cost of operating all departments,
and secondly It allocates funding in the Capital Budget especially for the items listed. This means that only that Item can be
purchased and the funding cannot be switched from line item to line Item. This ensures that the needs in capital arena are

CENTRAL MASS MOSQUITO CONTROL
The Central Massachusetts Mosquito Control Project (the Project) currently provides its services to 36 cities and towns
throughout Middlesex and Worcester Countles. The Project's headquarters is located at 111 Otis Street, Northboro MA. Tours of
the Headquarter or visits to field work sites may be arranged by calling the office In advance. Please call (508) 393-3055 during
business hours for more information. The Project practices Integrated Mosquito Management (IMM), blending state of the art
methods and techniques with expertise, experience, and scientific research to provide our member communities with
environmentally sound and cost effective mosquito control.
During 2003 the Project received 5,331 requests for service from town residents and officials. We scheduled five hundred and
ninety (590) adult mosquito operations in residential areas, and four hundred and seventy six (476) larviciding operations. A
total of over 4,000 pounds of Btl (Bacillus thuringiensis israelensis) was applied by helicopter to 2 towns, Chelmsford & Billerica,
and 6,204 pounds by hand throughout our service area to area wetlands to reduce the emergence of adult mosquitoes. This
represents over 2,040 acres of wetland that was treated with this mosquito-specific bacteria, significantly reducing adult
mosqulto populations in these areas. Twenty-eight thousand, nine hundred and fifty (28,950) catch basins were treated with
larvicidal briquettes to control the mosquitoes that seek out these cool dark wet areas to breed, including the Culex mosquito, a
major target for West Nile Virus transmission. Four thousand, eight hundred and ninety five (4,895) culverts were cleaned In an
attempt to eliminate unnecessary standing water and reduce mosquito breeding. This work was done In conjunction with
cleaning, clearing and digging of eighty five thousand; nine hundred and forty eight (85,498) feet of streams, brooks and ditches.
This represents almost sixteen and one-half miles of waterways which were cleaned and improved by Project personnel in 2003.
The Mosquito Awareness Program which we offer to elementary schools and other civic organizations in our district has become
very popular. Project staff meets with students, teachers or concerned residents to discuss mosquito biology, mosquito habitat,
and control procedures. Much of the presentation Is directed towards what children and their families can do to prevent
mosquitoes from breeding around their homes. Slides, videos, coloring books and other handouts make this an Interesting
program. This program is tallored to meet the needs of the specific audience. Eight hundred and twelve (812) students attended
these programs.
As part of our effort to reduce the need for pesticides we continue to expand our wetlands restoration program. By cleaning
clogged and overgrown waterways, mosquito breeding can be reduced and drainage areas are restored to historic conditions.
Bti mosquito larvicide is used to treat areas where mosquito larvae are found. We routinely check known breeding sites kept In
our database, but also encourage the public to notify us of any areas they suspect could breed mosquitoes. Our field crews will
investigate all such requests and treat the area only if surveillance gathered at the time shows an imminent threat of mosquito
emergence.
Our goal Is to handle all mosquito problems with education, wetlands restoration or larviciding, but we recognize that there are
times when adult mosquito spraying is the only viable solution. In such cases specific areas are treated with elther hand-held or
pickup truck mounted sprayers if surveillance gathered at the time shows sufficient numbers of adult mosquitoes present to

warrant program. an application. This allows residents and town officials to exclude areas under their control from this, or any part of our
The Project's surveillance program monitors adult mosquito and larval population density, and is the backbone for prescribing
various control techniques. Specialized mosquito traps are deployed throughout the Project's service area to sample for
mosquitoes that may be transmitting mosquito-borne diseases. In conjunction with the Mass Department of Public Health we
sample in areas suspected of harboring WNV and other viruses.
Educational pamphlets are available to anyone interested in learning about mosquito control and the services provided by the

SPECIAL SERVICES
The Montachusett Regional Vocational Technical School District, in the last calendar year, has provided special education
services to 486 students. Presently, there are 263 Identified eligible students who are receiving special education Instruction In
academic and vocational education. Our mission is to comply with Public Law 105-17, and the 1997 amendments to the
Individuals with Disabilities Education Act (known as "IDEA-97") and Chapter 766 of the Acts of 1972, MGL C.71B (603 CMR
28.00) to provide a free appropriate public education (FAPE) to students with disabilities in the least restrictive environment.
We affirm the need for higher expectations of student performance, preparation of students for employment and Independent
living, more comprehensive coordination of supports with other agencies, high quality professional development, incentives for
whole school educational approaches that promote inclusion of special education students in regular education classes.
The student support services at Monty Tech are available to all students. We have a full-time school nurse (R.N.) with a part-time
assistant (CNN) who administers prescribed medications, performs state mandated health screening tests and provides, when
necessary, health Information for the student's Team meeting. We have a full-time school social worker who is able to
31
===PAGE 34===
participate on Teams and assist students who have needs concerning finances, family Issues, living space, maternity issues,
health issues, and food and clothing. Our students have access to the services of a full-time adjustment counselor and a part-
time school psychologist. Both of these people are available for scheduled counseling sessions and emergency treatment or
crisis intervention. Additionally, there is a Director of Guidance and four other certified guidance counselors available for
counseling services for students In grades 9 -12. Our speech therapist is contracted to service students per week. Two
vocational resource aides work with the special needs students in their trades to make sure that the trade is appropriate to the
students' capabilities and aspirations. All of our teachers with special education certification are considered in the "highly
qualified" category of NCLB.
The Monty Tech Program for Deaf and Hard of Hearing Is a day program for students in grades 9 - 12 and was established in
August 2001. It is providing a comprehensive vocational and academic educational program for students Identified as being
sensory impaired in the area of hearing. Presently, there are seven students being served by one certified teacher of the deaf
with two full-time Interpreter assistants and two part-time Interpreter assistants and a speech pathologist. All of these students,
except one, are placed in regular education classes with assistance from interpreters. The climate and culture of the Monty Tech
environment have made for a very successful Integration of the deaf program. The staff and students are dedicated to respectful
interactions within our school and community to maintain a positive, caring and safe environment for all of our students.
The Special Education Program at Monty Tech Is reviewed every May in accordance with regulatory requirements. The results of
this evaluation are used to Improve the special education procedures and programs at Monty Tech, The Parent Advisory Council
will participate in this review and evaluation. They will have the opportunity to give Input on program needs, program
implementation, and program evaluation and improvement.
The Montachusett Regional Vocational Technical School District adheres to federal and state law regarding confidentiality of
personally identifiable student information. Specifically, this school adheres to Massachusetts state regulation 603 CMR 23.00.
Student records at Monty Tech are kept in a secure location in the school's Guidance Department. It is understood that parents
and eligible students have the right to inspect all portions of the student record upon request, within a two-day timeline.

Principal Keith Hochstein
Despite reductions in programs due to budget cuts, Turkey Hill Middle School had a terrific year during 2003-2004. There are a
variety of reasons for this statement; some of these relate to the academic success of our students and some are due to the way
our students Interact in the building and the opportunities that students have to enjoy their middle school years.
First our MCAS scores indicate that once again the academic program is strong and students are benefiting from improved
programs and improved teaching. In the fifth grade our students took the Science and Technology test for the first time these
scores were reported by the DOE. 62% of our students obtained an Advanced or Proficient rating compared to 51% at the state
level. Only 4% of our students were in the Warning category compared with 15% at the state level.
In the sixth grade all students take the Mathematics test. 66% or two-thirds of our students scored In the Advanced or Proficient
categories versus 42% at the state level. In addition only 12% of our students obtained a Warning category versus 26% at the
state level.
In seventh grade all students take the English/Language Arts test. 71% of our students scored in the Advanced or Proficient
categories versus 65% at the state level. Even more importantly only 2 of our students or 4% obtained a Warning catégory
versus 7% at the state level.
In eighth grade students take both the Mathematics test and the Science and Technology Test. In Mathematics 46% of our
students scored in the Advanced/Proficient categories versus 37% at the state level and 17% obtained a warning versus 33% at
the state level, In Science and Technology 51% of our students scored in the Advanced or Proficient categories versus 32% at
the state level and only 13% obtained a Warning category versus 31% at the state level.
What do all of these statistics mean? There Is no doubt that we expect to do better than the state average. However, in almost
every category we are 10 percentage points or more above the state averages and some were even higher. Also of importance
were the low numbers of students in the Warning category versus the state numbers,
One other aspect of these scores that Is Important is called Adequate Yearly Progress (AYP). The DOE tells us that regardless of
where any public school system falls in relation to state scores, every system must make "adequate yearly progress." Knowing
that one class of students may be better than another, the DOE rates all systems every two years in this manner. They can
compare how much progress each system has made vis a vis how much they expect the system to make. This Is the most
Important ranking. So far Turkey HIII Middle School has exceeded our AYP expectations. The scores that we obtain this spring
will be averaged with last year's scores to tell us whether or not we have again exceeded state expectations. Our goal will
always remain to exceed state averages.
These results occur because of dedicated and professional teachers, and Turkey Hill Middle School Is blessed with many such
teachers. In addition, the Improved scores are a reflection of the ongoing, high-quality professional development that Is a
trademark of the Lunenburg Public Schools. Our staff has embraced a number of new professional development initiatives. Our
"Results" philosophy continues to grow. One faculty meeting each month, all teachers meet in subject-area teams to discuss
student work, test scores and other information related to academic progress. As a result, teachers plan strategies focused
around how to Improve academic performance. These meetings provide valuable time for teachers to reflect on their teaching,
to plan Joint activities and to improve student learning.
Teachers also engaged in a number of other activities: At several faculty meetings this year, the faculty met to discuss the new
mission of the Lunenburg Public Schools that is found in the District Improvement Plan. An important part of this mission is the
development of students who are "life-long learners". Teachers met to discuss and initiate strategles that would help our
students achieve this goal. Today's students will face a variety of changes In jobs and Job requirements, so it is essential that
they understand how to be a continuous learner. Through the office of Professional Development, all staff continued to be
trained in a teaching philosophy called Understanding by Design. Through this effort, teachers explored the enduring
understandings and essential questions related to their subjects. Teaching in this way encourages students to develop the
fundamental concepts related to all subjects and thus be better able to analyze, retain and use knowledge. Lastly teachers
continued their efforts to "map" their curricular areas thus reducing gaps and repetitions and ensuring that all students are
provided a high-quality learning experience.

education students, free-reduced lunch, and Title I). T.C. Passios met AYP in Math for all students and for all subgroups. In
English/Language Arts the AYP was met for Title I and free and reduced lunch student but not for special education students.
This data helps guide the school councils, administrative team and Results teams to plan for appropriate Interventions,
programming and professional development.
Ensuring all students improve requires a coherent and comprehensive plan that continually supports school structures and
collaboration focused on student learning. Results Teams, Understanding by Design Training, our professional development
program, the new teacher Induction program and continuous review of programs all support student learning.
Results Teams meet one time per month during faculty meeting time. The teams use data to set goals, plan teaching strategies
and assess the results. This process ensures a regular review of student performance and effective teaching strategies.
A majority of our teachers have been trained in Understanding by Design. This assessment process focuses on developing
assessments to Improve student performance. Through ongoing analysis of student results, refinement of units and increased
awareness of effective assessments and instructional practices, student performance will improve. The UBD units provide
additional measures in non- MCAS subjects and provide additional curriculum materials for new teachers.
Ongoing and sustained professional development continues In a range of areas. This year graduate level courses were offered
In Understanding by Design, Teaching Smarter, Standards-based Mathematics, Differentiating Math Instruction, Enduring
Historical Understandings, The Teenage Culture, Teacher as Researcher, Habits of Effective Teachers and Reading
Comprehension.
This year Title I services have expanded to Include a part-time math specialist and an extended day program. Additional math
support is provided in classrooms at T.C. Passios and the Turkey Hill Middle School. The extended day program provides
additional time on learning for Title I students. Technology Integration specialist provides a range of technology workshops and
support building level technology Initiatives.
Our new teacher induction program provides ongoing support for new teachers to the district. New teachers participate in a two-
day orientation, have a trained mentor and receive peer coaching during a three-year process. New teachers participate In
professional development focused on skills and strategies needed for beginning teachers.
SUMMARY
The Instructional Services Office has a strong partnership with the Individual schools, administrative team, faculty and
community. The combined and integrated efforts of all parties ensure continued focus on improving student performance. The
Office of Instruction strives to provide teachers, administrators, students and the community with the necessary data,
educational research, professional development, support and resources needed to Improve student performance.

The focus of the school maintenance effort for FY04 was renovations to heating systems at Turkey Hill Middle School and
heating and ventilation system renovations at the Passios Elementary School and Lunenburg High.
A $313,000 renovation project for the Turkey Hill Middle School heating system was completed in September 2003. A federal
grant for $178,000 helped to reduce the cost to the town. An additional grant of $7,700 from the State Department of Revenue
further reduced costs for removing the 10,000-gallon underground fuel tank. Norian/Siani Engineers Incorporated of Waltham,
MA designed the renovation project and Dillon Boller Service Company of Fitchburg was awarded the contract for the work, The
scope of the project included removing the two existing bollers, underground fuel tank, domestic hot water tank and heater and
circulation pump. Replacement equipment included high efficiency gas fire boilers.
A $1,550,000 renovation mechanical upgrade project for the Passlos Elementary School and Lunenburg High School, which
began In July 2003, is expected to be completed in March 2004. The project is being funded from an appropriation approved at
the May 2003 town meeting and election. Dillon Boller was the low bidder for the project and was awarded the contract for the
work. The scope of the project included replacing two 50-year-old boilers, conversion from steam to hot water bollers,
replacement of the domestic hot water systems and heating control system for both schools. At Passios Elementary School, the
project Included replacement of heat circulation pumps, cafeteria unit ventilators and air handlers for the teacher lunchroom and
the Learning Center. At Lunenburg High School the project included replacement of the high school gym and auditorium air
handlers as well as air conditioning for the auditorium and replacement of all classroom unit ventilators. The large scope of this
project made coordination of the renovation effort while continuing to support full use of the building a challenge. The faculty,
students and the general contractor worked together to ensure this important project continued to move forward to completion.
In Fiscal Year 05, the focus of capital maintenance will be prepare a energy performance contract proposal for all schools.
Particular emphasis will be to upgrade lighting at all buildings to high efficiency lighting.
A multiyear program to bring all schools up to compliance with the Americans with Disabilities Act continues to suffer from a
lack of funding and will likely stretch into FY06.
The Lunenburg Track and Field Athletic Association continued site preparation for the building of a soccer and track facility
between the High School and the Middle School. Work Is on going on an astro turf like playing surface for the field.
At the Primary School, the age of electrical wiring and condition of the heat distribution system at the Primary School remain a
constant concern. Leaking condensation return lines for the heating system required numerous highly disruptive minor repairs.
Construction of the new Lunenburg Primary School continues at 1401 Massachusetts Avenue. Future uses for the current
Primary School continue to be studied.

7.0 Non-Conforming Uses, Structures and Lots
7.1 Non-Conforming Lots Increased requirements respecting lot area, frontage, width, yard and similar dimensions
provided in this Bylaw or amendments thereto shall be subject to the exceptions provided in Section 6 of Chapter 40A
of the General Laws and shall not apply to a lot for single and two-family residential use which at the time of the
recording or endorsement, whichever occurs sooner, was not held In common, ownership with adjoining land,
conformed to the then existing requirements and had less than the proposed requirement but had at least five thousand
(5000) square feet of area and fifty (50) feet of frontage.
7.2. Building, Structures and Uses already in Existence; Abandonment
7.2.1. A non-conforming building or structure, or a non-conforming use of a building, structure or land, which has been
abandoned, or which has not been used for more than a period of two (2) years shall not be resumed, provided that
where the non-conforming use Is agriculture, horticulture or floriculture, non-use shall not constitute abandonment
under this Bylaw, and the non-conforming use may otherwise be resumed, unless such non-use was maintained for a
period of more than five (5) years.
7.2.1.2 Voluntary demolition of a non-conforming structure without permission of the Zoning Board of Appeals shall
constitute abandonment.
7.3 Non-conforming Uses
7.3.1.
A lawfully non-conforming use of a building, structure, or land shall not be altered, extended, changed or
substantially extended, except to a conforming use, unless authorized by the board of appeals pursuant to 7.3.2.
7.3.1.1. Alterations to a legally non-conforming use that: a) do not reflect the nature and purpose of the original use; or
b) result in a use different In quality, character, or degree from the original use and not ordinarily and reasonably
adapted to the original use; or c). result in a use that is different in kind in its effect on the neighborhood than the
original use, shall constitute a change or substantial extension of the original use.
7.3.1.2. A non-conforming use has been substantially extended If the total of all floor area plus open ground area
devoted to such use on the premises has been increased by more than fifty (50) percent over the total S0 devoted at the
time the use first became non-conforming. This Section shall not apply to residential use of single and two-family
structures.
7.3.2
No non-conforming use shall be extended, altered, or changed unless approved via a special permit issued by
the Zoning Board of Appeals upon its finding that such extension, alteration or change will not be substantially more
detrimental than the existing non-conforming use to the neighborhood.
Non-conforming Structures
7.4.1.
Except as provided in Section 7.4.2., a lawfully non-conforming building or structure shall not be
reconstructed, extended or altered except pursuant to special permit Issued by the Zoning Board of Appeals upon its
finding that such reconstruction, extension or alteration will not be substantially more detrimental than the existing
non-conforming structure to the neighborhood.
7.4.1.1.
Any reconstruction, extension or alteration of a non-conforming structure that increases an existing
nonconformity or results in the creation of an additional nonconformity shall require a variance from the Zoning Board
of Appeals in addition to the finding set forth in Section 7.4.1.

===PAGE 87===
7.4.2. Reconstruction, extension, alteration or structural change to a single-or two-family residential, non-
conforming structure, may be allowed, upon a determination provided that such reconstruction, extension, alteration,
or structural change meets all current zoning requirements, is not more substantially detrimental to the neighborhood,
and does not Increase the non-conforming nature of the structure.
7.4.2.1 Notwithstanding Section 7.4.2. reconstruction, extension, alteration or structural change (collectively
"alteration") of a single-or-two family residential, lawfully non-conforming structure shall be deemed not
to increase the non-conforming nature of said structure and shall be eligible for a building permit without review by the
Zoning Board of Appeals, if:
a)
the structure is located on a conforming lot and the proposed alteration will comply in all
respects with the Bylaw, or
b)
the structure is located on a lawfully non-conforming lot, and the proposed alteration will
retain the structure's existing footprint, will not increase an existing non-conformity or
result in the creation of an additional non-conformity and will not increase the total cubic
feet of the structure.
7.4.2.2
Reconstruction, extension, alteration or structural change to a single-or two-family residential, lawfully
non-conforming structure located on a non-conforming lot, that increases the total cubic feet of that structure, shall
constitute an increase in the non-conforming nature of said structure.
7.4.3.
No reconstruction, extension, or alteration to a non-conforming structure shall be permitted that results in lot
coverage greater than thirty percent (30%), except for determination by the Zoning Board of Appeals.
7.4.4.

moved, reconstructed, replaced or substantially altered, except as authorized by a finding of the Zoning Board of
Appeals, pursuant to Section 7.4.1.
A building or structure devoted to a non-conforming use (whether In whole or in part) and a building or structure non-
conforming as to height, setback or yards may, if damaged or destroyed by fire or other accidental cause, be repaired
or reconstructed within the same portion of the lot, to the same or lesser dimensions, and used as before, without a
finding as provided In either Section 7.4.1 or Section 7.4.2, provided that such repair or reconstruction Is substantially
completed within two (2) years of said damage or destruction, otherwise, such building shall be repaired, reconstructed
and used only in conformity with the provisions of this Bylaw. The Board of Appeals may, however in a specific case,
authorize the extension of the two (2) year time limit or authorize a special exception from the requirement of
