MEMORIAM
THUSNELDE K. DRUMM - Nelde or Tiz, as most people knew her, was a resident of Lunenburg since 1940 and was
known for her style and grace. She was a graduate of Boston University School of Art and taught art in Lunenburg and
surrounding communities and was supervisor of the art department for Lunenburg Public Schools until her retirement
in 1977. She was a practicing silversmith as well as a painter, winning numerous awards. She co-authored the book
"Lunenburg, The Heritage of Turkey Hills" with Margaret Harley, published in 1977. The book is still on sale at the Town
Hall. A ready and capable volunteer for the town, United Parish of Lunenburg, the Fitchburg Art Museum, the
Lunenburg Historical Society and many other committees and organizations. She exemplified the meaning of
commitment to community and love of the arts.
WILLIAM R. PROCTOR SR. - Bill Proctor was born and raised on Clark's Hill, site of Samuel Page's homestead,
the first settler of Lunenburg. Bill was a 1947 graduate of Lunenburg High School. He was a farm boy from a large
family, and though he and his wife Bea traveled all over the world, Lunenburg was where his heart was. Bill was a
familiar face in town. His business, William R. Proctor Inc. recently celebrated 50 years in business. He helped build
many of the roads in town, particularly in the Hickory Hills area. Bill was an avid hunter and fisherman and was a
founding member of the Lunenburg Sportsman Club. Raising a family in Lunenburg, Bill was involved in Little League,
Pop Warner football, and Lunenburg Old Home Days. He was a member of the Lunenburg 250th Anniversary Committee
and twenty-five years later helped organize the Lunenburg 275th Anniversary celebration. His dedication to the town
will be missed.
DONNA SMETANA Donna was a devoted employee of the town's library for over 10 years. She passed away
unexpectedly in July 2006. She worked tirelessly and served in many capacities at the library over the years and her
calm, cheerful presence is sorely missed by staff and patrons allke.
MARGUERITE SOROKA the passing of Marguerite Soroka in December of 2006 was a great loss to the Town of
Lunenburg. She was a Library Trustee and Chairman of the Board for many years as well as an active member of the
United Parish of Lunenburg. Her other town memberships included the Lunenburg Historical Society, the Bookworms,
Lunenburg Women's Club and Smith College Alumni Association. Without her tireless effort the dream of a new
Lunenburg Public Library would never have been realized.
ROBERT L. WARE a long time Sunny Hill Road resident and fourth generation principal In the law firm of Ware
and Ware, Bob served the legal needs of numerous town residents during his 52 years of practice as an estate planning
and conveyance attorney for Fitchburg Savings Bank. By his nature approachable and a conciliator, Bob also served
on the Lunenburg Zoning Board of Appeals from 1964 to 1975 and on the Route 13 Study Committee from 1990 to 1995.
Working with his mother Susan Ware, he handled the legal work for the Lunenburg Memorial Fund, a resource known in
the community for supplementing funding for many town agencies and committees. Bob recently was instrumental in
doing the legal work that redirected 4H funds to the founding of the Chester Mossman Teen Center. Bob's memory,
along with that of Nelde Drumm, was recently honored with the renewal of a neighborhood garden on the corner of
Sunny Hill Road and Elm Street. Bob's lifelong respect for the Lunenburg Police and Fire Departments was made very
personal when they made two rescue calls to his home during the last few months of his life. He was grateful and
relieved to see the familiar faces which gave both personal and professional care during his and his family's time of
need.

BOARD OF SELECTMEN / SEWER COMMISSIONERS
Following the Election In May 2006, Robert H. Bowen was re-elected as Chairman, Thomas A. Alonzo was elected as Vice-
chairman, and Steve deBettencourt was elected as Clerk of the Board. Newest member Paula Bertram took her seat on the Board
along with current member Daniel W. Cronin. Fiscal 2006 marked the first full-year for our new Chief Administrative and Financial
Officer, Daniel M. Keyes. Fiscal 2006 also marked the Initiation of the new Board of Sewer Commissioners. This role in overseeing
the Town's sewer system and enterprise fund was formerly performed by the Board of Selectmen. In order to transition to the new
Board, two members of the Board of Selectmen, Steve deBettencourt and Paula Bertram, were appointed to one year terms on the
Board of Sewer Commissioners. The Commission has been working diligently to establish a sewer department budget, and to
bring the current sewer project to closure.
With the conclusion of the current phase of the sewer project, and pursuant to the vote of Town meeting, the Town has undertaken
the preparation of a Comprehensive Waste-Water Management Plan to assess any future sewering needs for the Town. The much
heralded growth In the Town of Lunenburg Is coming In fits and starts. With approximately 3600 existing residential housing units,
projections of between 1000 to 1200 new units in varlous stages of approval or conceptual proposal raised serious concerns as
the fiscal year began. Many factors, not the least of which has. been the slow down In the residential real estate market, have
tempered the urgency, and should allow us some much needed time to adequately plan for the Impact of such dramatic new
growth on our infrastructure, and on our budget. Under the auspices of the Planning Director, an ad hoc land use committee has
been formed to assist with this necessary planning. Lunenburg has also established "planned production" and approved an
affordable housing "40R" district for the construction of 204 rental units. These are critical tools that will Increase the local control
our land use boards, particularly the Zoning Board of Appeals, will have over the approval of "40B" affordable housing projects In
the future.
Fiscal 2006 found the town settling in to its magnificent new buildings, the public safety complex, the primary school, and the new
library. Work and planning still remains regarding the vacated buildings in the Town Center. The old public safety building is
scheduled to be demolished pursuant to the vote of Town meeting, and the Susan Brown House, which received no blds In a
recent attempt to sell it, will again be put out to bld. Funds have been appropriated to conduct a study with respect to the needs of
Town office space which may utilize some of the other buildings. Town meeting has approved the Community Preservation Act,
which If it passes at the Annual 2007 election, will provide much need funds to accomplish some of these things. The school
department has recently completed a study of its facilities, which may mean that a proposal for the construction of a new high
school may be brought before the voters in the coming years.
As fiscal 2007 comes to a close, we find ourselves at a fiscal crossroads in Lunenburg. We have begun the important task of
planning for our financial future by developing a five-year financial forecast. Like any forecast, it is an evolving document that will
change as each revenue and expenditure assumption is reviewed and modified. Nevertheless, it is a critical tool for our financial
planning and budget analysis. Utilizing that tool, and after careful analysis and Input from the departments, and town committees
such as Capital Planning, the Assessors Office, the Planning Board and the Finance Committee, the budget proposed by the CAFO
calls for a 1.9 million dollar override pursuant to proposition 2 1/2 This figure represents the collective judgment of the revenue
needed to operate the town. If it does not pass, additional cuts will need to be made in all departments to balance the budget. By
a vote of 4-1, the Board of Selectmen decided It was time to put this Important question before the voters.
The Board extends a special "thank you" again this year to the Lunenburg / Townsend Rotary Club for decorating the Gazebo at
Christmas and. the Turkey Hill Gardeners for the seasonal Town Hall decorations and flowers in the upper common. We would
also like to acknowledge the generous donations that were received and utilized so that the Town could continue with the
traditional Band Concerts on the Common and any donations to this cause would be greatly appreciated.
Your Board has enjoyed working in service to the Town, working with each other and with you the citizens. The Selectmen would
also like to acknowledge the Individuals who work so hard for the Town and those who volunteer their time and commitment by
serving on the various Boards, Commissions and Committees within the Town.
Please feel free to direct any calls to our office staff, Daniel M. Keyes, Chief Administrative Financial Officer, Laura Williams, Chief
of Staff / Chief Administrative Assistant or Linda Douglas, Administrative Assistant @ 978-582-4144. If they cannot assist you,
they will forward your call to the appropriate authorities. Our office hours are Monday, Wednesday and Thursday 8:00 AM to 4:00
PM, Tuesday 8:00 AM to 6:30 PM and Friday 8:00 AM to 12:30 PM.

CHIEF ADMINISTRATIVE FINANCIAL OFFICER REPORT
Daniel M. Keyes, Chief Administrative Financial Officer
It has been a full year serving the citizens of Lunenburg as your Chief Administrative Financial Officer and much has been
accomplished yet much more remains. The first and most important Issue was to set an agenda so all departments could work
together for the common good. One of the major concerns facing the department heads and their employees were the unresolved
collective bargaining agreements for the laborers, clerical, fire and police unions. During this past year we have entered into
negotiations and have settled some of the major Issues regarding these contracts, One of those is the skyrocketing cost of health
insurance and we have been able to address that issue to the resolve of both parties.
New growth has become a topic of much concern for the citizens of Town. With the Planning Department seeing an increased
Interest by developers to build between 1000 and 1200 units In Lunenburg the impact on our infrastructure as well our schools,
public safety and daily operations of government will mean an increase In added services for the town and with that come added
costs. These will Include affordable housing (40B) as well as smart growth and the planned housing of the new Emerald Park.
The Meadow Woods Trailer Park issue, which has come under the scrutiny of the Board of Health and the Department of
Environmental Protection (DEP) had to be addressed. The town applied for economic relief to the Department of Agriculture for a

CAPITAL PLANNING
The Capital Planning Committee, as noted in past years, follows the Charter's Direction to present FY plans and Capital Program
expenditures. The Committee's capital budget is presented through the Chief Administrative and Financial Officer to the Board of
Selectmen and the Finance Committee. The final plan is voted at Town Meeting.
The FY yearly plans determine the needs of the governmental departments for the coming year. The FY yearly plan includes the
needs of the service departments, the DPW, Fire and Police. It further Includes the schools, library, technology, land use
departments and the chief operating offices. The Capital Program Plan deplcts the long range needs of the Town such new
buildings and/or major building renovations.
The FY'07 yearly plan, as determined by the Capital Planning Committee from the requests of all departments, was not passed in
May. 2006. The plan was drastically cut and re-presented at the December 2006 Special Town Meeting.
The Special Town Meeting passed the following:
Police Department at Two (2) Police Cruisers $73,000.00
Fire Department . Turn Out Gear $10,000.00
Fire Department . Fire Hose $15,000.00
DPW Department - 6 wheel Dump Truck w/plow/sander $55,000.00
School Department - ES Alarm/Security/Door Replacement $46,100.00
Technology Department - Annual replacement of Town Technology $50,900.00
The recommendation of the CPC for the FY'07 plans was $630,086.00. The criteria of "Ability to Fund" required the CPC to
drastically reduce the plan to $370,761.00. In FY'07 and as noted above, the further reduced bottom line of $250,000.00 was
approved. Therefore the above expenditures did not come close to the amount to meet the needs.
Each request Is analyzed according to the Criteria, which is Mandates, Safety, Need, Crisis, Cost Effectiveness and Ability to Fund.
Each request is reviewed with the Department Heads and managed by a determination of the criteria along with urgency of the
needed Item. The requested expenditures are the tools that provide services of safety and protection, the management of
government and the means to keep facilities and grounds in good condition. The CPC works through the criteria effectively until
it comes to "Ability to Fund."
The Committee, as in FY'07, has to recommend on the basis of deferral. Deferral brings consequences such as deficient capital
items today, backlogged capital items, Inflation of cost, poorly maintained Infrastructure, Increase in annual operating expenses
and not least of all, lesser service to the public.
The Capital Program was not funded in FY'07. Past Capital Program articles reports show that the heating system for the
elementary /high schools has been reimbursed to the town. Monies expended for the fire truck was $298,000. Total payments for
the Library and Public Safety Buildings was $641,002 and the first payment reimbursement for the primary school was $513,448.
Each year, we drag out the "Big Man-Little Man" analogy. It is a comparison of the Omnibus Budget (Operating) and the Capital
Budget. This comparison is not a negative against operating needs but a plea for the Town to realize that Capital upkeep must
have the support of a continuous balance In Capital Spending. Continuous Balance of Capital Expenditures meets the criteria of
Safety, Need and Cost Effectiveness. Balance can not be obtained when the capital needs is continuously deferred.

CENTRAL MASSACHUSETTS MOSQUITO CONTROL PROJECT
The Central Massachusetts Mosquito Control Project (the Project) currently provides its services to 36 cities and towns throughout
Middlesex and Worcester Countles. The Project's headquarters is located at 111 Otls Street, Northboro, MA. Tours of the
headquarters or visits to field work sites may be arranged by calling the office In advance. Please call (508) 393-3055 during
business hours for more information. The Project practices Integrated Mosquito Management (IMM), blending state of the art
methods and techniques with expertise, experience, and scientific research to provide our member communities with
environmentally sound and cost effective mosquito control.
During 2006 the Project received eight thousand and fifty five (8,455) requests for service from town residents and officials. A total
of over six thousand (6,000) pounds of Bti (Bacillus thuringlensis israelensis} was applied by helicopter in 2 towns, Chelmsford
& Billerica, and five thousand, nine hundred and fifty nine (5,959) pounds by hand throughout our service area were applied to area
wetlands to reduce the emergence of adult mosquitoes. This represents over one thousand, one hundred and ninety one (1,191)
acres of wetland that was treated with this mosquito-specific bacterlum, significantly reducing adult mosquito populations in these
areas. Thirty three thousand, nine hundred and eighteen (33,918) catch basins were treated with larvicidal product to control the
mosqultoes that seek out these cool dark wet areas to breed, including the Culex mosquito, a major target for West Nile Virus
transmission. Seven thousand, three hundred and ninety three (7,393) culverts were cleaned in an attempt to eliminate
unnecessary standing water and reduce mosquito breeding. This work was done in conjunction with cleaning, clearing, and
digging of one hundred and seventy six thousand, one hundred and eighty one (176,181) feet of streams, brooks and ditches. This
represents over thirty three.(33) miles of waterways which were cleaned and Improved by Project personnel In 2006.
The Mosquito Awareness Program which we offer to elementary schools and other civic organizations In our district has become
very popular. Project staff meets with students, teachers or concerned residents to discuss mosquito biology, mosquito habitat,
and control procedures. Much of the presentation is directed towards what children and their families can do to prevent
mosquitoes from breeding around their homes. Slides, videos, coloring books and other handouts make this an interesting
program. This program Is tallored to meet the needs of the specific audience. Two thousand, two hundred and fifty (2,250)
students attended these programs.
As part of our effort to reduce the need for pesticides we continue to expand our wetlands restoration program. By cleaning
clogged and overgrown waterways, mosquito breeding can be reduced and drainage areas are restored to historic conditions.
Btl mosquito larvicide is used to treat areas where mosquito larvae are found. We routinely check known breeding sites kept In
out database, but also encourage the public to notify us of any areas they suspect could breed mosquitoes. Our field crews will
Investigate all such requests and treat the area only If surveillance gathered at the time shows an imminent threat of mosquito
emergence.
Our goal is to manage all mosquito problems with education, wetlands restoration or larviciding, but we recognize that there are
times when adult mosqulto spraying is the only viable solution. In such cases specific areas are treated with either hand-held or
pickup truck mounted sprayers if surveillance gathered at the time exceeds a pre-determined threshold to warrant an application.
This program is offered on a request-only basis, and the exclusion process allows residents and/or town officials to exclude areas
under their control from this or any part of our program.
The Project's surveillance program monitors adult mosquito and larval population density, and is the backbone for prescribing
various control techniques. Specialized mosquito traps are deployed throughout the Project's service area to sample for
mosquitoes that may be transmitting mosquito-borne diseases. In conjunction with the Mass. Dept. of Public Health we sample in
areas suspected of harboring WNV and other viruses. One thousand, five hundred and twenty eight (1,528) pools (collections) of
mosquitoes totaling thirty nine thousand, five hundred and six (39,506) specimens were tested for mosquito-borne viruses this
year. One (1) pool of Cs. morisitans in Leominster was confirmed to be infected with the EEE virus late in the season. Two (2)
pools of West Nile Virus were confirmed, both in Westborough in the Culex species. Two (2) human cases were identified with
WNV, one in Billerica and one in Webster. No human cases of EEE were Identified, and there were no cases of WNV or EEE in
horses in our service area.
Educational pamphlets are available to anyone interested in learning about mosquito control and the services provided by the
Project, and these Items are routinely stocked in member Town/City Halls and libraries. Display boards with Information on our
program are rotated through area Town Halls throughout the year. We also have a website, www.cmmcp.org that has extensive
information on mosquito biology, our control procedures, etc. This website has become a model for other Mosquito Projects and
has been widely used throughout our service area and beyond.
We would like to thank you for your support during 2006 and we look forward to helping you and your community with Its
mosquito problems in 2007 and beyond.

PLANNING BOARD
The Planning Board membership has two new members, Joanna Bllotta and Robert Salla. They were appointed to fulfill the terms
of Deborah Seeley and Gary Prime until the next election. Deborah Seeley was a long term member and Is greatly missed for her
questioning deliberations and thoughtful decisions. Gary Prime was on the Board for a short period of time and left for health

serves as Planning Director.
In review of the year, the Board approved 17 ANR plans (approval for frontage lots on a Town Approved Way). The Board has
Issued approvals for multi-developments which require Special Permits and/or Development Plan Review Approvals. Two major
projects that had thorough and full reviews are Tri-Town Landing, Youngs Road, a Chapter 40R submittal and Emerald Place at
Lake Whalom, Lakefront, a Zoning Bylaw by Right Project. The Board has had closure on the Harley House Project,
Massachusetts Avenue, to operate as an Inn; Lena Lane, Lancaster Avenue/Gibson Street, a 4 unit subdivision has been approved
for construction; Stone Farm Estates, Massachusetts Avenue; White Tall Crossing, Burrage Street; and Whites Woods, White
Street and Massachusetts Avenue are still in construction. Two commercial establishments were permitted; Trudie's Treats,
Lancaster Avenue and First Choice Limousine, Chase Road. Two applications for telecommunication towers were received, one
for 621 Chase Road and one for 270 Electric Avenue. Whalom Storage's original plan for 80,000 sq ft of self-storage, Electric
Avenue, was in review and withdrawn. A new revised plan was submitted on January 25, 2007. Essent Health Care,
Massachusetts Avenue, submitted an application for expansion In December.
The philosophy of Planning in this Community is to grow slowly with matching infrastructure. The Planning Board has
encouraged the village type of development; however, by right and mandated development, larger projects have been submitted.
Also, developers have placed conceptual proposals on the table which gives the perception of rapid growth. These conceptuals
will not be given full review until a statutory submittal is before the Board.
The issue of mandated growth was partly addressed with the Smart Growth Bylaw. The 40R project at Tri-Town Landing, which is
in the final stages before construction, will assist In meeting the mandated quota set forth by the State Inventory. Further, Planned
Production approval by the State for the Town will be effective in managing the timing of any future 40B projects once the Town
meets a percentlie of the State quota. This Is a process that gives the Town the opportunity to manage a timeline in this type of
development.
At the December Special Town Meeting, a Bylaw was passed that mandates that any conventional development must set aside a
percentage of structures to meet the affordable criteria. This process will help keep L'unenburg's standing on the top percentile of
the State Inventory. This allows the Town to be in charge of the rate of mandated growth. Conventional growth has not surpassed
the criteria set forth in the Bylaw.
The Planning Board's desire is to establish a strong land use department Ad Hoc Committee. The group has only met a few times.
The Planning Department is presently preparing a housing market study in the area, which will also reflect the State's pursuit of
small residential units, Including rentals, to present to the Ad Hoc Committee.
The Board has commented on the first draft report of the CWMP and has noted Input to the need of balancing in residential sewer
construction, while at the same time Investing in sewer construction that will assist commercial and industrial growth. Year 2006
has not been a year of growth in regards to residential sales and commercial/industrial enterprises.
Planning Board members serve as representatives on other Committees. James Halloran serves as the Planning representative to
the Montachusetts Regional Planning Commission and the School Feasibility Study; Robert Salla, as representative to the
Montachusetts Joint Transportation Commission; Emerick Bakaysa is the Planning Board representative to the Charter Review
Commission and Joanna Bllotta is the representative to Lake Shirley's 319 Study. The Board has appointed Marion Benson,
Planning Director to represent the Board on the Capital Planning Committee.

FIRE DEPARTMENT
This year the Fire Department has responded to Increased calls for service as well as multiple requests for plans review of new
construction. This will continue to be the trend as the town changes and grows over the next few years. As these demands
Increase, so will the need to increase our staffing of both call and career firefighters. The Increased demand for service also

Last year the town's first ladder truck was commissioned into service. This year, I want you to know that the ladder truck has and
will continue to play a vital role in the preservation of life and property. The purchase of the ladder truck has, without question,
enhanced fire ground safety and allowed us to perform quicker and safer roof. operations.
We have collaborated with the cities of Leominster and Fitchburg to create a regional dive team. For the first time In our history,
the Lunenburg Fire Department has a dive team with four certified divers and five shore tenders on the team. The three
communities applied for and received a grant to purchase equipment to help outfit the divers. Considering the number of bodies of
water In Lunenburg, a dive team is an obvious need that offers more protection to the citizens of Lunenburg. By working together,
both citles and our town benefit by having more trained personnel available to all.
Training Is an ongoing requirement of the fire service. in addition to routine training, several of our members completed training in
hazardous. materials rescue. This course prepares rescuers to enter a hazardous environment in fully encapsulated suits In order
to rescue those injured or exposed. This course was developed with the Local Emergency Planning Committee Chairperson. We
are also diligently planning and training for dealing with a pandemic out break.
We continue to provide prompt response to calls for service but also place a high priority on training and increasing our
preparedness for unforeseen events, In order to better serve the residents of our town. We are your first line of defense and we are
proud to serve you.

SPECIAL SERVICES
During the 2005-2006 school year, the Montachusett Regional Vocational Technical School District provided special education
services to over two hundred students. In September 2006, the Support Services Department Is working with approximately 197
students.
The revised law, Individuals with Disabilities Education Act (IDEA-04), took effect July 1, 2005 with official and final part B
regulations being completed on 9/16/06. It provides for more services to students transitioning from Monty Tech to careers,
college, or further training. Since the intent of this law is to educate as many students as possible in the general education
classrooms, the staff at Monty Tech has embarked on a major shift in how we teach and support students with disabilities. The
major focus of special education services will be Inclusive education using universal design to ensure that every student with a
disability is educated In the "least restrictive environment."
The student services at Monty Tech are available to all students. We have a full-time school nurse with a part-time assistant who
administers medications, performs state mandated health screening tests, and provides, when necessary, health information to
the student's IEP team meeting. We have a full-time school social worker who is able to participate on teams and assist students
who have needs concerning finances, family issues, homelessness, maternity, health issues, and proper food and clothing. Our
students have access to the services of a full-time adjustment counselor and a part-time school psychologist. Both of these
people are available for scheduled counseling sessions and mental health emergency treatment or crisis intervention. We recently
added a full-time psychologist to complete all testing and evaluations for Initial and three year re-evaluations. A full-time speech
pathologist is available to service students with disabilities, assess students, and consult with all teachers in the building.
The Special Education Program at Monty Tech is reviewed every May in accordance with regulatory requirements. The results of
this evaluation are used to improve the special education procedures and programs at Monty Tech. The Parent Advisory Council
participates in this review and evaluation.
The Montachusett Regional Vocational Technical School District adheres to federal and state law regarding confidentiality of
personally Identifiable student Information.

TECHNOLOGY
Monty Tech continues to provide technology resources, which exceed both state averages and benchmarks. Our Inventory
Includes workstations using both Windows and Macintosh operating systems. The budget generally allows us to keep the age of
our workstations at 5 years or less, which Is somewhat less than Ideal. However, placement of systems based on applications
requirements allows us to continue to productively utilize older systems.
Overall network performance, especially Internet traffic, has been an ongoing concern. Last year, we tripled the available Internet
bandwidth resulting in a significant Improvement with regard to internet access. In a typical day at Monty Tech, over 550,000 web
pages are accessed by our students and staff. As reported last year, upgrades to network infrastructure, particularly old category
3 wiring remains a priority. The Installation of managed switches has allowed us to control traffic, which might otherwise have
problems on the older parts of the network.
We reported last year that we were decreasing the emphasis on laptops. This issue was revisited by the administration, and we
added significantly to our wireless laptop Inventory. Wireless access points in the school continue to expand. We have also
added a terminal based Windows Computer Lab using the Citrix System. All software resides and processing occurs on a single
server in the Technology Office, reducing the time required to Install software, upgrades and perform other maintenance tasks.
Along with increasing the Internet bandwidth, we also installed a new firewall, which effectively controls content filtering, virus and
hacking attacks. A new e-mail system has provided marked Improvement with regard to controlling spam.
Probably, the most significant challenge to the Technology Office revolves around insuring that network performance remains at
acceptable levels. The numerous servers in place, as well as network traffic problems, requires a considerable allocation of
resources on a daily basis.

COMMUNITY BUILDING
In January, the entire student body was treated to a piano performance by recording artist and planist Brittany Maler. Brittany Is a
17 year old young woman who is blind and autistic. She also has a remarkable talent as a planist, and travels the United States
performing for audiences, spreading the message that a disability doesn't have to keep you from excelling in something that you
love to do. Brittany currently performs over 15,000 songs from memory, and adds about 2000 songs per year to her repertoire,
practicing up to 12 hours per day, Her inspiring performance was first seen by Jenna Jacobs, Best Buddies President, at the
National Best Buddies Conference this summer, where Jenna was S0 moved that she came home determined to convince the LHS
chapter to raise the funds to bring Brittany to perform in Lunenburg. The chapter, through fund raising efforts, raised over $1400
to sponsor both the performance for the LHS concert and an evening performance open to the public. Both shows proved to be
moving experiences for the audiences, as well as enjoyable sing-alongs, including requests from "Plano Man" and "Bohemian
Rhapsody" to "Rent". What was most moving was the acceptance of our students for Brittany as just another teenager with a
particular talent.

TURKEY HILL MIDDLE SCHOOL
Last year Turkey HIII Middle School was the only middle school in the entire Commonwealth to win the prestigious Compass
School Award and, in fact, the only middle school to win the award in four years. This year the Department of Education bestowed
still another honor on Turkey HIII; THMS won the John Monbouquette School Improvement Leadership Award. The DOE surveyed
all the past winners of the Compass Award, of which there are many, to determine which schools have continued to make
progress above what is expected on MCAS tests. Out of all of these schools only thirteen schools scored above expectations In
both English/language arts and mathematics. Turkey Hill Middle School was one of the thirteen! However, when the DOE focused
just on mathematics, Turkey Hill Middle was the very top scoring compass school in the entire commonwealth. What does this
mean? When the DOE looked at the best performing schools based on Improvement In MCAS results in both ELA and
mathematics THMS was one of the very best, and when they looked at just mathematics we were the best of the bestl What a
terrific honor for the students and teachers of Turkey Hill. What a terrific honor for all K-8 teachers who share this award. What a
terrific honor for the parents of all the students and the Town of Lunenburg.
In addition to the above awards THMS distinguished Itself In other ways. The Just4Kids website compares results on MCAS
scores from comparable kinds of schools. This year Lunenburg was placed with schools like Carlisle, Westford, Lexington,
Lincoln, Sharon, Wellesley, Weston, etc. all of whom spend a great deal more as judged by per pupil spending than does
Lunenburg. Yet Turkey HIII Middle continues year after year to perform as well as these schools. In fact our results are as follows:

The school athletics program continued in its second year at Turkey HIII. Once again our students competed in cross country,
field hockey, football, cheerleading and boys/girls basketball. It is a terrific experience for our students to represent themselves
and their school in athletic contests. The lessons learned from hard work, team work, as well as the camaraderle add a great deal
to the personal side of attending Turkey HIII. Student Government, Yearbook, and skilng continue to round out the opportunities
students have at THMS.
Another significant accomplishment for THMS was the increase in the number of students taking algebra in eighth grade.
Previously about 33% of our eighth graders took algebra; this year we have raised that number to 66%. Previously we were
slightly below the state average; now we are well above it. This change represents the constantly Improving mathematics program

students are doing just that as seen in the increased percentage; It reflects the rigor of the math program.
THMS spent a great deal of time providing time for our staff to develop common assessments and Understanding by Design units
of Instruction. Both of these Insure curriculum consistency across the grade levels. When all students in a grade are taught the
same units of instruction or use the same science lab techniques, or have the same writing experiences, all students Improve and
all students are challenged.
Another highlight of the year was a visit by Terri Francona, the manager of the Boston Red Sox. A student at THMS won a national
contest sponsored by Raytheon and because of that Raytheon chose our school as one of the two schools that Coach Francona

It was a grand and glorious year for Turkey Hill Middle School. As always we want to thank the citizens of Lunenburg for
supporting us and giving us the opportunity to enjoy success.
Thank you, Lunenburg.

LUNENBURG PRIMARY SCHOOL
Cynthia Daukantas, Principal
The Lunenburg Primary School continues to strive toward educating the total child. We utilize a variety of Instructional methods
and modalities in our approach to meet the Individual needs of every child. We believe that the success of our programs is
dependent upon a healthy and productive partnership between home and school. Toward that end, parents must play an active
role In many of the dally programs and routines of their child's educational program.
With an enrollment of four hundred twenty-four students, class sizes range from eighteen to twenty-four. Because research
indicates that class sizes of twenty and under in the early years provide lasting educational benefits to young children, we strive to
keep our kindergarten through grade 2 classes smaller.
During the summer of 2006 summer school funding was limited. Through fundraising efforts, which Included two book fairs,
School Linked Services was able to provide the funding for kindergarten and first grade children to receive Instruction for the
month of July. Mrs. Lisa Stone, kindergarten teacher, and Mrs. Barbara Nelson, grade 1 teacher, were excited about the progress
that all twenty children made. This progress was further noted in our grade one and two fall reading testing. I am hopeful that
through our continuing fundraising efforts, we will-be able to offer support to both kindergarten and grade one children during the
summer of '07.
Through the support of parents and to meet the needs of parents and children, two full day tuition based kindergarten programs
were created again this fall. We are excited about offering a second full day program to kindergarteners, and look forward to
when we can offer this program to all five year olds. We are extremely pleased and proud that our efforts in securing grant funds
to begin the process of planning for full day kindergarten for all students has resulted in a State Transition Planning Grant of
$30,000. This money will allow us to begin the process of a self-study through NAEYC In anticipation of national accreditation, as
well as to begin purchasing some much needed materials/equipment. We are hopeful that Lunenburg will be able to secure some
portion of full day kindergarten implementation funding for next fall.
The Primary School continues to strive toward academic excellence. Reading testing was completed this fall and again In January
on every child in kindergarten, grade 1, and grade 2. The mid-winter testing shows significant Improvements In all areas of
reading (phonemic segmentation, fluency, letter Identification, sound symbol). We are hopeful that our end of year testing will
validate that all children are reading on grade level. According to our School Improvement Plan, it is our goal that 80% of our
students at each grade level will read on grade level In June.
This year the faculty has worked to create quarterly assessments in math to monitor children's progress. Each administration of
this assessment includes an open response problem. Teachers have met to discuss their Individual classroom results as well as
to plan the needed Interventions for those children not meeting the standard. Our School Improvement Plan identifies the goal
to be 80% of students at each grade level will attain 80% of the standards in math.
The Primary School Improvement Plan focuses upon reading, writing, and math, as well as revising / updating our report cards and
writing exemplars. As the curriculum changes, our report cards needs to reflect the current standards so that we can
communicate student progress to parents. At this point in the year, report cards have been updated at all grade levels and will be
submitted to the School Committee for their approval In May as part of our School Improvement Plan update.
The Primary School Council meets the first Thursday of each month to discuss school Issues and to develop its annual School
Improvement Plan. Parents and community members are welcome to attend. Members of the Council for the 2006 . 2007 school
year are:
