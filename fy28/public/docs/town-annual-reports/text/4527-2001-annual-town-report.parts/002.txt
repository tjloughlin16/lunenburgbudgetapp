HIGH SCHOOL
Michael G. Barney, Principal
Lunenburg High School is particularly proud of two major accomplishments this year. After submitting our one-year report
to the New England Association of Schools and Colleges, we continue to be fully accredited with the Association. This
process, which takes place every ten years, includes a two-year self-study, public report and an evaluation from a visiting
team of professional educators. Our faculty worked extremely hard to implement recommended changes and will continue
to improve in all areas to better serve our community and its most precious resource, the children of Lunenburg.
The Class of 2003 also had a tremendous showing in the first round of MCAS testing which is a state requirement for
achieving a diploma. Almost 99% of our students passed the mark in English Language Arts and 95% passed the
Mathematics portion of the test on the first try, This is a tribute to the hard work of the students as well as the faculty and
curriculum teams who continually review, improve, and align our curriculum to the state frameworks.
The High School continued several traditions including our annual Homecoming Parade and 37th annual Competitive Class
Plays, as well as beginning a new one, the Alumni Tallgate before the Homecoming and Thanksgiving games. We invite all
LHS Alumni back home to all our events.
Academically, we piloted a new Interdisciplinary course this year called "Inventions", in which students research an
invention of the past, present its historical significance and place in time, then design and build a prototype. It was an
exciting first year and a big hit with the students who took the course. We also sponsored our first "Career Day", when
forty-five area professionals generously shared their time and expertise with our entire student body. In small groups, they
talked about career preparation, education and practical experience of the real world. This day was a tremendous success
and we would like to again publicly thank all the participants. We also placed several of our students into internship
opportunities this year, expanding their education beyond the walls of the high school.
Our athletic programs have done well this year. Our Field Hockey team was Mid-Wach C league champions and division 2
district finalists. Our girls' winter track and girls' basketball teams were both Mid-Wach C league champions, and all our
winter sports teams have qualified for district play. My particular congratulations to Coach Stachowicz, completing his 39th
year of coaching at LHS, and Coach Lamothe for his induction Into the Massachusetts Baseball Hall of Fame.
Through the generosity of the Chester W. Mossman trust, our greenhouse has been rebuilt, along with a new potting shed,
which will enhance and support our excellent horticulture program. Lunenburg High School would also like to recognize
the Herculean efforts of the Lunenburg Track and Field Association, their board, and scores of volunteers whose dream will
soon become reality. Their efforts are truly appreciated and will touch several generations of young people in Lunenburg.
We certainly appreciate all they do.
Several of our students realized dreams individually, including winning awards in the prestigious Boston Globe Scholastic
Art Show, placement in the district and state band and chorus, breaking several records in track and field (school and meet
records), conversing with Sir Arthur Clarke via satellite in Sri Lanka from WPI and the achievement of a full athletic
scholarship to Boston University.
It has been a year of tragedy and triumph, and we look forward to the future with continued optimism, hope and
determination.

TURKEY HILL MIDDLE SCHOOL
Keith Hochstein, Principal
The 2001-2002 school year at Turkey Hill Middle School had a number of important highlights and significant accomplishments.
MCAS scores in all grade levels improved dramatically. Organizations and activities which challenged students of varied
abilities were initiated or expanded. The School Improvement Plan, designed by parents, teachers and administrators, continued
to serve as a vehicle to enhance the academic program and to meet the personal needs of students. The focus to enhance the
physical plant continued to make the school a more attractive environment and thus increase the pride our students have in their
school. Lastly our students engaged in a variety of altruistic activities which were designed to serve others and to teach
valuable lessons. We are very proud of the students of Turkey Hill Middle School.

Last year, for the first time, all students took one or more MCAS tests. Fifth graders took both science and history, however,
because it was a practice test, scores were not reported. Our sixth graders took the test in mathematics. The math test remains
the hardest of all the MCAS tests. That being said, 50% of our students scored in the advanced or proficient category and only
20% received a warning. The data with which we compare these results is the state performance because this was the first year
the sixth graders took the test. At the state level a total of 36% scored in the advanced or proficient category and 33% received a
warning. This puts us 14% higher in the top two categories and 13% lower in the warning category.
Our seventh graders took the English/Language Arts test, and this also was a first time test for them. 74% of our students
ranked in the advanced or proficient category compared to 55% at the state level. Only 2% of our students received a warning
compared to 12% of students across the state.
Our eighth graders also did quite well. In ELA, 78% of our students ranked in the advanced or proficient categories; this
compares to 67% at the state level. Only 3% of our students were in the warning category compared to 8% for the state. Our
total ELA average was up 4 points, and this represents our best score everl
In 8th grade math 46% of our students received an advanced or proficient score compared to 34% at the state level. 14% of our
students received a warning compared to 31% at the state level. Our average score was up 6 points, and this again represents
our highest score ever attained!
Our 8th grade history and social studies scores were comparable to the state scores. This test remains a problem for all schools
because the Department of Education has yet to agree on the Frameworks. Our average score was higher than previous years
but no one is happy with the results. Almost all public schools feel the same way, however the problem lies with the DOE's
inability to finalize their work.
Overall our students did very well. We improved in almost all categories, and we continue to Improve our average scores in
math, ELA and history/social studies. While we expect to do better than state scores, this year we were significantly ahead of
the state in most if not all areas. We view these tests as important, but they are only one barometer of our schools success. We
continue to teach reasoning and thinking skills, the ability to make good decisions, the ability to use knowledge in an ethical and
humane fashion and the desire to help other people both in Lunenburg and around the country.
Turkey Hill Middle School continues to Initiate programs and strategies to challenge students of all levels. This year students
have had the opportunity to write, illustrate and publish their own books. A school newspaper was published in the second half
of the year. Independent study projects are provided for students who express an interest in a specific topic. Field trips to
destinations of historical or cultural interest take place, and students do appropriate research around the site. Programs such
as Junior Great Books continue to provide challenge and enjoyment.
Activities outside the regular class continue to serve as a source of skill building for all students. Student Government provides
leadership opportunities for students. There are a myriad of tests and olympiads which are open to all students that provide
additional challenge. The robotics team continues to bring home first place trophies from competitions all over New England.
This year for the first time our yearbook was created and designed by students themselves again providing outlets for student
creativity and Initiative. Individual teachers or teams organize activities, field trips or speakers which serve as enriching
opportunities for students.
Through our School Improvement Plan the entire faculty engaged in activities which improved teaching strategies. Through
professional development workshops planned around differential instruction and curriculum mapping, teachers developed units
of instruction designed to challenge students of all levels. These professional development activities form the heart of our
program. Teachers became more aware of individual student ability and readiness and designed lessons to meet the needs of
all students.
Our school continued to explore avenues to improve the physical plant. Last year we began our cafeteria beautification project
which culminated last summer with the purchase of new curtains and valences for the stage. This effort as well as all the
painting was done at no cost to the town. The curtains were paid through student fundraising; the painting of the walls and
ceiling, the materials and the new wooden front to the stage and the design were all provided free of charge by generous citizens
of Lunenburg.
This year we are raising funds through dances and other activities in order to landscape the front courtyard. By placing flowers
and colorful shrubs we hope to make the school more attractive for our students.
Lastly, the students at Turkey Hill demonstrated the desire to extend themselves in order to help others. In a variety of ways

THOMAS C. PASSIOS ELMENTARY SCHOOL
Cynthia Daukantas, Principal
\The Thomas C. Passios Elementary School continues to strive toward educating the total child. We utilize a variety of
instructional methods and modalities in our approach to meet the individual needs of every child. We believe that the success of
our programs is dependent upon a healthy and productive partnership between home and school. Parents play an active role in
many of the daily programs and routines of the school.
This year continues to be a challenging and exciting year for the students, faculty, and staff of the Elementary and Primary
Schools. With a combined enrollment of seven hundred students, class sizes continue in the twenty to twenty-four range, with
the younger children benefiting from the smaller class sizes.
The summer of 2001 proved to be very beneficial to many children in Lunenburg. A number of academic programs were offered
to the children: a five week program for children with special needs in grades preschool through grade 4, and a five week
program for children in Grades 1-4 who were experiencing difficulty in the areas of reading and math. Funding for the Grades 1
and 2 programs was provided through School Linked Services; State MCAS Grant monies supported the Grade 3 and 4 program.
As the Elementary School continues to analyze our students' results on the Massachusetts Comprehensive Assessment System
(MCAS) and revise curricula and strategies, we continue to grow as an excellent educational facility. We are extremely proud of
the work the faculty continues to undertake each year. As teachers continue to reflect on their teaching practice, students
continue to benefit.
Our results on the State Grade 4 MCAS Tests Indicate that in the areas of both English/Language Arts and Math, the number of
Grade 4 students performing in the "failing" category continues to decline. Children in Grade 3 were administered their first
MCAS Test last spring in Reading. We are proud to say that no child failed the Grade 3 Reading Test. Individual Success Plans
were developed on those children who scored in the lower end of the 'needs improvement' category with the intent that
additional time/teaching would make a difference.
This year the Elementary School Improvement Plan focused on the areas of reading comprehension and writing across all
curricula areas. At this point in the year, the School Council is proud that we have accomplished many of our goals. This fall we
implemented a writing assessment schedule/practice in which all students in grades kindergarten through four were asked to
write on a given topic. Students' writing was evaluated using a rubric (scoring guide). All students were assessed again in
March and again in May/June. By implementing this program, teachers and parents will be able to document individual student
writing progress and re-teach as needed.
We are pleased to report that our Reading Assessment practice has also been re-evaluated and revised. Each child in grades 1-
4 will be evaluated twice each year in reading; in October and March. New individual reading folders have been implemented to
document student progress as well as strategies for improvement.

OFFICE OF INSTRUCTION
Loxi Jo Calmes, Director
During this past year the Office of Instruction continued to focus on the three areas critical to the District's mission: (a)
curriculum development (standards), (b) assessment and evaluation (measures), and (c) continuous improvement for ALL
students. Following is a summary of activities and results in each of these areas.
Standards
Revisions to Mathematics, Science & Technology, and Integrated Health curricula are at various points of completions as we
initiate the second generation of aligning local curricula to state standards. Teams of teachers with representatives from each
grade level reviewed the newly adopted state frameworks and were involved in the process of rewriting local curriculum. Before
this school year ends we will initiate a review of the English/Language Arts curriculum.
Annually, faculties in all the schools are involved in reviewing MCAS results for each curricula area. As a result of these
reviews at various levels of the organization (grade/department, school, and district) we continue to identify strengths and
weaknesses within our curriculum and instructional practices. We have implemented a local assessment system to annually
monitor student progress in mathematics.
We continue our commitment to a unified educational system for ALL students that values diversity and avoids pitting one group
against another. A review of MCAS results Indicates that like their non-disabled peers, students with disabilities in Lunenburg
perform above the state average in English/Language Arts. Performance in mathematics, however, is weak and we have identified a
series of professional development activities aimed at improving these results.

Strong commitment to providing Special Education Services

Strong communication between the administration and staff

BOARD OF ASSESSORS
The Board of Assessors and its staff are responsible for the valuation of all real estate and personal property subject to taxation.
Assessed valuations are based on "full and fair cash value" as of the January 1st preceding each fiscal year.
Our office requires a substantial amount of information in order to carry out its valuation mandate. Much of the office's effort
centers around maintaining ownership records, tax maps, and property characteristic data. This data is constantly changing.
Fiscal Year 2001 saw a significant increase in new building site approvals, home construction, and improvements to the existing
housing stock. The resulting increase in inspection activity, time spent in setting up new records, maintaining existing data, and
dealing with all of the political and economic implications brought on by growth kept the office unusually busy.
This year saw the office migrate to the new CSC-CAMA system. This windows based assessment program provides substantial
improvements over our old system. For example, property photos can now be captured digitally and electronically pasted onto
our assessment records. The new database upon which the program is based is much more flexible and allows for easier
querying and reporting. Another advantage of the new system is that it can be integrated with our Geographic Information
System (GIS).
In an effort to enhance the Town's Geographic Information System (GIS), the office went out to bid to have new aerial
photography and digital orthophotography prepared. The contract was awarded to Kucera International, Inc. of Willoughby,
Ohio. The digital orthography will serve as the foundation for a complete re-map of Lunenburg.
Our department welcomes Fred Liatsis to the Board of Assessors. He replaces outgoing board member, Ernie Sund who served
the office well over the past twelve years. We are pleased to report no staff changes the same crew of Norma Frost as
Administrative Assessor, Dorothy Musgrove as Principal Clerk, Janet Leavitt as inspector, and Harald Scheld as Assessor carry
out the Board's directives. Collectively they bring approximately 55 years of experience to the office.
Finally, it should be noted that in December of 2001, the Townsend Board of Assessors voted to pull out of the Tri-town
agreement with Ashby and Lunenburg. Having been a successful program initiated by outgoing Lunenburg Assessor Ernie
Sund in 1994, the Townsend Board of Assessors now believes that its Town's needs warrant hiring its own professional
assessor.
We thank the Town for its support of our office and the efforts we have made to insure that tax revenues flow to the Town in a
consistent and timely manner.
