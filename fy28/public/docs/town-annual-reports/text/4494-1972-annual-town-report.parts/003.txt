School Nursing Report
for 1972
E.NICBEL
The "Lunenburg Public Health Nursing Service" has two full-time Registered Nurses-Mrs. Doris Lahti and
Mrs. Gladys Barney, and one part-time Registered Nurse--Mrs. Shirley Carpenter, who works five hours a day
when schools are in session.
The above staff is hired by the Town to perform Visiting Nursing as well as School Nursing.
The Nashoba Associated Boards of Health offer the following programs in the school program--which require
signed permission from a parent with the exception of the Educational Dental Programs:
German Measles (Rubella) and Mumps Vaccine to certain grades
Grade 1 only - Trivalent Oral Polio Series or Polio Booster for those who have previously
completed oral series
Grades 2, 5, 8 - Dental Fluoride Program given by Dental Hygienist
Grades 1 & 4 - Educational Dental Programs by Dental Hygienist
Grade 1- Tine Testing
Grades 1 & 11- Diphtheria and Tetanus Booster and Small Pox re-vaccination
We are fortunate in being able to offer these programs to our students which keeps Lunenburg Schools immuni -
zation rate at a high level.
Dr. John DeLeo, School Physician, annually examines students in Grades 4, 7, and 10, with the exception
of those students who have returned a Physical Form completed and signed by their own doctor. Dr. DeLeo also
examines all athletes, who also have blood pressure taken and urine "tes-taped" for sugar.
Vision and Hearing Testing is done annually on all students, Kindergarten through Grade 12. Notices of
failures are mailed home to parents, with a doctor's form attached. This form must be returned to the Nurses' Office
after the child has been seen by an Eye or Ear doctor in order for the school to follow the doctor's recommendations.
In 1972, "The Future Nurses Club" at the High School met twice a month during activity period with their ad-
visor, Mrs. Gladys Barney. In an attempt to create interest in medical fields, speakers and films were used, A major
project undertaken by these girls was the making and delivering of May baskets for the Pearl Brook Housing Program.
Without the help of the Administration and School Personnel, the School Health Program could not be carried
out successfully. We thank you all.
Respectfully submitted,
DORIS M. LAHTI, R. N.
Supervising Nurse

School Lunch Program Report
for 1972
The 1971-72 school year has been a successful and changing one for
the School Lunch Program.
In the period of 167 lunch days, we served 226,029 Type "A"
lunches to students and 11,088 Type "A" lunches to adults. We purchased
277,601 one-half pints of milk which includes the special milk sales.
We now employ twenty-three full-time cafeteria workers with a
cook-manager and a baker, along with general workers in each school.
We purchased very little. bread last year as our bakers do a great job on dinner rolls, hamburg and hot dog
rolls, and french bread, as well as cakes, cookies and other fine desserts.
Early in the 1971-72 school year we started planning for the Satellite Hot-Lunch Program for the Annex. We
experimented during the Thanksgiving and Christmas lunches as well as a few other times. This program, to the de-
light of most of the third graders, started on a daily basis on April 24, 1972. The lunches are cooked in the Turkey
Hill Middle School kitchen and then are packaged individually and put into insulated metal containers in order to
keep them hot or cold. Two ladies from the cafeteria travel to the Annex with the lunches and serve them to the
children.
In another development, rising food and labor costs as well as fewer government-supplied commodities, made
it difficult for the program to meet its financial obligations and therefore, permission was requested from the State
Bureau of Food Services to raise the price of lunches from 30¢ to 35¢ for students and 50¢ to 55¢ for adults. With
the granting of this permission, our new prices went into effect on April 3, 1972. The 5¢ raise was a big factor in
keeping our program on a self-paying basis.
The cook-managers from the schools were given the
opportunity the last week of June to attend the Summer Con -
ference at the University of Massachusetts sponsored by the
State School Lunch Office. Mrs. Nass from the Elementary
School and Mrs. Haueisen from the Middle School took advan-
fage of this worthwhile opportunity.
In closing, I wish to thank Dr. Holland, our Superinten-
dent, Mr. Goodrich, Administrative Assistant, Mrs. Ginny
Welch and office staff, Town Personnel, Custodians, and
School Lunch Personnel, for their help in keeping our program
a success.
E.N.
Respectfully submitted,
(Mrs.) LUCY W. CHARTRAND
School Lunch Supervisor
