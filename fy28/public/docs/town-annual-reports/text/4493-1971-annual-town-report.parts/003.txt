JOHN F. HAYNES
1957 - 1971
John became a member of the Custodial Staff
on September 3rd, 1957, when he was hired as Night
Custodian with duties divided between the Jr.-Sr.
High School and the Elementary School. On April Ist,
1961, he was transferred to serve as the Day Custodian
at the Elementary Annex and remained at this school
until the last six months of his employment when he
was transferred to the Turkey Hill Middle School.
From September Ist, 1967 to June 30, 1970, he
served as the School Attendance Officer and was com-
mended for his efforts in performing this duty.
Because of ill health, he found it necessary to
retire effective July 8th. In accepting his retirement
notice, the School Committee expressed appreciation
for the contributions he had made to our schools over
his fourteen years of service. A pleasant and happy
retirement is wished to him by all of US.

MRS. ELIZABETH J. GOULD
1958 - 1966
We were all saddened by the news that "Betty" Gould,
a former teacher and Department Chairman in the Lunen-
burg Jr.-Sr. High School, had passed away after a long
illness on October 13th in Manteca, California. Follow -
ing her graduation from Fitchburg State College in June,
1958, Mrs. Gould began teaching in Lunenburg the follow-
ing September as a teacher of Senior High Social Studies
and English. In September, 1961, she was appointed as
Social Studies Department Chairman and held this position
until June, 1966, when she and her family moved near
Washington, D. C.
Betty Gould was a dedicated teacher and the Class of
1961 upon the occasion of dedicating the 1961 Supplement
of the school yearbook to her, summed up the tremendous
love and affection they had for her:
"This individual has, in our opinion, given not only
her time and patience to the Senior Class, but also herself.
Guiding US through our many activities, during this last
year, she has left with US a feeling of great appreciation
and lasting gratitude. Because of her complete faith and
trust in the Class of 1961, we dedicate this the first sup-
plement "Mother" of the Turkey Hills Echo to our very special
Mrs. Elizabeth J. Gould."

School Nursing Report
for 1971
The Lunenburg Board of Health employs two full-time registered
nurses, Mrs. Doris M. Lahti and Mrs. Gladys V. Barney, and one part-time
registered nurse, Mrs. Shirley M. Carpenter, who works five hours a day only
when schools are in session. The above nurses provide the Town's Visiting
Nursing Service, as well as School Nursing.
A total of 112 visits were made to school children for Physical Defects, Absence from School, and follow-up
on Vision and Hearing Failures. As much as possible, the nurses telephone parents to discuss any problems.
Dr. John DeLeo, School Physician, annually examines students in Grades 4 - 7 - 10, with the exception of
those students who have returned a Physical Form completed and signed by their own doctor. Dr. DeLeo also
examines all school athletes, who also have their blood pressure taken and urine "tes-taped" for sugar.
In March, 1972, we will again offer the German Measles and Mumps Vaccine to certain grades. These
programs and the ones listed below are given through the cooperation of the Nashoba Associated Boards of Health.
Grade 1 only -
Trivalent Oral Polio Series or Polio
Booster for those who have previously
completed the oral series.
Grades 2, 5, 8 -
Dental Fluoride Program givenby
Dental Hygienist.
Grades 1 & 9 -
Tuberculosis Tine Testing with follow-
up on positive reactors.
Grades I & II -
Diphtheria and Tetanus Booster and
Re-vaccination Program.
These programs, which are well accepted, all require permission slips signed by a parent.
Vision and Hearing Testing is done annually on all students in Kindergarten through Grade 12. Notices of
test failures are mailed to parents with a doctor's form attached. This form contains important information on your
child and should be returned to the Nurses' Office after the Ear or Eye docdor has seen the child.
Mrs. Barney attended a series of four all-day meetings on the Handicapped Child in the fall, and Mrs.
Lahti also attended several meetings regarding School Health.
We would like to personally thank the Lions Club for the Massachusetts Vision Tester their organization
purchased when the Middle School opened and also the Kindergarten Slides for the Vision Machine and some other
valuable vision-testing equipment which the Lions purchased for US this year.
The Nurses wish to thank the Superintendent of Schools, the Principals, and all other School Personnel for
their help in making the School Health Program so successful.
Respectfully submitted,
DORIS M. LAHTI, R. N.

School Lunch Program Report
FED
for 1971
My Third Annual Report of the School Lunch Program, covering the
school year September 1970 to September 1971, shows that we served 204, 28I
paid "Type A" Lunches, 17,077 free lunches and 11,066 lunches to adults.
We also sold 36,717 half-pints of milk to students under the Special
Milk Program and gave 6,743 as well as selling 6,698 half-pints to adults. In
all, considering milk given workers, a total of 282,582 half-pints of milk were
purchased and consumed in the 1970-71 school year.
Commodities, bought and distributed by the United States Department of Agriculture, were not as abundant as
in previous years with the total valuation of commodities received in the Lunenburg Schools being $17,868.40.
During the school year we had four teacher released-
time days. On these days, hot lunches to students were not
served; however, the cafeteria personnel took furns serving
a $1.00 buffet luncheon to attending teachers.
We are working on a satellite hot lunch program for the
Elementary Annex. We have served several hot lunches at the
Annex which have been enthusiastically received by both the
third grade students and teachers. At this time, I would like
to thank Mr. Finneron, third grade teachers and the cafeteria
personnel at the Turkey Hill Middle School who helped make
our pilot efforts a real success.
At the Jr.-Sr. High School, the cafeteria personnel
experimented with choice menus which were found to be
quite popular. We also had ala-carte items available on many
days.
Hot Lunch participation this year was 68% in the Jr.-
Sr. High School, 77% in the Turkey Hill Middle School, and
66% in the Thomas Passios Elementary School.
In the one hundred and sixty-nine (169) lunch days we averaged 1,405 "Type A" Lunches per day for all
schools.
In closing, I want to express my thanks to Dr. Holland, our Superintendent of Schools; Mr. Goodrich, our
Administrative Assistant; the School Committee; the Principals and Assistant Principals; Town Personnel; Custodians
and all the School Lunch Personnel, who have cooperated with me to make our School Lunch Program successful.
Respectfully submitted,
LUCY W. CHARTRAND
School Lunch Supervisor
