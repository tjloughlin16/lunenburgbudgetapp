MEMORIAM
EVELYN M. COLVIN
1909 -2004
Evelyn worked for the Town of Lunenburg for forty years, the last ten
without retirement withholding because she was well past retirement age.
Talk about Dedication. In her spare time she loved to refinish furniture and
braid rugs. Her favorite place to vacation was at "Hoppies" on Cape Cod.
She died at 95 years old after a "short illness". This is typical Evelyn
fashion. She prided herself on her good health despite the daily hot dog
lunch and smoking!!! One of her favorite expressions was "only the good die
young and I'm gonna live forever". Maybe not forever, but she really did
have a good run of it!! She will be missed.
MARJORIE STILLMAN
1915 - 2004
Marjorie Stillman lived on the Stillman farm since she married in 1941.
Mrs. Stillman formerly was the director of the Lunenburg School Lunch
Program and served on the Lunenburg School Committee for many years.
She was an active 4-H leader, a skilled seamstress and an avid gardener
whose perennial borders are 60 years old.
IRENE P. PROCTOR
1901 - 2004
Irene Proctor at the age of 103 was a lifetime Lunenburg homeowner. She
graduated from Lunenburg High School and attended Williams College. She
and her husband owned and operated a dairy farm in town for many years. A
portion of their land known as "Proctor Park", which contained
approximately 11 acres on Elmwood Road, was given to the Town to be used
for recreational purposes. As one of the Town's oldest residents, Irene was
the recipient of The Boston Post Cane. Mrs. Proctor was a member of the
Lunenburg Women's Club, the Historical Society, a Red Cross Volunteer and
she loved to travel.

BOARD OF SELECTMEN / SEWER COMMISSIONERS
The search for CAFO continued with Chariman Joseph Bilotta and Vice-Chair Steven deBettencourt serving as Board
representatives to the CAFO Screening Committee. After their latest search failed to yield any candidates meeting
the technical qualifications of the Charter, Daniel Cronin replaced Joseph Bilotta's vacated seat on the Screening
Committee with Steven deBettencourt. As a result of the Town resuming its search to fill the vacancy in accordance
with the Town Charter, the Screening Committee decided to obtain the services of a consultant. Bennett Yarger
Associates, Scituate, MA was chosen for the task of assisting the Committee in this procedure. During this process
Laura Williams was appointed Acting CAFO and worked mutually with Town Accountant Karen Brochu in continuing
to fulfill the additional responsibilities of this position.
The Phase 1, Contract II and Electric Avenue sewer installation projects continued throughout the year and as the
trenches and laterals must settle throughout one freezing season the final paving is expected to be completed by the
end of 2005. Upon completion of the final paving and repairs to personal properties the Board will be assessing the
final actual betterments to all property owners along the sewered routes. We realize that these projects have caused
quite a navigational challenge to residents and business owners and the Board has been sympathetic to the Towns
plight and will continue to work with the Contractors and property owners to accomplish the final completion of the
sewer phase with the least impact as possible.
Representatives from the Board are also serving on the Building Committees for the Public Safety Complex, the
Primary School, and the Library. The Public Safety Facility was finished in December and the department's
relocation to the new facility was accomplished by the end of 2004. Construction continued on both the Primary
School and Library and completion for these two buildings is expected to be the fall of 2005. The former Fire/Police
station located at 7 School St., and the Police Annex at 950 Mass Ave., are currently being utilized by the DPW
department for storage of various grounds equipment. The future use of both of these buildings as well as the
Library and old Primary School building have been the focus of numerous ad-hoc committees which have been
charged with identifying future needs of town government. Reports have been submitted to the Board regarding
future space and parking needs. Also included in these reports are the current conditions and sizes of all the
buildings anticipated to be vacated. This information is being utilized by the most recently formed ad-hoc Town
Center Planning Committee to determine the best future use of all these structures and the property on which they
stand. They will be reporting back to the town sometime during fiscal year 2005.
The Board extends a special "thank you" again this year to the Lunenburg / Townsend Rotary Club for decorating the
Gazebo at Christmas and the Turkey Hill Gardeners for the seasonal Town Hall decorations and flowers in the upper
common. We would also acknowledge the generous donations that were received and utilized so that the Town
could continue with the traditional Band Concerts on the Common and any donations to this cause would be greatly
appreciated.
Your Board has enjoyed working in service to the Town, working with each other and with you the citizens. The
Selectmen would also like to acknowledge all of the individuals who work so hard for the Town and those who
volunteer their time and commitment by serving on the various Boards, Commissions and Committees within the
Town.
Please feel free to direct any questions to our office staff Laura Williams or Linda Douglas @978-582-4144. If they
cannot assist you, they will forward your call to the appropriate authorities. Our office hours are Monday,
Wednesday and Thursday 8:00 AM to 4:00 PM, Tuesday 8:00 AM to 6:30 PM and Friday closed.
As of this printing, the Board wishes to recognize the passing of our long term Chairman, Joseph F. Bilotta who
unexpectedly passed away on February 7, 2005. He will be truly missed.

ANIMAL CONTROL OFFICER
Kathleen Comeau, Animal Control Officer
2004 has been a very busy and we have had many injured and lost dogs. Again, as in the past year, people have NOT licensed
their dogs. Not licensing the dogs means that if hit by a vehicle and severely injured the owner doesn't know or is not able to
make medical decisions for their pet. If a stray is picked up they do not get home and are only held by law for 10 days. I will be
issued a list of unlicensed dogs from the Town Clerk's office and will be issuing fines. If these fines are not paid I will be
bringing these people to Court.
Licensing your dog is so easy and so important to the animal's life and safety. A special "thank you" to those people who are
faithful licensing their dogs every year!
BUILDING DEPARTMENT / ZONING OFFICER
The Building Inspectors office is located on the second floor of the Town Hall. Office hours for the Building Department are
Monday & Thursday 8:00 AM until 3:30 PM, Tuesdays 8:00 AM until 6:30 PM, Wednesday 8:00 AM until 6:00 PM and Fridays 8:00
PM to 12:30 PM by appointment. The Inspector is available in the office daily and inspections are by appointment. Michael J.
Sauvageau is the Building Commissioner and Zoning Official for the Town of Lunenburg. We would like to extend a warm
welcome to our new Assistant Building Inspector James Maillet. Jim is a local contractor from Westminster and joined the
Building Department in July 2004. Lisa Normandin serves as the Administrative Assistant to the Building Department
Building activity in Lunenburg has been steady in the residential districts. The Building Department issued 384 permits in the
year 2004. There were 51 new homes constructed, also several garages, barns, additions and renovations, and 25 woodstove
permits. Permit fees in 2004 totaled $109,850.00. Currently the Town is In the process of reviewing a Comprehensive Permit
(40B) application for Lunenburg Estates, 1229 Massachusetts Avenue, Lunenburg, MA. This is a 10.4-acre parcel and 64
condominium units are being proposed. Sixteen of the units are deemed to be affordable. In addition to the 40B project, an over
55 residential condo area is proposed near 792 Massachusetts Avenue (behind the former Stone Farm package store). This
project will contain 60 units and is being proposed by JCJ Inc., West Boylston, MA. and the final approvals are expected in early
2005.
The Building Department is responsible for re-inspecting all buildings of assembly each year. This is to insure that all exit signs;
fire extinguishers and safety mechanisms are working properly. The buildings Include the schools, churches, restaurants and
any other such buildings where people assemble. The new library is underway and ahead of schedule. The primary school is
under construction and moving along as well. The public safety building is now complete and occupied. The newly installed
sewer line is in place and will increase development in Town. Many developers have inquired about large parcels in the areas of
the sewer project.
Residents should be aware that Massachusetts Law requires that all persons, partnerships and corporations who bid or perform
residential contracting (reconstruction, alterations, additions etc.) for work exceeding $1000.00 be registered with the
Commonwealth. This office will enforce this provision, however you should ask your contractor about his/her registration.
Please contact this office with any questions about this requirement or call for any other information you may need on Zoning
requirements or Building Code issues.
CABLE ADVISORY COMMITTEE
Lance D. May, Chairman
The Lunenburg Cable Advisory Committee was established in 1971 as an advisory committee to the Board of Selectmen on
matters relating to the establishment and enforcement of a negotiated cable agreement between the cable provider, this
committee and accepted by the Board of Selectmen, acting in their capacity as "the Licensing Authority for the Town". As the
current cable license expires in May 2007, the Cable Advisory Committee has started preliminary discussions towards the
eventual renewal of a cable license agreement for the Town of Lunenburg. For the remainder of 2005, the Committee is working
towards settling the groundwork with a public hearing sometime this fall and a survey to follow, as our concern is to provide to
the Lunenburg subscribers the opportunity to voice their concerns and ask questions. Additionally, the "Leap" Committee
(Lunenburg Public Access) is an arm of the Cable Advisory Committee and currently broadcasts the Selectmen's Meetings on
Tuesday night, (repeats on Thursday nights) and the School Committee meetings once a month from the Town Hall on the first
Thursday of the month.
I would like to thank the following members for their contributions to this Committee: Edward Dextraze, Derek Dufresne, Jim
Parsons and Jay Schwartz.

CENTRAL MASS MOSQUITO CONTROL
The Central Massachusetts Mosquito Control Project (the project) currently provides its services to 36 cities and towns
throughout Middlesex and Worcester Counties. The Project's headquarters is located at 111 Otis Street, Northboro MA. Tours of
the headquarters or visits to field work sites may be arranged by calling the office in advance. Please call (508) 393-3055 during
business hours for more information. The Project practices Integrated Mosquito Management (IMM), blending state of the art
methods and techniques with expertise, experience and scientific research to provide our member communities with
environmentally sound and cost effective mosquito control.
During 2004 the Project received six thousand, five hundred and seventy-four (6,574) requests for service from town residents
and officials. A total of over six thousand (6,000) pounds of Bti (Bacillus thuringiensis israelensis) was applied by helicopter in
two towns, Chelmsford and Billerica, and five thousand five hundred and eighty-seven (5,587) pounds by hand throughout our
service area were applied to area wetlands to reduce the emergence of adult mosquitoes. This represents over two thousand,
three hundred and seventeen (2,317) acres of wetland that was treated with this mosquito-specific bacterium, significantly
reducing adult mosquito populations in these areas. Thirty thousand, seven hundred and sixty-one (30,761) catch basins were
treated with larvicidal product to control the mosquitoes that seek out these cool dark wet areas to breed, including the Culex
mosquito, a major target for West Nile virus transmission. Five thousand and nine (5,009) culverts were cleaned in an attempt to
eliminate unnecessary standing water and reduce mosquito breeding. This work was done in conjunction with cleaning, clearing
and digging of one hundred and thirty four thousand, nine hundred and fifty-one (134,951) feet of streams, brook and ditches.
12
===PAGE 16===
This represents almost twenty-five and one-half (25-1/2) miles of waterways which were cleaned and improved by project
personnel in 2004. Three ponds were reclaimed in 2004 resulting in seven thousand sixty-four (7,064) square feet of pond area
restored.
The Mosquito Awareness Program which we offer to elementary schools and other civic organizations in our district has become
very popular. Project staff meets with students, teachers or concerned residents to discuss mosquito biology, mosquito habitat,
and control procedures. Much of the presentation is directed toward what children and their families can do to prevent
mosquitoes from breeding around their homes. Slides, videos, coloring books and other handouts make this an interesting
program. This program is tailored to meet the needs of the specific audience. One thousand, eight hundred and forty-four
(1,844) students attended these programs.
As part of our effort to reduce the need for pesticides we continue to expand our wetlands restoration program. By cleaning
clogged and overgrown waterways, mosquito breeding can be reduced and drainage areas are restored to historic conditions.
Bti mosquito larvicide is used to treat areas where mosquito larvae are found. We routinely check known breeding sites kept in
our data base, but also encourage the public to notify us of any areas they suspect could breed mosquitoes. Our field crews
will investigate all such requests and treat the area only if surveillance gathered at the time shows an imminent threat of
mosquito emergence.
Our goal is to manage all mosquito problems with education, wetlands restoration or larviciding, but we recognize that there are
times when adult mosquito spraying is the only viable solution. In such cases specific areas are treated with either hand-held or
pickup truck mounted sprayers if surveillance gathered at the time exceeds a pre-determined threshold to warrant an
application. This program is offered on a request-only basis, and the exclusion process allows residents and/or town officials to
exclude areas under their control from this or any part of our program.
The Project's surveillance program monitors adult mosquito and larval populations density, and is the backbone for prescribing
various control techniques. Specialized mosquito traps are deployed throughout the Project's service area to sample for
mosquitoes that may be transmitting mosquito-borne diseases. In conjunction with the Mass. Dept. of Public Health we sample
in areas suspected of harboring WNV and other viruses. One thousand thirty (1,030) pools (collections) of mosquitoes totaling
eight thousand, two hundred and eighty (8,280) specimens were tested for mosquito-borne viruses this year, and two (2) pools of
Cs. Melanura, one each in Billerica and Wilmington, were confirmed to be infected with the EEE virus late in the season. Two (2)
horses in these towns were confirmed with EEE but no human cases were reported. No West Nile Virus was reported in birds,
mosquitoes, horses or humans in 2004.
Educational pamphlets are available to anyone interested in learning about mosquito control and services provided by the
Project, and these items are routinely stocks in Town/City Hall and libraries. Display boards with information on our program
are rotated through area Town Halls throughout the year. We also have a website, www.cmmcp.org that has extensive
information on mosquito biology, our control procedures, etc. This website has become a model for other Mosquito Projects
and has been widely used throughout our service area and beyond.
We would like to thank you for your support during 2004 and we look forward to helping you and your community with its
mosquito problems in 2005 and beyond.
COUNCIL ON AGING
Under Massachusetts General Law, Chapter 40, Section 8B, Councils on Aging were established to Include setting of local policy
for the administration of elder programs or services and developing, coordinating and conducting such activities which
advocate for elder issues. This would also include educating the community at large concerning the needs and the resources
available to elders. "It is the mission of the Lunenburg Council on Aging to advocate for improved quality of life for all seniors
through supportive services, programs and education".
The Eagle House Senior Center is a gathering place for persons sixty years of age or older. Services and programs, both at the
center and in elder's homes, encourage elders to maintain healthy lifestyles. Our senior population have raised their families and
lived their lives in this community. By providing resources to help them remain in this community we add to our towns diversity
and development. In addition to center's social activities, fitness/ health programs, and educational opportunities we promote
volunteerism and Intergenerational collaboration. Outside the realm of the center activities, we provide Information and referral
services, a home delivered meal program, transportation, outreach home visits, and "elder at risk" reporting to proper agencies.
13
===PAGE 17===
In FY'04 Outreach serviced 471 individuals with 2192 units of assistance. The Outreach worker establishes in-home visits and is
responsible for intakes, referral with a follow up, and oversees the Friendly Visitor Program, which provides companionship to
elders living alone. If you or someone you know needs a home visit, please call our Outreach worker at the senior center.
A trained and certified SHINE (Serving Health Information Needs of Elders) Counselors provides free assistance to elders with
health insurance inquiries, food stamps, or social security and pension questions. Many changes are expected and have already
occurred in the Medicare system. The SHINE counselor can help people navigate through this complicated and often difficult to
understand system.
The second annual Pancake Breakfast was held in conjunction with the American Legion Post 283 Memorial Day services in May.
It is heart warming to see citizens of all ages coming out to honor our veterans. We also conduct a fall program where our High
School students interview persons who lived during or served in World War II.
Our monthly newsletter provides up to date information for elders. The Friends of the Eagle House Inc provide funds to mail the
center newsletter three times per year. A subscription for the additional eight months mailing is available for $3.00 per year.
Monthly health screenings and other clinics such as flu immunization are conducted through Nashoba Nursing and Hospice.
Nutritious meals are served daily both at the center and delivered by volunteers to elder's homes under the Meals on Wheels
Program. The center participates in the Elder Brown Bag food distribution program to eligible residents. Fitness classes such
as line dancing, senior aerobics, chair aerobics, and a walking club are offered. Annual Tax Assistance is also provided. Other
educational and social programs are presented throughout the year, many sponsored by the Friends of the Eagle House
Incorporated. A handicap accessible van is available Monday through Friday to residents 60 years or older providing
transportation to shopping, medical appointments, part time jobs, visits to nursing homes and of course to all activities at the
Eagle House. Non-elder residents can apply if they are "transportation disabled". The Council on Aging also arranges medical
transports to Boston and Worcester facilities. The senior center remains a focal point in the community for the coordination of
all elder services. We work in conjunction with Central Mass Agency on Aging, Montachusett Home Care Corporation,
Montachusett Opportunity Council, Montachusett Area Regional Transit Authority, Nashoba Nursing and Hospice Service, Mass
Councils on Aging, and the Executive Office of Elder Affairs.
In our FY'04 annual report to the state Executive Office of Elder Affairs we provided 25,093 documented units of service to 1182
elders, of these 17390 units were for wellness and support services. In addition 1491 units of service were provided to non-
elders. Our volunteers contributed 3408 hours of service to the Town of Lunenburg. In order to maintain our operation, we
depend on our volunteers and other In-Kind donations. In-Kind services represent tangible goods or services not paid for from
the Town budget. These include individual donations of items, and any type of assistance from the Friends of the Eagle House
or others. The estimated value of the FY'04 In-Kind was $27,370.00. Monetary donations to assist the Eagle House should be
made to: Friends of the Eagle House Inc., 25 Memorial Drive, Lunenburg, MA 01462.
The Director chairs the Senior Citizen Property Tax Work-Off Committee established at the May 1999 Town Meeting which finds
eligible senior citizens for job placements requested by town departments. Seniors who are eligible for this program can receive
up to a $500.00 abatement on their property taxes.

DEPARTMENT OF PUBLIC WORKS
John M. Rodriquenz, DPW Director
Public Works combines the Highway, Park, Cemetery, Sewer, and Facilities divisions into one department sharing and
organizing personnel and equipment to serve the residents of our town more economically and efficiently. Due to a reduction in
funding there has been a distinct transition from a preventative maintenance approach to one that is more reactionary. Nowhere
is this more apparent than in the road-resurfacing program.
Chapter 90 state funding has been reduced by one-third throughout the Commonwealth. Locally this allocation continues to be
directed toward roadways within the sewer program in order to assure a satisfactory repaving of disturbed surfaces. The
rehabilitation of streets outside the scope of the sewer project has been virtually eliminated. The town is also faced with state

burden on public works personnel and will increase budget amounts particularly as engineering consultants are required. The
vehicle maintenance facility, a hazardous waste management program, and a town wide phase II separate storm system all
require identification, mapping, permitting, and a continued plan for improvements and increased public awareness.
Nevertheless the town has experienced tremendous gains in infrastructure with the completion of Contract II Phase I sewer
installation and the complete rehabilitation of Rte. 13 funded through a Public Works Economic Development grant.

HOUSING AUTHORITY
The Lunenburg Housing Authority was founded in 1968, and is charter to oversee, manage and develop state-aided housing with
the Town. Within this charter, the Authority, in participation with other community leaders, also has responsibility to assure
adequate sources of affordable private housing for Lunenburg. The Authority currently manages 48 subsidized apartments
under Chapter 667, known as Pearl Brook Housing for the Elderly. Our family housing is adjacent to the Pearl Brook
Apartments and consists of six townhouse style apartments consisting of two and three bedroom units.
Applications for state-aided housing may be obtained at 131 White Street, Lunenburg or by calling 978-345-7655 or 978-537-5300
(in Leominster). To be eligible for elderly housing, applicants must be at least 60 years old at the time of application. All other
applicants, including handicapped and disabled individuals must be of legal age to enter into a lease. The Authority will grant a
preference for Lunenburg residents. Veterans that qualify will also receive a preference. Rent is based on your household's
annual adjusted gross income. Elderly residents will pay 30% of their adjusted income, while residents of our family housing will
pay 27% of their adjusted income and all utilities (excluding water and sewer).
In 2004, the Leominster Housing Authority continued their successful partnership with the Lunenburg Housing Authority,
offering administrative services to both Lunenburg's family and elderly conventional housing. The Lunenburg Housing
Authority believes that the management of contracting services with other agencies, in terms of quality management services, is
a valuable tool for success. This cooperative effort has allowed the Lunenburg Housing Authority to offer greater services and
assistance to our clients while maintaining our strong leadership and involvement in the community.
The Authority made a number of improvements to the Pearl Brook development including an upgrade to the community room
building and installation of a new septic system.
The Authority has maintained its membership in the Massachusetts Chapter of the National Association of Housing and
Redevelopment Officials (NAHRO). Participation in NAHRO helps assure that the Authority is up-to-date with programs and
funding sources available for use within Lunenburg.

The Planning Board foresees continuing growth due to sewering of the community, changing demographics of the area and the
State mandate of affordable housing. The Board recognized "Smart Growth" as part of the answer to managed growth. With the
passing of the new State law, MGL Ch 40R, the Board determined that process and procedures can assist in balancing
development and environmental protection. Smart Growth policies include redeveloping, concentrating development, restoring
and enhancing the environment, conserving natural resources, expanding housing opportunities, providing transportation
choices and regional planning. Not all the key points are meaningful for Lunenburg, but the majority of Smart Growth policies
will assist in managing the growth of the community while protecting its amenities and also provide choices of locations and
financial incentives. The Board, after attending seminars and working with consultants, presented a summary of the 40R
benefits through its regulations at the Public Forum. The Board, working with the Board of Selectmen, will submit to the State
for certification and submit to Town Meeting a bylaw for Smart Growth. The benefits of 40R over 40B are that 40Rs present a
viable alternative to the 40B mandates because it allows Town input by the right to design overlay districts and place
development in strategic areas; 40Rs also assist in providing countable housing stock and provide financial incentives. In
addition, and presented at the Public Forum, was the Planning Board's study of Impact Fees and Mitigation Measures. Since
Impact Fees are illegal in the State of Massachusetts and mitigation measures are directed to the effect of individual
development projects on the areas of the community, the Board has written a policy and procedure guideline for mitigation by
developers. Some of the items and issues that can be mitigated are road improvements, safety devices, public safety and public
works equipment and other capital improvements if related to the effect of the development.

from our Reserve staff and will be graduating from the Police Academy in March of 2005.
Low staffing levels continue to be an Issue for our Police Department. The number of full-time officers is significantly lower than
recommended levels for a community our size. As our community grows, as well as the communities around us, so do the
number of calls for service the officers respond to. In FY 04 the Police Department logged 12,630 incidents, an increase of 1,511
from FY03. Notable increases were seen in the number of 911 calls for service, residential and commercial alarm responses,
breaks into motor vehicles, motor vehicle accidents, vandalism and suspicious activity complaints. Decreases were noted in
areas such as housebreaks, trespassing complaints and some larcenles.
For the past five years I have been requested to produce and operate within a total operating budget that has either been
decreased, level funded or level serviced with increases dictated only by contractual agreements. Significant cuts have been
made to overtime, which includes training, court time and speed enforcement monles. The time has come to reinvest in our
Police Department's operating budget In order to provide not only a proper reactive emergency service but also a proactive
response to the needs of our community and the officers who serve.
Our department continues to be successful in obtaining Grant Funding to supplement our budget. State and Federally funded
grant monies were obtained to continue initiatives such as the "Click it or Ticket" and "You drink, You drive, You loose"
campaigns in traffic enforcement. These initiatives provide funding for extra patrols to enforce seat belt use, speed enforcement
and drunk driving laws during peak travel times and holidays. A grant from the Governor's Highway Safety Counsel will provide

SPECIAL SERVICES
During the 2003-2004 school year, the Montachusett Regional Vocational Technical School provided special education support
services for over four hundred students. In September 2004, the Special Education Department is supporting two hundred and
thirty-one identified eligible students who are receiving special education instruction in academic and vocational education. Our
mission is to comply with Public
Law 105-17, the 1997 amendments to the Individuals with Disabilities Education Act (known as "IDEA-97") and Chapter 766 of the
Acts of 1972, MGL c.71B (603 CMR 28.00) to provide a free appropriate public education (FAPE) to students with disabilities in
the least restrictive environment (LRE).
One of the major goals of the Individualized Education Plan for each student is to ensure successful completion of all graduation
requirements leading to a diploma and successful transitioning to post high school life.
The student support services at Montachusett Regional are available to all students. We have a full-time school nurse (R.N.) with
a part-time assistant (CNN) who administers prescribed medications, performs state mandated health screening tests, and
provides, when necessary, health information to the student's IEP team meeting. We have a full-time school social worker who
is able to participate on teams and assist students who have needs concerning finances, family issues, living space, maternity
issues, health issues, and food and clothing. Our students have access to the services of a full-time adjustment counselor and a
part-time school psychologist. Both of these people are available for scheduled counseling sessions and mental health
emergency treatment or crisis intervention.

students in grades 9-12. Our speech therapist is contracted to service approximately fifty students per week.
The Special Education Program at Montachusett Regional is reviewed every May in accordance with regulatory requirements.
The results of this evaluation are used to improve the special education procedures and programs at Monty Tech. This past

TECHNOLOGY
Monty Tech continues to provide high quality technology resources to our staff and students. Thanks to the continued support
of the School Committee, we currently maintain a student to computer ratio of just over 2 to 1, exceeding the state benchmark of
3 to 1. The majority of our computers are Category 1, meaning that they have power performance and interfacing features
consistent with current industry standards. Unfortunately, the recent financial climate in the state has forced us, for the first
time, to cut back in technology allotments. The results are starting to be felt with a significantly higher percentage of computers
falling into Category 2.
All of our classrooms and computer systems are fully networked, again exceeding the state average by a wide margin. This
connectivity allows our instructors to freely integrate the use of electronic resources into their curriculum. This has been
especially important, as we strive to improve our MCAS results.
We have expanded our wireless networking capacity S0 that approximately one-third of the building is now wireless accessible.
The wireless laptop mobile labs continue to be not only very popular but a very effective way to deliver electronic resources.
Expanding this capability remains a high priority.
Shops and programs which are heavily computer oriented, such as Graphic Communications, Information Technology, Drafting
Technology and Office Technology, and most recently the revised Health Occupations Program continues to use the most recent
versions of applicable software providing our students, in many cases, with exposure to systems that are more current than is
being used by prospective employers.
Our TV studio broadcasts morning announcements each day, with a student crew. This area also produces a variety of
multimedia resources for the rest of the schools, including digital video editing and DVD production. Communication with staff
continues to migrate toward all electronic. Voice and e-mail for all staff and a web-based daily bulletin have been in effect for
some time. We have started a conversion of school forms to interactive and web-based. Teachers now have access, from home,
to the Student Information System, and other school resources. The Information Technology run Help Desk provides a first-rate
support system for troubleshooting, repair and maintenance of all aspects of the technology network at Monty Tech.

Update on our Strategic Plan 2003-2006:
Goals and Objectives
1. To foster the talents, skills, and potential of all students
1.1 To expose all students to a variety of opportunities to explore individual interests and talents.
Progress: On-Going. A survey was developed by the District Improvement Plan Committee in order to better articulate
in measurable terms what we want to accomplish.
1.2 To develop a process and structure for implementing transition plans for each student.
Progress: Completed for the transition years between the buildings.
2. To ensure that staff and programs are conducive to instructional excellence.
2.1 To develop and implement a process of data driven decision-making.
Progress: Complete. Our Results Teams meet at least monthly to review student performance data and set targets for
improvement. These Teams utilize MCAS results as well as local and classroom assessments. Additionally, School and District
Improvement Plans, as well as the budget, are developed based upon data and need. As part of our annual No Child Left
Behind Consolidated Grant, we review data in five areas: 1. Student achievement in math, reading and English/Language arts; 2.
Student achievement for students with limited English proficiency; 3. Highly qualified teachers and paraprofessionals and high
quality professional development; 4. Safe and drug free schools; 5, High school graduation rate.
2.2 To create a professional development program based on data and need.
Progress: Complete. The Lunenburg Public Schools uses the above process as the vehicle to gather data and then
plan professional development based on needs. The data collection process provides the administrative team with
substantial information to plan for the professional development needs of the district. Professional development needs
are also based on needs identified by teachers and become part of their individual professional development plans. All
the collected data is used to develop a comprehensive professional development plan. The Instructional Services
Director in collaboration with the administrative team develops a yearlong plan and calendar of offerings. A
combination of graduate level courses, professional development days, study groups, task forces and individual and
team projects are offered to meet the varying needs of staff. In addition, the building administrators and special
services coordinator use the data and professional development plan to define faculty and department meetings during
the school year. The goal of the professional development program is to provide high-quality, sustained, job-embedded
professional development based on the needs of the district. Annually the School Committee adopts the Professional
Development Plan, which summarizes the prior year's activities with impact as measured by a survey and outlines the
plan/offerings for the next year.
2.3 To identify staffing needs and make recommendations for hiring staff.
Progress: Complete. Staffing needs are based upon the instructional-curriculum review and budget development
processes.
3. To use technology as an integral component of teaching, learning, and management.
3.1 To develop a district technology plan to address equipment and staffing needs.
Progress: In-process. This school year we added instruction in computer technology to the THMS curriculum.
3.2 To provide professional development to raise the level of appropriate technological use in the instructional
program and to improve communication. Progress: On-going. We are working on a process to link assessment or
other data to specific Individual professional development to needs. We are also considering a survey of graduates
regarding their level of technology preparation as a result of our instructional program.
4. To provide facilities that meet staff, program, and community needs.
4.1 To complete a needs assessment for building transitions related to construction of the new primary school. Progress:
Complete. Our educational and logistical plans for the reconfiguration and move were developed and are in the
process of being implemented.
4.2 To develop and implement a centralized process for building use. Progress: We are recommending that this objective
be clarified, as there is already a process for building use in place. According to members of the Improvement Plan
Committee, this objective was to address the notion of a community calendar. There is a critical need for a viable
website in order to accomplish this outcome. The Computer Advisory Committee supports out-sourcing the website
and options for funding are being pursued.

HIGH SCHOOL
Michael Barney, Principal
Lunenburg High School had another very successful and exciting year in academia, athletics and extracurricular areas.
During the summer, many of our returning sophomores and juniors spent time designing and filming an orientation video for
our incoming freshmen, to help them through the initial trepidations of entering high school. Many thanks to our student
leaders and the LHS TV crew for this well received and helpful venture for the "new kids" on the block.
The High School recognized scholars and parents with our first Honors Breakfast this year, bringing parents and students
together for coffee and kudos to recognize their academic excellence.
Our STEP program (Senior Transition Experience Program) continued into its second year, providing opportunities for
community-based learning experiences for seniors. This program continues to gain momentum as our younger students
now set their sights on this experience as part of their future planning.
Our Mathematics team ranked 9th this year out of 83 teams at the seventeenth annual WPI math meet in Worcester and is
having another successful season at the top of the NORWOC math league.
Our entire Social Studies Department engaged in a three-year partnership with Fitchburg State College, Mount Wachusett
Community College and several other local school districts in a grant called "Teaching American History," a graduate level
program to bring primary sources back to students in the classroom. We are the most well represented district in central
Massachusetts, which speaks to the dedication and enthusiasm of our staff and speaks to the wonderful resources that they
are and will become for our students. Our entire English Department participated in a course on "Adolescent Literacy,"
focusing on the teaching of reading strategies, which will improve our instructional practices and student learning.
The LHS community extends its heartfelt thanks and appreciation to the Lunenburg Track and Field Association for their
continued efforts to make a dream a reality. Your relentless focus and energy to provide future generations of children a
beautiful facility on which to run, play and compete is something that is treasured by all of us. Thank you.
The fall season saw our Blue Knight Football team represent LHS in the 2004 Super Bowl, with girls' Soccer and Field Hockey
making the district tournaments. The Cross Country teams competed through the district championships, with Sadiea
Williams winning both the league and district championship meets, a first In LHS history. Our athletic program was also
proud to adopt a new curriculum from "Get Psyched Sports," promoting positive environment and visualizing success.
Beginning with the winter season, every coach and athlete was trained in this program, and we were only the second high
school in the nation to adopt it across the board in the athletic program. We are proud of our student-athletes and their
positive contributions to our community both on and off the field, track and court. This program will serve as a model to
other communities at the local, state and national level. Our boys' winter track team was the league champ, with our girls'
team placing second. Our boys' and girls' basketball teams and hockey team all qualified for tournament play. Our boys'
and girls' track teams sent several representatives to the state meets, and I'm certain our spring teams will follow sult.
The Unity Project, a collaboration between the Lunenburg Public Schools and Dr. John Woodall, began this year as a project
designed to enable students to Identify Issues that they feel are important to address to improve their school environment,
and also to empower them to use their self-identified strengths to focus their energies to be part of the solutions to these
prioritized issues. The goal of this project will be to empower ALL students to become engaged in the community at some
level, since we feel as a community that everyone has a personal strength to offer as a direct match to a need in the school.
