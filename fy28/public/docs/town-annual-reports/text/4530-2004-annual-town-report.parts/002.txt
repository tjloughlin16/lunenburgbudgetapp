TURKEY HILL MIDDLE SCHOOL
Principal: Keith Hochstein
Turkey Hill Middle School enjoyed a very successful year based upon a number of criteria. As a middle school, the academic
and personal needs of our students are the major focus, and with both of these criteria our school registered significant
accomplishments.
Our academic goals focus on the development of skills, attitudes and knowledge. It is our hope that the parents of our students
are pleased with the intellectual growth of their children. Report cards and progress reports document student academic
progress. Student attitude towards school is a harder criteria to gauge but we are proud to say that our attendance rate this past
year averaged between 96 and 97 percent. This tells us that students enjoy coming to school, enjoy the environment and enjoy
interacting with their friends and teachers.
Of course the criteria that is on everyone's mind is MCAS results. We believe that success of MCAS is a major responsibility for
this school. Our teachers work diligently, intelligently and professionally to prepare students for success on these tests.
Everyone knows that all students must pass the MCAS in 10th grade in mathematics, ELA and soon science in order to graduate.
Our teachers feel strongly that when students have success on MCAS at our level, the students leave us well positioned to have
success at higher levels.
We are happy to once again report that students at THMS met the goals established by the Department of Education and made
Adequate Yearly Progress (AYP). We administer half of the tests given in the entire system, which means that we need to
prepare students in three different subject areas and in four different grades.
In Grade 5, students are tested in Science and Technology. 60% of our students attained Advanced or Proficient scores versus
55% at the state level; 8% of our students scored in the Warning category versus 13% at the state level.
In Grade 6, students are tested in Mathematics. A website entitled Just for the Kids compares schools of equal type including
criteria such as socio-economic status. Last year our 6th grade students finished fourth out of one hundred and sixty two
schools in this survey. Our scores this year compare favorably with last years. 62% of students scored in the Advanced or
Proficient categories versus 43% at the state level. Only 4% or about five students scored in the Warning category versus 25% at
the state level.
In Grade 7 where our students are tested in ELA, our results are even better! 85% of our students scored in the Advanced or
Proficient category versus 68% at the state level. Only 1% of our students scored in the Warning category versus 7% at the state
level. We have raised our Advanced and Proficient percentage from 63% in 2001-2002 to 71% last year and now 85% this year!
In 8th grade, our students are tested in Mathematics and Science/Technology. In Math 54% of our students were In the
Advanced and Proficient Categories versus 39% at the state level. 12% scored In the Warning category versus 29% at the state
level. We have improved our percentage in Advanced and Proficient from 34% in 2001-2002 to 46% last year and now 54% this
year. Warning scores have gone down from 28% in 2001-2002 to 17% last year to 12% this year.
In Science & Technology 49% in the Advanced and Proficient categories versus 33% at the state level; 15% of our students
scored in the Warning category versus 31% at the state level. We do not put too much emphasis on comparing our scores to
state scores. When the difference between the two scores is this much, it is certainly worth noting.
There is another method of judging a school's success with MCAS that is becoming quite popular; It is a website entitled Just for
the Kids. This website compares schools based on the number of continuously enrolled students who took the MCAS, the
number of economically disadvantaged students and the number of Limited English Proficient students in the school. In other
words, they compare schools that have similar populations. Recently they published their results, which you will find below but
you can look them up yourself at www.just4kids.org.
In fifth grade Science and Technology we were compared with 290 other schools from towns like West Boylston and We finished
79th; this places us in the top 73%. In sixth grade mathematics we were compared to 256 other schools including Amherst,
Newton and Brookline, and we finished 16th; this places us in the top 94%. In seventh grade ELA we were compared with 206
other schools including Brookline, and we finished 11th; this places us in the 95%. In 8th grade mathematics we are compared
with 209 schools including ones in Newton, Brookline and Grafton, and we finished 16th; this places us in the 95%. Lastly in 8th
grade science we are compared with 209 other schools including ones from Newton and Nauset, and we finished 13th; this

disaster. Their efforts resulted in a donation from Turkey Hill Middle School to the Red Cross in the amount of $2,200. What a
generous and altruistic effort by the sixth graders and all our students!

time, all students have direct instruction in technology. Depending on grade level, students had classes in keyboarding, MS
Word, MS Excel, MS Power Point, Internet research and peripherals. In a very well equipped technology lab, our students gained
the technology skills they need to compete and succeed in the upcoming years.
Our THMS Homework Committee will submit to the School Committee by June the 1st a homework policy in the Lunenburg
Public Schools. We hope this will ald students, parents and teachers to understand their responsibilities and thus perhaps
reduce stress on all.
Initiated by our counselors and members of the faculty, a peer mediation program was started this year. This program enabled
students to resolve personal and emotional issues by receiving support from their peers. It is one more way to create a school
environment that is appealing to students.
Quite frankly, this report could go on and on. We held our first Spelling Bee and our winner represented us in the Sentinel &
Enterprise contest. However, all of these accomplishments represent a picture of a school that seeks to make continual
improvement for the betterment of our students. We always hope that the residents of the town think positively about THMS, and
we appreciate all of their support.

THOMAS C. PASSIOS ELEMENTARY SCHOOL
Principal: Cyndy Daukantas
Both the Thomas C. Passios Elementary and the Primary Schools continue to strive toward educating the total child. We utilize
a variety of instructional methods and modalities in our approach to meet the individual needs of every child. We believe that
the success of our programs is dependent upon a healthy and productive partnership between home and school. Toward that
end, parents play an active role in many of the daily programs and routines of their child's educational program.
This year continues to be a challenging and exciting year for the students, faculty, and staff of both the Elementary and Primary
Schools. With a combined enrollment of six hundred thirty students, class sizes range from eighteen to twenty-three. Because
research indicates that class sizes of twenty and under in the early years provide lasting educational benefits to young children,
we strive to keep our kindergarten through grade 2 classes smaller.
During the summer of 2003 summer school funding was limited. Through fundraising efforts, School Linked Services was able
to provide the funding for twelve kindergarten children to receive instruction for the month of July. Mrs. Lisa Stone, kindergarten
teacher, was thrilled with the progress that all twelve children made. This progress was further noted in our grade one Fall
reading testing. I am hopeful that through continuing fundraising efforts, we will be able to offer support to both kindergarten
and grade one students this summer.
Last spring, eleven of us from both the Primary and Passios Schools spent four days at Lesley University in Boston, working
with a team of renowned experts in the area of literacy. The Leadership Team has shared this learning with the entire staff of
both schools during our full professional development day last November. During our early release professional development
time, the entire faculty of both schools continues to develop lessons focusing on the five essential elements of literacy:
phonemic awareness, phonics, fluency, vocabulary, and comprehension.
The Primary and Elementary Schools implemented DIBELS (Dynamic Indicators of Basic Early Literacy Skills) for all
kindergarten students and grade one students this fall. This assessment monitors student progress in various literacy areas
determined critical for literacy proficiency: initial sound fluency, letter naming fluency, phoneme segmentation fluency, and
nonsense word fluency. Using DIBELS all children are evaluated in the fall, at mid-year, and again at the end of the year.
Students scoring in the risk category are provided additional instruction with the teacher, as well as an opportunity to use the
Lexia Early Literacy Computer Program on a regular basis to reinforce concepts. Although teachers have always been able to
identify the progress students make in a given amount of time, this monitoring system documents this progress. With
documented need, good instruction and continued monitoring of skills, every student can learn.
This year the Elementary School Improvement Plan focused on the areas of English Language Arts for students with disabilities
and those receiving Title I support and Math for all students Including those with disabilities and receiving Title I support. We
continue to work on these areas, using the diagnostic support of our Testwiz program, Dibels testing, Lexia Learning System,
our newly implemented Everyday Math Program, as well as our revised delivery model in Title I. Our second goal called for the
establishment of a transition team to assist in the transition from a Pre-K to 4 school to a Pre-K to 2 and a 3-5 school. Two
transition teams were developed to work on each educational level and worked for several days throughout the summer of 2004.
Follow-up meetings were held in the fall and findings/ recommendations were shared with the faculty. Transition teams
developed belief statements, as well as a vision for each of the schools. Recommendations for staffing next fall are based upon
those visions. Our final goal of piloting a full day kindergarten program has been extremely successful. We created an
extended day kindergarten program for twenty-one students, on a tuition basis. Based upon the classroom teacher's
observations, parent input, and our recent Dibels testing, every student has benefited from the full day program. This option will
be offered to parents again next year.

INSTRUCTIONAL SERVICES OFFICE
Karen Martin, Director
During this past year the Instructional Services Office continued to focus on three areas critical to the District's
mission: (a) curriculum development, (b) assessment and evaluation (measures), and (c) continuous improvement for
ALL. Following is a summary of activities and results in each of these areas.
STANDARDS
The School Committee formally adopted the English/Language Arts curriculum in December, and implementation of the
program will begin in January. Curriculum adoption monies purchased needed materials and resources, and
professional development this year focused on literacy. The History/Social Studies curriculum is undergoing revisions
and peer review and will be presented to the School Committee in June. Textbooks and materials will be purchased to
align with the new curriculum. In January the Instructional Technology Task Force will begin a yearlong curriculum
development process. A systematic, on-going curriculum review process ensures standards are met and realigned as
needed.
MEASURES
The seventh administration of the Massachusetts Comprehensive Assessment System (MCAS) took place in the spring
of 2004. In Lunenburg 93% of the 10th grade students met the required benchmark for graduation in Mathematics and
97% of 10th grade students met the required benchmark for graduation in English/Language Arts. Overall, ninety-one
percent (91%) of 10th grade students passed both required tests.

CONTINOUS IMPROVEMENT FOR ALL
The No Child Left Behind legislation provides an opportunity to carefully examine performance results for all students.
Every district in the nation received Annual Yearly Progress results for this mid-cycle. The Lunenburg Public Schools
met AYP goals in the aggregate (all students) but did not meet AYP for the students with disabilities subgroup in
English/Language Arts. This data helps guide the school councils, administrative team and Results teams to plan for
appropriate Interventions, programming and professional development.
School structures and collaboration focused on student learning ensure all students improve. The Lunenburg Public
Schools has Results Teams, Understanding by Design training, a comprehensive professional development program
and new teacher induction program to support student learning.
Results Teams meet one time per month during faculty meeting time. The teams use data to set goals, plan teaching
strategies and assess the results. This process ensures a regular review of student performance and effective teaching
strategies.
A majority of our teachers have been trained in Understanding by Design. This assessment process focuses on
developing assessments to improve student performance. Through ongoing analysis of student results, refinement of
units and increased awareness of effective assessments and instructional practices, student performance will improve.
The UBD units provide additional measures in non-MCAS subjects and provide additional curriculum materials for new
teachers.
Ongoing and sustained professional development continues in a range of areas. This year graduate level courses were
offered in Understanding by Design, Curriculum Implementation, Mentoring with Meaning, Five Essential Elements of
Reading, Adolescent Literacy, and Wonder Years-Developmental/Social Education.
This year Title I services include a part-time math specialist and an extended-day program. Our Title I tutors provided
additional classroom support for our Title I students in ELA and math and the extended-day program provides
additional time on learning for Title I students in math.
Our new teacher Induction program provides ongoing support for new teachers to the district. New teachers participate
in a two-day orientation, have a trained mentor and receive peer coaching during a two-year process. New teachers
participate in professional development focused on skills and strategies needed for beginning teachers.
SUMMARY
The Instructional Services Office has a strong partnership with the individual schools, the administrative team, faculty
and community. The combined and integrated efforts of all parties ensure continued focus on improving student
performance and strive to provide teachers, administrators, students and the community with the necessary data,
educational research, professional development, support and resources needed to improve student performance.

SPECIAL SERVICES DEPARTMENT . SPED
Elaine E. Blaisdell, Student Services Coordinator
During this past year, the Special Services Department continued to focus on our district mission of continuous improvement for
ALL students. The faculty and staff in the Lunenburg Public Schools take pride in their ability to provide equal access to the
curriculum for all our students. Administrators, regular and special educators, and parents collaboratively work to develop
Individual Educational Plans for students. The plans developed are rigorous and provide the necessary specialized instruction
for students to be successful in the least restrictive environment. Currently there are two hundred and seventy-two (272)
students with disabilities receiving some form of special education or related service in Lunenburg on an individual educational
plan. This represents 14.86% of our total student population. This is slightly less than the state average of 15.57 %. Of the total
number of students receiving services, nine students received services outside the district during the 2004/2005 school years. Of
the nine students, one is in a collaborative placement, seven are in day placements and one is in a residential placement. In
Lunenburg, an additional forty-seven students identified with disabilities receive accommodations under a 504 plan.
Our efforts this year have focused on improving our students' ability to meet the Adequate Yearly Progress mark in regards to
MCAS. There has been a district-wide drive to examine our students' scores, identify areas of deficit and provide the necessary
instruction to remedy the areas of identified need. We have accomplished this through our schools' results teams, at Individual
Educational Team meetings, and through individual teacher efforts in the classroom. Our results teams have examined
individual student scores and identified effective methods and strategies to teach students the skills they need to improve their
performance. At Individual team meetings, parents and teachers engaged in active discussions about the best methods to
address individual deficit areas. As a result, in many cases we have increased opportunities for students to learn concepts by
examining student schedules. In some cases the student receives a double Language Arts or Math class providing the student
with needed additional instructional time in an area of identified weakness.
Another area of significant change this year is the reactivation of the Parent Advisory Counsel of Special Achievers in
Lunenburg (PACSAL). Over the past several years, we have had parent advisors that could be contacted to provide Input and
guidance on various topics related to Special Education, but we lacked the parent leadership to organize an active PAC. We now
have a PAC that can provide parents in the district with organized times for meetings, guest speakers on related special
education topics, and support for our schools and parents regarding special education matters. Meetings have been scheduled
for the second Tuesday of each month at 7:00 p.m. in the Lunenburg High School Teacher Resource Center. The executive
board is made up of Co-Chair: Kelly Harvey, Co-Chair: Liz Peterson and Secretary: Sandra Osborne. Since their first meeting in
October, they have completed numerous tasks that will make a difference for special achievers in Lunenburg. To name a few
things, they have raised funds for speakers, provided a voice for special education students at the school committee meetings
and other parent committees, established a website at pacsal.com, and engaged in dialogue with the local police department to
set up an early search and rescue system for students with non-verbal special needs in the area. At the PACSAL meetings, they
have established a short business meeting and provided speakers on various special education topics for parents in the district.
In May of 2004 we participated in a Department of Education Mid Cycle Review; a two-member Massachusetts Department of
Education (DOE) team visited Lunenburg Public Schools to evaluate the special education services on the implementation of
specific criteria. The team reviewed our student records and procedures to evaluate our compliance in twenty different areas.
For example, they examined student Individual Educational Plans for development and content, examined our procedures for
determination of placements and determining extended-year services for students, looked at parent involvement throughout the
IEP process, examined our policies for providing services for the homeless, and visited the schools looking for compliance for
laws related to handicap accessibility.
The Special Services Department worked hard to gather the necessary documentation to show our ongoing compliance to state
and federal laws. We were given favorable feedback by the team regarding our ability to comply with the regulations. Some
areas of concern cited by the DOE team related to facilities and their accessibility. We were cited for accessibility of the gym at
the Turkey Hill Middle School and accessibility of bathrooms at the T.C. Passios Elementary School. It was felt that stronger
documentation of transitional services provided in the district was needed on the IEP, as well as more consistency with writing
measurable goals and objectives on IEP's. Additionally, we were cited for not having an active Parent Advisory Council.

DIRECTOR OF FACILITIES AND GROUNDS - SCHOOLS
John Londa, Director
The focus of the school maintenance effort for FY05 was improvements to the School Department's compliance with the
American Disabilities Act (ADA). A $74,000 Foundation Reserve Grant received in October 2004 funded the ADA improvements.
Using capital funds as well as the grant, railings that meet Massachusetts Building Code were added on ramps and stairways at
the Turkey Hill Middle School and Lunenburg High School. At the Passios Elementary School, the women's room is being made
handicap accessible and wider entry doors were installed in the accessible entrance. An inclined wheelchair lift is scheduled to
be installed in April 2005 at the Turkey Hill Middle School to Improve access to the gymnasium. Additionally, door hardware
changes to convert from doorknobs to door levers are expected to be completed at all schools by the end of the fiscal year.
Repairs to the Elementary School parking lot and installation of wheelchair ramps on the sidewalks at the Elementary School
and Middle School will be completed in late spring using a combination of capital funding and the Foundation grant.
Development of a long-range facilities plan that moves from an approved concept to a phased plan for renovation of Lunenburg
High School, Passios Elementary School, and Turkey Hill Middle School was discussed at a School Committee Forum on
facilities. In general, consensus seemed to be that Lunenburg High School was the next building that needed to be addressed. A
subcommittee to look at the issues was formed to investigate and determine the scope of renovations needed for the High
School.
The initial phase of construction of the Town library included a new driveway entrance for the Passios Elementary School, which
was completed before school opened. Additionally, construction of sewer connections for school buildings, except Lunenburg
Primary School, began in July resulting in schools being connected to the new sewer system in November 2004.
Capital improvements for FY05 included $50,000 in replacement flooring for hallways at Lunenburg High School and three
classroom sets of furniture for each building. Replacement floor work is expected to continue in FY06.
The Lunenburg Track and Field Athletic Association continued work on a soccer and track facility between the High School and
the Middle School. Paving was completed for the track and in the late fall of 2004 materials for an Astroturf-like playing surface
for the field were delivered to the site for installation in the spring of 2005.
Preparation for moving into the new Lunenburg Primary School at 1401 Massachusetts Avenue continued; construction at the
site lags the contract completion date resulting in a delay in occupancy of the new building. The Primary School will be opened
as a school for the 2005-2006 school year. Future uses for the current Primary School continue to be studied by Town planners.
In Fiscal Year 06, the focus of capital maintenance will be to prepare an energy performance contract proposal for all schools.
Particular emphasis will be to upgrade lighting at all buildings to high efficiency lighting and energy savings.

ARTICLE 26, To see if the Town will vote to amend Town By-Law Article XV, Section 9 by deleting in its entirety
(Adjustment Policies) and replacing with "Layoff Procedure Recall Policy", on file in the office of the Town Clerk; or
take any other action relative thereto. Board of Selectmen recommendation @ Town Meeting. VOTED UNANIMOUSLY as follows:
Layoff Procedure If the Town decides to layoff employees, it shall determine from which classification(s) layoffs shall
be made. Layoffs shall then be made based on seniority within the classification.
"Seniority" shall be the length of an employee's continuous service measured from the employee's most recent
appointment date; provided an employee's seniority may be reduced for periods of unpaid leave, unless otherwise
prohibited by law.
If two or more employees in the same classification have the same seniority, the employee who has the shorter term of
continuous employment with the Town shall be laid off. If there are two or more employees in the same classification
with the same seniority and the same term of continuous employment with the Town, a part-time employee shall be lald
off before a full-time employee. If the there are still two or more employees in the same classification, the CAFO shall
determine which employee shall be laid off.
An employee that is scheduled to be been laid off shall have the right to bump other employees in lower classifications
with less seniority, provided that the CAFO, in his/her sole discretion, but with consultation of the Department Head,
shall make a determination that the employee retained is qualified and able to perform the duties of the lower
classification, subject to disability discrimination laws.
Employees who have been laid off from employment are responsible for keeping the Town advised of their current
address for the purpose of potential recall from layoff.
Recall Policy - If the Town conducts a recall from layoff, recall shall be offered in reverse order of layoff by
classification provided the laid off employee is qualified and able to perform the duties of the classification to which the
employee is being offered recall, subject to disability discrimination laws.

the layoff.
The Town will notify an employee of a recall opportunity by certified letter (or other verifiable method of delivery) to the
