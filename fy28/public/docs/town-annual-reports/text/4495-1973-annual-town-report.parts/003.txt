School Lunch Program Report
"You've entered the school lunch service at the worst time in its history." These were the
words oft times repeated by salesmen coming to my office. This only made me more determined to
meet the challenge head on.
School lunches to me are a very important part in a child's education. Where else can he par-
ticipate in so many new food experiences and yet be assured of a well-balanced meal. As you know,
we are required to serve a "Type A" lunch by the State.
Many changes have been made as we push forward to greater efficiency in service and to an in-
crease in participation.
Do you realize we have a huge baking program--rolls, breads, cookies, pastries, and that we
prepare our own pizza, lasagne, stews, etc. in our cafeterias?
Our menus, published weekly, will show you the variety of food in your school lunch program.
By preparing attractive, tasteful foods, we are endeavoring to educate your child to good food
habits which will become a natural thing in his daily living.
A financial report for the school year 1972-73 for the Lunenburg School Lunch Program follows.
Respectfully submitted,
MARJORIE H. STILLMAN
School Lunch Supervisor

School Nursing Report
for 1973
The "Lunenburg Public Health Nursing Service" is part of the Board of Health. Three Registered
Nurses are employed--Mrs. Doris M. Lahti and Mrs. Gladys V. Barney, working full-time and Mrs.
Shirley M. Carpenter, working 5 hours per day when schools are in session. Mrs. Marion Walsh is
also employed part-time to do clerical work. All three nurses participate in a combined Visiting
Nursing and School program. Dr. John DeLeo is the School Physician.
A total of 129 visits were made to parents of school children for physical defects, absence
from school and follow-up on vision and hearing failures. The nurses also contact parents as much
as possible by phone to discuss problems students might be having.
In January, Marlene Lashua from "The Epilepsy Foundation", gave a very interesting and educa-
tional program to teachers and nurses. This program helped us all recognize the "Epileptic Child"
and suggested kinds of help that are available for such children.
Mr. Kerkos Kassabian from Northeastern University, gave a 10-week course at the High School for
"Care and Prevention of Athletic Injuries". Mrs. Barney participated in this course.
In the area of Continuing Education, all nurses attended the course, "School Health Program",
held in West Boylston. This course consisted of six, four hour sessions and proved extremely
valuable to our staff.
The Nashoba Associated Boards of Health offer the following programs in the schools--each pro-
gram requires signed permission from parents with the exception of the Educational Dental Program:
German Measles (Rubella) and Mumps
Vaccine to certain grades.

who have previously completed oral

We are fortunate in being able to offer these programs to our students; a factor which keeps
the Lunenburg Schools immunization rate at a high level.
Vision and Hearing Testing is done annually on all students, Kindergarten through Grade 12.
Notices of failures are mailed home to parents with a doctor's form attached. This form must be
returned to the Nurses' Office after the child has been seen by an Eye or Ear doctor in order for
the school to follow the doctor's recommendations.
The Nurses wish to thank the Administration and School Personnel for their help in making the
School Health Program so successful.
Respectfully submitted,

Montachusett Regional Vocational Technical School District
The Annual Report for 1972 indicated an enrollment of 889 secondary students, 148 post high school students in
health and other occupational fields, and 277 in Grades 9 through 12 on the waiting list. As of the October 1, 1973
census, we have enrolled 914 students in Grades 9 through 12, and 182 post high school students in health and other
occupational fields for a total of 1,096 day-school students. Wehave a number of former dropouts and high school
graduates seeking occupational development who are in available seats which are not occupied since we do not, as
yet, have a full Junior and Senior class. We would expect that our peak enrollment will occur at the next census
when an expected 200 will be enrolled in this school. (The original design of the building was for 1,000 day-
school students.)
Mr. Thomas F. Markham, Jr., was hired to replace the former Adult Education Coordinator and will assist in
special project services, including assisting in the coordination of district-wide projects. Again this year, repre-
sentatives from several of the local industries have made numerous requests for short-unit courses to update specific
skills, such as, numeric control for machine operation, fluid power control techniques, sophisticated welding meth-
ods, electronic mensuration and quality control methods, domestic and commercial heating and air conditioning serv-
ice certification programs, and a course to prepare adults for a Fireman Second Class State License. Also, evening
course offerings for adults have increased from six, which were offered in the Spring of 1972, to thirty-one courses
now in operation for the 1973 Fall term
hence, providing greater utilization of our plant facilities.
During the 1973 Spring and Fall terms, 1, 038 adults were prepared for gainful employment through our evening
occupational programs, representing an increase of 521 adults over the number enrolled during the 1972 Spring and
Fall
terms our first year of adult programs. In serving additional students on the secondary level, reach-out
and spin-off program development have received greater momentum as the result of the approval of a project propo-
sal and the action of our School Committee to implement the project proposal; therefore, giving more mechanical and
structural aid in enlarging the scope and effort of Montachusett Regional.
To date, twenty students have participated in Data Processing programs on an after-school basis. These are
students who are not enrolled at our school but are regular students from the sending schools in Fitchburg and Win-
chendon who participated in a support program to enhance their business education. Interaction programs com-
menced with Mount Wachusett Community College with the advent of a Career Mobility Program; i.e., students who
complete certain post-graduate health programs at Montachusett Regional may continue their education with ad-
vanced standing at Mount Wachusett and obtain a degree. Further studies are being made to developa contractural
relationship in cooperative use of facilities and student relationships. Numerous donations of equipment and sup-
plies have been received, including steel, an electric automobile, new automobile engines specifically designed
for learning purposes, mechanical equipment, industrial supplies, etc.
Our Dedication Ceremony was held on May 6, 1973, at which U.S. Senator Edward W. Brooke, U. S. Con-
gressman Robert F. Drinan, State Senator Robert A. Hall, State Representative Gerald P. Lombard, and members of
the State Board of Education lauded the school, its philosophy and methodology, as well as the graduation of over
500 students who have already benefited by successful entrance into careers and jobs.
Montachusett Regional's second graduation exercise washeld on two separate days since the auditoriumwas not
large enough to house both the post-graduate and secondary graduations at the same time. Our first class graduated
students who were transferred to our school from the phased-out Fitchburg Vocational School, and who came to this
school with advance standing. With very few exceptions, all the students were employed prior to their graduation.
An Open House was held on March 26 and 28 with over 4,000 people attending from all of the communities in our
District.
The Governor's Advisory Council on Vocational Technical Education selected Montachusett Regional as one of
two schools to evaluate for purposes of developing materials for its report as required by the State Plan for Vocational

Education. At the "open hearing" the school was again lauded for its accomplishments with students, and it was in-
dicated that this school can set an example for its methodology, enthusiasm of staff and students, and accomplish-
ments of tasks necessary to prepare responsible and economically self-sufficient citizens for entrance into the com-
munities.
Last year, we had fifteen clubs. This year, our students have developed more extracurricular activities inclu-
ding an annual series of events such as a "Welcome Freshmen" which takes the form of a carnival. Our first football
game was won by our first football team! Other accomplishments include an annual Horse Show. This year's pro-
ceeds went toward paying for the electronic scoreboard and a summer camp scholarship for a deserving student. The
very successful Drama Club play "Flowers for Algernon", the Yearbook "The Odyssey", the school newspaper -
"The Growl", are all evidence of student identity and enthusiasm within the school. Since there are no study per-
iods in the school schedule, these activities are conducted during an activity period, as well as after school.
The Student Council, together with the Student Advisory Council, made several recommendations to the School
Committee which were discussed, accepted, and implemented including a smoking plan allowing for smoking only
if parental approval has beengiven, policing of the program through the students themselves, together with a Student-
Faculty Discipline Board. Among the of the students in their skill areas are the completion of a ran-
ger home at the Boy Scout Camp in Gardner, the repair to a bandstand in Ashby, the building of benches for the Fitch -
burg parks, the manufacturing of many specialty tools and parts for the Water and Highway Departments of the City of
Fitchburg, and other community services such as repair of vehicles and numerous printing services.
All new teachers have cipated in a summer workshop on individualize progression methodology. Teachers,
additionally, participated in a Project Decision Value Clarification Seminar which included a Drug Workshop. Fed-
eral monies received for project proposals amount to $567, 158 to date $126,000 of which has been received this
year. In addition, we have received 50% reimbursement on all operating costs as provided by Chapter 74 and 40%
for construction capital costs and interest.
At this writing, the administration is preparing the next annual budget. Every effort is being made to reduce
our per-student operating cost, in spite of the escalation in the purchasing index. While there was an attempt in the
legislature to reduce reimbursements to the cities and towns under Chapter 779, member towns are still entitled to
the 15% reimbursement under this chapter as members of a regional district. With Montachusett Regional's entitle-
ment of 50% reimbursement on all net operating expenses, and 100% reimbursement on student transportation under
Chapter 74, we hope to keep the assessment to the cities and towns at the minimum without impairing the objective
of preparation for educational competency, good citizenship, and gainful employment.

Extension Service
This year, as in the past, Lunenburg youth have had 4-H Club work available to them. The active clubs are:
The Lunenburg Bridle Path 4-H Club, under the leadership of Mrs. Lois Camber and Mrs. Diane Moore; The Cookie
Crumblers, with Mrs. Agnes Price as leader; and Creative Mind Unlimited lead by Mrs. Dot Connell and Mrs. Ruth
Anderson. Town Committee Officers are: Town Chairman, Mrs. Ruth Anderson; Secretary, Mrs. Jean Stockwell;
and Treasurer, Mrs. Marcie Gauntlett. Trustees are Mr. Raymond Proctor and Mr. Richard Bishop.
The 4-H Cookie held in October was in charge of Mrs. Brenda Kimball. The top cookie sale camperships,
sponsored by the Town Committee, went to Andrea DeFazio, Patty Cutting and Cindy Smith. Also, individual club
camperships were awarded to a member from each of the clubs. This year a bus trip to the Eastern States Exposition
on 4-H Day and a trip to the Ice Follies in Boston were arranged by the Town Committee and enjoyed by all present.
It was with regret that we accepted the resignation of Mrs. Yvonne Bourque, a club leader and Town Committee
Secretary, who has given many years of service to 4-H work in Lunenburg.
In addition to cooking projects, The Cookie Crumblers Club completed other projects, such as sewing and
knitting. This year they proudly completed a Broomstick Lace Afghan for Christmas. Miss Heidi MacCracken, a
college student, was an observer and helper at several of the club meetings for which she will receive credits for
courses she is taking in school. The Club ended their year with a trip to Whalom Park and dinner at a nearby res-
taurant.
The Crafts Club have made a variety of different items. Liquid embroidery, decoupage, stenciling and cera-
mics were just a few. In this club we try to teach new and different things each year, and to do things that the
children are really interested in. A Splash Party and Cook-Out finished off the year.
The Horse Club members attended clinics at the University of Connecticut and in Bolton, Massachusetts. They
participated in the 4th of July Parade in Fitchburg and the Home Coming Parade held in Lunenburg. Ten club mem-
bers attended horse camp at Camp Marshall in Spencer. Two horse shows were held this year--a 4-H Show in June
and an Open Show in October.
A competitive trail ride for Horse Club members was held as was a trail ride for parents with judging being
done by club members. Mr. James Brooks held a longeing clinic at the Rod and Gun Club at which time he worked
with individual club members and their horses.
Many Horse Club members participated in Demonstration Night as did other club members. After giving their
demonstrations in Lunenburg, they competed in Oxford, on a County level, and in Bolton on a State level. Miss
Karen Allen was one of three from Worcester County chosen to represent US at the Eastern States Exposition in Spring-
field. In Springfield she competed against other 4-H members from all the New England States and represented
Lunenburg and Worcester County well. Congratulations Karen!
All in all the clubs have been busy this year, but there is always room for new members and for interested
parents for helpers and leaders.
