BOARD OF SELECTMEN
The wheels of Town government have churned for another year and, in spite of the
budget crunch, we have been able to keep up with all the routine work of the office
and make some innovative improvements as well. We have issued licenses for
restaurants and sale of liquor, inspected and permitted all the earth removal sites in
Town, and held numerous public hearings.
Cooperation among Boards has been the keynote of several undertakings. Together with
the School Committee and the newly formed Insurance Advisory Committee, we are
working to study and recommend changes in our health insurance structure. The
Planning Board, the Innovative Housing Study Committee, the Housing Authority, the
Ad-Hoc Housing Partnership Committee and the Selectmen have worked to put together a
plan to form a Housing Partnership. With this many people working on a project, you
might think that it would never be completed. But - not so! The organizational plans
have been developed and the land use plan is awaiting approval from the State.
The Route 13 Study Committee has reported that they are meeting on a regular basis
with their goal being to determine short term and long term solutions to the traffic
problems in the Route 13 area.
Our newly formed Fleet Maintenance Department has been working successfully since
last May to ensure that vehicle repairs are made in a cost-effective and timely manner.
A new preventive maintenance program for our aging vehicles is looking very promising.
The implementation of this project is another example of cooperation to achieve
improved use of our resources. Believing that this would be a better way to do business
the Police, Fire, Schools and Highway Departments each contributed a portion of their
budgets to form the new consolidated service.
Paving of Ruth, Parmenter and Johnson Streets has been completed and the engineering
for the improvement of Sunset Lane is underway. It has been a goal of this Board to
encourage citizens to undertake such projects, making them full recipients of all the
Town's services. At this moment, engineers are busy designing the intersection in the
Center of Town in preparation for the installation of a traffic light.
The waste oil heating system has been installed and is operating in the Highway
Garage. You no longer need to worry about how to appropriately dispose of this nasty
stuff. Warm us with your used oil!
Our Classification and Salary Schedule for Town employees has been entirely revised
from the ground up. It has been twelve years since this was last done. The new
statute provides for compensation based on the qualifications required for a particular
position and internal consistency of pay for different positions. A second phase of the
project will introduce a significant departure from past practice merit raises.
With regret, but also with pride, we accepted the resignation of Tim Hillman as our
Town Counsel. His job was to keep us out of legal trouble. He did S0 successfully,
with character and good humor. May he serve long and well in his new role as Judge.
You Board has enjoyed working in service to the Town, working with each other, and
with you, the citizens.

BOARD OF HEALTH
Nineteen ninety marked the Board of Health's separation from the Board of Selectmen.
Newly elected members Kenneth R. Baker, David W. Rodgers and James L. Deming spent a
good deal of time familiarizing themselves with local and state health and
environmental codes, laws and regulations.
Board of Health meetings were held twice monthly throughout the year. It is
increasingly evident that health and environmental problems are growing at an alarming
rate. An example of this was a complaint regarding contaminants leaching into area
groundwater from the old Hickory Hills landfill. Testing of wells in the area was
conducted and, fortunately, no contaminants were found. This potentially complex
problem was alleviated in part due to the cooperation of area residents; the Lunenburg
Water District for taking professional water samples; and the Finance Committee for
granting the funds for lab testing.
A major component of the Board of Health's workload will be careful and intelligent
planning for the health and environmental safety of the Town's residents. Our
Landfill's life must be extended as long as possible. Presently, solid waste disposal
options appear to be financially staggering. Please join us, your Board of Health, in
participating and working together for a better Lunenburg.

BOARD OF ASSESSORS
The Board of Assessors and its staff completed a second year of property valuation
using the CAMA software program. During this time, many refinements have been made
to each detail of property assessment.
As part of continuing education of the Board of Assessors and its staff, Judy Holden
received the professional designation of Massachusetts Accredited Assessor and Lisa
Normandin completed the Department of Revenue's Assessment Office Procedures course.
In the spring of 1990, Jim Noonan relocated to Texas and submitted his resignation.
We wish Jim the best of luck and want to express our appreciation for his efforts on
behalf of this office. We welcome Paul Gagliardi as a new member of the Board; Paul
will be filling Jim Noonan's term of office. Paul's background is in the field of
property appraisal and he has already been a great asset to this office.
The Board of Assessors and its staff looks forward to working with taxpayers in
resolving questions they may have regarding their assessments.

RITTER MEMORIAL LIBRARY
The least controversial observation about 1990 at Ritter Memorial Library is that it was
a far better year than the preceding one. The Library was open for 52 weeks in 1990,
offering all the usual services as well as new programs and experiences for the
community.
Materials circulated totalled 54,556, significantly more than the 51,309 total for 1988,
the last "normal" year. Adults borrowed 19,766 items; young adults, 1,256; and
children, 33,534. Using figures from a recent survey, a conservative estimate of the
number of Library visits by the public is 21,000 per year.
Programs to please all ages included Actress Ladjamaya with dramatic recreation of "The
Black Experience" in February, and in December, Jim Douglas with whaling songs and
slides, and David Polansky, musician and songwriter. "Elderfest", co-sponsored by Very
Special Arts, was tailored to the needs and interests of the Town's senior citizens who
responded with enthusiasm, Children's programming included craft workshops, pre-school
story hours, movies, the annual Arts Festival, nature walks and shows, Prime Time
sleepover, writing workshops and storytellers. After a summer with no reading program,
they turned out with more interest than ever in 1990. Also, 237 children signed up to
read or be read to and many of them returned two or three times each week to enjoy
special events. During the school year, classes from the Primary School and local
nursery schools visited on a two-week schedule, and the Children's Librarian regularly
read a story to as many as 200 children in that time frame. Fully one-fourth of the
Library service is in support of formal education for all ages.
Invaluable help has come again this year from the Friends of the Library. Their zest
for raising funds for programs of every type, for awards and prizes, and for equipment
and furnishings, and their frequent volunteer help, is one of the most positive aspects
of Library operation for both Trustees and staff. Plans are now being made for the use
of more volunteers, since a shrinking budget points to fewer paid staff hours in the
future.
The Lunenburg Memorial Fund, the Arts Lottery, the Women's Club, and individuals have
contributed vital support to the Library in the past year. Gifts were received for
materials in memory of John Kiernan, Ralph Benjamin, Kay Guiou, and Eleanor Gutherie.
In addition, it has been most encouraging to receive some substantial gifts of money in
recent months. Plans are underway for replacing and expanding the shaky shelving in
one of the children's rooms, painting walls, and possibly replacing worn carpet in that
area with funds given in memory of Lee and Dana Woodward, combined with a $2,000
donation from the Lunenburg Rotary Club. At last year's Annual Town Meeting, $3,000
was voted for repairs and maintenance to the roof and gutters to prevent the need for
costly future work.
It seems that the experience of closing the Library for five weeks in 1989 should be
history by now, but 1990 was marked by one common theme of concern still expressed
almost daily in various ways. "Are you going to stay open this year?" "Do you have
enough money?" "Thank goodness you're open again!" "I still can't believe what

not yet received to piece out this year's operating budget before the end of June, and
the pessimistic predictions for next year's funding, the Library Trustees themselves are
still dealing with the same concerns.
Both the regional library system and the Town are encouraging the Library to do long-
range planning. The services and programs that have been developed by an
enthusiastic staff working with meager funds are evidence of what can be accomplished,

discouraging prospect for both staff and Trustees, not to mention Library users. The
need for support by believers in quality library service is greater than ever.
PLANNING BOARD
The Planning Board processed eighteen ANR Plans for calendar year 1990. Commercial
and industrial development requiring development plan reviews were: The Village,
Ecological Fibers, Exxon and Fairlane. We have completed Heritage Credit Union and
Exxon. Presently, the Village, Ecological Fibers and Fairlane are in stages of
construction and are being monitored to insure the development plan review conditions
are met.
The Goodrichville subdivision has been signed off under the statutory empowerments of
the Planning Board. The FDIC still has the Woodland's project under their jurisdiction.
The Maplewood project is being presented to the Planning Board conceptually, but a
formal submission will be made by the time of this publication. The new plan will
present the golf course as a separate entity, modify the Blueberry Hill Village
subdivision, and eventually present a Planned Residential Area Housing Plan.
In September of 1990, the Planning Board presented a new Zoning District By-law
entitled "The Recreation District" to the Special Town Meeting. The Town Meeting
passed the new District and Maplewood Golf Course became the first District mapped.
The Planning Board's Ad-Hoc Committees have continued their studies. Presently, the
Innovative Housing Study Committee has presented a draft of the Town of Lunenburg
Housing Plan to the EOCD office. This Plan will be completed and presented at a public
hearing. The Sewer Feasibility Study Committee has presented a report which includes
a recommendation for a Master Plan Study for implementation of a community sewer
system. Under the Long Range Resource Study Committee, the Planning Board is
participating with the Capital Improvement Planning Committee and the Accountant's
office on a Town-wide inventory. The Town Circulation Analysis Study Committee has
presented its primary report which the Planning Board has presented to the Board of
Selectmen. In other Growth Management Plans, the Board is monitoring regional
happenings as they affect the Town of Lunenburg. Two major issues are the potential
second airport sited at Fort Devens and the proposed recycling composting facility at
Pioneer Park in Leominster. The Board is working closely with the Board of Selectmen
on these issues.
The Planning Board will continue to work in these difficult times to implement the
Growth Management Plan. It will further update planning by innovative and protective
methodologies.

HOUSING AUTHORITY
As 1990 came to a close, the Housing Authority and it's staff were able to look back
and see solid gains in the Authority's operation. After many months of work, through
the cooperation of Fire Chief Dennis Carrier, the Authority has received funding
approval to upgrade the fire alarm system at 131 White Street. This work is long
overdue and the Authority is grateful to Chief Carrier for his participation in our
efforts.
The Authority replaced all of it's incandescent light fixtures in the common areas with
new fluorescent fixtures. We anticipate a sizable reduction in our energy consumption.
This retrofit could not have been possible without the cooperation of Fitchburg Gas and
Electric.
Bessie Lindsay resigned from the Authority this year. Her resignation was accepted
with reservation, and we extend our sincere gratitude for Bessie's service to the Town
of Lunenburg. A tenant from Pearl Brook Apartments, Alice (Betty) Doe, was appointed
to fill the remainder of the term.
During 1990, the Authority received twenty-three new applications for housing
assistance. Of the total received, sixteen were for elderly housing and seven for
family housing. At year end, our waiting list totals were: Elderly (667-1) - 9, and
Family (705-1) - 48.
The Housing Authority will continue to work with the Town of Lunenburg to maintain
the current level of service to it's tenants, and to provide new housing opportunities to
those individuals and families within our community in need of such assistance.

POLICE DEPARTMENT
In 1990, the Lunenburg Police Department, along with the City of Fitchburg and the
Town of Ashby, applied for a drug grant to establish a task force between the three
communities. The purpose of this task force is to effectively reduce and control drugs
and drug-related crimes in our cities and towns. We were successful in our endeavor
and received $24,000 from the federal government plus the Town's matching funds of
$8,000. This $32,000 in funds will enable the Town to hire one additional police
officer to act solely as a drug investigator, and to pay for wages, training and
equipment needed to do the job. This will be the first time in eleven years that we
have been able to hire additional help, and will enable us to fight the very real war
against drugs in our community. This year, our drug arrests rose to 27, compared to 16
in 1989. I would like to thank the Board of Selectmen and the Finance Committee for
their support and faith in us by approving the $8,000 in matching funds, thus making
it possible for us to obtain the additional $24,000.
Through a joint effort between the Superintendent of Schools and the citizens and
businesses in our Town and the surrounding area, we were able to send Patrolman
James Marino to a two week (80 hours) Drug Abuse Resistance Education class.
Patrolman Marino will be bringing the D.A.R.E. program to our Middle School. He will be
teaching five classes to the 5th grade level, three days a week, for seventeen weeks.
I would also like to thank the Lunenburg Police Relief Association for the donation of
$15,500 to upgrade our computer system and for the purchase of honor guard uniforms.

FIRE DEPARTMENT
The year 1990 proved to be a very busy one for the Fire Department. An analysis of
the calls shows we responded to 85 more ambulance calls than the previous year and 22
fewer fire-related calls. The total number of calls for the year was 637, which is an
increase of 63 calls from the previous year. The breakdown is as follows; Hazardous
Materials - 13; Structure - 43; Alarm Activations - 52; Investigations - 15; Mutual Aid
- 20; Medical - 9; Illegal Burning - 12; Service - 15; Motor Vehicle Accidents - 15;
Outside Electrical - 3; Motor Vehicle - 16; Ice/Water Rescue - 5; Brush/Grass - 23;
Rubbish - 3; Other - 3; Rescue/Ambulance - 390.
As you can see, our activity and emergency calls continue to increase at a time when
budgetary constraints are eroding our ability to provide these basic services.
Unfortunately, as the economic times get worse, the demand for our type of service
increases, i.e. more medical problems and more fires. Unless the Town can come up
with enough money to adequately fund the public safety departments this year, I feel
that we are going to really start to feel the effects of the past few years of
underbudgeting. The effects of "hold the line" budgeting are now starting to really
hamper our operation. Somehow we have to find a way to reverse this trend and
adequately fund the departments that we depend on for our life safety and property
protection.
On the other hand, Town Meeting voters have been good to the Fire Department in
replacing old and worn out apparatus. The firefighters need good apparatus to protect
the Townspeople in emergency situations. Reliable apparatus also makes our job a little
safer for us. Our future capital needs include the replacement of our pumper/tanker, a
1966 Four Wheel Drive that carries most of our water to outlying areas of Town. This
truck should be replaced within the next two to three years. We also need a ladder
truck for the safety of the firefighters while ventilating roofs during a fire and also to
assist in rescue operations. The replacement of the Fire Station is long overdue. It
has been pointed out in studies over the years that we lack adequate space for
apparatus, maintenance and storage areas.
The members of the Fire Department take great pride in protecting the Townspeople
from the devastating effects that are caused by fires or medical emergencies. We thank
you for your past support and look forward to your support in these hard times.

COUNCIL ON AGING
This has truly been a year of great accomplishment. Under the leadership of our
Chairperson, Elvira Z. May, the members of the Council have each assumed a share of
the total responsibility and, as a direct result of their dedication and hard work, many

Priority number one continues to be our Meals on Wheels program serving the needs of

HISTORICAL COMMISSION
The boundaries of the Lunenburg Center National Historic District are now marked by
wooden markers, designed and constructed by Bill Perkins, appropriately decorated and
painted by Amy Ashman, a former Lunenburg High School Student, and installed on
metal pipes by the Highway Department. These markers appear at the eastern and
western ends of the Historic District on Massachusetts Avenue, on Leominster Road near
West Street and on Lancaster Avenue at Rolling Acres Road. These being the present
boundaries of the District, plus Main Street and parts of Chestnut Street, Highland
Street and Whiting Street.
At present, the Commission is researching the possibility of having the North and/or
South Cemeteries, or parts thereof, included in the National Historic District. Pertinent
material from Boston has been received, along with information indicating it is more
difficult to have burial grounds included as a Historic District, compared to buildings.
However, your Historical Commission will continue to pursue this possibility.
The Historical Commission meets in the Historical Society Building on the second
Wednesday of the month at 7:30 p.m.
BICENTENNIAL COMMISSION
The Lunenburg Bicentennial Commission was established in October 1986 to help
celebrate the bicentennial of our Constitution. The original group consisted of
Chairman, James Hays, Vice Chairman, Ronald Lamothe, Secretary, Marjorie Karabatsos,
Treasurer, Nelde Drumm, Richard Bishop, Jean Hobbs, and Elizabeth Burnap. Later,
Eleanor Whitney was appointed to the Commission. The first year, an essay contest on
the meaning of the Constitution was conducted. The winners were Holly DeNike, Kevin
Szocik and Farzana Kahman. Also, a program to celebrate the 200th anniversary of the
signing of the Constitution was held Thursday, September 17, 1987 at 7 p.m. in the
High School Auditorium. On October 2, 1987 the Lunenburg Constitution Ball sponsored
by the Lunenburg Rotary Club took place.
In 1989, an essay contest on the Bill of Rights was undertaken with the help and
support of the North Worcester County Bar Association. The first place winner was
Seth Heim, Alana Jones was second and Kerry O'Brien came In third. In 1991, the
emphasis will continue on the Bill of Rights. The 200th anniversary will be celebrated
December 15, 1991. This year we received the resignation of Jim Hays. A special
thanks goes to Jim for his outstanding leadership. Good luck Jim.

SOLID WASTE ADVISORY COMMITTEE
The Solid Waste Advisory Group had an exciting year. As promised at last year's
Annual Town Meeting, we submitted a comprehensive report on future plans to the
Board of Selectmen and the Board of Health. After an enthusiastic reception of this
report, we have moved on to it's implementation. Fully 8% of last year's trash has
been kept out of the landfill through our recycling efforts. This year with planned
improvements in our recycling area and the continued support of our fellow citizens, we
will easily be able to keep 25% of the current waste load from being buried. In
addition, we are planning for the inevitable closure of our present landfill operation
with programs that include technical improvements to our current site and regional
solutions to increase our efficiency.
In 1991, our all-volunteer effort will continue to refine these immediate and long-term
solutions to the solid waste problems of our Town.

TOWN CLERK, TAX COLLECTOR AND TREASURER
Last year I said it was a year of change and challenge, this year. it
has become a challenge to change. Due to cuts in these three
administrations, most of my staff has gone on reduced hours and
beginning in May 1991, the office will close on Fridays. The trend
appears to continue as we will be asked to cut budgets another 6% for
FY92. These cuts are sure to cost more than the wages that would
otherwise be paid to my staff, which is the only place cuts can be made.
The workload continues to increase and the challenge becomes what to do
first, what shall we let wait? Anything we leave for later becomes lost
revenue. Again my staff has to be commended for their efforts to get
the job done. And I am grateful for their cooperation and support.
The States situation is bleak, the local aid payments continue to be
semi-annually (December 30th and June 30th) and if that's not bad
enough, they're being paid late! This forces the Town to borrow in
anticipation of revenue, something we are doing more and more of. The
difference now is that we are paying higher interest rates on the
borrowing, while receiving lower interest rates on our investments. The
banking industry has been somewhat shaky since I have taken office, the
Treasurers creed used to be YIELD first, going for the highest interest
rates available, then LIQUIDITY and SAFETY. Now the highest interest
rates usually indicate trouble in the bank offering the rates. We are
now collateralizing our accounts for safety, however. with this safety
comes loss of interest income.
Quarterly tax billing continues to be discussed. While the collector in
me says No, No, No, the treasurer in me says Yes, Yes, Yes. The
Assessors do not want to do quarterly billing, they prefer instead
legislation which is currently pending which would allow communities to
move the billing dates up a month. This would not give us the more even
cashflow that quarterly tax payments would thus reducing significantly
the need to borrow in anticipation of revenue. I would be remiss
however, if I don't point out that doing quarterly tax billing would
increase the workload for the three departments mentioned. Increases in
these budgets would have to be made for manpower and equipment which
would be offset significantly by the interest paid for borrowing.
As of July 1990, we were able to obtain a 100% collection rate for
Fiscal Year 1990 Real Estate Taxes. This involves either the collection
of the tax or filing a tax title lien at the Registry of Deeds on those
properties with outstanding tax balances.
The Town Clerk's office was very busy this year with the Annual
Elections in May, State Primary in September and State Elections in
November. In addition to the Special and Annual Town Meetings in May,
Special Town Meetings were held in February for the question of a
Regional School with the Town of Shirley. (this required a ballot), and
in September primarily to establish a Recreation District. During the
peak of voting at the November election we experienced a power failure
that left Lunenburg voters with the perfect excuse (if they don't like
the outcome) they were voting in the dark!

RESOLUTION
WHEREAS the Town of Lunenburg faces growth pressures from an
increase in the rate of development within the community; and
WHEREAS the Town of Lunenburg faces additional growth pressures
from an increase in development in the region, over which the Town has
little control; and
WHEREAS the Town of Lunenburg has limited resources with which to
address growth pressures; and
WHEREAS the Town of Lunenburg has established an on-going and
continuing planning process to identify and address its needs; and
WHEREAS a Strategic Planning Study, which identifies the effect of
growth on the Town from development within the region has been
completed; and
WHEREAS a Growth Management Plan, which identifies strategies to
address growth pressures has been completed;
RESOLVED:
That the Town of Lunenburg endorse the Growth Management Plan and
the Strategic Plan, prepared by the Lunenburg Planning Board and dated
April 1989 and June 1989, respectively, in order to protect the natural
resources of the Town, accommodate future growth. and to enable the
provision of services in an orderly manner, and further resolved that
the Town boards and committees work cooperatively with the Planning
Board and the Board of Selectmen to implement the plans.
