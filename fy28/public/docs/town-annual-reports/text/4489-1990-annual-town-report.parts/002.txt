SUPERINTENDENT'S REPORT - CONCETTA A. VERGE
During the past few years I have consistently reported to our community
that because of the dismal fiscal state of our Commonwealth, the Lunenburg
School System has been underfunded and has sustained significant cuts in
staffing and previously Town-funded programs.
I have then elaborated on the creative means by which the
administration, staff, parents and community have provided many of the
opportunities formerly funded by the town to our students through
alternative means.
It would seem that my job has been to announce the bad news and the good
news in the past few Town Reports.
Once again; the bad news is that public funding of the schools continues
to diminish in an environment of escalating costs; and yes, additional
programs, services and staff were eliminated in the level funded budget of
FY'91. However, the good news is that the spirit of cooperation and giving
of oneself is pervasive among the staff and administration, parents and
educational community. These groups have worked diligently to diminish the
negative impact on our students that such reductions have made.
Administrators and staff have accepted extra responsibilities in order
to pick up the slack created by elimination of over 30 staff members in a
two year period. Parents have come forward in large numbers to volunteer in
the schools, in order to assist classroom teachers in many capacities.
Parents and community members have extended fundraising efforts and absorbed
well over 50% of the funding of athletics in Lunenburg as well as a
significant amount of funding required to support the school music program.
Meanwhile, the administration has been as creative as possible in an attempt
to bring Non-Town generated funds into the schools. These funds include
increased and significant Non-Resident Tuition dollars which have helped to
maintain staff and programs. New and/or additional grant funds have also
been generated. Individual teachers and small groups of teachers have also
pursued grant funds as has The Lunenburg P.T.O. in order to supplement the
curriculum and programs previously supplemented by Town Funds. No stone has
been left unturned in the quest to maintain the quality education program
for which Lunenburg is noted.
I very much appreciate the support I have received from the staff,
parents and community which has allowed me to persevere in the fight to
maintain the quality educational opportunity our children deserve. I also
greatly appreciate and applaud the efforts of the administrators, staff, and
parents which have greatly contributed to our ability to maintain such
quality. Without this school/home/community cooperation and effort, the
Lunenburg Schools would be facing mediocrity.
School year 1991-92 does not look to be a promising year financially.
Even more reductions in staff and programs will be made. However, WE cannot
and will not give up on pursuing quality for our young people. Less and
less in the schools will be funded by the Town (public funding). More and
more, if we maintain some of our programs will have to be funded in an
alternative fashion (private funding). It is our fervent goal to weather
these economically critical years without compromising educational
opportunities for our youngsters and to look forward to better times and the

Positive School climate and morale seem to defy the fiscal crisis in the

Several new and on-going activities foster a sense of education, care,
and awareness among children, staff, and parents.
The Study Skills Program is a six-week, in-class program directed at
grades three and four. Children learn specific school behaviors,
organization skills, and study strategies necessary for learning.
The Developmental Guidance Program is an in-class program presented to
all children at the school. The curriculum is designed to build self-esteem
and to help children address and understand appropriate feelings.
Communications, problem-solving, and decision-making skills are stressed.
The Child Study Process involved a team of guidance counselors,
teachers, parents, and administrators who meet by request to discuss
interventions for children experiencing difficulty within their classrooms.
The Weekly Home Memo (Pitter Patter), distributed each Thursday,
highlights important ongoing and upcoming information for parents.
The Monthly Newsletter (Panda Patter), distributed at the end of each
month, features more in-depth news.
A monthly Professional Development Day has been established system-wide
to allow for teachers' continued education. Children are released at the
Elementary School at 11:55 on such days while teachers pursue such topics as
mainstreaming children, new teaching strategies for language and math,
cooperative learning and classroom space allotment.
The Before/After-School Day Care Program provides supervised care for
children one hour prior to and two and a half hours after the close of
school. The children play games and take part in craft activities in the
Elementary School cafeteria and in the Primary School gymnasium.
First Grade Math Program: All first grade teachers in the T.C. Passios
School are using an innovative way to teach math this year: "Mathematics
Their Way", an activity-centered mathematics program published by
Addison-Wesley.
A goal of the activities is to develop understanding and insight of the
patterns of mathematics through use of concrete materials. A variety of
familiar materials gathered from the child's world enable a teacher to begin
where the child is in his or her world, and with them to gradually build a
bridge to the adult world of abstraction.
In addition to being child-centered, all of the activities in the
program are fun. The children use manipulatives that are small, safe and
easy to handle such as lima beans, toothpicks, M & M's, Cheerios,
bottlecaps, etc.

MIDDLE SCHOOL PRINCIPAL'S REPORT
(Mary Cringan)
The Turkey Hill Middle School enrollment is currently at 473. The
largest population areas are grades seven and eight.
We are educating adolescents who will be young adults in the year 2000
and therefore we have implemented changes in our focus. These changes have
been recommended by the Carnegie Council on Adolescent Development and will
vastly improve the educational experiences of all middle grade students.
Parental involvement has increased this year. We are pleased that
parents are volunteering to help the students as well as the staff. As many
as fifty volunteers help us with jobs such as early bus duty, library aides,
math tutors, reading to students, clerical duties, Nature's Classroom, field
trips, special events and computer aides. These volunteers have allowed us
the opportunity to expand options for our students.
All grade five students are participating in the DARE (Drug Abuse
Resistance Education) program. Jim Marino is our DARE officer. DARE is a
substance abuse prevention education program designed to equip school
children with skills for resisting peer pressure to experiment with drugs
and alcohol. Fifth graders are also participating in the SOC (Student Ocean
Challenge) Program. Students are tracking the routes of sailboats as they
race around the world. This program offers an exceptional opportunity for
our children to learn about the oceans, world cultures, geography, history
and our global environment through a multi-disciplinary study of
contemporary ocean voyages and expeditions. We are proud of the teaming
that is taking place in grades six and seven. All students in grade eight
are exposed to a foreign language and will take part in a Consumer Fair in
April. The school committee is committed to making Turkey Hill Middle
School an exemplary middle school.
Our students continue to score well above the state average on the
Massachusetts Educational Assessment Test and the Massachusetts Basic Skills
Test.
Jean E. Killeen, a long time educator in Lunenburg, retired in June 1990
after 33 years of service. During her career, she served as a business
teacher, department head, assistant principal of both the middle and high
school and finally principal of the middle school. The students and staff
wish Jean and her husband Dick well on their retirement.

HIGH SCHOOL PRINCIPAL'S REPORT
(Frank A. Sambuceti)
Lunenburg High School offers a comprehensive program of studies for
students in grades 9-12. Since over 70 percent of the graduates of
Lunenburg High School typically pursue some form of higher education our
curriculum provides a strong program of college preparatory courses in
English, mathematics science, social studies and foreign language.
Complementing this academic core are quality offerings in business
education, art, music, technical arts and physical education.
Lunenburg High School also provides an extensive co-curricular and extra
curricular program and our students are encouraged to participate in the
many clubs, organizations, activities or interscholastic athletic activities
which are offered. Among the numerous student clubs and activities are:
Debate Club, Youth and Government Club, Yearbook, Student Council,
International Club, Math Team, National Honor Society, Nature/Environment
Club, S.A.D.D., Latin Club, Ski Club, National Art Honor Society and Future
Business leaders of America. Additionally L.H.S. sponsors athletic teams in
baseball, basketball, cross country, football, golf, soccer, field hockey,
tennis, track and softball.
School pride and a sense of community have always been a hallmark of
Lunenburg High School. Events during the 1990-91 school year have once
again demonstrated the deservedness of that reputation. The first of these
was the tragic death of "Noom" Hongsmatip a sophomore who succumbed to
injuries sustained in a varsity soccer game last fall. One essential
characteristic of a good school is that it cares deeply about the values of
every member of that institution. This quality was very much in evidence
during the most difficult days of that traumatic event. As LHS's new
principal, I was impressed and deeply moved by the manner in which our
faculty extended compassion and support to our students and by the clear
sense of mutual concern and humanity which was SO apparent.
The other significant event impacting the High School this year was the
Comprehensive Evaluation conducted in late October by the New England
Association of Schools and Colleges. Copies of the N.E.A.S.C. Report are
available at both the Ritter Memorial Library and the High School Library
and it is hoped that they will be widely and carefully read. It is
important to note that the Visiting Committee also acknowledged those areas
of traditional strength at the High School. Among the commendations was
their belief that L.H.S. was "a happy, satisfied and confident school with
faculty and students expressing a mutual respect the students feel
comfortable and accepted (They) exhibit enthusiasm and satisfaction with
the faculty. Apparent also is a level of pride Teachers enjoy job
satisfaction. The faculty are dedicated and well prepared. Parents
indicate a positive feeling that the school is doing a good job with
academics and support services."
Perhaps N.E.A.S.C.'s most concise and insightful assessment of what
Lunenburg High School is about is reflected in the following observation:
"A dedicated and competent faculty, staff and administration work together
to serve the Lunenburg community. There is a genuine and sincere
understanding of and commitment to the welfare of all."

A school's basic mission of course is to provide the very best
educational services possible for the students it serves. Lunenburg High
School has a long and proud tradition of academic excellence and the
accomplishments of our pupils this year continue to reinforce that
reputation. Student performance on standarized achievement tests, the
National Merit Scholarship program, the statewide Academic Decathalon
Competition, the number of early college admissions and traditionally large
percentage (over 70%) of Lunenburg High School graduates who successfully
pursue post secondary education are all clear indications that Lunenburg
High School maintains its adherence to educational excellence.
It is our belief that a school also has a responsibility to provide
learning opportunities for all members of the larger community of which it
is an integral part. The High School is very proud of its efforts to
introduce an Adult Education Program for the citizens of Lunenburg. It is
our hope that this Spring's pilot program will grow in the years ahead and
contribute significantly to the intellectual, cultural and social life of
this community.
Lunenburg High School believes that an educational program that aspires
to excellence must be rigorous, relevant and comprehensive. Significant
curriculum revisions will go into effect next year which will increase
overall graduation requirements while specifically emphasizing coursework in
science and the arts. Nevertheless, while acknowledging that administration
and faculty are deeply committed to the concept of a comprehensive high
school, the N.E.A.S.C. Report warned that "the realities of reduced
financial support, declining enrollment and other external factors have
eroded the comprehensive nature of the High School." The Report further
states: "it has been obvious throughout the visit that finances underlie
many of the problems." The report concludes by saying: "It is the hope of
the Commission that this evaluation report will stimulate the staff,
administration and other responsible school officials to strive to make a
good school even better."
This is a challenge that we at Lunenburg High School readily accept.
The recommendations for changes and improvements in program, educational
facilities and physical plant are very clear. In the years ahead we project
steadily increasing high school enrollments (1990 - 410 students; 1995 -
470+ students; 2000 -- 515+ students). The need to not only maintain but to
improve and expand our level of educational services is genuine and
unequivocal. The good will and support of the entire community will be
absolutely essential if we are to succeed in our mutual goal of making
Lunenburg High School the very best place it can be, a school of which we
can all be justly proud.
