Memoriam
MARGARET P. HARLEY
July 13, 1907 - May 22, 1987
Margaret, a lifelong citizen of Lunenburg, taught
in the Lunenburg Jr. -Sr. High School for 35 years
before retiring in 1969. During her last 11 years
at Lunenburg High School she was Guidance
Director. Several scholarships have been named in
her honor.
Margaret received numerous awards throughout her
lifetime which included being the former 1925
world champion javelin thrower. Margaret was a
member and past president of the Board of
Directors for the Heritage Community Credit Union;
the Lunenburg Historical Society; the Friendly
Seniors; and the LHS Alumni Association. She was
also former Treasurer of the Lunenburg's Women's
Union.
Margaret served as a member of the Lunenburg Park
Commission. She was co-editor of the "Heritage of
Turkey Hills: History of Lunenburg", and was also a
member of the 250th Anniversary Committee. Shortly
before her death, she was honored by the Girl
Scouts for 75 years of service to that
organization.
Margaret will always be remembered for her years
of dedicated service to the Town, and for her
humor and love of life.
RICHARD A BINGHAM
January 7, 1923 - February 28, 1987
Dick was a salesman for the Fitchburg Paper
Company for 43 years and lived in Lunenburg from
1947 until 1984 when he retired to New London, New
Hampshire. He served with the U.S. Marine Corps in
the Pacific Theater during World War II.
He was a member and secretary of the Lunenburg
Fire Department for more than 35 years and was
given an honorary membership upon his retirement.
Dick was one of the volunteer radio dispatchers on
Sunday mornings for many years. He was active in
other Town committees, 30 years of which as
Chairman of the Town Caucus Committee.
Dick's warmth and friendliness made him a person
one did not easily forget. He was noted for his
cheer and enthusiam at the Annual 4th of July
bonfires, of which he was the Master of
Ceremonies. Dick's love and concern for the Town
of Lunenburg was evident in all aspects in which
he participated.

Board of Selectmen
The year 1987 was a busy one in Lunenburg. The Board of Selectmen's Office went
through quite a few changes this year. We bid farewell to Selectman Walter Keeler and
Selectman Paul Ralston. Their support and commitment to Lunenburg is much appreciated.
Two new Selectmen joined us this year. Selectman Joseph Bilotta was elected in May and
Selectman Janet Goodrich came on board in December.
Executive Secretary Barry Stell left our office in May. We'd like to say thanks to
Barry for the many long and dedicated hours he put into his job, and we wish him well in
his future endeavors. A special thanks is given to Fran Vaughan for her work as Interim
Executive Secretary, and to Jeannine Mendolusky who filled Fran's job during our search
for a new Executive Secretary. In October, Jane Mahony came on board as the Town's
Executive Secretary. We welcome her and look forward to working with her.
Several ongoing issues in Town experienced a great amount of progress this year.
One of these issues is the continuation of the Clean Lakes Diagnostic Study of Lake
Shirley. Phase 1 of this Study was largely completed this year with a proposal submitted
to the State for movement into Phase II.
Town Hall remains a serious problem. However, the Town has indicated a desire to
renovate the existing structure rather than build a new one. The Building Committee is
moving forward on a proposal of building renovations for the existing Town Hall.
On other Town Building projects, the Building Inspector will be working with the
Montachusett Regional Vocational Technical School on needed repairs to the Jones House.
The Board of Selectmen has been discussing the potential renovation of the Memorial
Building for possible use as a Senior Center. This project will undoubtedly get a great
deal of attention in the coming year.
Spring brought two unfortunate incidents to Lunenburg. The heavy Spring rains
caused a near collapse of the Lake Shirley Dam. Since Spring, a great deal of time and
money has been spent to secure the dam temporarily while reconstruction plans are
developed. One stumbling block was overcome in a Special Town Meeting in August wherein
the Town voted to accept the ownership of the dam. This acceptance allowed State
Management. financing of the proposed reconstruction through the State Department of Environmental
The second incident that occurred this Spring was the School oil spill. This
accident could have been disasterous had it not been for the diligent efforts of the Fire
Department and the Civil Defense. Thanks to the assistance of Senator Padula, much of the
cost of the clean-up was borne by the State.
As the immediate impacts of the oil spill fade, the ever-growing concern over
hazardous waste issues heightens. Hazardous waste contingency plans are being developed
by the Fire Department and the Civil Defense; a household hazardous waste collection day
is being considered; and an inventory of underground tanks is being discussed. This issue
will be one of increasing concern toward which the Town must turn its attention.
As the Board of Selectmen, we would like to thank the Building Inspector, the Police
Chief and Town Counsel for their continued efforts to enforce the unregistered car by-law.
Due reduced. to their diligence, the number of unregistered vehicles present in Lunenburg is being
In an effort to seek a more sound and adequate financial base for Lunenburg, we have
re-established the Industrial Development Committee. Their task is to research the impact
of industrial growth on Lunenburg, and, if that impact is deemed positive, to work with
the various other Boards and Committees in attracting industry to Lunenburg.

Board of Health

Boards of Health. Since 1931, Nashoba Associated Boards of Health, as agents to the local a
boards of health, has provided a range of services to its member communities. As
reflection of changing times, Nashoba's duties have expanded from traditional tasks, such
as nursing visits and disease prevention, to increasing contemporary needs for social work
and environmental health protection. Nashoba is working to meet not only a wider range of
services, but also to hone the professional skills demanded in these specialty areas.

Town Counsel
The area wide real estate boom continues to consume large amounts of time from this
office. This year the amount of Town Counsel's time spent on applications for subdivision
approval filed with the Planning Board increased. Those files included an application by
Reservoir Realty Trust for a definitive subdivision plan. This ended in litigation over
the Planning Board's ability to protect public ways outside of the subdivision. The legal
review of the application of F & H Realty Trust to subdivide a large parcel of land by
Hickory Hills Lake also required a great deal of time.
It is expected that 1988-1989 will continue to require attention to land use matters
due to the recent applications to develop Maplewood Golf Course and the former Levine
property.

Board of Assessors
The Board of Assessors wishes to thank William Reed and Bernard Paton for their many
years of service to the community and to wish them well in retirement. John Male and
James Noonan have been appointed to fill the vacancies on the Board, and they will serve
until the next annual election in May. Also leaving the Assessors' Office was Jacqueline
Keeler. We thank Jackie for her many years of service to this Board and wish the Keelers
the best of luck in Vermont. Judith Holden was hired to take over as Chief Clerk.
At the Annual Town Meeting, voters passed money articles to provide for an Assistant
Assessor and for the FY'89 revaluation. The Board is in the process of interviewing can-
didates for the Assistant Assessor position. With all the changes in the office and on
the Board, it was felt, and the Department of Revenue concurred, that the revaluation
should be postponed for one year. The computer that was voted at the Special Town Meeting
has been installed in the office and is currently being loaded with the Town's Tax Base
information. This will aid significantly in the FY'90 full revaluation. The new tax maps
that the Department of Revenue recommended for the revaluation have been completed.
However, after careful review, it was determined that several maps could be consolidated.
This is currently being done.
Property values in Town have increased considerably in the last few years with signif-
icant shifts in relative values of various types of property. The value of land in gen-
eral, and waterfront land in particular, has increased much more than the value of build-
ings. In order to correct the resulting distortions, it will be necessary for the
Assessors to review all property in the community. The most difficult part of the revalu-
ation is very likely to be the determining of a schedule of land values. The Board will
seek the voluntary aid of members of the community, members of other Boards, and appraisal
and real estate consultants. The tax rate for FY'88 was set at $17.80 per thousand with a

Auditor
I have examined the financial statements and books of accounts of the Town of Lunenburg in
accordance with Massachusetts General Laws Chapter 41, Section 50 (Auditor-Part Time
Requirements), for the fiscal year July 1, 1986 thru June 30, 1987. Cash book receipts
and disbursements were verified, cash balances were proved by the reconciliation of the
bank balances with statements furnished by the banks of deposit. (See schedules below).
My examinations were made in accordance with generally accepted auditing (G.A.S.B.)
standards.
The books and accounts of the Tax Collector were examined, including real and personal
property, excise taxes and miscellaneous receipts.
Consistent with the practices of municipalities in the Commonwealth of Massachusetts, the
Town does not maintain historical cost records of its fixed assets (Land, Plant, Buildings
and Equipment including furniture and fixtures). I recommend a complete inventory and
cost up-date be initiated.
Sample tests were made on School invoices and receiving records. Also, a review of the
Lunch program, invoices, inventory and receipts control was made.
I suggested the Town Treasurer continue monthly reconcilation of bank statements with
payable registers as all checks may not be returned with statements. My examination found
other minor discrepancies which have been corrected. I certify this report states my
opinion of the accounting procedures of the Town of Lunenburg and is correct to the best
of my knowledge.

Housing Authority
The Lunenburg Housing Authority is in its 17th year in the operation of the facili-
ties at 131 White Street, known as the Pearl Brook Housing for the Elderly. Presently, we
have forty-seven apartments occupied by single persons, and one occupied by a couple.
The tenants have organized the Pearl Brook Tenants' Association which meets on a reg-
ular basis, and arranges interesting projects for the benefit and enjoyment of its mem-
bers. Alice Doe holds the office of the President and is the principal liaison between
the tenants and the Authority members.
The tenants are provided with weekly transportation. The shut-ins are provided with
"Meals-on Wheels", and the Nashoba Associated Boards of Health provides the Well-Oldster
Clinic on a monthly basis also Home Health Care provides household duties and shopping.
The requirements for occupancy are that the applicants be at least sixty-two years of
age, and in need of good housing. Rents are variable in that no more than 25% of income
can be charged for rent which includes heat, water, and electricity. The rising cost of
energy makes it very difficult for the Authority to keep within their budget.
The Lunenburg Housing Authority received a grant for $320,000 under the Chapter 705
Family Housing Program. The Authority plans to build three duplex buildings consisting of
three 3-bedroom units, and three 2-bedroom units on one site at 131 White Street for
low/moderate income families. Drawings are being prepared by the Architectural firm of
Tully & Ingersol of Cambridge for the three duplex buildings, subject to the approval of

Library Trustees
Is there anything more frustrating than carefully budgeting your time to include a
quick stop at the Library, only to find the door locked because it is Wednesday?
Unfortunately, the answer is "yes"--it is possible that finding the Library open when you
need it will be even less likely in the coming fiscal year. In the best of the "good old
days", the Library budget was never overly generous, never provided more than minimal
staff (who, incidentally, kept the Library open an extraordinary number of hours per
week), and never allowed adequate building maintenance. Then Proposition 2 1/2 passed and
the Library budget was substantially reduced. Two years ago, a plea to voters at town
meeting partially restored needed funds and raised the pay scale to a more equitable
level. However, a sharp cut last year necessitated eliminating open hours on Tuesday
evening and all day Wednesday. As the Trustees announced at that time, " the cumula-
tive effect of six years of inadequate funding has become evident. As the demands for
services have grown, it has become impossible to find time for book ordering and process-
ing, program and budget planning, and record keeping. It is also increasingly difficult
to hire staff at the current pay scale. The Town's financial picture is not improving,
present services are to be maintained in FY'89, a budget increase of 3.3% plus a reason- If
and the staff cannot continue to compensate for the Library budget limitations
able cost-of-living increase is required.
Library building needs are extensive, but the Trustees' first priority has necessar-
ily been a long-existing problem with the septic system. The Building Committee is now
seeking the best answer to this problem, with the intention of having it corrected in the
spring. An unexpected windfall of State money has made possible some much needed work on
the building. Two wooden doors were replaced with metal doors having panic hardware, and
a built-in supply closet was constructed. A tier of book shelving was purchased to re-
lieve overcrowding. It is hoped that increased State Aid can continue to be applied to
capital improvements rather than disappearing into the line item budget.
The summer of 1987 found the Library inundated by enthusiastic participants in the
reading program. On the first day, an average of over one book per minute was checked out
for ten straight hours! Story hours and activities for pre-schoolers, and service to
Primary School classes continued throughout the school year. Essential support for chil-
dren's services was provided by the Friends of the Library, who supplied many materials
and volunteer help. They also donated free passes for four Boston museums, seasonal deco-
rations, children's video-cassettes, reading program book awards, and literature racks.
They sponsored the Children's Art Festival, adults' and children's programs, and craft
workshops. The staff and Trustees have come to depend heavily on the Friends' reliable
support.
Townspeople appreciate the new sign on the lawn, which replaced a badly deteriorated
one. Materials were supplied by the Memorial Fund in memory of Annie Whitcomb. Robert
Fickett donated his time and talent to design, build, and install the sign, assisted by
several civic-minded contractors and individuals. Funds were also given in memory of Jane
Bascom, Ralph Benjamin, Lucy Chapdelaine, Dr. James Congdon, and John Joyce.
Arts Lottery Grants provided five new prints for the Benjamin Collection, museum
passes, a professional storyteller, a visiting author, and partial funding of the mari-
onette show, "Sleeping Beauty". The Library was awarded a $500 matching grant to purchase
books about the United States Constitution by the National Endowment for the Humanities,
with the local $500 coming from memorial gifts. Another grant, obtained from the State by
the Pepperell Library on behalf of seven area libraries, including Lunenburg, has supplied
a large selection of videocassettes for circulation. Tapes will rotate among the seven
libraries, providing greatly expanded capabilities for service.
Assistant Director, Elizabeth Vezina, left in May to take a library position at
Cushing Academy. Ruth Colburn was hired to fill the position.

Planning Board
The Planning Board's main focus in FY'87 continued to be with the growth issue and
overseeing land use. The Board is working with the implementation of Woodland at Hickory
Hills (Lake Shore Village) development. This is the first Planned Residential Area per-
mit. Project meetings are held every two to three weeks with the Project Manager, Robert
D'Onfro, and representatives from the Planning Board, Conservation Commission, Building
Official, Fire Department, Highway Department, and Nashoba Associated Boards of Health,
Lunenburg Water Department, and others. These meetings have proven to be very fruitful,
and complete records on the day-to-day progress of this project enabled the Planning Board
and other Departments to maintain a working knowledge of progress thus insuring
compliance.
The Board has received a preliminary application for a residential area around the
newly proposed golf course. The parcel is located adjacent to the former Maplewood Golf
Course. The process of this application will receive the same careful scrutiny under the
procedural study of compliance in the Zoning By-Laws and the Rules and Regulations
Governing the Sub-Division of Land in the the Town of Lunenburg.
Two conceptual plans have been discussed with the Board. A large parcel of land on
Route 2A and another parcel on Lancaster Avenue. We have worked with 34 ANR (Approval
Not Required) plans. These plans determine frontage on 117 lots. It should be noted that
these lot divisions significantly attribute to the growth status of the community.
The Board is concerned about the 50 (fifty) foot frontage portion of the Town's
Dimensional By-Law. The 50% reduction was intended to aid individuals with little
frontage, but adequate back land. It was not intended to subvert the Sub-Division Control
Law. The By-Law as presently written has lead to the submission of plans showing numerous
cuts off the road and very undesirable planning.
One of the Board's priorities in fiscal 1988 is water. The Water Resource Study
Committee, a sub-committee of the Planning Board, is still working toward the realization
of adequate Town water in the future. Their report can be found immediately following
this Report. The exploration of methodology for protection of the newly identified well
sites is taking place at this time.
The Planning Board has initiated another sub-committee entitled The Task Force for
the Study of Varied Housing. Its charge is to assist the planning process in assessing
Lunenburg's housing needs, evaluate the supply, and propose strategies for varied housing.
The Task Force report will be incorporated as part of the Growth Management Plan. There
is also an in house study concerning optimum loading point of community services and
conditions.
The Growth Management Plan is in progress. Several months have been spent gathering
data and at this time it is in the process of being analyzed. A questionnaire is being
designed to gather input from Lunenburg citizens. This input will be incorporated in the
final plan. The Board supports the need to reactivate the Industrial Commission and will
come before the Town Meeting with a basic Industrial By-Law. The Industrial Commission
will need the support of such a by-law to define the premise for any industrial enterprise
in the community. In addition, the Board will ask for an Article to support the funding
of mapping for a suggested Commercial/Retail Commercial/Industrial area. This too will be
in conjunction with our Growth Management Plan.
The Board continues to work toward the basic philosophy that growth and change cannot
be controlled, but good planning and careful attention paid to compliance of Zoning By-
Laws and the Rules and Regulations Governing the Sub-Division of Land in the Town of
Lunenburg protects the Town character, and the quality of its life for its residents. In
this respect, the Board is continually working towards the updating of the Zoning By-Laws
and the Rules and Regulations Governing the Sub-Division of Land in the Town of Lunenburg.
Meetings have been held with the Board of Selectmen, ZBA, Highway Department, Board of
Health, and other Town Boards for input in this work. Several changes will be suggested
at our Annual Town Meeting. Some of these changes are housekeeping for minor update and
others are to correct deficiencies.
The Board recognizes, due to the increased work load, a need for a staffed office.
The Planning Board is a working board, devoting many hours to their Planning Board respon-
sibilities, as well as serving as liaisons on other committees. However, the duties of
the Planning Board and the day to day implementation of the necessary paper work demands a
working Planning Board office.

Water Resource Study Committee
The Water Resource Study Committee, a sub-committee of the Planning Board, was formed
in 1984. It consists of two members of the Planning Board, two members of the Lunenburg
Water citizenry. District, one member of the Conservation Commission and two members from the general
The Committee was charged with conducting an in-depth water study, consisting of
qualitative and quantitative analysis upon which to base future zoning and watershed pro-
tective by-laws. The Committee was to report to a joint meeting of the Board of
Selectmen, Finance Committee and Planning Board, not later than December of 1984, and to
report to the next Town Meeting. The Committee, using professional consultant services,
reported a need to identify potential well sites.
In 1985, the Town voted to continue this Committee and appropriated $20,000 for this
purpose. The Lunenburg Water District matched the funds, and the firm of D. L. Maher
Company was hired. The Committee recommended that the highest priority should be given to
sites outside the present well fields watershed. Maher's report identified seventeen
potential sites.
In 1986, the Committee reported to the Annual Town Meeting, and the Town voted to ap-
propriate $15,000 to pay for more drilling on sites. Five sites have been found with po-
tential for being future water supplies. They are: Site #1 Hickory Hills, Site #4 Lake
Shirley, Site #5 Lake Shirley, Site #7 Reservoir Road, and Site #16 Goodrich Street.
These potential well sites, if brought into existence, will insure the Town of Lunenburg a
valued and critical resource for both health and independence for the next fifty to one
hundred years.
At this time, there is work being done with Town Counsel, the Lunenburg Planning
Board and the Water Resource Study Committee to develop some methodology to protect these
sites for the future. The Water Resource Study Committee urges the community to think to-
wards the future, both immediate and long range and provide the means to protect these
sites and bring them to fruition.

Cable Advisory Committee
The Cable Advisory Committee meets monthly for the purpose of monitoring the cable
service and the license compliance of the current cable service provider in Lunenburg.
In 1987, the primary task of the Committee was to evaluate the request by Cablevision
Systems of Woodbury, N.Y. to assume the cable license held by Adams Russell Cable Systems
since 1981. The Committee members examined the documents provided by Cablevision,
requested additional information, consulted with the State Cable Commission, conferred
with Cable Advisory Committees from other towns, conducted a publicized open hearing, and
held several meetings to assess all the data collected. The Committee concluded that
there was no evidence to suggest that Cablevision would be unable to carry out the
provisions of the license held by Adams Russell Cable System. The Committee did note that
Cablevision made several commitments that would enhance the quality of the cable service
in the Town. These commitments included the following:
1. Wiring additional areas of the Town although no promise was made to wire the
entire town.
2. Upgrading the system to include 54 or 55 channels.
3. Rebuilding 1988. the cable system to accommodate the additional channels by September
4. Studying the need for additional channels that would be more attractive to
Lunenburg residents.
5. Heeding the needs of the elderly.
6. Training high school students on the operation of video equipment so that more
local programming can be developed.
7. Responding to customer complaints within 24 hours.
8. Freezing of rates for as long as possible.
As a result, the Committee members voted to recommend that the Selectmen accept the
transfer of the cable license from Adams Russell Cable Services to Cablevision Systems
Corporation. Subsequently, the Selectmen approved the license transfer.
Any resident who has a concern about the cable service provided the Town is
encouraged to contact the Committee members or to attend the monthly meetings. The
members of the Committee wish to thank the Board of Selectmen, and the personnel in the
Town Hall for their help and cooperation during the past year.

Police Department
The Lunenburg Police Department for the last 2 - 3 years has been finding it
increasingly hard to keep up with the calls we receive. This is due to the lack of
manpower and monies in the budget. Please remember that this is your Police Department
and that we are committed to providing a safe community for you to live in. We are
particularly sensitive to helping the elderly and young people of our Town.
The Lunenburg Police Department is seriously undermanned. Presently, we run with two
officers assigned per shift. With the Town experiencing continued growth, this manpower
level is extremely low. There has been no increase in manpower in over eight years. Other
towns in our population category have generally 3 - 6 more men on the Department and most
of these towns do not border two cities nor do they have three recreational lakes and an
amusement park which draw thousands of people in the summer.
The investigative and service demands have increased to the point where at times it
is hard to provide even minimal service. I ask you to please support my request for more
personnel. At the very least, we should have a program of adding one or two additional
officers per year over the next couple of years.
Again, I thank the citizens of Lunenburg for their support and I thank all the
elected and appointed officials in Town who I feel have been very supportive of the Police

replacing apparatus, as we have trucks that are over 20 years old.
As you can see from the number of calls, we are a very busy department. As the Town
rapidly grows, we can only expect to be even busier. We must make every effort to keep
fire protection ahead of this growth. We have to make sure that we have reliable
apparatus and adequate manpower to protect the Town. Tax dollars that are spent on public
safety are wisely spent. We ask for and appreciate the support that the Townspeople give
to the Fire Department.
The most critical problems that we now face are: 1. Having enough money budgeted
for operating the Fire Department. 2. Replacement of apparatus on a timely basis and the
purchase of a ladder truck. 3. The construction of a fire station that will adequately
house our apparatus and give us the needed room to operate. These are areas that have to
be met if we are going to continue to offer adequate fire protection to a growing
community.
I would like to take this opportunity to thank all Town Officials for their help and
assistance during the past year. Also I want to thank all our firefighters and ambulance
personnel for their dedication and work to make this Department what it is today.

Conservation Commission
The year 1987 has been one of the busiest for the Conservation Commission. The job
of the Conservation Commission is to protect our Town's wetlands through the enforcement
of the State Wetlands Protection Act. It is important to stress that the goal of the
Commission is to work with developers and builders to assure that a project can go forward
in a manner that protects valuable wetlands. It is not the Conservation Commission's duty
or goal to stop development, but only to insure that it occurs in a responsible and safe
manner with regard to the protection of water resources.
The primary accomplishment of 1987 has been the drafting and passage of a local
Wetlands Protection By-Law. This By-Law will give the Commission the ability to build
upon the existing State Wetlands Protection Act and will go into effect in the spring of
1988 when the regulations to support it are finalized and approved at a public hearing.
Throughout the year, the Commission has continued to monitor the Houghton's Mill and
Lake Shore Village developments to assure protection of wetlands on these sites. In
addition, several new developments were reviewed and appropriate Order of Conditions
issued. The Commission wishes to continue to stress the importance of protecting wetlands
because of the need for clean water resources and the need for preserving wildlife
habitat.
In 1987 the Commission issued 5 Cease and Desist Orders and 42 Orders of Conditions,
held 48 hearings, conducted 141 site inspections, and provided 351 service calls.
The plan for improving the wildlife habitat on the Conservation lands, through a
forest management program, is in its second year of implementation. This plan will enable
the Commission to establish appropriate habitats that will support the stocking of game
birds and support greater numbers of existing species.
This year the Commission has identified several parcels of land that are worthy of
protection and which would make excellent conservation and recreation areas. Hopefully,
some of these parcels can be acquired by the Town in the near future.
The Commission wants to bring to the attention of the Townspeople the damage done to
Conservation lands by three and four-wheeled all-terrain vehicles (ATV). The purpose of
these lands is to provide open space for passive recreation opportunities and for the use
of snowmobiles on designated trails. Unfortunately, the use of ATV's inhibits hiking,
cross country skiing and snowmobiling by destroying and eroding trail surfaces.
Therefore, the Commission asks for cooperation in keeping these vehicles off Conservation
lands.
The Commission would like to thank the Boy Scouts and the Lunenburg High School
Nature Club for their maintenance work in the Cowdrey Nature Center, especially in the
rebuilding of bridges.
It is with regret that the Commission accepted the resignation of Chris Fors, Karl
Hanson and Joe Bilotta. Their contributions were much appreciated. The Commission
welcomes Rick Godin and Betsy Weaver as new members.
The Commission continues to urge the Townspeople to come forward with any
conservation questions and strongly encourages people who are initiating construction
projects to request a determination if they have any doubt as to whether the project falls
within areas protected by State and local wetland laws.

Building Committee
The Building Committee had a busy year in 1987, which included a personnel
reorganization, three architectural design projects, a construction contract for the
Police Station Lock-up Facility, and a continued discussion of the acute need for Town
Hall office space.
The Special Town Meeting in 1986 authorized a Space Needs Study at the Ritter
Memorial Library. The study calls for a three-story addition to the westerly side of the
main building. This proposal would add approximately 4,800 square feet of space to meet
the growing needs of the community. The estimated project cost is $900,000.
In addition to the need for more space, the Library's existing building is in need of
several remodeling and maintenance projects. The most pressing of these is the
replacement of the septic system. This repair was delayed for several months while
waiting for the findings of the Town Center Septic System Study Committee. The
Committee's report recommends a tie-in of several town-owned buildings with the school
septic system. However, this project has no funding and the Library system is now
failing. Therefore, the Building Committee studied two alternatives for a new on-site
system. Working with the Library Trustees, the Building Committee has determined that the
best available solution is to relocate the restrooms to the entry level and install a new
septic system in front of the building. This solution will minimize future maintenance of
the septic system. The new relocated restrooms can be integrated into the proposed future
addition.
The Police Station Lock-Up Facility moved closer to reality this year. Faced with
few alternatives because of the loss of space in Fitchburg and a State mandate that
requires each town to provide lock-up space, the 1987 Annual Town Meeting authorized
design and construction of three cells and necessary related improvements at the Police
Station. The Building Committee worked with the architect and Police Chief to develop a
plan that met all of the needs of the Lock-Up Facility and provided improved security at
the Station. Unfortunately, the construction bids received were much higher than the
monies appropriated at Town Meeting.
Therefore, the Building Committee and the Architect went back to the drawing board to
re-work the plan and provide only the bare minimum required to meet the Massachusetts
State Standards and to bring the project within budget. A construction contract was
awarded and the project should be completed by summer 1988. It is important to note that
the new jail cells will meet only the minimum standards. There is still a severe need for
security improvements to facilitate a satisfactory environment at the Police Station.
The Town Hall is an issue that the Building Committee feels is most urgently in need
of resolution. At the 1987 Annual Town Meeting, the Building Committee held a straw vote
to determine which of the four final proposals it had studied would be supported by the
Town. The proposals were as follows: A. Move existing Town Hall Building to Memorial
Drive, build a new addition and renovate the existing building. (173 votes) B. Convert
the Primary School Annex to Town Hall. (11 votes) C. Construct new Town Hall Building
on Memorial Drive. (61 votes); D. Renovate and reconstruct existing Town Hall (306
votes). The Building Committee recommended Option A to the Town as its choice for a
facility that would accommodate the future growth needs of the Town and preserve the
existing historic Town Hall building at a cost comparable to the other options. However,
the straw vote results indicated a majority opinion in favor of Option D. The Building
Committee has reviewed the 1986 Space Needs Study by Hammer, Kiefer and Todd Architects,
and determined that this is a viable option for providing Town Hall space to meet the
existing needs of the Town. It does not address future growth needs.
The Building Committee will, at the 1988 Annual Town Meeting, request that the Town
of Lunenburg appropriate monies for the preparation of Architectural and Engineering
Design Plans to renovate and reconstruct the existing Town Hall. These plans, once
complete, will provide a comprehensive solution to the deplorable conditions existing
presently at the Town Hall.

been donated.

case in the lobby of the Town Hall, which is being used for various announcements of
Council activities.

Lunenburg Historical Commission
The Lunenburg Historical Commission meets regularly on the second Wednesday of each
month in the Historical Society Building. For the past two years, we have been
researching and compiling an inventory of old buildings and other sites around the Town
Center, preparing for application as a National Historic District. This application was
made last year through the Massachusetts Historical Commission, and has been approved by
the State. Things seem to move slowly on the national level, but we expect to hear
favorably from the National Historic Register in the spring of 1988. When such acceptance
is definite, the Lunenburg Historical Commission will turn its attention to implementation
of the placement of markers designating the limits of the District, and assistance to
property owners who might desire plaques on individual buildings within the District.
The Lunenburg Historical Commission also supported the successful effort to preserve
and (in the future) restore the present Town Hall, which will, of course, be within the
Lunenburg Center National Historic District.

Mont. Regional Vocational Technical School District
The year 1987 was a milestone in accomplishments for the Montachusett Regional
Vocational Technical School. of the 163 graduates in May, 76% obtained employment; 6%
joined the military; and 10% opted for higher education at various institutions. The
1987-1988 school year opened with a consistent enrollment of 1,014 students with a dynamic
and challenging curriculum which was initiated with the employment of a new Curriculum

constituents in the District, an improved and expanded evening continuing education

adults to meet the ever-changing skill requirements of the work force.

be useful throughout their lives, Monty Tech arranges to have speakers address assemblies
and workshops on a wide range of subjects. Attorneys, judges, sports figures,
environmentalists, career-oriented experts, Vietnam veterans, and many other speakers add
interest to the classes at Monty Tech.
Twenty-Seven Monty Tech students competed in the Vocational Industrial Clubs of

and qualified for the State V.I.C.A. competitions. Shelley Abell, of the Dental Assistant
Program, went on to the nationals, and received a certificate for placing in the top 25%
of the competitors.

have been made this year which compared to forty-nine placements at this time last year.
Placements in the carpentry, electrical, and plumbing areas are again the highest.
Many students and instructors donated their time and expertise to the Lunenburg Kids'
Kingdom in October. This is an example of a project ideally suited to the talents at our
school.

Town Clerk, Tax Collector and Treasurer
1987 marks my fifth annual report as Town Clerk, Tax Collector and Treasurer.
This written report is followed by important financial information regarding Fiscal Year
1987 as well as the minutes of Town Meetings and Elections. I also wish to thank other
town departments for working with my offices through continuing communication.
Lunenburg is once again faced with many pressures that are related to growth in
both the Massachusetts economy and our population. That impacts on governmental services
including responsibilities under my direction. Restructuring the Town's government has
been offered as part of the solution to these concerns. However, at the Annual Town
Election in May, the voters sent a clear signal that the proposed charter was not wanted.
Of those voting at the largest turnout for a town election, 61% were against the charter.
The proponents have returned the charter for another vote at the 1988 Town Election,
although nothing has changed over the year. The elimination of the elected positions of
Tax Collector and Treasurer will further isolate the public from government. Through the
establishment of a Town Manager form of government with many appointed officials, the
balance of power will become too centralized. Voters should think twice about what will
be lost before they vote.
With an appropriation of $5,000, the Computer Study Committee was able to begin
the process of computerization of the Town Offices. With the purchase of an IBM AT
compatible personal computer, printer and software for word processing and spreadsheets,
my staff has begun the learning process and has adapted many of our current operations to
the software. A course in computer management that I took during the fall hastened the
learning process. However, more terminals, memory and software are needed to update
Lunenburg's financial operations.
Treasurer
The Uniform Municipal Accounting System (UMAS) was first in effect for the
entire 1987 fiscal year. The Massachusetts Department of Revenue has reviewed our chart
of accounts, recommended a few changes and approved our system. The conversion and
maintenance has been accomplished at no cost to the town through assistance of the town's
independent auditors Melanson and Greenwood of New Hampshire. As UMAS is a coordinated
numbering system of accounting, it will be invaluable when we put our financial operations
on computer. For instance, we will be able to search our records and determine how much
the town is spending on utilities or wages.
Over the last few years, voters at Town Meeting have been reducing the amount
spent on capital needs in favor of current operations, as well as dipping into our savings
accounts such as free cash, stabilization fund and the reserve fund. However, once these
are depleted, the town will have to make cuts in services. Those cuts should not be
delayed if it means further eliminating healthy reserves. Once these savings accounts are
gone, they are gone forever. In light of this concern, I have been working with the
Executive Secretary, Jane Mahony, to develop a long-term capital equipment planning
process combining the use of debt and the stabilization fund. It will succeed only if
voters support efforts to adequately fund capital needs.
Tax Collector
The Assessors are currently using the Computer Aided Municipal Appraisal (CAMA)
system for valuation of real estate in town. Other tax collectors and I have met with the
Massachusetts Department of Revenue to provide recommendations as they develop software
for the use on the tax collector's side of the CAMA system. It is hoped that Lunenburg
may be a site for testing the new system, possibly as early as fiscal year 1989.
Property taken into tax title at the end of fiscal year 1987 amounted to the
smallest number of parcels in recent years. This was due to the extra work put into tax
collection in the months of May and June. By maintaining our 100% tax collection rate, we
assured that the town has a healthy cash flow, thereby avoiding the need for temporary
borrowing and the additional costs involved.

Registrar of Voters
The Board of Registrars continued in its efforts to register to vote any and all
eligible citizens. This has included special voter registration sessions before state and
local elections, cooperating with the High School Civics class by registering students at
the High School, and going out to the households of handicapped persons. Anyone who is
unable to get to Town Hall should call to arrange to be registered by the registrars.
The new precinct arrangement will be in effect for 1988, resulting from the 1986
state census figures. There will be added costs involved due to the need for additional
personnel to operate the election. This should be offset by some of the savings from the
electronic voting devices.
The Board also is responsible working with the Town Clerk on the annual street
listing and census. The information obtained in this process is vital to the operations
of the police and fire departments, as well as other town boards and offices. Other
duties include holding hearings on the eligibility of persons to be voters in town and for
working to set up and run all elections. The Board consists of Karen Kemp, Sophia Mortis,
and George Rollins, Sr. Robert Ebersole serves as Clerk of the Board.

MAY 9, 1987 SPECIAL TOWN MEETING AND 1987 ANNUAL TOWN KEETING
Saturday, May 9, 1987
The meeting was called to order at 9:00 A.M. by Town Moderator Richard J. Bishop, a quorum having been declared present. The meeting pledged allegiance
to the United States Flag. The call to and closing of the warrant were read by Town Clerk, Robert J. Bbersole.
The meeting gave a round of applause for Executive Secretary Barry Stell who is leaving for other employment.
Matthew Pearson, 57 Page Street presented a plaque to Nancy Sholl, 276 Northfield Road, in appreciation of her years of service on the Conservation
Commission and Planning Board.
David Rodgers, 82 Highland Street was recognized and asked that the Town recognize the years of service of the following persons who have retired from
employment from the Town: Emily Foster 15 years Library; Rachel Niemi 12 years Treasurer's office; Hazel Lacoss 18 years Cafeteria; Bila Hallfors 33
years Teacher; Raymond Sideleau 6 years School Custodian; and Walter Keeler 25 years various town boards, nost recently 3 years Board of Selectmen.
At 9:15 A.M. it was voted to recess the Annual Meeting and take up the Special Town Meeting Warrant.

ARTICLE 3. To see if the Town will vote to amend Article XV of the Town's By-laws entitled "Salary Administration Plan of the Town of Lunenburg*by adding
thereto a new Section ,25 which shall read as follows:
"SECTION 25. DISCIPLINE POLICY
Items which constitute grounds for disciplinary action against an employee include: chronic absenteeism or tardiness, insubordination or refusal to work,
infraction of work and safety rules, threat of physical violence against fellow employees, use of profane language to a supervisor or fellow employee, or
offenses of comparable gravity or severity.
Upon determination by the Department Head that disciplinary action is warranted, the following procedures shall be implemented:
STEP 1. 1st Offense - Written warning.
STEP 2. 2nd Offense - A formal written reprimand notifying the employee of a one day suspension and that one further offense shall result in a three day
suspension without pay.
STEP 3. 3rd Offense - Employee will receive an immediate three day suspension without pay and a written notification that any further offense shall be
grounds for dismissal.
STEP 4. 4th Offense - Immediate dismissal of the employee.
The offense for which disciplinary action is taken may be any combination of the items described above. For example, offense number one could be
tardiness, offense number two could be absenteeism, and offense number three could be refusal to work or insubordination. If an employee goes twelve
months without a further offense, the first offense beyond twelve months shall be disciplined at the level of step one.
The following infractions are grounds for the immediate dismissal of an employee for just cause by the Department Head. Just cause may include, but shall
not be limited to the following:
Willful neglect or non-performance of one or more assigned duties;
Demonstrated incompetence in the performance of one or more assigned duties;
Behavior that seriously interferes with the normal operation of the municipality, the department, or any member of the workforce;
Insubordination, which shall mean a refusal to carry out a direct order:
Dishonesty in the performance of assigned duties;
Chronic absenteeism or tardiness without reasonable excuse;
Unauthorized possession or use of alcohol or an unprescribed controlled substance during any period of assigned work;
Municipal theft.", or act anything thereon. (Submitted by Personnel Committee) VOTED UNANINOUSLY.
ARTICLE 4. To see if the Town will vote to amend Article XV of the Town's By-laws entitled "Salary Administration Plan of the Town of Lunenburg* by
deleting the last sentence in the first paragraph under SECTION 19 SICK LEAVE, and inserting in place thereof the following sentence: "Use of such sick
leave must be occasioned by sickness or injury.", or act anything thereon. (Submitted by Petition) VOTED UNANIMOUSLY.
