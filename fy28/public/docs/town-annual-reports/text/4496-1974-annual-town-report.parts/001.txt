Board of Selectmen: left to right - Sherwood G. Winchester, Chairman Rudolph Masella, and Normand J. Thompson.
It is the privilege of the Board of Selectmen to bring to the attention of the residents of Lunenburg certain
events which have taken place during 1974--events which affect not only the year 1974, but also many years to
come, These events were distinguished because they were deliberations by groups of interested citizens in addition
to deliberations by official Town Committees. This voluntary participation of interested citizens in addition to the
official Committees is a most healthy development and should be encouraged. It is only through concerned action
by all people that Lunenburg can have a healthy, progressive and constructive growth.
It is unnecessary to list in detail the events of the past year on a day to day basis, and even to list capital
projects, as these. are all covered by the reports of the many Town Committees and Town Department Heads elsewhere
in this Report.
However, there is one thing that should be underlined, and that is the growing cooperation between the various
Town Committees. This kind of cooperation can lead to the elimination of duplication of efforts with an ultimate
savings of Town funds. The Board of Selectmen suggests that in reading the 1974 Town Report, one carefully notes
the cooperation of Committees. The Board suggests that in 1975 a series of workshops be set up where all elected and
appointed officials can meet to mutually discuss their obligations and responsibilities. Such study groups could con-
tribute greatly to efficiency in government.
As more services are demanded to meet the modern concepts of a progressive and expanding community, costs
must inevitably rise. The Board of Selectmen feels that the rising costs for all services in all departments must be
given their rightful priority. With each passing year the operation of government requires greater effort on the part
of all charged with public responsibility.
As Selectmen, we can certainly utilize constructive thoughts and suggestions to aid and assist this office in the
proper promotion of our community. It has always been and remains the desire of the Board of Selectmen to improve
all local functions in order to give our citizens the best possible services at the least possible cost.

Board of Assessors
The Board of Assessors in line with our policy of maintaining the program established by the revaluation of
1970, has continued to annually view each parcel of property in Town. In view of the decision of the State Supreme
Court that all property in the Commonwealth will be valued at 100% of full and fair market value, the Assessors can,
using a percentage multiplier, raise or lower the valuations of all property in the Town to reflect the affects of in-
flation or recession on the value of property. Contrary to the views expressed in several recent newspaper articles
that enforcement of this decision will cost the taxpayers a huge sum of tax dollars to upgrade, your Assessors wish
to assure the voters that this process can be completed by the existing staff at no additional cost to the Town.
The total value of Real Estate has increased to $52,009,970.00 in 1974 as compared to $51,014,700.00 in
1973. Personal property valuation increased to $2,908,970.00 in 1974 as compared to $2,712, 180.00 in 1973.
We find the increases are due to new construction, additions and conversion of summer cottages to year-round homes
in the lake areas. The Board was pleased to announce the $44.00 tax rate for Fiscal Year 1975 and feels that this
reduction of $7.00 demonstrates that an effective effort by all the voters can hold down expenditures in an infla-
tionary economy.
Mrs. Ine 1. Pihlaja was hired to do the clerical work for the Assessors' Office filling the vacancy created by
the resignation of Mrs. Joan E. Souliere. Mrs. Pihlaja formerly worked in the Tax Collector's Office for fourteen
years. Mrs. Gloria J. Knight, a former part-time employee in the Assessors' Office, was hired to work full-time
under the Emergency Employment Act at no cost to the Town.
The clerical staff has had considerable new and additional work this year, including coding of all property as
to residential, forest, agricultural, commercial or industrial status in order to start the Chapter 61A Agricultural
Land Program. Under this program, agricultural or horticultural land can be so designated, if qualified, thus re-
sulting in considerable savings. This program, while very beneficial to the career farmer or fruit grower, involves
severe penalties for selling land for other purposes during the first ten years of the program.
The Board of Assessors wishes to remind taxpayers of certain exemptions that have been enacted concerning
the value of residential property, owned and occupied as a domicile by persons in various groups for whom the pay-
ment of taxes could constitute an undue burden. These exemptions may not exceed certain specified amounts. If
a taxpayer feels that he qualifies for any of the following exemptions, he should contact the Assessors' Office and
request an application which lists all the requirements that must be met in order to qualify.
Section 17 - Widows, minors whose fathers are deceased, and persons over 70 - $2,000 in valuation or a tax of
$175, whichever is greater.
Section 22 - Disabled or wounded veterans, or their wives, widows or parents $2,000 to $10,000 or a tax from
$175 to $875, whichever is greater, depending on degree of disability.
Section 37 - Blind persons - $5,000 valuation or a tax of $437.50, whichever is greater.
Section 41 - Persons aged 70 or over - $4,000 valuation or a tax of $350, whichever is greater.
Section 42 - Unmarried widows of police officers or firefighters killed in the line of duty $8,000 valuation or a
tax of $700, whichever is greater.
Section 43 - Minor orphans of police officers or firefighters killed in the line of duty - $8,000 in valuation or a
tax of $700, whichever is greater.
Tax deferral for certain persons 65 years of age or over. (This isa new addition to the law whichallows
Chapter 59, a property owner over 65 years of age with gross receipts of under $20,000 annually to place the taxes
Section 5 on his domicile and one acre of land on a deferred payment plan, the taxes to be paid upon settlement
Clause 41A of the estate plus 8% interest.)
The Board of Assessors wishes to reiterate that the Board and the clerical staff are available to any taxpayer
requiring assistance or information. We wish to thank the citizens of Lunenburg for the cooperation shown to US
throughout the year.

Park Commission
The Summer Recreation Program ran very smoothly under the leadership of our very able Director, Richard
Stachowicz. The Muscular Dystrophy Fair netted more money than ever before-$174.00, thanks to our two fine
Playground Supervisors, Gail Okerman and Barbara Gervais. Youngsters spent a number of hours working on Arts
and Crafts under the careful tutelage of Kimberly Craffey and Stephanie Markham. Some of the highlights were an
inter-playground bike race, a trip to the Alice G. Wallace Planetarium, the Cinema, picnic-swim at Willard Brook,
a trip to Benson's Animal Farm, bubble gum and watermelon hunts, etc.
We also support a number of other programs such as the High School Basketball League, Junior League Street
Hockey and Basketball under the fine supervision of James Harley and Alan Cartwright, Morning League Baseball
with Steven Esposito and Alan Cartwright handling the reins, Men's Softball League and Girl's Softball under the
skillful handling of Chuck Winneberg.
Mr. John Therrien conducteda very successful Tennis Program this past summer. The adult group for beginners
only was very well attended. The program concentrated on basic fundamentals and technique. John cited the need
for additional courts, for increasing the adult program to two nights a week, and for lessons at Wallis Park in the
Whalom area.
The Town Beach under the experienced and capable direction of Gerald Beloin was a beehive of activity.
The lifeguards as usual were very dependable and always "on the alert". Robert Clarke initiated Water Polo this
past summer. It is hoped that with advanced publicity this program will be more successful next summer. The Swim
Program reached a number of youngsters--over 500 to be exact. This program includes life saving also. The Tiny
Tot Program (24-36 months old) and the Adult Swim Program were very worthwhile. June Riddle is in charge of the
Swim Program and is a real asset to the Town. The Lunenburg Red Cross has assisted with these programs for a number
of years.
We are very proud of the appearance of all of our facilities thanks to the dedicated supervision of Robert
Garneauand the very capable assistance of Andre Garneau and Richard Harper. These people save the Townspeople
a lot of tax dollars each year.
Anumber of projects have been completed this past year. The chain link fence was completed at Lesure Field.
Concrete slabs have been poured for the baseball team benches at Marshall Park. The back-stop at Marshall Park
was painted as was the fence at Wallis Park. The undergrowth at Lesure Field was cleared out, greatly improving
the appearance of this facility. The "Quonset Hut" at Wallis Park has been demolished and the retaining wall at
the Town Beach was not completed because of insufficient funds. We plan to supplement this with $3,000.00.
We also help in a number of other programs such as: Pop Warner Football, Junior Basketball, Open-Gym in
the winter, Teen-Age Basketball, Young Men's Basketball, skating at Marshall Pond, AAU Track Meets, Men's
Golf League and Couples' Scotch Foursome Golf League.
There are several projects "in the wind" for another year. We needa new tractor for mowing and other chores.
Additional tennis courts are badly needed and it is hoped that four such courts can be constructed on land adjacent
to the High School parking lot. In a period of austerity and limited travel it is hoped that more Townspeople will
turn to a more localized form of recreation. There is a need for a good softball field and one or two more baseball
fields. All of these projects depend upon your support. If you feel that they are worthwhile, please give US your
support.

Any report on the conversion by all of the Cities and Towns in the Commonwealth from a calendar year to a
fiscal year would most certainly contain SOME GOOD NEWS and SOME BAD NEWS.
In Lunenburg, the GOOD NEWS is - that in 1974 we did not have to borrow in anticipation of revenue (in
1973 we borrowed $1,050,000 with interest totalling $18,804); that we earned $55,668 worth of interest on Rev-
enue Cash Invested (in 1973 we earned only $6,372 or $12,432 less than interest paid out); that the net effect of
the difference in terms of savings on next year's tax rate will be $1.20; and that, in general, property owners seem
to favor paying their real estate taxes on a semi-annual basis.
The BAD NEWS is that although raccounting is now on a fiscal basis, the Town Report is still on an annual
one; that there is considerably more processing and bookkeeping involved in the double billing system; that record
property owners as of the previous January 1 must be billed for the tax year commencing in July and ending in June;
that, as a result, the benefits of abatements granted to such record owners are more apt to inure to the benefit of the
wrong person-the new owners; and that for this reason and because thebills themselves are complicated, taxpayers
are finding the double billing confusing and difficult to understand. Bills are now being filed with the Legislature
which would allow for two distinctly separate real estate tax bills per year.

Motor Vehicle Excise billing continues to be a source of annoyance to Taxpayers and Tax Officials alike. An
examination of excise bills issued by the State Department of Motor Vehicles has shown that approximately 25% of
themare in error. The resulting confusion and inconvenience to vehicle owners and to the municipality which processes
these bills is immeasurable, as is the cost involved. City and Town officials throughout the State are calling for a
complete revision and correction of the system presently employed by the Registry of Motor Vehicles.
A particular problem with the Excise exists in the Town of Lunenburg due to the fact that a large percentage of
our residents have a Fitchburg mailing address. This means that the forms completed when a vehicle is registered show
Lunenburg as the placeof principal garaging but also bear a Fitchburg address. All too often the Registry carelessly
processes such bills to Fitchburg and the Fitchburg Board of Assessors and/or Tax Collector don't get too upset about
collecting the money which should rightfully be offsetting Lunenburg's Real Estate Tax Rate. If you should receive
a bill from the City of Fitchburg on a vehicle which is garaged in Lunenburg--DON'T PAY IT! Instead, contact the
Fitchburg Board of Assessors, advise them that you live and garage your car in Lunenburg and request that the Fitch-
burg bill be abated and sent to Lunenburg. You will benefit in two ways. The excise paid to Lunenburg will save
you money by offsetting the amounts which would otherwise be raised in Real Estate Taxes, and you will have longer
to pay the bill as it will not be overdue until 60 days after it is reissued by the Town of Lunenburg. The $302,792
in Motor Vehicle and Trailer Excise collected in calendar 1973 reduced the 1975 fiscal year tax rate by $5.50 per
thousand

The Board was involved with five subdivisions in 1974. One new subdivision, Heritage Hill off West and

Two new subdivisions have been submitted--one "Laurelwood Realty Trust" on Chase Road was submitted as a
preliminary plan and contains nine lots. The final Definitive Plan has not as yet been approved. The other new
subdivision is called "Prescott Place" and is located off Leominster Road north of Kilburn Street and contains fifteen
lots all of which, according to plans, will contain two-family rental homes. A final Definitive Plan was submitted
in early December, but upon advice of the Planning Board, the owner decided to recall it in its entirety and plans
to resubmit in the future.
The last subdivision, which involved the court case, was Whalom Estates. It is located on West Street and
Hollis Road. The plan which was approved in 1960 contained 195 lots, many of which were less than one-half acre
in size. Twenty-nine lots were developed some years ago. The Board, upon reviewing the remaining subdivision,
recommended that it be upgraded to current zoning and subdivision by-law standards and, consequently, rescinded
the 1960 approval. Subsequently the Town was taken to courtand the court held that the subdivision, before further
development, must be brought up to the current standards as defined in the Protective By-law.

Library Trustees
Lunenburg is using its Libraryl As usual, the books, magazines, records and films circulated at a very pro-
lific rate over the past year. Adult books went out 17,658 times, children's books 23, ,094, magazines 214, and
recordings 424. Interlibrary loans numbered 173, and 104 films were lent. The amazing thing, though, is that peo-
ple are beginning to view the Library as much more than just a book place. They now see it as a place to learn,
no matter what the means. They meet here to discuss such diverse topics as drugs, conservation, crafts and tran-
scendentalism. They come to gaze at art exhibits, to join in craft displays, to enjoy a sing-along or an afternoon
tea, or to enjoy a movie they might not get the opportunity to see elsewhere.
Much of the credit for this lively interest in the Ritter Memorial Library is due to the efforts of the "Friends
of the Library", both for last year's revitalization of the meeting room and for their planning of many stimulating
events. Upcoming is a series of "interest" lectures and demonstrations by authorities in many fields.
Along with regular activities, we hope in the coming year to update our holdings in important areas. Also,
we plan to prepare interesting Bicentennial displays and to increase information on learning disabilities. We also
hope to reach people who for a number of reasons cannot get to the Library, and to obtain some audio-visual equip-
ment for those who cannot attain the information they need in regular book form.
Of the programs already existing, some are noteworthy because of their great success. The Children's Story
Hours have enjoyed nearly perfect attendance and enthusiasm by both the children and their parents. The films shown
at the Library have attracted consistently good turnouts. The Film Society, sponsored by the Library, has done an
outstanding job on both the selection and promotion of first-rate films, with people from outside this immediate lo-
cale remarking on the fine offerings.
All in all, things are certainly happening at the Library. There is a new Librarian and Assistant Librarian,
who along with the rest of the staff, hope that the residents of Lunenburg will continue to support, use and enjoy
the wealth of information and entertainment they can find at the Ritter Memorial Library.

250th Anniversary Committee
The Committee to plan the 250th Anniversary of the Town of Lunenburg was expanded to seven members in
1974, and all are involved in planning an observance of the Town's 250th Anniversary which will occur in 1978.
The actual date of incorporation as a Town was August 1, 1728. As it looks now, not only will this date be ob-
served in some fashion, but a series of events will be held during 1978.
The calendar for the observance is in the process of being finalized. Plans include a Kick-Off Dance com-
bined with a Winter Carnival in the opening months of 1978, a Celebration Week sometime in the late summer, and
a Ball to be held after Labor Day, The Lunenburg Rotary Club has undertaken the sponsorship of the Ball, and it is
hoped that all Town organizations will follow suit to conduct some of the activities.
The Historical Society is working actively on the writing of Lunenburg's History which is to be published.
From this, a pageant will be staged and will be presented sometime during the 250th Program. If interest warrants,
both a foot and mounted militia will be formed.
Funds for permanent markers for the Town's oldest houses and major historical sites will be sought in anarti-
cle requested for insertion in the warrant for the 1975 Annual Town Meeting. In 1976, a budget will be prepared
and voters will be asked to approve funds for the celebration. All of the meetings of the 250th Anniversary Com-
mittee are open to those interested in participating, or who have ideas on what should be included.

Conservation Commission - Town Forest Committee
The Conservation Commission and Town Forest Committee have four major goals and responsibilities: 1. To
set aside at least 4,000 of the Town's nearly 20,000 acres for the purpose of protecting and preserving our Town's
character and natural resources; 2. To develop these areas with recreational, educational, and aesthetic programs,
including fishing and hunting in designated areas; 3. To conduct investigations and to hold public hearings related
to the Wetlands Protection Act; and 4. To help other groups in the community concerned with air pollution, water
pollution, roadside beautification, and other projects in natural areas. To these ends, the Conservation Commission
has, during 1974:
A. Asked chases. for, and received from the voters at Town Meeting, a sum of $50,000.00 to be used for land pur-
B. Through a self-help program approved by the Department of Natural Resources acquired a parcel of 30
acres off West Townsend Road formerly owned by Mrs. Dhu Johnson.
C. Accepted a gift of land from General Builders Supply, Inc. This wooded lot is located in the Rangeley
Road/West Street area.
D. Recommended that Lancaster Avenue, Mulpus Road, Northfield Road, Cross Street, Holman Street, and
Elmwood Road be designated as Scenic Roads.
E. Continued the Naturalist-At-Large Program, which each month guides Townspeople on Town-owned
and private properties and provides interpretive talks at each. In Maya record number of 75 persons hiked
into Woodruff Meadow, the first tour to be held on privately owned land.
F. Assisted elementary, middle, and high school teachers and students using Town-owned lands for field
trips and worked with the Ritter Memorial Library in preparing a display entitled "Tips for Outdoor Ex-
plorers".
G. Continued to serve as the regulatory agency of the Massachusetts Wetlands Protection Act, conducting
two public hearings. The Commission also issued six requests for compliance, conducted six site inspec-
tions, and issued one cease and desist order.
Other events which mark the year 1974 include: Acceptance of the Worcester County Conservation District
OUTSTANDING CONSERVATION COMMISSION Award for our programs in 1973; Investigation into wetlands map-
ping and zoning; Vandalism on Town property has continued to be a problem such as fires, broken bottles and cans
strewn over trails, destruction of signs and fences, which give sorry testimony to respect for the common lands. As
a safety measure, the old building at the Ben Normand area and the old Boy Scout cabin off Gilchrest Street have
been scheduled to be razed.
Mr. Jeffrey Dodge and Mrs. Connie Fleming have been appointed as Associate Members to assist US in re-
search, site visits, property supervision and clerical work. A fund has been established in the memory of Mr. Robert
Burton, Lunenburg resident and sportsman, by his friends and family.
The Conservation Commission and Town Forest Committee are struggling in a race with time. Open spaces
are less and less conspicuous in Lunenburg, squeezed between the tax bill and the developer's shovel. More con-
struction means fewer wildlife, fewer trails for hiking and skiing, fewer fields for hunting, and fewer woodland
streams for fishing.
To preserve the quality of our life and our "greenbelt" character, we must be able to keep up with the many
small feet of progress. Our woodlands, our wetlands, our waterways, our wildlife, and our people all need room
to breathe.

Development & Industrial Commission
Another year has passed with limited activity by the Development and Industrial Commission--based primarily
upon the fact that whatever inquiries were madeby either industry or developers resulted in no further discussion once
the matter of an adequate water supply was discussed. The plans this Commission formulated for limited industrial
growth in small parks away from the population centers as a means to broaden our tax base may never be realized so
long as water remains a problem.
We all know what a difficult time the Water District has had regarding expansion. The District hasbeen turned
down repeatedly in its attempt to improve service for its present users. As each new user is added, and they must be
according to law, service will eventually suffer. As each day passes, the cost to make any improvements is accel-
erating. The Development and Industrial Commission feels this area needs special attention now. We can no longer
afford to be like an ostrich with its head in the sand. It is not so unrealistic to consider facing a problem of water
rationing in the future, especially during a dry period. The drought of a few years ago cannot be overlooked, es-
pecially when there are developers approaching the Town now with the intent to build more new homes and many of
these would be located within the Water District.
For these reasons the Development and Industrial Commission will propose at Town Meeting a Water Study Com-
mittee be immediately set up to review the Town's water supply so that there is an overall plan developed for the
future. This plan, complete with timetables and estimated costs where possible, must clearly define the best alter-
natives open to the Town and/or Water District, so that the District's users and the Town's taxpayers are afforded
maximum protection and service.
