Retirements
EFFECTIVE AUGUST 29, 1974 - MR. ALFRED A, MORIN
"Fred", as he was known to his co-workers, began as a Junior Custodian in the Passios Elemen-
tary School on October 5, 1970. Although his tenure with the school system was short, he gave
conscientious and commendable service in the performance of his duties as one of the "housekeepers"
of the Passios School.
EFFECTIVE AUGUST 31, 1974 - MR. RICHARD H. DUSTIN
"Dick" was elected by the Lunenburg School Committee on
July 30, 1962, as Assistant Principal of the Passios Elemen-
tary School, with duties to begin August 20, 1962. Prior to
Lunenburg, he had had previous experience of eighteen years--
2 years in Wells, Maine; 3 years in Plainfield, Mass. and 13
years (the last 6 as Supervising Principal) in Middlefield,
Conn. Dick served in the U. S. Army from 1941 - 1946 with the
Signal Corps with rank of Major in charge of the Signal Center
in New Delhi, India, for the China/Burma/India theater of
operation.
On June 20, 1966, the Lunenburg School Committee elected
Dick to the position of Principal, replacing Tom Passios who
had resigned to join the Department of Education. He served
in this capacity until his retirement effective August 31,
1974. In a letter sent to Dick on December 13, 1973, the
Lunenburg School Committee expressed their sincere apprecia-
tion for his positive leadership during his twelve years in Lunenburg and commented on his contri-
butions to the growth and current quality of the elementary school.
On Sunday, November 3, 1974, the community tendered a reception for Dick in the Passios
Elementary School, at which time a brief program was conducted with gift presentations made by the
Lunenburg 4-H Club and the Reception Committee. As a result of one of the gifts, Dick has spent
his first months of retirement in Hawaii and has been the envy of all of us this past snow-filled
winter.
EFFECTIVE DECEMBER 31, 1974 - MRS. DORIS M. LAHTI
Doris began her duties as Assistant part-time School
Nurse on March 15, 1954 when increased enrollment made it
necessary to hire an additional school nurse. Over the years,
she has assumed the position of Supervising Nurse and at the
time of her retirement, there were two other nurses working
under her direction.
An Open House is being planned for Doris for Sunday,
March 2, 1975, at the Turkey Hill Middle School, at which
time, the community and school personnel may express its
profound thanks and appreciation to this dedicated and con-
scientious lady for her outstanding service to the Town and
Schools for the past twenty-one years.

DEATHS DURING 1974
of former school employees
January 9, 1974 (in Philadelphia, Pennsylvania) - Miss BLANCHE E. LANCEY
Miss Lancey was born in Lunenburg, November 7, 1891, and was a teacher and principal in the
Lunenburg School System for forty-five years before her retirement. She was a graduate of Lunenburg
High School and the Fitchburg Normal School (now Fitchburg State College), as well as a piano
teacher for many years. She also taught in Hillsboro, NH; Saxton's River, VT; Townsend, MA; and
Ridgewood, NJ.
Blanche first began in Lunenburg in the old Center School building on November 9th, 1914 as
teacher of "A" intermediate. An additional room had to be opened at this level due to increased
enrollment. From September, 1926 to June, 1945, she was a teacher of Grade Four for three years,
Grade Five for two years; and Grade Six for fourteen years. From September 1945 to June 1953
(eight years), she continued as teacher of Grade Four, as well as assuming the duties of Principal
of the Center School. In September, 1953, Miss Lancey transferred to the new elementary school,
continuing her teaching duties at the Grade Four level. She remained in this position until her
retirement on June 30, 1958. Many residents and employees of the Town of Lunenburg were former
pupils of Miss Lancey and have many fond memories of their association with her during the years
she was a teacher In the Lunenburg Public Schools.
April 17, 1974 (in Fitchburg) - Mr. JOHN F. MUNRO
John was born in Fitchburg on October 20, 1916 and
attended schools there. He was an army veteran of World War
11 and served in the European Theater. He was awarded a
Purple Heart.
John first began as custodian in the Lunenburg School
System on December 16, 1953 when he was hired to fill a cus-
todial vacancy in the Center School where junior high school
grades were being housed. He remained in this building until
January 3, 1961, when he transferred to the Passios Elementary
School. On July 21, 1967, he transferred to the Senior High
School as a day man, replacing Mr. Al Balcom, who was retiring.
At the time of his death, John was a custodian at the Turkey
Hill Middle School.
Mr. Munro served the schools well with over twenty years
of dedicated and devoted service. John, though a quiet and
unassuming man, left his mark on the custodial operation and
his loss was felt by all.

School Lunch Program Report
by MARJORIE H. STILLMAN
School Lunch Supervisor
A sizable undertaking in this department was the remodeling of the T. C. Passios Elementary
School and Lunenburg High School kitchens. This was realized through 75% funding by the State
Department of Food Services. At the Passios School, a double convection oven, two-burner hot-top
stove, two-compartment steamer, three-section refrigerator, baking table and bins, pot washer,
proofer and small utensils were purchased. At the High School, a double convection oven, three-
compartment sink with pot washer, two-burner hot-top stove, and small utensils were acquired. At
both schools, walk-in freezers, surplus property donated from the State's phased-out food
assistance program, were installed. Thus, both kitchens were modernized and made more efficient
through local funds of $4266. and State funds of $12,798.
