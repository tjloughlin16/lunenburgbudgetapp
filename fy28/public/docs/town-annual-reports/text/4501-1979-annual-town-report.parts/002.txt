The decision to close schools is made by the Superintendent of Schools after consulting with
the Highway Superintendent and the Police Department to determine the condition of the roads.
The practice is to close schools only in case of extreme weather conditions or unforeseen
emergency; therefore, when schools are in session on stormy days, parents are urged to exercise
their personal judgment as to the wisdom of sending their children to schools.
The following schedule of No-School Signals will be in effect for the current school year:
TWO SETS of two (2) blasts on the horn at the Central Fire
Station indicates no school at the following times for the
groups indicated:
6:30 AM Grades 6 through 12
7:15 AM Grades Kindergarten through 5
10:00 AM PM Session, Kindergarten
Announcements will be carried on the regular broadcasts of Radio Stations WEIM and WFGL,
Fitchburg; WLMS, Leominster, as well as WTAG and WSRS (Stereo 96) in Worcester;and WBZ in Boston,
as promptly as the decision is made.
The cancelling of school sessions requires the completion of numerous separate telephone
calls as rapidly as possible. The crowding of telephone circuits can mean that the signal center
or bus contractors might not be notified on time. Parents can help by not allowing their children
to make unnecessary calls. Your cooperation and understanding will be appreciated by all con-
cerned.

WILLIAM C. ALLARD, Ed. D.
It is a privilege to submit my
Second Annual Report as Superintendent
of Schools in Lunenburg. This report
covers the year ending December 31,
1979.
As you read the reports of the
other administrators and principals,
you will receive a more detailed
accounting of the events that have
taken place in the schools. The pur-

the total aspects of the Lunenburg
Public Schools.
This past year has been for me
personally a very rewarding year. It
has provided me with the opportunity
to be involved with the staff and
supportive personnel on a continuing
basis. I have been most pleased by
this opportunity because it has
afforded me the chance to be more in-
formed and knowledgeable about the
entire school system. Although we do
not face many of the problems that
large school systems are facing during
these difficult times, we are experi-
encing the difficulties of a declining
enrollment and the rising inflation of
goods and salaries. Much can be said
of the support that this community
gives to its schools but the greatest
contribution is made by the students
who attend our schools. They are very
dedicated and sincere learners. All
of us associated with them on a daily

perseverance to utilize the talents of
our staff to its fullest in order to

fine achievements and thank their
parents for providing them with a
supportive environment.

- DECLINING ENROLLMENT AND REALLOCATION OF STAFF -
During the past year we have worked very closely with the Principals and the Lunenburg Edu-
cation Association in an attempt to reflect the results of a declining enrollment on our staff.
Last year we eliminated three (3) full-time teaching positions, a teaching aide and made some
consolidations in other positions within the school system. This year we are projecting at least
the elimination of two and one-half (2½) positions. Along with these reductions it is encumbered
upon us to continually look at the possibility of reallocation of our staff. We therefore may
have to set our schedules at the Middle School and the High School on the same schedule so that
we might share our staff between these two schools more effectively than under the present sched-
ules. We are thankful for the School Committee's willingness to understand this problem and to
leave it to the Superintendent to make recommendations in this area.

- LANGUAGE ARTS AND THE BASIC SKILLS IMPROVEMENT POLICY -
The Massachusetts Department of Education has produced a Basic Skills Improvement Policy
which affects all school systems in the Commonwealth. The Policy includes the major language arts
headings (reading, writing, listening, speaking) and mathematics. Although program plans for
reading and mathematics are not required until August 1980, with plans for listening and speaking
not required until August 1981, members of the Lunenburg School System spent much time in 1979 on
aspects of the Basic Skills Improvement Policy.

McCarthy, English Department Head, as chairman. Grade levels for testing students were established
using State Department Guidelines and decisions were made about procedures for implementing the
regulations.

be tested using the Stanford Achievement Tests (testing used for a number of years), and eighth
grade will be tested by the Massachusetts Assessment of Basic Skills - Reading. Other informal
measures will be used in addition to the Achievement Tests
writing will be tested in grades three, five and eight formally and in other grades

- EMPLOYING AIDES -
It has become necessary to employ additional aides to assist with the instruction of a few
severely involved children. Prior to the implementation of Chapter 766, these children would not
have received the benefits of regular school placement. Formerly, the children would have been
transported to other cities and, in some instances, live away from home and family. Because the
students require such individual attention, regular class teachers and special needs teachers need
aides when such a child is assigned to the class. We now have some children who are integrated in
regular classes largely through the excellent work of the aide.
- SCREENING FIRST GRADERS -
The motor (coordination) training program for young children is available in regular educa-
tion. Children who would gain from this service are identified through first grade screening.
This same screening enables the faculty to bring services to children in the areas of speech,
counseling, language and perceptual abilities at an early age.
- COUNSELING FOR SPECIAL NEEDS -
The major advance of this year is in the employment of special needs adjustment counselors.
The regular guidance counselors work with most of the adjustment problems of childhood and adoles-
cence. The adjustment counselor receives referrals from guidance counselors and other school per-
sonnel wherein the needs are more severe. Lois Davenport and Deborah Hoffman-Scoggins make home
visits, contact community resources for the child and family and sponsor parent education groups
with weekly evening meetings to assist parents in coping. Their work has brought an important new
dimension to the mental health and school success of selected children. Indeed, the Lunenburg
children and parents are fortunate to have the benefit of the training and experience of these two
new faculty members. Commendation goes to the School Committee for their sensitivity to the rela-
tion of physical and mental health to children's success in school.
- COOPERATING IN PLACEMENTS -
Lunenburg has cooperative arrangements with neighboring towns wherein a child with a diffi-
culty may require grouping with peers of similar age and development. Similarly, the Special Needs
Educational Collaborative continues to enroll children with low-incidence problems beginning as

Services provided by the Lunenburg school health program begin with three and four year old
screening, includes the assessment of health status of entering kindergarten students, routine
screening testing for all students, involvement in Chapter 766, and progresses through health
appraisals, counseling, self-care education, accident and illness prevention, and appropriate
referrals for care.

tive Medicine, co-sponsored by the Massachusetts Medical Society Auxiliary and Harvard Univer-
sity Extension Program. Subject areas included epilepsy, sleep disorders, juvenile arthritis,
common skin problems, headaches, carcinogens, and use of generic drugs.

Immunization monitoring continues, with an emphasis on upgrading the immunization status of
transfer students. The entering of foreign-born students necessitated the adoption of criteria
for entry that requires a negative tuberculosis report, and proof of immunization. An immunization
clinic is held once a year in conjunction with Nashoba Associated Boards of Health. With the
approval of school physicians, required immunizations or tuberculosis testing are available at the
school for transfer students at any time during the year.
Also carried out in conjunction with Nashoba Boards of Health is the dental program. The
hygienst offers cleaning and fluoride treatments for students in grades two, five and eight;
examinations for students in kindergarten and grade three, and dental health programs for grades
one and four.
Tuberculosis testing for certification is provided for school personnel, bus drivers, and
substitutes. Other staff health services provided are yearly blood pressure screening and flu
shots, and blood pressure monitoring on request, as well as being available for health consulta-
tion and questions.
The regular student screening programs--vision, hearing, and scoliosis--is being expanded
this year to include blood pressure screening for students in grades six and eight.
The school nursing staff serve as members of the safety and health education curriculum
committees, and not only serve as resource people for teachers presenting health education seg-
ments in the classroom, but also actively participate in the family living, and first aid classes
at the elementary and high schools, and instruction of CPR on the high school level. Also, the
nurse at the high school serves as a counselor for students with health-related problems. All of

dents.
