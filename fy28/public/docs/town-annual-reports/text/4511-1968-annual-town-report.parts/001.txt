The Chairman Reports
Word to the Wise
It has been predicted that unless corrective action is taken at the State and local levels taxes could rise as much
as 50% in the next three years. This prediction in the wake of this year's $21.00 property tax rate increase and in
view of a sizeable increase next year is a grim reminder of the indisputable fact that our Town, like many another
throughout the Commonwealth is most assuredly faced with a fiscal crisis unless we can find a quick and effective method
of bringing the spiraling property tax under control.
In plain English ,one may ask "how did we get into this mess, and what's more important, how the devil do we get out."
The property tax is the foundation of the entire Massachusetts tax structure and provides approximately one half
of all taxrevenue raised in our state. Supplementing the property tax and likewise coming out of your pocket and mine,
are the State Sales and Income Taxes which monies instead of being paid to US directly with no strings attached are chan-
elled through the State Treasurer and apportioned back to the cities and towns riddled with burdensome conditions, pro-
visions, restrictions, stipulations and limitations which more often than you think raise whatever cost they are supposed
to be offsetting to a ridiculous point of no return.
The Commonwealth of Massachusetts is terribly over-committed and is also caught up in a National picture. This
over-committment which has been building up for several years has now been compounded to a staggering degree by a
billion dollar major educational bill and a run-away Medicaid program. Unless some relief is forthcoming our state is
headed for real difficulty in terms of economic growth, for industry will not be attracted to thiskind of a "climate" and
some already located here will leave.
It is already a fact that Massachusetts is in the 50th position growthwise and its manufacturing segment is so large
that when it is in trouble it pulls the whole economy down. Few in a position to know will deny that we are getting
dangerously close to a level that neither we as individuals or industry can stand. However, the state alone cannot be
held responsible for this dilemma nor are you, the property owner, or we as local officials completely blameless. Like
citizens everywhere, you want and often demand better education, better police protection and more and more improve-
ments and personal services. Many of you who have "moved out to the country" expect the same kind of services you
received in the city.
CARL W. PROCTOR

Many of you, in fact the majority, have seldom if ever attended Town Meeting and the majority who do attend
never oppose or even ask questions about the amount voted. It is your Town and it is your meeting - you can and should
question every item that is proposed. In order for a property owner to realize any relief he has got to decide what pro-
grams are most important and oppose the rest. He should not assume that just because an item- any item - is sponsored
by a Town Department or Board or approved by the Town Finance Committee, it is therefore altogether necessary. We
all have our "sacred cows" and it is no disgrace that we do so, but it is not always in the Town's best interest to milk
them dry.
In the last few years the voters and the Finance Committee have been too good and too lenient in approving Town
budgets. As a result there isn't a Department in Town that couldn't fasten its belt a little bit tighter, including the
School Department which after all does account for approximately 70% of the budget. We have heard over andover
again "you can't touch the school budget or the School Committee will take US to court. It is doubtful that Lunenburg's
School Committee would flout the will of the voters to this extent. Whether or not they should be, the requirements for
becoming a School Committee member are after all no greater or then the requirements for any other Town office,
nor are School Committee members any more or less fallible. The chances are, therefore, just as great percentagewise
that as much could be cut from the School budget as could be reasonably cut from other Town budgets and that this
could be done without impairment to the education of our children.
That townspeople and officials alike do not like to stand up and be counted, particularly in opposition to another-
wise worthy and popular cause, is a sign of our times. It therefore behooves public officials to take the bull by the
horns and show greater leadership in this regard.
Although a long range solution to the dilemma of the Real Estate owner maybe to restructure the whole set upso
less falls on the property tax, and an ideal but unrealistic short range solution would be the immediate location in our
Town of just theright kind of industry, neither of these is likely to occur in the immediate future. Therefore, although
it is not easy to quickly and realistically. curtail or cut back programs already in progress, we must act now to take
whatever steps are necessary to squeeze every of fat from municipal budgets. The Chairman of this Board has there-
fore proposed that the 1969 budget of the Finance Committee be voted with the following provision: "That each and
every item be reduced by 5% including the School Department budget." As the 1969 budget will be well in excess of
two dollars this would mean a savings of approximately $10.00 on the 1969 tax rate. The matter of what specific
items the 5% should apply to should of course be left to the discretion of the Department Heads.
Yes, you read it right, a Selectman, in sense, is proposing a Taxpayer's revolt in the Town of Lunenburg, such
action to take place at the Annual Town Meeting which will be held in the Junior-Senior High School Auditoriumon
Saturday, March 1, 1969 at 1:30 P.M. WILL YOU BE THERE?

Town Clerk
Duties Prescribed
Most people still think of a Town Clerk as that person who registers births, deaths and marriages; and sells
hunting, dog and fishing licenses ,sits on the stage at Town Meetings; oversees elections and when called upon to do SO
performs an occasional marriage ceremony as Justice of the Peace. In fact, the General Court, over the years has added
constantly to the duties and complexities, problems, authorities and responsibilities of this office and many of a Town
Clerk's duties stem from various segments of State and County Government.
The Town Clerk relates to nearly every town official in some way, as well as to all boards and committees. At
the State level he has duties and responsibi lities under the jurisdiction of the Secretary of State, the Director of Accounts
the Attorney General, the Division of Fisheries and Game and the Public Works division. At the County level, he
relates both to the Commissioners and the Courts.
Among the many duties and authorities of the Town Clerk which are prescribed by law, those relating to elections and
Town Meeting are the Most complex. In the case of Town Meeting, failure to comply with literally hundreds of legal
requirements can often invalidate actions taken by the citizens in town meetings or cause challenges which result in court
actions and unfortunate publicity. Likewise, failure to observe any one of the hundreds of laws relating to the registra-
tion of voters, the conduct of elections or the certification of results can and often does erupt in legal problems and
again, unfavorable headlines. The problems of legality would be relatively simple were it not for the fact that the laws
are being changed constantly.
Every election day each Town Clerk In Massachusetts stakes his reputation on the actions and activities of appointed
wardens and other election officials. Since he must certify all results, it is the Town Clerk who is guilty when public
officers fail to perform and irregularities result. The penalties are heavy: $1000 fine and up to 12 months in jail.
On almost a daily basis, Town Clerks are required to make decisions or give interpretations of a legal nature, yet few
if any are attorneys or have legal backgrounds. In making these legal or quasi-legal judgments, Town Clerks place their
reputations on the line and even their jobs if successfully challenged. Those areas of activity in which legal problems
are frequently present include the administration of the Commercial Code involving chattel mortgages, certification of
death, all aspects of elections and especially those relating to custody of records during contested counts; legal, on-
the-spot judgments in his capacity as Clerk of the Board of Registrars; the recording and certification proceedings
eminating from Town Meeting actions; and actions such as adoptions which require knowledge of municipal law. In
many other areas, too numerous to mention, the Town Clerk must walk a legal tight rope; is a sitting duck because his
is the "office of record". He must be a social worker without sociological degree or experience in the handling of
delicate matters such as legitimacy and the rights of any citizen to view public records.
One of the vital legal responsibil of Town Clerks which most citizensare unaware of, involves actions taken by
the voters in Town Meeting. Most citizens assume that once money is voted at Town Meeting, it is immediately avail-
able for use. Such is not the case. Certification must be made by the Town Clerk for every dollar voted before it may
be spent. Duties related to Zoning and Subdivision Control are complex in nature and require close cooperation with
Planning and Appeals Boards.

No Relief In Sight
What are the basic causes for the spiralling tax rates in Lunenburg and other Massachusetts' Communities? The
relatively simple and obvious answer to thisquestion is: - The increased cost of government at thestate and local levels.
In 1946 the annual State Budget for Massachusetts was $140 Million as compared with the $1.5 Billion in 1968.
Lunenburg's increased cost of government and its effect on the local "tax picture" can be observed from the following

Personnel
Prices versus Wages
The rise of prices coupled with a corresponding increase in wages has caused the Personnel Board to recommend
more realistic salary schedules for various Town departments.
This Board, in preparing salary readjustments, has considered the need to provide incentive to employment in.
Town departments as well as the Town's ability to pay for the services of these departments. Also considered was the
exemplary job being done by present town employees.
The Personnel Board continued its policy of updating the Salary Administration Planadopted by the Town in1965
in order to better fulfill the needs of town employees. An article will be inserted in the 1969 Annual Meeting Warrant
which calls for amendments to the Personnel By-Law, based on revisions recommended by this Board.
The resignations of Chairman Phillips F. Lewis and his replacement, Albert W. Surprenant, were received with
regret. Mr. Surprenant has continued to assist the Board on a consulting basis.

Conservation
For a Better Tommorrow
No longer is it necessary to
justify conservation. Conservation
is recognized and accepted as an
important part of every community
program. It helps control taxes, it
makes property more valuable, it
makes a community a more desirable
place in which to live and guar-
antees the human race a longer pe-
riod of time to live on this planet.
Someone make a better offer!
The aims of the Conservation
Commission and the Town Forest
Committee have not changed. They
are: 1. To set aside 4000 acres of
the Town's nearly 20,000 acres to
be under the control of a conserva-
tion program for the purpose of pro-
tecting, preserving and increasing
its natural resources; 2. To have
this area used as much as possible
by the inhabitants of the Town for
recreational, educational and aes-

program and a forest improvement program on these 4000 acres so that future revenue may be returned to the fown;
4. To help other groups in the community interested in air pollution, water pollution, roadside beautification and

It is hoped to add about 100 acres of land this year. The Commission would like to set aside areas in different

to conservation, but to the whole community.

Industrial Development
Make Haste Slowly
The Commission has had approved, in 1968, the rezoning of some land off Massachusetts Avenue for parties that
have expressed interest and at present is awaiting the final judgement of parent companies on this and other tracts.
Options are still held on some of our land by a commercial real estate developer and its ultimate beneficial use is
still hoped for.
Your Commission is not looking for just any industry but rather one that will be an asset to the Town and also
one which would be compatible with the Town's environment, and believes that it is in the best interest of the Town
to "make haste slowly".
Although there has been Cf rather limited interest shown by industry to locate in Lunenburg, several commercial
establishments, which are of value to the community, have come into the Town in the past year. Adjoiningarea commu-
nities are having space problems and this should bring Lunenburg's land areas more to the forefront.
We are looking for more positive commitments in the coming year, and hope for definite and encouraging news
for next year's report.

Permanent Building Committee
Space Needs Appraised
The new Building Committee was established at the 1968 Town Meeting and held an organizational meeting with
the Selectmen in October of 1968. The Committee wasgiven the responsibility of having "charge and direction of the
construction and reconstruction of all buildings owned, leased or occupied by the Town, excepting minor repairs and
renovations".
During the short termof its existence, this Committee has contacted other towns regarding the operation of their
Permanent Building Committees, obtained basic information on existing structures and has laid the foundation for the study
of present structures and building needs for the Town. The Committee plans to gather and maintain information on build-
ing space and requirements from the various Departments in the Town in order to assist them in economic expansion.
As a result of meeting with the Town Highway Department and visiting the present location, the Committee
found the facilities to be inadequate for the housing of equipment and operations and recognizes the antiquated and
cramped conditions under which the Highway Department must work. The Committee has recommended that no additional
money be spent on this old structure and has requested sufficient funds in the coming year to study the cost and avail-
ability of a new structure and to make a land survey for a new building.
The Committee is also aware of increased space needs for the Police and Fire Departments and plans to meet with
these Departments during the coming year to obtain a better appraisal of their requirements.

Audit
Report of State Audit
As directed by you, I have made an audit of the books and accounts of the Town of Lunenburg for the period
from August 1, 1965, the date of the previous audit, to July 31, 1968, and submit the following report thereon:
The records of financial transactions of the several departments receiving or disbursing money for the Town or
committing bills for collection were examined, checked and verified by comparison with the records of the Town Treas-
urer and the Town Accounting Officer.
An analysis was made of the ledger accounts kept by the Town Accounting Officer, the receipts being checked
with the Treasurer's books, and the payments being compared with the warrants authorizing them and with the Treasurer's
records. The appropriation accounts were checked with the Town Clerk's records of Town Meeting proceedings, and
the recorded transfers from the reserve fund were verified by comparison with the authorizations of the Finance Committee.
A trial balance was taken off and a balance sheet, which is appended to this report, was prepared showing the
financial condition of the Town on July 31, 1968.
The books and accounts of the Town Treasurer were examined and checked. The cash book was footed and the
recorded receipts were compared with the Accounting Officer's books, with the departmental records of payments to the
Treasurer, and with other sources from which money was paid into the Town treasury. The payments were compared with
the warrants approved by the Selectmen and with the Accounting Officer's records.
The Treasurer's cash balance on July 31, 1968 was proved by examination of the Savings Bank books, by recon-
ciliation of the bank balances with statements furnished by the banks of deposit, by verification of the certificates of
deposit and by actual count of the cash in the office.
The payments on account of maturing debt and interest were proved with the amounts falling due and checked
with the cancelled securities on file.
The payroll deduction records for Federal and State taxes, the Retirement Systems, the Credit Union, the Com-
munity Fund, Group Insurance and the Teachers' Association dues were examined and checked. The deductions were
proved, the payments to the proper agencies were verified, and the balances on hand were reconciled with the ledger
controls.
The records of tax titles and tax possessions held by the Town were examined and listed. The amounts transferred
from the tax levies to the tax title account were verified by comparison with the Collector's books, while the redemp-
tions were compared with the Treasurer's recorded receipts. The tax titles and tax possessions on hand were listed,
reconciled with the Accounting Officer's ledger controls, and checked with the records at the Registry of Deeds.
The securities and savings bank books representing the investments of the trust and investment funds in the custody
of the Town Treasurer were examined and listed. The income was proved and the other reported transactions and bal-
ances were verified.
The books and accounts of the Tax Collector were examined and checked in detail. The commitments of taxes,
excise and assessments were reconciled with the Assessors' warrants, the recorded collections were compared with the
payments to the Treasurer, as shown by the Treasurer's and the Accounting Officer's books, the abatements, as entered,
were checked with the Assessor's records of abatements granted, the transfers to the tax title account were verified,
and the outstanding accounts were listed and reconciled with the controlling ledger accounts.
The outstanding tax, excise and assessment accounts were further verified by mailing notices to a number of
persons whose names appeared on the books as owing money to the Town, the replies received thereto indicated that
the accounts, as listed, are correct.
-22-
===PAGE 25===
The records of deferred street assessments were examined. The payments in advance were compared with the
Treasurer's recorded receipts, the amounts added to the tax levies were verified, and the apportionments due in future
years were listed and reconciled with the Accounting Officer's books.
The Town Clerk's financial records were examined and checked. The collections on account of dog and sport-
licenses issued, and from laneous charges, were verified and compared with the payments to the Town Treas-
urer and to the Division of Fisheries and Game.
The records of departmental cash collections by the Board of Selectmen and the Inspector of Weights and Measures,
as well as by the Police, Health, Highway, School, Library and Cemetery Departments, and by all other departments
in which money was collected for the Town, were examined and checked. The payments to the Treasurer were veri-
fied and the cash on hand in the several departments, as well as the petty cash advances, was proved by actual count.
The surety bonds of the several Town officials required by law to furnish them were examined and found to be in
proper form.
In addition to the balance sheet, there are appended to this report tables showing a reconciliation of the Treas-
urer's cash, summaries of tax, excise, tax title and departmental accounts, together with schedules showing the trans-
actions and condition of the trust and investment funds.
For the cooperation extended by the several Town officials during the progress of the audit, I wish, onbehalf
of my assistants and for myself, to express appreciation.

Police
The Time Has Come
The time has come for Lunenburg to take a good look at its Police Department.
There is need for supplementing our manpower, for with our present complement of men,
it is becoming increasingly difficult to provide the police protection and services to which
our residents are entitled. Our population is in excess of 7,800 and the summer season
increases this to approximately 10,000. An abundance of commercial and recreational
facilities attract pleasure seekers twelve months of the year.
Data compiled by the Massachusetts Police Association indicates that based on our
permanent population alone, we should have a police force of at least eight full-time
men. The absence of 24 hour coverage in a town with a population of over 5,000is an
unusual condition and should be a matter of great concern. Salaries of present officers
range from $400 to $600 below the average of other communities in our population range.
Acting Chief Hobbs
During the past year, there were 59 house breaks, or attempts, resulting in losses of $38,000 in cash and mer-
chandise. There were 24 breaks or attempted breaks into business establishments, which amounted to an additional
$11,000 in losses. Lack of sufficient manpower to spend more time investigating meant that only a low percentage of
these crimes were solved, and only a small portion of property recovered.
Fingerprint identification has enabled US to solve some crimes. The Lunenburg Police Department does not have
the necessary equipment or trained personnel to use this scientific procedure to its best advantage. We must rely on
area departments and the State Police to assist US at their convenience. This area, and that of the use of photography,
will need to be given more consideration as more and more detailed evidence is being demanded by the courts.
Last year, private enterprise expended approximately $20,000 more than the entire police department budget for
paid police services. All administrative duties of these assignments are handled by the Police Chief. Complete and
accurate record keeping, to satisfy inquiries of the government, attorneys, insurance companies and citizens, means
more paper work. New laws, such as Firearm Identification and other regulations, require the compiling and tabula-
tion of pertinent information.
The demands on the Department were compounded this year by the absence of Chiet
Sherwood G. Winchester whose accident and illness ultimately led to his retirement after
many years of dedicated service to the Town. The resignations of two patrolmen and the
apppointment and necessary training of two replacements, left US even more short-handed.
The Board of Selectmen has shown a recognition of the problems confronting the Police
Department. They have authorized the hiring of clerical help for limited hours which,
hopefully, will increase in the coming year. A prudent program for adding manpower
will be instituted with approval of the budget. The Personnel Board is considering new
salary schedules to bring wages more in line with the changing times.
Retiring Chief Winchester
We must think in terms of increasing the size of our department to provide 24hour cover-
age and to give adequate service to our residents. A study must be made of present traffic
control devices and future needs. Chief Hobbs recommends that a traffic consultant be engaged to advise US on how to
relieve traffic congestion and hazards in the center of Town and in the school areas. Starting salaries must be adjusted
to attract dedicated and intelligent young men to police work.
Lunenburg is a proud Town and its people want the best. We want to give them the best, and we, too, want to
look with pride, not frustration, on our department. Let US hope that the next few years will see this accomplished.

Tree Warden
Beat the Beetle
The Dutch Elm Disease has been called the cancer of frees. A national organization known as the Elm Research
Institute has been founded by prominent conservationists to mobilize the nation's scientific resources to save the American
elm from extinction. Dutch Elm disease is killing over one million elms per year, and at this rate of loss the elm can
become extinct in one generation.
It is believed that beetles feed on elms because their bark lacks the chemical which in other trees acts as a
deterrent. To change the characteristics of elm bark is a goal scientists expect shortly to reach. Researchers have also
determined that an effluvium given off by female beetles and perceptable over long distances to other beetles is a major
attraction to feeding areas. This effluvium has now been isolated and synthetically produced in the laboratory and is
expected to become commercially available in the near future. By means of such an attraction, beetlescan be lured
to predetermined areas for wholesale destruction by related insecticides.
In 1968, Lunenburg lost 110 elm frees which were cut down and burned. The Department of Natural Resources
gave three days of "scouting" and seven days of work amounting to $800.00 to assist in the detection and removal of
diseased trees. Approximately 58 samples were taken by the State and sent to the Shade Tree Laboratory, University
of Massachusetts, for culture. Fourteen maples were planted along our roadsides to replace shade trees thathad to to be
cut down for various reasons.
A total of $7, 425.51 was spent by the Tree Warden in 1968 on the tree program and for control of Dutch Elm
Disease, $163.80 of which was reimbursed by the County.

Dog Officer
Problems With Man's Best Friend
One of the major problems confronting the Dog Officer during 1968 was the investigationand control of damage
done to both wild and domesticated animals. Dozens of chickens, domestic ducks and geese as well as wild fowl,
were killed by dogs. The Dog Officer also investigated reports of dogs killing livestock as large as swine and small
heifers. The majority of the cost of the above damage was reimbursed by the County, as, according to Sections 161
and 161A of Chapter 140 of the General Laws, any person who suffers loss by the worrying, maiming or killing of his
live stock or fowl by dogs, outside the premises of the owners or keepers of such dogs, may inform the Selectmen who
shall investigate the damage done and determine whether the same was inflicted by dogs, and, if so, appraise the amount
thereof. The appraisal is then submitted to the County for reimbursement to the property owner. No person may be
reimbursed for damages inflicted by his own dog, nor can he be reimbursed for any damage by any dog if, at the time
such damage was inflicted, he himself was the owner or keeper of an unlicensed dog of the age of three monthsor older.
It is the Dog Officer's belief that all of the wild dog packs which presented such a problem in the past have been
destroyed or broken up. Even with the removal of seventy-eight dogs, the dog population increase has not slowed down;
as a matter of fact abandoned dogs, particularly in the lake areas, have greatly increased the dog population. The
Departments of the Army and Navy have cooperated with the Dog Officer in tracking down offenders in three cases
involving military personnel who had left dogs behind when they transferred out of this area.
Fewer licenses were Issued in 1968 than in the previous year. Ascertaining that each dog is licensed will, of
necessity, have to be one of the first projects undertaken by the Dog Officer in April. Too much time and effort is
wasted by the Dog Officer on problems which would not exist if the dogs involved were properly licensed. The addi-
tion of the dog owner's telephone number on the Town Clerk's copy of the license will be requested as part of an im-
proved control problem. The resignation of our present Dog Officer has been accepted with regret and the newly ap-

Nashoba Health
A Combined Effort
The sixteen member towns of the Nashoba Associated Boards of Health are: Ashburnham - Ashby - Ayer - Berlin -
Bolton - Boxborough - Dunstable - Groton - Harvard - Lancaster - Littleton - Lunenburg - Shirley - Townsend -
Tyngsborough - Westford. "Nashoba" demonstrates the fact that, by grouping together, small rural communities can
benefit fully from scientific advances in the fields of preventive medicine and community health. On its own, however,
the small town is severly handicapped because it cannot afford the technically qualified personnel to provide even
minimum standards of community health. The wisdom of the original decision of the member towns to form the Nashoba
Association was never more justified than today. The following information was included in the report of Henry T.
Smith, Director of the Nashoba Associated Boards of Health.
The past year has been a gratifying one for the Staff of the Nashoba Health Department. Old programs have
continued to produce satisfactory results and new programs have been successfully initiated. Members of the Nashoba
Associated Boards of Health have met four times during the year and have indicated that the services received from
Nashoba have met expected goals. The professional staff hasbeen maintained with few vacancies occurring. It is still
difficult to find some permanent employees, particularly in sanitation; fortunately part-time professional peoplehave
been available and willing to work in their spare time to assist during peak periods of work demand.
During the fall of 1968, sixteen hundred and twenty-eight residents of the Nashoba Health District participated
in the Nashoba Diabetes Detection Clinics which were held in each of the sixteen member towns. From this group 36
persons were definitely confirmed to have diabetes and are now under treatment of their own doctors. Lunenburg had
130 participants.
Mrs. Audrey Pond, Medical Social Consultant, has handled a wide diversification of referrals; coming from
physicians, community nurses, schools, courts, and Well Child Conferences. There havebeen 255 situations in Nashoba
involving 788 contacts of which 6 situtions and 12 contacts were in our community. During 1968 the Nashoba
Nutritionist operated two days per week through August under Mrs. Ping Lee, who found it necessary to resign when
her husband left Fort Devens. Over 100 home visits were made to 37 cases referred to her by physicians for aiding
patients needing special diets. Meetings and consultations with schools, day care centers, camps, nursing homes and
special diet groups numbered 53.
The nursing service under the direction of Miss Jane Kerrhas hada busy and rewarding year. Twelve Well Child
Conferences conducted by qualified physicians, were held in our community with 123 children making 165 visits.

Councilfor Aging
A Major Milestone
This Council was formed by the Board of Selectmen in 1966. Its purpose has been to adviseand assist the aging
group of citizens in fulfilling definite needs for housing, recreation and service which might be otherwise unavailable
to them.
During 1968 an allowance of $300.00 was supplied by the Town as in the previous year, to provide group recrea-
tion and conduct further inquiry into the needs of some 12% of the population, now making up this segment of the Town.
The Committee is now awaitinga central meeting place in the newhousing development. The Council feels that
its sponsorship and activity in regard to housing was rewarded by the whole-hearted endorsement of the voters on Nov-
ember 20, 1967 of the establishment of the Lunenburg Housing Authority, and the activity of the Authority in 1968,
resulting in securing $720,000 for the Town to build a 48 unit development on White Street.
The Council will be anxious to in any way assist those engaged in this development, and has been assured of
cooperation by the Authority in laterplans for recreational and other services to those living in the project and other
residents who are in the "65 and over" group. Since the Housing Authority is a State governed board it may be helpful
to anyone interested in renting in the new buildings to have a knowledge of its present membership, namely; Rev. John
M. O'Brien, Chairman; Frank H. deBettencourt, Vice-chairman; George R. Wallace 3rd., Treasurer; Rev. Leon R. Oliver,
Asst. Treasurer; and Ann P. Hall, Secretary. An Executive Director will be named during this year to handle many
of the business affairs of the Authority.
The Council acknowledges the tireless efforts of the Housing Authority, through many difficulties of site selection
this year, and expresses its thanks to the Authority and those State and Local officials who in any way assisted the
progress of this endeavor.

Sewerage Study Committee
Planning and Study
Meetings of the Sewerage Study Committee were held regularly on the second Tuesday of each month. In add-
ition arrangements were made to meet with another nearby community which had recently undertaken the Installation
of a public sewer and' in June, this committee met with the Town of Shrewsbury's Sewer and Water Commissioners,
Town Manager and Town Engineer. The progressiveness of the growing Town of Shrewsbury was in inspiration to our
members and it was felt that the guidance given by the officials with whom we met was most valuable. Actual construc-
tion of Shrewsbury's public sewer was started in 1962 some eight years after the Initial study was begun. At the time
of our visit the system. was available to about 1200 takers or a little better than 10% of the town. This graphically
points out the fact that these projects can take a very long time.
The chairman of our committee attended a discussion on a proposed sewerage treatment system for the City of
Fitchburg, by the firm of Camp, Dresser, and McKee, Consulting Engineers. In their proposal, two separate treatment
plants were recommended; one of these, to be located In the easterly section of the city, was designed to accommodate
sewerage effluent from the Town of Lunenburg.
In September, a joint meeting was held with the Planning Board, Board of Selectmen, and Sewerage Study Com-
mittee at which time representatives of the engineering firm of Morgenroth Associates, who had been engaged in a pre-
liminary sewerage study for the Planning Board for the past few years, gave an outline of work accomplished to date.
During this joint meeting, the Planning Board recommended that a new committee, to be known as a Sewage Advisory
Committee, be appointed to assist their Board and engineers when necessary. An article has been Inserted in the 1969
Town Meeting Warrant which, If voted, would establish such an advisory committee.
In conclusion the members of the Sewerage Study Committee urge the voters to support continued planning and
study with regard to a public sewer for the Town of Lunenburg and encourage the Planning Board to push forward to
enable our Town to promote better living by such an undertaking.

Veterans
Lest We Forget
The function of the Veterans Service Department falls
into two major categories - Services and Benefits - both of
which are governed by State and Federal rules and regulations:
Services: Your local department is involved at the State
level in that the Treasurer of the Commonwealth has allocated
to US the responsibility of acting as their distribution agent for
the Vietnam Bonus Applications. In this capacity we assisted
93 Vietnam veterans and aided the parents of three veterans
killed in action. This Department aided three veterans of the
Vietnam conflict to achieve their goals through the GI Bill of
Rights, by entering them in the educational program of the
Veterans Administration. We also assisted many disabled vet-
erans in their claims by acting as their Power of Attorney and
representing them at the hearings of the Veterans Administra-
tion. In many instances we have aided the widow of a veteran
by filling out her application for pension.
Benefits The budget for 1968 was exceeded by approx-
imately 100%. In addition to the rise in doctors' fees, hospital
Veterans' Agent, Alfred H. Gauthier
charges, and cost of medication, contributing factors were the
labor disputes at the Fitchburg Paper, New England Telephone and Weyerhaeuser Paper Companies. Although only
four veterans actually applied, in processing their applications it was determined that their four families included 32
dependents.
Qualified veterans requiring hospitalization should avail themselves of the facilities of the Veterans Administra-
tion Hospital in the area. Either this department or a family doctor is willing and able to advise any veteran of the
proper administrative procedure. Many patients, upon returning to their homes, must have special medication, a family
physician in attendance, and other types of aid.
As each day presents problems which involve this Department, a supply of the various forms for pensions, hos-
pitalization, insurance, and medical reports are kept on hand to dispense whenever the need arises. We will continue
to give all the assistance that we are able to, and will not refuse any veteran the right to apply for aid; however, the
final approval rests with the State Department.

Travel With Confidence
As the Town grows, the request for services and road improvement increases proportion-
ately. Following is an explanation of services rendered and projects accomplished by the
Highway Department on the Town's 76.37 miles of accepted roads, with each paragraph rep-
resenting a separate appropriation or account.
Chapter 81 - Work done under this account is primarily for maintenance. Approximately nine
miles of bituminous surface was treated with 39,551 gallons of asphalt and 2, 325 yards of sand
for cover. Drainage was installed in the following roads: Townsend Harbor Road, Main Street,
Cross Road, Pond Street, Howard Street, Hollis Road, Youngs Road and Electric Avenue,
Catch basins were cleaned and roads patched throughout the Town and a bituminous concrete
surface treatment was applied to West Townsend Road. Roadside mowing was carried out all
over Town. Calcium chloride was applied to Fish Street, Round Road and Watt Street. Our
Wayne Sweeper has cut the cost of our sweeping and spring cleaning program and has proven
to be a money and time saving investment. Other work performed under this account included
Superintendent of Streets
cutting back shoulders, brush cutting, gravelling and repairing guard rails.
Richard W. Brown
General Highway Maintenance - Extensive highway maintenance was performed on Watt Street, Round Road and Pond
Street including widening, gravelling and installation of drainage. Pipe and a catch basin were installed on Charlton
Street and a culvert and catch basin were installed on Holman Street. Throughout the Town streets were patched and
swept, roadsides were mowed, brush was cut and calcium chloride was applied where necessary.
Chapter 90 Maintenance & Construction - A bituminous concrete surface treatment was applied to Shirley Road, West
Townsend Road and Fort Pond Road. Portions of Shirley-Leominster Road were reconstructed, widened and surfaced.
The portion of Leominster Road which was widened and reconstructed the previous year, approximately four-tenths of
a mile commencing at the Leominster Line, was surfaced. Construction and widening of a section a year is planned
until the entire length of Leominster Road is completed.
Road Oil and Tar - Round Road, Watt Street, Reservoir Road, Fish Street, Highland Street, Fairview Road, Howard

was spent in combating abnormal ice conditions. As a result, the total cost in 1968 of keeping our roads and sidewalks
safe and open to travel was a record high of $47,369.00.
Road Machinery Maintenance - If our roads are to be maintained efficiently, equipment used must also be properly
maintained. Items purchased from this account included gas, oil, grease, diesel fuel, acetylene and oxygen, grader

wheel truck was purchased from "war surplus" and a sander and Austin Western grader were also purchased.
