- SPEECH-TO-PRINT PHONICS -
This year a newly developed program, "Speech-to-Print Phonics", to provide a good start in
reading, has been instituted in the first grade.
Phonics has been taught in accordance with the basic readers and reinforced by the phonics
program as outlined in the Developmental Reading Program compiled for the Lunenburg Elementary
School. This controversial topic has been the concern of the Reading Department and for several
years various isolated phonic programs have been studied and tried with a limited number of
classes in grades one through three on an experimental basis.
A few years ago, twenty seven first grade studies were conducted with the aid of federal
funds to determine what method, if any, produced the highest reading achievements with first
graders. Conclusions drawn from these research projects were:
1. Methods that had heavy phonics did better than those which used only the basic reader ap-
roach.
2. The three best predictors of first grade reading success were:
a. Knowledge of letter names
b. Phoneme identification (ability to identify separate sounds in words)
C. Mental age

School Nurse
The Board of Health hires two registered nurses, Mrs. Doris M. Lahti and Mrs.
Gladys V. Barney, who not only take care of the School Nursing but also are
responsible for the Visiting Nursing Service in Lunenburg.
Vision and Hearing testing is done on all school children annually. Notices
of failures are sent home by mail to parents. It is very gratifying that most
parents receiving notices are having their child checked by an eye or ear special-
ist. Many conditions receiving early treatment result in making school easier for
these children.
A total of 232 visits were made to school children and their parents for:
health supervision, absence from school, physical defects and follow up vision and
hearing notices.
Dr. John DeLeo, School Physician, does physical examinations annually on
students in grades 4 - 7 - 10 except for students who have returned a "Physical
Form" stating that they have had an examination by their own doctor.
All athletes are checked by the school doctor. These students also have blood

School Lunch Program
This is my sixth annual report of the School Lunch Program and it covers the school year,
September 1967 to September 1968. The Thomas C. Passios Elementary School cafeteria remained
closed but the Special Milk Program was in effect in all schools.
There were 117,204 paid "Type A Lunches" served to high school students as well as 3,346
free lunches served to high school workers and needy students. 6,056 adult lunches, including
those of the cafeteria staff, were also served. There were 171 lunch days for an estimated
740 lunches served daily. Our high school lunch count will be reduced next year when the seventh
gradeis moved into the Middle School. 247, half-pints of milk were sold and 12,521 half-pints
of free milk were provided under the United States Department of Agriculture Special Milk Program.
7,160 half-pints were sold to adults.
The School Lunch Program received $13,031.55 in Federal Commodities, a slight increase over
the previous year. But food costs continued to climb and financially the year ended with approx-
imately the same balance as last year. The opening cash balance for the school year in Sept-
ember, 1968 was $4,804.97 after all bills
were paid in June. Our program is required
to operate on a non-profit basis but due
to rising costs we found it necessary to
request permission from the State School
Lunch Office to raise the price of the
school lunch to 30c. This request was
granted and went into effect with the
opening of school in September, 1968.
This action was necessary in order to keep
the program self-supporting.
Participation percentage-wise for the
Jr. -Sr. High School was 80% according to
attendance.
The annual Spring School Lunch Work-
shop was held the Narragansett Regional
High School in Baldwinsville during the
