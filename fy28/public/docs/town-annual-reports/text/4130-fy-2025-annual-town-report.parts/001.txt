In Memoriam 
 
 
 
John Francis Lattanzi was born to John and Frances (Lynch) Lattanzi on April 1, 1936, in Yonkers, 
New York. He grew up in Yonkers and Washington, New Jersey. In his youth John developed a life-
long affinity for mischief, playing rousing piano by ear, and a love of outdoor pursuits. He graduated 
from Lafayette College in 1958, served as an officer in the U.S. Army, then embarked on a 30-year 
career with the American Can Company. 
 
He met his life-long partner, Christine Uhl, in 1961. They married, purchased a camp on Sebec Lake in 
Maine and started their family soon after. John and Christine raised their family in Cranford, NJ and 
Geneva, NY before moving to Lunenburg, MA in 1982. 
 
Over the past 43 years, John served his community in various roles on the Lunenburg Personnel Board, 
Conservation Commission, and Historical Society. John was a member of Saint Boniface and Our Lady 
of the Lake Churches, the Lunenburg Sportsman Club and the Sebec Lake Association. 
 
John leaves behind his beloved wife of over 61 years, and their five children; John Lattanzi Jr. and his 
wife Karen of Honolulu, HI; Jean Proctor and husband Dan of Lunenburg, MA; Peter Lattanzi and wife 
Dina of Cumberland, ME; Paul Lattanzi and wife AnnMarie or Yarmouth, ME; and James Lattanzi and 
wife Allison of Fitchburg, MA. John also leaves behind 15 grandchildren and his younger sister, Jane 
O’Connell, of Durham, NH.

In Memoriam 
 
 
 
Robert Joseph Saiia of Lunenburg, MA, affectionately known as Bob or Bobby, passed away peacefully on 
April 3, 2025, after a long battle with dementia. Bob was a loving husband to Donna and a devoted father to his 
daughter Tricia Back and son Michael Saiia. 
 
Born in Buffalo, New York (1944), Bob was proud of the days he spent working at The Bankers Life Insurance 
Company in Buffalo, NY~ later Principal Financial Group, where he was known for his dedication and hard 
work. As his career progressed, he was promoted and moved his family to Westerville, Ohio, where he became 
a proud Buckeye fan. His colleagues remember him as a kind and supportive mentor who always had a joke to 
share. He always held dear the memories of his days as a camp counselor at Camp Weona, just outside of 
Buffalo, NY. He also had a passion for coaching, whether it be his own children or once they were grown, 
volunteering as a football coach at Lunenburg High School. Bob also was very proud of the time he served on 
the Lunenburg Planning Board. 
 
Known as the life of the party, Bob valued every friendship, especially his lifelong friends from Frontier High 
School, where he graduated in 1962, affectionately calling themselves, HAAC-Hamburg Attitude Adjustment 
Club. Each and every friendship he made throughout his 81 years of life left an impact on him and he couldn’t 
go anywhere without making a new friend. His infectious laughter and warm spirit made him a beloved friend 
to all. 
 
Bob loved listening to Jazz, Frank Sinatra and Andrea Bocelli. He loved traveling to Italy and Europe with his 
wife Donna, but his favorite place to travel was Indiana where he could spend time with his children and 
grandchildren. He was the best Grandpa around to his two grandchildren, Renee and Anthony; they were his 
pride and joy and he would do just about anything to put a smile on their faces. He was always their biggest 
cheerleader and supporter and loved attending all their various events and activities. Bob adored his time with 
his family and his dogs, loved sports and he especially loved celebrating his Italian heritage. He took great pride 
in his roots and enjoyed sharing stories of his family's traditions and history. 
Bob is survived by his devoted wife, Donna Saiia, daughter Tricia Back (Robert Back), son Michael Saiia, 
grandchildren, Renee Back (19) and Anthony Back (18), stepchildren Christina Gallagher and Michael 
Gallagher, sister Kathy Krasowski, nieces Tracy Ensminger (Jeff Ensminger) and Julie Reinstein (Craig 
Reinstein), Linda Saiia Ross, and his five great nephews and one great niece.

MILES 
Lunenburg was settled in 1718 and incorporated in 1728. It occupies a land area of approximately 26.4 
square miles. Lunenburg is located in north central Massachusetts approximatel y 43 miles from  Bos-
ton, It is bordered on the west by the Town of Ashby and the City of Fitchburg, on the south by the 
Town of Lancaster and the City of Leominster, on the east by the Town of Shirley and on the north by 
the Town of Townsend. 
Local legislative decisions are made by an open town meeting.  Subjet to the legislative decisions 
made by town meeting, the affairs of the town are generally administered by an elected five member 
Select Board and a Town Manager and local school affairs are administered by an elected seven 
member School Committee.
Lunenburg is a semi-rural community located in northern Worcester County. Lake Whalom and the for-
mer Whalom Amusement Park delineated the Whalom District from the remainder of Lunenburg. This 
district was developed as a housing base for many factory workers working in the mills and factories of 
Leominster and Fitchburg. In many ways the characteristics of the Whalom District closely reflect charac-
teristics of these cities more than of the rest of Lunenburg. The Hickory Hills Lake area was developed as 
a private community comprised largely of vacation cottages. Shirley Reservoir is a public water body also 
developed with mainly vacation cottages that have now been modified to year-round residences. 
Commercial and industrial development is permitted in several areas of town, primarily along  the two 
major arteries (Route 2A and 13) and the town's peripheries, due largely to the close proximity of 
Leominster's and Fitchburg's sewer and water systems. Although many farms and orchards have slowly 
developed into single family house, farming continues on in Lunenburg.  In 2013 the Annual Town 
Meeting voted to establish an Agricultural Commission to represent, preserve and enhance farming in 
the commujnity and to administer the "Right to Farm" bylaw.  
In 2014 Lunenburg Town Meeting established the Architectural Preservation District ("District") bylaw 
to regulate construction, demolition or renovation of existing "Contributing Property" structures as 
defined  by the bylaw with an established bounded district.  The bylaw was introduced at Town Meeting 
along with the Village District Bylaw, which created an overlay zoning district to encourage the 
economic vitalization of downtown Lunenburg, the Architectural Preservation District Bylaw ensures 
that it will not come at the expense of the town's historical character and integrity.

REPORT OF THE TOWN MANAGER 
Fiscal Year 2025 
Fiscal Year 2025 saw significant transition in the Office of the Town Manager.  The Office of the Town 
Manager was led by three individuals over the course of the year: Heather Lemieux, who departed in the 
summer of 2024 after eight years of dedicated service; Carter Terenzini, who served ably as Interim Town 
Manager from August 2024 through early February 2025; and Jennifer Warren-Dyment, who was appointed as 
Town Manager and began on February 4, 2025. Throughout these transitions, department heads, Town staff, the 
Select Board, and the many volunteers who serve on our boards and committees provided the continuity and 
commitment that kept Lunenburg moving forward. 
Below are the highlights of the key topics and accomplishments from FY25 within the Office of the Town 
Manager. 
Leadersh
ip Transition 
Town Mana
ger Heather Lemieux submitted her notice to the Select Board in June of 2024.  Her last meeting 
with the Select Board was the evening of July 23, 2024, during which she offered heartfelt remarks on her 
service to the community.  She expressed gratitude to residents, board members, staff, business owners, and 
state officials, reflecting on her seven and a half years of service. She outlined major accomplishments, 
including long-term staffing plans for public safety, significant capital improvements, enhanced ADA 
accessibility, and infrastructure projects such as Complete Streets initiatives, Summer Street improvements, and 
bridge funding. She emphasized that her proudest achievements were the relationships built during her tenure. 
She clarified that her resignation was not requested by the Board and attributed her decision, in part, to a loss of 
trust. She concluded by thanking her family for their support and reiterated her commitment to having served 
Lunenburg with dedication and integrity. 
Carter Terenzini was engaged as Interim Town Manager beginning in August 2024. He brought extensive 
municipal management experience, including several prior engagements in the Interim role, and assumed the 
position immediately.  As Interim Town Manager, Mr. Terenzini not only kept the day-to-day operations 
running smoothly, but also advanced a number of capital projects, completed the obligation of ARPA funds, 
navigated multiple vacancies, and made notable strides in improving employee engagement. At his final Select 
Board meeting on January 28, 2025, Interim Town Manager Terenzini presented his concluding report and was 
thanked by the Board for filling vacant positions, committing ARPA funds, and fostering greater employee 
participation during his tenure. Board members expressed appreciation for his work navigating challenging 
circumstances. 
With the Interim position filled, the Select Board stood up a Town Manager Screening Committee and hired the 
search firm of Community Paradigm Associates.  Per the Town Charter, The Town Manager shall be appointed 
by the Select Board from names submitted to it by a Town Manager Screening Committee which shall consist of 
1 member of the Select Board, designated by it, 1 member of the School Committee, designated by it, 1 member 
of the planning board, designated by it, 1 member of the Finance Committee designated by it and the Town 
Moderator, who shall serve as the chair and call all meetings of the Town Manager Screening Committee. 
Whenever the position of Town Manager shall be vacant, the Town Manager Screening Committee shall be 
convened and shall solicit candidates, review applications, interview and rate candidates and submit the names 
of qualified candidates to the Select Board who shall appoint a Town Manager only from the names so 
prepared and submitted to it. 
The Screening Committee, Chaired by Town Moderator Tim Murphy, also included Tom Alonzo of the Select 
Board, Peter Beardmore of the School Committee, Chris Menard of the Finance Committee and Matt Brenner 
of the Planning Board.  The Committee worked with Community Paradigm to develop the Position Statement 
for the Town Manager Role, screen applications, interview semi-finalist candidates, and recommend a list of 
finalists to the Select Board for their consideration.  Much of the work of the Screening Committee is conducted

Three finalists were publicly interviewed by the Select Board on January 14, 2025.  They included Angeline 
Lopes Ellison, Jennifer Warren-Dyment, and John Woodsmall.  The next evening, the Board moved (4-1) to 
appoint Ms. Warren-Dyment as Town Manager pending successful negotiation. On January 28, 2025, the Select 
Board approved the contract with Ms. Warren-Dyment as Town Manager.  Ms. Warren-Dyment began her first 
day on February 4, 2025, and began immediately engaging with department heads, the Select Board, and the 
budget process that was already well underway. 
Select Board Composition and Goals

Financial Matters 
FY26 Budget Development 
Interim Town Manager Terenzini began the FY26 budget process, working with the departments to develop 
budget requests and with Finance Director/Town Accountant Ezequiel Ayala to develop revenue projections.  
Town Manager Warren-Dyment continued the FY26 budget process after her arrival. She held meetings with all 
departments in January and worked to understand the picture of revenues and expenditures and made initial 
revenue adjustments.   
By March 2025, the FY26 budget picture had come more fully into sharper focus. The FY26 budget presented 
to the Select Board on March 19, 2025, included reductions across a number of areas, including cuts to the 
Pavement Management Plan (which were offset by Chapter 90 funding). 
On May 3, 2025, a $49,374,465,00 balanced budget was presented and approved at Town Meeting.  The budget 
was subsidized by a $500,000 appropriation of free cash to the Lunenburg Public Schools (LPS) to fund FY25 
and avoid an override allowing town and school leaders to have time to jointly plan for FY27. 
The need for additional state funding was a theme that persisted through the development of the FY26 budget 
with town and school leaders advocating for increased Chapter 70 and Unrestricted General Government Aid 
funding.   
Budget Task Force 
The Budget Task Force, formed by the Select Board, brought forward recommendations that were reviewed at

the February 11, 2025 meeting.  The Task Force concluded there were three structural causes to the state of 
disrepair in Lunenburg: 1) Proposition 2 ½ funding; 2) lack of competitive employment packages; and 3) an 
overly complex financial structure for the town.   
Recommendations of the Task Force included 1) consolidating town investment accounts and funds; 2) a 
targeted diagnostic audit; 3) implement the prudent investment rule; 4) review the viability of the Community 
Preservation Act; 5) Green grants for HVACs; 6) solar on old town dump; 7) solar on school lots; 8) Brooks 
House lease; 9) review trash and recycling programs; 10) designate additional roads as town roads; 11) 
implement a high deductible health plan; 12) increase health insurance opt outs; 13) payroll and purchase order 
streamlining; 14) standardize and improve workflow for town finances; 15) review school solar contracts; 16) 
create regular review process for school fees; 17) create regular review process for town fees; 18) change 
warrant delivery; 19) consolidate town and school snow removal and ground maintenance; and 20) determine 
viability of split tax rate. 
Other Financial Mentions 
Town Manager Lemieux had obtained a grant to work with the Division of Local Services (DLS) to draft an

Infrastructure, Facilities, and Capital Projects 
Flat Hill Road Bridge 
The Flat Hill Road Bridge replacement over Catacoonamug Brook was the most significant and most 
challenging capital project of FY2025. After years of planning, the project, funded by a previously secured 
$500,000 Small Bridge Grant and supplemental MassDOT funding, was awarded to Kenefick Corporation. The 
road was closed beginning March 7, 2025, with an anticipated reopening of April 25, 2025 and a detour route 
put in place and communicated widely to the public.  However, the water level in the lake had already risen 
significantly by the time work was to begin, and Kenefick’s water control plan proved inadequate for the 
conditions. No work was performed on the structure, and Kenefick submitted a request for a one-year extension, 
which was denied.  The bridge remained closed for the remainder of FY25 after a MassDOT Underwater Bridge 
Inspection Team inspection found additional section in the structure.   
Town Building Projects 
• The Town Hall Roof Replacement and Chimney Restoration project was one of the major capital
investments of FY25. The contract was issued in December 2024 under Interim Town Manager
Terenzini, with the chimneys prioritized first as they were anticipated to generate the most change
orders. Winter weather slowed progress, but by March 2025 both Town Hall chimneys were complete
and the roofing work had begun. By April 2025 the Town Hall roof was nearly complete. The contractor
then transitioned to the Ritter Memorial Building, completing chimney repointing, flashing repairs, slate
tile replacement, and gutter and trim work. The project was substantially complete by May 2025.
• The Library Teen Room expansion was completed during FY25. The project included construction of
two new offices (Phase 1), expansion of the teen room itself (Phase 2), and replacement of the children’s
story craft room floor.

ARPA Fund Obligations 
Completing the obligation of all American Rescue Plan Act (ARPA) funds before the federal deadline was one 
of Interim Town Manager Terenzini’s most urgent and consequential priorities during FY2025. The Town had 
received approximately $3.5 million in ARPA Coronavirus State and Local Fiscal Recovery Funds (SLFRF), 
and the federal requirement had evolved from a definition of “committed” funds to requiring that all dollars be 
under contract by December 31, 2024. This created a compressed and challenging timeline.  
The ARPA-funded project portfolio being moved to contract during FY25 included a wide range of municipal 
investments including supplementing existing projects on prior year capital plans as well as additional facilities 
and infrastructure side projects, public safety upgrades, school projects, Water District projects, software 
upgrades, and funding for community programs.   
By the end of December, all funds were obligated.  When Interim Town Manager Terenzini presented his final 
report to the Select Board on January 28, 2025, he cited the successful commitment of ARPA funds as one of 
the signature accomplishments of his tenure. Chair Nuzzolo drafted a press release to communicate to the 
public the full scope of ARPA investments the Town had made on behalf of the community.   
 
Personnel 
 
Staffing remained an ongoing challenge and priority throughout FY25. Key staffing actions during FY25 
included: the completion of the Police Chief search and appointment; continued recruitment of the Land Use 
Director; the search and recruitment of the Senior Center Director; the filling of the Assistant to the Sewer 
Business Manager position; the recruitment for the Conservation Agent; a vacancy in the position of Executive 
Assistant to the Town Manager and Select Board to name a few.   
One of the most significant recruitments of FY25 was the search for a new Police Chief following Chief 
Thomas Gammel’s retirement. Applications for the Police Chief position closed in early February 2025. A 
Screening Committee comprised of the Fire Chief, COA Director, Superintendent, a Chief from another 
community, a Town Manager from another community, and Town Manager Warren-Dyment reviewed all 
applications and conducted interviews on March 12, 2025. Promoted from Lieutenant and then Interim Chief, 
Jeffrey Thibodeau, was sworn in as Lunenburg’s Police Chief at a well-attended swearing-in and pinning 
ceremony held on April 18, 2025.  Select Board Chair Caesar Nuzzolo served as Master of Ceremonies and 
PACC produced a video of the ceremony for those unable to attend in person. 
After 30 years of service, Sue Doherty retired as COA Director on December 31, 2024.  After a recruitment 
process, Deb Goodsell was hired to take over the helm at the Center.  Deb brought significant experience to the 
position, having served in Clinton for ten years as their Director.   
After a sustained vacancy in the role of Land Use Director that was vacated by Adam Burney, Christopher 
Reilly was hired in September 2024. The position was vacated again in April 2025 and recruitment continued

BOARD OF
 ASSESSORS 
Under the umbrella of Massachusetts state and local town laws, Lunenburg's Assessing Organization will work 
with town operations and our citizens to meet our vision for delivering a fair and equitable taxation.  The 
Lunenburg Board of Assessors will evolve and administer a series of “Best Practices” methodologies consistent 
with our Vision. These practices will ensure equitable treatment and compassion for all of our citizens. 
The Boar
d of Assessors has empowered an Assessor Organization that has the capability to evaluate and assess 
all property at its full and fair market value.  This is accomplished through a rigorous measurement of property, 
collection of appropriate data, analysis and maintenance of all property tax data records. Additionally, the 
Assessor Department administers a motor vehicle excise tax, real estate and motor vehicle tax abatements, and 
all statutory tax exemptions as allowed by law. The Assessor Organization will work cohesively with other 
town departments. 
The las
t year has seen a lot of progress in restoring the fully functional Assessors’ Office.  
• We conti
nue to be fully staffed after several years of being understaffed. Our staff includes a full time
Principal Assessor, a 19-hour Data Collector and a 32-hour Assessing Administrative Assistant.
• The Assessing office and files have been reorganized from the disarray in 2023.
• Assessing Policies and Procedures have been established.
• A sales inspection program has been established to follow-up on sales and review property data in MLS.
Permit inspections are conducted weekly and a cyclical program has been established with the
inspection of over 500 properties annually to comply with the DOR Directives from the 2024
revaluation.
• We successfully improved several exemptions by loosening eligibility income and asset limits
qualification for Seniors and increasing exemptions amounts.  We also worked on developing a new
“Senior Means Tested Tax Exemption” for implementation in the fall of 2025 (FY2026).
The Boar
d of Assessors’ efforts at restoring the trust of Lunenburg taxpayers include scheduling all our 
meetings during evenings (with both PACC coverage and Zoom access) so everyone can participate more 
easily.  The Board has also developed a Property Tax program for use at the Senior Center and has held a yearly 
senior tax exemption presentation to create awareness of the available real estate tax exemptions for the seniors.   
We continue to improve visibility and access by redesigning of the Website, including analytics and 
explanations provided the Massachusetts DOR / Division of Local Services, Massachusetts Association of 
Assessing Officers, and UMass among others.  The office staff strives to provide exceptional service that the 
Lunenburg taxpayers deserve.

BUILDING DEPARTMENT
The Town of Lunenburg Building Department is Located in the Ritter Memorial Building located at 960 
Massachusetts Avenue. The Building Department works to efficiently, effectively, and consistently ensure a 
safe, sustainable, and vibrant community through effective oversight of construction and development activities 
by enforcing the Massachusetts state Building, Plumbing, Gas, and Electrical Codes, The Architectural Access 
Board Regulations, and Lunenburg’s Zoning Bylaw and Town Code. The Building Department also plays a 
critical role in assisting business through the process of obtaining permits and ensuring compliance with all 
relevant codes and regulations. A commitment to being accessible, responsive and to providing a high level of 
customer service empowers us to be a partner the community, property owners, businesses, contractors, and 
developers can rely on when needed. The Building Department maintains this commitment by:
• Ensuring t
he public has consistent access to effective and courteous assistance in obtaining information,
permits or approvals related to construction, building or zoning regulations and providing clear
information about regulations, permitting requirements and associated fees.
• Maintaining a consistent level of communication by responding to all inquiries and applications in a
timely manner.
• Enforcing building codes, regulations, local codes, and safety standards to
guarantee the safety of occupants and the community.
• Improving and optimizing the permitting process to facilitate efficient and timely approvals for
construction projects while maintaining thorough and consistent review procedures.
• Utilizing technology to streamline internal processes, enhance data management, and provide better
online services for applicants and property owners.
• Regularly reviewing and assessing departmental processes and policies, seeking opportunities for
improvement, efficiency, and effectiveness in serving the community.
• Coordinating with other departments to ensure a streamlined approach to permitting and enforcement as
needed.
• Receiving and evaluating feedback from the public and other interested parties, and implementing
changes as needed to ensure the department goals are being met.

CEMETERY COMMISION 
 
Throughout the past year, the Cemetery Commission met regularly with DPW Director William Bernard and 
Cemetery Superintendent Todd Holman to review day‑to‑day operations, maintenance needs, and long‑term 
planning for Lunenburg’s public burial grounds. The Commission also conducted several on‑site visits to North, 
South, and Fish Street Cemeteries to assess current conditions and identify upcoming needs. 
 
The Cemetery Commission is an elected three‑member board with staggered three‑year terms. Its 
responsibilities include establishing cemetery regulations, overseeing care and management of public burial 
grounds, and laying out lots and pathways in both existing and newly acquired cemetery land. Daily operations 
are handled by the Town Manager and delegated to the DPW Director, as the Cemetery Division operates 
within the Department of Public Works. 
 
Overview of Cemetery Properties 
• North Cemetery (Holman Street) 
Approximately 22 acres, with about 10 currently active. The property includes open land suitable for 
near‑term expansion and additional wooded areas for long‑term development.  23-acre wetlands parcel 
also borders the site. 
• South Cemetery (Page Street) 
About 6 active acres, with limited remaining expansion capability. 
• Fish Street Cemetery (Fish & Lincoln Streets) – Goodrich Family 
A historic 0.06‑acre family plot with five marked graves and additional identified burial locations. 
• West Street Terrace – Arnold, Litch, & Moulten Families 
A small historic family cemetery located on private property.  
 
Both North and South Cemeteries remain open for lot sales and burials. 
 
Projects and Improvements 
Significant progress was made on the North Cemetery backlot expansion this year. Town Meeting appropriated 
$50,000 in May 2025, and the Commission contracted with Dillis & Roy for engineering services. Preliminary 
plans were submitted.  The bidding process for construction will be in Fiscal Year 2026. 
 
Administrative improvements included purchasing a secure file cabinet for cemetery records and continuing 
support for the CIMS software system, which offers digital mapping, ownership records, and public search 
tools. 
 
Maintenance efforts included: 
• Rebuilding roughly 150 feet of stone wall, completed by Amanda Construction at the South Cemetery. 
• New concrete pad for flag poles at the South Cemetery, completed by Mike McAllister. 
• Installation of 20 new cedar trees to replace aging hedges by Lakeview Landscaping at the North

CONSERVATION 
The Town of
 Lunenburg Conservation Commission is a seven-member commission, each appointed to 3-year 
terms by the Select Board, whose duties are codified under the Conservation Commission Act MGL chapter 40 
s 8C. The Commission enforces the Massachusetts Wetland Protection Act MGL chapter 131 s 40 and Chapter 
239 of the local bylaws for the protection of wetlands.  The Commission also manages, and controls town 
owned lands retained for conservation and passive recreation use. 
The fis
cal year 2025-26 was a very busy and active agenda containing both public hearings and meetings for a 
wide range of projects from commercial development, subdivisions and small single family upkeep projects and 
larger scale industrial/commercial proposals.  The Commission also held numerous inspections on weekends 
prior to meetings to familiarize themselves with the upcoming proposals that would appear on the agenda. 
During this period the Conservation Commission supported 28 NOIs, 36 Administrative Determinations, 18 
RDAs, 34 COCs, and numerous other requests.  
The Conse
rvation Commission continues to implement forest stewardship plans developed using grants as 
outlined in the last annual reports. The funding from the sale of harvested timber provides the seed money to 
make it possible for the Commission to apply for grants to maintain and improve Lunenburg’s Conservation 
Lands. The Conservation Commission continues to execute on the awarded MASS Recreational Trail Program 
Grant that was awarded in 2023. The purpose of this grant was to fund the engineering design and permitting 
work to create parking access in Table Rock and Rayno Conservation Areas. The grant scope also covered 
improving Accessible Access to the Cowdrey Conservation Area as accessed from Townsend Harbor Road. In 
early 2025, the permits were issued for Table Rock and Rayno Areas. This effort is ongoing to complete the 
engineering design and permitting work in the Cowdrey. Members of the commission applied for two additional 
grants for the upcoming year.

through supportive services, programs, and education. Councils on Aging were established under Massachusetts 
General Law Chapter 40, section 8B, to include setting policy for the administration of elder programs or 
services and to develop, coordinate, and conduct such activities which advocate for this population’s issues.

On May 3, 2025, members of the public gathered in the Lunenburg Middle/High School and participate in the

Committee has reviewed all monied articles, recommended updates, held public hearings, and has voted on our 
official recommendations. This warrant contains five budgetary articles that are worth highlighting: 
• Article 10: Omnibus Budget article that provides most funds for the operations of town (both municipal 
and schools). 
• Article 12: One-time, free cash funding to the School Department, breaking from Lunenburg’s

• Article 11: A Citizens Petition requesting a Proposition 2 ½ override of $2,099,337.

The first three articles are related to operational budgets and service levels for the Town. This report will 
summarize the impact on planned operations, and detailed presentations will be given at Town Meeting. The 
last two articles are debt exclusion articles resulting from years of planning and design work by two 
commissions.

Many Town Meeting articles relate to operational budgets. Lunenburg, like most municipalities in the

Fire Department Operations 
The Fire Department currently operates three shifts covering 24/7 town operations, leading to a 48-hour, six-
day-per-week schedule. Sundays are covered by call and per-diem staff, with 62% of coverage provided by 
career firefighters. Of the 197 Massachusetts Fire Departments providing 24/7 coverage, Lunenburg is one of 
only six operating with three shifts. Most departments use a four-shift model, making Lunenburg less 
competitive in hiring and retention. The shift expansion plan remains on hold for another year. 
Borrowing 
The town employs two types of borrowing: 
• Regular Debt, typically for smaller projects within the Tax Levy Limit.
• Excludable Debt, requiring Town Meeting and ballot approval, which does not permanently increase
taxes—once the debt is repaid, the tax rate decreases.
According to the Massachusetts Division of Local Services, “Municipal debt is an important tool for city and 
town governments to finance the purchase of expensive capital assets and to construct and maintain municipally 
owned infrastructure.  However, debt cannot become overburdensome to either the community’s property tax 
paying population or to the budget. Capital purchasing and/or improvements should ideally be planned with its 
priorities analyzed and reviewed by a capital planning committee seeking to coordinate community planning 
with debt affordability and aiming to present its recommendations to municipal leaders and to the public.” 
The Town policy states that: 
• Principal and Interest on Regular Debt remain below 4%.
• Excludable Debt remains below 11%.
• Combined debt remains below 14%.

Children’s Services Department Highlights

229 children regististering their participation.  These readers completed a total of 222,000 minutes of

programs, and check out their next books to read.

The Lunenburg Public Access Cable Committee (PACC) is responsible for the long-term plan to manage the 
Public Access Channels available to the Lunenburg community through Comcast, the town’s only cable-
service provider. 
The overall strategy includes providing and improving equipment to acquire, record, broadcast and re-
broadcast programming.  Original content is also conceived and produced by PAC.  Town events/meetings 
and school activities, as well as outside sources of programming, are all provided to increase the viewer’s 
knowledge of what is happening around town.
