HIGH SCHOOL
Lunenburg High School is pleased to announce that all members of the Class of 2003 passed the MCAS in both English
Language Arts and Mathematics. We are proud of all our students and faculty for meeting the challenge in the first year of state
competency determination for the issuance of a diploma. LHS also posted a performance rating of "High" in both English
Language Arts and Mathematics this year from the Massachusetts Department of Education, showing an improvement rating of
"Above Target" in both areas for Cycle II.
It has been an exciting year for many other reasons. We have resurrected both the Multicultural Festival and the School Musical
("Grease") this year, as well as carrying on traditions such as Homecoming and the 38th annual Competitive Class Plays. Our
students have traveled both domestically and abroad, to Florida, France and Italy. Our athletic teams have performed admirably,
with our football team sharing the league title, girl's cross country going 7-1, our boys track team winning the league and District
E championship, our boy's basketball team being named as the sportsmanship award winners, our hockey team achieving
league co-champ status, the girl's soccer teams qualifying for district play as well as winning the league sportsmanship award,
and our girl's basketball team winning the Mid-Wachusett C championship as well as the Mount Wachusett Invitational
Basketball Tournament.
Our music students participated in the Massachusetts Instrumental and Choral Conductors Association Festival for high school
ensembles, as well as Band Day at UMASS. Our construction class completed the restoration of the spire for the town hall. We
instituted a new applied graphic design course in the art department. As you can tell, our students are always doing something
new and exciting at LHS, and are fine representatives of our school and community.
We are looking forward to the implementation of a revised schedule next year to allow for the creation of the STEP program
(Senior Transitional Experience Program), a community based elective program for seniors involving an internship, community
service or college experience. We look forward to the development of this program as a way to meet the needs of all our
students and to foster our district's mission of preparing our students for life-long learning and responsible community
membership.
As always, we thank the community of Lunenburg for its continued enthusiastic support. We welcome your Involvement In our
school community through event attendance, volunteering and supporting our students in any way you can. This positive
partnership helps us continue to provide an excellent education for our greatest resource, our youth.

TURKEY HILL MIDDLE SCHOOL
During the 2002-2003 school year, Turkey Hill Middle School continued in a variety of ways to pursue excellence. The faculty
and administration view this pursuit not as a static accomplishment but rather a fluid process. As such, significant gains were
made in some areas and lesser gains in other areas, but everyone at THMS seeks excellence and continual progress.
This past year the staff at THMS engaged in meaningful, rewarding and profitable faculty meetings that revolved around the
"Results" philosophy. There are three faculty meetings per month. Teachers met with colleagues from the same subject at one
of these meetings per month as well as on our half-day workshops to discuss curriculum and Instruction. First they scrutinized
the MCAS Item Analysis to ascertain our students' weak skill areas. Next they shared common teaching strategies to determine
the best technique for teaching those skills. They established obtainable goals for students to accomplish and then taught or re-
taught the skill using the agreed upon strategy. The following month at the next "Results" meeting they revisited their
accomplishments as well as areas that needed further refinement. Teachers greeted this process with excitement. It provided
ongoing professional development focused on data and results. The "Results" approach provided teachers with the opportunity
to share successful Instructional practices, to dialogue about the art of teaching and to discuss areas of concern.

This process as well as our subject area task forces continued our focus to promote consistency and standardization among
teachers. Curriculum mapping is the name of this effort, and the goal is to ensure that all children receive an educational
program that has the same high quality standards. This is an ongoing process. Each year teachers make adjustments in their
program to match the Standards of the Massachusetts Frameworks. This year THMS met the performance targets in all areas as
established by the Department of Education. As our school moves into Cycle 3 of this program, our goal will always be to meet
or exceed target goals.
Turkey Hill Middle School took a big step forward in another area. For the first time our students had access to a computer lab.
Twenty-five computers were installed in the library, and all were connected to the Internet. This was long overdue. Technology
education remains a high priority for all students, and we cannot allow this school to lag behind. With the addition of this lab, all
teachers can take an entire class to the library to do research, to create Power Point presentations, to design science labs and
do a variety of other activities. The effort to improve the physical plant continued. A community school partnership again used
funds ralsed by the school to remodel an area of the school; this year we chose to renovate the front office counter, doors and
trim. There is now a brand new look to this important focal point of the school. Two years ago the cafeteria was renovated, last
year the front courtyard was re-landscaped and now the front office is revitalized. It is all the result of terrific school-community
partnerships, and it was all done no cost to the town!
THMS continues to improve our ability to meet the needs of all students. This year the Guidance Team created a system which
increases the level of communication among teachers. Designated as the Student Data Sharing System, the system ensures that
information about a student is shared with all of the student's teachers. If a student is having an academic or social problem, the
system begins to accumulate information and Initiates a sharing system so that all teachers are aware of the problem. Lastly we
have Initiated a new tradition here at THMS. Our eighth grade class will travel to Washington DC in June. This class trip serves
many functions. It reinforces lessons learned in social studies about the history and geography of Washington; it provides
students first hand examples of lessons learned in civics; and it gives our graduating class a chance to make friendships, to
have memorable experiences and to Increase the positive feelings they have about their middle school experience.

THOMAS C. PASSIOS ELMENTARY SCHOOL
The Thomas C. Passios Elementary School continues to strive toward educating the total child. We utilize a variety of
instructional methods and modalities in our approach to meet the individual needs of every child. We believe that the success of
our programs is dependent upon a healthy and productive partnership between home and school. Toward that end, parents play
an active role in many of the daily programs and routines of their child's educational program.
This year continues to be a challenging and exciting year for the students, faculty, and staff of the Elementary and Primary
Schools. With a combined enrollment of seven hundred students, class sizes continue in the twenty to twenty-four range, with
the younger children benefiting from smaller class sizes.
The summer of 2002 proved to be very beneficial to many children in Lunenburg. Again we were able to offer a number of
extended learning opportunities to children: a five week program for children with special needs in grades preschool through
grade 4, and a five week program for children in Grades 1-4 who were experiencing difficulty in the areas of reading and math.
Funding for the Grades 1 and 2 programs was provided through School Linked Services; State Academic Support grant monies
supported the Grade 3 and 4 program.
As the Elementary School continues to analyze our students' results on the Massachusetts Comprehensive Assessment System
(MCAS) and revise curricula and instructional strategies, we continue to grow as an excellent educational facility. We are
extremely proud of the work the faculty continues to undertake each year. As teachers reflect on their teaching practice,
students benefit.
We are pleased to report that the Thomas C. Passios Elementary School met its targeted Improvement goal as determined by the
Department of Education. Results on the State Grade 4 MCAS Tests indicate that in the areas of both English/Language Arts and
Math, the percentage of Grade 4 students performing in the "failing" category continues to decline, while those performing In the
proficient and advanced categories increases.
This year the Elementary School Improvement Plan focused on the areas of open response questions and math, particularly
geometry and measurement. At this point in the year, the School Council is proud that we have accomplished many of our
goals. On all professional development half days, Ms. Maria Marolda, educational consultant at Children's Hospital, has provided
additional math training to all teachers and support staff in grades K-4.
We are pleased to report that our Reading Assessment practice has also been re-evaluated and revised. Each child in grades 1-
4 will be evaluated twice each year in reading; in October and March. New Individual reading folders have been Implemented to
document student progress as well as strategies for improvement.

its annual School Improvement Plan. Parents and community members are welcome to attend. Members of the Council for the

BOARD OF ASSESSORS
The Board of Assessors and its staff are responsible for the valuation of all real estate and personal property subject to taxation.
Assessed valuations are based on "full and fair cash value" as of the January 1st preceding each fiscal year.
Our office requires a substantial amount of information In order to carry out its valuation mandate. Much of the office's effort
centers around maintaining ownership records, tax maps, and property characteristic data. This data is constantly changing.
Fiscal Year 2002 saw a significant increase in new building site approvals, home construction, and improvements to the existing
housing stock. The resulting increase in inspection activity, time spent in setting up new records, maintaining existing data, and
dealing with all of the political and economic implications brought on by growth kept the office unusually busy.
In an effort to enhance the Town's Geographic Information System (GIS), the office went out to bid to have new aerial
photography and digital orthophotography prepared. The contract was awarded to Kucera International, Inc. of Willoughby,
Ohio. Kucera completed the fly-over in February, 2002 and delivered the orthophotography in April. The digital orthography will
serve as the foundation for a complete re-map of Lunenburg.
Our department saw no changes to the composition of Board of Assessors. At their May meeting, the Board voted Chris
Comeau to serve as Chairman. In July, Norma Frost tendered her resignation, desiring to undertake new opportunities. She
agreed to continue on with the office for up to 2 years in order to train a new Administrative Assessor and oversee a smooth
transition. In October, the Board appointed Barbara LeFebvre. Continuing to serve the Board are Dorothy Musgrove as Principal
Clerk, Janet Leavitt as Inspector, and Harald Scheid as Regional Tax Assessor.
We thank the Town for its support of our office and the efforts we have made to insure that tax revenues flow to the Town in a
consistent and timely manner.

since its' establishment in 1967. Other retirements mentioned were Fire Chief, Dennis Carrier, Police Officer, Alphonse
Baron, and Building Commissioner, Roger L. Williams. The Moderator led a special thanks to Mr. & Mrs. Bigelow
Crocker for the land donation for the Library. The Annual Town Meeting was adjourned to take action on the Special

ARTICLE 29. To see if the Town will amend the Zoning Bylaw by adding to Section 6.0, General Provisions
Affecting All Districts, a new Section 6.7., Demolition Delay To read as follows:
6.7
Demolition Delay
6.7.1
The intent and purpose of this Section is to protect the historic and aesthetic qualities of the Town of
Lunenburg by preserving, rehabilitating or restoring whenever possible, significant buildings or
structures which constitute or reflect distinct features of architectural or historical resources of the Town,
by promoting continued private ownership and utilization of such buildings and by providing owners of
significant structures with time to consider alternatives to demolition, thereby promoting the public
welfare and preserving the cultural heritage of the Community.
6.7.2
Definitions: In addition to the Definitions in Section 2.0. of this Bylaw the following definitions apply to
Section 6.7.
6.7.2.1.
Application: an application for a permit for the demolition of a building, which shall include a photograph
of the building taken within the past year.
6.7.2.2.
Permit: a permit issued by the Building Official for demolition of a building pursuant to an application.
6.7.2.3.
Demolition: the act of pulling down, destroying, removing or razing a building or structure.
6.7.2.4.
Significant Building: any building or structure that the Planning Board determines, in conjunction with
the Historical Commission, Building Official and Fire Chief meets one of the following conditions:
a) Listed on the National or State Registries of Historic Places or is the subject of a pending
application for listing of historic places said registries or,
b) Included in a local registry of Historic Places or is on a current or pending inventory prepared by
the local Historic Commission or,
c) Importantly associated with one or more historic persons or events, or with the broad
architectural, cultural, political, economic or social history of the Town of Lunenburg or the
Commonwealth of Massachusetts or,
d) Historically or architecturally important in terms to period, style, or method of building
construction.
e) And seventy-five (75) years old or more.
6.7.3
Procedure:
6.7.3.1.
An application for a Demolition Permit will be made to the Building Official who shall within five (5) days
forward a copy of the Demolition Permit application to the Planning Board. Said application shall Include
a written narrative that Includes the following:
a) Sketch showing the location of the building or structure with reference to neighboring properties.
b) Photographs
80
===PAGE 81===
c) Description of the age, architectural style, historical association and importance of the building to
be demolished.
d) Reasons for demolishing.
6.7.3.2.
Demolition Plan Review by the Planning Board
a) Within thirty (30) days after receipt of said application, the Planning Board shall hold a demolition
plan review meeting to determine the applicability of the delay procedure or the determination of
non-applicability of the delay procedure. Said meeting will be advertised in the local newspaper
and posted in the usual manner one (1) week prior to said meeting.
b) Written reports shall be presented by the Historic Commission, Building Official and Fire Chief
and the applicant pertaining to the applicability or non-applicability of delay procedure of the
building or structure. The Planning Board shall determine the status of the application based on
the reports noted above. The written report of the determination taken shall be given to the
Building Official within seven days (7) of the closing of the review meeting.
c) If the Planning Board determines the building or structure is not a significant building under this
bylaw, the Building Official may issue a demolition permit upon receipt of the notification.
d) If the building or structure is determined to be a significant building under this bylaw, the
Building Official shall Impose a demolition delay up to one hundred eighty (180) days from the
date of notification, in which time the applicant must notify the Planning Board that a bona fide,
reasonable and unsuccessful effort was made to locate a purchaser to acquire the property at fair
market price for the building or structure and who has agreed to preserve, rehabilitate, restore or
relocate same or has agreed to alternatives to demolition, and or accept conditions noted in the
applicability determination.
e) The owner/applicant shall provide written evidence through documents that attempts have been
made to sell, preserve, move, rehabilitate or restore said building. Evidence shall include a
listing of the building with a real estate broker, an estimate of rehabilitation or restoration of the
structure or an estimate for moving the building.
6.7.3.3.
Emergency Demolition
6.7.3.3.1.
Nothing in this bylaw shall restrict the Building Official or the Fire Chief from ordering the demolition of a
significant building determined to present a clear and present danger to the safety of the public, which
only demolition can prevent.
6.7.3.4.
Non-Compliance
6.7.3.4.1.
The Building Official is empowered to institute any proceedings in the law or equity as he deems
necessary to obtain compliance with the requirement of this bylaw to prevent a violation thereof.
6.7.3.4.2.
No building permit shall be issued with respect to any premises upon which a significant building has
been demolished in violation of this bylaw for a period of two (2) years after the date of the violation.
or take any other action relative thereto. Board of Selectmen recommend approval. VOTED (YES 89 NO

AN ACT AUTHORIZING THE TOWN OF LUNENBURG TO ESTABLISH A CAPITAL IMPROVEMENT TRUST FUND AND A BOND
AND NOTE PROCEEDS INVESTMENT FUND.
SECTION 1. Notwithstanding any general or special law to the contrary, the Town of Lunenburg may establish and maintain a
special account known as the Capital Improvement Trust Fund, and may raise and appropriate money therefor.
SECTION 2. The Town of Lunenburg may appropriate to the fund by majority vote at an annual or special town meeting in any
year any amount not to exceed 10 per cent of the amount raised in the preceding year by taxation of real estate and tangible
personal property. The aggregate amount of the fund at any time shall not exceed 10 per cent of the equalized valuation of said
town as defined in section 1 of chapter 44 of the General Laws.
SECTION 3. The Town of Lunenburg may, by a By-Law enacted by a 2/3 majority vote at an annual or special town meeting,
direct that a fixed percentage of the Town's "Free Cash" as certified by the Department of Revenue in any given fiscal year and a
fixed percentage, not to exceed 10%, of any new growth in tax revenue as certified by the Bureau of Local Assessment in any
given year, be deposited in said Capital Improvement Fund.
SECTION 4. The fund shall be maintained by the Town of Lunenburg as a separate account. The treasurer of the town shall be
the custodian of the fund and may Invest the funds in such separate account in the manner authorized by sections 55 and 55A of
chapter 44 of the General Laws. Any interest earned on the fund shall be credited to and become part of such separate account.
SECTION 5. The Town of Lunenburg may appropriate by majority vote at any annual or special town meeting any principal and
Interest within the fund for any capital purchase or principal debt payment for any capital purchase. Capital purchase is defined
as any item with a life expectancy of more than 5 years.
SECTION 6. Notwithstanding section 53 of chapter 44 of the General Laws or any other general or special law to the contrary, the
Board of Selectmen of the Town of Lunenburg may establish a special fund or funds in the town treasury into which shall be
deposited income derived from the investment of the proceeds of all notes and bonds issued for any capital building project and
funded through a Proposition 21/2 debt exclusion for the renovation, reconstruction, construction and original equipping of said
town's buildings. The town treasurer shall be the custodian of these monies and shall make an accounting of the funds to the
Board of Selectmen. Except as provided for In Section 7, any amount in such special fund shall be applied solely to the payment
of capital costs or debt service for the building project that the money was borrowed for and no amount shall be spent on such
projects in excess of the amount appropriated therefore without an additional appropriation by town meeting and upon
authorization by the town treasurer.
SECTION 7. If, upon declaration of the town department or committee responsible for oversight of a project referenced herein
that the project is completed, there remains a balance in the special fund attributable to that project, that balance shall be
transferred to the General Fund of the town.
SECTION 8. This act shall take effect upon its passage.
