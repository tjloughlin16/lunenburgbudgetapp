MEMORIAM
LESTER P. FOSTER
1923 2002
One of the regular faces will be missing when you go to vote at the next election. Lester enjoyed being a warden
and greeting people with a smile while guiding their ballots into the box. His greatest pleasure was sports. At
one time he was an avid tennis player and received several trophies. He belonged to the Senior Golf League at
Maplewood Golf Course and bowled with the Seniors at the Mason Bowling Alley. When his boys were young, he
was President of the Little League and helped coordinate many fund raising events. Lester was a regular at the
Tuesday afternoon bridge tournaments at the Eagle House Senior Center. He also enjoyed playing cards and
games with his grandchildren. For many years he was an accountant at Webber Lumber and for Guaranty Bank
in Worcester. He retired in 1985.
RAYMOND A. POWELL JR.
1944 2001
Ray was a lifelong resident of Lunenburg and operated R.A. Powell Construction, later changed to Powell Stone
and Gravel from 1969-2001. He served in the U.S. Army and was stationed in Germany. Ray, as most people
know, was very active in town affairs and was particularly generous with his time and made several donations to
varlous endeavors. He was very interested in the youth of Lunenburg and served for many years as the
president of Little League and Pop Warner. He was also a great friend to the Park Department and the updating
of its facilities. Through Ray's extreme generosity, a new Little League Field was Installed at Marshall Park as
well as a new fence around the lower field. Ray was involved in many other projects In Town that benefited all of
Lunenburg. He was someone that you could always count on if you needed help. It is fair to say that Ray was
"one of a kind" and will be greatly missed.
WILLIAM F. REED
1923 2002
Bill and his wife Nancy were married for 57 years and had two sons and two grandsons. Bill was a Navy Veteran
of World War II, Korea and Vietnam Wars. He joined the Navy in 1941 retiring in 1965 attaining the rank of E-9
Master Chief Machinist Mate, the highest enlisted pay grade. He then worked at Parks-Cramer Company In
Fitchburg. He was active in the Boy Scouts, Historical Society and a lifetime member of the Lunenburg
Sportsman's Club. Bill was a member and Chair of the Board of Assessors for fifteen years. During those years
he tirelessly worked to provide fair and equitable representation for taxation. Bill never wanted to spend a nickel
unless It was necessary. He truly was a friend to the taxpayer. Later he served briefly as a member of the
Finance Committee but his health was not cooperating and he was forced to resign. That didn't keep him from
expressing his opinion-one never had to guess where Bill stood on any issue. He will be missed.
GEORGE ROLLINS SR.
1914 2002
George Rollins Sr. came to Lunenburg with his wife Beatrice and their two children in 1956. In June George and
Beatrice celebrated their sixty-fifth wedding anniversary. George worked at American Can for more than 30
years. George also served the community in a variety of areas: as a Boy Scout Leader, good old-fashioned
census taker, past master of the Aurora Lodge of Masons, President of the Friendly Seniors, and member of the
Bowling League. George kept himself very busy but what he will be remembered for most is his infectious smile
and friendly hello on Election Day. George will be missed by all.
ROGER L. WILLIAMS
1941-2002
Roger L. Williams was born in Nashua NH and raised in Pepperell MA. He was employed as a teacher for eighteen
years prior to his appointment 1983 as Bi-Town Building Inspector for the Towns of Townsend and Lunenburg.
He moved to Lunenburg in 1988 at which time he was also appointed full time in the capacity of Building
Inspector/Zoning Officer for Lunenburg. He retired from his position with the Town this past year due to health
issues and made our town his home until his death in October. Roger's warmth and friendliness made him a
person one did not easily forget. His loyalty and dedication to the Town will long be remembered.

BOARD OF SELECTMEN/SEWER COMMISSIONERS
The Board of Selectmen/Sewer Commissioners entered into two contracts for the next phases of sewer construction in town.
Residents will be having a perplexing task navigating around town during the next construction season, as contractors will be
working on the permanent traffic lights at the intersection of Whalom Road and Electric Avenue. Construction crews will also be
reconstructing and resurfacing Electric Avenue from the Leominster/Lunenburg town line to the intersection of Route 2A. The
construction on Contract II of the sewer system will also commence, with work beginning at the Whalom Lake rotary and a
second crew will also be working somewhere along Massachusetts Avenue. (Route 2A).
A number of properties have already connected to the recently completed phase of the sewer line, and It Is anticipated that
properties along this next phase will be able to connect before the end of 2003. The Board also negotiated for the purchase of a
portion of the Hartwell property located on Massachusetts Avenue, chosen as the site for the Public Safety Complex. This site
as well as the site for the new Primary School will be incorporated into this next phase of the sewer project.
The Board notes with regret the recent resignation of the first CAFO, William J. Gustus. Bill has accepted a similar position with
the Town of Lynnfield and has agreed to help Lunenburg on a consulting basis as the Town begins its search for the next CAFO.
In accordance with the Town Charter, this search begins with the CAFO Screening Committee and they will make
recommendations to this Board for the top candidates. During the hiring process the Board has appointed Town Accountant,
Karen Brochu to the position of Interim Chief Financial Officer and Chief Administrative Assistant, Laura Williams as Interim
Chief Administrative Officer.
Former Selectmen, Michael J. Sauvageau also resigned as a Board member and accepted the position of Building
Inspector/Zoning Officer due to the recent retirement of Roger L. Williams. The Board also recognizes the recent retirement of
Fire Chief Dennis Carrier.
Renovations were made at the Town Hall including a new heating/air conditioning system for the second floor and the painting of
Town Hall. A survey was taken at Town Hall to find out from the residents which color they preferred; White or Tan with Green
doors and shutters. The final outcome was to go with the color.
Two new appointments were made this year with Scott Glenny nominated as the new Fire Chief and John Rodriquenz chosen for
the new position of DPW Director.
On a personal note the Board would like to recognize the strength and commitment of former Board member Ronald Lamothe
during his recent battle with health issues. As III as Ron had been he attended nearly all of the Board meetings and continued in
his tenure as coach for many of Lunenburg's student athletes.
The Board extends a special "thank you" to the following: Lunenburg/Townsend Rotary Club for decorating the Gazebo at
Christmas and the Turkey Hill Gardeners for the Town Hall decorations and flowers in the upper common. The Turkey Hill
Gardeners were also Instrumental in the Installation of the irrigation system for Upper Town Common. This was accomplished
through a generous grant from the Sidewalks and Grounds Trust Fund.
We wish to recognize the recent passing of Roger L. Williams, former Building Inspector/Zoning Officer who passed away in
October. He will be remembered for his gentle demeanor and his willingness to assist property owners in navigating through
town government. He will be deeply missed by this Board and the community.
Your Board has enjoyed working in service to the Town, working with each other, and with you the citizens. They especially
want to thank our CAFO, Bill Gustus, for working so well with all town departments and committees, his expertise will be missed.
Please feel free to direct any questions to our office staff Laura Williams, Interim Chief Administrative Officer or Linda Douglas,
Administrative Assistant @ 978-582-4144, if they cannot assist you, they will forward your call to the appropriate authorities. Our
office hours are Monday, Wednesday and Thursday 8:00 AM to 4:00 PM, Tuesday 8:00 AM to 6:30 PM and Closed Fridays.

BUILDING INSPECTOR/ZONING OFFICIAL
The Building Officials office is located on the second floor of the Town Hall. Office hours for the Building Department are
Mondays 7:00-3:30, Tuesday 8:00-6:30, Wednesday 8:00-3:30 and Thursday 7:00-3:30 PM; closed Friday. The Building Inspector
is available in the office daily and inspections are by appointment only. Michael J. Sauvageau became the new Town Building
Official/Zoning Officer in June of 2002. Harry Cullinan serves as the Assistant Building Inspector and Lisa Normandin is the
Administrative Assistant to the Building and Zoning Departments.
On a personal note we would like to recognize Roger L. Williams, the former Building Official, who passed away in October.

CENTRAL MASSACHUSETTS MOSQUITO CONTROL
The Central Massachusetts Mosquito Control Project (the Project) currently provides its services to 35 cities and towns
throughout Middlesex and Worcester Counties. The Project's headquarters or visits to field work sites may be arranged by
calling the office In advance. The Project practices Integrated Mosquito Management (IMM), blending state of the art methods
and techniques with expertise, experience, and scientific research to provide our member communities with environmentally
sound and cost effective mosquito control.
During 2002 the Project received 3,252 requests for service from town residents and officials. We scheduled four hundred and
sixty-six (466) adult mosquito operations in residential areas, and four hundred and twenty eight (428) larviciding operations. A
total of over 4,191 pounds of Btl (Bacillus thuringiensis Israelensis) was applied by helicopter in 2 towns, Chelmsford & Billerica,
and by hand throughout our service area to area wetlands to reduce the emergence of adult mosquitoes. This represents over
838 acres of wetland that was treated with this mosquito-specific bacteria, significantly reducing adult mosquito populations In
these areas. Twenty-six thousand, eight hundred and thirty-two (26,832) catch basins were treated with larvicidal briquettes to
control mosquitoes that seek out cool dark wet areas to breed, including the Culex mosquito, a major target for West Nile Virus
transmission. Four thousand, one hundred and eleven (4,111) culverts were cleaned in an attempt to eliminate unnecessary
standing water and reduce mosquito breeding. This work was done in conjunction with cleaning, clearing and digging of one
hundred four thousand, five hundred and ninety-six (104,596) feet of streams, brooks and ditches. This represents almost
twenty miles of waterways which were cleaned and Improved by Project personnel in 2002.
12
===PAGE 13===
The Mosquito Awareness Program which we offer to elementary schools and other civic organizations in our district has become
very popular. Project staff meet with students, teachers or concerned residents to discuss mosquito biology, mosquito habitat,
and control procedures. Much of the presentation is directed towards what children and their families can do to prevent
mosquitoes from breeding around their homes. Slides, videos, coloring books and other handouts make this an interesting
program. This program is tailored to meet the needs of the specific audience. As part of our effort to reduce the need for
pesticides we continue to expand our wetlands restoration program. By cleaning clogged and overgrown waterways, mosquito
breeding can be reduced and drainage areas are restored to historic conditions. Btl mosquito larvicide is used to treat areas
where mosquito larvae are found. We routinely check known breeding sites kept In our database, but also encourage the public
to notify us of any areas they suspect could breed mosquitoes. Our field crews will investigate all such requests and treat the
area only if surveillance gathered at the time shows an imminent threat of mosquito emergence.
Our goal is to handle all mosquito problems with education, wetlands restoration or larviciding, but we recognize that there are
times when adult mosquito spraying is the only viable solution. In such cases areas are treated with either hand-held or pickup
truck mounted sprayers if surveillance gathered shows sufficient numbers of adult mosquitoes present to warrant an
application. This program is available on a request-only basis, and the exclusion process allows residents and town officials to
exclude areas under their control from this, or any part of our program.
The Project's surveillance program monitors adult mosquito and larval population density, and is the backbone for prescribing
various control techniques. Rain gauges are set out and data is collected by our surveillance crews in an effort to predict when
mosquito breeding will occur. Specialized mosquito traps are deployed throughout the Project's service area to sample for
mosquitoes that may be transmitting mosquito-borne diseases. In conjunction with the Mass Dept. of Public Health we sample
in areas suspected of harboring WNV and other viruses.
Educational pamphlets are available to anyone interested in learning about mosquito control and the services provided by the
Project, and these items are routinely stocked in member Town/City Halls and libraries. We also have a website, www.cmmcp.org
that has extensive information on our procedures. This website has become a model for other Mosquito Projects, and has been
widely used throughout our service area and beyond.
We would like to thank you for your support during 2002 and we look forward to helping you and your community with its
mosquito problems in 2003 and beyond.
CONSERVATION COMMISSION
The Lunenburg Conservation Commission has seven members appointed by the selectmen who are charged with administering
the Massachusetts Wetlands Protection Act and the Lunenburg Wetlands Protection By-law. They are also responsible for
management and acquisition of conservation lands and assisting the public with wetland and ecological issues and concerns.
In 2002 the Conservation Commission continued to be committed to managing and preserving open space
Matthew Marro, Conservation Agent, successfully completed a Massachusetts Forest Stewardship Program with the assistance
of a $2000 grant from the DEM - Division of Forests and Parks. Over the years the Forest Stewardship Program has supported
about 50 town projects across the state. It has sought to encourage activities that bring community members into the forest for
learning and teamwork, at the same time making sure that ecological, wildlife habitat, recreational or sustainable wood
production goals are being met. The grant has been used to develop a Forestry Management Plan for the Small Town Forest, a
215-acre parcel of land owned by the town located on Mulpus Road. There is only one trall in this wooded parcel and the
Commission would like to expand the trail system, enhance the wildlife habitat, provide signs, Identify and explore the harvest
and bio-diversity Issues.
Joyce Hachey, Administrative Assistant, represented the Commission in the nominating process for The Squannacook Region
ACEC (Areas of Critical Environmental Concern). The focus of the project was to seek the Commonwealth's designation of
ACEC. An ACEC is an area or ecosystem formally designated by the Secretary of Environmental Affairs as containing
outstanding critical ecological and cultural resources worthy of protection and preservation. A total of 26 ACEC's have been
designated In the 26 years the program has been in existence. The designation directs state agencies to preserve, restore and
enhance the resources of an ACEC, such as public water supplies, open space, farmland and forest lands, historical buildings
and archaeological sites, wildlife habitat, and surface waters, wetlands and floodplains. The area, known as the Squannassit, Is
a 70 square mile geographic region formed around the Squannacook, Nashua, and Nissitissit Rivers. It extends from Groton
westward to Ashby northward to New Hampshire and contains extraordinary diversity of landscape and ecology. Secretary
Robert Durand announced the designation of the Squannassit Area in December.

HIGHWAY DEPARTMENT
John M. Rodriquenz, DPW Director
A formal consolidation of personnel and equipment has combined the Highway, and Sewer departments together with the
Cemetery, Parks, and Facilities divisions into a single Department of Public Works. This restructuring is built upon a strong
foundation of teamwork and cooperation already in place in order to provide a service to residents that is more economical and
efficient. Sharing of resources is necessary to maintain Town properties at a high level and to oversee an increasingly
expansive infrastructure.
We appreciate the efforts of the Capital Plan Committee and the generosity and support of the residents that allowed for the
procurement of the following Items: one ton pick-up truck with plow, a wood and brush chipper, and a six-wheel dump truck with
plow and sander attachments. The purchase of new equipment continues our vehicle rotation program and enhances
Interdepartmental use.
The following is a breakdown of line item allotments and expenditures for the fiscal year:
VEHICLE MAINTENANCE
All public works vehicles, fire apparatus, and police cruisers are fueled, serviced and repaired through this program centralized
at the highway garage. The Police Department was allocated $20,000 and spent $29,264. The Fire Department expended $9,933
of their $10,000 allocation. The School Department also exceeded their allocation of $3000 by incurring expenses of $5,014. The
Highway Division was appropriated $108,093 an expended $83,700, the balance being used to cover the overages of other
departments.
TOWN BARN
The Highway Division facility is located at 520 Chase Road. Costs to operate the physical plant; heating oil, electricity, telephone,
janitorial supplies, office equipment, and postage totaled $13,470 and $9,898 was encumbered to rebuild, in part, the
underground fuel and gasoline storage tanks.
TREE REMOVAL
As tree warden, the Public Works Director oversees all work done to trees within the Town right of way. Many problematic trees
must be handled by a professional service due to their size or location. Whenever possible DPW employees will work with the
sub-contractor or the power company in order to reduce the cost. The entire $9,000 appropriation was expended.
TRAFFIC SIGNS AND DEVICES
Electricity charges for caution and school zone flashers, and the traffic signals at the four corners of Whalom Rd. are paid from
this account. Street line painting, crosswalks, stop bars, and parking spaces are also a part of this line item as well as
standardized warning and street signs. Because of the sewer project and other road construction only $3,300 was spent the
balance was encumbered to the next year at which time final paving will be completed.
GENERAL HIGHWAY MAINTENANCE
The cost of paving and patching materials, drainage supplies, roadside mowing, and street sweeping are funded through this
line. Also the purchase of hand tools, small equipment Items and the subcontracting of tasks beyond the scope of the DPW and
not covered by State funding is paid for through this account. Because the fiscal overlaps the construction season money is
often encumbered to the next season. Such was the case this year: $100,000 was the budgeted amount with $48,000 brought
forward. A total of $131,219 was used ad an additional $17,3000 was forwarded to the next fiscal year.
LABOR AND OVERTIME
An amount totaling $270,711 was budgeted to cover wages for Highway Division payroll including clerical staff, lead mechanic,
and one assistant mechanic. The total amount was expended. Emergency calls (excluding winter operations) paid at an
overtime rate totaled $2,250 exceeding the $2,000 allotment.
SNOW REMOVAL
During winter months, clearing the roadways of ice and snow is the primary function of the DPW. The department is in a
cooperative with fourteen other communities in order to insure the best possible price per ton. Sand that is mixed with salt to
treat roadways is screened by town personnel and hauled to the highway garage during the summer. During winter weather
emergency subcontractors and public works employees combine over twenty pieces of snow fighting equipment to clear and
maintain the 100 miles of roadways, sidewalks, and parking lots throughout the Town. An appropriation of $135,000 was totally
expended but did not go into deficit. Clearly an unusually mild winter season.
SEWER CONSTRUCTION
Phase I of Contract I sewer installation proceeded throughout the entire construction season within the Whalom area of town.
The main line piping was completed with house laterals installed to each property line. A major pumping station was built on
Dana Street that will in the future become an integral part of Contract II as well. Whalom Road was milled and repaved and the
sidewalk running the entire length of the roadway was completely rebullt. Any of the side streets entering Whalom Road that
were not repaved will be completed in the Spring of 2003.

HOUSING AUTHORITY
The Lunenburg Housing Authority was founded in 1968, and is charted to oversee, manage and develop State-aided housing
within the Town. Within this charter, the Authority, in participation with other community leaders, also has responsibility to
assure adequate sources of affordable private housing for Lunenburg. The Authority currently manages 48 subsidized
apartments under Chapter 667, known as Pearl Brook Housing for the Elderly. Our family housing is adjacent to the Pearl Brook
Apartments and consists of six townhouse style apartments consisting of two and three bedroom units.
Applications for State-alded housing may be obtained at 131 White Street, Lunenburg or by calling 978-345-7655 or 978-537-5300
(in Leominster). To be eligible for elderly housing, applicants must be at least 60 years old at the time of application. All other
applicants, including handicapped and disabled individuals must be of legal age to enter into a lease. The Authority will grant a
preference for Lunenburg residents. Veterans that qualify will also receive a preference. Rent is based on your household's
annual adjusted gross Income. Elderly residents will pay 30% of their adjusted Income, while residents of our family housing will
pay 25% of their adjusted Income and all utilities (excluding water and sewer).
In August of 2002, the Leominster Housing Authority began administrating the Lunenburg Housing Authority. The Lunenburg
Housing Authority believes that the management of contracting services with other agencies, in terms of quality management
services, is a valuable tool for success. This cooperative effort has allowed the Lunenburg Housing Authority to offer greater
services and assistance to our clients while maintaining our strong leadership and Involvement In the community.
The Authority has maintained its membership in the Massachusetts. Chapter of the National Association of Housing
Redevelopment Officials (NAHRO). Participation in NAHRO helps assure that the Authority is up-to-date with programs and
funding sources available for use within Lunenburg.

SPECIAL SERVICES
The Montachusett Regional Vocational Technical School District currently provides special education services for two
hundred and sixty-seven students. Our mission Is to comply with Public Law 105-17, the 1997 amendments to the Individuals
with Disabilities Act (known as IDEA-97") and Chapter 766 of the Acts of 1972, MGL c.71B (603CMR 28.00) to provide a free
appropriate public education (FAPE) to students with disabilities in the least restrictive environment.
Students are Identified, evaluated, and, when necessary, special education services are prescribed to help them
progress effectively in regular and special education classes to develop their individual educational potential. All students'
educational plans are developed by the prescribed Team with parental involvement and Individualized to meet their needs.
Parents are invited to participate in initial, review, or re-evaluation Team meetings and are notified in their primary language. Our
Parents' Rights Pamphlets are in English and Spanish. Physical and occupational therapy services are provided on an as-
needed basis (speech therapy is provided to forty students per week).
We have created the position of Special Education Vocational Resource Teacher, who works with the special needs
students in their trades to assure that the trade is appropriate to the students' capabilities and aspirations and they will achieve
to their highest potential. This was recommended by the Department of Education during their Spring 2002 audit.
The Montachusett Regional Vocational Technical School District Program for the Deaf continues to provide a
comprehensive vocational and academic educational program for qualified students Identified as being sensory impaired in the
area of hearing. Presently, there are six students from the district being served by one certified teacher of the deaf, with three
full-time interpreter assistants and two part-time Interpreter assistants and a speech pathologist. These students are In regular
education classes with assistance from Interpreters. The climate and culture of the Monty Tech environment have made for a
very successful Integration of the hearing impaired students.

SUPERINTENDENT'S MESSAGE
Loxi Jo Calmes, Acting Superintendent
By action of the School Committee on November 6, 2002 the Lunenburg Public Schools adopted a five-year strategic plan that
will guide the operation of the schools. While always important, in times of fiscal emergencies like the one the Commonwealth
and by extension the Town and the District are experiencing, the strategic plan provides a validated format for decision making
and resource allocation. The group that developed the plan represented a cross section of the community, which Included
students, parents, faculty, administrators, a member of the school committee and community leaders. Serving on this
committee were: Christie Letarte, Keith Hochstein, Susan Cavaioli, Carol Higgins, Anna Keegan, Mary Ann Weisman, Chad
Adams, Milree Keeling, Elaine Murphy, Roger St. Jean, Cynthia Daukantas, Colleen Stillman, Michael Barney and Loxl Jo Calmes.
Our Vision: The Lunenburg Public Schools prides itself on excellence. We provide a child-centered environment that challenges
all learners to achieve their highest potential. This is accomplished through a partnership with a supportive community.
Our Mission: We prepare our students for life-long learning and responsible community membership.
Our Guiding Beliefs-We Belleve:
Education is a community partnership.
Learning is reflective, creative, ongoing and takes place in all environments.
A successful school actively engages students and staff.
All students have talents and can excel.
Professional growth is fundamental to instructional excellence.
All students are capable of learning and are entitled to instructional excellence.
Staff and students are entitled to a safe, healthy and positive learning environment.
An atmosphere of mutual respect is essential for learning.
Our Goals and Objectives:
1. To foster the talents, skills, and potential of all students.
1.1. To expose all students to a variety of opportunities to explore individual interests and talents.
1.2. To develop a process and structure for implementing transition plans for each student.
2. To ensure that staff and programs are conducive to instructional excellence.
2.1. To develop and Implement a process of data-drive decision-making.
2.2. To create a professional development program based on data and need.
2.3. To identify staffing needs and make recommendations for hiring staff.
3. To use technology as an integral component of teaching, learning, and management.
3.1. To develop a district technology plan to address equipment and staffing needs.
3.2. To provide professional development to raise the level of appropriate technological use in the instructional program
and to Improve communication.
4. To provide facilities that meet staff, program, and community needs.
4.1. To complete a needs assessment for building transitions related to construction of the new primary school.
4.2. To develop and implement a centralized process for building use.
4.3. To work in partnership with the community to promote present and future capital projects.

The "No Child Left Behind" Act Influenced much of our district and Improvement work throughout the year. This Act is the
reauthorization of the federal Elementary and Secondary Schools Act. The intent of the legislation is to ensure every child in the
nation receive a quality education. High academic proficiency levels, highly qualified teachers, a safe, drug-free and conducive
learning environment, and all students graduating from high school are the goals of the legislation. Our schools are working with
the Massachusetts Department of Education on a Consolidated District Planning Initiative. Starting last spring the NCLB Team
began the district planning process. Student data has been reviewed, performance gaps Identified, and Improvement objectives
are in the process of being developed. The next steps include identifying research-based strategies to address the improvement
objectives and developing a three-year strategic plan. This collaborative, data-driven, and research-based process will ensure
students and teachers receive the needed resources to address our Improvement objectives.
The fifth administration of the Massachusetts Comprehensive Assessment System (MCAS) took place in the spring of 2002. In
Lunenburg 96% of tenth grade students met the required benchmark for graduation in English/Language Arts and 90% met the
required benchmark for graduation in Mathematics. Overall, eighty-seven percent (87%) of tenth grade students passed both
required tests. Forty students (31%) met the performance criteria to apply for the Stanley Z. Koplik Certificate of Mastery.
Currently, ALL students attending Lunenburg High School have met the MCAS graduation requirement and upon completion of
the local graduation requirements will leave with a diploma.
MCAS performance Is reported by level (Advanced, Proficient, Needs Improvement, and Warning). Students at the Advanced
Level demonstrate a comprehensive and In-depth understanding of rigorous subject matter and provide sophisticated solutions
to complex problems. Students at the Proficient Level demonstrate a solid understanding of challenging subject matter and
solve a wide variety of problems. Students at the Needs Improvement Level demonstrate partial understanding of subject matter
and solve some simple problems. Students at the Warning Level demonstrate minimal understanding of subject matter and do
not solve even simple problems. In grade 3 results are reported in only three categories, Proficient, Needs Improvement, and
Warning.
