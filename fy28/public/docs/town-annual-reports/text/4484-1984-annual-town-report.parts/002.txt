Vital Statistics
Following are those records of Births, Marriages, and Deaths that either occured in our Town or records
which were forwarded to this Town from other municipalities in the State. Lunenburg residents are urged to
notify the Town Clerk if In-State records have not been received and recorded. If a Birth, Marriage, or Death OC-
curred outside the State, the same may be recorded in the Lunenburg Town Clerk's office if certified records
are presented. In some cases the Town Clerk's records are supplemented by records of burials which appear
elsewhere in this report. In order for these records to also be Included in the official Vital Statistics records, the
family of the deceased should present a certified copy of the death certificate to the Town Clerk. In accordance
with recent changes in the General Laws marriages which occur here but relate to licenses which were obtain-
ed elsewhere in the State are no longer included in our official Town Records.

Annual Report of
Superintendent of Schools
It is a privilege to submit my seventh annual report
as Superintendent of Schools in Lunenburg. This annual
report covers the year ending December 31, 1984.
As you read the reports of the other administrators,
you will receive a more detailed accounting of the events
that have taken place in the schools. The purpose of this
report is to summarize the total aspects of the Lunenburg
Public Schools.
WHERE ARE WE NOW?
I feel that this question needs to be somewhat held as
the issue of the year. Most citizens have their interpre-
tation of the answer to this question. Most teachers, stu-
dents and parents can give an assessment of the situation.
The school committee members, who are exposed to more
information and documentation than anyone beside theSuper-
intendent of Schools, have their own assessment. As the
WILLIAM C. ALLARD, Ed. D.
chief executive officer of the Lunenburg Public Schools, I
Superintendent of Schools
must be in a position to explain and answer this question
to all of the citizens in Lunenburg.
We have come through some very difficult times. The sudden cut back of monies available to the school
department after the passage of Proposition 21 required us in a very short period of time to lay off many
staff members and cut our expenses. We were not afforded the luxury of time to affect these changes and con-
sequently it has taken time and effort to recapture a number of those losses. This has been accomplished by
a slow and deliberate process. Over those years we have made an attempt to improve our programs, update our
staff through inservice courses, apply for and receive a number of federal and state grants, introduce new
programs and seek out the most qualified staff available for any position that became open. All of these
efforts have resulted in placing us here today. I could write a long litany of programs and needs that we
have addressed over the years to indicate our relative position of strength. Suffice it to say that we have
what we have today because of the efforts of everyone.
We are a quality school system. I say that because all evidence used to indicate this has been applied
to us. For example, our graduates do get accepted to excellent schools. All state reports filed indicate
that our students score well above average on their work. Our daily attendance to our schools is on the
average 95%-96%. Our individual programs bring credit to our schools and our students. Our athletic teams
are well staffed with students and they are well coached. We are in compliance with all mandates from the
State Board, of Education and we have received commendations on our special education programs, basic skills
programs and our financial accounting. We feel that we provide many opportunities for student, parent, staff
and community input. Organizations such as L-CAN, SADD, Principals' Advisory Committees, student organiza-
tions, Lunenburg Education Association, Lunenburg Public Schools Community Relations Committee are available
for input and discussions. The bottom line is that we do have an outstanding staff of teachers, principals,
secretaries, custodians, food service personnel available on a daily basis to service the needs of the stu-
dents, parents and citizens of this community.
We are now, however, just short of making this one of the top school systems in the state. We need more
staff to implement programs that are either missing or partially implemented. I am referring to additional
staff in the music department, libraries, foreign language and science areas. We also need to increase the
funding of our athletic budgets to remove the burden of fundraising from the booster clubs. These are a few
of the items that address the issue of providing more for our young people. We, in administration, will
attempt to provide for an increased growth and accountability for our programs.
JANUARY 1984 - JUNE 1984:
Although we had a successful year in completing our academic school year, this was a particularly
difficult time because of a very tight budget. As you will recall, we received an increase of 4.3% over the
previous year to run the schools. Since this operation is highly labor intensified, we made the decision to
cut the expense portion of our budget by $124,000. over the previous year's expenditures. Those cuts show
up during the year, but they become very noticeable by the year's end. However, in spite of our financial
difficulties, we completed the year quite well. The school department finished the year with all bills
paid and in compliance with all state mandates. I would dare say, however, that we did end the year with
our cupboards bare!
Much of the administrators and school committee time during this period was spent in preparing for the
FY-85 budget presentation to the Finance Committee and to the town meeting. This presentation was based on
-91-
===PAGE 94===
the fact that the present FY-84 budget was not adequate to support a quality educational system. It was
obvious to all who had any knowledge about the school system that we needed a great financial boost in the
expense portion of our budget. We also needed to get on with a proper maintenance program for our
facilities.
At the annual town meeting in May 1984, the town voted a budget increase of 10.4% in the school budget.
In addition, we received support for two articles. One article supported $70,000. for the repairs to the
high school roof and the other supported $31,000. for installation of a wheelchair lift to be installed at
the high school. We ended the school year making plans and preparations for the new school year.
SEPTEMBER 1984 - DECEMBER 1984:
After the town meeting in May, we immediately set out to provide this town with the proper staffing for
the opening of school in September. In addition, we proceeded to purchase the necessary materials, text-
books, and equipment purchases necessary for the opening of school. Furthermore, we went out to bid for the
continuation of the roof repair at the high school and also sought bids for the wheelchair lift to be in-
stalled at the high school. We worked very diligently to seek out the best available staff to fill new
positions or replacements for existing positions. By September, we were prepared for the opening of the
school year buoyed by the increase in the budget and an opportunity to put this school system back on the
direction of reclaiming some of the quality it had lost over the past few years.
WHAT DID A 10.4% INCREASE IN THE LUNENBURG PUBLIC SCHOOLS BUDGET ATTAIN?
In this portion I will attempt to provide the reader with an overview of what has been accomplished
with a total budget of $4,367,500., an increase of 10.4% over the previous FY-84 budget. Let me at the out-

for schools will always guarantee high quality educational experiences for our children.
All of us share in the pride of an excellent school system here in Lunenburg, and recognize that the
everyday operation of the schools takes a major portion of our finances. We know that curriculum, programs,
maintenance of facilities, discipline and the quality of life in our schools are already of high achieve-
ment, but with the increase in funding we have been able to respond to a number of problem areas.
I will divide this portion into three parts to clarify what the 10.4% increase provided in the areas of
facilities, staff, and curriculum.

THE FUTURE:
We are on the verge of unprecedented attention to public schools in this country. The care and concern
given to this topic nationwide gives credence to the pride that Americans take in their public schoolsystem.
In Lunenburg, excellent schools have been a tradition. Excellent schools, even as a tradition just do not
happen or continue to exist. They require the deliberate, long-range planning of a community. The citizens
of Lunenburg have always realized the impact that excellent schools have on the value of their real estate.
The existence of quality schools should have higher ideals. It is these ideals that I direct the future
goals of the system.
1. In Lunenburg, each child will receive the maximum attention available to develop his/her talents to
the fullest.
2. We will continue to maintain our facilities so as to provide an excellent quality of life for the
students attending and also to protect the investment this town has made.
3. We will provide avenues of communication for all citizens, parents, students and staff for input as
to the direction they wish the system to proceed.
4. Each adult working within the Lunenburg Public Schools will have one purpose in mind. They are here
to provide caring service to the children who attend.
5. The administration will provide the community with adequate and well documented information as to
potential growth and development of the school system.

The decision to close schools is made by the Superintendent of Schools after consulting with the High-
way Superintendent and the Police Department to determine the condition of the roads. The practice is to
close schools only in case of extreme weather conditions or unforeseen emergency; therefore, when schools
are in session on stormy days, parents are urged to exercise their personal judgment as to the wisdom of
sending their children to school.

DEVELOPMENTS IN THE HIGH SCHOOL CURRICULUM:
The English Department has continued its emphasis on developing writing through the offering in this
school year of a one semester Writing Workshop course. Here students can get intensive faculty assistance
with creative and expository writing. Another objective of this course is an attempt to reestablish the
school literary magazine, and the course has been enthusiastically received by students and parents. Another
new offering is an English Department course in Accelerated Reading. This semester course answers the need
for motivated students to increase their rate in reading, as well as comprehension skills which have life-
long benefits.
The Business Department has received a federal grant to expand the offerings in Business and Office
Practice. Utilizing newly acquired electronic typewriters, new calculators, and transcription hardware, stu-
dents will be better prepared to undertake automated office work leaving high school.
The Science Department is offering a newly developed grade nine course, and Introduction to Physicaland
Biological Science. This course is designed to provide more science experience for those students who might
not otherwise enroll in this department as freshmen. Utilizing a number of high interest laboratory units,
the course presents the scientific backgrounds of issues and problems which are familiar to students intheir
daily lives as citizens and consumers. This course is part of the general academic program emphasis in grade
nine. It is also hoped that it will lead to an increase in student interest in later courses such as Chem-
istry, Physics, and Advanced Biology.
