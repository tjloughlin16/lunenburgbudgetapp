UPDATE ON THE ACADEMICS:
Having received our most recent standardized test results, and having interpreted those results, we are
most pleased to report that the students at. the T. C. Passios Elementary School compare very well to the top
third of the nation, academically. Scores in the basic skill areas of reading, math, language, and writing
are very high.
First graders "drilled" in reading skills.
Fifth graders "boning up" in the school library.
This information assures us that the programs incorporated in the school have been very effective. It
also indicates that we should continue in the direction we have been pursuing. Therefore, we will continue
to stress carefully monitored instruction in reading, math, writing, and mechanics usage, incorporating the
use of Achievement Monitoring Programs (AMP), writing rating systems, and reading management systems.
Since work has been completed in putting those programs into place, we are now directing our attention
toward the content areas of science and social studies. This year we are studying our social studies curric-
ulum and text. We are evaluating both, with the intent of revision of the kindergarten through grade five
curriculum. A new text was purchased for school year 1984-85. The task of the staff and administration is
to insure that a sequential, logical, K-5 program is established, maximizing the use of the new text and
incorporating meaningful supplementals.

AFTER-SCHOOL PROGRAMS INITIATED:
A need has long been expressed to provide organized after-school activities for the elementary school-
age child. Unfortunately, funding such programs appeared to be an impossibility. However, this year, a new
approach was taken, and a very successful, well subscribed After-School Activities program is ongoing at the
Elementary School.
During the summer and early fall, after-school activity proposals were drafted and presented to the
superintendent and school committee for their consideration. The proposals requested a minimum, school
system contribution to help support the after-school activities. However, the proposals stated that the
major portion of funds for the program would be generated by "users fees".
Since the "users fees" concept was new to the elementary level, parent and staff input was sought, as to
the feasibility of incorporating this concept to fund the program. The response from both groups was most
positive. Hence, the after-school activities on a "users fee" basis, were implemented.
Sports activities being offered this year are: soccer, hiking, cross country skiing, poly hockey,
wiffle ball, gymnastics, volleyball, T-ball, and precision rope jumping. Art activities will consist of
papier mache', cross stitch, and ceramics class.

The After-School Activities have been a great success. Additional sessions have had to be scheduled in
order to respond to the numbers of children wishing to participate.
It is the goal of the school to teach the children meaningful skills in the arts and athletics, in

are accomplishing this through the Elementary After-School Activity Program.

SERVICES PROVIDED TO PARENTS OF ELEMENTARY STUDENTS:
In response to requests for assistance in parenting issues, Lois Davenport, Elementary School Guidance
Counselor/School Psychologist, has offered parenting workshop sessions. Mrs. Davenport, a trained Active
Parenting Program Instructor, has conducted morning and evening sessions of two-hour duration, for periods
of six weeks a workshop.
Active Parenting is a program designed to teach parents strategies to become more effective in dealing
with their children. The video tape/discussion format is non-threatening, interesting, and appealing to_
parents.
Active Parenting Workshops have been so popular with
parents that Mrs. Davenport will conduct two more six-
week sessions prior to the end of this school year.

In Memory Of
ROBERT C. COLEMAN
Less than a year after his retirement as primary school custodian, Bob Coleman passed away on May 13,
1984 in the University Hospital in Worcester. As well as being a custodian with the school department, he
also owned and operated a sewing machine repair business for many years. In addition, Mr. Coleman was a
well-known musician, playing as a bassist through the north county with the George Nyman Trio for many
years. Bob was born in Kalamazoo, Michigan and lived many years in South Ashburnham and the past twenty
years in Lunenburg. He was a Marine Corps veteran of World War II and a survivor of Pearl Harbor. He left
his wife, Rita (Watt) Coleman, three sons, four daughters, seven grandchildren and several nephews and
nieces.
Retirements During 1984
SALLY A. McCUE
September 1, 1959 - June 30, 1984
Mrs. McCue is a graduate of Fitchburg State College and had taken graduate courses at the same institu-
tion while a member of the elementary teaching staff. Sally came to Lunenburg as a teacher of grade five in
September 1959, having taught previously in East Hartford, Connecticut and Fitchburg, Massachusetts. She
served the school system over the twenty-five year period with dedication, competency, conscientiousness,
and dependability. She was very outgoing and a joy to have around. We all miss her, but hope she is enjoy-
ing her retirement to the fullest. (See also Report of Elementary Principal)
PRISCILLA A. FREEMAN
September 1, 1964 - June 30, 1984
Mrs. Freeman received her bachelor's degree from Tufts University in 1950 and taught in Athol, Sudbury,
and Groton, Massachusetts before joining the elementary staff in September 1964 as physical education
instructor. She retired in June 1984 after twenty years of dedicated service. Priscilla was soft spoken
but with definite ideas for her classes and 'a big concern for the students under her tutelage. She is
-
missed by all the children who were fortunate to have had her as a teacher. May she enjoy many years of
retirement. (See also Report of Elementary School Principal)
DONALD T. KENNEY
January 2, 1969 - June 30, 1984
Mr. Kenney became a member of the custodial staff of the Lunenburg Public Schools on January 2, 1969
when he began as a junior custodian at the high school. The following September he transferred to the
middle school until January 1971 when he transferred again to the elementary annex. In November of 1973,
Don was appointed as senior custodian at the Passios Elementary School replacing someone. who had retired.
When the senior groundsperson/utility person retired in April 1979, Don applied and was appointed to this
position. When this position was deleted in 1981, Don returned to the Passios Elementary School as senior
custodian until his retirement on June 30, 1984. Don brought to all the positions that he held a sense of
maturity, pride, concern and care which are' so essential in a school environment. Presently, Don is a
resident of Englewood, Florida, enjoying his retirement to the fullest, we are sure. (See also Report
of
Elementary School Principal)
M. NOREEN KITTREDGE
September 1953 - June 30, 1955;
January 1960 - June 30, 1984
Mrs. Kittredge received her bachelor's and master's degrees from Fitchburg State College and during her
tenure in Lunenburg, completed thirty hours credit beyond her master's.
Noreen came to Lunenburg in September 1953 as a teacher of grade one, having taught the previous year
in Springfield, Massachusetts. In June of 1955, Noreen resigned to accept the position of Supervisor at the
Fitchburg State College Training School. However, she returned to Lunenburg again in January of 1960 to
finish out the school year in grade three. In September of the same year, she transferred to a grade one
position and taught at this grade level until her retirement in June 1984. Noreen was a sincere and
committed individual and a highly sensitive, warm and caring person. She is missed by all; we wish her the
best in her years of retirement. (See also Report of Elementary Principal)
