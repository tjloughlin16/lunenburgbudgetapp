MEMORIAM
ELLEN KEMP
1921 - 1989
Ellen Kemp-something rare--a life-long
resident of Lunenburg. She was born Ellen
Harrington at 1325 Massachusetts Avenue.
Ellen graduated with the Class of 1940 and
lived the rest of her life at 1170
Massachusetts Avenue. Ellen's interest
in the people of the Town showed in her
variety of volunteer positions. She started
as a Cub Scout Den Mother, and worked her
way through to the Historical Society,
Personnel Board, Council on Aging and
Building Committee. Her long nursing
career made it natural for her to be active
in the Red Cross and a member of the
Nashoba Associated Boards of Health. She
will be remembered for her caring and
dedication to the Town of Lunenburg.
LAUREN W. DAVIES
1911-1989
Lauren Davies served as Lunenburg's
Inspector of Weights and Measures from
1979 to 1989. He was born in Fitchburg,
but lived in Lunenburg since 1952. He
served as a Master Sergeant in World War
II as part of the European Theater of
Operations. He was a member of the
Lunenburg Taxpayer's Association and was
employed by the B & M Railroad for over
twenty years. He will be sadly missed.

BOARD OF SELECTMEN
Janet Goodrich was elected to her first full term as a Board member after completing a
year and a half of an unexpired term. Janet's new term began with a very difficult
year for the Town financially. The Commonwealth's budget problems were directly felt
by Lunenburg when over $300,000 was withheld from the Town's cherry sheet. To
further complicate matters, the quarterly distribution of State aid was changed to a
semi-annual distribution, thus forcing Lunenburg to borrow money for operating
expenses.
The Town held two Special Town Meetings this year to deal with the financial
problems. One held in July authorized an override of slightly over $300,000. The
second Special Town Meeting was held in September to balance our budget after the
local aid cut. To further deal with the Town's growing fiscal problems, Lunenburg
received $20,000 in emergency assistance funds from the State. This money will go
into next year's budget to help defray additional cuts.
July 1, 1989 brought with it a newly created position, i.e. Town Accountant. Rebecca
Whitten was hired to this position and has been organizing the accounting department.
The Annual Town Meeting this year established a separate Board of Health, its
members to be elected at the next Annual Election in 1990. On other health matters,
the sanitary landfill has been improved significantly as the result of a new contract,
and well regulations were initiated for individual water supplies.
In continuing the betterment program, three roads were authorized for betterments:
Ruth, Parmenter and Johnson Streets all of which are located in the Lake Shirley area.
The close of 1989 came with a particularly joyous holiday celebration. Mrs. Edith
Joyal donated a beautiful Christmas tree for the Town Center. All the decorations
were donated as was the labor to trim the tree. All this led to a delightful Christmas
tree lighting ceremony--hopefully the first of a fine Lunenburg tradition. The Board
wishes to extend its thanks to all the volunteers who have given of themselves to the
Town over the past year.
TOWN COUNSEL
This year my efforts on the Town's behalf were much more diversified than in past
years. Code enforcement, land use and water issues were the areas where I spent
most of my time. The now infamous "barking dog case" coupled with the push to
remove abandoned and junk cars resulted in a great deal of court time.
Even though the real estate boom has abated, zoning and land use related issues have
again taken up a great deal of time. Because of the technical nature of these areas,
zoning, planning, and conservation are among the most demanding municipal positions.
Lunenburg is fortunate to have experienced personnel on these Boards. Because of the
competent, experienced membership on these Boards, my time spent was limited.
Finally, the Selectmen's efforts in assisting those Lunenburg homes on the Leominster
Water system have met with mixed success. A great deal of time was spent meeting
with Leominster officials and officials from the State Department of Environmental
Protection. Senator Padula also assisted in attempting to resolve the problem. To
date, despite a great deal of time and effort, there has been no resolution.

BOARD OF ASSESSORS
The year 1989 was a very busy and significant one for the Board of Assessors and it's
office staff. The single most visible accomplishment was the successful completion of
a full 1990 revaluation with subsequent certification by the Department of Revenue.
In addition, efforts continued to automate office procedures and to utilize computer
capabilities to minimize costs as well as provide additional information and services.
Judy Holden completed her first full year as Assistant Assessor, and played a major
role in the successful in-house revaluation efforts. Lisa Normandin and Nancine
Brooks, as part-time clerks, effectively handled many of the on-going office tasks.
All staff members have participated in the automation efforts. Tax Base information
- 5 -
===PAGE 8===
for the entire Town was loaded onto the computer; tax bills were computer-generated,
and parcel lists for office and public use are now prepared on the computer. The
CAMA software was successfully installed and used in the revaluation.
The 1990 revaluation was completed by the Assessors using the CAMA software.
Certification was obtained from the Department of Revenue, and tax bills were mailed
on schedule. The importance of this was clear, in view of the fiscal uncertainty
associated with State decisions on local aid levels and the corresponding Proposition 2
1/2 override. The Board of Assessors would like to take this opportunity to express
particular appreciation to Assessor John Male for his efforts.
Voters at Town Meeting determine the amount of money to be raised by taxation. The
goal of the assessment process is to apportion this tax burden equitably among Town
taxpayers based on the full and fair market value of real property. Property values
increased substantially in the recent past and are reflected in the assessed values.
This was accompanied by a corresponding decrease in the tax rate. Throughout the
revaluation the Assessors attempted to explain the process, hold public meetings on
State programs available to landowners, conduct impact hearings with individual
taxpayers and, in general, to make available for public review the facts on which the
revaluation was based. We wish to express our appreciation to the taxpayers for their
active participation in this understandably sensitive and necessary undertaking. On
an on-going basis, the Board of Assessors intends to continually update property data
to ensure that the tax burden is apportioned as accurately as possible.
There are various tax exemptions available to qualifying taxpayers. Complete
information may be obtained at the Assessor's Office in Town Hall, or by phone at

RITTER MEMORIAL LIBRARY
The year 1989 has been without question the most unusual year in the seventy-nine
year history of Ritter Library. In January, Fairway Construction Company began work
on long-planned renovations. The following months tested the endurance of both
Library users and staff as service continued with but one brief shutdown when a
trench for pipes was jackhammered across the main floor. On completion of the job in
April, new bathrooms accessible to the handicapped were available on the street floor
in what had been the staff area, and many years of plumbing disasters were over.
The staff area was relocated downstairs in the former bathroom space, providing
substantial improvements.
The remodeling dust had not settled when the Finance Committee announced its
recommendation for balancing the FY90 budget by among other things, closing the
Library. At the request of other Departments, the Library Trustees made no plea for
funding at the Annual Town Meeting, the idea being that nothing short of a closed
Library would induce voters to pass an override large enough to help all Departments.
The Library shut down in late June and the staff was laid off. In late July a second
override attempt succeeded and Ritter Library reopened with services quickly returning
to normal. Although the children's summer reading program had been cancelled, twice
weekly activities were planned for them in August and other usual summer activities
were advanced to the fall.
In addition to the completed renovations, another positive aspect in this difficult year
has been the number of successful programs, starting with a Twelfth Night Celebration
in January as the climax of the "10,000 Page Challenge" adult reading program. Black
History Month was observed in February with poetry reading and song. In May, the
Friends of the Library hosted an open house honoring all Librarians, present and
retired living in Lunenburg, and the Children's Art Festival. United Nations Day in
October was celebrated with an international cafe featuring ethnic food donated by
area restaurants and caterers, children's costumes and games, music, and speaker
Mable Garis, former United Nations representative and author of the Uncle Wiggly
stories. The second annual Prime Time Sleepover took place in November and was
enjoyed by seventy-five third and fourth graders who spent the evening producing a
mime circus and sleeping in the United Parish Hall with numerous adults chaperoning
and cooking breakfast.
Other children's activities included the usual pre-school story hours for about one
hundred registered children, craft programs, Library services for the Primary School,
and a six-week workshop with children writing, illustrating and binding books which
they themselves presented to the Library. Helping school children to locate and
utilize material for assignments is one of the Library's most demanding ongoing
functions.
- 7 -
===PAGE 10===
The Friends of the Library have been involved in virtually every program for children
by supplying materials, food and/or volunteer help. In the same manner, they are a
part of most adult programs and, in fact, every facet of Library service. Their
unfailing support is a major morale booster for staff and Trustees. Lunenburg
Memorial Fund has again been generous to the Library with funds for books in memory
of Raymond Chapdelaine and others. Arts Lottery grants made several programs
possible. Memorial gifts have been received for Ralph Benjamin, Paul Mason, Edward
McConnell and Harry E. Donovan.
Total circulation of materials for 1989 was 43,461. Comparative circulation figures are
without meaning in a year featuring the previously described disruptions. Another
unfortunate result of the temporary closing of the Library was that funds for pressing
building needs were voted down at Town Meeting. The wooden gutters, and in some
sections, the overhang behind the gutters are rotting out, possibly beyond repair. It
is time for a few ounces of prevention, including exterior painting, before another
building begins to go the way of the Town Hall.
Much credit is due the Library staff who continued to provide service as usual amid
clouds of plaster dust, noise, drafts and disarranged materials and work space. They
moved heavy furniture and countless books, and moved and cleaned them again when
construction was finished. They then worked on through several months of
uncertainty about their jobs and benefits in the midst of the fiscal crisis which
culminated in four weeks of unemployment. Yet they returned to work in August and
began at once to make up for lost time by providing all of the usual services and
programs plus exciting new ones.
Even the weeks without Library service did not reveal all of the cost to Lunenburg of
the Library's ongoing financial struggle. Long range plans, whether for expanding
services, improving the collection, or providing adequate maintenance of the building
are useless when the question of whether or not the Library will remain open is
paramount for months at a time. In fact, short range planning in these circumstances
takes disproportionate amounts of time, and often eventually proves futile. The
demoralizing effect on staff, Trustees and those who depend on service cannot be
overstated. The Library is one of the Town's major assets and, as such, is
underutilized because lack of funds for staffing has sharply curtailed open hours.
This spacious, handsome building is centrally located and in relatively good condition.
Instead of reducing the availability year by year, the Town should be maximizing its
use as a source of information and recreation, a meeting place, and a focal point of
the community.
PLANNING BOARD
This year has been a little different for the Planning Board because of the area
banking problems, State problems and the present economy. We processed twenty-three
ANR (Approval Not Required) plans involving fifty-three lots. Last year we processed
thirty-six plans with eighty lots. A great deal of our work has been citizen and
developer contact for assistance in land use.
We are still working with the Williams and Goodrichville Subdivisions. The Villages on
Blueberry Hill await construction start. Woodlands at Hickory Hills, at the time of
this writing, is still tied up with the FDIC. The Maplewood Golf Course project is
expected to file as soon as the Department of Environmental Protection Agency
approves their submittal. Commercial Development Review plans are in process with
the Heritage Credit Union, the Village and the Exxon Station. No further submittals

have been processed on Levine Farm, Lancaster Heights, King Arthur Estates or
Northfield Woods. Contact has been made with the Planning Board concerning Samuel
Page Marketplace. There have also been commercial and industrial inquiries.
The Growth Management Plan/Strategic Plan was presented at a public meeting on
October 16, 1989. These plans became the addendum to the Master Plan update of
1983. In these documents, strategies were prioritized in zoning, facilities,
infrastructure, funding, etc. Seven strategic actions were presented in the plan. Five
of them involve study committees. They are the Long Range Resource Committee,
Sewer Feasibility Study Committee, Transportation Circulation Analysis Committee,
Innovative Housing Study Committee and Handbook Committee. These Committees have
representation from all major Boards, Committees, Commissions, Department Heads and
citizens of the community.
The charges of these Committees are summarized below:
Long Range Resource Study Committee: To develop and evaluate a data base of
resources, and devise a methodology for municipal facilities, policies, capital and
budgetary plans.
Sewer Feasibility Study Committee: To assess the need for sewers, investigate
feasibility of connections with already established plants, and research legal
requirements and procedures.
Innovative Housing Study Committee: To research inclusive data for housing
strategies including methodologies and implementations. To write collected data into a
Housing Plan for the Town of Lunenburg for approval by the Board of Selectman and
Planning Board.
Traffic Circulation Analysis Study Committee: To identify road conditions, traffic
problem areas, existing traffic levels and propose recommendation of implementation
methodologies for improvements of same.
Handbook Committee: To establish a workbook providing the community with
information for procedures in land use and other municipal regulations.
Two other action plans were strategically planned. One is a Regional Monitoring
System. The Board has contacted twenty communities, however, the response at
present is 25%. Further contacts will be made. The other plan is a Regional Review
System. Funds are needed to carry out this plan properly. A simple start, however,
has been initiated by sending to communities copies of letter addressed to the
Massachusetts Environmental Protection Agency (MEPA) reviews listed in Environmental
Monitor. Cover letters are written to enlist regional concern.
The Zoning By-law annual review by the Planning Board coincides with the writing of
this report. The Planning Board is considering updates on the Sign, Water Supply
Protection District, PRA Bylaws as well as minor additions and adjustments to the By-
Law. The review consists of a review meeting with Town Committees and Department
Heads. Professional input is included and a public hearing is held before the Town
Meeting vote.

The Planning Board's thrust in recent years has been geared toward protective
compliances in both the Zoning Bylaw and the Rules and Regulations Governing the
Subdivision of Land. This thrust has been proper because of the problem areas our
Town has in land use.
This Board hears loud and clear the mandated direction of planning in these times.
This mandate can be looked on as two-fold, moral and statutory. While the Board
still honors protection, we will have to encompass emphasis on correction of problems
and fulfillment of basic needs. The direction should be developed by strong data
based methodology and flexible criteria. This approach can then provide the
community with the fulfillment of our charge as described in the Zoning Enabling Act,
Chapter 40A, and Chapter 41.
LUNENBURG HOUSING AUTHORITY
The Lunenburg Housing Authority is charted to oversee, manage and develop State-
aided housing within the Town. Within this charter, the Authority, in participation
with other community leaders, also has responsibility to assure adequate sources of
affordable private housing for Lunenburg. The Authority currently manages 48
subsidized apartments under Chapter 667, known as Pearl Brook Housing for the
Elderly.
Broadening its role in providing assisted housing, the Authority oversaw the
completion of Chapter 705 Family Housing on property adjacent to the Pearl Brook
facilities during the late summer. Currently occupied are three modern duplex
buildings each containing two and three bedroom apartments. A primary goal in
creating this family housing was to give the tenants maximum responsibility for the
maintenance of their dwellings, yards and parking areas.
Developing affordable single family and rental housing units within Lunenburg provides
both social and economic benefits. Young families and children of Lunenburg families,
as well as the people who provide important services within our Town, can afford
housing in this community. New families can relocate here, where they can contribute
to our fine community life.
Members of the Housing Authority have been very active participating with other Town
Boards throughout the year in order to better understand local housing needs, and to
provide housing opportunities for the future. Most notable participation was with the

FIRE DEPARTMENT
During calendar year 1989, the Fire Department responded to 575 incidents. The calls
are classified as follows: Helicopter Landing - 1; Sprinkler Alarms - 6; Structure 50;
Motor Vehicle - 14; Motor Vehicle Accidents - 12; Investigations - 34; Explosions - 1;

The Fire Department continues it's struggle in trying to find enough resources and
money to operate and provide the basic fire protection and medical assistance that our
Townspeople demand of us. It is getting very difficult to survive in this climate of
economic uncertainty. Fire protection and ambulance service are two basic services
that the public demands. Unfortunately, they cost money. When the Town turns a
deaf ear to our requests for budgetary money or apparatus replacement, we all suffer.
We protect your lives and property, but we can't do it without the proper apparatus or
a realistic budget to adequately fund our operation.
For four years I have stressed the need to replace Engine 1. I will ask again in
1990, as this truck has some very serious problems. We have had to downgrade the
duties of this truck and are not using it to its full capacity. In addition, we have
two other trucks that have major problems that have to be addressed either through
major repairs or replacements. Our space needs and the need for a ladder truck are
still severely hampering our operation, as I have stated for the past two years. We
can only put these problems off for SO long before something drastic happens. We
always seem to wait for something catastrophic to happen before we realize that we
should have taken care of the problem beforehand.
The officers and members of the Fire Department ask for the support of our Town
officials and the public in our endeavor to provide this community with adequate fire
and medical services. I wish to express my sincere thanks to our firefighters and
EMTs for their dedicated service throughout the year.

CONSERVATION COMMISSION
During the 1989 calendar year, the Conservation Commission issued 37 Orders of
Conditions, 8 Wetland Determinations, conducted 130 on-site inspections, answered 300
service calls, and issued 5 Enforcement Orders.
The primary goal of the Commission remains to enforce the Wetlands Protection Act.
The Commission is also responsible for acquisition and management of Town
conservation land. During 1989, both wetlands enforcement and land acquisition have
grown increasingly complex. Our greatest need is to hire a professional conservation
officer.
- 21 -
===PAGE 24===
The Commission successfully secured two adjacent critical resource areas for permanent
protection. This newest conservation area is located within the Town's aquifer
protection district at the corner of Page and Arbor Streets. This area is mostly
wetlands and contains a freshwater stream which flows into the wellfields area.
Another acquired parcel of land, located on Howard Street, will be used to gain access
to several hundred acres of land-locked conservation land.
This year the Commission also initiated a new State-mandated fee system developed by
the Massachusetts Department of Environmental Protection (formerly DEQE). The fees
required under the local Wetlands By-law were rescinded when the stronger State fees
were issued. The new fee structure will result in an increase in Town revenue. We
feel this new revenue should be put back for conservation uses.
In November, the Commission issued an Order of Conditions to the Maplewood Golf
Course developers after lengthy hearings and many hours of work by the Commission
and a wetlands scientist. This "permit" was almost immediately appealed by the DEP
to allow them adequate time to evaluate a project of such magnitude.
An extremely urgent need for the Commission is to be provided with adequate office
space. In the spring of 1989 the Commission was forced to vacate the limited space
we had been using in the Brooks House. Most of the Commission's files, along with
desk and phone, were moved into the Park Department building. These cramped
quarters are also shared by the Town Nurse. At this point, we are continually
plagued by telephone problems and an inability to access files which are spread
throughout the Town. The Commission urges the Town to assist us in finding
permanent office space AND to hire a part-time professional conservation officer.
Over the last two years much of the required work has been done on a full-time basis
by our college intern, Chris Fors. After this period of volunteer work, Chris worked
on a paid eight hour a week basis to keep the Commission business rolling smoothly.

TOWN FOREST COMMITTEE
The Town Forest Committee membership consists of Scott W. Foster, Richard A. Bursch
and Chairman Kenneth P. Paton. All three members are lifelong residents of the Town
of Lunenburg, and have a sincere interest in preserving the Town Forest lands.
The Committee, with the aid of Forest Consultant, Robert Leupold, completed a contract
to thin an 8-acre plot off Burrage Street. Crowded and diseased trees were marked
for removal, and then cut and sold for cordwood by Contractor Charles Griffin. The
quantity of cordwood removed was approximately 60 cords. Mr. Griffin paid a lump
sum price for the cordwood, and a portion of those funds were used to pay Mr.
Leupold's fee. In addition to marking the trees, the Forest Consultant prepares
contract documents and manages the contract to insure the work is carried out in a
manner agreeable with the Town's interests. Additional proceeds from the thinning
contract will be used to enhance Town Forest lands. The work performed at the
Burrage Street property is one phase of the Town Forest Committee's Management Plan.
The goal is to improve growing conditions for healthy trees, and to enhance the forest
for wildlife and timber production.
The Committee was able to overcome a myriad of obstacles to complete a land swap
between the Harold Alatalo family and the Town of Lunenburg. The deal consisted of
swapping 2.025 acres of Town conservation land off Mulpus Road for 15.077 acres of
private land off Howard Street. The acquired land off Howard Street will be managed
by the Lunenburg Conservation Commission. The Town Forest Committee began the
process of accomplishing the land swap in May 1987, and encountered a surplus of
"red tape". The deal was finally consummated upon passing legislation by the
Massachusetts State Legislature. The acquired land off Howard Street is strategic in
that it provides access to 177 acres of Town land set back from the road. The cost
to the Town was less than $500, and the monies spent were for land survey, Registry
of Deeds, and legal costs.

TOWN CLERK, TAX COLLECTOR AND TREASURER
I am pleased to present my first report as Town Clerk, Tax Collector and Treasurer. It has been a year
of change and challenge. The office of Town Accountant was established as of July 1, 1989 and the
accounting functions have been successfully separated from the Treasurer's office. I look forward to the
continuing cooperation with Becky Whitten, Town Accountant. Her help and experience has allowed
me quickly to learn many of the ins and outs of my position.
My staff is to be commended for all their hard work in the everyday functions of these offices. Whether
it is their collecting money from taxpayers or advising callers what department should be able to help for
problems, my staff is concerned that the job is done well and on time. I am grateful for their
cooperation and support.
A special thank you to Bob Ebersole for his continuing assistance in explaining how his systems were
set up when he held office. He has proven to be a good source of information regarding Lunenburg
and how state law works.
TREASURER
As a result of the State's continuing fiscal crisis, cherry sheet aid distribution is now made on December
30th and June 30th instead of on a quarterly basis. Coupled with the town's appropriation of all free
cash surpluses over the years, the town has been put into a borrowing mode. Based on cash flow
projections, we borrowed $2,500,000 to help us through the lack of cash from the state. It appears that
this trend will continue in the future. Town Department heads are being contacted to arrange
purchasing schedules to coincide with estimated receipts to help reduce borrowing needs. Additionally,
I am able to help offset some of the borrowing costs by investing borrowed funds for as long a period
as possible.
In addition to seeking to increase revenues the town continues to seek as many ways to reduce
expenditures. Along these lines two committees have been established, composed of department heads,
one on Fleet Maintenance and the other on Cooperative Purchasing if all departments utilize the system,
it should result in savings for all concerned.
TAX COLLECTOR
As a result of state law changes, the possibility exits for the town to change to a quarterly tax billing
system. This may be inevitable in view of the state's deepening financial woes. It is certain that local
aid will continue to be received on a semi-annual basis thus continuing the need to borrow in
anticipation of revenue. My staff and I have been attending the Massachusetts Collectors' and
Treasurers' Association seminars on the implementation of this law. This may be the only alternative
available to reduce or eliminate almost certain short-term borrowing needs. However, it will create an
additional workload on the Collector's staff which has already been reduced in the past. If we were able
to fully computerize the collection system and obtain lock-box services, there could still be the need for
additional staffing. We will be calculating the projected costs and benefits and expect to report to the
1990 annual town meeting. As much work for this process will be generated in the Assessors' office. we
will be working together to find the best system for the town. Over the last year it has been through
continuing cooperation with both the Assessors and their staff that we have increased inter-office
communication.
As of August 1989, we were able to obtain a 100% collection rate for Fiscal Year 1989 Real Estate
Taxes. This involves either the collection of the tax or filing a tax title lien at the Registry of Deeds on
those properties with outstanding tax balances. This assures that the town is protected in the event of a
sale or bankruptcy.

TOWN CLERK
Thanks to the efforts of the Computer Advisory Committee and Board of Selectmen, the Town Clerk's
office obtained an integrated census software package. Census forms for January 1, 1990 were printed
in house and returns are being inputted by Sophia Mortis and Emily Foster. The use of this software
will provide us with more timely and accurate street lists, voting lists and census information. This
system will automatically print drop voter notices for those who have moved out of town or have not
returned their census forms. Additionally, we will be able to provide computer tapes for candidates and
state committees, further bringing the office into the current century.
Continuing a process set up my predecessor, we were able to have more microfilming done of important
but old and cumbersome records. This service was provided by the Northeast Document Center, well
known for its excellent work especially for historically sensitive material. During tight financial times,
this may appear to some to be an inappropriate use of funds. However. these are permanent records,
have great historical value and are used on a regular basis by both town officials and citizens at large.
Once destroyed, the information on the records would be lost forever.

W.RUTH PENNIMAN

Following are those records of Births, Marriages, and Deaths that
either occurred in our Town or records which were forwarded to this
Town from other municipalities in the State. Lunenburg residents are
urged to notify the Town Clerk if in-State records have not been
received and recorded. If a Birth, Marriage, or Death occurred
outside the State, the same may be recorded in the Lunenburg Town
Clerk's office if certified records are presented. In some cases the
Town Clerk's records are supplemented by records of burials which
appear elsewhere in this report. In order for these records to also
be included in the official Vital Statistics records, the family of
the deceased should present a certified copy of the death certificate
to the Town Clerk. In accordance with the General Laws, marriages
which occur here but relate to licenses which were obtained elsewhere
in the State are no longer included in our official Town Records.
