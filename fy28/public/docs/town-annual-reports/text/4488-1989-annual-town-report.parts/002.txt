SUPERINTENDENT'S REPORT
CONCETTA A. VERGE
Despite the very dismal fiscal time we are all experiencing, the Lunenburg
Public Schools have engaged in an array of positive, exciting, productive
experiences and continue to pursue academic excellence for the young people in
Lunenburg.
This, of course, would not have been and would not be possible without the
aid, the support and work of hundreds of school supporters and the extensive
donations they provided as part of their work for the schools.
When virtually all athletic funds were cut from the school budget last
summer, the Unified Boosters pledged to raise funds to support varsity and
junior varsity sports. The influx of unanticipated tuitioned students from
Shirley and the funds from the tuition these students provided, allowed the
Unified Boosters some relief. However, the Unified Boosters performed a
yeoman's task in their major assisting of the funding of athletics at
Lunenburg High School.
The Lunenburg Music Aiders also came forward to support the instrumental
and choral music programs for grades K-12. Dollars were generated to fund
music items and stipends which had been cut from the School Department
Budget. As a result of the efforts of the unselfish and committed music
supporters, we have been able to run instrumental and choral music programs
again this year.
Unfortunately, the fiscal state of the Commonwealth of Massachusetts has
worsened rather than improved, which again puts the schools at risk and
threatens not only these but other integral school programs. The
administrators, staff and School Committee are extremely reluctant to ask the
continued support of our very tired boosters, music aiders and volunteers in
order to keep essential programs within the system alive.
It has been through the cooperative efforts of the School Committee,
administration, staff, parents and community members who believe in the need
to maintain a quality educational program for our young people that the
schools have managed to meet the needs of our students.
The goals of the Lunenburg School Committee, listed below which
established in a team effort on the part of the administrators and School
Committee and are in keeping with the Lunenburg five year Strategic Plan.
These goals set the direction for the School System and address the needs of
our students and the dilemma in which the School System finds itself.

Strategic Planning
The Strategic Planning Task Force and Strategic Planning Planning Team
submitted the Lunenburg Schools initial five year Strategic Plan to the
Lunenburg School Committee. The Strategic Plan was unanimously supported by
the School Committee. Administrators, parents and community members are
involved in the implementation of this plan.
There are three major areas which will be addressed during the next five
years. They are: The Curriculum chaired by teacher Mariam Cronin with
Principal William McSheehy serving as vice chairperson; Professional
Development chaired by Claire Ketelarr, teacher, with Robert Jarvis,
Principal, serving as vice chairperson; and School and Community with teacher
Sandra (Reed) Laserte serving as chairperson and Jean Killeen, Principal, as
vice chairperson.
The tasks/goals of the Strategic Plan that these three large committees
(which encompasses several small subcommittees) are addressing this year are
the following:
CURRICULUM:
Collaboratively delineate K-12 objectives and scope and sequence in
each area of study, to include basic skills, critical thinking,
writing and research skills.
Identify curricular areas to be integrated.
Develop schedules and procedures for continual curriculum review.
Explore the implications of curriculum mastery and determine any need
that may exist for in-service training.
Develop the criteria for an interdisciplinary project to be completed
prior to graduation.

===PAGE 70===
PROFESSIONAL DEVELOPMENT:
Examine teaching schedules and assignments with the purpose of
providing time for cooperative planning and implementation.
Explore ways to improve the status and compensation of teachers.
Use the strategic plan to establish direction and priorities for
professional growth and development programs for both teachers and
administrators.
Examine the effectiveness of the existing leadership roles and
structures and determine their compatibility with the strategic plan.
Identify and provide leadership opportunities at all levels of the
organization.
SCHOOL AND COMMUNITY:
Build open lines of communication with all segments of the community.
Develop areas and activities in which the community utilizes school
facilities and resources and shares in its ownership.
Develop school-business partnerships.
It is hoped that as the various subcommittees progress in their efforts,
more and more parents and community members will join the effort.
Although the tasks of the Strategic Planning Subcommittees are ambitious,
we are very excited about the direction the system is taking and the work we
are accomplishing. We perceive most of these objectives to be doable.
A review of the Plan and of the tasks accomplished will take place each
year prior to the end of the school year. The plan or revision of it must be
re-submitted to the School Committee for approval each spring. Once year
one's goals/objectives are accomplished, we must develop an additional set of
objectives for year five. This insures that the objectives of the five year
plan do not become stagnant or repetitious. Instead, we keep revising and
planning for the future in a minimum of five year blocks.
School/Business Partnership:
A School/Business Partnership has been established this school year.
Co-facilitators of this group are Burt Goodrich, from Digital Corporation,
representing business and Sheila Flynn, Assistant Principal at Lunenburg High
School, representing the schools. Various members of the business community
who live in our town are involved in this group as is our local college
(Fitchburg State College). Representatives from the Private Industry Council
and Chamber of Commerce are also a part of this group, as are staff and
administrators from. the schools.

The major focus of the School/Business Partnership this year will be to
define its roles and functions. It is anticipated that the School/Business
Partnership efforts will be mutually beneficial to the schools and the
business community. We believe that professionals in the work force can and
should help the schools develop programs and objectives that will insure that
our young people, upon exiting Lunenburg High School, will be prepared to
enter the world of work. Advice and technical assistance from those who are

business partnership.
In turn, the schools can provide businesses with interns or students

The possibilities are limitless and exciting. We look forward to an

Regionalization
The Lunenburg/Shirley Regionalization Planning Board has been diligently
working during the past year to develop a Regional Implementation Agreement.
That agreement was approved by the Commissioner of Education for the state of
Massachusetts, Bond Council, and the Department of Revenue, as a result of the
efforts of a committed board who followed an arduous schedule
Members of the Planning Board from Lunenburg are Jane DeNike (Co-chair),
Martha Moore, and Richard Beardmore. Marcella Collier joined the Planning
Board earlier this year replacing Martha Moore. Members of the Regionaliza-
tion Planning Board from Shirley are Dorothy Wilbur (Co-chair), Michael
Johnson and James Pringle.
We are now awaiting a vote from both towns as to whether they should
combine to form a regional school district. Special Town Meetings will be
held in Lunenburg and Shirley on February 6, 1990 to make this decision.
The formation of a regional school district is sought primarily for
educational reasons. However, recently the fiscal situation became so dismal
that the new and additional funds which would be given to the new regional
school district have become of paramount importance. If the towns of Lunen-
burg and Shirley do not combine to form a regional school district, both
communities' schools will face the potential of severe cuts in programs,
personnel, and services. Forming a regional school district with Shirley just
may save the two school systems and will remove some of the financial burden
of funding the schools from the two towns. Hence, we sincerely hope to see
that region formed.

BOOKS AND BEYOND
Children will travel under the sea this year as they participate in the annual
Books and Beyond program. "Mysteries of the Deep" is the theme of this year's
reading incentive program.
Children in grades one and two may move their whale and dolphin markers to
each of the eight stations after they have read fifteen books; while children
in grades three and four may move their starfish and octopus markers after
they have read 300 pages.
Children will receive special prizes each time they reach a different
station. They may participate in the program as many times as they choose;
however, prizes will be awarded upon completion of each station only two
times. Gold medals will be presented in May to all children who reach the
eighth station.
The reading incentive program, now in its third year, strives to place a
positive emphasis on recreational reading, while reducing the number of hours
of television viewing among children. Individual progress is charted on the
central bulletin board in the school lobby. The program is under the direc-
tion of Specialist Nancy Rahaim.
GOOD GRIEF PROGRAM HELPS CHILDREN
How to help children and adolescents when a friend is terminally ill or
dies was the topic of an address given by Sandra S. Fox, Ph.D., ACSW, to all
Lunenburg School staff on November 15 at the Turkey Hill Middle School.
Dr. Fox, Director and Founder of the Good Grief Program of the Judge Baker
Children's Center, Boston, stressed the importance of forming a clear and
supportive crisis intervention plan of action prior to needing such a plan.
She posed some hypothetical situations and drew from her wide range of
experience explaining how children think and how children grieve. She
described the four psychological tasks of understanding, grieving, commem-
orating and going on. "It makes you wonder how kids going through this
experience can learn anything during the course of the day in school," she
said.
Caring Committees have been formed in all three Lunenburg Schools to
assist members of the school community in responding to the needs of students
in times of bereavement. The Elementary School Caring Committee has outlined
a list of twelve tasks to be followed in the event of a crisis situation. The
privacy of the family is of the utmost importance at all times.

DUSO PROGRAM
DUSO, the Dolphin, is one of the puppets used by the Guidance Department
in the implementation of the developmental guidance program. His name is an
acronym for Developing Understanding in Self and Others.
DUSO and Mrs. Davenport, Guidance Counselor, visit the classrooms
regularly with songs, stories, activities, and discussions designed to help
students learn interpersonal decision-making and problem-solving skills. The
children find the format and the puppet easy to relate to and look forward to
sharing thoughts and feelings with DUSO.
An example of the children's identification with the puppet was the first
grader who stopped by Mrs. Davenport's office to ask why she had not visited
his classroom on the previous day. After an explanation was given to him for
her absence, he asked if she would let DUSO know that he had been hit in the
eye with a rock, but that he was okay.
Developmental guidance is based on the belief that guidance is a necessary
part of the regular curriculum for all children, not simply for "children with
problems."

CURRICULUM
This year, we have initiated a new approach to grouping in order to
alleviate the tracking practices of the past. Our new leveling concept has

nationally as an area of concern. We at Turkey Hill Middle School are

volunteers have allowed us the opportunity to expand options for our stu-
dents. It is reasonable to report that without the parental assistance we
would not have been able to continue the Books and Beyond Program or offer a
Library/Reading Program to our sixth grade students. The assistance from
parents with Nature's Classroom, grade level field trips and in house assem-
blies and special events is also much appreciated.

pation presented by the Lunenburg Parks and Recreation Department.

ROBERT A. JARVIS
Lunenburg High School continues to provide a first-rate, quality education
for all its students. The Lunenburg School Committee has made a commitment to
offer a comprehensive educational program in order to meet the needs of all of
our students. The comprehensive educational program at Lunenburg High School
includes: Advanced Placement courses for the academically gifted; college
preparatory courses for students who has post-secondary educational plans; art
and music enrichment courses for all students; Industrial Technology courses
in graphic arts, automotive, materials processing, woodworking, photography
and mechanical drawing; and a Business Education curriculum for students

Shirley. These students opted to attend our high school because our reputa-

these forty students will be the vanguard of a much larger group of Shirley
students pending the vote on regionalization scheduled for Tuesday, February
6, 1990.

The Class of 1989 was very successful with their future plans, 55% went on
to four year schools and 29% went on to two year institutions. Some of the
institutions of higher learning where members of the Class of 1989 matricu-
lated in the fall of 1989 are: Babson College, Bentley College, Boston
College, Boston University, Brandeis University, Clark University, Colorado
State University, Columbia University, Fairfield University, Fordham Univer-
sity, Holy Cross, McGill University, New York University, Pennsylvania State
University, Skidmore College, Syracus University, Tufts University, Vassar
College, University of Miami, University of Vermont, University of Rhode
Island, University of Chicago and State University of New York, to name just a
few.
The decline in secondary (9-12) enrollment was stemmed in 1989 due to the
enrollment of forty students from Shirley. The October 1, 1989 student count
was 443.
In addition to a quality educational program, Lunenburg High School offers
an extensive extra and co-curricular activities program for a school of our
size. Students may opt to become involved in such varied extra-curricular
programs as: National Honor Society; International Club; Students Against
Driving Drunk (S.A.D.D.); Latin Club; Computer Club; Math League; Ski Club,
etc. To enrich our curriculum all students are exposed to an assembly program
and field trips (Federal Reserve Bank, district courts, Worcester Art Museum,
North Shore Music Tent; Kennedy Museum of Science; Cape Cod National Sea-
shores; field biology trip to Rockport; Sage-Allen; Computer Museum in Boston,
etc.
