TURKEY HILLS
They are gone now, wild birds once so plentiful
they named our geography, this aviary community.
Gone too are those in North Cemetery,
whole walking paths bring peace and silence amid the old stones.
We know where we are.
We mark place by our own houses and
by the blue Stillman silos and McMillan greys
standing watch to the south,
the crest of pines in Lanni's fields,
water tower blues, the backyard railroad car,
and steep, green grasses of the cherry hills
leading up upward to hope.
After traveling and coming up the rise to town center,
one knows the curves, turns, hills and rhythm of these roads
are as familiar as the shape one's foot leaves in wet sand.
We take in our landscape and let its colors and shapes touch us.
Community is a sense of place,
of towers, buildings pointing upward, churches and spires
but also rooted, grounded, connected.
It is the faces of those we know and love
and those with whom history is shared
that also gives the hues and tones to our world.
A project, a fair, a playground, an eagle's house, barn-raising,
together in the dark watching a school play,
at the soccer field, at the dance, and in the church choir
we share the burden, catch the joy and build community.
We know who we are.
At night, the clock tower on the town hall glows with white light
a beacon calling us home.
The many ladders hung so high tell time
of many hands joining in renewal and repair.
In this day's work, we color the landscape of these Turkey Hills for our children.
Carol Picard
written for Town Meeting May, 1995

MEMORIAM
GEORGE "CHICK" G. MUNYON, JR.
1935-1995
Chick was born in Clinton, Massachusetts on March 11,
1935. He graduated from Leominster High School in
1953 and moved to Lunenburg in 1955, when he married
his wife Betty Caswell. He came to work for the Town of
Lunenburg in 1961. He worked for the Highway
Department for 33 years, with more than 25 years of
service as the Highway Superintendent. He also served
the Town of Lunenburg as a Reserve Police Officer for 29
years. Chick was a life member and past president of the
Worcester County Highway Association and a life member of the Board of Directors of
the Massachusetts Highway Association. On August 8, 1995 Chick celebrated his 40th
wedding anniversary with his wife Betty. His loyalty and dedication to the Town of
Lunenburg over his 33 years of employment will not be forgotten. It is people like
"Chick" who helped make Lunenburg what it is today.
MEMORIAM
LEO M. "BUCKY" PADULA
1924-1995
Bucky, a life long resident of Lunenburg, was born and
brought up on the family farm which is located on
Leominster-Shirley Road. He graduated from Lunenburg
High School, served in the navy during World War II, and
graduated from the University of Connecticut. Truly a man of
the soil, after graduating from college he ran the family dairy
farm and, during the same period, served the Lunenburg
post office as a rural carrier. For the last thirty years he
worked in the family tractor agency and raised beef cattle as an avocation. Bucky coached
Lunenburg little league for many years. He and his wife Mary were married for fifty years
and during all of that time Bucky graciously hosted myriad local, state and national office
holders including governors, congressmen and presidents, while the Padula home served
as campaign headquarters for many a political aspirant. He loved this town and his country
and served them well.

BOARD OF SELECTMEN
The wheels of Town government have churned for another year and 1995
has shown the Town progressing through a number of changes. Many of
which would not have been accomplished without the dedication and
cooperation of various Town officers, departments, boards, committees,
and commissions.
The capping and final closure of the Sanitary Landfill was completed in the fall of 1995. According
to the Department of Environmental Protection, the Town is responsible for maintenance and
testing of the site for the next thirty years.
The sewering of the Town has progressed slowly and the Town was the recipient of a 5,600 foot
sewer line along Massachusetts Avenue in the Spring of 1995. A number of property owners along
this stretch of pipe also tied on to the sewer line throughout the year. The Board of
Selectmen/Sewer Commissioners re-opened discussions with the City of Fitchburg on extending
sewer services to the Summer Street area of Town and construction in this area is expected to
take place in the Spring of 1996. The Annual Town Meeting in May 1995 voted to appropriate
$165,000 for the development of a Facilities Plan, this engineering and planning study will
determine town-wide wastewater management needs and solution for the next 20 years.
The Town Hall renovation/restoration work has been ongoing through most of 1995 and the
installation of wheelchair lift to the second floor conference room was begun late in the fall. It is
anticipated that the lift will be up and running by Spring of 1996.
In conclusion, the Board would like to thank the many individuals who work for the Town or who
volunteer their time and commitment on behalf of the Town by serving on many boards,
committees, and commissions. Without their hard work and dedication the successes we have
had during 1995 would not have been possible.
BUILDING INSPECTOR/ZONING OFFICIAL
The Building Inspector's office is located on the second floor of the Town Hall with
regular hours Monday through Friday 8:00 A.M. until 4:00 P.M. Roger Williams, the
Building Inspector, is available in the mornings for calls and questions; inspections
are made by appointment only.
Building activity in Lunenburg has rapidly risen in 1995 especially in residential districts. A total of
267 permits were issued in 1995. Fifty-one new homes were built as well as a two unit condo,
reflecting an increase of 22 new homes since 1994.
Residents should be aware that Massachusetts Law requires that all persons, partnerships and
corporations who bid or perform residential contracting (reconstruction, alterations, additions etc.)
for work exceeding $1000.00 be registered with the Commonwealth. This office will enforce this
provision, however, you should ask your contractor about his/her registration. Please contact this
office with any questions you may have about this requirement or call for any other information you
may need on Zoning requirements or Building Code issues.

CABLE ADVISORY COMMITTEE
This is the third year that I have had the privilege of serving as chairman of the Lunenburg Cable
Advisory Committee (CAC) and reporting its efforts. The CAC consists of four members who are
appointed annually by the Board of Selectman. The CAC advises the Board of Selectman on issues
regarding the Town's cable telecommunications policy. As one can well imagine, given the rapid
change of technology, it is necessary to constantly keep abreast of the trends in the
telecommunications industry.
The CAC meets monthly. If you have any issues regarding cable service (or lack thereof) in the Town
of Lunenburg, please feel free to either come to the CAC's monthly meetings (time and location
posted at the town hall) or contact any member of the committee. Pat O' Malley is the CAC's
secretary, Lance May is the committee's longest serving member and Ed Dextraze is the CAC's
former chairman. Please feel free to contact any member of the committee.
I'd like to take this opportunity to thank the members of the CAC for their efforts and the Board of
Selectman for their support. In addition, I would especially like to thank the citizens of the town who
provided their comments to the CAC. Citizen participation is the cornerstone of creating a
telecommunications policy that helps to address the needs of all citizens of the Town of Lunenburg,
whether or not they are a cable subscriber.
During the last year, a number of issues were addressed by the CAC. The current license that
confers on Cablevision Inc., the right to do business in the Town of Lunenburg expires this year. The
CAC continues to negotiate with Cablevision Inc. to create a renewal agreement that is in the best
interest of the citizens of the Town of Lunenburg. The CAC is striving to provide as much cable
service to the community as possible, including a data network and access channel for the Town.
The Mulpus Road area of the Town was finally wired for cable service. The citizens of Mulpus Road
should be congratulated for their public participation. They were able to bring cable service to that
area of the Town by the shear will of their collected efforts.
As I mentioned previously we are currently negotiating with Cablevision Inc. to renew their license. 1
urge all citizens to let their wishes be known so we can make intelligent and informed decisions.
Now is time for all citizens to let their voices be heard to shape the telecommunication policy that will
carry the Town of Lunenburg into the next century.
Leon Weaver, Chairman
Lunenburg Cable Advisory Committee
CAPITAL PLANNING COMMITTEE
The Capital Planning Committee was mandated by Town Meeting and is representative by members,
Steven deBettencourt of the Board of Selectmen, Richard Runtagh, Finance Committee, Diane
Dellicarpini, School Committee, Marion M. Benson, Planning Board and Elaine Murphy member at
large from the Citizenry.
Capital Planning Committee is preparing the FY'97 Capital Plan for the Town Meeting. The
Committee seeks capital requests from all Boards, Departments and Commissions. Each request
requires documentation according to the Criteria for Capital Spending. They are mandates, safety,
needs and available funding. The major components of the plan are expenditures, improvements
and maintenance.
6
===PAGE 13===
The Finance Committee estimates revenues available for capital spending. The plan must fit into this
financial framework. The present policy of the Capital Planning Committee, Finance Committee and
the Board of Selectmen is to work within available revenues and avoid borrowing. Exempt borrowing
(extended borrowing) shall be reserved for only large long lasting Expenditures or Improvements.
The Capital Planning Committee holds several consensus meetings of Boards, Commissions and
Departments to assess the requests by priority through consensus of the governmental body and to
ascertain that each request meet the Criteria. The Board of Selectmen manages many service
departments and participates in the request review as to the direction of their departmental
requirements. This is a long and tedious process. Priorities are established and some very
important items are postponed because of lack of funding.
The Committee's final report is based on a broad scope. This enables the citizenry to receive some
assurance that all issues are attended to in proportion. This years plan will reflect expenditures that
will assist toward the continuing accreditation of the high school, address the safety issues of
proper safety equipment for the Fire Department, recommended purchasing of equipment for
services, upgrading building heating systems, roofing and repairs, replacing roads with new
drainage, updating and maintaining the governmental computer system, and providing purchases
toward the preservation of records.
The Committee is projecting a Long Range Capital Plan. Major items are being prioritized to be
purchased in segments from two to five years increments. A Vehicle Rotation Plan is being refined
to plan equipment purchases over evenly spaced time so replacement is economically feasible and
moneys are not being wasted with costly repairs and maintenance.
Work is being done with the help of the Town's Treasurer and Accountant offices to produce a Debt
Structure Plan and Financial Monitoring System. The Committee is working with the Planning Board
on a Long Range Space Needs Study which will also include a component for the recreational and
activities space needs in the Community.
This Committee is aware of the need and responsibility to communicate with the citizenry.
Newspaper articles and Public Access TV will be presenting the Capital Plan. The Community needs
to know the Capital Cost that is part of providing safety services, education, governmental services,
cultural and amenities to maintain the quality of life that reflects the character of Lunenburg. Each
Fiscal Year Plan does not show the real needs but only the portion that, by priority, should be
addressed now. The future work is this Committee is to project all of the needs and work on the
methodology to fulfill these needs in a feasible and economically procedure. This Committee is
committed to spending appropriately on Capital items or as one member said, "to do more with less".
This may require the willingness to do some things differently.

CENTRAL MASSACHUSETTS MOSQUITO CONTROL PROJECT
The Central Massachusetts Mosquito Control Project (CMMCP) currently provides its services to 28
cities and towns throughout Middlesex and Worcester Counties. The Town of Dracut became a
member of the CMMCP as of July 1, 1995. We welcome them and look forward to providing our
services.
The project's headquarters is located at 111 Otis Street, Northboro MA. Tours of the headquarters
or visits to field work sites may be arranged by calling the office in advance.
The CMMCP practices Integrated Mosquito Management (IMM), blending state of the art methods and
techniques with expertise, experience, and scientific research to provide our member communities
with modern, environmentally sound, cost effective mosquito control.
The Mosquito Awareness program which we offer to elementary schools in our district has become
very popular. Project staff meet with students and teachers to discuss mosquito biology, mosquito
habitat, and control procedures. Much of the presentation Is directed towards what the children and
their families can do to prevent mosquito larvae are included with the presentation, and are left in the
classrooms so that students can watch them develop. Slides, videos, handouts and coloring books
help to make this an interesting program.
As part of our effort to reduce the need for pesticides we continue to expand our water management
program. By cleaning clogged and overgrown waterways, mosquito breeding can be reduced,
wetlands are restored, and water quality is improved. Last winter we acquired and refurbished a
wood chipper to be used by our water management division. A new dump truck and utility trailer
were purchased this fall to replace our 1975 truck and trailer.
Bti mosquito larvacide is used to treat areas where mosquito larvae are found. We routinely check
known breeding sites, but also encourage the public to notify us of any areas they suspect could
breed mosquitoes. Our field crews will investigate all such sites and treat if needed.
Our goal is to handle all mosquito problems with water management or larviciding but we recognize
that there are times when adult mosquito spraying is the only viable solution. In such cases
residential and recreational areas are treated with either handheld or pick-up truck mounted
sprayers.
The project's surveillance program monitors adult mosquito and larval population density and is the
backbone for prescribing various control techniques. Rain gauges are set out and data collected by
our surveillance crews in an effort to predict when mosquito breeding will occur.
The project's video "Working for You" is available to anyone interested in learning about mosquito
control and the services provided by the Central Massachusetts Mosquito Control Project.

CONSERVATION COMMISSION
The Lunenburg Conservation Commission is an appointed body of volunteers who have been
empowered by the Commonwealth to administer the Wetlands Protection Act regulations. There are
7 members all of whom live in Lunenburg. The Commission conducts regularly scheduled meetings
on the first and third Wednesday of each month for the purpose of:
assisting applicants with the forms used with the Massachusetts Wetlands Protection
Act, CH. 131, S. 40 and the Town of Lunenburg General Wetlands Protection Bylaw;
conducting public hearings as required for filing Requests for Determination of
Applicability, Notices of Intent and Abbreviated Notices of Intent;
holding work sessions to manage Commission owned property;
conducting planning sessions to address conservation concerns;
holding informational meetings with interested residents; and
supervising enforcement of the public's interest as set forth in the Massachusetts and
Lunenburg wetland regulations.
In 1995, the Commission issued 40 Orders of Conditions, 10 Determinations of Applicability, 5
Certificates of Compliance, and 4 Enforcement Orders. These documents were the result of
numerous on-site inspections.

capping of the town landfill;
participating in Department of Environmental Protection wetland delineation
workshops and the annual meeting of the Massachusetts Association of Conservation
Commissions;
marking of a snowmobile trail on, and construction of a new bridge in, the Cowdry

clearing a hiking trail in Proctor Park;

HISTORICAL COMMISSION
The Lunenburg Historical Commission was re-activated in April of 1995. The Commission's primary
goal is to preserve the unique records of the Town. A partial inventory of the records this year
identifies over 300 documents. The earliest of these records start in 1719, nine years before
Lunenburg was erected. The inventory included the Lunenburg Proprietors' Records (two volumes)
which provide metes and bounds describing the original grants of land to the first settlers of
Lunenburg.
Town records must be made available to any U.S. citizen upon request. Unlimited and uncontrolled
access to 275 year old documents, however, will soon destroy them. Public Law 66 defines the
disposal schedule for all public records as well as mandating which records must be preserved.
Historical Commissions are responsible to develop plans to preserve the non-disposable records
which towns have created.
Proper preservation of records will assure that the original information is always available. Purchase
and proper use of the microfilm\microfische reader printer requested in this years town warrant will
reduce the need to handle our most precious documents. Many records have been put on microfilm
and microfiche, but many more must be microfilmed, or otherwise replicated, to preserve the
originals. Currently, the Town Hall vault houses these valuable records.
The most likely threat to the records is fire, as witnessed by the loss of several town halls since the
1950's in Massachusetts. If such a fire should engulf the Town Hall, it is likely that all records would
be destroyed. While this would be a substantial loss, the safety of personnel working in and using the
building is of greater concern.
The most feasible means of preventing such a disaster would be the installation of a sprinkler
system. Such a system would minimize damage in case of fire and provide protection to people-as
well as the valuable records.
In order to meet our responsibilities as a Commission we have needs which must be met. Basically,
we need a work space and a vault (which meets state standards) for the protection of archives. We
intend to request these in the near future.

MONTACHUSETT REGIONAL VOCATIONAL TECHNICAL SCHOOL
Superintendent-Director Stratos G. Dukakis is pleased to report that Monty Tech began its twenty-
fifth year of operation the week of August 28. At that time, school officials were wrapping up
preparations for the start-up of the new Cosmetology program (the school's twenty-first), introducing
a new student incentive program, and entering the final phase of the 10-year re-accreditation
process. On-going advances in the school's technology continue to remain high on the
Superintendent's list of priorities.
The 1995-96 school year officially got under way Tuesday, August 29, with freshmen and new
upperclassmen reporting to school for the first time. Members of the Class of 1999 include students
from the 17 communities comprising the Monty Tech Regional School District, as well as School
Choice students. Returning upperclassmen attended classes the following day. Enrollment figures
are in line with recent years.
School official are planning a yearlong celebration to commemorate the twenty-fifth anniversary of
Monty Tech's opening. A committee is spearheading plans for the numerous events and activities
scheduled to take place throughout the year. The festivities officially got under way September 14,
with the First Annual Scholarship Golf Tournament. Held at Westminster Country Club, the fund-
raiser netted approximately $7,000 which is earmarked for scholarships and other student activities.
The celebration is expected to culminate in the spring with a gala weekend event.
A new incentive program, known as "Bulldog Pride," was established this year and has been very
well received by both staff and students. The program is modeled after one established by a voc-
tech school in Butler, PA, that has been very successful. It aims to increase student attendance,
decrease discipline problems and create a better school atmosphere. Throughout the school year,
students are rewarded with a variety of prizes, such as VCRs, televisions, cassette players, and gift
certificates. A grand prize drawing at the end of the year will include five previously owned cars that
have been refurbished by the Automotive Technology and Auto Body trades. By all accounts, the
program is a huge success.
This year, Monty Tech entered the second and final phase of its re-accreditation process. In order to
maintain accreditation by the New England Association of Schools and Colleges, Inc. (NEASC),
schools are required to undergo a renew process every 10 years. Monty Tech completed the
required self-evaluation phase last year. The second and final phase was conducted in October,
when a committee comprised of 22 educational professionals selected by NEASC spent four days at
the school assessing the quality and effectiveness of all vocational-technical, academic and ancillary
programs. A report of their finds will be forthcoming.

Veterans' Memorial Physical Education Complex at Mount Wachusett Community College, Gardner.

Education.

FIRE DEPARTMENT
During the calendar year 1995 the Fire Department responded to a total of 1059
incidents. Of this number, 378 were rescue/ambulance calls, 323 were fire related
and 358 were inspections. The total ambulance and fire related incidents showed
an increase of 34 over last years total. This is the fourth year in a row that our fire
incidents have shown an increase. This is due to increased growth in the town and
increased demand for services by the townspeople.
Overall condition of the fire department is very good. We were fortunate to place a new pumping
engine into service this past year. This engine carries a large amount of water and hose. It is
designed and built with all amenities that make it a first class fire truck. With this piece of apparatus,
we now have a modern fleet of fire trucks with the oldest being 1984. With the reliable apparatus that
we have now, our firefighters can be assured that the equipment is going to allow them to perform
their duties safely with the maximum efficiency.
In July we added one more full time Firefighter/EMT for the daytime hours. This will allow us the time
and manpower during the weekdays to guarantee that service to the townspeople will be available
immediately, and at the same time we will be able to cover the station for a second call or continue
the usual daily business. Having enough help also guarantees our firefighters that their equipment
will be in top working condition when they need it. This position is working out very well and we
thank the town boards and taxpayers for making it possible.
22
===PAGE 29===
Areas that we are still lacking are in the station itself. We desperately need more room to be able to
function adequately. We are very crowded and don't have any maintenance space. Some of our
vehicles have to be stored outside which is not good in this climate. Snow and cold weather delay
the response of these vehicles if we need them.
Although our firefighting apparatus is in good shape, our most used vehicle, the ambulance, will need
to be replaced in two years, 1998. The ambulance we have will be 10 years old at that time. Due to
the constant use and age, this replacement will be a must then. Estimated cost to replace it will be
approximately $80,000.
It takes a lot of difference people to make this Department function as well as it does. Sometimes
these people don't get the credit that they deserve, especially the dispatchers that take and
dispatch our calls. Without their dedication and attention our job could be a lot harder and more
dangerous. Also, our secretaries, the Board of Selectmen, the Finance Committee, and other
boards that we constantly work with and support us deserve a lot of credit. Our firefighters are
second to none in the performance of their duties and it shows every time they go out the door. Their
efforts are appreciated and I thank their families for their quality time away from the family unit.
Without this understanding, we would not have the Fire Department we have today.

LUNENBURG HOUSING AUTHORITY
The Lunenburg Housing Authority celebrated its' Twenty-Fifth Anniversary in 1995. The doors to the
Pearl Brook Apartments opened during October 1970. Over the years these doors have provided
decent, safe, and affordable housing to many senior citizens of Lunenburg and the region. We are
fortunate to have some original residents still residing at Pearl Brook.
The Authority made improvements to the Pearl Brook Apartments Community Hall with better lighting
and a fresh coat of paint. Many residents have expressed their approval and thanks for these much
needed improvements.
On a larger scale, public housing programs are in the midst of great change, on both the federal and
state levels. In the past our elderly housing program was dedicated to serving only elders. Recent
legislation has expanded the Lunenburg Housing Authority's elderly/disabled housing program to
serve more non-elderly disabled. The production of affordable family housing has ended, as the
need continues to grow. The Lunenburg Housing Authority's family housing program has full
occupancy and a substantial waiting list.
The Board of Commissioners continue to pursue means that will make the Lunenburg Housing
Authority self-sufficient. The Authority is mandated to limit rental fees to 30% of its' residents'
income, while assuming payment of all utilities. Therefore, reducing utility costs continues to be a
major goal.
During 1995 the Authority received thirty-two (32) applications for housing assistance. Fifteen (15) of
these were for elderly housing assistance, and seventeen (17) were for family housing assistance.

BOARD OF HEALTH
The first substantial changes to the Massachusetts Title V Regulations concerning the citing,
construction, inspection, repair and upgrading of on-site sub-surface disposal systems became
effective in March of 1995.
These changes have had an immediate and substantial effect on many of the residents of our
community and will continue to impact anyone who intends on buying, selling or improving their
property.
In addition to the individual aesthetic and financial impacts that have already been seen by many,
there is an impending townwide impact looming ahead.
Many of the Town owned septic systems, especially in and around the center of Town are either
presently in failure, or will fall into that category shortly.
The Board of Health is convinced that the only way to resolve the problems associated with septage
disposal in an environmentally acceptable and financially affordable way, particularly in certain
highly developed sections of Town, is to actively pursue the expansion of a public sewer system.

In October of 1990 Lunenburg High School underwent a rigorous accreditation evaluation by
the New England Association of Schools and Colleges. Over the past five years the LHS faculty and
administration have worked very hard to implement the numerous recommendations contained in
that Evaluation Report. Considerable progress had been achieved in this endeavor. By last spring,
the point at which our major Five Year Progress Report had to be submitted to the Education

Report. The following is excerpted from the text of their letter:
The Commission on Public Secondary Schools, at its September 17-18, 1995 meeting,
reviewed the Special Progress Report of Lunenburg High School and continued the school's
accreditation, but placed the school on warning for its failure to adhere to the Commission's
Standards for Accreditation on Curriculum and Instruction, Library Technology and Media Services,
and Financial Support.

library media center, the inadequate size and breadth of the print collection, the limited library
technology, the library's inadequate seating capacity and furniture, and the adverse impact on the

SPECIAL EDUCATION DEPARTMENT
The Special Education Department of the Lunenburg Public Schools provides services to eligible
students, ages 3 to 22 with mild to severe disabilities. Services provided include: academic
instruction, academic support and learning disabilities tutoring. Related services in the areas of
speech and language therapy, occupational therapy, physical therapy, and counseling are also
provided.
The philosophy of the Lunenburg Public Schools' Special Education Department is congruous to
state and federal laws which emphasize that handicapped students have the right to be educated
with non-handicapped peers. Special Education is seen as a means to support, not supplant, regular
education. The emphasis on special education, locally and nationally, is on "inclusion", a term used
to describe the premise that special education students be taught in regular classroom settings not
in segregated settings. Support from special education teachers is provided within regular
classrooms. Support is also provided in special education classrooms as deemed necessary and on
an individual basis. In Lunenburg, inclusion is not a new concept. It has been a consistent practice
for several years.
Over the past few years, changes in state and federal regulations have resulted in the formulation of
guidelines for determining eligibility to receive special education. These guidelines emphasize that
all efforts must be made to meet the needs of students in regular education prior to a referral for
37
===PAGE 44===
special education. Students referred for special education must receive a comprehensive
evaluation. This evaluation must conclude that a disability exists and that the disability prevents the
student from making effective progress in regular education. The special education department in
cooperation with the regular education administration and staff has adopted and practiced a highly
effective pre-referral system. Many of our students have been able to progress effectively and
participate in regular education classes and activities successfully as a result.
Parents have a vitally important role in the education of their children. For special education children
it is equally important. A parents advisory council for parents of special education students meets
monthly and has planned a variety of activities. These activities include: guest speakers, a
newsletter, and a parent support group. Any parent of a special needs child is welcomed to join.
The FLLAC Collaborative is an educational collaborative governed by Superintendents, School
Committee members and Special Education Directors. The collaborative develops programs and
services for students who are unable to be educated in their own public school districts. FLLAC
services many students from surrounding cities and towns. In keeping with the philosophy of
inclusion, there are currently 5 FLLAC programs housed in the Lunenburg Public Schools. These
programs provide comprehensive special education services to students with moderate to severe
disabilities. By housing these programs in our public schools, students are given opportunities to
participate in many public school activities and classes on a regular basis. This kind of collaboration
has been mutually beneficial students.
The Special Education Department is proudly committed to meeting the very special needs of our
students through the provision of quality education. There are many exciting challenges in the days
ahead brought forth by education reform. The support of parents, the community, and the staff,
students and administration will insure that the quality education reputed in Lunenburg will continue.
We look forward to another exciting and productive year.
