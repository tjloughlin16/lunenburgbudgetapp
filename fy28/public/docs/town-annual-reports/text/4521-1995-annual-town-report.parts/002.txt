As I begin my third term in these offices I would like to thank all those who have helped
and supported me over the years. Special thanks to my staff who are always there to
help those in need of the services these offices provide.
In August of this year the Town sold a bond issue of $1.9 million dollars to be repaid
over the next fifteen years. The majority of this borrowing was $900 thousand for the
landfill closure and $633 thousand for capital expenditures voted the past two years.
Lunenburgs' bond rating was dropped from an A to Baa, this is still an 'investment
grade' and the interest rate of 5.18% reflects the change in rating.
Cited in Moody's rating recap was strained, although somewhat improving financial
position. What this means is that in past years we spent all that we expected to receive
and then some for current operating expenses and little or nothing set aside for
emergencies or maintenance of town owned property. Fortunately, this trend has begun
a reversal. In the past three years the finance committee has recommended, and town
meeting has supported, the funding of the Stabilization Account that is currently at $290
thousand dollars, the highest it's been in the last ten years. These funds can be
appropriated at a town meeting for any purpose and is generally referred to as the
Towns' savings account.
Tax Collections were once again 100% for fiscal 1996. This means that the tax was
either collected or the property placed in tax title through the filing of a lien at the
Registry of Deeds, thereby protecting the Towns interest. Late in calendar 1995 the
Towns largest tax title account was paid in full, recovering approximately $250 thousand
dollars in taxes. This collection will significantly enhance our financial position specially
in light of the severe winter we are facing.
The Town received two computers this year from the Secretary of
States office enabling us to go on-line with the Central Voter Registry
in Boston. What is the old saying about looking a gift horse in the
mouth? The equipment is fine but the programs are time consuming
and limited in scope. The system has been fraught with problems
causing many Town Clerks and Boards of Registrars to run dual
systems, especially for the annual census. That is the case here. We are maintaining
the old system for voting lists and street listing. We are not allowed to add any software
or hardware to the state provided system. As a result, a voting list that would be fifty
pages on our system becomes two hundred pages using the new system. We will
continue using both systems at least until the elections in the fall of next year.
The good news is that voter registrations are on the rise. We do not know if it is
because of the ease of registering by mail or if it's interest in the presidential election in
1996. Whatever the reason it's welcomed.
