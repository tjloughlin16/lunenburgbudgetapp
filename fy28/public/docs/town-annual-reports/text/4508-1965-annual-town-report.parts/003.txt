School Nurse
The School Nursing Program is performed by the Town Nurses, Mrs. Doris M.
Lahti and Mrs. Gladys V. Barney, who also are responsible for the Town's visiting
nursing services.
During 1965, 447 visits were made to school children for: follow-up on some
defects found by the School Doctor, vision and hearing failures, health supervision
and absence from school. Some transportation was supplied to doctors and clinics
for needed attention.
The present school year has presented problems, particularly in the vision testing,
which is a very important part of any school health program. Each year many new
children are picked up asvision failures and many others with glasses are referred
back to their eye doctor. Because of lack of proper testing space at the High School
and Annex, the testing has consumed a great deal more time than in the past. Our
present vision machine requires twenty feet of testing space. We are in hopes that
next year we will have one of the new vision machines which can be used in nurses'
offices, not requiring any space except a table.
We are able to keep our school immunization program at a very high level with
the programs offered by the Nashoba Board of Health, as follows:
Diptheria and Tetanus Booster and re-vaccination against small-pox for Grades 1,
6, & 11.
Sabin Oral Polio to new students who have not had immunization and booster
doses to children in Grade 1 who previously completed oral series.
Tuberculin Tine Testing was administered to students in Grades 1 and 9, with
follow-up on any positive reactors and their families.
Dental flouride treatments for Grades 2, 5, and 8 given by the Dental Hygienist.
The above programs require written permission from parents and have certainly
been well accepted with very few refusals.
In closing, we wish to thank Superintendent, Mr. Brown; Principals, Mr. Carey
and Mr. Passios; school faculty and personnel, without whose support and coopera-
tion we would not be able to carry out successfully any of our school health programs.
Respectfully submitted,
DORIS M. LAHTI, R. N.
School Nurse

School Lunch Program
This is my Third annual report of the Lunenburg School Lunch Program and is
for the school year, September 1964 to September 1965.
During this period we fed 194,640 students the Type "A" lunch. Of these,
6,015 were free lunches served to high school workers and the needy. In addition to
these, we served 11,459 lunches to adults including the staff. There were 175 lunch
days for the Elementary School and 177 for the Jr. -Sr. High School. These amount to
an estimated 1,171 lunches served per lunch day. We sold 164,387 half-pints of
milk under the United States Department of Agriculture Special Milk Act Program as
well as 5,245 half-pints to adults.
Financially, we fared very well and there was an opening balance of $10,106.50
for the beginning of the school year in September 1965 after all bills were paid in June.
Commodities in the form of butter, flour, rice, rolled wheat dried milk, beans,
peanut butter, ground beef, cheeses, corn meal, lard, turkeys, chicken, roast beef,
canned fruits, etc. donated by the U. S. Department of Agriculture were generous in
amount and contributed greatly to the financial success of the program. It is note-
worthy to point out that the School Lunch Program is self-supporting with the excep-
tion of the School Lunch Supervisor's salary, fuel and electric costs. These com-
modities are available during different months and the quantity allotted is based on
the average number of students fed. The total valuation of these donated commodities
was $20,561.50.
Participation percentage-wise for the year in the Jr. -Sr. High School was 75%
according to attendance and 71% according to membership. In the Elementary School,
participation according to attendance was 68% and 64% according to membership. Such
percentages represent what is considered to be very fine participation.
Personnel in the Jr. -Sr. High School consist of Mrs. Lucy Chartrand as the
Head Cook and Mrs. Gertrude Solovei as the Baker, while the general workers are
Mrs. Ethel Nolan, Mrs. Mary Thibault, Mrs. Gloria Beaulac, Mrs. Mary Haueisen,
Mrs. Anita Forest and Mrs. Ella Nicholson.
Personnel in the Elementary School are Mrs. Rita Seguin, Manager; Mrs. Hazel
Whitney, Head Cook and Mrs. Bertha Geigner, Baker. General Workers are Mrs. Gen-
evieve Nass, Mrs. Annette Thibodeau, Mrs. Phyllis Colello, and Mrs. Ellen Brown.
The success of our program depends upon these people, many of whom have
been with Lunenburg for some years as faithful and conscientious workers.
At the Thanksgiving dinner this past November, we served 1,601 "Type A"

lunches, while for Christmas dinner, we served 1,552 lunches, both featuring turkey
and gravy with ice cream for dessert. As always at the Elementary School, the tables
were appropriately decorated by the students and teachers.
The Annual Spring School Lunch Workshop was held in Holden for three days
during our April vacation. Mrs. Rita Seguin from the Elementary School and Mrs.
Lucy Chartrand, Mrs. Ethel Nolan and myself, attended from the Jr. -Sr. High School.
Mrs. Seguin and I attended the Supervisors and Managers Conference at the
University of Massachusetts, June 28th - July 2nd, 1965, the theme of which was
"FUNCTIONS OF SCHOOL FOOD SERVICE MANAGEMENT". Lectures and courses were
given by the staff of the School Lunch Office in Boston, as well as by the staff at the
University.
On Friday, November 8th, in conjunction with the Teachers' Convention, the
personnel of both schools attended the School Lunch Convention for Worcester County
held at the Tahanto Regional High School in Boylston. It was a most successful meet-
ing and featured bread and roll making.
I wish to express my thanks to you, Mr. Brown and to the School Committee;

do I wish to thank Mr. Archambault and his custodians for the assistance we receive
throughout the year and my own personnel for the hard work and dedication to the
lunch program.
