As a school's enrollments decline, there are certain adjustments that must be made. One of
the major adjustments is in the area of staffing. At the present time, there are five sections of
each academic area in the sixth grade, except for reading, in which there are six; five sections
of each academic area in grade seven; and six sections of each academic area in grade eight,

as the number of classes is reduced from six sections to five. This fact necessitates that certain
staff members teach two and three different subject areas, at more than one grade level in several
cases. In other cases, the middle school must share its staff members with the high school and
the elementary school. The schedule of classes represents this fact very clearly, since there is
little room for flexibility across the grade levels.

grade move will continue to be discussed throughout the remainder of this school year.
CURRICULUM REVISION:
The strength and responsiveness of the middle school's program have not diminished because
of the enrollment adjustments. The faculty and administration view this period as one of great
value to the examination and revision of our existing programs. Regular review of the curricular
program occurs each year, with different areas receiving attention each year. During the course
of this year, staff has reviewed and revised science curriculum for grades six through eight,
incorporating life science in grade seven. Reading curriculum in grades six and seven has been
revised, with updated scope and sequence of skill attainment for both grades and with new
materials created for improving comprehension in grade seven by Mrs. Sandralee Turner and Mrs.
Donna Crisci. Writing objectives for all three grade levels will be revised before the conclusion of
the year and will be coordinated with those of the elementary school. Middle school teachers
employ the writing as a process methodology for the improvement of writing skills and are utilizing
the writing folders produced by THE NETWORK in Andover, MA. This method highlights revision
and editing on the part of the student, with frequent conferences with the teacher during the
process of completing a composition.
Renewed social studies and math curriculum were presented to the school committee and
accepted, due to the efforts of the teachers in those subject areas. Changes in curriculum occur
all the time, as needs arise in society.

BASIC SKILLS TESTING AND STANDARDIZED TESTING:
Each year our sixth and seventh grade students are given the Stanford Achievement Test to
ascertain their achievement in a wide variety of academic areas. Grade eight students are given
the Differentiated Aptitude Test in order to determine strengths and weaknesses and to help
place them more effectively in grade nine. All eighth graders are given basic skills tests in the
areas of math, reading, writing and listening in the spring of the year. This year will be the
last of the testing under the old state law, and new testing will commence in the fall of the next
school year. The minimum standards must be met by all students or they are required to take
intensive courses in the areas of their deficiencies at the high school. All students in Lunenburg
have passed the minimal competencies tests by the eleventh grade's conclusion. We also exempt
very few students each year for special needs reasons. All math teachers this year are admin-
istering pre and post-tests on their curriculum to measure the effectiveness of our curriculum.
The middle school teachers are strong believers in accountability of instruction.

efforts. In 1985, industrial arts students were trained and placed in work sites which included
businesses in printing, furniture refinishing, concrete products, auto body, general woodworking
and building maintenance.
