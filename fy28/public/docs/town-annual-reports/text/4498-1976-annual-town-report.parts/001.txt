Board of Selectmen
Board of Selectmen: left to right - Rudolph Masella, Chairman Normand J. Thompson and Sherwood G. Winchester.
The Bicentennial Year 1976 found Lunenburg confronting many of the same problems we have been struggling
with in recent years -- more State mandates, more Federal controls, more infringements upon our Home Rule. The
feelings of pride and greater appreciation of our country brought about by the many events of the celebration during
the year, unfortunately, did little to solve the problems of the overburdened taxpayer. Ever increasing costs, re-
quests for services, demands by State and Federal government, have made the task of administering local government
an even more difficult task. The question of how we can best guide our Town, giving the best possible services at
the lowest possible costs, is a never ending problem.
Your Board of Selectmen find that more than ever this type of problem takes more and more of their time and
that the only reason Lunenburg has come as far as it has, as successfully as it has, is because of the numbers of dedi-
cated people who are willing to give freely of their time and talents to wrestle with the Town's problems. We applaud
each and every one of them -- Lunenburg would not be the desirable Town it is without them.
Solid waste disposal is a perfect example of the problems confronting the Town. We know that to handle our
solid waste under the present governmental regulations will be very costly. We also know that given the right condi-
tion it could be less costly, more effective and could help our energy problems. The question is "How can we make
this ideal solution come about without creating an ineffective agency which will ultimately strangle US with unreason-
able higher costs ?". Regionalization appears to be the answer to many of these problems, but is fraught with the
perils of uncontrollable bureaucracy.
Like it or not, certain State and Federal monies become available to US through the Montachusett Regional
Planning Commission. We must make every effort to see that this Commission serves US and that we do not end up
serving the Commission. We must be ever vigilant in preventing the possibility that a bureaucracy will overrun US.
This will mean a closer scrutiny of the activities of this agency and Lunenburg should be in the forefront in this effort.
Your Selectmen have continued their involvement with the Massachusetts Selectmen's Association and the
Massachusetts League of Cities & Towns in an attempt to workwith other communities with similar problems in order
to resolve some of them. We are no longer an isolated community and by communicating with and working with our
fellow communities, we have a better chance of solving individual as well as collective problems.
We will not go into the details of all the functions of the Board -- elsewhere in this Report the various depart-
ments will tell you in greater detail than we could here what has happened this year. This Board is proud to have
had the opportunity to serve the Town of Lunenburg.

Board of Assessors
Board of Assessors: left to right - Chairman Bernard J. Paton, John F. Nicoll and William F. Reed.
The Board of Assessors wishes to report that 1976 appears to have been a slow year for new construction. The
majority of the construction during the past year has been additions to existing housing and additional outbuildings.
We would estimate the additional valuation coming into being during 1976 to be under a million dollars. Included
in this figure are a number of new house completions which were assessed on a percentage basis last year due to their
being in progress on January 1, 1976.
We wish to report that in compliance with the "Sudbury Decision" and the Commissioner of Corporations and
Taxation directives to bring the valuations of the Town to "full and fair cash value", we will, as of January 1, 1977,
increase all valuations by 10%. This increase will include all land, real estate and commercial property in the Town.
We are obligated to carry out the plan submitted to the Massachusetts Supreme Judicial Court by the Town. This plan
was outlined in our report to the Town in the 1975 Town Report. This action will have the effect of increasing the
total valuation of the Town, however, it will also lower the tax rate if the Town budgets were to remain the same as
last year, and the total amount of money a person would pay for taxes would also remain the same.
The Board of Assessors is requesting funds through an Article at the forthcoming Annual Town Meeting. We
wish to change our present method of recording land transactions in the Assessors' Office and acquiring topographical
maps made of a size to be usable in the field. Our problem is that the aerial maps now being used to show property
lines are becoming worn from twenty-six years of use. Each sale is now drawn onto the existing photographs by hand
in ink and the lots are described by the owner's name and area of the tract of land.
We propose to have topographical maps drawn from our photomaps, reduced to a manageable size and a new
numerical system of recording ownership established. Each tract of land in Lunenburg would have a number, a card
file would show the owner's name. In the event of a sale a new card would be filled out and the change would be
complete.
The existing photomaps would be available for viewing of property, but would not be maintained as to future
changes. We do feel that topographical maps in a convenient size to be used in the field have long been a necessity
to assist the Assessors in carrying out their duties.

Planning Board
Planning Board efforts in 1976 were concentrated primarily in the following areas: (1) The study of the up-
dating of The Rules and Regulations of the Planning Board of the Town of Lunenburg Governing The Subdivision of
Land and the approval of these new regulations on September 15, 1976. Our requirements are now in agreement with
State standards under Subdivision Control Law, Chapter 41, Sections 81K to 81GG. (2) The initiation of a new up-
date of the Protective By-law of the Town of Lunenburg. This is in response to a State mandated requirement under
Chapter 808 of the Acts of 1975 requiring that all cities and towns of the Commonwealth have individual zoning by-
laws in conformance with said Chapter 808 by June of 1978. The review of our By-law will be a continuing study in
1977. (3) The review and final approval in April of the Laurelwood Subdivision off Chase Road. (4) The week to
week approval of subdivision lots not requiring subdivision approval.
Additionally, in response to a request from the Growth and Development Committee for a growth and develop-
ment policy for Lunenburg, this Board recommended that Lunenburg should: a. maintain moderate growth; b. en-
courage growth of light, clean industry; C. encourage growth in established growth areas; d. enact zoning that
would be structured to the capability of the land, taking into consideration the water supply and the ability of the
land to absorb sewage. Finally, the Planning Board approved the revised HUD Floodplain Map of Lunenburg sub-
mitted by the Department of Housing and Urban Development. This approval came after lengthy study with our wet-
lands intern, Mr. Richard Defieux.

Park Commission
The 1976 Summer Program was conducted under the direction of Richard Stachowicz and was highlighted by a
very successful Muscular Dystrophy Yard Sale at the Center and Wallis Park playgrounds. The playground activities
at the Center were under the direction of Chris DeFeo for most of the season and her originality made the program
very exciting for the children in attendance. The Wallis Park program is also well attended, but there is concern in
that an older age group is attending the program and has posed a serious problem to playground instructors. The Arts
and Crafts program was conducted very capably by Stephanie Markham and Cindy St. Arnaud. The Town Beach saw
a decrease in attendance this year due in large part to the absence of a raft. The swim classes, however, were very
well attended and Gerry Beloin and his staff are to be commended.
Although most of the Park Commission programshave been running smoothly, it has become more and more ap-
parent that we are in competition with the area cities and the State for personnel to operate our summer programs.
Our current wage scale for lifeguards and playground instructors puts US at a serious disadvantage in securing qualified
people as we are unable to match what these other agencies are offering.
The replacement of aging playground equipment is now becoming a problem. It is hoped that the annual re-
placement of at least one or more of these items could be accomplished in the future. Since the removal of the old
quonset hut at Wallis Park, there has been a need for some kind of shelter where tables can be set up for the Arts
and Crafts program.
During the year the Park Commissioners met with representatives of the School Department and updated the
Mutual Use of Facilities Agreement. This will now be an annual process in orderto insure the best use of the facil-
ities of both departments for the benefit of the public. During 1976 the Commissioners considered the possibility of
expanding the summer playground activities so as to include a program at Normand Park similar to those conducted
at the Center and Wallis Park playgrounds. If enough interest in such a program is shown over the coming year, the
Commissioners will try to include such a program in the budget so that it could begin in the summer of 1978.
The most notable accomplishment this year for the Park Commission was the completion of a new foundation for
the Town Beach House. The work was done by the John Morin Company of Fitchburg under the direction of Raymond
Bourque of that company. The new foundation will hopefully add many more years of usefulness to this building.
Additional work has yet to be done to the rest of the structure and should be accomplished before opening this season.
Finally, the Park Commissioners would like to thank all who have helped make our programs successful and a
word of thanks to Bob Garneau and Greg Bingham who have done an excellent job of maintaining the parks and play-
grounds for the Town's use and enjoyment.

Cemetery Commission
During 1976 the Cemetery Commissioners met as scheduled throughout the year. Two equipment purchases were
made in 1976 one beinga new truck cab and chassis (the old body was repaired and painted before installation on
the new chassis) and the other purchase was a new riding tractor mower to replace the old one. In the spring, both
Cemeteries were seeded, fertilized and lime applied to enhance the grounds and insure healthy vegetation, and the
grounds were given special grooming in preparation for Memorial Day.
Throughout the year much maintenance work was done including the repair of old monuments to preserve the
appearance of the Cemeteries. To protect the monuments from damage through the use of conventional weed cutting
implements, a new monofilament weed cutter was purchased thus permitting closer trimming near monuments, trees and
bushes without any damage. In continuing the maintenance program, the road hedges were trimmed and some paint-
ing was done to the Cemetery Department office building. The following table lists the names of persons buried in

Development & Industrial Commission
It has been the thinking of the Development & Industrial Commission over the past several years that water,
its supply (quantity and quality), distribution and storage, is of primary concern to the future growth and develop-
ment within the Town of Lunenburg. It is fairly common knowledge that any potential industrial development has
been discouraged by lack of water. The Lunenburg Water District which operates separately and independent from
Town government, services two main areas of Town the Center and the Whalom District. These areas are residen-
tial and constitute our highest population density. This Commission is firm in its stand that there should be no in-
dustrial development within the confines of the present Water District. Industry, as we see it, should be included
in the Town's overall growth plan, but it must be contained away from population centers. For these reasons, we
have earmarked two areas of Town as future industrial sites one in the extreme south end of Town along Leominster-
Shirley Road and the other in the north end of Town on Route 13 (Chase Road).
The Development & Industrial Commission's concept of industry for Lunenburg is small and light those firms
that would only be interested in water for fire protection and not for process use. These would provide their
own sewerage disposal. They would be the type of industry that is clean and noiseless and who would desire location
in a small, well-landscaped industrial park. They would draw their employees from neighboring cities and thereby
their work force would primarily be commuters who reside outside the Town. It is our contention that keeping in-
dustry out of Lunenburg will in no way alter our growth picture. As our neighboring cities and towns develop in-
dustry, we will pick up our proportionate share of new residents.
The main thrust behind this desire to develop industry within Town is to control growth. By developing two
potential industrial sites in the outlying district and perhaps changing the Protective By-law to include industrial
zoning, we stand a chance to limit residential growth in these areas. Despite the changes in lot size in outlying
areas, there has been little curtailment of growth in these areas. In fact, the contrary has happened -- new homes
are springing up in areas that are too close to potential industry. This can only lead to future problems between
homeowners and industry. Without some type of zoning change, neither the homeowner nor industry know where
they stand, and it isn't fair to either.
Along with the concept of developing industrial sites to control growth, it is our feeling that these parcels
of land be buffered with large tracts of conservation property. This will limit the size of industry and prevent its
gradual spread toward population centers. Our largest existing industry is located in the south end of Town with its
own water supply and there is the potential of Leominster water being run into an industrial park on the Leominster-
Lunenburg Line, Therefore, it would seem a plan for zoning regulations and conservation buffering be formulated
now so that the Town is in control of the situation.
It is our belief that through proper planning, industry can become a key factor in controlling overall growth
within Lunenburg so that we maintain our image as a rural bedroom community. Our views are not focused in rapid
uncontrolled growth or stopping growth, but rather on controlling our growth rate so that we might protect and im-
prove upon what we already have.

The Lunenburg Public Health Nursing Service, a division of the Board of Health, offers a comprehensive com-
munity health program to the people of Lunenburg. This includes Visiting Nurse Services, the School Health Program
and Board of Health Nursing follow-up. To perform these services four registered nurses are on the staff: Janet N.
Pace, Nursing Supervisor, Shirley M. Carpenter, Jean E. Hobbsand Marie B. Richards. Barbara D. Eley is employed
part-time as the Agency's secretary.
The goal of the Nursing Service is to provide a comprehensive community health program designed to meet the
total health needs of Lunenburg; via care in their home and programs in the community. To ensure that this goal is
met, an evaluation of the Agency is done annually by the Professional Advisory Committee, Chaired by Dr. Heimberg.
This Committee also meets periodically to review and recommend practices and programs of the Agency.
Annually, the Nursing Service is surveyed and certified by the Federal Government so as to be eligible to care
for Medicare, Medicaid, and other private insurance patients. The fee for a Nursing Visit remains $10.50, either
through patient or third-party payment; although no person is ever refused care because of inability to pay. The serv-
ices offered to the residents of the community are skilled nursing care in their homes by the Agency Nursing Staff and
under the guidance of their physician and by contractual arrangement, Home Health Aides and Medical Social Serv-
ices. The Agency also has a contract with Burbank Hospital's Home Health Care Program which offers supportive serv-
ices such as physical, occupational and speech therapy.
Well-Baby Conferences are held monthly through the Nashoba Associated Boards of Health. Dr. Joos,a Pedia-
trician from Bolton, is at each clinic where physical examinations and immunizations are offered free-of-charge to
infants and preschoolers. This service is meant to supplement that of a family's regular physician by increasing the
health services available to Lunenburg children.
Well-Oldster Clinics are held monthly at the United Parish Building for people of Lunenburg, 55 years of age
and over. Blood pressure checks are done regularly with urine screening and blood sugar tests done periodically.
During 1976 the Dental Hygienist from Nashoba Associated Boards of Health was present to do oral examinations.
Dr. James Dawson, a podiatrist, discussed foot problems and Mary Gardner, a nutritionist with the New England
Dairy Council gave a presentation on nutrition for the elderly.

Council on Aging
The Council on Aging's proudest accomplishment of 1976 has been the inauguration of the "Meals on Wheels"
Program. The program officially began in October, delivering hot meals to the elderly shut-ins, three days a week.
From Octoberuntil the end of December a total of 335 meals have been served to an average of ten persons, on each
of the days. During these first three months of the program a total of 709 miles were travelled by an eager group of
volunteers, supervised by Mrs. Doris Carrier. The Council is most grateful to the following volunteers: Marie Gibbs,
Terry Chapdelaine, Edith Carey, Kathy Draskoczy, Tona Xenakis and Mrs. Carrier for their interest in donating their
time for such a worthwhile effort.
The purpose of instituting this program is two-fold -- to accommodate an elderly shut-in, living alone, by pro-
viding a hot, nutritious meal at a maximum cost of fifty cents per meal, and by having a cheery visitor come into
their home and share a few minutes of what would otherwise have been a very lonely day. With the experience of
knowing that the program has been a complete success in a three month period, the Council is now planning to expand
the "Meals on Wheels" to include fifteen persons, five days a week, beginning with the next fiscal year (July 1).
Success in a venture of this nature can only be attained with the cooperation of the School Committee, the School
Lunch Program and personnel, the Montachusett Nutrition Project, all of the volunteers, and the Lunenburg Public
Health Nursing Service.
With the planned expansion program, volunteers, both men and women, young or old, are desperately needed.
Anyone interested in serving as a volunteer may contact Mrs. Doris Carrier, Beal Street at 582-9959. Referrals and
requests may be made by calling the United Parish House office at 582-6572 from 9AM until 1PM Monday through
Friday. It should be understood, however, that any requests made must be on the basis of physical need only, since
not everyone is eligible for this program. Evaluations for such requests are made by the Lunenburg Public Health
Nursing Service.
Another accomplishment of the Council was initiating the mailing of an informative Newsletter, reaching ap-
proximately 950 Senior Citizens, on a quarterly basis. The purpose of this Newsletter is to keep the retired persons
informed of the many benefits regarding Social Security, insurance, programs and discounts offered by various busi-
nesses in the area.
The Fourth Annual Christmas Dinner was enjoyed by the 139 persons in attendance at the High School and was
followed by a musical program under the direction of Donald Adams. The Council sponsored two bus trips during the
year, one to Gloucester and the other a foliage through the Mohawk Trail. Both trips were well attended. The
Council was saddened by the loss of Roger W. Jones, who passed away in July, and regrets the resignation of
Howard O. Russell.

Conservation Commission
The Lunenburg Conservation Commission met twenty-four times in regular meetings this year and went on ap-
proximately twenty inspection tours of problems relating to the Wetlands Act. Potential purchasers of homes or land
for a house lot should be aware that such an Act exists. Involvement in securing the proper permits and developing
conditions imposed by this Act are expensive and time consuming. During the Annual Town Meeting of 1975, the
Town approved the appropriation of $2000.00 to secure a Wetlands Survey.
For general information, it is the responsibility of a builder and/or property owner to contact the Conservation
Commission for a determination under the Wetlands Protection Act. Generally, any proposed construction, recon-
struction or alteration which could result in removal, filling, dredging or alteration of land within 100 feet of any
creek, river, stream, pond, lake, swamp or fresh water wetland, may require the filing of a written notice by cer-
tified mail to the Conservation Commission.
Thanks to the Audubon Society and their Environmental Intern Program, the Commission was ableto secure the
services of Mr. Richard Defieux who is trained in geological surveys. The results of this survey are maps of the
surficial water resources, geological water resources, a ground water favorability map and an updated and more
accurate HUD Flood Plain Map.
These mapshavebeen put to good use not only by the Conservation Commission, but alsoby the Planning Board
and the Water Study Committee. The report made by Mr. Defieux is very detailed and accurate. The Commission
is thankful for an organization such as the Audubon Society for making such a program available.
Our water resources are invaluable and irreplaceable. The availability of the information in this survey will
help US protect and conserve the water that keeps Lunenburg green and beautiful.

The 1975 report of the Sewerage Advisory Committee predicted what has turned out to be a highly optimistic

system is deemed to be the best answer. Final cost numbers including a detailed analysis of our financial arrange-

Water Study Committee
At the present time the Water Study Committee does not feel that further studies be undertaken by means of
commissioning a professional engineer. We do feel that a continuing study by Townspeople be done, preferably within
five years. This continuing study should not only monitor the development within the Water District, but should also
study various means of assisting the Water District to provide service to the growth areas of the Town. This study group
should be convened at the discretion of the Selectmen and/or the Planning Board. These conclusions are based upon:
a. The needs of the Water District users appear to be satisfied by the District's plans and proposed programs.
b. The immediate requirements of the individual and mini-district users appear to be satisfied with locally
drilled wells.
C. The report of the Lunenburg Growth and Development Committee indicated a consensus that a slow and
controlled Town growth pattern is desired to maintain a rural atmosphere.
Presently, water is supplied to the people of Lunenburg in two ways:
1. Private systems - these are mostly individual systems supplying one or two dwellings but occasionally up to
as many as twenty dwellings. These last mentioned systems "mini-district" are groups of private homes
attached to a small system such as found at Hickory Hills or Lake Shirley.
2. Water District - the Water District covers approximately 1500 homes or buildings of which approximately

health agencies and will also meet the needs of the Water District for the near future.

Highway Department
Rebirth of the Highways. From the 1830's on, high-
ways took second place to the railroads in the building of
our nation. While city streets were maintained, country
roads were left to deteriorate. Meanwhile the railroads
crossed the continent and reached into every area of the
country - until by 1880 there were more miles of rails than
of surfaced roads.
It was the bicycle, oddly enough, that first focused
attention on the deplorable state of the roads. In 1885,
the low-wheeled "safety bicycle" was introduced and it
touched off a bicycle boom. But the bike riders found the
going extremely rough - and soon they were organizing for
better roads.
The cyclists found themselves some powerful - if
unlikely - allies in the farmers and the railroads. The
farmers began to realize that good roads would give them
the freedom to take their produce to market when the price
was best. And the railroads became convinced that im-
proved roads would extend the area from which they could
draw traffic.
George G. Munyon, Jr., Highway Superintendent
In 1892, the National League for Good Roads was
founded and just a year later the Federal Government re-
sponded by opening the Office of Road Inquiry. The Office was the first federal involvement in road building since
the last appropriation for National Road in 1838.
By the 1880's the technology was there to build better roads. The development of the steam engine brought
the invention of many important labor and money saving devices for the road builders; steam-powered jaw rock crusher
was invented in 1858. Add to that the steam shovel, steam roller, steam mortar-mixer and the development of both
Portland and bituminous cement surfaces. All of these changes in the late nineteenth century set the stage for a re-
birth of the highway. But by 1904 the Office of Road Inquiry reported only 141 miles of roads surfaced with some-
thing better than water-bound macadam (small stones).
Finally, it was the automobile that gave the necessary impetus to road improvement. With the appearance of
the Model T, the auto was no longer a luxury few could afford. The motor truck began to find its place as an im-
portant form of freight transport. With the advent of World War I, it became evident that the nation needed a net-
work of highways to provide relief for the railroads and a viable alternative means of transport for the defense
of the nation. The highway boom had begun. By 1920 there were ten million motor cars on the roads, and a year
later the nation's first national highway system was enabled by the Federal Highway Act. A new era of road con-
struction had begun. (Excerpt from ITT Industrial Credit Co., New England Construction Magazine)

Dog Officer
Approximately 1,340 calls were recorded during 1976 with most of the calls concerning lost dogs. This was a
very encouraging year with regard to the issuance of dog licenses aided by a letter program instituted with the help
of the Town Clerk's Office. This letter program will be instituted again in 1977 and should aid in the licensing pro-
gram. I have found in most cases of dog problems, that if they are dealt with on an individual basis with follow-up
measures taken, neighbors--in a spirit of cooperation--resolve many of the problems.
At this time I wish to commend the efforts of the residents in close proximity to the Elementary Schools who
have kept the problem dogs confined during school hours so as to minimize problems occurring at the schools during
recess hours and at bus times. With the confinement of these problem animals, injuries to children while on Town
property were kept to a minimum. Our most troublesome area is still in the Whalom where there remains a large
number of dogs running at large during the daylight hours. These animals congregate at the school bus pick-upareas
and after the pick-ups are made these animals run in packs harassing residents. An individual effort is needed by
residents of the Whalom Area to keep a close check on their dogs. With the onset of winter and the heavy snow cover,
our streets are hazardous enough without packs of dogs running in and out of traffic.
Since the enactment of the by-law concerning confinement of unspayed female dogs during the breeding season,
there has been a significant decrease in the number of packs of dogs in our more heavily populated areas. Our most
difficult problemnow at hand is with dogs molesting livestock and fowl--most often resulting in money losses to owners
of the dogs and often the death of the animals due to the nature of the injuries inflicted. In all but one case, dog
owners were notified and the problems resolved by mutual agreement.
I would like at this time to thank the following departments for their assistance during my recent illness: the
Highway Department, Police Department, Fire Department, Dispatchers and the Town Clerk and her staff. With their
assistance, this department did not have a lapse in service to Town residents.

ARTICLE XIX
REGULATIONS OF CERTAIN ACTIVITIES ON WHALOM LAKE
Section 1. Definitions. The following words as used in this By-law unless a different meaning is required by the context, or as
specifically prescribed, shall have the following meanings:
(a) "Vehicle(s)" shall include all motor vehicles both registered and unregistered including but not limited to snow vehicles,
recreational vehicles, motorcycles etc.
(b) "Public Access" shall mean the public access located in the City of Leominster.
(c) "Law Enforcement Officer" shall mean any police officer of the Town of Lunenburg or City of Leominster, fish and game
wardens, members of the State Police and employees of the Commonwealth having police powers on public lands.
Section 2. No person, group of persons or organizations shall operate, or cause to be operated, any vehicle on Whalom Lake without
a written Permit from the Board of Selectmen of the Town of Lunenburg or other Municipal or State Agency duly authorized by law.
Section 3. Every application for a Permit made by an individual shall be signed by the applicant therefor, who shall give his or her
full name and home address.
Section 4. Every application for a Permit made by a partnershipshall state the full names and home addresses of all the members of
the partnership and shall be signed by a majority thereof.
Section 5. Every application for a Permit made by an association shall signed by a majority of the members of the governing body
thereof, who shall state their full names and home addresses.
Section 6. Every applicant for a Permit required by the provisions of Section 5, Chapter 110, General Laws, to file certificate
stating the real name of a person conducting a business, shall file with his application a certified copy thereof.
Section 7. Every application for a Permit made by a corporation shall state the full names and home addresses of the president,
treasurer, clerk and secretary, directors and manager or other principal representative of the corporation. It shall be signed by some
officer duly authorized by a vote of its board of directors or other similar board. A copy of such vote certified by the clerk or sec-
retary of the corporation, together with a copy of the certificate of its organization, shall accompany the application. A copy of
the vote appointing its manager or other principal representative shall also accompany the application.
Section 8. All applications for Permits shall be made upon blanks furnished by the said Board of Selectmen and shall be fully answered
in detail and shall be typewritten or legibly written in ink. Applications written in pencil, in whole or in part, will not be accepted.
Any false statement contained in any application shall be a cause or ground for refusing to grant the Permit or for suspending, can-
celling or revoking a Permit already granted.
Section 9. As many police officers shall be assigned to duty at Whalom Lake as may reasonably be deemed necessary by the Chief
or Senior officer in charge of the Police Department of the Town of Lunenburg in order to properly and effectively police any activity
thereon which will involve the operation of vehicles, whenever in the judgment of the Chief or such Senior officer, there is a like-
lihood that there will be a substantial number of participants in, and/or persons present to witness the activity. The expense incurred
in providing such officers shall be borne by the applicant for the Permit.
Section 10. No person shall operate a vehicle on Whalom Lake who has not attained the age of sixteen and one-half (16½) years.
Section 11. No vehicle shall be operated on Whalom Lake within five hundred (500) feet of a residence, or in a negligent manner
so that the lives and safety of the public might be endangered or the property of others might be damaged.
Section 12. It shall be unlawful to possess or keep in and upon a vehicle on Whalom Lake any alcoholic beverage or any controlled
substance as defined by General Laws, Chapter 94C, as amended.
Section 13. No vehicle or any part thereof, shall be allowed to remain on Whalom Lake later than sunset on any day and, in default
thereof, shall be subject to being towed or otherwise removed and impounded at the expense of the owner.
Section 14. It shall be unlawful for any person to discharge in or upon Whalom Lake any pollutant or to discard therein or thereupon,
litter of any kind or upon the ways adjacent thereto, including Lakefront Road, Prospect Street, Lakeside Avenue and in and about
the vicinity of the public access area. Whoever violates any part of this section shall be punished by a fine of not less than One
Hundred Dollars ($100.00) nor more than Three Hundred Dollars ($300.00).
Section 15. No vehicle shall enter upon Whalom Lake except by the public access.
Section 16. The Permit of any holder thereof who, in the conduct of the activity permitted, commits or permitsany violation or in-
fraction of any part of this By-law shall be subject to modification, suspension, cancellation or revocation.
Section 17. Whoever violates any part of Sections 2, 11, 12 and 15 hereof, shall be punished by a fine of not less than Twenty-
Five Dollars ($25.00) nor more than One Hundred Dollars ($100.00).

Lunenburg has combined the offices of the Town Clerk, Tax Collector and Treasurer since 1916, with one full-
time person serving in the three capacities. Throughout the years, this combination has proven to be an excellent
way of overcoming the inherent weaknesses in the stereotype of small town government, wherein each office is held
by a part-time, often unavailable official. Our Town Office has been able to overlap its various functions, and
handle all sorts of Town business without referring people from here to there and back to get answers or assistance.
Not only does this system permit US to serve the
public more competently, but also to use personnel more
effectively, by channeling their energies in different di-
rections as peak workloads occur in one or another of the
three "departments".
