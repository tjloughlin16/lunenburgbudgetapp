QUESTION NO. 4 - PROPOSED AMENDMENT TO THE CONSTITUTION
Do you approve of the adoption of an amendment to the constitution summarized below, which was approved by the General
Court in a joint session of the two branches held July 15, 1968, received 181 votes in the affirmative and 66 in the negative, and
in a joint session of the two branches held May 15, 1969, received 208 votes in the affirmative and 54 in the negative
Summary - The proposed amendment to the Constitution amends the existing constitutional provisions which direct the division
of the Commonwealth into 240 representative districts and 40 senatorial districts. It provides for a census of the inhabitants of each
city and town in the Commonwealth in the year 1971 and in every tenth year thereafter. The census in 1971 is to be the basis for
determining the representative and senatorial districts for the ten-year period beginning January 1, 1975. Each subsequent census
shall be the basis for determining the districts for each ten-year period beginning the fourth January following each census, so that
the census in 1981 will determine the districts as of January, 1985, etc.
The amendment provides that the house of representatives shall consist of two hundred and forty members and the senate of forty
members. In its first regular session following each census, the Legislatureshall divide the Commonwealth into two hundred and forty
representative districts of contiguous territory and forty senatorial districts also of contiguous territory. Each representative district
shall contain, as nearly as may be, an equal number of inhabitants according to the census. Each senatorial districtshall also contain
as nearly as may be an equal number of inhabitants according to the census. A representative district shall not unite two or more
counties, towns, or cities, or parts thereof, or a city and a town, or parts thereof, in one district unless the same is unavoidable.
Further, no town of less thansix thousand inhabitants shall be divided in forming representative districts. It is also provided that the
County of Dukes County and Nantucket County shall each be a representative district, notwithstanding the foregoing. The senatorial
districts, each of which shall elect one Senator, shall be formedwithout uniting two counties or parts of two or more counties, unless
the same is unavoidable.
Each representative shall be an inhabitant of the district for which he is chosen at least one year immediately preceding his
election, and each senatorshall be an inhabitant of the district for which heis chosen at the time of his election and shall have been
on inhabitant of the Commonwealth for at least five years immediately preceding his election. Any representative or senator who
ceases to be an inhabitant of the Commonwealth shall cease to represent his district.
The amendment also provides that the Legislature (1) may by law limit the time within which judicial proceedings may be in-
stituted calling in question any such division and (2) shall prescribe by law the manner of calling and conducting elections for the
choice of representatives and of ascertaining their election.
Finally, the amendment annuls the existing constitutional provisions with respect to apportionment of representatives andsena-
torial districts, although those provisions will remain in effect until January 1, 1975.

"4-H in Action"
The year 1970 saw a further surge in 4-H activity in Lunenburg. Although two clubs were dropped, the total
membership has increased to approximately seventy members who belong to six active clubs. The Bridle Path 4-H
Club led by Mrs. Martin Moore, who took over from the founder Lois Camber in the Spring of 1970, now numbers
forty-eight members. All these youngsters own or have the complete care of a horse.
Other operating clubs are the Mixed Breeds Dairy Club led by Mrs. Edmond Bourque, the Kitchen Kapers Food
Club led by Mrs. Roger Raboin, the Foods and Sewing Club led by Mrs. Henry Anderson, a new Foods Club started
by Mrs. Agnes Price and the Pie Crust Kids Foods Club led by Mrs. Dean Gauntlett.
The year began with a 4-H Exhibit and Demonstration Night heldon January 30th in the United Parish Building.
Mr. & Mrs. Leo Jarkko, 4-H leaders in Fitchburg, were the judges for the demonstrations. On February 20th, a
Horse Health and Training Seminar was held in the Parish Building sponsored by the Bridle Path Club. On March
20th, several local members travelled to Worcester to participate in the County Demonstration Run-Offs.
On April 11th, the members of the Bridle Path Club attended a 4-H Horse Clinic held at the Townshend-
Morgan Farm in Bolton on the fundamentals of Fitting and Showmanship. In April Mrs. Howard Camber,along with the
4-H judging team, travelled out to the University of Massachusetts to attend a State Leaders Field Day held in Grinnell
Arena. This Field Day was sponsored by the Department of Veterinary and Animal Sciences and the Division of 4-H
and Youth Programs at the University.
The first of two horse clinics was held on May 29th with Dr. James T. Wright administering the shots for
tetanus, shipping fever and encephalitis. The second clinic to complete the series of innoculations was held on
June 19th. On June 13th, eleven local members attended the Junior Northeast Achievement Day held at Camp
Marshall in Spencer. All members participated in demonstrations and exhibits. They were accompanied by Mrs.
Thomas Currie and Mrs. Dean Gauntlett. Lunenburg members participated in the County 4-H Auction held on June
3rd by collecting and transporting items to the event held in Sterling. On June 14th, the Bridle Path Club sponsored
a 4-H Horse Show held at the North Leominster Rod & Gun Club which featured twenty-seven English and Western
classes. The response from other clubs was tremendous. The show was under the direction of Mrs. Howard Camber
and Mrs. Martin Moore.
Mrs. Ruth Anderson began her club in October of 1969 as a foods project. In February of 1970, her members
wanted to sewand started with culottes which were exhibited at the annual Grange Fair. The club has now expanded
to some ten members and includes both cooking and sewing. In the Summer of 1970, Richard J. Bishop ,a teacher
in the Lunenburg School System, was officially appointed as a Trustee of the Lunenburg 4-H Fund. The two other
Trustees are Roger Bigelow and Raymond Proctor. Other active members of the 4-H Town Committee include: Jean
Stockwell, Theresa Maki, Marcie Gauntlett, Susie Logan, Helene Bishop, Lois Camber, Carolyn Jokinen, Marjorie
Stillman, Richard Dustin, Ruth Anderson, Yvonne Bourque, Gloria Currie, Diane Moore and Barbara Raboin.
The Mixed Breeds Dairy Club under the supervision of Mrs. Yvonne Bourque participated in the 19th Annual
4-H Dairy Tour and Judging Contest which took place in the summer. The Don Rovin Farm in Sterling wasvisited
where Jersey judging was held and the Chedco Farm in Berlin where Guernsey judging was carried out. The group
then went to St. Benedict Center in Harvard where Holstein judging was held. The Lunenburg judging team consisted
of Judith, Carol and Karen Bourque and Martha Paquin.
October 4th to the 10th was proclaimed as National 4-H Week across the country and saw the beginning of the
Lunenburg 4-H Cookie Sale with Jean Stockwell again as Chairman. Twenty-six members of the Horse Club rode in
the Homecoming Parade on October 17th.
Officers elected in October for the 1970-1971 year were Chairman, Mrs. Dean Gauntlett; Secretary, Mrs.
Robert Stockwell and Treasurer, Mrs. Theresa Maki.

INTRODUCING OUR SCHOOL SUPERINTENDENT
On August 1,1971, Dr. William R. Holland came to Lunenburg as Superintendent of Schools.
Dr. Holland completed his Doctoral studies in school administration at Boston University in June,
1970. He holds a Master's Degree from the University of Massachusetts and a Bachelor's Degree
from the University of Rhode Island. Dr. Holland's experience covers all areas of education,
including teaching, administration, school building problems, and curricula & program develop-
ment.
Under Dr. Holland's leadership, the Lunenburg School System should be a dynamic, ever
improving, superior system. Our progress will be a studied, carefully planned, and thoroughly
carried out process involving administrators, faculty, students, as well as segments from the
community at large. His style, his appreciation of people and the part each plays, his warmth and
sincerity have already been experienced by many.
The School Committee knows that with a Superintendent of Dr. Holland's character, ability,
and background, coupled with an effective faculty, staff and administration, Lunenburg will
continue to achieve the educational goals which we know each citizen of Lunenburg desires for
his children.

It is an honor to submit my first annual report as Superintendent of the Lunenburg Public Schools. This is
the 101st such report preserved in the Town records and covers the year ending December 31, 1970.
My appointment as Superintendent became official on August 3, 1970, the effective date of the resignation

Mr. Brown served as Superintendent since August 1962. During his tenure, he tirelessly, wisely and intelli-

THEMES OF THE 70' S
Looking back on 1970 and ahead to 1971 there are two basic issues that dominate the educational scene in
the beginning of this new decade. The first issue deals with financial support of education especially on the local
level. The major problems associated with school support through the local property tax are numerous and quite
complex. The fiscal crisis has arrived and won't disappear until other alternatives for school support are found.
In short, the local property tax can no longer support or in the future sustain our present educational program. A
major tax overhaul is needed on the State and National levels. Recently, there are hopeful indications on both
the State and local level that some tax reform is in sight. Such indications are welcome because it is becoming
near impossible to administer the schools of the urban-suburban 1970's under fiscal systems of the agrarian 1920's
and 30's. A positive by-product of this financial squeeze is a demand by taxpayers to have our school system run
more efficiently and with more creative skill. Accountability has suddenly become the in-phrase in educational

The Elementary Annex
A critical look has been taken this year at the Elementary Annex, a 1925 facility that currently houses 180
third grade youngsters and 7 teachers. A 25-page position paper that was prepared by our staff and presented to
the School Committee outlined a five (5) year long-range plan to update the Annex both physically and educa-
tionally. The plan, a result of several months of study, calls for spreading necessary expenditures in areas of
heating, sound-proofing, plumbing, electricity, etc., over aperiod time--many of these expenditures, e. g.,
a hot lunch program, have been mandated by the State while others are required or very strongly urged by the
State Building Inspector. Many recommendations contained in the report also call for changes which require very
little or no money. The need for updating the Annex is best explained in the Introduction of the report:
"The purpose of this study was to carefully study and assess the Elementary School Annex in terms of its
physical and structural adequacy as well as its educational adequacy. Is this a building that can physically con-
tinue to be used in the years ahead? Can the building serve the demands of changing times and directions in edu-
cation? Can it meet the "minimum" educational quality standards set by the State for elementary school programs

in the 1970's and 1980's? Can the Town of Lunenburg afford to close the Annex and undertake a new building
program that would include placing an addition on the Passios School and possibly an addition on the High School?
A search for some answers to these questions was the major reason for undertaking this study.
"After a careful review and as a result of the data compiled in this report, it is our professional opinion
that:
(I) Lunenburg is in no financial or psychological position to undertake a new building program.
(This didn't take much study to determine.)
(2) The Elementary School Annex with an agreed upon long-range plan of renovation and improve-

adequate elementary educational facility for the next fifteen years. This assumption is based

on the secondary level due to the opening of the Montachusett Regional Vocational School and
to some internal scheduling changes we could make to accommodate any slight or unanticipated
enrollment increases in this period of time. The assumption is also based upon the fact that a

SCHOOL COMMITTEE ACTIVITIES
To say that 1970 was a busy, hectic and rewarding year for School Committee members would indeed be an
understatement. In addition to hiring a new Superintendent, the Committee again spent considerable time
involved in the collective bargaining process. Of particular note were the contract negotiations with Lunenburg
custodians.
Custodians joined the AFL-CIO Custodial Union last Spring and contract negotiations began with the Union
representative and our custodians in June. A two-year, forty-one page master contract settlement was signed in
late December, a settlement that represented many clock hours on the part of all parties, especially school
committee members who met separately almost weekly for this purpose. The end result of the negotiations was a
very fair settlement that represents the best interests of the town, the school system, and the custodians.
Currently, the School Committee and Lunenburg Teachers' Association are involved in contract negotia-
tions. For reasons stated in the introduction of this report, salary discussions are particularly difficult this year
and at this point there is a possibility that a salary agreement with teachers will not be reached until sometime
after Town Meeting. The possibility of extended salary negotiations is not a happy thought. In the long run, no
95
===PAGE 98===
one actually wins and the school program could suffer. The dilemma the School Committee faces in these times of
rising costs is one of sustaining a sound and modern educational program while at the same time considering the
ability of the town to pay. Unquestionably, public trust and confidence in School Committees is being tested in
an unparalleled way. Fortunately, Lunenburg has a history of enthusiastically supporting the recommendations
and actions of its School Committee. This past trust and confidence is primarily responsible for the fine quality of
education that currently exists in Lunenburg. Such public confidence must continue if we are to maintain and
improve our present educational program. Undue political pressure that challenges or denies the legal authority
of the School Committee can only result in a lowering of educational progress and quality in Lunenburg Schools.
The sincere dedication and commitment of present School Committee members should not go unnoticed. I am con-
fident that the community will continue to give the School Committee their wholehearted support this year as in
past years.

DEATH
WILLARD E. POWELL
1965-1970
On March 15, 1965, Willard E. Powell was elected by the Lunen-
burg School Committee to the position of Custodian in the Lunenburg
Jr.-Sr. High School. Willard served in this capacity until his unexpec-
ted death on December 1st of this year. He graduated from Lunenburg
High School in 1938 and served in the Medic division of the U.S. Army
during World War II. Willard performed his duties as School Custodian
with great devotion and sense of pride. He will be sadly. missed by
many.

RETIREMENTS
MRS. GLADYS L. BROWN
1931-1938
1948-1970
Mrs. Brown began her teaching duties with the Lunenburg School
System as Gladys A. Leonard in September 1931 when she was hired as
teacher of Grade I in the old Center School. She remained in this posi-
tion until June of 1938 when she resigned to be married. In November of
1948, when the schools went on double sessions, Mrs. Brown's services
were again engaged, and she became the teacher of a combination Grade
2 & 3 class. The following September, she transferred to the Brooks
House and taught a combination Grade 3 & 4 class, and then from
September 1950 through June 1955, she taught Grade 4.
In September 1955, Lunenburg began its first special class for the men-
tally retarded and at this time, Mrs. Brown assumed the responsibility.
She remained in this position until her retirement, effective August 21,
1970.
The following letter of commendation was sent to her by the Lunenburg School Committee and Superintendent
of Schools and aptly expresses the sentiments of everyone: "The members of the Lunenburg School Committee and
myself, would like to take time to express our genuine appreciation of the exceptional service which you have
rendered to young people over the twenty-nine years you have served in Lunenburg. Your record of service is
enviable. Your quality as a unique person filled with human warmth and dedicated interest on behalf of children,
the schools, and the community, are deeply appreciated."
Although Gladys has "retired" as a permanent full-time teacher, we are happy to see her around the schools
quite frequently as a "substitute".
WILLIAM F. MORAN
1962-1970
"Bill" first began his employment with the School Department on
April 30, 1962, when he was hired as a Custodian in the Jr.-Sr. High
School. When the new Turkey Hill Middle School opened, Bill was
unanimously appointed as the new Senior Custodian by the School
Committee in September 1968, assuming his duties effective December
30, 1968. He remained in this position until December 30,1970, when
it was necessary for him to retire by law.
On January 17, 1971, a Testimonial was held for Bill at the Middle
School where school personnel, relatives, and friends, presented him
with gifts and a money tree. A short program was held at which time
the Chairman of the School Committee presented him with a plaque
honoring his years of dedicated service. Bill Moran's sense of humor,
leadership, and efficiency, will be missed--we wish him much happi-
ness in his retirement years.
