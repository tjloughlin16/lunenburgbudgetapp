CLASSROOM LANGUAGE INTERVENTION PROGRAM:
Another area of weakness we found prevalent with a number of our students was language develop-
ment. As a matter of fact, many students began to show up as language deficient in grades three
and four, culminating in speech and writing deficiencies. Formerly, when this occurred, students
affected were put on Individualized Educational Plans and pulled out of class for special service
(therapy) with a speech and language specialist. By increasing the time of one of our formerly
part-time speech and language therapists and through the curriculum development efforts of another
speech and language therapist, Allan Wilson, and the building administration, a program was
developed in which language needs of all students are addressed in the regular classroom. Many
more students are reached through this program called CLIP (Classroom Language Intervention
Program) while regular classroom teachers who are involved in CLIP are receiving valuable in-
service.

IN MEMORIAM
P. ROBERT CORMIER (May 31, 1938 - May 29, 1982)
We were all saddened by the death on Saturday, May 29, 1982 of Bob Cormier. Bob was graduated
from Assumption College in Worcester receiving an AB degree in June 1960. In September of the same
year he came to Lunenburg as a teacher of English and languages in our junior-senior high school.
During his tenure with us he served as language department head as well as school yearbook advisor.
He attended Notre Dame University graduate school in South Bend, Indiana and received a master's
degree in theology and linguistics from the University of Rennes, Brittany, France.
Due to his serious illness, he found it necessary to retire on March 31, 1980. In his letter
to the school committee and the superintendent, he indicated that "teaching for me was far more
than a livelihood--it was a life-style. Lunenburg served me well during my nineteen years, as I
hope I may have been of some service to Lunenburg. My world of teaching could not have been, and
would not have been possible, without the support of three persons who stand as pillars in my
school life--Mr. Richard Carey, Mrs. Helen Brockelman and Mr. Anthony Falcone. The first was
considerate in more ways than one in my behalf; the second, brought to me the life of the school
in so many ways; and the third, is the best friend a man is blessed with. For nineteen years,
Lunenburg was a major part of my life. There is a void which cannot be filled."
And there is a void for many of us with Bob's passing.
Bob is survived by his mother, Mrs. Viola "Billie" (Smith) Cormier, and two brothers, William
J. Cormier of Leominster and John M. Cormier of Lunenburg.
WILLIAM F. MORAN (December 19, 1900 - June 1, 1982)
Bill passed away on Tuesday, June 1, 1982 in Burbank
Hospital after a long illness.
He was a former owner and operator of Maple Hill Dairy
in Lunenburg and was employed from April 30, 1962 to December
31, 1970 as a custodian in the junior-senior high and middle
schools. He served as senior custodian at the Turkey Hill
Middle School from December 30, 1968 to his retirement. A
reception in his honor was held in the library of the middle
school on January 17, 1971 which was attended by school
personnel, family and friends, including former Superintendent
of Schools Ralph W. Brown. Robert Antonucci welcomed guests
and Richard Bishop was the master of ceremonies. A plaque
was presented to him by Walter Keeler (Chairman of the school
committee) honoring his years of dedicated service. Tribute
was also given him for his exemplary service by Superinten-
dent of Schools William R. Holland and Floyd Barney, Principal
of the middle school. A gold pendulum clock was given to him
by Harris Karis on behalf of the school faculty and a flower
and money centerpiece by Arthur Archambault, Supervisor of
Buildings & Grounds on behalf of the school department.
Bill is survived by two daughters, two sisters, five
grandchildrén and one great-grandchild.

Tax Collector,
Treasurer Reports
1953 - 1982:
1955. "The Treasurer makes annual debt and service payments and borrows money, and on December 1, 1955,
borrowed $985,000.00 as authorized by Article 13 of the warrant for the 1955 Annual Meeting. This bor-
rowing was done at the rate of 2.50%
1956, "Included in the 1957 Warrant is an article which calls for a sum of money to renovate and enlarge the
Town Office. This would be accomplished by eliminating the wall between the present office and a kitchen
which is no longer used. An increase in administrative funds has also been requested for 1957."
1958 "One of the most time consuming, yet rewarding, projects undertaken by this office is the preparation of
the Annual Town Report. The "Offset" process was employed in the printing of this year's report. This
means that the copy was furnished the printer ready for the camera. Although included in the cost of the
book, this year, is the purchase of an IBM Executive Model electric typewriter, a considerable savings
should be realized in the future as a result of preparing the book in this manner."
1960. "Although unable to answer every one of the many and diversified questions which were asked at the
Town Office, such as, 'What is the proper attire for the roller skating rink?', 'What is the best method of
disposing of swarming bees?', 'What do you do with dead skunks in the road?', and 'Is deer meat which
has remained in a freezer for two and one-half years still edible?', we were able to ascertain that a permit
is required to keep a racoon, did endeavor to supply the names of President-elect Kennedy's newly ap-
pointed cabinet members, and trust that, in general, we were able to satisfactorily dispose of the majority
of the problems with which we were confronted."
108
===PAGE 111===
1961 "In the ten year period from 1951 to 1961 the volume of work in the Town Office has more than doubled.
The Town Clerk is grateful to Mrs. Sylvia Oksanen and Miss Joyce White, who work in the Town Office on
a full time basis, and to Mrs. Ine Pihlaja, who is employed part time, for their loyal, conscientious, and
expeditious handling of the numerous and varied tasks which they are called upon to perform. However,
in spite of our best efforts, we find that we cannot keep pace with increased work loads and have, there-
fore, requested the services of an additional clerk commencing in March 1962.'
1962. "The image of Massachusetts Town Government has been described as a rural settlement with a white
town hall, staffed principally by a town clerk and numerous part-time boards, several colonial-style churches
abutting an elm-dotted common, a small commercial center where business is transacted on a first-name
basis in a rather leisurely, friendly manner, and a long tradition of local participation in town affairs. Al-
though, fortunately, this picture is an accurate image of the outward appearance of our Town, a closer
look at the "balance sheet" discloses that Lunenburg's development, through the years, into an urban
community performing many of the same municipal functions as far larger towns do elsewhere, has been
masqued by its colonial facade.
Realizing that a form of government which was adequate and suitable for the needs of people twenty-five
years ago could not cope with today's municipal problems without considerable modification, we have
endeavored through continuous organizational improvements, more effective utilization of personnel and
equipment, and the up-dating of facilities, to reshape the various functions of the Town Office so as to
meet new challenges and new situations and yet preserve much of the time-honored traditions and customs."
1965 "The unusually large number of independent boards, commissions and departments which characterize
the administrative structure of most Massachusetts Towns has been described as an administrative
weakness. In order to overcome this weakness more and more towns are consolidating administrative
service functions into one department for efficiency of operation, more effective use of personnel and as
a convenience to the townspeople. In Lunenburg the administrative function of the Board of Selectmen,
Board of Health, Zoning Board and Licensing Authority are combined with those of the Town Clerk, Tax
Collector, Treasurer and Registrars of Voters."
1966 "Although we must certainly recognize the merit in and necessity for rigid control of municipal financing
we might well criticize the State system of accounting. It is ironic that the State prescribes a system of
accounting wherein we commence spending monies in January which are 1. voted the following March;
2. raised by property taxes which are billed the following June; and 3. actually received mainly in the last
three months of the year. As an example, $695,569 of the $989,593 in 1966 real estate taxes assessed
were received after October 1, 1966 even though this is the bulk of the money with which we are supposed
to run our Government during the entire calendar year 1966. This means that the Treasurer must borrow
money in anticipation of the taxes and other anticipated revenue until such revenue is received."
1972 "In 1969, the State Legislature passed what has come to be known as "The Fiscal Cycle Bill", which has
far-reaching effects on every city and town in the Commonwealth. The original Bill was so poorly conceived
that in 1969 and again in 1970 the Legislature postponed action on it. In 1971, 23 pages of corrections
were drafted, and the Bill was once more placed before the Legislature, whereupon in spite of protests
by the State Division of Accounts, who were much concerned over the errors, omissions and inconsistencies
of even the corrected version, it was passed. Further amendments and corrections are being worked on
even as the Bill goes into effect.
One of the main features of this legislation is to effect twice-a-year billing of Real Estate Taxes. In the 18
month transitional year, two part bills will be mailed in June for the entire 18 month period, but only 2/3 of
the bill, or 12 months worth, will become overdue on November 1. The last 1/3 will become overdue on
April 1 (to be changed to May 1). In subsequent years 12 month bills will be mailed in June, with 1/2 be-
coming overdue on November 1 and 1/2 becoming overdue on May 1. This means that the banks will be
paying over tax monies twice a year, in October and April. The net effect on the taxpayer is that he will be
paying the tax for the first half of a calendar year six months earlier than heretofore. The advantage to the
Town which this "speedup" represents is difficult to evaluate in advance. Theoretically the extra payment
will save the Town money, because we will not have to borrow as much in anticipation of revenue, and
therefore will pay out less interest. However, the increased costs of extra clerical services involved in the
duplicate billing process will probably equal or even exceed the savings on interest."
109
===PAGE 112===
1974. "Motor Vehicle Excise billing continues to be a source of annoyance to Taxpayers and Tax Officials alike.
An examination of excise bills issued by the State Department of Motor Vehicles has shown that approxi-
mately 25% of them are in error. The resulting confusion and inconvenience to vehicle owners and to the
municipality which processes these bills is immeasurable, as is the cost involved. City and Town officials
throughout the State are calling for a complete revision and correction of the system presently employed
by the Registry of Motor Vehicles."
1977 "Effective cash management is generally recognized as essential to good fiscal management. This is par-
ticularly true as mounting costs have placed ever-increasing pressures on local revenues, and we must
find ways to meet increased costs while placing a minimum additional burden on the taxpayer. Many of
the policies and procedures that are only now being introduced to many local governments for use in
investing so-called "idle cash" (money that is available in the treasury but is not needed for current day-
to-day financial transactions) have been employed in our Town for many years, and in Fiscal 1976 and
Fiscal 1977, for instance, resulted in total interest earnings in excess of $125,000. Even so, the Treasurer/
Collector's office is in the process of reviewing and improving collection, deposit and disbursement pro-
cedures to assure even greater availability of funds to meet cash needs and for investment purposes."
1980 "It is assumed that Lunenburg taxpayers, by their overwhelming vote on the subject, do want and expect
Lunenburg officials to make every attempt to comply with the 2½ Mandate, and do want and expect to be
provided with the very best Government possible within the constraints of Proposition 2½.
In recent years, new emphasis has been placed on the need for improved Financial Management in view
of what is deemed to be the precarious financial condition of some Massachusetts cities and towns. This
year, even more emphasis is being placed on Financial Management because of Proposition 2½ and
there is a growing concern that cuts in financial budgets will result in loss of income that will far surpass the
small amount of savings realized by reducing financial office staffs. At present, Lunenburg's tax bills go
out on schedule, tax collections are right up to the minute, our accounts receivable do not include any
back property taxes, our tax titles are foreclosed as quickly as possible and we cash manage to the hilt."
1981 "In spite of the constraints of Proposition 2½, Lunenburg is extremely healthy. We ended Fiscal Year '81
with an unrestricted fund balance of $520,155 and no uncollected Real Estate taxes. Our Fiscal Year '82
tax bills were mailed on time and Real Estate taxes were received on or before the usual November 1
deadline. We have not had to borrow in anticipation of revenue since 1973. We were able to weather the
first year of Proposition 2½ without any appreciable reduction in services and, even with a substantial
reduction in our teaching staff, our present pupil/teacher-supervisor ratio is under seventeen. We do not
at the present time anticipate further reductions in staff in Fiscal Year '83 and could inflate current oper-
ating budgets an estimated seven percent and still absorb a $100,000 decrease in State Aid. Current
prognostications by State Legislators notwithstanding, we are confident that State Aid will not be cut in
1982 for that type of thing simple does not happen in the State of Massachusetts during an Election Year."
1982. As predicted, in 1982 (an election year) the State did indeed come through with the additional aid and the
Town was able to carry on in Fiscal 1983 without any further reduction in services. In my opinion, Fiscal
year 1984 is, however, going to be tough - It may be time to call on the voters at the polls to effect the
"override provision" of Proposition 2½. The F.Y. 1983 tax rate was $19.40, or $5.60 under the $25.00
permitted by Prop. 2½. What "Prop. 2½" says is that it may be necessary for a city or town to increase
its tax levy more than 2½ percent a year. In fact If inflation continues to average more than 2½% a year
and we never voted to override, eventually we would have no real estate taxes and neither would we have
any town government. That is to day, it was not the intent of this legislation to never increase spending more
than 21/2% over the previous year, it was instead the intent of "Prop. 2½" to allow the voters at the polls to
authorize any such spending, rather than leaving it up to a smaller nucleus of people at Town Meeting.
Accordingly, the April Ballot will contain a question which asks you to authorize the expenditure of an
additional $93,387. A "yes" vote will add approximately 50 cents to the F.Y. '84 tax rate, and will cost
the average taxpayer approximately $35.00. It appears to me that this is a reasonable request and that
the $93,387 is needed to maintain last year's level of service.
