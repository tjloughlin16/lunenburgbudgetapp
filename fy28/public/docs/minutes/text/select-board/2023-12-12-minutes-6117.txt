Vote on 3rd Quarter Preliminary Tax Bill Insert
Chair Jeffreys commenced a substantive discourse aimed at enlightening taxpayers concerning
the third-quarter preliminary tax bill, which has been impacted by delays in the certification of
property values. The Board underscored critical points including the ramifications associated
with an anticipated larger-than-usual increase in the fourth quarter. This increase is necessitated
by the requirement to account for adjustments entirely within the concluding quarter, diverging
from the customary spread across two quarters.
To ensure taxpayer clarity, Chair Jeffreys articulated a detailed notification intended to inform all
taxpayers of forthcoming tax billing adjustments and the extension of deadlines for abatements
and exemptions. He emphasized the need to highlight that the fiscal year 2024 valuation process
and tax rate adjustments will only manifest in the last quarter, stressing the significance of
planning, particularly for individuals reliant on fixed incomes.
The Board emphasized transparency and preparedness in managing taxpayer expectations,
advocating for the detailed insert as a foundational tool to promote awareness and insight before
the issuance of actual bills.
Moreover, clear directives were provided regarding flexible payment arrangements for those
requiring budget management, proposing the option to make payments in advance. This
flexibility is devised to prevent taxpayers from facing unexpected financial burdens, thereby
facilitating budgetary preparation in anticipation of substantial billing adjustments necessitated
by scheduling shifts. Subsequently, a motion was presented to approve the inclusion of this
detailed insert with the third-quarter bills, alongside the recommendation that it be printed on
official town letterhead

Initial Discussion: Town Vision and Mission Statements
Chair Jeffreys proposed creating a distinct vision and mission statements for Lunenburg to
clarify the town's purpose and long-term goals. Drawing from successful organizations, these
statements would guide decision-making and align efforts towards common objectives. Chair
Jeffreys suggested setting time in January for an in-depth discussion to address community
issues and ensure alignment with the town's master plan. He emphasized a collaborative
approach involving diverse stakeholders to craft statements that reflect the community's needs.
Integrating SMART criteria—goals that are Specific, Measurable, Attainable, Relevant, and
Time-bound-was recommended to drive development over the next decade. The mission and
vision aim to serve as strategic guides and resonate with residents, embodying Lunenburg's
values and aspirations.

COMMITTEE REPORTS
School Committee: Member Emkey provided detailed topics: Key issues included efforts to
locate class officers from several Lunenburg High School classes to address the matter of
unclaimed class funds. A preceding article in the local ledger listed the classes requiring
assistance with class officers for the graduating years of 2003, 2007, 2010, 2016, and 2018.
Significant dialogue revolved around the Turkey Hill window engineering capital request, set to
be deferred to fiscal year 2026. This maneuver arose amid deliberations concerning the
Massachusetts School Building Authority's (MSBA) Accelerated Repair Program, underscoring
frustration with MSBA's procedural timelines, impacting strategic planning efforts. Following
these discussions, Mr. Beardmore volunteered to draft a letter intended for the MSBA, proposing
a review in the forthcoming meeting. Additionally, a pressing concern acknowledged was the
upcoming fiscal year 2025 budget challenges, notably stemming from the diminishing ESSER
funds, coupled with anticipated increases in health insurance costs, speculated to reach up to
10%. This figure aligns with town-wide budgeting advice previously indicated by MIIA,
encompassing municipal and school financial planning. A letter for state legislators discussing
these fiscal hurdles was drafted during the meeting.
