WORKSHOP: OLD PRIMARY SCHOOL
• Meeting with Ray Dunetz on preferred concept for Landscape Design for 30 School
Street
Vil. Dunetz noted this is the 3rd community meeting regarding the 30 School Street site design.
The process started in December 2021 with a site walk with the Select Board and town officials
to get input on what might happen at this park. A community meeting was held in early
December to generate comments and was followed up with an online survey. Based on the
feedback we drafted two options and presented those in mid-January and issued another online
survey. Between then and now we have refined the Master Plan to capture everyone's input and
are looking to finish everything by the end of the month.
At community meeting #1 we talked about the history and some inspiration about what this park
could be. We also talked about the condition of the old primary school and generated a lot of
feedback from the first online survey. It was surprising how well it matched the requests for
proposals for this project. Folks really wanted a town green, community space for the farmers
market and concerts, to enjoy nature with pollinator gardens and to have an opportunity to picnic
and have enough parking to accommodate all those new uses.
===PAGE 2===
bit of sense that you are in the building. A large 40 x 70 foot pavilion shade structure will
provide programming opportunities and a place for the farmers market. One big design element
requested was a strolling path. We created a handicap parking area at the entrance to the park
with an accessible path that goes all the way around. We added picnic tables close to the parking
area and away from the pollinator meadow that we wanted to incorporate into the plan for some
nature interpretation and learning. A large lawn area can be used for a variety of purposes. A
heavily favored concept was an amphitheater with a band shell that would match the pavilion
with granite seating that is nestled into the hillside with shade trees on either side. We would
buffer the southern property from sound and from the park itself with plantings. We placed two
restroom facilities near the entrance that would require utility connections such as water service,
electrical and sewer. At the forefront of the building there will be an ornamental gate that allows
vehicles to drop off their wares or allows emergency vehicles access. We have an interpretive
sign that talks about the old Primary School. The parking area was reorganized from 25 to 48
spaces and 12 new spaces were redeveloped behind the Ritter Building. We created accessible
walkways and crosswalks and narrowed the wide opening from Massachusetts Avenue to a 24
foot wide two-way lane. Also incorporated is pedestrian level, dark sky compliant lighting. We
also considered placing ambient lighting under the pavilion and running electric service to the
band shell for performances. While doing the estimate we included utility upgrades. He asked,
would the Town want to install irrigation there?
Mr. Dunetz showed some conceptual drawings on how things might look. He stated it is a big
project and suggested construction phases over a time period in order to help finance it.
The first phase would be hazardous materials removal and demolition. The second phase would
be incorporation of some of the park elements at the upper level. The third phase would be to
bring in some more amenities with the concert area and restrooms and the final phase is the
roadwork. He had distributed the cost estimate to the Select Board. We have taken the cost
estimate and added the design contingencies, permitting and soft costs and portioned it to two to
four phases. The estimator expects a 4% increase per annum in costs due to inflation.

costs. Mr. Dunetz stated that would be a conservative cost and in some ways it is better to go
out to bid for the full design and then bid out as separate documents because everything kind
of ties together. Mr. Alonzo asked about the Portland Blues being tied into the existing septic
rather that sewer. Mr. Dunetz pointed out the sewer hookup is very close to the area where
they would be placed. We don't know the condition of the septic as of yet.
Mr. Jeffreys asked Mr. Dunetz if he saw any other areas of opportunities for changes that
would result in savings. Mr. Dunetz stated the restrooms in the alternative pre-manufactured
wooden structure would be a significant savings versus the Portland Blues. But they would be
less vandal proof. Also less lighting could be another option and the curb work and paving

pavers inside the building footprint could provide opportunity to solicit donations and donor
bricks, but we could go to concrete. Concrete would give you another impervious surface. Mr.
Jeffreys noted there was opportunity for people to donate trees and some benches as he saw a
community approach to help furnish the park. He asked about water sources throughout the
park other than the irrigation. Mr. Dunetz did not plan water sources as the plantings should
be sustainable. Grass needs irrigation but we can do drought tolerant plantings as well as
native trees. Dunetz stated it could be incorporated into the next level as there is water on the
site.

five years later you are spending money to repair of renovate something you spent less on.

2. Review of Tax Lien Sale List and Request from Open Space Committee
Ms. Lemieux reported the Select Board has a list of the tax lien sale properties in their packets
this evening. This is a public auction of tax liens used to recover delinquent property taxes. The
Tax Collector advertises the properties in the local newspaper and a company or individuals can
bid on the listed property at the auction. The bidder in turn pays off all the delinquent taxes and
late fees, interest, etc. to the Town and the property owner must pay the bidder or risk
foreclosure. The properties continue to owe taxes to the town on an annual basis going forward.
Mike Toohill, Open Space Committee Chair, reported the committee is asking for four properties
to be removed from the list as they have potential for open space for the community.
Mr. Jeffreys inquired about the tax lien process, does the Select Board have to approve the list or
is this within the Tax Collector's function? Ms. Lemieux stated the SB has the ability to request
she remove them from the auction list.
Mr. Toohill stated 356 Hollis Road, 250 Hollis Road, 56 Carr Avenue are all adjacent to each
other. They are 4.7 acres, 19 acres and 11.5 acres. 101 Pleasant Street is separate property that is
adjacent to existing town open space. Further discussion about the properties ensued.

Discussion ensued about the increased enrollment at Monty Tech.

Tech. Suggestion was made to reflect the effect of the budget on the average property tax bill for

Minutes/Warrants/Action Items
There were no minutes for approval this evening. The board signed a Payroll warrant in the
amount of $902,574.08; a Payroll deduction warrant in the amount of $200, 425.65 and an
Accounts Payable warrant in the amount of $508,183.66.
Mr. Jeffreys stated he has requested a map showing all the town-owned properties.
Mr. Alonzo noted trash pickup complaints being reported on social media and that his street's
trash was not picked. People who put out their trash are concerned that animals are getting into
it. He understood the hauler has had to deal with driver shortages, Covid-19, etc., but hopefully
they will be addressing this. Ms. Lemieux stated the office as well as DPW has been fielding
phone calls, complaints and emails. We are in constant communication with EL Harvey.
Committee Reports
Mr. Jeffreys reported the Lunenburg Municipal Design Committee met Monday and he could not
attend but watched the video. There was general discussion about how to move forward with the
expanded charge. He was appointed as Chairman and their next meeting is Monday, March 7' at
6:00 p.m. The meeting will be to brainstorm and set milestones to achieve and the steps needed
to get there. He expected to focus on those for the meetings in March and April to take a look
back on work that has already been done and review the Vertex reports and Tappe Study.
