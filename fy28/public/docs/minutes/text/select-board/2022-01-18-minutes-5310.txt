to present two conceptual plans and their cost estimates

tonight. He reported he received 36 responses to the online survey about the site.

farmer's market and recreation programs to take place, a stone outline of the school building
around the pavilion with benches located at two ends, a new walking path with benches at
various locations, a pollinator meadow and picnic area as well as an amphitheater with a band

TOWN MANAGER PRESENTATION OF CAPITAL PLAN
What Qualifies as a Capital Project?
The Town's Policy on Capital Expenditures (Financial Policy Section 3.02) defines
capital projects as items that cost $20,000 or more and have a useful life of three years or
longer.
•
The Massachusetts Association of Town Finance Committees defines capital projects as
"major, non- recurring expenditures, for one of the following purposes:
• acquisition of land for a public purpose;
• construction of a new facility or external expansion or major rehabilitation of an
existing one. Examples of such town facilities include public buildings, water and
sewer lines, roads and playing fields;
• any planning, feasibility, engineering or design study related to a capital project or
to a capital improvement program consisting of individual projects.
• equipment for public improvements when they are first constructed such as
furniture, office equipment, or playground equipment;
• major equipment which is expensive and has a relatively long life such as a fire
apparatus, garbage trucks, and construction equipment.
Capital Planning Process
• The Capital Planning Committee (CPC) creates a Capital Plan that consists of all capital
requests submitted by each town department for the next five fiscal years.

Mr. Jeffreys thanked the Capital Planning Committee and Town Manager for all the work that
has occurred up to this point. He asked how the projects were selected.
Mr. Alonzo, Capital Planning Committee member, stated the urgency of the need and the
immediate effect it would have on the town and residents and/or buildings. In the case of the
TCP Building and the hazardous waste removal, it was a low score because we don't know
what's going to happen to that building at this point, there were other things that were more
urgent to prioritize. We tried to strike a balance across departments, projects and buildings. The
logistics of replacing people while we are remediating a building is tricky and was not
incorporated into the cost, so we really did not have an accurate cost of what that would be.
Mr. Jeffreys to Ms. Lemieux noted the total comes to $1.7 million but he thought we had about
$2.3 million and certified free cash. Ms. Lemieux replied we do have $2.6 million but we also
use that for the OPEB and Regular Stabilization accounts, as well as any possible money warrant
articles for town meeting.
Mr. Jeffreys asked is it your expectation that the 30 School Street project would be funded by a
combination of free cash or debt exclusion? Ms. Lemieux stated she did not know until she
knows the actual cost. He asked if rubberized undercoatings for DPW trucks is something we can
look into? Ms. Lemieux stated she would, but she did know they wash the undercarriages of the
trucks to eliminate salt and possible corrosion. Mr. Jeffreys believed the rubberized
undercoatings were more effective to prevent corrosion.
Mr. Dwyer asked why two dump trucks had a difference of $800 and $830 in quotes. Ms.
Lemieux stated they were independent quotes.
