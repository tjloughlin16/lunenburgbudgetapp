PUBLIC COMMENT: 
Ms. Adams attended the public hearing for the notice of the extension of the Lunenburg Water 
District. The article failed by a vote of 28-11. Many residents of Howard Street attended the 
meeting and were not interested in water being brought down the street. However the developer 
still wants to pursue water being brought down three miles of newly paved roads. She believed it 
was time to bring up the pavement moratorium as they continue to have the request. 
She attended the Community Outreach meeting via ZOOM by Green Leaf Botanicals last week 
for the proposed adult marijuana retail business and the attendance was very low. She also heard 
the Land Use Director state the Community Host Agreement was being reviewed but it was not 
on the Select Board agenda.  
Ms. Lemieux noted the agreement had been reviewed at a recent Select Board meeting and the draft 
has now been sent to Town Counsel. Upon consulting with Chairman Alonzo the next meeting with 
Green Leaf was to be held after the community outreach meeting. She agreed the lack of attendance 
and accessibility by the public for that meeting was poor. She spoke with Green Leaf representatives 
Mary Bentley and Attorney Poholek about holding another Community Outreach Meeting on March 
10th but this time allowing them to the Select Board Zoom account to run the meeting, which will 
ensure that it is recorded, and advertising the toll free number for our account to the public.  This 
will be re-advertised and re-noticed with the Zoom meeting information, the toll free number, and 
the email that was on the first notice if anyone wishes to submit questions, comments, or 
concerns.  Hopefully this will increase accessibility and attendance.  
 
ANNOUNCEMENTS:

plan on holding virtual public service change meetings on February 17 and February 24; from 6-
8 p.m.  We will put this on our website and circulate it through town wide email and send it to 
the Lunenburg Ledger.

Insurance Estimates 
• Liability and Worker’s Compensation include a 4% estimated increase. Actual renewals are

• Health Insurance Renewal estimate is 3% rate increase for active employees and 5% rate

• Post 65 Program: This past fall we participated in a program to move a small number of 
employees that were not eligible to receive free Medicare Part A as they did not contribute to 
Social Security. By participating it reduced our active renewal rate by 2%, reduces our total

Lunenburg residents that are ages 75 and older can get on the waiting list being administered by the 
Eagle House Senior Community Center by calling 978-582-4166. 
The State also activated a vaccination call center last Friday and persons75 and older can now call 
2-1-1 to speak with someone at the call center to set up an appointment. The call center hours are 
Monday thru Friday 8:30 am - 5:00 pm. The State has reported that they have booked 2,000 
appointments so far and are looking at expanding hours of the call center.

Regional Discussion on Vaccination Rollout Plan: 
The Town Administrators and Town Managers within the Nashoba Associated Boards of 
Health district held a follow-up meeting today to discuss how we can work together to create a 
regional site and bring more vaccines to our area. Jim Garreffi of the Nashoba Associated

call. Jim Garreffi provided information on the vaccination clinic that was done for First 
Responders in Boxborough, the clinics for 75 and older residents that were run last week, this 
week, and in the coming weeks. The consensus was that Devens is a prime location for a large 
regional site that is centrally located and that if al large number of doses can be shipped to our 
area, smaller clinics in different communities could be set up. 
Representative Harrington has taken the lead to address the lack of vaccines for our area with 
the DPH and the Administration and has been working with Masy BioServices of Pepperell 
about their site being a possible storage location for a large amount of vaccines to be stored. 
This business has 420 freezers that can store the Pfizer vaccine and 450 freezers that can store 
the Moderna vaccine. Jim Garreffi is going to meet with the owner of Masy BioServices and the 
legislators, through Representative Harrington, will support advocating for a mass vaccination 
site at Devens and an increase in vaccine allotment to this area. The Town Administrators/Town 
Managers of the Nashoba Valley will write a letter to the Governor, Lt. Governor, and Secretary

Vaccination Rollout: 
There are currently 125 vaccination sites in Massachusetts, and the Administration expects this 
will ramp up to 165 sites by mid-February. Over 120,000 appointments will be made available 
over the course of this week. It is expected to take several weeks for residents to secure 
appointments due to limited supply of vaccines from the federal government. Residents can visit 
www.mass.gov/COVIDvaccine to check when they are eligible to get the vaccine, find an 
appointment at a location near them, and get ready for their appointment. 
The Administration restated that there are more requests for vaccines than the State has in 
available doses. A greater percentage is being allotted to the mass vaccination sites with the 
rationale that mass vaccination sites are more efficient and from a logistical standpoint (staff, 
capacity, etc.) have the largest impact. 
 
Executive Order on Capacity Limits 
The Administration also announced that effective Monday, February 8th at 5:00 AM, 
businesses can operate at 40% capacity. This is an increase from an existing order limiting 
capacity to 25% for many businesses. 
The businesses include: 
 Arcades and Recreational Businesses 
 Driving and Flight Schools 
 Gyms/Health Clubs 
 Libraries 
 Museums 
 Retail 
 Offices 
 Places of Worship 
 Lodging (common areas) 
 Golf (indoor areas) 
 Movie Theaters (no more than 50 people per theater) 
 
Additionally, the Administration announced that current restrictions limiting gathering sizes to 
10 persons indoors and 25 persons outdoors would remain in place for the present time. All 
other orders and safety guidance remain in place throughout the Commonwealth as the state 
continues to fight COVID-19 and vaccination ramps up in all regions.
