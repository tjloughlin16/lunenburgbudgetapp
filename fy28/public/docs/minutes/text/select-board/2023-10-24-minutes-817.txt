Heather Lemieux: Last week’s presentation regarding the staffing shortages covered staffing needs by 
department, action the town is taking and next steps. Heather wanted to make a correction to the 
estimated hours for the seasonal cemetery employees – FY23 was 805 hours for the year or roughly 15.5 
hours a week. The hours presented did not account for holidays, vacation, personal, or sick time for any 
benefit eligible positions. Heather reviewed with the DPW Director and Cemetery Superintendent the 
total hours and how that time was split for the seasonal employees and from October 2022 to October 
2023 they worked a total of 2,431 hours with an estimated 500 to 560 hours spent on mowing. Heather 
met with DPW employees and concerns centered around wages, lack of equipment, lack of understanding 
of the department and the majority of employees need to work a second job or obtain government 
assistance. Our Highway crew consists of (2) Heavy equipment operators and the highway 
superintendent. The general consensus is that residents and administration do not understand or appreciate 
the range of responsibility of the highway department. They handle flooding, snow removal, stormwater 
issues, clearing roadside vegetation, remove trees, and help with repairing culverts. They assist other 
DPW divisions of sewer and cemetery, school, parks, facilities department. Heather requests the Board 
use their voice to support the employees and better understand the roles and responsibilities of its staff.  
Paula Bertram: (Business Manager of Sewer Department) Thanked Heather for coming to DPW to meet 
with employees. Paula mentioned morale is low, employees are being asked to do more with less, and 
responsibility increased but salary has not. Years ago the Town had 10 highway employees and now the 
department is down to three not including the superintendent. The town needs to invest in basic tools 
needed to get the jobs done more efficiently such as compressors, cement mixers etc. so they can 
streamline the workflow. Comments made by the Chair last week are why morale is low. When discussing 
mowing at the cemeteries at last week’s meeting they questioned why they were paying for 3,900 hours of 
time when only 600 hours of work is being done – as Heather articulated, the hours were incorrect. The 
cemetery superintendent is responsible for much more than mowing including assisting the DPW, meeting 
with grieving families, funeral directors, preparing graves, oversees completing burials and coordinating 
the installation of monuments and maintaining the cemetery grounds. The personnel and HR Board are 
encouraging employees to work with their supervisors if they don’t agree with the classification and job 
descriptions that’s are being presented and I’m glad to see there was movement on that topic. Paula 
continued to address wages, job descriptions, classification, budgets and workload to encourage the Select 
Board to work with Commission members and supervisors to address these needs.

2. Review Select Board Bylaws (FY24 Select Board Goal #3): Review Chapter 305: The 
discussion continues on whether to follow the current remote policy or revert to an earlier 
one. The Consensus is to observe the state’s stance on the matter and potentially align with it 
since it might be broader and more comprehensive than the town’s existing policy. Some 
members express the need for a blend between the old and new policies. A point is raised that 
the current policy allows flexibility for those who are sick or on business travel but not for 
those who simply prefer to stay home. There's a recognition of the benefits of in-person 
meetings for certain interactions and dynamics. Remote work is convenient, it doesn’t always 
capture the same level of engagement and energy as in-person meetings. Concerns about 
potential distractions and technical issues during remote meetings are also mentioned. It's 
emphasized that remote participation can make it easier for people to volunteer for town 
boards, especially those with challenging schedules. The aim is to remove barriers and 
increase participation. The in-person quorum requirement is discussed, with some members 
supporting it while others find it restrictive. The need for the chair to be physically present is 
questioned, but it is clarified that the chair can authorize someone else to chair the meeting if 
they are remote. Town Manager requested more time to review and consult with Town 
Counsel. Board agreed to revisit the review at the December meeting.
