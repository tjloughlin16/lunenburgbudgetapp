Ms. Brochu estimated Free Cash available will be $2,600,075.00 which is an increase of
$871,000 for the close out of fiscal year 2021. She noted that the closeout for fiscal year 2020
was $1,242,000 compared to fiscal year 2021 of $1,249,000. One of the things we did exceed
was our estimates on revenues. When Covid-19 originally happened, dates for penalties and
interest fees for motor vehicle excise and tax bills were pushed out farther. So a lot of that
revenue was received in fiscal year 21 versus fiscal year 20. When asked how the CARES Act
had influenced any of these numbers, Ms. Brochu stated the Town budgets would have had to
bear expenses of about $300,000 - $400,000 if the CARES Act did not reimburse the Town. We
would have seen a much lower appropriation closeout. We are still waiting for reimbursement
from FEMA for the federal reimbursements.

Meeting with Town Counsel re: 40B Comment Letter in response to Site Approval
Application from Pond View Commons, LLC (Kevin O'Brien)
Ms. Lemieux reported the Town needs to submit a public comment letter to MassHousing
regarding the 40B housing project proposed for Howard Street by September 2nd. We have
received 21 resident comments, a letter from the Planning Board, two letters from Hickory Hills
landowners, two reports from the Police Chief, a letter from the Water District and a letter from
the Sewer Commission.
Attorney Costa will review the components of our comment letter. Mr. Costa noted he provided
a 40B workshop recently. The process starts with the application to MassHousing. The Town
needs to formally compile the public comment letters. Ms. Lemieux and I will provide cover
letters.
When MassHousing receives those comments it will consider them in light of the application
that is before them and will make a decision on whether or not to issue a project eligibility
letter. This is a preliminary letter and doesn't bind the Town to issue a comprehensive permit.
It also doesn't bind the subsidizing agency to issue final approval if a comprehensive permit is
issued. The scope is very limited at this time. They look to see if the applicant is a qualified
entity; they look at the nature of the project to determine if there is sufficient land area; if there
are critical concerns that might prevent their endorsement. He stated in 9 out of 10 instances a
preliminary letter is issued. It could be 30-60 days before a letter is issued to the applicant and
the Town will receive a copy. The applicant will then have to compile its application in more
detail and apply to the Zoning Board of Appeals.
Ms. Lemieux noted the public comment letter will be formatted categorically such as traffic
safety, pedestrian safety, accessibility, project size, access and egress from the site, etc.
Mr. Jeffreys asked if MassHousing does not provide the subsidized funding for the project, can
the applicant go to another subsidizing agency. Attorney Costa stated they could
