Board of Selectmen present: Jamie Toale, Chairman; Damon McQuaid, Vice Chairman;
Phyllis Luck, Clerk; Katey Adams, Member
The meeting opened at 7:00 PM with the Pledge of Allegiance.
Mr. Toale announced in accordance with the requirements of the Open Meeting Law, please be
advised that this meeting is being recorded and broadcast over the Lunenburg Public Access
channel.
PUBLIC COMMENT:
Ms. Luck stated the Board of Selectmen has chosen to avoid any meaningful discussion on the
hiring of an Asst. Town Manager/Human Resourse Director. She expressed concern with
creation of a $60,000 per year, part-time managment position with full benefits which the Town
Manager stated to the Finance Committee won't save any money, but will create greater
efficiencies. Ms. Luck believed a planning process should be in play before undertaking a new
hire. She read the list of suggested responsibilities of the new position and asked how many
employees are recruited, oriented, hired and fired annually and how many employees leave the
town and seek employment elsewhere. In addition, a part-time Parks Director position was added
to the budget, to administer programming needs of the Parks Commission and to appoint
candidates. She advocated for an earnest discussion of the proposed positions.
Ms. Adams stated as we have been questioning the budget and staff positions, we have been
criticized for questioning the Town Manager. She looked as asking questions as leading to
thoughtfulness and creating solutions and ideas. Is this (the Asst. Town Manager position) where
we want to put that money?
Mr. Toale responded to the two public comments. What is questionable is the role of the Board of
Selectmen in this process. We clearly have a responsibility for the budget as a total and I believe
it is our right and obligation to question the budget at a macro level. When we choose to get into
details and try to assume what gets done within the operation of the town management, we tend
to make generalizations. He stated he was in the HR field for 33 years and it is consistently
under-appreciated in terms of the amount of work and responsibility on efficient operation. One
of the reasons we don't see more turnover is because we do have a relatively well-run town;
because the Town Manager is doing a lot of HR work. Our senior managers are doing a lot of
HR work, which is the ideal. But the fact that a professional HR person is to be part of that
process is the decision of the organization and is part of a $30+ million budget and when we
question down to that level of detail, we are not fulfilling our role as a policy-making board.
Dave Passios, 56 Whiting Street, felt like the Board is kicking the can down the road and will be
known as the Board that kicked many cans down the road because, "we are not quite sure, or we
don't have enough information." Make a decision. Let your Town Manager put forward a budget

Meeting with Chairman of Senior Property Taxation Work Off Program Committee-
Recommendation to Amend Senior Work Off Program Regulations
Bob Hamel, 10 Pleasantview Ave., stated the committee met on March 27" and Ms. Luck and
Ms. Lemieux were present. We discussed making changes to the program. We would like to
change the hourly rate from $10.00 to $12.00. We would like to increase the annual abatement
from $1000 to $1500. In addition, we would like to increase the maximum gross income allowed
to qualify for the program to $50,000 for a single household and $75,000 for a two person
household. We went before the Board of Assessors and they had no problem with that, they had
enough money to cover 12 applicants. Right now there are only six people working for the town.
Motion: K. Adams
2nd: P. Luck
To approve the proposed changes to the Senior Property Taxation Work-Off Program as
presented
Vote: All in Favor

whom would be by proxy a Planning Board member; one Conservation Commission member,
one Finance Committee member, and one member of the Board of Selectmen. Their charge is to
explore the possibility of that particular land acquistion that would follow the model as the Lane

I'm once again working with the American Cancer Society to help coordinate the annual Bicycles Battling Cancer ride which
takes place this year on Sunday, June 9, 2019 and I'm writing to ask for your approval for this charity ride to come
through Lunenburg. The selectmen and chief have approved the ride in the past and we would appreciate your continued
Bicycles Battling Cancer is an outdoor cycling fundraiser that offers 30, 50, 70, and 100 mile route options to cyclists. The ride
starts and ends in Marlborough, MA. This is not a race and cyclists will be told to obey the rules of the road and ride single file.
The ride utilizes about 2 miles of Lunenburg roads which are miles 16-18 of the 50, 70 and 100 mile rides (the first 25 miles of
these routes are the same; the 30 mile ride does not enter Lunenburg). We are expecting about 200-275 cyclists in these rides
and I expect they will be coming through town 8:00am-9:15am. You can see the complete 100 mile route
here: https://ridewithgps.com/routes/26476575. For convenience I have attached a map that shows just the
Lunenburg roads utilized for the event.
Our plan is to mark the course a few days before the event with paper arrow stickers and/or spray chalk. These course markings
will wash away in about a week and are essential to effectively and safely guide riders along the course.
Please let me know if you approve of the ride and if you require any additional information. Historically, I have been asked to
attend the Selectmen meeting and I am happy to do so again if required, but if you're comfortable enough with the event and
don't see the need for me to come I won't be offended! ®
Thank you in advance for your support.
