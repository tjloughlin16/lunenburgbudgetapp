A public hearing on a Notice of Intent by SafStor Real Estate Co.,LLC for a project that involves floodplain fill
and associated compensatory floodplain compensation.
Greg Roy (Dillis & Roy) represented the applicant and presented the project. Site historically used as an auto
dealership. Paved acreage is .995 acres (of 1.15 acres). The only concern is bordering land subject to flooding (as
defined by the Wetlands Protection Act) based on the newly published 2025 FEMA flood maps.
The proposal is to convert the existing use to a proposed 4-story climate-controlled self-storage facility. All units
will be accessed internally. The remaining property will be green space and flood compensation storage, reducing
impervious area by about 14%. Special Planning Board permits will be required, and it is expected the
stormwater calculations will be peer-reviewed by the Planning Board. This review may be of interest to the
Conservation Commission.
G. Roy reviewed the responses they will file to DEP questions received. Since the impervious area is being
reduced, it was requested that the Commission find it not necessary to conduct a Wildlife Habitat evaluation.
T. Doucette wanted to know if the trees behind the property are coming down. G. Roy indicated some will come
down, and he will identify which in further meetings. R. Bursch wanted to know how far it was from the edge of
work to the 100 ft boundary. G. Roy will return with that in formation. M. Wilson did a satellite image check and
calculated 500 feet. R. Bursch also asked for the 100-foot flood plain elevation. G. Roy responded "366". C. St
Germain wondered why G. Roy suggested the flood plain calculations went too far. G. Roy explained it was
based on the FEMA numbers versus the converted numbers to 1988 values. J. Pewtherer wondered if this area
was affected by the flooding in July and September of 2023. G. Roy did not know.

b. 247 Fire Rd 16 - RDA-25-16
A public hearing on a Request for Determination of Applicability by Robert Hart (Trustee) for a project that
involves drilling a water well 56 feet 6 inches away from the edge of Lake Shirley. Digging necessary water and
retention pits. Installing necessary silt fence and hay bales.
Robert Hart presented the project. The existing dwelling uses pond water for domestic water. Proposing a well
for reliable source of water. H. Jaime conducted a site visit. A permit was obtained from the Board of Health.
Need Conservation permission because it is within the buffer zone. J. Pewtherer shared uploaded
documentation. H. Jaime recommendations:
1. Install sediment controls
2. Silt fence, hay bales prior to construction and maintained until full stabilization.
3. Retention pits must be contained within upland areas
4. No slurry or drilling water can be discharged toward Lake Shirley.
5. Stockpiles must be covered and located outside of the 50-foot buffer.
6. Disturbed areas shall be loamed, seeded, or loamed seeded and stabilized immediately following
completion.
7. Pre-construction erosion control inspection is required.
8. Any modification to the location of the well must be approved prior to work.
9. All equipment, fueling, and maintenance must occur outside of the 100-foot buffer.
