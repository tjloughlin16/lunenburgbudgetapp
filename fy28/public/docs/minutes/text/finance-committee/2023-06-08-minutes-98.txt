Brian Lehtinen introduced himself as the new liaison from the School Committee to the 
Finance Committee. 
Rhonda Lizzio, 181 Reservoir Rd., said she recalled Mr. Jefferies mention there was 
heat damage occurring at the Primary School due to it being designed to retain heat and 
that she recalled problems with the fire alarms in that building a few years ago and that 
some investment was made to either repair or replace that she believed was also due to 
excessive humidity. She wanted to know if the project being put forward addresses those 
issues and if not, who is protecting the tax payers investment. 
4. Town Manager / Finance Director Report – no update 
5. New Business 
a.Primary School air conditioning project  
Dr. Burnham referenced the letter provided to the Finance Committee saying it outlines 
the proposed approach to accomplishing, at least to some degree, the original town 

===PAGE 2===

meeting vote approving the AC project. She said when they did more exploration 
following that town meeting a year ago that the engineers gave cost estimates that were 
in the millions and not the hundreds of thousands so they had to reassess to work within 
the available funding to accomplish the goal of air conditioning in the building. The 
proposal outlined is to use mini split ductless systems to provide some air conditioning 
into the classrooms. Dr. Burnham said that in response to Selectboard Chair Jefferies 
suggestion that an electrical engineer be consulted to ensure that the current electrical 
system would support this modified plan that she and Mr. Cassidy consulted with the 
engineer they used last summer and that the electrical service to this building is more 
than sufficient to support this plan. 
Mr. Cassidy said their goal is to get consensus of the boards that are stakeholders in this 
project and support that they are maintaining the intent of the original submission. He 
said there was concern that these were potentially residential units but that the did meet 
with the vendor today and it was described to him that there is not a lot of differentiation 
between commercial and residential in these units. 
Mr. Gray asked what the average life was using it the way we plan to use it. Mr. Cassidy 
said the average lifespan if they are well maintained is probably 20-25 years. 
Mr. Menard asked if that was in the warranty from the vendor as he doesn’t know of 
anything that would last 20-25 years anymore. Mr. Cassidy said he didn’t know the 
warranty but that he can reach out to the vendor. Mr. Watters added that he believes it 
would just be the condensers that would need to be replaced within that time frame and 
not the whole system. 
Mr. Gray asked if the current heating system was still in place and how the two systems  
would interact. Dr. Burnham said there was no impact and that she didn’t intend to use 
these units for heating. She said the intention is to use these units for reduction in 
humidity and alleviating temperatures in the building in warmer months. 
Mr. Menard asked Dr. Burnham and Mr. Cassidy to address the public comment earlier 
in the evening regarding heat damage occurring in the building. Dr. Burnham said that 
had been addressed previously and that Mr. Jefferies had toured the building and he 
noted that there were areas that showed signs of heat stress. 
Mr. Menard said that when he read the letter it said that the intent of this project remains 
the same – managing classroom air comfort – and wanted to know if the purpose was 
only for air comfort as stated or as asked earlier by a resident to manage the heat so that 
we don’t get heat damage. Dr. Burnham said the primary focus is to make sure they have 
classrooms that are a comfortable environment for teaching and learning and that the 
===PAGE 3===

added benefit is that by air conditioning those spaces you are reducing some of the heat 
stress. 
Mr. Gray asked if these units would be run after the school year ends during the summer 
to keep the heat down and the damage down. Dr. Burnham said yes that they would 
monitor and not allow the temperature inside to go above 78 degrees so would be 
looking to utilize those units even when the building wasn’t occupied. 
Mr. Watters asked Dr. Burnham to also address the public comment about fire alarms 
being damaged potentially from heat or humidity. Dr. Burnham that she wasn’t aware 
that any of the challenges they were experiencing with the fire alarms a few years ago 
was due to uncontrolled humidity but that the repairs have been made and they have had 
no issues since. 
Mr. Menard asked Mr. Passios to make a comment as he is the Finance Committee 
representative to Capital Planning. Mr. Passios (56 Whiting Street) said this project has 
been in the pipeline for upwards of 4 years. There were two iterations of it and questions 
started to come up as to how well vetted the project was initially. He said he is ok with 
what the school department has come up with as a plan. He said Capital Planning was 
approached for this iteration but they didn’t think they needed to reconvene as it did 
accomplish the original intent of the project. 
Mr. Lehtinen was asked if there was anything he wanted to add as the appointment 
member of the Capital Planning committee. Mr. Lehtinen said that the agreed with Mr. 
Passios that this was a climate control project. 
Mr. Menard asked what a vote means – if they voted yes, would it move forward, if they 
voted no would it not move forward. Dr. Burnham said that in talking with the town 
manager that because this is a fairly unique circumstance that her concern was that there 
be a consensus from the major boards in town to agree that this project falls within the 
original intent of the project proposed and approved at Town Meeting. 
Mr. Menard said it was his opinion this this project does not fall within the four walls of 
the original project which was for air conditioning in the entire school not for 2/3 of the 
school for climate control. He said that the question of whether or not we think this is 
the right approach given the fact that there was money allocated is ultimately a decision 
for the town to make at Town Meeting.  Mr. Menard questioned what they are being 
asked to do with the information presented. 
Mr. Cassidy responded that they are looking for support from the board to move forward 
with the project.

Mr. Menard asked if they were lined up to have the project ready to go this summer. Mr. 
Cassidy said yes, they could. 
Mr. Passios said he has the same concerns as Mr. Menard in regards to what you are 
supporting here and based on his knowledge thinks this may not be the right way to go 
and it might be just a band aid fix and that holding off another year and come up with a 
comprehensive project that covers the entire original intent. 
Mr. Watters said that although it may be a band aid fix and not entirely within the 
original scope of the capital plan request it is still within the spirit of the request which is 
to bring some relief to those classrooms. 
Mr. Balish said that from a process standpoint we have a whole capital planning process 
set up to have projects vetted and that what is being proposed is different enough from 
the original project that he doesn’t feel comfortable saying it should move forward.  
Mr. Menard agreed with that sentiment and that the process isn’t being followed. He said  
we need a real plan for this building and that this plan doesn’t fall within the original 
intent. 
Mr. Gray said that the issue really goes back to when this project was first proposed and 
that not enough leg work was done. He said one of the things we need to is have a better 
handle on capital projects so these errors don’t keep happening. He said he feels this will 
serve the purpose of providing some relief for the kids and that he would support it. 
Mr. Balish motioned to support the modified plan outlined in the letter from 
Lunenburg Public Schools. Mr. Gray seconded. 
Mr. Menard asked for clarification as the letter says $238,000 but at annual town 
meeting we voted for $232,800.  
Dr. Burnham apologized for the error and that they would work within the budget 
allocated for this project. 
On vote – motion defeated – 2 aye and 2 nay.
