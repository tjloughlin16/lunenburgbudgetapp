B. FY16 budget:
i. The DPW's requested target FY16 budget is $1,506,711, a $152,000 increase or
$10.1% over the FY15 appropriation. The town manager's recommended budget for
FY16 is $1,428,970, $74,000, or 5.4% over FY 2015.
The town manager said she would look closely at the energy cost line in the
DPW budget-which may require reallocation of funding within the DPW
budget, but would not require increasing the total DPW budget.
il. Jack said that the town manager's projected budget means the DPW would be able
to provide the same levels of service in FY16 as in FY15.
C. The Pavement Management Plan:
i. The current pavement condition index [PCI] of Town roads is 70. To maintain a PCI
of 70 requires an annual investment for maintaining the 89 miles of Town roads of
$700,000.
That $700,000 figure will be financed from Town capital funds as well as
Chapter 90 Highway funds from the State.
• Note that the $700,000 per year is not indexed for inflation: It will rise as
the cost of labor and materials required to maintain the roads increases over
time.
D. Ongoing services that the DPW provides:
i. Vehicle maintenance and fuel for the Police and Fire Departments.
ii. Maintenance of Town facilities.
• This includes covering utility costs for Town buildings except the library,
which means that the DPW has had to pay for utility rate increases.
ili. Tree removal: The DPW identifies and contracts for the removal of trees that pose a
hazard to traffic on Town roadways.
iv. Infrastructure projects:
• The DPW maintains special resources -the Shirley Dam and the capped
Landfill.
• Both the dam and the landfill comply fully with state and Federal mandates.
E. Future expenditure:
i. The Town's drainage and storm water system is aging and requires
renovation/rehabilitation to comply with government specifications.
il. Jack's current estimate is that this would cost between $300,000 and $500,000.
F. Current capital projects:
i. Repair and/or replacement of two culverts.
ii. Redesigning and engineering five intersections to make them safer for traffic.
•
Paula Bertram stated that there are Federal grants the Town may be able to
get to help pay for some costs of intersection redesign and engineering.
G. Ideas for raising revenues to meet future costs and improve Town infrastructure:
i. Special levy to pay for repairs, renovations, and/or replacing the Town's aging storm
water and drainage systems. (See point 4. E. above).
ii. Working with selected neighborhoods to fund small, selective road repair or repaving
projects that might otherwise be low priority projects in the pavement management
plan. This may be most feasible in densely settled neighborhoods, like the Whalom
ii. Firing/retaining a grant writer to help apply for grants to help pay for infrastructure
improvements-e.g. intersections, roads, stormwater drainage system.

5. Monty Tech FY16 Budget Presentation by Dr. Sheila Harrity, Superintendent and Ms. Tammy Lafalam,
Administrative Assistant
A. Dr. Harrity presented a summary of the accomplishments of Monty Tech last year including
participation in the student space flight experiment program and participating in three
competitions.
B. Tammy Lafalam reviewed the Monty Tech budget for FY16 and student tuition calculation.
i. In school year 2015-16 Lunenburg will be assessed a per pupil cost of $10,275. This
is a per student reduction from school year 2014-15 of $430.
ii. This year Lunenburg's enrollment and Monty tech will increase by seven students to
78 students in total.
ili. Therefore, because of the seven student increase, the Town will pay about $70,000
more than in school year 2014-15.
Note that Lunenburg is assessed at the statutorily minimum allowed, and
that the cost per student at Monty Tech exceeds $15,000 per year.
