3. Public Comment for prior Issues 
 
Dave Rogers expressed significant concern regarding the direction of the school district, including recent 
personnel turnover, financial oversight, and elementary student reading performance. He questioned the 
circumstances surrounding the departure of the district’s former finance director and referenced past 
financial problems within the school system as a reason for his concern regarding appropriate controls over 
expenditures and purchasing. Mr. Rogers also expressed particular concern regarding student reading 
performance and questioned what immediate steps were being taken to address the issue. He characterized 
the reported reading results as unacceptable and urged the district to take aggressive action, including 
considering greater involvement by parents, grandparents, senior citizens, and other community volunteers 
to assist students with reading. 
 
In response, Dr. Fortuna and members of the Committee discussed the district’s current financial and 
academic challenges. Dr. Fortuna stated that reading performance was a concern across grades one through 
five and agreed that the scores were unacceptable. She explained that literacy was the district team’s 
primary academic focus and described measures already underway, including reading interventionists, a 
senior volunteer program targeted toward third-grade students, and comprehensive literacy professional 
development for teachers in grades K–5. She stated that the district was directing available resources 
toward academics but could not guarantee that the reading concerns would be resolved within one year.  
 
The discussion also addressed the effect of limited funding on educational programming and class sizes, 
including the prospect of 31 students in a second-grade classroom, as well as reductions to athletics, middle 
school sports, and athletic transportation as resources were directed toward academic needs.

Mr. Rogers continued to urge the district to mobilize community resources and questioned why a larger 
volunteer effort could not be organized to assist students. He referenced a previous community effort that 
had raised funds to support Lunenburg athletics as an example of residents working collectively to address 
a problem. During the discussion, Mr. Rogers also referred to historical financial misconduct within the 
district and expressed concern about ensuring that appropriate safeguards were in place regarding 
expenditures and purchasing. A Committee member objected to any implication that current school or 
athletic department personnel were engaged in financial wrongdoing. Mr. Rogers clarified that he was not 
accusing current officials or employees of misconduct and stated that his comments were intended to 
emphasize the importance of financial oversight based on the Town’s past experience. 
 
The discussion then turned specifically to the recent departure of the former finance director. Dr. Fortuna 
stated that the former finance director had resigned without notice and that the district had received an 
email notifying it of the resignation without further discussion. In response to Mr. Rogers’s concern that 
another individual had immediately been hired to replace him, Dr. Fortuna clarified that she had not hired 
anyone to replace Mr. McNamara. She explained that the individual in question had been working with the 
district to mentor Mr. McNamara and assist him in meeting the standards of his position. Dr. Fortuna stated 
that the arrangement was scheduled to be finalized on the same day that Mr. McNamara resigned. She 
further stated that the individual had not been brought in as his replacement and characterized reports to the 
contrary as rumor and conjecture rather than fact. Mr. Rogers thanked the Committee and Dr. Fortuna for 
addressing his concerns and emphasized the importance of communication with the public.

5. New Business Action 
a. Additional Funding for Education 
 
Dr. Fortuna presented three possible scenarios for the use of an additional $418,000, contingent upon 
appropriation at Special Town Meeting. She emphasized that none of the proposals would be final 
unless the funding was appropriated. 
 
Dr. Fortuna reported that the district had received a state grant for 36 Ignite tutoring seats at a cost of 
$2,500 per seat. Because students could rotate through the seats, the administration determined that the 
36 grant-funded seats would be sufficient and that additional seats would not be funded through the 
general fund or the requested appropriation. This freed approximately $129,458 within the proposed 
$418,000 spending plan.

All three scenarios included restoration of high school athletic transportation. Dr. Fortuna reported that 
the verified transportation cost was approximately $58,880, which she rounded to $60,000. Of that 
amount, $10,000 would come from the additional appropriation and $50,000 from the athletic revolving 
account. She explained that the reduced cost resulted from scheduling more competitions closer to 
Lunenburg, increasing home competitions, and using actual schedules and prior-year spring costs to 
obtain a more accurate transportation estimate. Two passenger vans expected in November were not 
anticipated to become operational until approximately the spring because additional steps, including 
bargaining and driver training, would be required. 
 
Scenario 1 focused on financial accuracy and transparency and included an estimated $119,458 for an 
independent financial audit. Dr. Fortuna described a proposed phased review of school-managed 
ledgers, payroll, receiving records, shared costs, and other financial operations. A later phase involving 
full municipal ledger access would require Town authorization. 
 
Scenario 2 focused on reducing second-grade class sizes and expanding literacy support. Dr. Fortuna 
reported that 124 fully registered second-grade students would result in four classes of approximately 31 
students. The proposal would add a second-grade teacher, reducing those classes to approximately 24–
25 students. It would also provide $15,736 for literacy professional development and support at the 
middle/high school level, in addition to the $10,000 for athletic transportation. 
 
Scenario 3 focused on behavioral stability and intervention through the addition of a Board Certified 
Behavior Analyst (BCBA). Dr. Fortuna explained that the district currently had one BCBA serving the 
entire district and discussed the role of a BCBA in behavioral assessment, intervention planning, crisis 
stabilization, and staff support. She also discussed new state requirements affecting student behavioral 
interventions and the potential relationship between in-district behavioral supports and costly out-of-
district placements. The administration reported that 19 out-of-district placements were then anticipated. 
The Committee discussed the three scenarios, including the urgency of reducing the second-grade class 
size, the ability to hire a qualified teacher after the normal hiring season, the value of restoring a second 
BCBA, and the benefits of an independent financial audit. Dr. Fortuna stated that she was approximately 
95% confident that the district could hire a qualified second-grade teacher within a reasonable period. If 
the district were unable to fill the position, the matter could return to the Committee to determine 
another spending priority. 
 
The Committee also heard a statement prepared by absent member Mr. Sculimbrene regarding the 
Special Town Meeting warrant article and school funding. The statement expressed concerns regarding 
the accounting underlying the article, advocated for additional unrestricted school funding, discussed the 
treatment of health insurance costs within the school budget, and supported a comprehensive financial 
review of school finances. 
 
Following a straw poll regarding the three proposed scenarios, Scenario 2 received the most support. 
 
Ms. Gilman moved to accept Scenario 2 as outlined, including the addition of a second-grade 
teacher, $15,736 for literacy professional development, and $10,000 toward the restoration of high 
school athletic transportation, with additional transportation funding to come from the athletic 
revolving fund. Ms. Young seconded the motion. 
The motion passed, 3-0-1, with Ms. Young abstaining.

The Committee then reviewed Article 3, a citizen petition seeking $151,338.38 from certified free cash 
or other available funds to supplement the FY27 school operating budget for athletic transportation and 
the restoration of middle school sports. Because high school athletic transportation had already been 
addressed through Scenario 2, the Committee discussed what portion of the citizen petition remained 
applicable and the estimated costs of restoring sports that could still be offered during the current school 
year. 
 
The Committee discussed an estimated $19,349 to restore middle school boys’ basketball, girls’ 
basketball and outdoor track, including coaching stipends, transportation, officials and meet entry fees. 
Cross-country and field hockey could no longer be scheduled for the current year. The Committee also 
discussed an additional estimated $26,500 to restore high school ski and girls’ lacrosse, for a combined 
estimated cost of $45,849. 
 
Members discussed potential funding sources and clarified that Town Meeting could not appropriate 
school revolving funds or circuit-breaker funds, which were school funds and, in some cases, restricted 
to specific purposes. The Committee discussed whether it would support the citizen petition if the Town 
provided new funding for the requested programs. Members acknowledged that not every sport 
identified could necessarily be restored because of scheduling, participation and other practical 
limitations, but expressed support for following the intent of the petition if additional Town funding 
were appropriated. 
 
A motion was made and seconded that the School Committee support the spirit of the citizen 
petition if the funding came from a new Town funding source rather than existing school 
operating funds and, if the funding were appropriated at Town Meeting, that the Committee use 
the funds for the purposes requested. The motion passed unanimously. 
 
Dr. Fortuna subsequently confirmed that the district planned to remain with the 36 grant-funded Ignite 
tutoring seats. She stated that athletic transportation would be restored as soon as possible following 
Town Meeting appropriation and that the private transportation form would be removed if the funding 
passed. The Committee also discussed returning a transportation donation previously accepted for the 
football team to the boosters if transportation funding were restored.
