   Staff Update 
May 19th, Melanie attended the annual MLA conference. Some valuable sessions included discussion 
of book challenges and legal frameworks, civic literacy post-election, and updates on the current 
challenges around IMLS funding and cuts. I was certified as a Mental Health First Aid responded at a 
workshop on May 20, and the skills learned should prove valuable in referring those in need to 
resources that will help them and in reducing stigma around mental health challenges. 
I met with Deb Bookis and Tara Bastien of the schools to discuss ongoing parterships and 
collaborations. It seems as if these efforts may result in resumption of 5th grade visits! We plan to 
share resources and support around STEM topics as well.  
Town Administration will be initiating “Tri-Board” (Select Board, FinComm, and School Board) 
meetings starting this month. Their charge will be to advise on a comprehensive fiscal plan for the 
town’s future. This group will ask for more information, earlier about FY27 needs. I will be presenting 
in August about the library’s anticipated needs. This puts a little more pressure on Lydia and I to 
complete our staffing study earlier than we thought we might. 
The Conroy Memorial Garden is about half planted. Around 20 people gathered on May 24, including 
Robin, Lydia, Susan Conroy, and family and friends. Kevin Boussom is working on the sculpture and 
anticipates delivery before the fall. 
North End Subaru has expressed their commitment to another year of support for the Month Music 
Series, and their intention to support us long-term. Both Library and Jason from North End are 
appreciative of the partnership. 
   Programs 
Mother’s Day Planting 2.0 had 38 attendees! Our Enchanted Plants program brought 16, despite 
competition with the rescheduled Cruise Night. Niki’s Weight Strengthening sessions has seen a dip 
in attendance. A few program cancellations happened this past month due to low registration 
numbers, like the Women in Jazz performance, Sew Simple Sessions, movie night, and video game 
night. 
You may not have realized it but our magnificent Children’s Librarian Debbie Laffond held her final 
storytime in May. Her retirement will come in the Fall, and she will not deliver the Fall storytime 
sessions.  
Coming up: Summer Reading! Registration is ongoing, events start 6/23, including free summer 
lunches! 
   Outreach 
We have been reaching out to potential partners for outreach services, including Arrive Lunenburg 
and Tri-Town Apartments, and Smokestack Coffee Roasters. We are assembling a Welcome to 
Lunenburg and the Library packet of resources.

 STORY TIME WITH LOCAL AUTHOR STEPHANIE LUCIER 
Local, beloved elementary teacher Stephanie Lucier came by to share her story based on her 
cat Binx: Lucky believes that he is the unluckiest cat in the world. He has tried every good luck 
charm he can think of and still he finds himself involved in silly mishap after mishap.  But with 
the help of his mom and positive thinking, Lucky perseveres and learns that luck is something 
you get to make for yourself! 
Ms. Lucier teaches at the Lunenburg Primary School and has a passion for writing. She 
currently resides in Massachusetts, with her family and her own little "Lucky" named Binx. The 
children were very excited to have their teacher as a guest reader and really enjoyed the story 
and accompanying craft.

Summary of Last Month’s Programs & Events 
➢ On Saturday May 1st we had 12 people join us for the final session of the music clinic series. Due to 
their success, we will negotiate continuing the series in the fall of this year.  
➢ We hosted a fabulous Floral Design Workshop on Tuesday, May 13th. There were 15 people in 
attendance, and it was a very engaging group that made the workshop a huge success! 
➢ The annual Spring Puzzle Swap that took place on Saturday, May 17th was another successful swap 
event. We had 52 people visit to exchange 472 tickets for selections from the 656 donated puzzles. 
➢ Wednesday May 21st we postponed our Monthly Music Series program with jazz flutist Galen Abdur-
Razzaq on Zoom for a tribute to women in jazz performance. We will most likely reschedule in late 
winter or early spring when virtual programs may be more appealing. 
➢ On Friday May 23rd, we hosted an opening reception for the art exhibit: Lunenburg High School AP & 
Honors Art Thesis Show. That was very well attended, with an estimated 58 people visiting the 
reception. The exhibit ran May 17th-31st and is something we will continue to do with the school in the 
coming years. 
➢ The library sponsored author Varla Ventura for a unique talk on botanical folklore based on 
information from her book Enchanted Plants on Wednesday May 28th. There were 16 people at this 
informative and engaging talk, despite the draw of Cruise Night happening concurrently outside.  
➢ On Thursday May 29th the folk-rock duo Creeque Alley returned to perform. One of our most popular, 
well-received, and enthusiastic performers, we were very excited to welcome them back! There were 
25 people enjoying the music and singing along that evening. 
This Month’s Programs & Events 
➢  Local Lunenburg resident and personal crisis coach Pamela Dussault visited on June 4th for a talk on 
managing caregiver burnout. There were 11 people here for the program and it was very well 
received, with calls for more support groups and workshops to be held at the library in the future. 
➢ On Thursday June 12th we will host a return visit with Mark & Raianne for a folk duo concert as part of 
the Monthly Music Series. 
➢ There will be a summer puzzle race on June 17th. 
➢ For a creative workshop, the library will be holding a fused glass pendant making class on June 18th. 
➢ And as part of summer reading, on June 25th there will be a “bad art” night for patrons of all ages to 
come to the library and make creative artwork using odds and ends pieces from the craft supply. It 
should be a fun event to mark the beginning of summer!

Recurring Program Update 
➢ Book clubs are doing well with excellent discussions at our monthly meetings. 
o The 100 Books Bucket List book club meets every first Tuesday morning. We average 9 
attendees at each meeting.  
o The Brighter Days book club met every second Wednesday evening. It is being rebranded 
as Between the Bookends book club, where we will read through suggestions from our 
regular readers instead of working on a set theme for the year. There are now 10 
consistent members of that discussion group.  
➢ Stitchcraft knitting and crochet group attendance remains steady at 5-6 per week.  
➢ The monthly Board Game Brunch programs have been moderately successful. There were no 
participants in April, but 3 new people for May’s meeting. 
➢ Monthly Movie Nights have been dragging with the weather change for spring. The May program had 
no one attend. We may take a break for the summertime and bring it back for the cooler weather. 
➢ The Tuesday Tune-Up Sessions with Niki Holtzman started strong but have already tapered off. 
These are bi-weekly mini exercise classes for former attendees and new patrons. This is a 
compromise between the weekly meetings we usually do for a six-week series in fall and winter, and 
the break we normally take in the busy summer months. We had 11 people in our first class on 
Tuesday May 6th, canceled the May 20th session, and only had 3 at our June 3rd meeting. 
Virtual Program Update 
➢ There are no virtual programs booked in May or June, but there are several on deck for July and
