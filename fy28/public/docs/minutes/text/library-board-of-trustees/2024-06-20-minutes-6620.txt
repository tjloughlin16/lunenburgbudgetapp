The Children’s Room has been so busy. It seems like most afternoons when I go in, there are around 
a dozen people at least. Lots of new faces, many families, including lots of dads!  
I participated in the annual meeting of the Massachusetts Library Aid Association to discuss the 
annual call for applications. This year will the be last opportunity for the ~$10,000 scholarship, 
though the grants for $500-1,000 for MLIS coursework or library-wide staff professional 
development will still be available. 
The Town Manager has resigned effective Aug 4, so we will be transitioning to an interim TM, and 
eventually to a new TM. 
Lydia and I have been working on transitioning to compliance with the new Revenue Turnover Policy 
for the town. The library’s revenue amounts are small compared to other departments, so we have 
been working with the Finance Dept to ensure the impact is not onerous on library staff. We are 
required now, though, to turnover revenues each week, and create receipts for each transaction 
performed. 
   Programs 
Concerts continue to draw significant crowds, a welcome shift from the cooler months. Sons of 
Blarney brought in 50+ last week, and 48 for Abe Ovadio’s jazz guitar a couple weeks before that. 18 
prospective beekeepers and more 50 people came to our tie die program. I anticipate Mike 
Familant’s Bigfoot program Tuesday this week will be entertaining. Kaitee has successfully launched 
a Teen Advisory Board with about half a dozen teens volunteering to help. Her gardening and craft 
programs continue to offer excellent experiences, though attendance remains low. Video game 
night, however continues to be a very successful program with attendance. 
As always the team in the Children’s Room has put together an absolute blast of an event calendar. It 
amazes me how many offerings they have organized for our SRP participants. The Poo Museum, 
Rocket building, The Science of Magic, a Safari on the Serengeti and so many other entertaining and 
educational programs! 
   Building & Maintenance 
I am awaiting a design for the EV charger project, anticipated sometime this summer. As of Monday, 
we are waiting for the HVAC component of the teen room reno estimate, a small piece, but 
apparently has been hard to get anyone to assist. 
Some repairs to our concrete walkways have been done near the staff entry, and more should be 
done near the handicap parking space. Tile in the women’s restroom is scheduled for Wednesday of 
this week, when the library is closed. Our hose faucet is being repaired on the Memorial Dr. side of 
the building.

HIGHLIGHTS FOR MAY: 
• Mother’s Day Planting with Robin- The kids and families returned to repot the seedlings that 
they had planted a month ago. Thanks to support from Robin, we had a very successful 
sprouting using seed trays, a warming blanket and grow lights- all which can be used again for 
future plantings.  The kids did a great job decorating their pots to make a wonderful, home-
grown gift! 
• STEM PROGRAMS- We hosted 2 S T E M programs with The Ecotarium this month and 1 
program with Food Explorers.  The kids learned how to make Three Cheese Pizza Bites and 
Fruit Cobbler using various cooking techniques.  The Ecotarium presented 2 programs: 
Mission: Earth and Space where they got to “uncover the mysteries of the grandest science 
of them all through hands-on experiments and demonstrations!  They also got to use a 
telescope to check out some nearby topography.  The 2nd program, Turtle Travels, explored 
how turtles survive in different habitats and how their behavior changes when humans move 
in.  They also got to meet a live turtle, handle real turtle biofacts, and gather information 
about turtle adaptations and life cycles.  
• Music and Movement class with Monument Music School- We had our last class with 
this great series.  The kids really enjoyed the music and movement activities that were 
provided each month- hopefully we can bring them back next year!  
• Weekly Story times for toddlers and preschoolers ended in May.  We had a great 
year!  Many new crafts, stories and music activities were introduced and, hopefully, we 
started the kids off to a life-long love of reading and their library! 
• Cruise Night- The Lunenburg P T O sponsored a Classic Car event which took place on a 
beautiful evening in May. Many different organizations in town took advantage of this great 
opportunity to entertain the kids, recruit new members and provide yummy snacks!  We had 
a table, and a bubble machine, set up which drew a lot of attention!  We registered 60+ kids 
for summer reading, introduced the Summer Reading Program for 2024 and had a chance to 
say hello to many of our library friends.

Trustee Report 
Adult Services Department 
June 2024 
 
Summer Reading 2024 will be kicking off on Sunday, June 23rd on Beanstack! We have several gift 
cards from local businesses that were either donated or purchased at face value as our prize giveaways 
this year. These include Cherry Hill ice cream, Lanni Orchards, Roots Natural Foods, The Bull Run, and 
Dick’s Market Garden. Our goal is to get some better engagement in the program before it ends on 
August 3rd, but hopefully it will be a fun summer activity for adults to join and win some great gift cards! 
 
A few outstanding programs we hosted in late May and June include: 
➢ Jazz guitarist Abe Ovadia performed at the library on Friday May 24th as part of the monthly music 
series. Due to the heat and discomfort in the community room, we moved the performance to the 
main hall of the building. This was our first attempt at hosting a concert in this space in the library, 
and I think it was a great success! We had 48 people join us for the program. 
➢ Leominster Credit Union gave a presentation on mortgage basics on Wednesday May 29th at 7:00 
pm. We had 3 attendees, but the information was fantastic, and the presenters were very 
knowledgeable on every aspect of the financial process for homeownership. 
➢ At the request of a patron, we started a biweekly “Adult Community Chat & Color” on Thursday 
mornings in June. The first session was on June 6th at 10:30 am in the conference room. We only 
had the patron who requested the program attend, but we have some creative ideas on how to grow 
membership over time.  
➢ Beekeeper John Hedly visited on Tuesday June 11th to give an in-depth talk on beekeeping for 
beginners. 18 prospective beekeepers joined us for his informative presentation that evening. 
➢ Debbie and I ran a family tie-dye event on Thursday June 13th with two sessions between 5 and 7 
pm. There were over 50 people in attendance, ranging from seniors to toddlers, making a variety of 
tie-dyed shirts with every color under the rainbow. 
➢ An Irish music concert took place on Friday June 14th with “Sons of Blarney”, fronted by the Lurias. 
Originally scheduled as an outdoor event, we moved indoors to the main hall again for another 
successful performance. We had 54 people join us for this concert. 
Looking ahead, here are the upcoming June and July events at the library: 
➢ Mike Familant, a bigfoot hunter and expeditionist, is coming on Tuesday June 18th in the evening to 
talk about the evidence he’s found across the United States.  
➢ As part of summer reading, we booked Joy Marzolf to talk about “On Safari in Tanzania: Wildlife of 
Serengeti and Ngorongoro Crater” on Monday June 24th 
➢ Blue Cross Blue Shield will give a presentation about Medicare on Wednesday June 26th 
➢ Summer Reading Mystery Travel Kit registration deadline is June 30th  
➢ The Community Supported Meditation: Silent Sessions will start biweekly on Tuesday July 2nd  
➢ There will be a summer puzzle race for teams of four people on Tuesday July 9th  
➢ A new book club will be starting on Wednesday July 10th at 6:30 pm for readers looking for happy, 
uplifting, or inspiring stories
