   FY 25 Budget 
 
   Staff 
Best SRP ever! This summer was incredibly successful at the library. Between the hot weather, hot 
meals, and our wonderful library staff, participation was at pre-pandemic levels, and we served more 
than 650 lunches to children and families. 292 child readers reading 222,000 minutes; 28 teen readers 
reading 71,664 minutes; 74 adult readers reading 135,315 minutes. 394 readers total, 428,977 minutes 
total! Closing in on half a million minutes read over the course of about 6 weeks! 
With school back in session, our crowds after school have not quite reached last year’s levels yet. As 
homework increases, I would anticipate growing numbers. Furthermore, the Boys and Girls Club 
were understaffed and unable to make Power Hour available until last week. Highlight: Kaitee’s 
presence has really eased the adjustment for new students visiting and made the return of veterans 
comfortable as well. 
We are currently housing the Plan Lunenburg town model in the center of the main hall. This display 
has sparked many conversations during the last month or so. I am currently trying to find a way to 
host the 5th public meeting at the library on Oct 5th.

LUNENBURG PUBLIC LIBRARY DIRECTOR’S REPORT 
Staff was trained in First Aid and the use of an AED by members of the Lunenburg FD on August 16. 
Then, staff was treated to a nice lunch before we opened. Thank you, Friends of LPL! This week’s 
staff meeting will include a training for our new Hoopla resource delivered by Melanie. 
I participated again in the Massachusetts Library Aid Association board meetings. We selected 
scholarship recipients on August 21st.  
I have been invited to participate in a Banned Books Week panel at Fitchburg State next Thursday. I 
have also been requested by the Interim Town Manager and Capital Planning Committee to 
“mentor” other department heads in preparing for this year’s presentations. I have met with the 
other department heads, presented the committee’s asks for presentations, discussed with Chief 
Gammel his approach to presenting, and have delivered a Powerpoint template and list of tips. I am 
working on organizing a workshop for presenters to practice and share feedback next week or the 
week after. User’s Council quarterly meeting will happen this week, Tuesday afternoon. 
Our LSTA proposal was not approved for funding. Our additional ARPA requests were also not 
funded. I will meet with Ellen Dorian of Main St. Bank on Monday Sept 23 to discuss proposed 
projects with her. 
We have shifted our staff meeting time to Wednesday mornings (from Tuesday mornings) and still 
plan to have these completed before the library opens. We also completed our FY24 department 
reports for the Annual Town Report at the conclusion of the year. More clear for everyone and we 
will submit it in February when the town prepares that report. 
Mentoring/skill development: Lydia and I are trialing an arrangement for her to select all books for 
the adult collection. She and I will work together to help her develop her selection skills even further. 
Joslyn is applying her film expertise (degree in film) to selecting movies and anime for the collection. 
Debbie is also mentoring Joslyn on as many Children’s Room tasks as possible to develop her 
comfort with these responsibilities. Lydia and Kaitee and collaborating on the Monthly Craft & Chill, 
and this opportunity has allowed Lydia to pass some programming and general library knowledge on 
to our newcomer. 
   Programs 
Fall brings a number of new-look programs to the library. Some rebranded book discussion groups, 
some teen club experiences, and lots of collaboration between staff.  
Book Groups: LPL Kids(ages 3-5), Listeners Book Club, Teen Manga Club, In-betweeners (ages 10-13), 
BYOBook (drop in discussion), Angst Appreciation (adult readers of YA lit), Eagle House Readers, 
Baking Book Club, Brighter Days Books Club, and Tuesday Morning Book Club. 
Concerts continue to bring good size audiences. Kaitee and Lydia’s collaboration on a “date-night” 
Craft and Chill program offered a paint your partner program early in the month that was very 
successful bringing new faces to the library.

   Building & Maintenance 
The new landscaper however has been weeding around the building, and the grounds look fantastic. 
Although there was a small mix up with some of our natives in the patio area, it prompted effective 
communication. Bill Tyler has been working to mulch around the building and the results are 
spectacular. 
Mini-splits were installed to serve the Community and Conference Rooms. The HVAC system 
continues to need regular and costly maintenance, though one technician did diagnose an issue on 
one roof unit that was causing the system to only achieve 50% air flow. The building has been 
downright chilly since. 
We anticipate keyless entry to be installed in all town buildings this year. We also anticipate the 
security camera upgrades to go out to bid by October 1. 
   Technology 
I have resumed regular meetings with the IT department now that Casey has returned from parental 
leave.  
The four adult chromeboxes were replaced. The sound equipment in the Community Room has been 
repaired and is on its way back to the library. This should enable us to install the projector 
permanently from the ceiling of the room to prevent unnecessary damage and ease set up for 
outside groups. 
Our Library of Things will be moved to display opposite the main desk and will hopefully get even 
more attention there.

HIGHLIGHTS FOR JULY & AUGUST: 
• Our Cultural Cooking Events were a big hit!  The kids prepared food from 
Hawaii (Spam Sushi), France (crepes), Japan (Ramen bowls), Texas (fFrito Pie), 
and Puerto Rico (Pina Coladas- without the rum of course!) We were able to 
have community involvement for 3 of these events as we learned about recipes 
from France, Puerto Rico and Hawaii! 
• Animal Adventures stopped by with a wide variety of animals to show the kids: 
Alligator, Snake, Chinchilla, Ferret, Hedgehog, Rats, Bunnies, Turtles Tortoises, and 
Frogs, Geckos, Bearded Dragons, Tegus, Spiny Tailed Lizards, and a Blue Tongue Skink! 
Kids and animals are always successful programs and the group from Animal Adventures 
does a great job of entertaining and educating participants.   
• Poop Museum- Wow, this was a very popular program!  Soooo much information 
about animals and their poop techniques... the kids were enthralled, and the parents 
were aghast- good times for all! 
• SUMMER OLYMPICS AT THE LIBRARY- 40 kids and families too part in this fun 
event.  The kids could participate in 5 different events from Javelin Throwing to Long 
Jumping.  Lots of enthusiastic Olympians at this event! 
• SRP FINALE EVENT- 75 kids and families came to the library for a summer reading 
program finale they won't soon forget! One Up Games set up a 9-holes mini golf (inside the 
library) along with a variety of video games on different consoles. For the last hour after the 
library closed they turned on LED lights to play mini golf in the dark!  
• SRP ICE CREAM SOCIAL- We provided the ice cream and everyone brought their favorite 
topping for this fun event.  One of the highlights, besides the ice cream, was a visit from 
Officer McNamara and Hank- always great to see these two!

Trustee Report 
Adult Services Department 
September 2024 
 
Summer Reading 2024 was a major success for the adult services department. We had 74 readers log 
135,315 minutes and enter 309 tickets into prizes on Beanstack. This was a substantial increase from 
previous years, which is incredibly encouraging for sustainable growth in the coming years.  
 
A few outstanding programs we hosted this summer include: 
➢ A nature themed talk about animals who can be seen on Tanzanian Safari by The Joys of Nature 
was held June 24th and attended by 11 patrons of various ages. 
➢ 22 crafty attendees made beaded dream tree sculptures at our craft night program on July 24th 
➢ The summer puzzle swap had 60 people exchange 400 tickets for new to them puzzles from the 
550 puzzles that were donated prior to August 17th making this puzzle swap our best attended 
summer swap event since starting the program! 
➢ A Caribbean steelpan player entertained 21 patrons on August 19th as part of our monthly music 
series programs to great acclaim and rave reviews. 
➢ Finishing out the summer, Kaitee and I hosted our first Friday evening craft & chill program on 
August 30th for pairs of participants to paint their partners while snacking on charcuterie 
selections and sipping on a fruit juice mocktail. We had 17 people join us for this first event; many 
of whom fit in the difficult to reach "new adult” age bracket of 20–40-year-olds. This will be a 
monthly program that we hope to grow over time. 
Looking ahead, here are the September events at the library: 
➢ The monthly music series presents the Alex Minasian Jazz Trio on Monday September 9th 
➢ A September puzzle race for teams of four to complete a 500-piece puzzle the fastest will be on 
Monday September 16th  
➢ Greg Burns from Oak Tree Forge will be onsite for a forging demonstration on Wednesday 
September 18th 
➢ Patrons will learn about the history and music of the street organ with Tony Gangi on Wednesday 
September 25th 
➢ The September meeting for the Friday night craft & chill group will be on September 27th for patrons 
to make fermented salsa, snack on chips and dips, and sip on virgin margarita mocktails 
➢ We are also participating in Monday evening zoom author talks for the month of September, hosted 
by the Ashland Public Library 
➢ Book clubs continue to be successful with 9-13 people attending the 100 Books Bucket List meetings 
on the first Tuesday morning or the Brighter Days meetings on the second Wednesday evening. The 
baking book club will be moving to the library community room every first Wednesday evening 
starting October 2nd
