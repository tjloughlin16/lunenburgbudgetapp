The SWTF welcomed Rob St. Germain, thanking him for taking the time to meet with us. Members
had copies of the EPA - Soak Up The Rain Webinar Series presentation on May 14, 2019: Reaching
Public Consensus:, Stormwater Funding in Ashland, MA (attached). Rob went through the
presentation, adding bits of information as he proceeded. He pointed out that Ashland had been a
Super Fund site, due to contamination of its waters by a chemical plant. As a result, most Town
residents are on Town water and do not use wells. Also, they are keenly aware of issues that may arise
from polluted waterways. Ashland also has a significant aging population, so many folks remember
the Super Fund issues very clearly. Ashland used THINK BLUE materials and joined the Central Mass
Regional Stormwater Coalition (CMRSWC) to save valuable dollars and get expert help. They realized
that most people don't attend meetings or forums, so used Public Access TV to help with marketing.
Folks in Ashland are familiar with government helping them keep Ashland clean. They have a "Green
Up Ashland" program where folks adopt roads. They get different colored trash bags and fill them
with trash found along their "adopted" road(s). There is an arrangement with the Town's trash hauler
to pick up these bags. Ashland also already had several utilities utilized by most Town residents,
including sewer, water, and trash, so another utility was less intimidating than it might be elsewhere.
Ashland "scrubbed" the Municipal Separate Storm Sewer System (MS4) permit requirement budget
from an initial cost of $2.4 million down to $1.4 million. They also looked carefully at
consultant/contractor quotes to reduce costs. In one instance, using MASS GIS, they were able to
reduce the cost of a contract from $120,000 to about $20,000. They wanted to get the most bang for
their buck.
Ashland's Stormwater Advisory committee looked at different options for assessing the Stormwater
Utility Fee. They felt the fee had to be fair and equitable for businesses, residents, seniors, and low
income. To keep it simple, they adopted a 2 tiered approach. Tier 1 included all single family home
owners, condominium owners, and owners of undeveloped parcels, who are charged $8.75 per quarter
($35/year). There is a reduced rate for qualified low income seniors, but no other reductions are
possible at this time. Tier 2, which includes all multiple family dwellings, businesses, and properties
exempted from property tax (schools, churches, government buildings), is based on a charge of $ 0.80,
per 100 square feet of impervious area. The SAC pledged to endeavor to keep the fee constant for the
first 3 years of the program. They expect to receive about $300,000 in revenue as a result of the fee
(Ashland issues 6,500 tax bills).
The first bills will be issued this summer; they are hoping all goes smoothly. Rob agreed to send out an
electronic copy of Ashland's IDDE bylaw, and to send updated information as is available. The SWTF
