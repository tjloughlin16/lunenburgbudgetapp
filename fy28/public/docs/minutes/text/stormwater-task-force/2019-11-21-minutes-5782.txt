Nick Cristofori passed out an agenda (attached) he'd created to step us through CET's progress in providing
professional engineering services for NDES Phase II MS4 Permit compliance.
1. Project/Task Status Update
Task 1 - Annual Report: submitted in a timely manner to meet the September 30 deadline
• Task 3 - IDDE Field Work: completed 5 field days; attempted to locate 145 outfalls; located 111
structures (98 outfalls, 9 culverts, 4 swales). Did not locate 34 structures; will attempt to locate these in
2020. Of interest is that there are ~ 250 "outfalls" in the IDDE Plan, ~ 380 in GIS.
Task 4 - Operation and Maintenance (O & M) Plan; there needs to be a listing of all facilities (schools,
DPW, Public Safety, etc.), that could be possible sources of pollutants (trash, oil, gas, etc.). Nick will
send a copy to the SWTF when completed, for input. There is a requirement that each of these facilities
have an operation and maintenance plan; the goal is that these plans will be created over this winter.
• Task 13 - Catch Basin (CB) Optimization Plan: Nick has sent a draft to DPW Director Rodriquenz for
his perusal. Catch Basins need to be cleaned in a timely manner so that the sumps are never more than
50% full, therefore a plan has to be created to insure that this happens.
• Task 14 - Street Sweeping Plan: was to be done in Year One, but wasn't; has now been completed. It is
basically a map of Town, indicating which roads need to be swept annually, which more often. Roads
that connect to the Nashua River Watershed need to be done more often, for example, because of the
phosphorous impairment.
• Task 15 - Winter Road Maintenance Plan: the DPW Director has the draft of this plan; Nick needs to
follow up with him on this.
• Task 5 - Public Education: Nick forwarded information to the SWTF; we will discuss at a future
meeting.
Phyllis Luck asked for clarification that all of the above tasks need to be completed in Year Two. Nick
responded that the Dry Weather screening in Task 3 needs to be completed by the end of Year Three.
2. Next Steps
Task 7 - Stormwater Utility: any interest? There was a great deal of discussion about the possible
benefits of creating separate funding through a Stormwater Utility, as well as the disadvantages of
moving these costs out of the budget and charging residents a separate fee to fund these expenses. The
SWTF decided to put this initiative on hold and discuss at length at the next meeting.
• Task 8 - Regulatory Mechanism Assistance: this is an important issue that needs to be dealt with now.
The Town's bylaws and regulations must meet the new requirements of the MS4 permit. Any changes
to the current stormwater bylaw that may be necessary, need to be ready to meet the mid-March
submittal requirement to appear on the warrant for the May Town Meeting.
Nick and Adam will get together next week to review, discuss, and then Adam will set up a meeting
with the Conservation Commission Agent, Building Commissioner, Adam, and Nick. The goal is to get
this going in December.

5. MVP Planning Grant - Municipal Vulnerability Preparedness Program Grant (MVP) provides
funding for communities to prepare for climate change and improve climate resiliency. The Town
Manager and Land Use Director had already spoken to Nick about this. The Town is in the process of
applying for the $15,000 Planning Process grant. If awarded this grant, the Town will work with an
MVP provider (CEI has 5, including Nick Cristofori) to work with Town personnel to identify areas of
Town that are vulnerable to flooding and/or could be prone to becoming inaccessible in the event of a
natural disaster, and to then host a public forum in a holistic process to obtain additional input and
discuss these findings. The workshop must be either one 8 hour workshop or two 4 hour workshops;
typically these are held on weekdays. Participants would be applicable Town employees and the public.
January 15 is the deadline for completing the application. Nick would like to have it wrapped up the
week after Thanksgiving, latest. If awarded this grant, Lunenburg would have to complete this process
by June of 2020. Upon becoming an MVP Community, towns are eligible for grants which cover a wide
range of activities, and can be substantial in amount. About ½ to 2/3rds of the communities in the State
have obtained this status.
