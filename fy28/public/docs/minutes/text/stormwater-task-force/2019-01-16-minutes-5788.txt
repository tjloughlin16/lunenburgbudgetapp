Proposal for YEAR ONE 2018-2019 Management (SWTF) MS4 Permit and Proposal for YEAR ONE 2018-
2019 Management MS4 Permit, both received by the SWTF on 1/1/19. Ms. Luck asked why the contractor
would have presented two proposals rather than one, incorporating all tasks. Further, why tasks under Minimum
Control Measure 6, "Good Housekeeping", were also split between the two proposals. DPW Director
Rodriquenz posed that this was a question for the contractor; Adam Burney offered several reasons why the
contractor may have separated the two seemingly intertwined proposals. It was clear that the "two proposal
approach" was not at the Town's request; therefore, clarification from the contractor was needed.
There was consensus among the group that the proposals were not cost effective for Lunenburg. Phyllis Luck
pointed out that the hourly rate for MWC representative Ed Himlan, had gone from $50 to $90; the rate for DCI
representative Mike Clark, from $100 to $150. Several specific items were discussed and it was determined
that the proposals needed to be negotiated and/or re-scoped as they exceeded the $40,000 - $50,000 that the
Town has available within the FY19 budget.
The group then looked at the latest version of DCI's "Cost Estimate For: Town of Lunenburg's General Permit
Small MS4 Permit, Years 1 - 5, and Summary, received via email earlier today. Again, the consensus was that
these estimates were beyond the Town's funding ability. It was hoped that the SWTF could take on the Public
Education and Outreach tasks, which would reduce those costs by about half. Phyllis Luck brought up that in
her work with the Central Mass Regional Stormwater Coalition (CMRSWC), about fifteen of the 30 member
communities complete all of these activities in-house, using the templates, workshops, and other resources
made available to them by the CMRSWC, and that perhaps the Town could take on some of these tasks in-
house. Town Manager Lemieux felt that the Town's employees did not have sufficient time to assist with any
of these tasks.
There was discussion about seeking other quotes for the work, but consensus was that there wasn't enough time
to do that and complete the Year One requirements of the MS4 Permit, so that the best course was to look more
closely at the proposals, determine what could be eliminated, what could be scaled back, and what could be
renegotiated for further cost savings.
The Town Manager suggested that DPW Director Rodriquenz, Land Use Director Adam Burney, and SWTF
Chair Phyllis Luck set up a meeting with Mike Clark, originator of the proposals, to discuss further. Phyllis
Luck offered to set up the meeting.
Phyllis thanked all for their inputs.

DESIGN CONSULTANTS, INC. (DCi) is pleased to submit this proposal for Professional
Engineering Services for YEAR ONE 2018-2019 Management of General Permit for Stormwater
Discharges from Small Municipal Separate Storm Sewer Systems (Permit) in Lunenburg,
Massachusetts (Town). DCi and our teaming partner, the Massachusetts Watershed Coalition (MWC)
will assist the Town in implementing and managing the Permit requirements.
This proposal provides Minimum Control Measures (MCM) 3 and 6 required in the first year of the
permit.
SCOPE OF WORK
TASK 1 - SMALL MS4 PERMIT COST ESTIMATE
The DCi Team will prepare an estimate of probable costs for the 5-year Permit Life. This estimate
will be in the form of an excel spreadsheet. The spread sheet will consist of five tabs with the detail
of the yearly permit costs. The costs will roll up to a summary page the details the permit costs through
the five year life of the Permit. Each year will be formatted so that it can be used as a stand-alone
document and included as back up for the permit roll up page. The DCi Team anticipates providing
this with thirty days of receiving the executed contract.
TASK 2 - ANNUAL REPORT
The DCi team will prepare the annual report for each of the permit years. As discussed, the permit
year closes on June 30 with the annual report due by September 30 of the same calendar year. The
first year's annual report period May 2018 to June 2019. The following years are a July to June period.
The DCi team will collect and summarize required information. It is expected that the US EPA will
provide a template for the annual report. The DCi Team will submit a Draft Annual Report to the
Town thirty days prior to the required submittal date. Following review with the Town, the DCi Team

TASK 4 -MCM 4: ILLICIT DISHCARGE DETECTION AND ELIMINATION (IDDE)
DCi will prepare a draft IDDE Program. This IDDE Program will meet the requirements of 12.3.4.6
of the Permit.
This Program will clearly identify the responsibilities of the lead Department
responsible for implementing the IDDE Program as well as other departments that may have
responsibilities of other aspects of the Program. The IDDE Program will include written procedures
to meet Permit requirements, including procedures for dry weather outfall screening and sampling,
and for catchment investigations.
DCI will assess and priority tank the Town's outfalls based on the potential to have illicit discharges
and Sanitary Sewer Overflows (SSO), and impact on public health. A priority order for screening
outfalls will be developed. Each outfall will be ranked as Problem Outfalls (known or suspected
discharges), High Priority Outfalls (near public beaches, recreational areas, drinking water supplies, or
shell fish beds), Low Priority Outfalls, and Excluded outfalls. Develop a schedule to inspect cach High
and Low Outfalls within the first three years of the Permit.
A catchment investigation procedure will be developed in accordance with 92.3.4.8 b. of the Permit.
This procedure will:
• Identify maps, historic plans and records, and other sources of data to identify system
vulnerability factors within each catchment,
• Include a manhole inspection methodology, and
• Establishes procedures to isolate and confirm sources of illicit discharges.
As part of this effort, DCi will need access to the Towns GIS system to obtain information on each
outfall.
DCi will provide the IDDE Program, the Priority Order Spreadsheet and Schedule, and Catchment
Investigation Procedures in electronic format to the Town.
TASK 5 - MCM 6: GOOD HOUSEKEEPING
The DCi Team will develop and inspection and maintenance procedures and frequencies for all
municipal stormwater treatment structures (not including catch basins). As required by the Permit,
DCi will inspect each structure. DCi has budgeted five man days for this work.
