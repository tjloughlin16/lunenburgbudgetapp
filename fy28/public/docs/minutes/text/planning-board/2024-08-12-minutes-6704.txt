CERTIFICATES OF COMPLETION 
1295 - 1325 Massachusetts Avenue 
Greg Roy (Dillis & Roy) offered to answer any questions from the Board about the As-Built plan. 
M. Allison asked why the finish floor elevation was raised by one foot on the As-Built plan (Graves 
Engineering, Inc review, comment 2). Dan Proctor (1529 Mass. Ave) said it addressed a 
groundwater issue and left the height under 30 ft.  
 
M. Allison asked whether there had been any feedback from the Building Commissioner on the site. 
R. Harris relayed the Building Commissioner’s explanation that the bank won’t release any of the 
units to be sold or occupied until a Certificate of Completion was issued for the Stormwater Permit of 
1295 Mass Ave. G. Roy confirmed all of the Stormwater construction for the 1295 was completed. 
 
M. Allison asked whether the Board was signing off on the complete Stormwater before or after 
construction. R. Harris clarified that the Board was being asked to sign the Certificate of Completion 
for 1295 Mass. Ave’s specifically, while the Certificates of Completion for 1315 and 1325 Mass Ave’s 
were upcoming decisions after the rest of the buildings were finished and the final site stability had 
been confirmed. He further clarified that although one Stormwater Permit was issued for the whole 
site, Certificates of Completion could be issued on an individual lot or address basis. 
 
M. Allison asked whether the Board would be requiring a Performance Guarantee for the other sites. 
R. Harris said that it wouldn’t be necessary given GEI’s recent review. 
 
M. Allison moved to issue a Certificate of Completion for 1295 Mass Ave’s Stormwater 
improvements for that portion of the project. G. McLeod seconded. 
Voice vote, passed unanimously. 
 
BOARD DISCUSSIONS 
Advisory Board Notification Procedures 
L. O’Connell gave context. Lots 1 and 3 at 20 Kilburn St were recently converted out of Chapter 61B 
for Residential Use. The Notice of Intent (NOI) dated 2024-05-15 was given to the Planning 
Department, although the date of receipt isn’t known due to staffing changes and onboarding 
difficulties. The Open Space Committee (OSC) also wasn’t notified when the NOI arrived with the 
Planning Department, and ultimately received it from them with short notice. He opined that the 
===PAGE 3===

infrequency of the Chapter conversion process caused the lack of clear staff procedure on handling 
them. 
 
G. McLeod agreed and said it was important to immediately notify the OSC. He mentioned their 
request to be notified when any parcel of >=5km currently within Chapter comes before the Planning 
Board, and asked where the data on properties currently within Chapter was available. R. Harris 
explained that there should be a table in the appendix of Lunenburg’s Open Space Recreation plan 
listing all parcels under Chapter 61, 61A and 61B. He also mentioned that the Assessor’s Office 
should have this data. 
 
M. Allison noted that previous discussion about streamlining this process between the OSC and 
Planning Board was taking place before the previous Land Use Director left, and it likely fell through 
the cracks. He agreed that the OSC should be immediately notified when the Planning Department 
receives an NOI. 
 
M. Allison, G. McLeod and R. Harris discussed the function and timeline of Chapter 61 conversions. 
R. Harris reiterated that although the Town only has 120 days to decide whether to exercise its right 
of first refusal, it has a following period of time for the Town Meeting process and acquire the funding 
to actually make the purchase. 
 
Legal Advisory – Affordable Homes Act 
R. Harris explained that recent amendments to the Affordable Homes Act regarding Accessory 
Dwelling Units would likely require changes to the zoning bylaw, and would go into effect February 
2025. 
 
G. McLeod opined that this restricted the regulatory options available to local communities, as well as 
individuals’ property rights, similar to the MBTA Communities Act.  
 
R. Harris replied that the State had taken greater control over certain uses (i.e. solar, agricultural, 
educational or religious uses), and also doing so with Accessory Dwelling Units. 
 
ABUTTING COMMUNITY NOTICES 
R. Harris explained this new agenda item, saying he suggested it so that Board members would have 
an opportunity to discuss any notices of interest (i.e. for properties close to the Town Line).
