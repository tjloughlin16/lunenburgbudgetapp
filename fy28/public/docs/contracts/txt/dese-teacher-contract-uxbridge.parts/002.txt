Family, Medical, and Small Necessities Leaves of Absence
Purpose. The Family and Medical Leave Act ("FMLA") of 1993 allows eligible
employees twelve (12) weeks of unpaid leave ("FMLA Leave") per year under the
circumstances outlined below. Employees may take leave for the following reasons:
1. birth of the employee's child or placement of a child with the employee
3. the employee is unable to perform the functions of his or her position
because of a serious health condition.
Note: "Serious health condition" is defined by law and refers to in-patient care, and in
some instances out-patient care, by a medical provider.
B.
Use Paid Leave First. Employees are required to use certain types of accrued
or available paid leave first, as part of the twelve weeks of FMLA leave, before
commencing the unpaid portion of the leave. Employees who take leave because of the
birth, or placement of a child or to care for an ill spouse, parent or child must first use all
accrued vacation and personal time, in that order. Employees who take leave because of
their own serious illness must use all accrued sick, personal and vacation time, in that
order.

1250 hours in previous 12 months. To be eligible for leave under this policy an employee
must have been employed by the Town for at least twelve months, and must have worked

Curriculum Council
The role of the Curriculum Council is to complete the self-study and develop/redesign phases of the curriculum
review process in the necessary focus areas. The curricular focus areas will change year to year based on the
district needs and only focus area(s) deemed appropriate will convene each year. The expected focus areas for
the following school year will be announced by May 31st. Each focus area will have multiple council members
working cohesively with their Captain to complete the phase(s). Additionally, members will work
collaboratively to ensure vertical alignment. Any UPS teacher is welcome to sign up to be a curriculum council
member for that year, and those meeting the qualifications can apply to be the Captain of a focus area.
Curriculum Review Phases
The Curriculum Review process is broken into four phases, to be completed in each of the appointed focus
area(s). The phases include self-study, develop/redesign, implement/monitor, and evaluate.
1. The Self-Study phase begins with an in-depth examination of the current curriculum and its resources,
including the alignment to the state standards. Relevant data will be considered in this process. At the
conclusion of this phase, action steps will be developed for the next phase.
2. The Develop/Redesign phase will begin working through the determined action steps, redesigning
the current curriculum scope and sequence, and developing new elements as necessary. If needed,
new programs will be explored.
3. The Implement/Monitor phase will be completed by teachers in the focus area to test new curriculum
scope/sequence and resources. Throughout this phase, reflections and data on student progress will
be collected.
79
===PAGE 83===
4. The Evaluate phase includes reflection on the implement/monitor phase. Both qualitative and
quantitative data will be reviewed to assess the new curriculum. At the conclusion of this phase,
action steps will be developed to revise the curriculum to better meet the needs of UPS.

Curriculum Council Captain
Curriculum Council Captains will act as the coordinator within their focus areas, ensuring the council has a
clear direction and action plan. They will serve as a liaison between the curriculum council and
administration.
Responsibilities
• Assist in facilitating the curriculum review process by leading council members through the
designated phase(s) in their focus area
• Phase 1 - The Self-Study phase begins with an in-depth examination of the current
curriculum and its resources, including the alignment to the state standards. Relevant data
will be considered in this process. At the conclusion of this phase, action steps will be
developed for the next phase.
• Phase 2 - The Develop/Redesign phase will begin working through the determined action steps,
redesigning the current curriculum scope and sequence, and developing new elements as
necessary. If needed, new programs will be explored.
• Ensure the written curriculum is updated throughout the review process.
• Collaborate with administration and teacher liaisons to ensure that curricular needs are addressed.
• Identify professional development needs for the grade-level/content focus area(s)
• Assist in facilitating curriculum inservice half days
Time Requirements
• Attend two hours of meetings per month.
• Meetings can be either building-based or district-wide.
• Meetings will occur October through May and the dates/times and the meeting schedule will
be shared in September.
• Should a meeting need to be rescheduled, the new date will be communicated at least two
weeks in advance.
• For consistency, meetings will try to be rescheduled on the same weekday.
• Curriculum council captains and members will be consulted to determine a new
meeting date that works best.
• Attend four Curriculum Council Captain meetings run by administration
• Each meeting will last no more than 90 minutes.
• Meeting schedule will be shared by September 1st. Should a meeting need to be rescheduled,
the new date will be communicated at least two weeks in advance.
• For consistency, meetings will try to be rescheduled on the same weekday.
• Curriculum council captains will be consulted to determine a new meeting date
that works best.
