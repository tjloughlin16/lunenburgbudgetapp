The pattern generalises: buildings is the first category done across every fund, and the
same shape (a category, every fund that pays for it, what it bought, why a surplus year left
money) is meant to repeat for the other surplus categories. Hub D (school funds) is the
fund-by-fund view of the same journal; this is the category-by-category one.

## What counts as building money (measured 19 October 2026, to be declared in journal.py)

| part | rule | FY2023–FY2026 |
|---|---|---|
| General fund, operations and maintenance | fund 0100, DESE function 4000–4999: custodians, utilities, upkeep (the surplus pages' three categories) | about $1.56M–$1.74M a year |
| Capital articles | every fund numbered 3xxx (`<n> CAPITAL PLAN`, single articles) | $45,000 to $530,023 a fund-year |
| Green Communities grant | fund 2583: Primary School heat pump, refrigeration controls, weather stripping | paid FY2025–FY2026 |
| Revolving funds, function 4000–4999 | 1306 school facilities use (rental receipts, landscaping, repairs); 1312 Extended Day's rent; 2200 lunch equipment | small |

The function code is DESE's, as the district codes it; nothing is reclassified by us.

  (Galaxy Integrated, $105,728). Whether new systems meant fewer general-fund service calls on

  FY2026 (checked across every fund).
