On format: the spreadsheet is the ask, and both is better

The XLSX export is what we need. The sheet gives the full coded account —

0100-3-300-2710-04-4-65-1-511024

— and the PDF gives a label with the code removed. That fourth segment is the only thing joining the district’s budget to the Town’s books, which is why the category comparison above works for FY2026 period 12 and no other period: that is the one report that arrived as a spreadsheet.

But the PDF alongside it is genuinely useful, so both is ideal. The printed report carries its own options page — account type, print totals only, the fund and period selected, whether revenue prints as a credit — and the spreadsheet does not. That header is how we know how a report was run, which matters as much to us as the numbers: a column of figures whose parameters we are guessing at is a column we have to hedge every time we quote it.

So: XLSX for the data, PDF for the criteria. If only one is possible, the spreadsheet.

Sending earlier years the same way would extend the category comparison backwards with no new report having to exist.

If any of this is not possible as described

Tell us which part, and that is more useful than the data. Everything above is our reading of what MUNIS can produce, assembled from the reports you have already sent. We are certainly wrong somewhere, and the shape of the mistake matters:

  • A parameter we have named wrongly — then the right name saves a round trip.
  • A report that does not exist in that form — then we would rather ask for what does.
  • A field that exists on the record but is not on the report as configured — this is the one we would most like to hear about. The object code is the example: it is on the account, and the Account Details we received did not show it. If there are other fields sitting one checkbox away, we do not know what they are.

If it is easier to send one month, or one department, as a sample before running anything at scale, that suits us better too. A wrong field list found on one month costs a day; found on five years it costs the whole exchange.

A question we cannot answer from the outside

Is there any way to see what state aid actually paid for?

Here is what we found, and we would rather be corrected than act on it. No revenue account in anything we hold carries a category code — 0 of 222 — and the state aid accounts share no organisation code with any expense account. Reading that, we concluded that Chapter 70 arrives unrestricted into the general fund and is thereafter indistinguishable from any other revenue, so nothing records which dollar paid which bill.

If that is right, it is worth us knowing, because it is the one question on this page we would otherwise keep asking for. We would stop, and say instead what can be said: how much aid came in, what the department spent, and what the town had to raise after aid.

If it is wrong — if there is a report, a fund structure, a grant schedule or a reporting requirement that ties aid to spending, even at a category level — that would be the most valuable thing in this whole exchange, and we would not have known to ask for it.

The same question applies, more narrowly, to the grants: is there anything that records which accounts a Title grant or circuit breaker reimbursement paid into?

Every figure here was produced by querying the documents the Town and the district have published or sent, on 3 September 2026. Where this document says a report could be produced, that is our understanding of what MUNIS can do rather than a claim about your system.
