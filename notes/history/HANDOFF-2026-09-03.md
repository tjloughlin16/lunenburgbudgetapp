So two cells were empty, not five, and they were not the ones named. The *shape* of the

called years absent that are not.

**The cause, measured.** `extract_line_history.py` reads 24 of the 85 documents on the
district's budget page. **35 of the other 61 hold twenty or more rows of figures each**,
including the FY25 final approved budget (expenses and salaries, 341 rows between them),
the March 2026 FY27 budget documents (269 and 297 rows) and the Town Manager's own budget

**What is done.**

- `extract_line_history.py` now writes `sources/data/line-history-coverage.csv` — one row
  per document, read or not, with the reason quoted from that document's own header line
  and its line number. It prints the unread ones on every run. Same rule as
  `search_minutes.py`: print the denominator, because a reader that finds nothing prints
  nothing and nothing reads as absence.
- The reasons sort into three shapes, and they want three different fixes:
  **single-column** (the header states one fiscal year; the reader requires two),
  **header wrapped** (the same table from a different PDF pass, kinds broken over six
  lines — `final-budget-document.txt` and the 3/23/26 FY27 budget fail this way while the
  2/24/26 one does not, which is why the site quotes February for a document March
  superseded), and **kinds not in the vocabulary** (the column is named for a scenario —
  `Needs Based`, `TM 031225` — and there are four of them for one fiscal year).
- `export_ledger.py` gives a cell a fourth state, **`unread`**, when documents covering
  that year sit in the archive with their figures never extracted, and carries the list
  into the cell. `DataRoom.tsx` renders it, and — the part that matters — `YearDetail`
  keeps `unread` rows out of the "Ask <publisher>" grouping entirely. That grouping is
  the mechanism that would have put ten held documents into a request.
- The year a document covers is taken from **page-top header lines naming at most seven
  fiscal years**, plus its title block. Six is the widest header any document in this
  archive ever resolved; a line naming fourteen is a history chart on a slide, and slides
  are short enough that the page-top test alone does not exclude them. Without the cap
  the matrix claimed we hold a line-level FY2010 budget — the same over-claim as calling
  a year absent, pointed the other way.

**The scenario question, answered: widen the grain.** `budget_figure` now carries
`variant` — the document's own name for a column, `''` for the documents that print one
column per stage. A scenario is not a disagreement; four FY27 columns are four proposals.
`v_line_budget_vs_actual` filters `variant = ''`, `v_budget_scenario` is the other side of
it, and every consumer of `line-history.csv` was given the same filter. Documented in
`notes/SCHEMA.md`. 1,000 scenario rows are held and named where none were held before.

**Two documents newly read**, both the March 2026 FY27 budget: `final-budget-document.txt`
and `fy27-budget-projections-as-of-3-23-26.txt`. 3/16/26 is still refused, and correctly —
its `Encumbrances to date` column names no kind at all, so the walker resolves ten columns
for eleven years and will not guess at the eleventh. A shifted row is the worst kind of
wrong because the series still looks like a series.

**And the reconciliation that proves it found something bigger.** FY26 FINAL BUDGET from
the two new documents against the 2/24/26 document already loaded: 185 of 187 lines
agreed to the dollar and two were absurd. `P.S. Teachers/Regular (1-2)` and
`E.S. Teachers/Regular (3-5)` carry a grade range in the line's own name; `split_row` cut

`(658)` and `(3D printer)` did the same thing one column over.

**The FY25 approved budget is in.** `single_column()` reads a document that prints one
year — the FY25 final approved budget, expenses and salaries, 341 rows, the document the
School Committee actually voted — under three guards, all of which have to hold: no line
anywhere names two fiscal years, at least twenty of the district's own four-digit function
group headers, and the year at a page top. **And then the extract has to tie to a total
the document prints for itself**, which is the guard that matters: with one column every
row contributes its first number, so a page of prose under a stray heading reads exactly
like a budget. Three of the first six documents this accepted summed to $777, $55 and $66
across forty-odd "figures", and nothing about the shape of the text said so — only the
total did. Refusals are recorded with the arithmetic that produced them:

    fy25-final-approved-budget-expenses  ties to TOTAL EXPENSES, +9 over 227 rows
    fy25-final-approved-budget-salaries  ties to TOTAL SALARIES, +1 over 114 rows
    fy19-school-dept-budget-approved     REFUSED: 20,194,355 against its own 20,190,110

**Three more defects the tie test exposed, each fixed:**

- **The sign was being dropped.** `Circuit Breaker ... (75% = 452,580)-452,580` entered as
  +452,580, and the FY25 expense lines therefore summed $905,169 over the document's own
  total — the offset counted once the wrong way and so twice in the total. A minus
  *touching* the digits, or brackets around them, is now a sign. A dash with a space after
  it is still the sheet's notation for an empty column, which is why adjacency decides.
- **A percentage is not a dollar.** Every one of these sheets carries a trailing "%
  increase" column, and on a row printing a dash for zero it slid into a money column:
  `Kindergarten Paraprofessionals 0 -100.00%` was read as an FY26 budget of 100.
- **A budget line lives under a function group header** — in the documents that use them.
  Rows above the first `1110 -` were being read, which is where budget lines called
  `making class sizes approximately` and `not be able to oversee sports in all three
  seasons` came from. Two were published as API endpoints. **The first version of this
  rule required a header of every document and was wrong**: the six FY19 per-department
  budgets — athletics, maintenance, health — are single-department documents that head
  their sections `EXPENSES` and `Athletic Personnel` and carry no four-digit code
  anywhere, so the rule silently dropped all six, and `Athletic Officials`,
  `H.S. Library Para` and `GRAND TOTAL ATHLETICS` for FY14–FY19 with them. It now applies
  only where the convention does, and two other tests carry the weight elsewhere:

- **A column header is a row of years, not a sentence.** Both are matched by "two or more
  FY tokens on a line". Every real header in the archive carries at most two tokens that
  are not fiscal years (`Supt. %` is the widest); every false one carries four, seven,
  eleven, or — for the community-forum notice — a hundred and twenty-one.
- **An enrolment table is the same shape as a budget table.** `FY18 FY19 FY20` over
  `Actual Budgeted Proposed`, one row per school. The FY20 recommended-budget presentation
  carries one, and it produced budget lines called `Lunenburg High School:`, `THES. K`,
  `grade` and `denominators` with values of 12, 8 and 5. Its median figure is 9; the
  median in the smallest real budget document here is 1,750. The cut is at $100 and it
  refuses four documents, each printing the arithmetic that refused it.

**And nothing removed a retired endpoint.** `build_api.py` writes one JSON file per line
and had no delete pass, so a line that stopped existing went on being served. 29 removed;
395 endpoints for 395 lines. An endpoint that answers with a retired figure is worse than
one that is missing.

**The tiebreak is no longer the filename.** The collapse sorted by document year alone,
and Python's sort is stable, so two documents about the same year tie-broke
alphabetically — 135 published figures decided by what a file was called. It now orders by
the date the publisher put on the document, taken from the district's own title for it or
its own title block; 60 of 87 state one, and the rest still fall back to the filename,
which is at least a stated rule now. The visible effect is right: the FY21 *approved*
budget of 13 June 2020 supersedes the FY21 *proposed* budget of 29 April 2020, where
before whichever sorted later did. 2,162 cells changed which document they cite.

**Net effect on `budget-vs-actual.md`**, all re-verified: 545 usable line-years across 139
lines, coverage 92–96% every year, and cells where two documents disagree down from 330 to
288. 398 line endpoints for 398 lines.

**Still to read: 32 documents**, each with its reason recorded. What they want:
