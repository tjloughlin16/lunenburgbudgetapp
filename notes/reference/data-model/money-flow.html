2 · It all lands in one pot

GENERAL FUND 0100 — $52,187,832

Every source above flows in here and loses its identity. Money in the general fund is fungible, and no record ties a source to a department.

So there is no line on this page from Chapter 70 to the schools. The town apportions the pot across departments by share when it presents a budget, and that is a fair convention — but it is a convention. The edge is not missing data. It does not exist.

The pension cannot be split. COUNT[Y] RET covers town and school employees together and no published document says in what proportion. Teachers are not in it at all — they are in the state system, whose cost Lunenburg never appropriates and never sees. The Worcester Regional Retirement System publishes an annual actuarial valuation by member unit; that is the document that would settle it.

Athletics is the clearest case, and it is only half here. The town appropriates $518,334 for athletics inside dept 300 (function 3510: coaches, transport, the athletic director, the trainer, insurance). Fees bring in a further $335,856 through a revolving fund — which does not appear in the town’s fund-balance report at all. That figure comes from the district’s own athletics documents. So athletics costs about $854,190 to run and the town’s budget shows $518,334 of it. Every fee-funded programme has this shape; athletics is just the one where both halves have been found.
