# How municipal health insurance works in Massachusetts, and what Lunenburg can actually do

**Researched 18 September 2026**, at TJ's request after the Town Manager suggested the
project may be publishing options that are not available to this town. Statutory text was
read from malegislature.gov section pages, not from a summary. Every figure attributed to
a municipality is **that municipality's own stated figure** unless it says otherwise —
section 3 exists mostly to make that distinction hold.

**Not published on the site as it stands.** This is the working file behind the
corrections to `/health-insurance-what-changing-the-plan-does`.

---

## The short answer to the Town Manager's point

He is right about the load-bearing one. **The 2011 municipal health reform cannot change
who pays what share.** M.G.L. c.32B §21(f): "The panel **shall not impose any change to
contribution ratios**." The reform moved *plan design* out of bargaining and left the
*split* exactly where it was — at the table, under c.150E and the town's §19 Public
Employee Committee agreement.

Three more the town genuinely cannot do:

- **Cut the employer share below 50%.** §7 fixes 50/50; §7A permits "more, but not less,
  than fifty per cent." There is no downward route in the statute at all.
- **Stop offering coverage, or decline to fund it.** §10: acceptance "shall not be revoked
  or rescinded" — a **ratchet**, one-way per vote. And §3: if town meeting does not
  appropriate it, the selectmen certify the cost to the assessors, who put it in the tax
  rate regardless.
- **Drop or means-test existing retirees.** *Galenski v. Town of Erving*, 471 Mass. 305
  (2015) struck down a 10-years-of-service prerequisite.

## What IS available, with the thresholds stated correctly

| Lever | Statute | Who decides | The catch |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Plan design** up to the GIC benchmark — copays, deductibles, tiers | §§21–22 | **Select board**, no town meeting | 30 days with the PEC, then a 3-person panel decides in 10 days, binding. Mitigation to employees capped at **25% of estimated savings** |
| **Join the GIC by agreement** | §19(e) | Select board + PEC | Approved by a **majority of the weighted votes** — *not* 70% |
| **Join the GIC unilaterally** | §23 | Select board | Must show savings **at least 5% greater** than plan design alone (§21(d)); locked in **3 years**, then 2-year intervals |
| **Medicare transfer** for retirees | **§18A** (§18 is repealed) | Acceptance | Only where the Medicare plan is of "comparable actuarial value" |
| **Opt-out payment** | §4 | CBA or employer discretion | Only for someone with other coverage **financed without any employer participation** |
| **Raise the town's share** | §7A / §9E | Town meeting or ballot | One-way: no ceiling, no route back |

**Two corrections to things commonly said in town.**

1. **The 70% threshold is not the agreement threshold.** §19 gives the retiree
   representative a 10% vote and splits the other 90% among units by eligible employees;
   an agreement passes on **a majority of the weighted votes**. The 70% applies only to a
   request to send an *interpretation dispute* to binding arbitration.
2. **25% is a ceiling, not a floor.** §21(f) caps what a review panel may designate to
   subscribers at 25% of estimated savings, including multi-year cost. What a town and its
   PEC agree to is negotiated. The usual vehicle is a **premium holiday** — one month
   without payroll deduction — because it is one-time and does not compound into the base.

**Where the decision happens matters for this project.** §§21–23 are a **select board**
action. Anyone searching town meeting warrants for a health-insurance decision finds
nothing, because that is not where it is taken.

## What Lunenburg actually does

**Lunenburg buys through the MIIA Health Benefits Trust** (Blue Cross Blue Shield of
Massachusetts as carrier partner) — a **joint purchase agreement** under c.32B §12. Not
Minuteman Nashoba, which Mass Retirees reports wound up its benefits on 1 July 2024, and
not self-insured: the DLS Self-Insured Health Trust Funds report returns no rows for
Lunenburg. Established by the town's own FY25 Budget Message, the 26 August 2025 joint
minutes, and the 9 February 2021 Select Board minutes.

| | |
|---|---|
| Statutory vehicle | **§19** PEC route, accepted 2008 (the minutes say Town Meeting; §19(a) as written says selectmen — unresolved, see below) |
| Active split | **75% town / 25% employee** |
| Retiree split | **25%** employee for most; **50%** for Medex 3 |
| FY27 town-side group health | **$3,695,425**, up **12.0%** on FY26 — and this excludes school *actives*, who sit in the school budget |
| Live now | PEC negotiations for FY27 and beyond, to be finalised by 31 March 2026 |

**Already tried:** the 2020 post-65 Medicare move (the town states it cut the active
renewal 2% and reduced OPEB liability by $5–6M — *stated, not established*), and an
opt-out pilot the Finance Director called "minimal" (FY27: $14,000, 10 participants).
**Never pursued:** the GIC, raised by Senator Cronin at School Committee in December 2024
and by the Town Manager in August 2025. **No §§21–23 action appears anywhere in the
archive.**

## The framing correction for the site

The 2026 GIC intake — thirteen municipal entities, the largest since 2011 — is **not a
savings story**. Eight of the thirteen were leaving the Hampshire County Group Insurance
Trust after a 20% increase and a dropped drug benefit. A Leyden selectboard member, to the
*Greenfield Recorder*: *"At least there's a little security that they're still going to be
around. It's more about security than cost."* A page citing 2026 as evidence that the GIC
saves money has the causation backwards.

**And every savings figure in this subject is a forecast by the party that wanted it
approved.** MTF collected nine first-year estimates in 2012 (Gardner $620,000, Milford
$170,000, Littleton $315,000 — the closest size match to Lunenburg). Municipalities owed
actual-or-foregone reports by 30 June 2012; **no published compilation of them exists.**

## Where the numbers live

- **DLS Health Insurance Expenditures** — `https://dls-gw.dor.state.ma.us/reports/rdPage.aspx?rdReport=ScheduleA.HealthInsurance.HealthInsExpenditures`
  Municipality × fiscal year, 351 towns, FY2002–FY2026. **Lunenburg: $1,834,821 (FY13) →
  $2,505,400 (FY23) → $2,894,390 (FY24) → $3,038,832 (FY25).** FY26 prints 0 because
  Schedule A is not due until 30 November — an empty cell, not a zero.
  **Two traps:** for self-insured towns the figure *includes the employee share*, and
  trust-fund-accounted towns' figures include workers' comp and OPEB — and nothing in the
  file says which town is which. Rule 11 applies: it is a net appropriation, not a cost.
- **DLS Personnel Expenditures** (`…ScheduleA.PesonnelExpenditures.PersonnelExpenditures`)
  carries `Total Employees` — the denominator that makes cost-per-employee possible.
- **PERAC OPEB Summary Reports** — **Lunenburg at 6/30/24: total OPEB liability
  $52,294k, net position $1,405k, net liability $50,890k, funded ratio 2.7%**, blended
  discount rate 5.17%. Ragged measurement dates and per-town discount rates make a raw
  cross-town ranking partly a ranking of actuarial assumptions.
- **Schedule A code is `910 Employee Benefits`, health insurance is reporting unit `914`**
  (DOR Bureau of Accounts guideline — the live PDF is the FY2016 edition).
- The GIC publishes **full-cost rates** but not any town's split: *"You do not pay the full
  cost rate. Your share depends on your city or town cost-sharing arrangement."*
- **CHIA** has no municipal cut; the **Division of Insurance** covers the merged individual
  and small-group market and is the wrong door entirely.

## What is NOT established, and what would settle it

1. **Lunenburg's PEC agreement is published nowhere.** It fixes the 75/25 split, the
   opt-out payment and the plan menu — the most load-bearing unpublished document in this
   subject. *Closes: the executed agreement, and the successor now in negotiation.*
2. **What the 2020 post-65 move saved.** The $5–6M OPEB figure is a budget slide, with no
   actuarial valuation in the archive — though §20A means one exists and was filed.
3. **Whether the 2011 reform delivered its forecasts, anywhere in the state.**
4. **Which purchasing vehicle each municipality uses.** No state dataset records it. Every
   town's arrangement is established only from its own documents.
5. **Whether §19 was accepted by Town Meeting or the Select Board in 2008.** The minutes
   say one, the statute as written says the other. *Closes: the 2008 warrant article.*
6. Grafton's and Oxford's GIC Implementation Notices — both must exist under §21(d), and
   neither is published. **The best comparison this project could obtain, by records
   request.**
