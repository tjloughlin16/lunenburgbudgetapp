Summary

Lunenburg has published an annual town report every year since at least 1959. Together the reports are a continuous record of the town’s finances, government and schools across more than six decades. We are grateful to the town officers, boards and committees who wrote and printed them.

We digitized every financial table in the 1959–2010 reports and checked each page against the totals the page itself prints. Of 1,818 such pages, 1,647 reconcile. The remaining 171 are recorded individually, each with the reason it cannot be confirmed and the document that would resolve it:

  • About 105 pages print a total that does not equal the sum of its own lines.
  • 17 pages are scanned too poorly to settle a digit.
  • 49 pages hold no checkable total. These are salary schedules, fee lists, charts and comparisons.

Nothing in this review suggests that funds are unaccounted for. A printed total that disagrees with its lines is, on its face, a typing or printing discrepancy. The town’s ledgers for that year would settle it.

Minor structural issues appear in about twenty editions: duplicated page numbers, pages bound out of order, and warrant article numbers that skip. These make the reports harder to navigate but do not remove content.

3. How the reading was checked

Tables were digitized with Amazon Textract and then tested against the arithmetic each page prints: columns that should sum to a total, and balances that should equal available funds less spending.

  • Misread figures. Where a figure appeared to be misread, an independent second reading of the page image was taken. A correction was accepted only when it matched exactly and a printed total depended on it.
    • 99 figures were corrected this way.
    • Every correction records the figure as printed.
  • Our own errors found and fixed in the process:
    • one page that had passed for the wrong reason;
    • six second readings withdrawn on audit;
    • a footnote (“Includes Escrow”) that changes how one 1985 total is read.

4. Points to keep in mind when using these figures

  • “Reconciles” describes a page, not every row on it. A page counts as reconciled when at least one of its tables closes on a printed total. Individual rows carry their own check status.
  • Rounding. Differences of under $1 are treated as rounding and are labelled as such wherever they occur.
  • Calendar years, not fiscal years. The 1959–2010 reports are calendar-year editions; 1973–74 covers an 18-month transition. Figures should not be compared directly with the fiscal-year reports from FY2011.
  • Tax rates compare only within one assessment. Revaluations in 1970, 1982 and 1989 break the series. The 1969 rate of $133.00 per $1,000 and the 1970 rate of $33.00 describe different valuations.
  • Staff rosters are lists of names. They give no hours, funding source or start date. A name appearing across years is not proof of one continuous career.
  • Population figures sometimes differ between editions for the same census year. For 1980, one edition prints 8,375 and a later one prints 8,710. Both are recorded.
