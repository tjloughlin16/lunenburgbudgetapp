# Who reads this, and what they need from it

**Step 6 of `notes/WRITING-AN-ANALYSIS.md`.** That file is the whole sequence for producing
an analysis; this one is the readers it gets checked against.

A review process, and the six readers it runs against. Applied to every analysis before it
is published, and re-applied when one is substantially changed.

**The personas are composite, and every concern in them is quoted from a real public
meeting.** Nobody here is invented, and no persona is a portrait of a named individual.
The quotes are from minutes this project already publishes; street addresses are dropped
because they are not needed to make the point.

---

## Why this exists

Every figure in these documents is checked by a script. Nothing checks whether the
document answers the question the reader actually came with — and a report that is
entirely correct and answers nobody's question is a failure that no verifier can catch.

The first run of this process, against `fy26-closeout.md`, found five things. One was a
booster president telling the School Committee *"we currently have more heads than we have
helmets"* in the same fiscal year the report describes an athletics equipment line
spending 44% of its budget. The report had both halves of that and did not put them
together.

---

## The six readers

### 1. The resident who has already decided the schools are not straight with them

**What they believe walking in.** Something is being hidden. Every unexplained number is
evidence. Two overrides were defeated and this is why.

> "the failure of the override was not primarily the result of a tactical error or a
> messaging problem as suggested by the school committee chair, rather, it reflects a
> deeper issue. Many Lunenburg residents have lost confidence in our public institutions,
> particularly our schools."
> — public comment, School Committee, 24 June 2026

**What they will do with the report.** Search it for the worst number and stop. If the
report reads as advocacy, it confirms what they think. If it reads as an audit, they will
quote it as proof of wrongdoing — which is equally a failure.

**What the report owes them.** Every open question stated as open, in the plain sentence,
not buried in a "what this does not show". The numbers that look bad shown at full size.
And the places the district visibly did the right thing, stated with the same weight —
because a document that only finds fault is not being believed, it is being used.

**The test.** Can they find the worst fact in the document inside thirty seconds? If not,
they will conclude it was hidden.

### 2. The resident who hears about this second-hand, mostly the hot topics

**What they believe walking in.** Very little, and most of it from a neighbour or a
Facebook group. They know there was an override, that it failed, and that sports were cut.
They have heard "the schools handed back half a million dollars."

**What they will do with the report.** Read the first screen. Maybe.

**What the report owes them.** A first paragraph that survives being repeated at a
kitchen table without becoming false. "The schools didn't spend $482,000" is what they
will hear; the report has to make the true version just as sayable — *"they spent 97.3% of
their budget, and the leftover is mostly big lines landing within a few percent."*

**The test.** Read only the first screen. Write down the one sentence they would repeat.
Is it true?

### 3. The resident close to people on the boards

**What they believe walking in.** That the people involved are trying hard, are not
crooks, and are being unfairly attacked. They may have heard the substance of a decision
before it was public.

**What they will do with the report.** Check whether it is fair to people they know by
name.

**What the report owes them.** Findings that name mechanisms rather than people. `CLAUDE.md`
rule 8 is the standing instruction and this reader is why: *findings arrive as what this
means for planning, never as what they got wrong.* An account spending against a zero
budget is a paperwork state, not an accusation, and the document has to be readable that
way by somebody who knows the person who signed it.

**The test.** Could the person responsible for the worst-looking number in the report read
it without feeling ambushed — while the number itself is still stated at full size?

### 4. The Finance Committee member

**What they believe walking in.** That the town's finances are tight, that they are asked
to approve things without documents, and that the school department is hard to see into.

> "Year End Transfers — The documents needed on this topic were not provided but should be
> available for the next meeting."
> — Finance Committee minutes, 11 June 2026

**What they will do with the report.** Look for what they are being asked to decide, and
for anything that contradicts what they were told.

**What the report owes them.** The control question answered explicitly: *what would you
have had to see, and when, to have known this was coming?* And every gap named as a
specific document with a specific holder, not as a lament.

**The test.** Does the report tell them one thing they could do differently next year?

### 5. The School Committee member

**What they believe walking in.** That they cut deeply, were told it was necessary, and
are now being told there was money left. That they voted a budget in good faith on numbers
somebody else prepared.

> "an unknown surplus of money that was not spent and instead was given back to the town"
> … "we had drastic reductions in services to students. We really could have used those
> funds to keep some of the positions."
> — School Committee minutes, 17 September 2025

**What they will do with the report.** Ask whether this is FY25 happening again.

**What the report owes them.** A direct answer to that question, early. If the mechanism
is different this year, say which and how it differs. If a line they voted to cut spent
money anyway, that is theirs to know before it reaches anybody else.

**The test.** Is "is this FY25 again?" answered before the reader has to ask it?

### 6. The Select Board member

**What they believe walking in.** That they hold the whole town's budget, that the schools
are two thirds of it, and that town-side departments are asked to absorb what the schools
cannot.

> "a discussion about separating an override into tiers, with the suggestion that some
> departments, particularly the school, may be asking for too much… this framing reflected
> the very problem that has plagued the town for years: pitting the 'town side' against
> the 'school side'."
> — public comment, School Committee, 10 March 2026

**What they will do with the report.** Compare the two sides.

**What the report owes them.** A comparison that is like-for-like and that refuses the
frame where it does not hold. Where the two sides differ, the difference and the several
explanations that fit it — never the flattering one alone.

**The test.** Does the report make a town-versus-school argument easier or harder to make
carelessly? It should make it harder.

## The people nobody writes reports for, and who show up anyway

Boosters, coaches, parents of a child in one programme. They come with one concrete thing.

> "Casey, the head coach put in a request to the athletic department to purchase five new
> helmets earlier this spring, we currently have more heads than we have helmets."
> — public comment, School Committee, 24 June 2026

**What the report owes them.** If a document says a category underspent, and that category
is the one their thing sits in, the document has to look. Not to allege that the money was
there — the request may have arrived after the order window, the line may be restricted,
reconditioning is not the same as buying — but because a reader with a helmet problem will
find the number whether the report addresses it or not, and it is better addressed.

**The test.** For every category the report says underspent, is there a named, concrete
thing somebody asked for in the same year? Look before publishing.

---

## The process

Run before publishing an analysis, and again after any substantial change.

1. **Read the whole document as each of the six.** Not a skim. Write down, for each: the
   first question they would ask, and whether the document answers it and where.
2. **Apply that persona's test.** They are one line each and they are pass or fail.
3. **Search the meeting archive for the document's own subject.** Every category the
   report says underspent or overspent gets a search of `/minutes/` for what residents
   said about that thing in the same year. This is the step that found the helmets.
4. **Record what changed.** Findings go in the analysis, not in a review file — a
   correction that lives only in a process document is a correction nobody reads.
5. **Re-run the verifier.** Persona fixes add prose and prose adds figures.

### What this process is not

It is not an editing pass for tone. Three of the six tests are about **what the document
omits**, and omissions are what a writer cannot see in their own work. It is also not a
substitute for the verifier: one checks that the figures are right, this checks that the
right figures are there.
