# What the people who decide actually need

**Research and design, 9 September 2026. Nothing here is built.** This document works
*demand-first*: it starts from the votes four bodies take, establishes from the meeting
record what is in front of them when they take them, and only then asks what this archive
can supply. The gap between the two is the point of the exercise.

TJ's brief, and the correction that shaped it:

> *"Think about what information, conclusions, analyses they need to make good decisions.
> What information might change how they decide things… Right now, all these conclusions
> come off as 'interesting' but not clear as to why they are 'important'."*
>
> *"I think you should think about this from the persona perspective and what they need to
> KNOW. Dont just reframe what we have in this light. You can even ignore the conclusions
> we have for now."*

**`notes/process/PERSONAS.md` does not serve this purpose and should not be retrofitted to.**
It has six readers and asks *does this document answer their concern* — a review test,
applied after writing. Four of its six are residents. Its Finance Committee, School
Committee and Select Board entries are drawn as *readers of a report*, and their tests
("is 'is this FY25 again?' answered before the reader has to ask it?") are about the
document, not about the vote. There is no Town Manager at all, because a Town Manager is
not an audience for a report — she is the author of the budget the report is about.

Both files should exist. PERSONAS.md stays the pre-publication review. This one is the
demand register that says what to build.

## 1. The Town Manager

**Jennifer Warren-Dyment.** Not a voting body, and the most consequential actor in the
cycle, because almost every number the other three vote on reaches them through her.

### What she actually does

| act | evidence |
|---|---|
| Meets department heads in the autumn and issues a target | `finance-committee/2025-09-25-minutes-7420.txt`: *"The Town Manager confirms there have been meetings with department heads over the last several weeks."* `select-board/2026-02-10-minutes-7643.txt`: *"Department Heads have been meeting with her and Finance Director Karen Barrett on FY27 budget elements."* |
| Issues a cut instruction when revenue will not carry the base | `finance-committee/2026-01-12-minutes-7597.txt`: *"Town Manager Warren-Dyment confirmed she had already issued a similar request to Lunenburg departments"* — a 5% cut exercise |
| Sets the budget-request shape | `finance-committee/2026-02-12-minutes-7645.txt`: *"Jennifer Warren-Dyment has requested the department heads provide a level service budget and provide an explanation for any line item above the level service request."* |
| Builds the revenue forecast | 12 Jan 2026 Tri-Board: total preliminary FY27 revenue **$51,331,376.99**, levy build-up, new growth, state aid held level pending the Governor's budget |
| **Apportions new revenue among town, schools and Monty Tech** | Same minutes: *"the school department would receive approximately $696,521 of that amount above FY26 levels, the town would receive approximately $573,000, and Monty Tech would receive approximately $36,000"* — described as *"the traditional apportionment approach"* |
| Drafts the warrant and the Town Meeting booklet | `select-board/2026-04-07-minutes-7752.txt` enumerates the contents, including *"a letter from the Town Manager explaining the override structure"* |
| Submits the capital improvement program | Charter §6-7, quoted by Town Counsel in `finance-committee/2022-04-14-minutes-2072.txt` |

### What she is accountable for, and to whom

To the Select Board, who appoint her. In practice she answers to all three boards at once
at the Tri-Board, and to Town Meeting for the omnibus article.

### What would change her decisions

**a. A defensible apportionment rule.** The single largest allocative decision in the town
— how $1,306,059 of new revenue is split three ways — is made by convention, and the
convention is not written down anywhere this archive holds. `money-gaps.csv` already
carries the row: *"The Town Manager's revenue apportionment worksheet — seen once; we do
not hold it."* Anything that made the convention explicit, or offered an alternative basis
(share of prior appropriation, share of non-discretionary growth, foundation-budget share),
would change how a number is set rather than merely how it is described.

**b. Which of her assumptions have historically been wrong, and in which direction.** She
held state aid level at the FY26 actual of **$11,244,422** pending the Governor's budget
(12 Jan Tri-Board). New growth was estimated at **$400,000** while FY25 actual came in at
**$518,000**, and the Select Board Vice-Chair argued the estimate was too high anyway.
Health insurance was carried at roughly $740,000 for the schools in January and came back
at **8.85%** in February (`finance-committee/2026-02-12-minutes-7645.txt`). Every one of
those is a forecast this archive could backtest against the town's own later numbers, and
none has been.

**c. What a fee actually recovers.** She raised the pay-as-you-throw expansion and the TCP
carrying costs in the same meeting, both as revenue-or-cost questions with no figure
attached. Rule 11's trap runs here: a line already net of fees cannot be used to model what
fee would make it self-funding.

**d. Free cash, before she has to budget against it.** The FY27 budget was built and voted
against an **uncertified** free cash figure: certification prep began in October 2025, the
FY25 closeout deadline was extended to 28 February 2026, and on 19 February FinCom was told
*"Free cash has not been certified but preliminary results suggest it will be in excess of
$3,000,000"* (`finance-committee/2026-02-19-minutes-7657.txt`). Article K then committed
$991,627 of it to capital.

## 2. The Select Board

Five members. They hold the warrant, and therefore the ballot.

### The named votes, quoted

1. **Open and close the Annual Town Meeting warrant.** *"Motion by Chair Nuzzolo to open
   the Annual Town Meeting Warrant on Tuesday, January 6, 2026, and close the warrant on
   March 16, 2026… The motion passed unanimously (5-0-0)."* —
   `select-board/2026-01-06-minutes-7584.txt`
2. **Approve the Annual Town Election Warrant — the act that puts an override on the
   ballot.** *"…noting it includes all ballot vacancies and two override questions… The
   motion passed unanimously (4-0-0)."* — `select-board/2026-04-07-minutes-7752.txt`. The
   same passage records that *"the School Committee voted to co-sponsor the warrant article
   but did not vote on the override questions themselves."*
3. **Recommend on every article.** *"Motion to recommend approval of Article M, Tier 1…
   The motion carried 3-1, with Member McLeod opposed."* — same file.
4. **Approve the Town Meeting booklet.**
5. **Call a Special Town Meeting.** Charter Art. 2 §2-5, quoted on both 2026 STM warrants.
6. **Tax classification** — the residential/commercial split hearing, 25 Nov 2025 (agenda
   only; no minutes in the archive).

### What is in front of them

The Town Manager's package, the FinCom report (which they may not edit — *"Town Manager
Warren-Dyment clarified this was the Finance Committee's voted report and not within the
Select Board's lane to edit"*), and the booklet, whose contents are enumerated in the
7 April minutes: election warrant, two letters, the FinCom report, the warrant and article
index, the Moderator's ground rules, *"a multi-year budget comparison (FY24 expended through
FY27 Balanced, Tier 1, and Tier 2)"*, a revenue and expenditure balance sheet, and inserts.

### What would change their decision

**a. What the town's voters actually do with an override — from Lunenburg's own record, not
from theory.** They set the tier structure and the number. The Town Manager warned at the
12 January Tri-Board that *"voters historically tend to select the lowest tier offered"* —
a claim about voter behaviour, offered without a figure. `sources/data/ballot-questions.csv`
holds seven questions from 2012 to 2025 with precinct tallies, **and no page or analysis in
this project reads it.** The site has an `/overrides` page that models override arithmetic
without ever consulting the town's voting record.

**b. Whether the town-versus-school frame survives a like-for-like comparison.** This is the
Select Board's standing temptation and PERSONAS.md already names it. The 12 January Tri-Board
is the sharpest instance: the School Committee Chair — *"The problem isn't that we have a
shrinking pie. It's that all of the groups think that we have separate pie and we don't"* —
against a Select Board member raising the state audit as a precondition for supporting an
override. What would settle a piece of it is arithmetic the archive can do: the town-side
department that carries **$1,262,376 of school retiree health insurance** is a fact about
where a line sits, not about who spends.

**c. What retiring debt frees, and when.** The 12 January discussion establishes that
non-excluded debt falling off the levy — DPW building design, a ladder truck, the Saliba
acquisition, artificial turf — *"gets absorbed into operating expenditures rather than being
set aside for new non-excluded borrowing."* That is a schedule. `report_debt` holds 354 rows
of town debt by purpose, FY2011–FY2025, and **nothing reads it**; eight of sixteen editions
were never extracted at all.

**d. The tax-bill consequence of each tier, per household, at the town's own valuations.**
They vote the tier; the resident feels the bill. `report_valuation` holds tax rate, total
valuation, accounts and levy for FY2011–FY2019 and is unread and OCR-damaged.

## 3. The Finance Committee

Seven members, appointed. Charter §2-3 gives them *"the primary responsibility to report to
town meeting"* on the budget and *"all warrant articles having a fiscal impact on the town"*,
plus the G.L. c.39 §16 powers, plus the duty to *"hold 1 or more public hearings"* — all
quoted by Town Counsel in `finance-committee/2022-04-14-minutes-2072.txt`.

### The named votes

- **Open and close the statutory public hearing** — opened 19 March 2026, continued, closed
  26 March.
- **Article-by-article recommendations** in three shapes: recommend approval, find no
  financial impact, defer to Town Meeting. Thirty-odd roll calls on 2 April.
- **The budget and the override.** *"The Finance Committee recommends approval 4-1-1"* on
  Article L; *"recommends approval 4-2"* on each of Tier 1 (~$2.4M) and Tier 2 (~$3.3M) —
  `finance-committee/2026-04-06-minutes-7747.txt`.

  warrant. The Chair noted on 12 February that if it would not fit, *"a QR code will be
  provided to direct residents to a concise summary of the report."*

**And one contested fact above all the others.** On 27 January 2026 the FinCom Chair
displayed a chart of school staffing FY16–FY25:

> *"He stated that it 'really irritates me when we say things like we're cutting staff when
> the data shows that staffing has gone up almost every year in the last 10 years.' He also
> disclosed that he had requested this data prior to last year's override vote and that it
> had been withheld by the former superintendent until after town meeting."*
> — `finance-committee/2026-01-27-minutes-7619.txt`

and the School Committee Vice-Chair's answer, in the same paragraph:

> *"…FY25 saw a downward turn, and that FY26 included significant layoffs, which was not
> reflected in the chart. She also noted that the student population's needs have increased
> substantially, with more students on IEPs, more English language learners, and more
> students entering below benchmark, meaning that even flat staffing represents a reduction
> in effective capacity."*

**That exchange is the central factual dispute of the FY27 override, both sides are making
checkable claims, and this project has never published either series.** Section 5 item 1
is the work that answers it.

| date | vote | file |

Plus, every meeting: warrants, line-item transfers, donations, grant applications. And
periodically: fees. The FY26 cycle shows the pattern whole — *"motion to vote on bus fees…
The rates would be $180 for 1 child, $270 for 2 kids or more and a $50 reduced fee… Motion
passes 4:1"*, then *"motion to accept the superintendents recommended budget with the change
of the ski team continuation to be funded by the bus fee **$25,787,474** and 3.6%"*
(`school-committee/2025-03-12-minutes-7098.txt`).

### The decision shape, stated plainly

**The School Committee does not vote one budget. It votes three or four, and it votes which
one is the override ask.** Restoration / needs-based → core (the override number) → level
service → balanced (no override). The Finance Committee asked every department for the same
three-way split. `fy28/public/data/model.json` already carries exactly these four scenario
keys — `restoration`, `core`, `level_service`, `balanced` — **and the words "restoration
budget", "core budget" and "balanced budget" appear in none of the seventeen published
analyses.** The model speaks the boards' language; the writing does not.

### What would change their decision

**a. What a dollar buys, and what removing it removes.** They already produce this and
nobody has captured it. `sources/district-budget/text/balanced-budget-slides-3-23-26.txt`
is a line-by-line list of positions with dollar amounts and a stated service consequence —
*"2.0 Classroom FTE at Primary ($205,019) … Increases class sizes significantly at the
foundational level"*; *".2 Music teacher at Turkey Hill ($9,682) … Grade 5 band instruction
will be eliminated."* The same shape exists back to FY20
(`fy20-budget-update-recommended-reductions-as-of-march-6-2019.txt`, which also prints the
Town Manager's guideline for that year: *"Town Manager's guideline FY20 budget 2.5%"*).

**b. Whether the cut they voted last year actually happened.** A cut list is a proposal.
The ledger says what was spent. `fy26-closeout.md` already found one case going the other
way — a kindergarten paraprofessional line published at −100% against which $99,064 was
spent. Nobody has run the general version.

**c. What school choice is worth, in and out.** They voted to open choice seats at the high
school as a revenue measure. `money-gaps.csv` carries both halves as unanswerable: *"What
Lunenburg receives for a child who chooses IN, and what the fall from 51 to 15 is worth"*
and *"What Lunenburg pays in school choice sending tuition, and for how many children"*.
They are deciding a revenue question against a number nobody has computed.

**d. Who is in the classroom now, against who was there ten years ago.** See below; this is
the same series the Finance Committee dispute turns on, and it is the School Committee's
best evidence.

**The window that matters.** Anything that will change a number has to land **before the
Town Manager's preliminary budget in mid-February**, because after that the argument is
about a document rather than about the inputs. Anything that will change how a choice is
*framed* has until the Tri-Board in early March. After the warrant closes in mid-March,
nothing new can be voted at all.

**Two structural findings fall out of the calendar itself.**

1. **The budget is built against uncertified free cash.** Not a criticism — the state's
   certification calendar and the town's do not line up. But it means the FY28 budget will
   again be set against an estimate, and the size of the historical error between estimate
   and certification is a computable, useful, unbuilt number.
2. **Nothing in the archive is the Town Manager's budget instruction to departments.** The
   cycle's first act — what departments are told to submit, on what template, against what
   growth assumption — is described every autumn and produced in none of them. It is
   partially recoverable secondhand: the district's own slides print the guideline for at
   least FY20 and FY25.

# Part 4 — What we hold and nobody has used

TJ: *"there may be data we haven't analyzed yet even in the data we have."* There is, and
some of it is the best evidence in the archive for the arguments the boards are actually
having.

## The four that matter most

**1. `dese_enrollment` — 1,194 rows, 1992–2026, per school and district, and no page or
analysis reads it.** It carries the entire student-characteristics series. For the Lunenburg
district, from the table:

| | FY2008 | FY2016 | FY2026 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| total | 1,824 | 1,567 | 1,568 |
| students with disabilities | 258 (14.1%) | 299 (18.8%) | 258 (16.3%) |
| English learners | 3 | 23 | **70** |
| low income | 10.0% | — | 25.2% |

**This is the direct test of the FY27 override's central dispute.** The Finance Committee
Chair's claim is about headcount; the School Committee Vice-Chair's answer is about need.
Enrolment down about 14% since FY2008, English learners up more than twentyfold, low-income
share up two and a half times, students with disabilities up two points as a share. Neither
of them has had this table put in front of them.

**2. `dese_teacher_subject` (5,527 rows) and `dese_teacher_grade_subject` (8,467 rows),
2008–2026, both unread.** Teacher FTE by subject, by grade band, per school, with
`students_per_teacher` printed by the state. Lunenburg district FY2010: 120.4 FTE, 14.1
students per teacher. This is the class-size and course-availability series, and it is a
statutory return rather than a hand-built sheet — rule 13a's good side.

**3. `ballot_questions` — 7 rows, 2012–2025, with precinct tallies, unread by anything.**
The site has an `/overrides` page that models override arithmetic without ever consulting
what this town's voters have done. The 2012 override failed 1,084–2,275; May 2024 passed;
May 2025's citizens' petition was not put on the ballot by Town Meeting; May 2026's two
tiers both failed. The Town Manager's *"voters historically tend to select the lowest tier
offered"* is a testable claim about this town.

**4. `dese_teacher_program_area` (659 rows, 2008–2026) and `dese_educator_workforce`
(840 rows, 2021–2023), both unread.** State-published special-education teacher FTE, and
paraprofessional headcount with retention rates. `money_gaps` already registers *"Why DESE
special education teacher FTE falls from 18.5 to 2.0 while total teacher FTE holds flat"* —
the anomaly was seen, registered, and then nothing was built on the series. The model's
6.49% special-education escalator rests on a paraprofessional budget line that rule 11 says
cannot be distinguished from grant money; here is nineteen years of a differently-sourced
count sitting beside it.

### 1. Who is in the classroom, and who teaches them — FY1992 to FY2026

**The decision it informs.** The Finance Committee's override recommendation and the School
Committee's staffing argument. This is the one contested fact both bodies are arguing from
and neither can check.

**Half of it is already published, and that is why nobody has noticed the other half is
missing.** `/school-staffing` carries the DESE FTE series — paraprofessionals 0.94 per
hundred pupils in FY2013, the lowest of thirteen districts, to 4.27 in FY2025, the highest;
teacher FTE 120.2 in FY2009 to 105.1 in FY2025 — from `dese_measure`, FY2009–FY2025. So the
*staffing* half of the dispute is answerable today and partly answered.

**What is entirely unread is the student half, and the subject-level detail.**

**What it would establish.** Enrolment, students with disabilities, English learners and
low-income share, per school and district, **1992–2026**; teacher FTE and the state's own
students-per-teacher figure, **by school and by subject**, 2008–2026; and the two plotted
against each other so that "staffing rose" and "need rose faster" can each be true or false
separately rather than shouted past one another.

**What it reads.** `dese_enrollment` (1,194 rows, 166 for Lunenburg, unread by anything),
`dese_teacher_subject` (5,527, unread), `dese_teacher_grade_subject` (8,467, unread),
`dese_teacher_program_area` (659, unread), `dese_educator_workforce` (840, FY2021–FY2023
only, too short to trend). Beside the already-used `dese_measure`.

**Shape.** A drill-in page under rule 7b — conclusions, then the categorical breakdown by
school and subject, then the raw series. Three-year spans marked as such per the house rule
about what this town's boards will look at.

**What it must refuse.** It cannot say that a staffing level caused an outcome, and it
cannot convert "more English learners" into "more staff were needed" — that is a judgement
the state's foundation budget makes explicitly and the district's budget does not. It must
also carry `/school-staffing`'s own caution forward: **DESE counts staff paid from grants,
circuit breaker reimbursement and revolving funds alongside those the town appropriates**,
so the FTE series cannot say who pays for a rise.

**Effort.** Moderate. The data is loaded and reconciled; this is a page and a verifier.

### 1b. An enrolment projection — because nobody in Lunenburg has one

**The decision it informs.** Every one of them. Enrolment drives the foundation budget, the
Chapter 70 denominator, the per-pupil figure, the staffing argument and the building
decisions, and **no forward projection exists anywhere in this archive.**

**What was checked.** `NESDEC` appears in exactly one file in 3,877 documents — a reference
to a *1997 NESDEC Master Plan Study* in an FY20 symposium deck. `"projected enrollment"`,
`"enrollment projections"` and `"demographic study"` return zero hits across the searchable
meeting record. And `fy27-and-the-override.md` already lists *"Enrollment projections"* under
its own open questions, so the override work hit this same wall and recorded it.

**What the district has published, once.** A table headed `Enrollment Projections FY21-FY23`,
by grade and by school, actuals FY10–FY20 with FY21–FY23 projected, census date 1 October,
marked draft — in `superintendent-39-s-fy21-budget-identified-needs.txt`,
`superintendent-s-proposed-fy21-budget-presentation.txt` and
`budget-hearing-superintendent-fy22-recommended-budget.txt`. And the FY26 cycle prices
against one: *"OOD Tuition — Line adjustment due to Enrollment Projection Decrease… $310,000."*

**Two routes, and the first is the better one.**

1. **Extract the district's own projection and measure its error.** The FY21–FY23 grade table
   into a CSV with stage, document and page, checked against `dese_enrollment` actuals for
   the overlapping years. A measured forecast-error record for the only projection this
   district has published is worth more than a new projection, and it is somebody else's
   number rather than ours. Under rule 13a it is a hand-built table and stays `stated`.
2. **Build a cohort-survival projection.** `dese_enrollment` carries grade-level headcounts
   `pk_cnt` through `grade_12_cnt` plus `sp_cnt` for **FY1992–FY2026** — 35 years of input.
   That is far past what a board that will not look two years forward currently uses. But it
   would be **ours**, so rule 3 colouring, and a stated limit.

**What it cannot do.** It cannot split into stayers and leavers: *"Which grades Lunenburg
children leave the district in"* is a registered gap, because `dese_town_enrollment` has no
grade column.

**Effort.** Route 1 low; route 2 moderate.

# Part 7 — Stitching budgets to outcomes without doing damage

TJ: *"If we JUST look at budget information, we draw incorrect conclusions… Budgets connected
to school outcomes, with implications on decisions."* He is right, and this is also the place
this project could most easily produce the confident wrong thing it exists to argue against.

## The refusal, stated first so it cannot be walked back

**No spending-to-achievement relation may be published from this data.** The existing peer
work already establishes why: the peer set is this project's own choice with no documented
criterion (`money_gaps`, *"Why these six districts and not six others"*), the low-income
spread across those districts is wider than the spending spread, and whether Lunenburg's
MCAS results are high or low for Massachusetts is itself registered as unanswerable. A chart
with dollars on one axis and scores on the other, drawn on six self-selected districts,
would be indefensible however carefully it were captioned.

**And there is a finding hiding in that refusal.** Across every School Committee and Finance
Committee minute in 2025 and 2026, **MCAS appears in budget discussion essentially never** —
the hits are the student representative announcing test week, the removal of MCAS as a
graduation requirement, and one advisory-council report on eighth-grade civics scores. The
boards do not use achievement data to make budget decisions. What they use is *service
level*: class size, sections offered, courses that will actually run, positions kept,
ambulances staffed.

> *"kindergarten classrooms were operating with 25 students and no aides"* — School Committee
> Chair, 12 January 2026 Tri-Board
>
> *"The idea of it being one of us to 30 of them, I don't know how anyone could do that"* —
> public comment, School Committee, 23 March 2026
>
> *"this is a list of all courses that can be offered, however, does not mean all courses
> that will be run"* — LHS Principal presenting the Program of Studies, 4 February 2026

**So the bridge to build is dollars → service level, not dollars → outcomes.** That is what
the decision-makers already reason with, it is measurable, and it does not require a causal
claim about quality.

## The mechanical linkages that ARE establishable

These are arithmetic. They are what "budget data alone misleads" actually means, and they
are decision-relevant on their own. Verdicts checked against the database, not assumed.

| linkage | verdict |
|---|---|
| **Enrolment → Chapter 70 aid, required local contribution, foundation budget** | **COMPUTABLE TODAY**, FY2007–FY2026 (formula outputs FY1993–FY2026), and largely built at `/why-we-only-get-minimum-aid`. The aid recursion `aid(fy) = aid(fy−1) + Σ increments` reproduces **to the dollar in 16 of 19 differenceable years** |
| **What one more or one fewer pupil does to aid, right now** | **COMPUTABLE, and the answer surprises people.** Lunenburg has been on the **minimum-aid floor** in FY2022, FY2024, FY2025 and FY2026, so the marginal aid is the floor divided by foundation enrolment — **$150 per pupil in FY2026**, not the $13,770 average foundation budget. The floor is reset annually by the Legislature and has been $30, $60, $104 and $150 in the four years it bound. Anyone reasoning that losing students costs the town a pupil's share of the budget is out by two orders of magnitude |
| **A child moving to Monty Tech moves the bill, it does not add to it** | **ESTABLISHED**, `monty-tech.md`, from DESE's own apportionment sheet |
| **A Monty Tech pupil carries about half again the foundation budget of a Lunenburg one** — $20,827 against $13,770 in FY2026 | **ESTABLISHED**, same document |
| **Enrolment falling raises per-pupil spending with nothing bought** | **ESTABLISHED**, `per-pupil-spending.md`, with the identity `(1+spending growth) ÷ (1+pupil growth) = (1+per-pupil growth)` checked per district FY2012→FY2025 |

Of $2,670,158 of grant funding that fell in FY2025, $1,127,647 fell where the general fund rose and $1,542,511 fell where nothing did |

Currently a registered gap, and the only blocked item on this list that is only blocked by a fetch |

**Three cautions that apply to anything built from the above.**

- **`nss_stage` in `dese_ch70_formula` is `budgeted` for FY2025–FY2026 and `actual` before.**
  Rule 1 forbids differencing across it.
- **Every `*-history.csv` carries `stage` — `proposed | settled | restated` — and
  `documents_disagree`.** `restated` was renamed from `actual` on 7 September 2026 precisely
  because the old name produced a written claim that twelve years of school actuals were
  available. They are the district's budget book, not the ledger.
- **The DESE tables stop before the live budget year.** Chapter 70, enrolment and circuit
  breaker run to FY2026; `dese_measure` and `dese_function_expenditure` stop at FY2025.
  **FY2027 Chapter 70 is not in this archive at all**, which is why
  `fy27-and-the-override.md` asks for it by name.

## The one honest sentence about outcomes

Where an outcome measure is used at all, it should be the district's own service statement,
quoted — *"Grade 5 band instruction will be eliminated"* — attached to the dollar figure the
district attached it to. That is a claim somebody accountable made in public about their own
budget. It is not our inference, and it is exactly the kind of thing a person voting at Town
Meeting is entitled to see beside the number.

# Part 8 — A proposed shape for the One Big Report

**Organised by decision, not by subject.** Not built here; this is the argument to have
before anything is written.

The site today has `/what-it-all-adds-up-to`, which collects 46 conclusions from 16 reports.
It is organised by topic — special education, where the money comes from, what the money
buys, where the children are, athletics. That is the shape of the archive, not the shape of
a decision.

**Proposed shape: five decisions, each answered.**

> **1. What is the gap, and what is driving it?**
> Not a list of things that grew. The pull calculation from rule 4 — share of budget times
> excess over the levy cap — and the fact that in FY27 two health insurance lines alone ate
> all the new revenue. Non-discretionary first, discretionary second, and the arithmetic
> that shows which is which.
>
> **2. What does each budget scenario buy, and what does it remove?**
> Restoration, core, level service, balanced — the boards' own four words. What each costs
> a household. What each removes, in the district's own published consequence statements.
> This is the cut register doing its job.
>
> **3. Who is in our classrooms, and who is teaching them?**
> Enrolment, need and staffing, plotted honestly, 2008–2026. The answer to the argument the
> Finance Committee and School Committee had in January and could not settle.
>
> **4. What can this town actually change, and what is set elsewhere?**
> Chapter 70 is a formula. The Monty Tech required minimum is 95.2% state-calculated. The
> levy grows 2.5%. Health insurance is a market. Against that: fees, service levels, capital
> timing, choice seats, the apportionment convention, and the override. Rank by pull, never
> by size — rule 4.
>
> **5. What would we have had to know, and when, to have decided differently?**
> PERSONAS.md's Finance Committee test, promoted to a section. The calendar in Part 2, the
> gap register, and the named documents. This is where "what we cannot answer" earns its
> place: not as a caveat but as next year's records request.

**Three rules for it.**

- **Rule 7a at the top.** No context section. The gap, then the scenarios.
- **Every section names the vote it informs and the date that vote is taken.** A finding
  with no vote attached goes in a reference page, not this one.
- **Rule 7 line held everywhere.** A figure is a fact; an explanation is a hypothesis; and
  a service consequence quoted from the district is the district's claim, labelled.

# Part 10 — Established from the record, versus inferred

**Established, with a file path and a quotation in the text above:** every named vote and its
wording; the FY27 calendar; the three-to-four budget scenario structure; the Tri-Board's
composition and that it deliberates rather than votes; the apportionment convention and its
figures; the Finance Committee's information demands and their dates; the staffing-chart
dispute and both sides of it; the FY27 override tiers, recommendations and failure; the two
2026 Special Town Meetings and the 17 September warrant deadline; the charter sections, as
quoted by Town Counsel inside Finance Committee minutes; the district's published cut lists
with dollar figures and consequence statements; the row counts, spans and sample values of
every table named in Parts 4 and 7.

**Inferred, and offered as judgement rather than finding:**

- **That the mid-February preliminary budget is the last point at which new information can
  change a number.** That is my reading of the calendar's shape, not a rule anybody stated.
- **That the boards do not use achievement data in budget decisions.** Established as an
  absence across 2025–2026 minutes, which is weaker than a presence. A grep that finds
  nothing is not proof nobody said it — 3,116 of 12,015 meeting documents have no text at
  all, and the FY27 budget workshops have no minutes.
- **The ranking in Part 5.** Mine, argued from Part 1, and the thing most worth arguing with.
- **That the cut register is rule-7 safe because the consequence is the district's own
  claim.** I believe this is right and it is exactly the kind of reasoning that has gone
  wrong here before. A quoted claim is still a claim, and the register must say so on every
  row rather than in a preamble.
- **That PERSONAS.md should not be extended to cover this.** A judgement about two documents
  doing two jobs.

**Two corrections made while writing this, kept in the text per the standing practice.**

1. **The first draft of Part 5 said the staffing series was unbuilt.** It is half built:
   `/school-staffing` already publishes DESE teacher and paraprofessional FTE for
   FY2009–FY2025. What is unread is the *student* half and the subject-level detail. Getting
   that wrong would have specified a piece of work that partly exists.
2. **The first draft of Part 7 said quantifying the grant-to-town shift needs the End of
   Year Financial Report.** It needs it for a named post or a named line. At **function**

   revolving for FY2009–FY2025, and `/when-grants-end` publishes the decomposition. The
   CLAUDE.md sentence saying nobody publishes the fund split predates that ingest and is now
   too strong as written.

**Not established and must not be restated as fact:**

- That staffing rose, or that it fell. Both were asserted in the same meeting from different

  absent; nothing in the record says why.

  marginal rate **while the town sits on the minimum-aid floor**, and the Legislature resets
  that floor annually — it has been $30, $60, $104 and $150 in the four years it bound.
- That the FY27 override failed *because* of any of this. The vote counts are not in the
