# The audit pass: find the argument before writing the summary

**Written 10 October 2026, from a second pass over `/analysis/transportation`.** TJ:

> *"the summary at the top feels like a nothing-burger. they seem like facts that dont tell
> any story or help me really understand whats going on. we need to find a model of how to
> process data that speaks to the biggest points in the data for that report... we need to
> find the contentious points and surface them, not just show blank metrics."*

The same complaint was made on 9 September (`WHAT-DECIDERS-NEED.md`: *"interesting but not
clear as to why they are important"*). That document fixed it from the reader's side, by
asking what each board decides. This one fixes it from the data's side, because the cause
is upstream of the writing: **a summary is a nothing-burger when the analysis never
compared anything.** A share, a total or a trend with nothing set against it is an
inventory. Nobody argues with an inventory, so nobody remembers it.

The transportation page's first card was *"Athletic buses were 6.4% of school
transportation spending in FY2026."* True, verified, and it answers nobody. The same data,
read as an auditor reads it, says the special education bus line closed FY2026 $67,556
past its budget with no transfer to cover it — and the page never said so.

## Where it sits

Between step 2 (*find the shape*) and step 3 (*write both halves*) of
`WRITING-AN-ANALYSIS.md`. Data in, decomposed, **then audited**, then written. Run it
before a single conclusion card is drafted, because a card drafted first gets defended.

## The eight tests

Run every one against the report's data. Each is a COMPARISON, and that is the point: a
finding is a number set against something it was supposed to equal.

| # | test | compare | the transportation case |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Budget credibility** | voted against spent, every line, every year. Look for a DIRECTION held for years, not one bad year | special education transport under budget six years running, then over |
| 2 | **Control** | spent against the REVISED budget. A line that closes the year overdrawn with no covering transfer; a transfer in to a line that then ends unspent | FY2024 both school-day lines past revised by $112,490; FY2026 special education by $67,556 |
| 3 | **Who pays moved** | the same cost across payers: general fund, a revolving fund, families, the state. Follow it into the fund it left | buses moved onto the town line; the fee fund went from deficit to surplus |
| 4 | **Said against did** | every quote on the record that states a number, a cause or an intention, against the ledger for the same year | "can not support" vs the fund's FY2025 receipts; "corrected" vs the FY2027 plan |
| 5 | **Locked-in growth** | contract escalators, step schedules, statutory rates against the 2.5% levy cap (rule 4), and against the RATE THE MODEL USES | contract +7.4% into FY2028; model assumes 6% |
| 6 | **Two sources, one quantity** | any figure printed twice: two sheets, DESE against the ledger, a minute against the book. The spread is the finding (rule 13a) | the two by-sport sheets disagree by 28% on the same sports |
| 7 | **An offset that masks** | net movement against GROSS movement (step 2). A cut on one line hiding growth on another | athletic buses zeroed; school-day buses +11.2% underneath |
| 8 | **Thin margin on a volatile line** | the next budget against the latest actual, for a line that has overrun recently. A comparison, never a projection (rule 1) | FY2027 special education transport is $16,662 above what FY2026 spent |

**And three cheap ones that catch sheet defects**, worth a minute each: identical figures in
paired rows (an allocation, not a measurement); a printed total that does not foot; a year
that is cheaper than the one before under a contract that only escalates.

## Ranking what leads

Every test produces candidates. The summary gets the ones that pass **the argument test:
would a member of the board that decides this line dispute the sentence, or have to answer
for it?** A sentence nobody would dispute is context; it goes below the fold or nowhere.

Then order by **dollars at stake, weighted by whether somebody can act on it this budget
season.** A $67,556 overdraft on a line being set for FY2028 now outranks a $3,592 transfer
in FY2012.

A card that leads must carry:

- **the comparison in the metric itself** — "$67,556 past its budget", never "$633,291";
- **who it matters to** — the board whose decision it touches, implicitly or by name;
- **its epistemic label** where it carries a cause (rule 7). The measurement leads; the
  explanation is a hypothesis, and says so.

## What this is not

**It is not permission to accuse.** Rule 8 still holds: this app explains how to fix the
problem. The audit pass is how findings are FOUND; rule 8 is how they are WRITTEN. So:

- a variance is a **question the budget has to answer**, not a charge — "the FY2028 line
  has to say which of these it is";
- an inconsistency between a statement and the ledger is shown **as two measured things
  side by side**, with the reconciling explanation named if one exists (the fee fund's
  FY2025 surplus came AFTER costs were reclassified out of it — say so in the same card);
- **credit where the ledger shows it.** Regular routes now sit on a fixed-price contract
  the budget carries to the dollar; that is a control working, and it belongs on the page.

**And it is not a licence to find a story where there is none.** If all eight tests come
back clean, the honest summary says the line behaves, and that is a finding too — rarer
than it should be, and worth one card.

## The test of the finished summary

Read the cards aloud to someone who has not seen the page. If they ask *"so what?"* after
any one, that card is an inventory. Replace it with the comparison that would have
answered them.
