Analysis, November 2026. Ranks are as each document prints them; anything marked *as heard* is from machine captions of a meeting recording, which are a finding aid, not a record.

## What it shows

**The two times the capital committee ranked the field, it put it 21 of 29, then 19 of 35.** Both years the Town Manager’s preliminary plan listed it among requests not included.

FY2024: a $50,000 feasibility study, Capital Planning Committee priority 21 of 29, Town Manager priority 18 of 29. FY2025: $200,000 for survey and drainage, priority 19 of 35 and 15 of 35. Both appear on the page headed as requests not included in the preliminary recommendation.

*Rests on:* The Town Manager’s preliminary FY2024 and FY2025 capital plan decks, which print the TM and CPC priority beside each request; for FY2025 the CPC’s own workbook agrees (sheet Ordered). *Does not show:* Whether the voted FY2024 and FY2025 plans included it: their item lists were not readable in the text we hold. For FY2024 the committee’s own sheet prints no rank number and ties the field on average with another request, sorting it one place higher; the rank is the Town Manager deck’s. And a rank says where it stood against that year’s other requests, not why.

**The schools ranked the field 5 of 6 for FY2024 and 7 of 9 for FY2025; by FY2028 it was first.** The FY2027 order, 4 of 8, and the FY2028 one were heard on recordings; no printed list is held.

These are the School Committee’s own priority orders for what it sent to the town, a different list from the town committee’s ranking. For FY2026 the field was not on the schools’ ranked list at all; the plan put its money a year later.

*Rests on:* The schools’ FY24 and FY25 capital presentations (printed rank tables); School Committee recordings of 1 October 2025 and 21 October 2026 (machine captions, a finding aid). *Does not show:* Why the order changed. Nothing here tests it: the field’s condition, other requests being funded, and a change in who sat on the committee all fit the same sequence.

**Both votes for field money were separate articles: $770,000 in 2016, $195,830 in 2026.** Neither came through a ranked capital plan; the 2026 result is as heard, the official one is not held.

In 2016 Town Meeting borrowed $770,000 by its own article while that year’s capital plan said it had no request to bond. In November 2026 a Special Town Meeting moved $195,830 from the general stabilization fund for engineering, 57 to 18 on the electronic count as the captions read it.

*Rests on:* The FY2016 annual town report (Article 28 and the FY2017 capital plan); the Special Town Meeting warrant of 17 November 2026 and the meeting’s recording. *Does not show:* The official 2026 tally and any board’s recommendation on it: no Town Clerk record or minutes of that meeting are held yet.

**The field’s estimate went from $800,000 in 2015 to $2,420,000 in the schools’ 2025 plan.** The 2025 figure adds hillside hardscape and drainage the 2015 one did not name.

The September 2025 draft splits it: $920,000 for hardscape and drainage, $1,500,000 for replacing the track and turf. The like-for-like part, turf and track, is $1,500,000 against the $770,000 Town Meeting borrowed in 2016.

*Rests on:* Capital Planning Committee minutes of 27 October 2015; the School Committee’s revised draft FY27-FY35 capital plan, September 2025. *Does not show:* What the work would cost if bid now: both are planning estimates, and a later figure of about three million dollars was only spoken at a meeting.
