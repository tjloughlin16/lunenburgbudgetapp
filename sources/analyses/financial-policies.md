# Financial policies: the rules, the draft, and the figures

*An analysis written by this project. Unlisted while it is reviewed. Built by `scripts/build_financial_policies.py`; every figure is recomputed by `scripts/verify_financial_policies.py`.*

## The short version

- On the latest certified free cash, **$3,354,370** (certified 3/11/2026, available in FY2026), the state’s draft policies would keep back **$670,874**, save **$838,592** and spend **$920,874** on set uses: **$2,430,340** in all, 72% of it. Today’s rules decide one share: **$335,437** to the retiree health trust. Each point of a share is worth $33,544.
- The draft sets **9 of 16** dials by rule that today’s policies leave open or set lower (our reading of the two documents).
- General stabilization was **6.24%** of the FY2025 budget, inside the draft’s five to eight percent, and 5th of 9 among the towns compared.
- The retiree health trust held **$1,736,976** against a liability of **$57,398,019** at 30 June 2025: **3.03%** funded. Paying the full actuarially determined contribution would have taken $1,943,838 more in FY2025.
- The 22 policy titles on the Finance Committee’s 3 December 2026 agenda match the April 2024 draft title for title. No policy text is attached to the agenda.

**This project recommends neither direction**: not to save more and not to spend more. It shows what each setting would do to figures the town has already published. The boards decide.

## Today’s rules against the draft

| Policy | Today (Select Board policies, Article III) | The draft (DLS, April 2024) | State law |
|---|---|---|---|
| Free cash | Stabilization to five percent, then at least 10% to OPEB, then special funds, then one-time uses | Target of five to seven percent of the budget; a minimum share for each use (table above) | Spent by Town Meeting once the state certifies it |
| General stabilization | At least five percent of the budget; all funds no more than ten percent; refill within about three years | Five to eight percent; a yearly deposit of 0.25% (summary) or 0.3% (text) of the budget; below five percent take at most one third and refill within two years | Any amount may go in; spending takes two thirds (c. 40, §5B) |
| Special purpose funds | Deposit sized in capital planning; two thirds to spend | Three to five percent of the budget together; at least 10% of free cash | Spending takes a majority (c. 40, §5B) |
| Reserve fund | $50,000, at most one half of one percent of the budget | No size set | Up to five percent of last year’s levy; the Finance Committee votes transfers (c. 40, §6) |
| OPEB | At least 10% of certified free cash | Keeps the 10% as one option; intends to pay the full actuarially determined contribution; a valuation every two years | Funding allowed, not required (c. 32B, §20) |
| Capital | A project is anything over $20,000 | Two to three percent of the budget, net of debt, in cash; 20% to 30% of free cash | |
| Debt | Regular debt service at most 4% of the regular levy; all at most 14% of the total levy | Within the levy 2% to 4% of the budget; all debt 5% to 11% of operating revenues | |
| Revenue estimates | No written rule | Local receipts at most 90% of last year’s collections without firm evidence; check each forecast against the year that followed | |

Today’s stabilization section still quotes two caps (ten percent of the prior year’s tax levy for a deposit, ten percent of equalized value in total) that the current text of c. 40, §5B does not contain. It also asks two thirds to spend from any stabilization fund, where the statute asks a majority for a special purpose fund.

What the law allows and what the state recommends are kept apart here. The statute sets the limits in the last column. The draft’s targets are what the Division of Local Services recommends **for towns like Lunenburg**, and the draft itself says the Town is to test them against its own finances before adopting them (pdf page 3). The Government Finance Officers Association recommends unrestricted general fund balance of no less than two months of regular revenues or expenditures ([GFOA, 2015](https://www.gfoa.org/materials/fund-balance-guidelines-for-the-general-fund)); that counts free cash and stabilization together. The FY2025 audit puts total general fund balance at $12,373,908, 25% of general fund expenditures.

## What this does not establish

- Whether the draft the Finance Committee will review is the April 2024 manual unchanged. The agenda lists the same titles and attaches no text.
- Which FY2025 general stabilization figure is right: the annual report’s or the audit’s.
- What the liability looks like in any actuarial valuation after the two held (2017 and 2018).
- Where the reserve fund’s $50,000 figure came from; no record found gives its origin.
- What the FY2028 figures will be. The worked example uses the latest certification; the next (as of 1 July 2026) was not in the state databank on 25 November 2026.
