Rationales 
 
Primary School Reading Specialist 
Currently, our K-2 class sizes are approaching thirty students. Research consistently shows that 
the optimal class size for maximizing foundational literacy and math skills in early primary 
grades (K-3) is no larger than 18 students. In classrooms of thirty, the variance in reading 
abilities is vast. For general education teachers, it becomes mathematically impossible to 
provide the individualized, one-on-one attention and frequent feedback that young, developing 
readers require. Consequently, students with early foundational gaps, particularly in phonemic 
awareness and decoding, are at high risk of falling behind because their specific needs cannot 
be fully addressed during whole-group instruction. 
Adding a dedicated Reading Teacher is a critical structural adjustment necessary to counteract 
the adverse effects of large class sizes. Research highlights several distinct advantages to this 
approach: 
● Research demonstrates that a child's brain is far more receptive to reading instruction in 
the early years. Catching and addressing reading difficulties in K-2 is significantly easier 
and more effective than attempting remediation in upper elementary or middle school. 
Strong intervention in these primary grades changes a child’s entire academic trajectory. 
 
● Studies indicate that students receiving reading interventions in small, targeted groups 
consistently outperform struggling readers who only receive whole-class instruction. A 
Reading Specialist can pull small groups (Tier 2 and Tier 3) to deliver explicit, systematic 
===PAGE 2===
phonics instruction, ensuring higher learning gains through focused dosage and 
increased engagement that is impossible to hide from in a small setting. 
 
● Specialized literacy professionals have the advanced training required to administer 
diagnostic assessments ,such as DIBELS, pinpoint exact skill gaps, and continuously 
monitor progress. This targeted approach prevents over-teaching skills students already 
know and focuses intensely on the specific orthographic or phonological barriers holding 
them back. 
While we cannot immediately reduce general classroom ratios to the ideal number of 18, we can 
provide the targeted support our students need. A dedicated K-2 Reading Specialist is not just a 
supplementary role; it is a necessary investment. By implementing evidence-based, small-group 
interventions, we can attempt to close early learning gaps and relieve the instructional burden 
on our general education teachers. 
THES Reading Specialist  
As students enter grades 3-5, academic demands shift dramatically. Students encounter 
increasingly complex texts in all subject areas, including science, social studies, and math, and 
are expected to independently comprehend this dense material. Research widely documents 
the "fourth-grade slump," a period where students lacking foundational reading fluency and 
vocabulary rapidly fall behind their peers when faced with this heightened rigor. 
At THES, this challenge is compounded by class sizes approaching thirty students. In an upper-
elementary classroom of thirty, the variance in reading proficiency is incredibly vast—often 
spanning multiple grade levels. For a general education teacher, providing the intensive, 
differentiated instruction required to close these gaps while managing a large cohort is 
mathematically and structurally prohibitive. Without targeted intervention, struggling readers in 
grades 3-5 risk permanent academic marginalization. 
To address the challenges of large class sizes and complex upper-elementary literacy 
demands, THES requires the clinical expertise of a certified Reading Specialist. A dedicated 
specialist provides the essential, evidence-based interventions that cannot be delivered during 
whole-group instruction in a class of thirty. 
 
● A specialist is trained to administer advanced diagnostic assessments to identify the root 
causes of reading struggles (e.g., deficits in advanced phonics, multisyllabic decoding, 
morphology, or complex comprehension). They provide intense, Tier 2 and Tier 3 small-
group instruction tailored to these specific deficits. 
● By remediating these targeted skills, the specialist ensures that students can access the 
rigorous grade-level content being taught in their general education classrooms. Without

this bridge, students who struggle with reading effectively lose access to instruction in 
science, history, and word-problem-based mathematics. 
● A Reading Specialist also serves as a critical resource for general education teachers. 
They can help classroom teachers interpret reading data and implement whole-class 
scaffolding strategies to better support the diverse reading levels within a class of thirty. 
A class size of thirty in grades 3-5 presents significant barriers to individualized reading 
instruction, but it is a barrier we can mitigate. By investing in a dedicated Reading Specialist, we 
provide a critical safety net for our most vulnerable learners. This expert instruction will help 
support THES students to have the reading comprehension skills necessary to access complex 
text. 
.5 Assistant Principal 
There is a need to transition the High School Assistant Principal position from a 0.5 Full-Time 
Equivalent (FTE) to a 1.0 FTE. This investment is essential to ensure a safe, supportive, and 
effective learning environment for our students and staff, addressing immediate challenges in 
school climate, student behavior, and administrative capacity. 
Recruiting highly qualified administrative candidates for a part-time (0.5 FTE) position is 
inherently difficult.Experienced and highly effective administrators typically seek full-time 
employment for career stability and benefits. A 0.5 FTE position significantly limits our ability to 
attract and retain top-tier talent, often leading to high turnover in a role that requires 
consistency. 
Converting this role to a 1.0 FTE ensures we can recruit from a robust pool of dedicated 
professionals committed to the long-term success of our high school, fostering stability in our 
leadership team. 
Recent data indicates a direct correlation between administrative staffing levels and staff 
morale.Our High School Climate and Culture Survey demonstrated that staff feel significantly 
less supported without a full-time Assistant Principal present. 
A 0.5 FTE limits the Assistant Principal’s ability to be a consistent, visible, and proactive 
presence in the building. A full-time administrator is necessary to provide timely responses to 
staff needs, offer consistent coaching, and maintain the strong, supportive culture necessary for 
effective teaching and learning. 
The reduction in support structures has directly impacted student behavior and well-being. 
We have observed a marked increase in student behavioral issues. This correlates directly with 
the minimal support we are currently able to provide. The loss of crucial support systems, such 
as our Bridge program, has exacerbated these issues, leaving a gap in services for our most 
vulnerable students. A 1.0 FTE Assistant Principal is vital to implementing proactive behavioral 
interventions, managing disciplinary issues effectively, and ensuring that students receive the 
timely support they need to succeed academically and socially. A part-time administrator can

our students require. 
52 Ignite Tutoring Seats Primary 
The consensus among early literacy researchers is unequivocal: if students do not achieve 
reading proficiency by the end of first grade, the likelihood of them catching up diminishes 
significantly. A 2025 study from the Center for Research and Reform in Education at Johns 
Hopkins University (CRRE) highlights this urgency, finding that 88% of students who are not 
reading at benchmark by the end of first grade remain behind a year later; only 1 in 8 catch up 
without intensive, targeted intervention. 
Conversely, the same study found that when students received effective early intervention and 
exited first grade on benchmark, 85% maintained their reading proficiency through the end of 
second grade without further intervention. This demonstrates that K-2 is not just a time for 
foundational learning; it is the critical window where educational gaps are either closed 
permanently or allowed to widen. 
Ignite is built upon the proven framework of high-dosage, near-peer tutoring. Research 
consistently identifies high-dosage tutoring, defined as small group or 1:1 instruction, occurring 
at least three times a week for 30 minutes, or daily for 15 minutes, embedded within the school 
day as one of the most effective strategies for learning acceleration. 
● The Johns Hopkins CRRE evaluation of the Ignite Reading program in Massachusetts 
(2023-2024) found that participating first graders achieved an average of 5.4 additional 
months of learning compared to national benchmarks. 
● The CRRE study calculated an effect size of +0.21 to +0.23 SD for the Ignite virtual 
tutoring model. This effect size is comparable to, or exceeds, the impact of large-scale, 
in-person tutoring programs, validating the efficacy of their structured virtual model 
● Ignite's targeted, 1:1 approach produces consistent gains across diverse student 
populations. Research indicates that multilingual learners (MLLs), students with IEPs, 
and students of color experience the same accelerated growth in foundational reading 
skills as their peers. 
● Ignite’s curriculum and tutoring methodology are heavily grounded in the Science of 
Reading, focusing explicitly on systematic phonics, phonemic awareness, decoding, and 
early fluency—the exact skills required to crack the alphabetic code in K-2 
While national research provides the foundation for this model, our own data from the previous 
academic year provides the most compelling case for continuing Ignite seats at the Primary 
School.  
Investing in Ignite tutoring seats for our K-2 students is a proactive, evidence-aligned strategy to 
prevent reading failure. The model’s proven ability to deliver over five months of additional 
learning, combined with the measurable success our own students experienced last year, 
justifies the continued allocation of resources to this program.

.4 Music Teacher LHS 
Currently, at 0.6 FTE, our ability to offer a diverse and comprehensive music curriculum is 
constrained. Increasing this position to full-time will directly address this limitation by: 
● Allowing the introduction of new music electives that cater to a wider range of student 
interests and skill levels. 
● Providing more sections of popular existing classes and ensuring more students have 
the opportunity to participate in the arts. 
●  Providing the educator with the necessary time to build and sustain a stronger, more 
visible music program within the school community. 
A key component of this proposal is utilizing a portion of the increased FTE to address a 
significant scheduling hurdle. The music teacher will teach one section outside of her primary 
certification area, specifically within the Physical Education department. 
● This strategic placement will directly help seniors fulfill their mandatory PE requirements, 
which are often difficult to schedule due to competing priorities. 
● By opening up a crucial PE section, we alleviate a major scheduling bottleneck. This 
flexibility is critical for upperclassmen. 
●  With their PE requirement satisfied more easily, seniors will have the schedule 
availability to enroll in advanced classes (AP, Honors) or other electives aligned with 
their post-secondary goals. 
Moving the Music Teacher position from 0.6 to 1.0 FTE is not merely an investment in the music 
department; it is a strategic maneuver that benefits the entire school's master schedule. It 
enriches our arts offerings while providing essential flexibility that allows our senior students to 
maximize their academic potential and pursue their individual areas of interest. The return on 
investment in student opportunity and schedule optimization makes this a highly advantageous 
decision.
