ABOUT US:
Nashoba Associated Boards of Health
(NABH) serves as the Board of Health
Agent for 16 member towns, providing
environmental and public health
services to the district. We are proud
to be one of the first regional public
health departments in Massachusetts.
Member communities include:
Ashburnham, Ashby, Ayer, Berlin,
Bolton, Boxborough, Dunstable,
Groton, Harvard, Lancaster, Littleton,
Lunenburg, Pepperell, Shirley, Stow,
Townsend, and the community of
Devens.
NABH'S MISSION
Nashoba Associated Boards of Health provides public health
services to member communities in North Central Massachusetts, in
partnership with each community's local Board of Health. We are
dedicated to serving all our community residents of any age,
particularly the underserved and at-risk. Our goal is to promote
healthy people, healthy families, and a healthy environment through
compassionate care, education, enforcement, and prevention.

ENVIRONMENTAL HEALTH SERVICES
The Environmental Division enforces State Sanitary and
Environmental Codes, Massachusetts General Laws, and
local regulations in partnership with state agencies like the
Department of Public Health (DPH) and the Department of
Environmental Protection (DEP). Our services include
housing inspections, sewage disposal systems, restaurant
inspections, public pools, bathing beaches, rabies control,
wells, and rubbish haulers.
Our staff collaborates with local Boards of Health and the
general public to address environmental and public health
concerns across the sixteen towns in the Nashoba region.
Additionally, our health agents are available in the Town
Hall of member communities and at the office in Ayer to
assist the public with questions or concerns they may have.

The Public Health Nursing team at Nashoba Associated Boards of Health aims to promote wellness
across all ages in our sixteen member communities following the Scope and Standards of Public Health
Nursing as outlined by the American Nurses Association. 
Core Services Provided:
We hosted 17 flu clinics throughout the year, providing community members with accessible flu
shots. These clinics played a key role in reducing the spread of influenza and safeguarding the
health of our neighbors.
Investigated Communicable Diseases:
We worked closely with the Mass. Department of Public Health to investigate and manage
communicable diseases.
Town Nurse Hours:
We held regular “town nurse hours” across the district, offering guidance, advice, and care to
local residents.
Information & Resource Referrals:
We provided clear health information and connected individuals with essential community
resources.
Consultation, Education & Outreach:
We conducted personalized consultations and community education sessions to empower
residents to make informed health decisions.
Outbreak Response:
When health emergencies arose, we responded promptly to contain and manage outbreaks.
Home Visits:
We carried out home visits to ensure that those who could not visit our clinics still received the
care they needed.

In 2024, the Nashoba Associated Boards of Health (NABH) continued to advance regional public
health through the ongoing support of two key grants: the Public Health Excellence (PHE) Grant
and the Contact Tracing and Case Investigation (CT/CI) Grant. Awarded in 2022, these grants have
supported the gradual expansion of NABH’s staff, strengthening its capacity to deliver critical
public health services.
 
Since 2022, the PHE Grant has enabled the onboarding of staff over time, including Kelli M. Calo,
Shared Services Coordinator (part-time); Kolby Beauvais and Cara Olejarz, Health Agents (full-
time); Jenna Montgomery, Public Health Educator/Communications Specialist (full-time); and
Meghan O'Neil, Food Inspector (part-time). This growing team has enhanced NABH’s ability to
provide inspections, community outreach, and health education, fostering a more robust and
adaptable public health infrastructure across participating municipalities.
 
The CT/CI Grant has further supported NABH by funding a full-time Public Health Nurse, Alicia
Lepardo, and enabling enhancements to the regional public health database, incorporating
advanced analytical tools to improve data tracking and reporting.
 
Together, these grants have strengthened NABH’s ability to meet evolving community needs,
improving disease surveillance, emergency preparedness, and equitable access to high-quality
public health services. Through a collaborative and resource-sharing approach, NABH has built a
resilient and modern public health system that continues to effectively serve all member
communities.

2024 Highlights
Nashoba Associated Boards of Health (NABH) achieved significant milestones in 2024, emphasizing public health innovation,community engagement, and regional collaboration.
Mental Health and Grief Support
Certified 1 staff as a Mental Health First Aid Instructor, leading two Adult Mental Health First
Aid certification classes to strengthen mental health awareness and response capacity.
Secured funding for a pilot grief support program through the Haley Cremer Foundation,
Emerson Health, and the Community Foundation of North Central MA.
Delivered a Grief & Healing presentation at the MA Women of Color Coalition North Central
MA Chapter Regional Meeting.
Community Health Engagement
Hosted Community Health Focus Groups across eight municipalities, gathering critical insights
on local health priorities.
Co-organized the Bridges to Health: Building Thriving Community Partnerships conference, in
collaboration with CHNA9, MPHA, MAHB, and OLRH, to advance regional collaboration and
health equity.
Participated in transportation planning for North Central MA through the “Future of Mobility”
initiative.
Worked with CHNA9 and the Healthy Aging Collaborative on age and dementia-friendly
initiatives in the region.
Health Care Access and Advocacy
Advocated against the proposed closure of Nashoba Valley Medical Center through public
hearings, local meetings, and attended a press conference, highlighting its critical role in regional
healthcare.
Launched the Nashoba Valley Health Care Access Visioning Project in partnership with CHNA9,
ASNCM, and HCFA, including visioning sessions focused on older adults, veterans, and the
general public.
Public Health Capacity Building
Conducted three opioid abatement funding technical assistance sessions for NABH
municipalities, supporting their efforts to utilize opioid settlement funds effectively.
Joined the State’s Community Naloxone Program (CNP) to improve regional access to life-saving
overdose reversal tools.
Participated in HRIA Overdose Rescue Training-of-Trainers to expand overdose prevention and
response capabilities.
Coordinated a State-sponsored flu and COVID vaccine clinic to increase vaccination rates.

Regional Collaboration and Leadership
Participated in the inaugural Nashoba Valley Health Planning Working Group to advance long-
term health planning efforts.
Initiated a virtual regional monthly meeting for Council on Aging (COA) leaders to enhance
communication and collaboration across municipalities.
Hosted the Annual COA & Outreach Appreciation Luncheon to recognize and celebrate the
contributions of local outreach teams.
Participated in key statewide public health conferences, including the Massachusetts Health
Officers Association (MHOA) and Massachusetts Public Health Alliance (MPHA) annual
conferences.
As we look back on 2024, Nashoba Associated Boards of Health extends our sincere appreciation
to our member communities for their trust, collaboration, and dedication. Through our collective
efforts, we tackled key public health challenges, strengthened local resources, and laid the
foundation for future initiatives that will continue to benefit the region.
As we move into 2025, we remain committed to serving our municipalities and fostering strong
