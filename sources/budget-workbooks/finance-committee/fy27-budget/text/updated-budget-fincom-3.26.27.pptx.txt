Reductions in Balanced Budget
Select Board: Contracted Services-  Level funded at $6,000.  Preliminary budget funded at $13,500.
Restored in Tier 1- This line was funded in the preliminary budget at $13,500.  The Town of Lunenburg contracts with John Guilfoil Public Relations LLC to provide professional crisis communication, media relations, and strategic content support. The Tier 1 request reflects the actual annual cost of maintaining the full service agreement. Funding the line at the FY26 level would require reducing or eliminating these services.
Town Manager: Contracted Services-  Reduced to $6,000 from level fund of $10,000
Restored in Tier 1- This line item provides the Town Manager’s Office with limited funding for professional services needed to carry out administrative functions that cannot be performed internally. The Tier 1 request maintains level funding with FY26 and reflects the typical annual cost of recruitments, consulting assistance, and small project support that arise during the course of the year.

Reductions in Balanced Budget
Land Use: Meetings/Schools-  Reduced from $5,050 to $3,050
Restored in Tier 1- This line item funds professional development, training, and certifications for three Land Use Department Heads: the Building Commissioner, the Director of Planning and Economic Development, and the Conservation Agent. Regular attendance at professional meetings, courses, and conferences necessary to remain current and compliant.
Economic Development Expenses-  New line of $3,100 is eliminated 
Restored in Tier 1- This line item is proposed to provide limited funding for activities of the Economic Development Committee (EDC), specifically the annual Town Official Meet &amp; Greet.  The balanced budget removes the funding entirely. If the line is not funded, the event would need to continue to be funded through donations, or not held. Strengthening the relationship between the Town and the local business-community has been an articulated goal of many Town officials and entities. 

Reductions in Balanced Budget
APDC Expenses-  Reduced from $500 to Zero
Restored in Tier 1- This line item provides a small amount of funding for use by the Architectural Preservation District Commission (APDC) for routine administrative expenses. 
Central Purchasing: Office Equipment- Reduced from $7,500 to $6,500 
Restored in Tier 1- This line item provides centralized funding for the purchase of office furniture and similar larger items that cannot typically be absorbed within individual departmental office supply budgets. The account is used primarily, but not exclusively, for Town Hall and Ritter Building departments.  Typical expenses include desks, chairs, file cabinets, tables, shelving, and other office furnishings needed to replace worn or broken items or to equip workspaces.

Reductions in Balanced Budget
Central Purchasing: Equipment Mtc.- Reduced from $7,000 to $4,000 
Restored in Tier 1- This line item funds centralized purchasing of common equipment supplies used across multiple departments, primarily for Town Hall and Ritter Building offices. Items charged to this account typically include copier and printer toner, ink, paper, postage machine supplies, and similar shared-use materials.
Police: Department Admin/Clerk- Reduced from $22,485 to $0.00
Restored in Tier 2- This line supports a 16 hour Admin who processes accounts payable for all the police department’s receipts and detail accounts. The impact of losing this position in the balanced and Tier 1 budgets will be that the Executive Assistant must absorb these duties.

Reductions in Balanced Budget
Police: Salaries Reserves/FF- Reduced from $47,898 to $22,976
Restored in Tier 1- This funding allow the Public Safety Lobby to remain open from 3PM to 7PM.   Some of the usual daily requests are accident reports, crime reporting, phone call inquiries for non-emergency assistance, obtaining reports or requests, and prisoner monitoring during those hours. The public will encounter a locked door after 3:00pm and must speak with a dispatcher through a kiosk who will let them enter the building’s lobby and call an officer to handle the call in the lobby. Also, after 3:00pm when someone calls the non-emergency business lines of the Public Safety Building, they will either leave a voicemail or be directed to Nashoba dispatch center for assistance. This will require an officer at the station to return the phone calls during those hours when the front lobby is closed after 3:00pm

Reductions in Balanced Budget
Fire: Firefighter Salaries (Per Diem)- Reduced from $291,070.34 to $225,000
Restored in Tier 1- This decrease effectively decreases the planned per diem coverage in the FY 2027 Budget request from 112 hours/week to 70 hours/week. The reduction in the balanced budget result in the Career Staff being supplemented by a Per diem FF/EMT 10 hours/day instead of 16 hours/day.  While the exact hours that are covered are yet to be determined, the Per diem Firefighter coverage would supplement the Career Staff during the day. This would leave evening coverage at 3 Career Staff.  Without the 4th Per Diem Firefighter in the evening, it will be more difficult to staff the 2nd ambulance or have at least 2 Firefighters to respond on an initial Fire call if the 1st ambulance is out.
Fire: Out of Grade Pay- Reduced from $5,000 to $3,000
Restored in Tier 1- This line finds firefighter working out of grade.  Although adequate for current staffing, with the addition of the 4th shift, there will be an additional shift officer. This will result in additional out of grade pay for some coverage for this individual. While the total amount in the line appears to be adequate, if it is exceeded, the funding will have to be drawn from another line as this is a contractual item.

Reductions in Balanced Budget
Fire: Outside Training- Reduced from $50,473.12 to $25,000
Restored in Tier 1- Although  this line has been underspent for several years, it is primarily due to not needing to cover overtime and shift coverage for a member attending Paramedic Training. With the addition of the 4th shift, it is more than likely that one or more hires will have to attend said training. While the amount in the balanced budget should cover a Paramedic Student, it leaves very little funding for other members attending training
Fire: Equipment Maintenance/EMS- Reduced from $46,000 to $45,000
Increased in Tier 1- This is the most volatile line in the fire expense budget as the EMS call volume has continued to grow, and the cost of consumable supplies and medications has continued to skyrocket. Annual maintenance on some EMS equipment such as cardiac monitors, stretchers and other hardware has also seen significant increases in recent years. While this line does have an increase over FY26, it is not in line with the FY27 Request. In years past, this line has gone over budget and transfers have been required. The line often drawn upon to do this has also been cut in the FY 27 balanced budget. This may necessitate drawing from more critical expense lines, further reducing our ability to maintain and/or replace firefighting equipment.

Reductions in Balanced Budget
Fire: Training- Reduced from $21,000 to $15,000
Restored in Tier 1- This line is used This line has been for hiring in outside instructors for training and covering tuition for Paramedic Students. It has been underspent in past years as there has not been a need to send anyone to Paramedic school since FY 2023. With the addition of the 4th shift it is anticipated that we will have to train at least one or more new hires as Paramedics. The amount left in the line should just cover one person for Paramedic School Tuition. This leaves no additional funding for bringing in outside instructors for EMT and Paramedic Continuing Education Training.
Fire: Recertification- Reduced from $3,150 to $2,000
Restored in Tier 1- This line has been reduced to being inline with recent actual expenditures.  However, with 3 new Full Time Staff, their recertification costs will need to be covered. 
Fire: Training Supplies- Reduced from $7,000 to $5,000
Restored in Tier 1- This line covers training expenses such as textbooks and other materials for Firefighter training. Other materials often includes materials to build training props and other consumable supplies

Reductions in Balanced Budget
Fire: Meetings/Schools Firefighters- Reduced from $6,565 to $4,000
Restored in Tier 1- While the cut is inline with past expenditures, costs have risen for many of the training seminars. There are also many new trainings which are coming to the forefront. (Lithium Ion Batteries, active shooter incidents, photovoltaic systems, etc.) These trainings were not “on the radar” a few years ago.  The cuts further restrict our personnel training to stay current with emerging safety threats.
Fire: Meetings/Schools- Reduced from $1,400 to $800
Restored in Tier 1- As above, except solely for the Chief
Fire: Capital Protective Equipment- Reduced from $20,000 to $18,000
Restored in Tier 1- This line covers Personal Protective Equipment (PPE).  This line was increased last Fiscal Year, and is just keeping pace with inflation with the goal of purchasing 4-5 sets of PPE per year. Even a small reduction cuts into what we can be replaced each year. Firefighting Protective Equipment has a lifespan of 10 years. The generally recommended practice is to have a spare set for personnel to allow proper decontamination and cleaning after each fire or hazardous materials incident.

Reductions in Balanced Budget
Fire: Capital Replacement Equipment- Reduced from $15,000 to $11,600
This line was funded in the preliminary budget at $15,000.  Increased in Tier 1- This line covers replacement of worn and obsolete firefighting equipment, such as hose, nozzle, thermal imagers and other miscellaneous tools which have seen significant cost increases in the last few years.  While this line stays level with FY 2026, the requested increase in the FY 2027 budget was to get ahead of expected cost increases.
Fire: Equipment Maintenance- Reduced from $15,000 to $10,000
This line was funded in the preliminary budget at $15,000. Increased in Tier 1- This line covers maintenance, replacement and repair of radio system components, including portables, mobile radios, repeater systems and pagers.  Leaving the budget at FY 26 levels will limit us on replacement and repair of broken or worn out portable radios, mobile radios and pagers. Costs on these items, like everything else have increased dramatically in the last few years. And the line has not been increased in over 10 years. The goal has been to replace 1-2 portable radios per year, but as the cost now stands at around $4500/radio for a fireground quality radio this is becoming increasingly difficult.

Reductions in Balanced Budget
DPW: Drainage- Reduced from $100,000 to $83,000
This line was funded in the preliminary budget at $100,000. Increased in Tier 1-  The Tier 1 increase is inline with the preliminary budget.  This line is used to replace drainage pipes, add catch basins, add pipes, clean swales, clean waterways, and other related drainage work.  Before a roadway is repaved, we repair the drainage systems to help improve the longevity of the new pavement and reduce flooding.  There is a backlog of drainage improvements that are needed throughout the Town. The impact that residents would see from not fully funding this line would include: areas that flood, will continue to flood; catch basins overflowing with water during heavy rain events, potentially causing flooding; citizens’ requests to improve drainage at or near their homes will not get done in FY 27. 

Reductions in Balanced Budget
DPW: Stormwater Management- Reduced from $110,000 to $100,000
Restored in Tier 1- This line funds stormwater vendor services to aid in MS4 compliance and some small stormwater project.  The balanced budget pulls the Town further out of compliance. 
DPW: Supplies- Reduced from $35,000 to $25,000
Restored in Tier 1- This line is for the purchase of supplies the DPW needs to perform various maintenance and repairs to roadways.  Purchases include crushed stone, riprap stone, loam, lumber, building materials, parts for small engine tools, etc.  Many of these supplies are purchased in direct response to citizen’s requests to make repairs that affect their property (mostly flooding). 

Reductions in Balanced Budget
DPW: Pavement Management- Reduced from $282,000 to $50,000
Restored in Tier 1.  This was reduced from the $600,000 DPW requested amount. This line was already reduced substantially in FY26.  In Fiscal Year 2016 Town Meeting voted to approve a $4,000,000 debt exclusion to fund the Pavement Management Plan (PMP) along with using Operating Budget funds and Chapter 90 funds for FY 2016 through FY 2022.  The plan in 2016 to fund paving with the Debt Exclusion also addressed funding for future years beyond 2022.  The plan was to add $50,000 per year to the Operating Budget for paving until there was $1.2M available per year for paving.  The $1.2M is a combination of Chapter 90 and Operating Budget.  The FY27 Chapter 90 apportionment is $718,718.29.

As part of a broader reorganization of Land Use and regulatory departments, staffing is being restructured to provide shared administrative support across multiple departments rather than maintaining separate full-time administrative positions within each office.
Board of Health: Purchase of Services: Reduced from $75 to $0.00

Reductions in Balanced Budget
Council on Aging: Assistant Meal Site- Reduced from $19,825.74 to $0.00
Restored in Tier 1- This line support 2, 9 hour Dietary Aides that provide essential operational support to the Meal Site Manager.  This includes meal setup, dining area sanitation (tables, chairs, and counters), dishwashing, basic food preparation, table setting, meal service, and coordination of the Meals on Wheels program, including distribution to volunteer drivers. Library: Staff: Reduced from $269,048.83 to $226,077.79
Restored in Tier 1- This reduction eliminates the position of Young Adult Services Librarian, a 30 hr./week employee.  The community would lose a key professional librarian role (1 of 5 Master’s-level librarians) who works with the important young adult demographic. The loss would be especially impactful shortly after town leaders committed a significant amount of capital to improving the Teen Room with more than $120,000 from ARPA funds. The reduction also decreases staff availability for absence coverage to meet operational safety requirements.   The library will need to eliminate structured after-school activities and Teen Advisory Board volunteer opportunities. Additionally, the community will see reduced quality and frequency of Summer Reading Program for Young Adults, book displays and collection maintenance, and outreach and partnerships.  Operational impact will include increased strain on remaining staff to cover evening shifts, potential scheduling gaps, negative impact on staff morale and increased difficulty to retain staff talent. To partially counter the morale and retention impact, the library might close one evening per week, but this reduces community access by 25% for evening space use, creating significant impacts on local groups such as Scouts, and would result in lost shifts for one Library Page. Other impacts will be less immediately visible but will reduce teen-focused programming and increase reliance on outside funding sources.  The Young Adult Services Librarian represents 11% of total staff hours and plays a critical role in supporting teen engagement, literacy, and safe after-school space. Reduced staffing and resources will directly affect service quality, accessibility, and the overall user experience for the community. "
