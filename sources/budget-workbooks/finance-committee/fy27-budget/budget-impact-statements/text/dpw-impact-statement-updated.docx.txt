
March 23, 2026
DPW Budget Impact Statement
Fiscal Year 2027 Proposed Balanced Budget – No Override
Bill Bernard, DPW Director
General Highway Drainage
Org 14112, Obj 521017
FY 26 Budget $83,000
DPW Request: $100,000.00
Balanced Budget: $75,000.00, which is a $17,000 decrease from the DPW requested budget
This line is used to replace drainage pipes, add catch basins, add pipes, clean swales, clean waterways, and other related drainage work.  Before a roadway is repaved, we repair the drainage systems to help improve the longevity of the new pavement and reduce flooding.  There is a backlog of drainage improvements that are needed throughout the Town.
The impact that residents would see from not fully funding this line would include:
Areas that flood, will continue to flood
Catch basins overflowing with water during heavy rain events, potentially causing flooding.
Citizens’ requests to improve drainage at or near their homes will not get done in FY 27.

Stormwater Management Projects
Org 14112, Obj 535052
FY 26 Budget $110,000
DPW Request: $450,000.00
Balanced Budget: $100,000.00, which is a $10,000 decrease from FY 26 and a $350,000 decrease from the DPW requested budget
This is a request to add $340K to meet MS4 Permit requirements, the additional work will include hiring contractors to clean catch basins and sweep roadways.  The $100,000 balance budget and the additionally $340,000 would get us to full compliance with the MS4 permit.  Although the DPW has the equipment to do this work, there are not enough personnel to complete it and perform all the other work required.  I estimate it would take 3 fulltime heavy equipment operators (HEO’s) 89 days to complete the required catch basin cleaning and sweeping (there are 127 days in May through October when the work would be completed).  The cost to hire 3 new HEO’s including benefits and the costs to operate the equipment would exceed the cost of hiring contractors for this work by $100,000.
The impact that residents would see from not fully funding this line would include:
Sand, dirt, debris, trash buildup on the sides of roadways.
Catch basins overflowing with water during heavy rain events, potentially causing flooding.
Continued and worsening impairments to Lake Whalom, Lake Shirley, and Hickory Hills Lake which include overgrowth of invasive plants, harmful algal blooms, turbidity (cloudiness), and mercury in fish tissue.
Potential fines from $5,000 to $50,000 per day from the EPA for non-compliance with the MS4 permit.

General Highway Supplies
Org14112, Obj 541018
DPW Request: $35,000.00, which is level funding from FY 26
Balanced Budget: $25,000.00, which is a decrease of $10,000 from the DPW requested budget
This line is for the purchase of supplies the DPW needs to perform various maintenance and repairs to roadways.  Purchases include crushed stone, riprap stone, loam, lumber, building materials, parts for small engine tools, etc.  Many of these supplies are purchased in direct response to citizen’s requests to make repairs that affect their property (mostly flooding).
The impact that residents would see from not fully funding this line would include:
Projects in response to citizens’ requests would be prioritized and some will not be completed in FY 27.
Less maintenance on the roadways would be performed resulting in water continuing to enter private property from the roadways, low shoulders, etc.
Less vegetation management on the sides of roadways.
Pavement Management Plan
Org 14112, Obj 587046

FY 26 Budget $279,000 – note this was reduced from the $600,000 DPW requested amount.  Note: The Pavement Management Plan was developed based on the scheduled $50K yearly increase
DPW Request: $650,000.00, which includes the scheduled $50,000 yearly increase
Balanced Budget: $50,000.00, this is a $600,000 decrease
The impact that residents would see from not fully funding this line would include:
The residents would not see a direct impact from the reduction as Chapter 90 funds would be used to continue the Pavement Management Plan.  This is to protect the multi-million-dollar investment the Town has made in paving roadways.
However, future culvert, bridge, and drainage pipe projects that could have been funded by Chapter 90 funds would be pushed out to later years or would require Capital Funds to complete.  Therefore, residents could potentially see flooding and failed drainage as a result.
Emergency repairs to culvert, bridge, and drainage pipes that would require Chapter 90 funds; thus, there could be reduced work done under the Pavement Management Plan.
In Fiscal Year 2016 Town Meeting voted to approve a $4,000,000 debt exclusion to fund the Pavement Management Plan (PMP) along with using Operating Budget funds and Chapter 90 funds for FY 2016 through FY 2022.  The plan in 2016 to fund paving with the Debt Exclusion also addressed funding for future years beyond 2022.  The plan was to add $50,000 per year to the Operating Budget for paving until there was 1.2M available per year for paving.  The $1.2M is a combination of Chapter 90 and Operating Budget.  Fully funding the Pavement Management Plan complies with the intent of the 2016 Town Meeting.  Residents may feel their vote in 2016 is not being honored if the funding is not restored.


New FY 27 Part-time Admin
DPW Request: $24,150.33
Balanced Budget: $13,000.00
This is a request to add a new position (Clerk – part-time, non-benefitted) to the DPW staff).  
The Executive Assistant is shared between the DPW, Facilities Department and Recreation Department.  The new Clerk will assist the Executive Assistant.  The work will include assisting with tracking spending on contracts, tracking costs per facility, accounts payable, time sheets, filing, etc.
The impact that residents would see from not fully funding this line would include:
Residents will not see a direct impact.
However, some work may be delayed as extra time is needed to reconcile accounts.
Vendors may not be paid within 30 days, resulting in unnecessary interest payments.

Overtime Cemetery Supervisor
Org 14911, Obj 513000
DPW Request: $4,000.00, which is level funding from FY 26
Balanced Budget: $3,500.00, this is a $500 decrease
This line is primarily used for Saturday burials or late afternoon weekday burials. It is also used for mowing before holidays to ensure the cemeteries are in great shape for visitors.  The Collective Bargaining Agreement for the DPW Workers Union expired in 2024.  Therefore, they have not had any cost-of-living increases in two years.  They are actively negotiating a new agreement.  All potential salaries increases are unknown.  Therefore, less hours will be available for this overtime.
The impact that residents would see from not fully funding this line would include:
Less burials on Saturdays.  However, I do not think we should ever deny a grieving family’s request to have a burial on a Saturday.  Therefore, transfers of funds of other line items within the Cemetery budget would have to be made; thus, reducing the work in those items.
Less after-hours mowing would result in un-mowed grass on busy holiday weekends in the cemeteries.  This has caused much anguish to visitors in the past.

Cemetery Fuel Charges
Org 14912, Obj 541019
DPW Request: $3,500.00, which is level funding from FY 26
Balanced Budget: $2,500.00, which is a $1,000 decrease
This line is used for fuel for cemetery vehicles and equipment (1 ton dump truck, backhoe, mowers, weed whackers, blowers, chainsaws, etc.).  Note: the price of fuel is increasing.
The impact that residents would see from not fully funding this line would include:
Less mowing would be accomplished.
However, we would likely transfer funds from other cemetery line items to cover any overages in this line item; thus, reducing the work in those items.
