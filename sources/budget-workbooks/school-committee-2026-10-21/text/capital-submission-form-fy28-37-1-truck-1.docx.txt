2. DESCRIPTION AND JUSTIFICATION
Description and scope: The current pickup truck has a blown head gasket, a cracked exhaust manifold, and extensive rust, making continued operation increasingly unreliable and costly. The vehicle has reached the end of its useful life and is no longer a dependable asset for the Lunenburg Public Schools.This vehicle serves as the school district’s **only backup plow truck**, making its reliability particularly critical during winter weather. A failure during a snow or ice event could significantly hinder the district’s ability to clear school driveways, parking areas, and access routes, potentially affecting the safe and timely operation of school facilities.Replacing this vehicle is therefore not simply a matter of upgrading aging equipment; it is a necessary investment in **operational reliability, winter safety, and continuity of school operations**. Funding a replacement now will reduce the risk of an unexpected breakdown, avoid continued investment in a vehicle with major mechanical and structural deficiencies, and ensure that the district has dependable backup snow-removal equipment when it is most needed.

Consequences if not funded: 1.Increased emergency repair costs: Continuing to operate the current truck could result in additional major mechanical repairs, with no guarantee that repairs will provide a reliable long-term solution.2. Cost of outside snow-removal contractors: If either of the district two plow trucks becomes inoperable during a snow event, the district may need to hire an outside contractor to plow school parking lots, driveways, and access roads. Emergency contractor rates can be significantly higher than the district normal operating costs.3. Reduced snow-removal capacity: With only two trucks, the loss of one vehicle would immediately reduce the district ability to clear snow efficiently and could substantially increase the time required to make school properties safe and accessible.4. Potential disruption to school operations: Delays in snow removal could affect the safe opening, continued operation, or timely reopening of school facilities following a winter storm.5. Safety concerns: Inadequate or delayed snow and ice removal can create hazardous conditions for students, staff, visitors, buses, emergency vehicles, and maintenance personnel.
