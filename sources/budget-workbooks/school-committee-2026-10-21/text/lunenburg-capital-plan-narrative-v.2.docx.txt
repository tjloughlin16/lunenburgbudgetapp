Lunenburg Public Schools Capital Plan
Fiscal Years 2028 through 2033 with a planning outlook through 2037
Superintendent recommendation for School Committee and Town review
My recommendation 
I recommend that we make the LMHS turf and track our first capital priority, followed by the Primary School playground and driveway. The engineering assessment at LMHS calls for work that cannot wait for our usual replacement schedule. At the Primary School, we already have design funding, giving us a way to prepare those projects for construction in FY28.
We cannot do everything at once. My goal is to give the School Committee and Town a clear view of our needs so we can agree on what comes first. The proposed years will help us plan, but we will need to adjust them as we learn more about conditions, costs, and available funding.

Where we are now
We have asked the Selectboard to place the $195,830 turf and track professional services request on the November 2026 Special Town Meeting warrant. I expect to have its response when I present this report and will share the outcome with the Committee. Construction funding would come back as a separate request. The Primary School playground and driveway designs each have $30,000 in FY27 funding.
Some projects have detailed quotes and assessments. Others need updated prices or more work to define the scope. I have identified those gaps throughout the report so that the estimates do not suggest more certainty than we have.
Our leading priorities
LMHS turf and track
The turf and track serve our students in physical education and athletics, as well as the wider community. The October 1, 2026 request describes the surfaces, installed in 2017, as being in poor condition. The replacement proposal also addresses drainage and related site work.
The October 15, 2026 assessment by Peter M. Reynolds, P.E., raises a more immediate concern. It identifies a 150-foot section of the lower retaining wall as being in a state of failure and a potential danger to the public. The engineer recommends immediate barricading to keep people at least 3 feet from the top and 6 feet from the bottom. He also reports that the upper wall and press box have moved approximately 8 inches and recommends that fall 2026 be their last season of use as they are. He calls for removal and wall replacement in 2027 before the bleachers return to use.
Gale’s December 16, 2025 proposal totals $195,830 for assessment, survey, geotechnical work, design, bidding, and construction phase services. If the warrant article moves forward and Town Meeting approves funding, we could begin the authorized work and sequence later services with construction approvals. Optional turf testing would add an estimated $6,000.
FY28 remains a proposed funding year for replacement, but the engineer’s recommendations for 2027 must guide the actual work. The earlier June 30, 2029 completion target cannot justify delaying immediate protections or the work needed before bleacher reuse. We need to confirm the protective measures and construction sequence with the engineer. The materials available for this report do not confirm whether the recommended barricades are in place.
The $3 million replacement estimate is preliminary. Gale’s scope includes the turf, track, bleachers, press box, two retaining walls, and drainage. Before seeking construction funding, we need to confirm that the estimate covers that work, determine whether it includes the professional fees, and allow for price changes and unexpected costs. Given these conditions, I recommend moving this project from its earlier rank of 9 out of 12 to our first priority.
Primary School playground and driveway
I recommend preparing both Primary School projects for FY28 construction. The FY27 design funding allows us to move that work forward now. We should plan the projects together to limit disruption and avoid digging up newly completed areas, while keeping their costs and approvals separate. The construction schedule will depend on completing design, securing funding, and allowing time for bidding.
The playground estimate is $309,256. The request describes the current equipment as being in good condition, so the reason for replacement is to provide equipment that better fits our youngest students and improves accessibility. A playground designer and input from those who use the space will help us confirm the equipment, accessible routes, surfacing, installation, and site costs.
The driveway and parking estimate is $500,000. Limited parking and congestion affect daily arrival and dismissal, and the request identifies insufficient room for emergency vehicles to maneuver. The design should address those concerns along with pedestrian routes and drainage. We will need emergency responders involved in that review.
Together, the construction estimates total $809,256. Coordinating the work may reduce repeated site work, but we have not assumed a dollar savings. We also need to check the construction estimates against the funded designs so that we do not count the same costs twice.

Next steps
The Town capital process
The Town Charter calls for an annually updated five-year capital program, submitted by the Town Manager to the Selectboard and Finance Committee at least 150 days before the fiscal year begins. Our responsibility is to bring forward clear school requests that explain the need, scope, cost, timing, proposed funding, and ongoing operating and maintenance costs.
The Town’s capital policy generally covers tangible items costing at least $20,000 with a useful life of three years or more. Items serving the same purpose may also qualify as a group. The smaller requests remain separate in this report. We will confirm with Town finance staff whether boiler design should be part of the larger replacement project.
Keeping the School Committee informed
Facilities, technology, and finance staff need to keep one current list of school projects. I will use that list to bring the Committee updates on costs, progress, and changes in timing before our annual submission to the Town.
Details staff need to resolve
The facilities forms repeat a total of $6,582,902, while the individual requests identified in the earlier review add to $6,432,902. We need to account for that $150,000 difference before relying on the forms’ total. The annual schedules in this report show the projects and estimates included in the current requests.
The roof form lists $1.5 million in the funding field and $700,000 under operating impact. This report uses $1.5 million provisionally. Facilities staff need to confirm the building, work, year, and intended amount.
Several forms put full project costs in fields meant for annual operating expenses. We have used the project-specific funding amounts as capital estimates where supported, but still need the actual maintenance, subscription, inspection, staffing, and energy costs, along with any documented savings.
Before final submission, staff will update older quotes, complete missing project information, and recommend funding for the smaller requests. Once we agree on timing, the individual forms should match the schedule. We will identify funding as approved only when that approval is confirmed.
