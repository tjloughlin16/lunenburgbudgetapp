DISCUSSION - 440 Howard St. - Special Permit
The meeting was held at Lunenburg Town Hall, 2"d Floor, Joseph F. Bilotta Meeting Room, 17 Main St.,
Lumenburg, MA 01462 on October 28, 2026, at 7:00pm. The public hearing was opened, and Chairman
Gravelle introduced the Board Members and explained the hearing procedures. Leigh Bain, Board
Secretary, read the petition into public record.
The applicant was represented by Greg Roy (Dillis & Roy). Mr. Roy stated the structure is to be adjacent
to the garage and attached to the house. It will share the current common driveway. He explained that the
property is in the outlaying district, which requires a twenty-five-foot (25') side yard setback. They are
proposing a twelve-foot (12') setback. The ADU has a 24'x30' proposed footprint and will be a single-
story structure, on a slab. The applicants feel this is the best spot as there is something on all sides of the
home. Mr. Roy explained that the septic tank is immediately in front of the property. They are working
with the Board of Health to permit the upgrade of the septic tank to a multi compartment tank and
increase the leeching field compacity. Chairman Gravelle confirmed the structure is Seven Hundred
Twenty Square Feet and that the Board is only looking at the side yard. Mr. Roy stated that is correct. Mr.
Gravelle stated that is well below the state limit of Nine Hundred Square Feet. Mr. Gravelle opened to
the board.
Patrick Callahan stated he was going to ask about placement, but that was answered, and there is
something on all sides of the property.
Chairman Gravelle asked why attached vs. detached. Mr. Roy stated the applicants went back and forth,
and he believes they settled on attached for ease of construction and they may be giving access to a bay in
===PAGE 2===
the garage. This unit is being built for parents/in-laws.
Having no further questions from the Board, Chairman Gravelle asked for public comment. Mr. Kevin
O'Brien, 460 Howard St., Lunenburg, MA 01462 stated he wanted to see where it will be located. He said
he believes he is the closest abutter and thinks this is a good idea, that ADU's are for more superior than in-
law apartments. He mentioned it does not affect what he plans to do with his property. Again, he stated he
has no problem and wishes them good luck. Having no further public comment, Chairman Gravelle looked
for a motion.
MOTION
Patrick Callahan motioned to approve the Thirteen Foot (13') relief.
Karin Menard, Second the Motion.
FINDINGS
In granting a Special Permit, the Board of Appeals found that the proposed use:
a) Will not be injurious or dangerous to public health or unduly hazardous because of traffic
congestion, danger of fire or explosion or other reasons.
b) Will not have a material adverse effect on the value of land and buildings in the neighborhood or
on the amenities of the neighborhood.
c) Will be operated with reasonable regard for order and sightlines if an open use.
d) Will not produce noise, vibration, smoke, dust, odor, heat or glare observable at the lot lines in
amounts clearly detrimental to the normal use of adjacent property.
The Special Authorization is granted with the contingency that Thomas and Kelley Pendergast, comply
with all licenses, regulations, statues and ordinances of any and all applicable state, local and federal board
or agencies with jurisdiction over the premises.
The Special Permit is subject to a periodic review by the permit granting authority (Zoning Board) or the
Enforcement Officer, to ensure compliance with the enumerated conditions. Non-compliance to these
conditions may result in revocation of this permit.
After closing the discussion, the Board acting in its capacity as the Special Permit Granting Authority,
granted the Special Permit with the above members present and voting as follows with respect to the
issuance of the Special Permit.
VOTE
Alfred Gravelle voted in favor of granting the Special Permit.
Patrick Callahan voted in favor of granting the Special Permit.
Karin Menard voted in favor of granting the Special Permit.
Anthony Nicastro voted in favor of granting the Special Permit.
Donald Gurney voted in favor of granting the Special Permit.
In making this determination, the Board specifically found that the granting of this Special Permit would
not contravene the purpose of the Protective Bylaws or the provisions of Chapter 40A of the Massachusetts
General Laws.
