The Chair called the meeting to order at 7:00 pm. This meeting is being recorded and televised for re-
broadcast on the Lunenburg Public Access cable channel.

that George was to be watching the clock.
Public comment Martha Moore, the Director of the Lunenburg Public Library, explained the library has
been asked to have twice-weekly meetings with the 5t grade. There are six classes coming for the rest
of FY2019. This changes the staff's working time as it take a number of hours every week to be there for
the kids and implement various library activities to accommodate their visit. It averages 10 hours
between four staff to do this work, which is outside of the scope of their regular work. At this point,
there is not money in the budget to pay for this. Martha had written an article for the warrant for the
special town meeting asking for an additional $7200.00 to apply to the library's salary line budget.
George asked for clarification of the amount; Martha stated $7200.00. Terri explained that Heather
rolled the request into the Article 1 of the town warrant to be explained during the special town
meeting. George asked for how long this additional funding would cover the need; Martha respond that
it would take them from three or four weeks ago through the end of May. Mark asked if this was to be
expected in the future; Martha did not know. The library staff have been mainly working on making sure
the students have cards and that they get an extended return due date. George asked what grade this
involved; Martha stated 5* grade, about 140 students, due to changes in curriculum. This will include
one teacher and one classroom aid per visit. The library staff are spread out to cover different areas and
needs of the library while Martha is at the front desk problem solving. Jay asked for if this was school
sponsored; Martha responded it was. Jay asked if the school was providing any funding; Martha stated it
was not. Jay asked when this was planned for; Martha stated it started about a month ago and it was
not planned for. Terri restated that it was not a collaborated effort between the library and the school.

Financial Officer Report There was an announcement that went to Terri that she will forward to the
committee. Karen discussed Free Cash. Regarding Free Cash, it was certified October 17. The original
estimate was $1,531, 000. The certified figure is $1,512,483. The retained earnings of the enterprise
funds were certified at $52,790 in water, $1,929,110 in sewer, and $1,086 in trash. George asked for
clarification about what the trash enterprise fund is. Karen stated it is when people purchase the yellow
bags for their trash, generating the revenue from which the expenses are drawn. Terri brought up that
there had been previous discussion between town finance and financial planning as to whether it made
sense to pay anything down. The response was that it will be a couple of years before there would be
any bonds available to use, that we can't do anything at the current but it will continued to be reviewed
to see if it would make sense to pay down in the future.
Review of amended investment policy: To discuss the change in the diversification policy because the
current is that no more than 10% of the town's investment be in a single institution. The revised would
be 25%. Karen pointed out the policy indicated that the investment would need to be fully insured or
collateralized. She went through the cash for the close of this fiscal year. Thirteen financial institutions
are utilized. The majority are less than 2%. Fidelity Bank at the close of the fiscal year was 10.53%. That
is for daily deposits, all checks that come into the town. Main Street Bank has all enterprise funds and
daily cash deposits. This was 16% at the close of FY2018. Unibank, the state depository account, had
22% at the end of FY2018. Harbor One, for the school construction, had 9%. Belmont, part of the school
construction, had 12%. Bartholomew, the trust funds, had 19%. All of them are fully insured even
though they were over the old investment policy of 10%. Karen stated her opinion that the 25% is
acceptable and that the funds are safe. The funds are covered 100%. John asked if she was referring to
the FDIC insurance. There was further discussion of other types of insurance depending upon what type
of bank or account is in question. Terri asked if the policy required the investments be in an interest
bearing accounts, or if there was any discussion around that. Karen responded that it does not
specifically state that, but that would be desirable investment behavior. Terri asked if the business
accounts are interest bearing. It was confirmed. Terri questioned if that should be part of the
investment policy. Karen responded that could be done. She will take a closer look to see if it is already
part of the policy. Mark suggested it would not be necessary, as it is an obvious action. Safety, liquidity,
and yield are considerations written into the policy when deciding on an investment instrument; further
indicating investments are to be made with the best rate of return. John stated he felt that safety and
liquidity take precedence over yield. Karen agreed. George asked a question to which Karen replied that

Terri: Town Charter. There are potentially two new items: rank order voting, but committee is not ready
to put it in the charter. It is a system whereby the elected official has 50% of the vote. It is intended to
level the field when there is a scenario where there are several candidates to choose from (a recent
election where there were 16 candidates for one position was given as an example). The other item was
if someone becomes a write-in candidate they are not able to be elected unless they have a number of
votes equal or greater than the number required to get on the ballot. There will be a report of these
items at the town meeting, and the first public hearing will most likely be in in December. As an aside,
the ham and bean supper at The Eagle House raised $997.00.
