1. Call to Order
Mr. Beardmore called the meeting to order at 7:00 p.m. and read a statement concerning the Open
Meeting Law, and how all public meetings are legally being held remotely.
This meeting was recorded and is viewable on YouTube at https://youtu.be/vSc5ybV_uRQ
2. Pledge of Allegiance
3. Announcements: Mr. Menard reminded committee members that Town Caucus is to be held Monday, March
14, 2022. Mr. Beardmore announced that members of the Finance Committee have been invited to the Boys
and Girls Club annual fundraiser on April 8, "Are You Smarter Than Our Sixth Graders."
4. Public Comment:
5. Chair Report: Mr. Beardmore made an appearance on the Fitchburg Public Access Channel, and said most of the
discussion was on international matters.
Mr. Beardmore also suggested the committee decide whether to add the FY2023 capital plan to an agenda of an
upcoming meeting before the public hearing on the town's budget.
6. Town Manager Report: Ms. Lemieux reported that Meadow Woods has paid their balance of back taxes, which
will close out and be turned over to free cash at the end of the year.
A warrant article in the amount of $100,000 to clean the Woodruff property will be proposed at Annual Town
Meeting after the Conservation Commission approved a Notice of Intent submitted by the town.
Ms. Lemieux reported that after receiving actual budget amounts for Monty Tech and regional dispatch, she
presented a revised capital plan to the Select Board. The new plan now totals $2,162,849, which is a $423,800
increase from the previous plan. The hazardous material abatement for the Ritter Building has been moved to
FY2024, in order to determine the scope of that project, and three projects have been added to the plan,
including upgrades to the senior center and installation of proper HVAC at the Primary School.
Ms Lemieux also reported that four more properties have been removed from the tax title list, and since the list
has been distributed, several property owners have paid their back taxes. The original list of 50 has been
decreased to 24 properties.
7. Budget Presentations:
DPW: DPW Director Rob Oliva presented his budget, and informed the committee he is not looking for
any increases, other than raises in salaries for the crew. Mr. Beardmore asked if any lines in the budget
have typically been underspent. Mr. Oliva explained that there are sublines under the "General
Highway" fund, and some of those lines have been overfunded, but those funds do cover other lines in
the budget. Mr. Menard commented that he remembers seeing what was actually spent versus what
had been budgeted in the past at Town Meetings in the past. Mr. Passios concurred. Mr. Oliva added
that he used his actual spending to formulate the FY2023 budget, and would submit that information to
the Town Manager for the committee to view.

Mr. Oliva talked about how the DPW is currently over budget in the snow account by
approximately $139,000 citing the icy weather and amount of salt the department had to use to treat
the town roads after storms. Mr. Beardmore asked whether more money could be appropriated ahead
of time in anticipation of going over the snow removal budget amount, rather than scrambling for funds
at the end of the year to balance the budget. Mr. Oliva replied that the current system works well for
Lunenburg, and for other communities, and increasing the snow removal line item would be a topic of
discussion for the committee and the Town Manager.
The five-year pavement management plan is in its fifth year, and $50,000 has been requested in
order to complete the projects in the plan. Mr. Oliva stated there is still $263,276 left of debt exclusion.
Mr. Menard asked how the retaining wall at the town beach would be funded; and whether it
should go in the capital plan. Mr. Oliva responded that the project should cost $21,000, and will be
covered under the facilities budget.
A $85,000 increase has been requested to compensate a facilities and grounds director.
Mr. Oliva detailed his $1.31 million capital plan request. Mr. Passios asked to edit the Page St
project to read Page St culvert, so voters could be aware of more detail before Annual Town Meeting.
Mr. Menard inquired whether $45,000 would be enough money to fully repair the Flat Hill Rd.
culvert without causing drainage issues for other streets nearby, and suggested drainage issues on
Cortland Circle are responsible for flooding on Flat Hill Rd. Mr. Beardmore added that if Cortland Circle is
the source of flooding issues on Flat Hill Rd, then the Cortland Circle Homeowners Association should be
funding their drainage problem. Mr. Passios stated that this issue has been discussed and debated for
several years, and this meeting is not the time to be discussing it. Mr. Menard retorted that there is no
public record of the discussion of the drainage at Cortland Circle. Mr. Menard said he has requested
information on the issue, and hasn't received any. Mr. Beardmore added that he hadn't heard of any of
the information discussed at this meeting, and that as a member of the Finance Committee he feels he
does not have enough information to recommend approval. Mr. Passios stated that the charge of the
Finance Committee is to approve or disapprove the town's capital plan in its entirety, and not to debate
individual projects on the plan. Mr. Beardmore and Mr. Menard disagreed.

9. Warrant Letter Content and Timeline: Mr. Beardmore requested committee members send what they think
should be included into the letter to him and Mr. Menard. Mr. Balish volunteered to construct the letter if
