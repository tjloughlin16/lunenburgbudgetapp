19 Island Road: NOI-25-24 Video link>
Notice of Intent by Hickory Hills Landowners Ine for the construction of a handicap parking area,
ramp, and dock
===PAGE 2===
Matt Marro represented the Hickory Hills Landowners Association. Stormwater calculations are not yet
complete. 19 Island Road is the beach area. The request is to add an ADA compliant ramp and dock at
the end of Park Street Avenue. Detailed dimensions were provided. While in the floodplain, no change
in elevation is proposed. This will replace the existing ramp. R. Bursch wanted to know if the ramp
would be for the public and was informed it would be only for residents of Hickory Hills and is within
the gated area. A. Burnap wanted to know how permeable the regrind surface would be. M. Marro
indicated that the packing of the regrind surface would likely make it non-permeable, hence the
addition of a gravel apron around it. The dense packing is required for a handicap space. J. Pewtherer
had questions about the hand railing. They will be tied to the ramp and are movable. H. Jaime wanted to
know if there would be digging required to build the parking space. Just enough for the regrind, but the
elevation will remain the same. R. Bursch was concerned that regrind will deteriorate and go into the
lake. With the gravel apron, it was felt that this concern was handled. R. Bursch strongly suggested
paving.

b. 922 Flat Hill Road: NOI-26-2 < Video link>
Notice of Intent by Mike Potasky for the construction of a single-family home with a garage
Lar Greene represented the applicant and presented the plan. The existing building will be torn down.
Board of Health concerns have been addressed. The proposed house is outside the 100-foot buffer. J.
Pewtherer noted that during the site walk it was clear that several trees had been removed for which we
have no record. L. Greene agreed that he was unaware of any record as well. It was also noted that DEP
has not been notified. L. Greene stated the homeowner mailed the filing but may have used the wrong
address, and they are investigating. J. Pewtherer shared site visit photos. Tree stumps will be added to
the plans. R. Bursch confirmed that there is no disturbance in the 50-foot zone. He also asked that they
forward the Board of Health's requirement to put the leach field outside the 100-foot zone. H. Jaime
wondered why the new building is not exactly over the old building, which would affect the well,
parking, and septic. L. Greene explained it was necessary to allow full access to the garage. Tree
remediation will be added to the plan. H. Jaime recommended a $300 fine for removing the trees
without permission, or additional replanting in lieu of the fees. Confirmed that none of the trees were in
the 50-foot or 100-foot buffer zones.

Notice of Intent by David Katralis & Korry Dow-Katralis for an addition and removal of stone patio
David Katrakis (189 Hemlock Drive) and Ryan Proctor (Dillis & Roy) presented. The applicant
presented the plan. Part A is the 20x33 foot porch. Part B is the stone retaining wall. Part C is the patio.
The patio would encroach the 50-foot zone by 82 sq ft. R. Proctor clarified that all existing
infrastructure would be removed. J. Pewtherer confirmed that the plantings at the water's edge are all
proposed and not pre-existing. DEP comments were reviewed, both related to the floodplain. M.
Larouche confirmed that the retaining wall is proposed. R. Bursch wondered if the previous work and
tree removal had been approved. J. Pewtherer found an RDA from December 2023 in SharePoint that
concluded with a negative determination. A. Burnap confirmed that all the proposed construction is
withing the 50-foot zone. While not on the plan, the applicant felt a subsurface system can be installed
in the grass area to cover the runoff. D. Katrakis is willing to remove an existing garage (tent over
metal) and plant more trees. R. Bursch noted that the plans indicate a concrete patio, but they
mentioned pavers. D. Katrakis noted that this change was due to H. Jaime's suggestions. The enclosed
porch will be over crushed rock. As the deck will be 7-8 feet high, the area will be used for storage.
Concern was raised about enclosing the porch on the elevated deck as well as the wall and filling inside
the 30-foot no touch zone.
John Fortune (Hickory Hills Association) noted that Hickory Hills owns the first foot of land above the
100-year floodplain mark and expressed concern that these plans were close to that property. He also
opposed covers over the patios without proper drainage. Bill Hudson (90 Hemlock Drive and Hickory
Hills Association) suggested another site walk since you can now see the entire area.
Motion:
R. Bursch, to continue to the April 1 meeting which should include:
• Updated plan showing the patio not being enclosed
• Removal of the retaining wall
• Show carport removed with plantings
• Remove the outdoor kitchen from the 50-foot zone
• Replacement of existing retaining wall
• Complete a second site visit
