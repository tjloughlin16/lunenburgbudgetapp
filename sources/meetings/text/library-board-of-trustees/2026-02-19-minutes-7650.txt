   FY27 Budget Update  
March 5 will be the Library’s presentation to the Finance Committee. The Town Manager is 
anticipated to be delivering her preliminary budget this week, a level service budget. A level 
service budget for the town is anticipated to need an override. 
   Staff Update 
Lydia performed to rave reviews while I was on leave. She efficiently took care of business and was 
acknowledged at last week’s Selectboard meeting by the Town Manager. She effectively monitored 
and mentored staff while I was unavailable. Debbie Laffond is helping as a temporary employee. She 
will take some time away during March and then return to help us during Lydia’s leave. 
Minna Scholten is our new Library Assistant, and we are excited to see how she can contribute. She 
seems to have picked up familiarity with circulation duties quickly, and is helping us with small 
projects in the Children’s Room. Lydia and I are continuing regular checkins with Joslyn and Melanie 
to maintain our availability for them as they work on their projects.  
We were awarded a grant from the Emerson hospital Christine Gallery Community Benefit Grant and 
will purchase memory kits that can circulated to Alzheimer sufferers and their caregivers. These will 
be housed at the LAAC and catalogued by the library.

Karen Barret is the new Town Finance Director, the Assistant Accountant continues. I have a meeting 
with Karen and the Town Manager on Friday of this week to discuss the preliminary budget for the 
Library. 
   Programs 
Joslyn has really gone above and beyond to organize and schedule a steady stream of teen/tween 
programs while we have had the vacancy in the Teen Room. We have also been lucky to have a 
volunteer interested in leading a weekly board game club and a Dungeons and Dragon group. We 
continue to hope that the numbers for those events will increase. Our Minecraft Tournament drew 
20 excited participants on Feb 14th. 
Children’s crafts have seen a positive response with 32 attendees for Sock Snowmen and 38 for Love 
Bugs. Toddler Yoga has been another positive note with 19 little guys in attendance this past month. 
Adult programs have seen some weather impacts, though many programs are seeing steady 
numbers. 15 attended the treasure journaling workshop, and 15 attended the sock hop concert. An 
interesting note: attendance at virtual programs this month climbed to 15 at the 5 events we 
advertised, a significant increase over recent months. Our Valentines Day movie showing brought in 
a small crowd who were thoroughly entertained last Friday night.  
Coming up: 2/18 Afrobeats Dance class for Kids, 2/18 Scottish Music concert, 2/19 STEM Olympics, 2/20 
Winter Salve workshop, 2/21 Winter Puzzle Swap, 2/27 Video Game Night, 2/28 next session of 
Toddler Yoga, 3/2 Bridie Wolejko art exhibit “Harmony and Discord,” 3/7 Music Clinic, 3/14 Candle 
Making sessions for kids and for teens. 
   Outreach 
Currently working on building a list of volunteers who might help us offer a Repair Clinic Outreach 
event. Melanie is working on scheduling a second presentation at Baker Brook, and will be visiting 
the Lunenburg Women’s Club to talk about library services at one of their two next meetings. 
We are have begun filling out our calendar for late Spring and summer. If you notice any potential 
visits, let me know. Right now, Cruise Night, May Vendor Fair, Artisan Markets, and several visits to 
local recreation spots (beach, pickleball courts, etc.) are what we are planning.  
▪ Building & Maintenance 
Bill Tyler has been helping out us with a some extra cleaning and maintenance around the library. 
He’s always a reliable and hard-working helper. We are still waiting for the final steps on the 
electronic lock system.

machine had been dialed in and used only a moderate amount of color toner, and this machine needs 
to be tuned in. The old machine also finally gave out (it limped along just long enough!) As the town

Summary of Last Month 
➢ The yoga and breathwork classes showed a lot of initial interest with full classes 
and long waitlists for the January sessions. However, we had nearly 50% no-show 
rates for each program. We will adjust class sizes to absorb some of these 
absences, and an email was sent to all February and March registrants to be 
considerate of their registration. Hopefully we will see improvement for the 
remainder of the classes. 
➢  The treasure journaling workshop on Friday, January 16th went really well. We had 
15 enthusiastic crafters attend and create unique hand-sewn journals out of 
recycled materials. It was highly reviewed and these presenters will be on my list of 
programmers to ask back in the future for more crafting events. 
➢ On January 24th we held our Sock Hop Golden Oldies party despite the frigid 
temperatures outside. There were 15 people in the audience for this very fun and 
engaging concert. Denis Cormier brought costumes and great energy to make this 
an unforgettable show at the library.  
➢ For our virtual program offerings, there were 16 total registrations across the five 
programs held this month. Some were more popular than others, but it is 
encouraging to see the engagement rising. There are six virtual events planned for 
February, so we will see if the trend continues or starts to dip as the year 
progresses.
