LUNENBURG PUBLIC LIBRARY DIRECTOR’S REPORT 
   Staff Update 
We are coming off the best statistical Summer ever for the library! Our SRP was a wild success, with 
more than 400 participants. Starting registrations at the beginning of June also supported the 
readers who were interested in starting as soon as possible. The majority of registrants signed up 
during the month of June. We also served more than 1600 lunches during SRP, thanks to Main Street 
Bank and the Friends! The summer was very busy and staff have been putting in the extra effort to 
make it a spectacular experience for all who visit the library! 
FY25 concluded with very positive statistics on the whole. Submitting ARIS in August, we counted 
68,459 visitors between July 1 and June 30. 77,543 physical items circulated (for a value of $1.148M), 
with another 34,363 digital circulations. These are all increases, with the digital circulations jumping 
almost 30% over FY24. 
The next two months promise to be quite busy for our staff. Debbie Lafond’s retirement will be in 
early October. The day for the public to visit her and express their appreciation will be October 6 
between 10 and 5. Our celebrations of the 20th Anniversary of opening this facility take place during 
October. 
We are engaged in two job searches, Children’s Librarian and a Library Page. We are about to hire for 
the Library Page position, and we will begin to invite applicants to interview for the Children’s 
Librarian position late this week or early next week. The Town Manager organized a town employee 
wide event in August, which continued to support a friendly, collaborative atmosphere for town 
employees. The Library’s own Marabeth Balboni won recognition for her excellence. 
I presented to the Tri-Board (Selectboard, School Board, Finance Committee) meeting about Library 
budget anticipated needs for FY27 and beyond on August 26. The presentation was much more 
detailed than my usual presentations and much earlier in the year. I also participated in the 
Massachusetts Library Aid Association scholarship selection process, although I was not able to 
attend in person this year. 
We are supporting significant effort to engage in professional development for both Joslyn and 
Kaitee. They have signed up for a number of courses and workshops over the summer and into the 
fall. Joslyn has worked really hard to develop a story-time calendar for September-December and will 
be delivering those story-times. This endeavor will be a hand-on learning experience to help her 
develop her Children’s Librarian muscles and to help the organization bridge a transition to a new 
Children’s Librarian. Kaitee is focusing on customer service and reader’s advisory skills. We are also 
attempting to rebuild our 5th grade visit program, and establish a partnership with the school 
librarian at the Middle-High School. So far, after school crowds are even bigger than last year! 
I will be targeting two grants this fall. One will be from Emerson Hospital and the other will be a 
Community Foundation grant. We will also be introducing at-home delivery for Meals on Wheels 
participants. If those home-bound individuals would like personally selected library materials, they 
can join our program and the books will be delivered and returned by the Meals on Wheels drivers.  
   Programs

FY25 library programs brought in nearly 7,000 attendees at 410 programs. We hope that this upward 
trend will continue as we focus this year on balancing staff time with engagement at events. 
Especially with the Children’s Room transition and other anticipated commitments for our wonderful 
staff, we will have fewer resources to direct toward programming, so this balance is very important. 
After a brief lull in activity during August, the school year has really blasted off to a quick start. Book 
groups have grown (11 for both Bookworms and In-Betweeners groups), and our first story time had 
solid turn out (30) for a musical theme.  We are endeavoring to plan and schedule programs a bit 
further out to help with our workflow and conscientiously selecting events to offer. Our first Paint 
Night was a booming success: with only 20 available spots, more than 30 people were wait listed. 
In our Teen Room, we are also offering an activity (less structured and more “come and go” 
atmosphere than a “program”) three days each week. We are also offering approximately one teen 
program per week with an immediate goal of increasing program attendance. Our focus here is to 
develop an expectation around activity and engagement being regularly available for teens, an 
expectation that has been missing for a few years. 
Coming up: Around the World Storytime (9/17), Teen Coding Club (starts 9/16), LBA Lunch and Learn 
with Jay’s Cryptic Creatures (snakes, 9/20), a rebranded “After Hours” video game night (9/26), 
Monthly Music Series: History and Legends through Song with Sultana (9/24), “Stamped” Discussion 
and Author Q&A (9/25), Flu Clinic (9/20), and Weight Strengthening with Niki (returns 9/30). 
Anniversary Celebrations will be an Opening Ceremony on 10/3 and Closing Event with concert on 
10/24. Scavenger Hunts for three different age groups will be offered as well. 
   Outreach 
Outreach has been moderately successful over the summer. Having the Friends participate makes a 
lot of sense. Moving into the Fall, we are targeting events like LBA’s Trunk or Treat and visits to 
Arrive and Tri-Town. These are data gathering engagements for us, and could be very useful as a 
chance to advertise Friends benefits. Smokestack Coffee Roasters is our next target for partnership. 
We will also be starting at-home delivery partnering with LAAC’s Meals on Wheels as mentioned 
earlier in the report. 
▪ Building & Maintenance 
Electricity costs are significantly higher this summer than in past years, perhaps a result of a fully 
functioning HVAC system. DPW patched our parking lot, and I have discussed with Facilities our 
future capital needs, including repairing sidewalks. 
Two Friends funded projects are in the works for the bidding process: Sail shade for patio and 
motorized shades in the Main Hall. Order submitted to Blockhouse for café furniture. 
Our projector shade motor was repaired early in the summer, and our projector cart is currently 
receiving some maintenance on its caster wheels. I tightened the wobbly tabletops in the 
Community Room and all seem to be functioning appropriately at the moment.

The sculpture for the Robert Conroy Memorial Garden has been delivered. It is even more inspiring 
and heart-warming than expected. A fantastic result from Kevin Boussom, the artist. 
The rainwater garden design is complete. The DPW and its contractor should be installing the design 
this Fall. This project will add a significant new demo space to the property and support education 
around dealing with storm water runoff for homeowners and for businesses. 
▪ Technology 
As part of our preparation for the Tri-Board presentation, I met with IT department (Casey, Mason, 
Kara) to discuss our future replacement schedules and needs. Our Technology plan and replacement 
schedule should be a part of that department’s presentations moving forward. 
It does seem that our ongoing hard drive issues in our public access computers are a result of 
improper selection of hardware. Deep Freeze is likely frying the hard drives, and we may need to 
upgrade to Enterprise quality SSDs to avoid the issue. We upgraded to cloud-based management of 
Deep Freeze to save significant time for Melanie.

• Joslyn did an excellent job planning and executing the weekly craft sessions for 
the kids. There was lots of enthusiastic participation in these events! 
• Weekly lunch and story time started off slowly but definitely gained momentum 
as we went along.  Offering a storytime event in the summer was a request that 
we have had many times, and it felt appropriate to offer this service. Sitting in the 
air-conditioned Story Craft Room, eating your delicious, free lunch while listening 
to a story or two definitely worked for many families. 
• Our Ice Cream Social was a big hit!  We provided the ice cream and invited 
families to bring their favorite toppings.  We had a well-organized set-up and lots 
of volunteer help which certainly contributed to the success of this event- and 
who doesn’t love a bowl of yummy ice cream shared with friends on a steamy, 
hot day! 
• We hosted a return favorite event this summer- Water Day. Using a couple of 
hoses, some buckets and sponges, a jar of marbles, and, of course, our lovely 
water feature- the kids were able to play with water in so many fun ways!  This 
fun event cost $0, required lots of sweat equity and help from an army of 
volunteers!   Kids and water- we couldn’t miss! 
• A visit with the Whale Mobile was also a star event of the summer.  Sitting inside 
a 40’ whale while someone educates and entertains you about these amazing 
creatures is an experience that the kids will remember for a long time!

• Another big hit of the summer was an alpaca story time with Kristi Mahoney.  
Ms Mahoney shared her book Alpacas Make Terrible Librarians (it was agreed 
that they do) and then we all got to visit with and learn about Jackie Chabot’s 
alpacas! 
• Kids and families got to meet Dr Fortuna, our new superintendent, here at the 
library.  Dr. Fortuna hosted a lively story time where she shared a couple of 
books about starting school and answered questions from the kids and families.  
She was wonderful- calm, relaxed and friendly! 
• On a warm, sunny Friday afternoon, kids and families gathered at the DPW 
building to “touch a truck” So many participants for this great event!  We were a 
presence at this and shared, along with information about our fall activities at the 
library, temporary truck tattoos, lollipops and lots of goodwill.  We are hoping this 
becomes an annual event! 
• And, lastly, a huge thank you to Main Street Bank for providing the opportunity to 
serve free lunches every Monday-Thursday. Many families came by every day 
to take advantage of this, and we know that for many of these it was fulfilling a 
need.  It felt great to be able to do this for our patrons.
