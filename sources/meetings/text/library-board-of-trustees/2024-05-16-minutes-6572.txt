All three of our pages will be leaving us this year, graduating and moving on to their next steps. Two 
have given their notice, and we have interviews this week and last.  
We will be piloting the implementation of Google Analytics for the Aspen discovery layer for 
CWMARS, and working the kinks of using Aspen to make a library website. I don’t anticipate this 
impacting the Friends portion of the site, and based on the information I have received, the website 
we create should then be a much more effective marketing tool for us. 
I fielded a long call 30+minutes with a mother who was disappointed with our Teen Room and 
Program Policy approved at last month’s meeting. We’ve hosted several MCAS testing sessions for a 
“virtual” school that serves this area. 
The Secretary of the Commonwealth’s office found in favor of our response to the request after our 
second revision. They have since opened a reconsideration of that finding at the insistence of the 
requestor. 
   Programs 
Our Spring puzzle swap was popular, though numbers were down a little. Still more than 500 puzzles 
changed hands and over 40 people. The photography exhibit was highly regarded as well. 30 people 
turned out to the reception. This is an exhibit that will be displayed in Billerica next. 
Robin has been helping with lots of growing/planting programs, including a Mother’s Day planting 
with the children. The plants that we have started over the last few months are growing and looking 
like they will be thriving when they go into the ground, especially Kaitee’s Salsa Garden starts. 
30 attendees at the first session of the ongoing meditations sessions! Nearly 70 at the Creeque Alley 
music performance! 
Kaitee will be developing an intro to coding workshop for teens, using some Raspberry Pi boards, 
and I think this offering will be very meaningful to Lunenburg patrons. 
   Building & Maintenance 
I’ll be meeting with a vendor on Thursday to answer some more questions on the EV chargers. They 
made a site visit and discussed the best potential places to put the chargers and infrastructure. Initial 
feedback was the front corner of the library lot near Memorial Dr, based on the hardware that is or is 
not on the various poles along Mass Ave. 
Teen Room estimate was delayed, still not received as of Monday. The Friends funded solar shades 
project is going out to bid finally, and the exterior maintenance funded by ARPA will be next. The 
building inspector confirmed that the sign fits the by-law, so I am awaiting an electric estimate for 
lighting. I”ll be having Tim prepare to install ASAP. 
Don and his new partner, Al, have been buttoning up the little lingering projects around town. They 
got our window sashes stained and have patched a couple of spots of trim. Robin Venezia and Bill 
Tyler have installed a brick border around the Pocket Pollinator Garden to keep the wood chips from

HIGHLIGHTS FOR APRIL 
• Solar Eclipse Viewing Party- Wow, what a great intergenerational event for our community!  
The weather was perfect, which made both sitting out on the patio and the actual viewing very 
successful.  Everyone seemed so happy to be able to have a place to not only view this historic 
event but to do so with others!  The simple snacks we provided, and the lawn games that were 
out added to the enjoyment of all. Good job, LPL!   
• STEM PROGRAM- We hosted 1 S T E M program with Chris Rodrigues this month.  The kids 
learned about Green Screen Technology which is used to create many of the special effects we 
see on television and in the movies. Using iPads, and some helpful applications, they learned 
about how this technology works and had an opportunity to create short videos of their own 
Using simple animated characters.   
• Food Explorers S T E M program- In this great cooking program the kids got to make Chili Mac 
and Cheese and Carrot Cake Energy Bites. They got to choose a variety of toppings for their mac 
and cheese, shredded carrots for the energy bites and made their own glaze.  The aromas 
coming from the Story Craft Room were so yummy! Some parents were amazed that their child, 
usually a picky eater, ate the foods they prepared! 
• Spy Week- The kids and families were enthusiastic participants in our April School 
Vacation week events as they had an opportunity to create their own spy kits, create a 
disguise for their official spy badge and then collect clues to solve the crime!  Who was 
the perpetrator of the diabolic crime, you ask?  Taylor Swift!  After Taylor broke into our 
library to make off with one of her favorite series, Nancy Drew, she had a change of 
heart and decided not to make off with the goods.  But she did hide them somewhere in 
the library.  The kids had to gather clues, decode them and then figure out who the 
criminal was and where she hid the books. Once we had the code, we were able to 
rescue our beloved books!

Trustee Report 
Adult Services Department 
May 2024 
 
A few outstanding programs we hosted in late April and May include: 
➢ The world hand drumming workshop on April 17th had 6 attendees who brought their own hand 
drums to practice Balkan rhythms along with the instructor’s clarinet melodies. It was a beautiful 
program that every participant enjoyed and would love to have again.  
➢ A stellar music concert by Creeque Alley brought one of the largest audiences to the library this year, 
with 68 people grooving to folk music of the 60’s and 70’s in the community room. Hearing the 
audience sing-along, laugh, and reminisce about songs from past years was a wonderful experience. 
➢ One of the biggest surprises for spring programming was the crowd we pulled in for the first 
community supported meditation night on May 7th. Emily Zaffino approached me earlier in the year to 
request a meditation group at the library, and after several discussions we refined our goals and 
ideas to this bi-weekly semi-guided community supported meditation circle. Emily leads the group in 
some basics of meditation to start each session, but it ends with quiet time for individuals to practice 
on their own. We had 30 people of varied experience join us, and we are looking forward to seeing 
how it grows over time. 
➢ The spring puzzle swap was another success for community driven programming. Held on Saturday 
May 11th, we had 45 people come to choose from over 500 puzzles during the swap. Despite being 
held quarterly, the swaps continue to maintain popularity in both attendance and donations.  
Looking ahead, here are the upcoming May and June events at the library: 
➢ Jazz guitarist Abe Ovadia will be performing on Friday May 24th as part of the monthly music series. 
➢ Leominster Credit Union is coming to the library to give a presentation on mortgage basics on 
Wednesday May 29th at 7:00 pm. 
➢ At the request of a patron, we are starting a biweekly “Adult Community Chat & Color” circle on 
Thursday mornings in June. The first session will be on June 6th at 10:30 am in the conference room. 
➢ Beekeeper John Hedly is slated to visit on Tuesday June 11th to give an in-depth talk on beekeeping 
for beginners.  
➢ Debbie and I will be running a family tie-dye event to prepare for the summer on Thursday June 13th 
➢ An Irish music concert is booked outside (weather permitting) on Friday June 14th with “Sons of 
Blarney”, fronted by the Lurias. 
➢ Mike Familant, a bigfoot hunter and expeditionist, is coming on Tuesday June 18th in the evening to 
talk about the evidence he’s found across the United States.
