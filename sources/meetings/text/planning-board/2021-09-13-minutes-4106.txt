APPOINTMENTS: 
733 Massachusetts Avenue - The project engineer, Alfred Berry,  the Project Manager , and the property owner Tuan Dao  for 
733 Massachusetts Avenue  were pre sent.  There was one existing home on the site.  The proposal was for three additional 
homes on the site, sharing one driveway.  Board members felt the proposal was contrary to the zoning bylaws and was not 
considered a Common Driveway as defined in the zo ning bylaw.  P. Bertram questioned  the maintenance of a single drive and 
sewer line for four homes.  T he owner anticipated a condo -type development with an association fee.  This would be a single 
driveway servicing four houses on a single lot.  The Project Manager inquired if the Board would be more amenable to a 55  and 
over development.  A. Berry said he didn’t see the difference between this proposal and  a condo-type development where the 
buildings were separated.  A. Burney responded that a condominium could be dissolved and divided into four individual lots via 
the ANR process.  The applicant likened his project to the proposed development next door at 711 Massachusetts Avenue , 
which consisted of four 2 -unit townhouse buildings .  A. Burney emphasized that 711 Massachusetts Avenue went through  a 
Zoning Board of Appeals process for the use and then came before the Board for site plan approval and stormwater permitting.  
The applicant inquired if they should approach the Zoning Board of Appeals and then com e back to the Board.  A. Burney noted 
that the current plan would not be permissible for site plan approval with the Board.  Townhouses with a shared wall would be 
permissible.   
 
496 Massachusetts Avenue- The Water District approached the Board in October  2020 for a De Minimis change to its Decision 
for the installation of a 24 square foot granite sign near the entrance.  As the signage exceeded the allowable size within the 
Limited Business/Residential District, the Board did not approve the request.  Ove r the ensuing months, the applicant had the 
request reviewed by Counsel.  It was determined the Water District was considered a governmental entity and would be allowed 
a sign up to 24 square feet.  The Water District returned to the Board for approval for  a De Minimis change for installation of a 
sign.  P. Bertram made Motion to approve a De Minimis change for 496 Massachusetts Avenue Lunenburg Water District 
to install the requested sign, M. Brenner Seconded, all aye.  Vote passed 5-0.

1263 Reservoir Road & 435 Leominster-Shirley Road (aka 535 Leominster-Shirley +/-)  
 ANR Plan- Plan presented by Chris Anderson, Hannigan Engineering.  The Plan would separate off approximately 30 
acres of land which would be associated with the anticipated project (known as Lunenburg Central).   (The address 
would be changed to 475 Leominster -Shirley Road once the ANR Plan was endorsed.)   P. Bertram made Motion to 
approve the ANR Plan for 1263 Reservoir Road and 435 Leominster -Shirley Road.  Motion was not seconded.  
M. Allison identified the entire  frontage as being covered by a utility easement.  C. Anderson specified that was for the 
water line relocation.  M. Allison responded the Plan specified utility, pole and power easements. C. Anderson noted 
there were several utility poles that crossed the  front of the property, feeding the Keating plant.  W. Hannigan stressed 
the pole easement was for the existing poles that fed the Keating property.  As the project would take the land away 
from Keating, an easement had to be provided to Keating so it could continue to get power.  The water line was 
anticipated to be relocated toward the front of the property , as shown on the revised site plan .  Its final location would 
be determined during construction.  W. Hannigan believed the water line and utility pole s would not be located in the 
easement shown on the ANR Plan.  A. Burney informed the applicant that Section 250- 5.1.B.(4)(b) stated, “Except for 
public utilities in place at the time of the passage of this provision, no more than  30% of required frontage  shall be 
encumbered by easements, drives, access strips, or ways of any type.”   M. Allison noted that if the applicant turned the 
proposed ten foot right -of-way easement (shown on the site plan) over to the town, there would not be the frontage 
issue.  A. Burney stated that this would be subject to the Building Commissioner’s interpretation.  W. Hannigan granted 
the Board an extension of time  for ANR endorsement  to September 30, 2021, in order for both he and A. Burney to 
discuss the interpretation with the Building Commissioner.
