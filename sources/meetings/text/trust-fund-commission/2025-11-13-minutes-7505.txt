1.  Scholarship  Gifts  Under  M.G.L.  Chapter  44,  Section  53A,  municipalities  may  accept  gifts  or  grants  of  money  or  
property
 
from
 
individuals,
 
corporations,
 
or
 
other
 
entities.
 
These
 
gifts
 
must
 
be
 
formally
 
accepted
 
by
 
the
 
appropriate
 
municipal
 
authority,
 
such

the
 
Board
 
of
 
Selectmen
 
or
 
City
 
Council,
 
and
 
may
 
be
 
expended
 
without
 
further
 
appropriation,
 
provided
 
they
 
are
 
used
 
for
 
the
 
purpose
 
specified
 
by
 
the
 
donor.
 
 
For
 
educational
 
purposes,
 
M.G.L.
 
Chapter
 
71,
 
Section
 
37A
 
allows
 
school
 
committees
 
to
 
accept
 
gifts
 
and
 
grants
 
for
 
scholarships.
 
These
 
funds
 
are
 
deposited
 
with
 
the
 
municipal
 
treasurer,
 
held
 
in
 
a
 
separate
 
account,
 
and
 
may
 
be
 
spent
 
by
 
the
 
school
 
committee
 
without
 
further
 
appropriation.
 
2.  Scholarship  Funds  via  Tax  Check-Off  M.G.L.  Chapter  60,  Section  3C  permits  municipalities  to  establish  scholarship  funds  
supported
 
by
 
voluntary
 
taxpayer
 
donations
 
through
 
a
 
check-off
 
option
 
on
 
tax
 
bills.
 
These
 
donations
 
are
 
deposited
 
into
 
a
 
special
 
account
 
and
 
managed
 
by
 
a
 
scholarship
 
committee,
 
which
 
includes
 
the
 
school
 
superintendent
 
and
 
appointed
 
residents.
 
The
 
committee
 
selects
 
recipients
 
based
 
on
 
financial
 
need,
 
academic
 
performance,
 
and
 
other
 
criteria.
 
3.  Scholarship  Trusts  and  Oversight  Scholarships  established  as  trusts  fall  under  the  governance  of  the  Massachusetts  Uniform  
Trust
 
Code,
 
codified
 
in
 
M.G.L.
 
Chapter
 
203E.
 
This
 
code
 
outlines
 
the
 
creation,
 
administration,
 
and
 
enforcement
 
of
 
trusts,
 
including
 
requirements
 
for
 
a
 
lawful
 
purpose,
 
identifiable
 
beneficiaries,
 
and
 
fiduciary
 
duties
 
of
 
trustees.
 
 
Importantly,
 
any
 
scholarship
 
established
 
as
 
a
 
trust
 
is
 
subject
 
to
 
the
 
management
 
and
 
appropriation

of
 
the
 
Board
 
of
 
Commissioners
 
of
 
Trust
 
Funds,
 
as
 
provided
 
in
 
M.G.L.
 
Chapter
 
41,
 
Sections
 
45
 
and
 
47.
 
This
 
board
 
is

for
 
managing
 
all
 
trust
 
funds
 
given
 
or
 
bequeathed
 
for
 
the
 
benefit
 
of
 
the
 
town
 
or
 
its

the
 
donor
 
specifies

The
 
board
 
must
 
invest
 
the
 
funds
 
prudently,
 
maintain
 
detailed
 
records,
 
and
 
report
 
annually
 
on
 
fund
 
activity.
 
4.  Investment  Practices  Trustees  must  comply  with  the  Massachusetts  Prudent  Investor  Act,  M.G.L.  Chapter  203C,  
which

trust

Trustees

economic

Additionally,

203D

and

trust

Trustees
 
may
 
adjust
 
between
 
principal
 
and
 
income
 
to
 
ensure
 
fair
