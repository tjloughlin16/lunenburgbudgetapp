Presentation of Special Town Meeting Articles Presentation of Brooks House Report by Stephen Wallace, Director of Planning and Economic Development  The Director of Planning and Economic Development, Stephen Wallace, presented the report requested by the November 2024 Special Town Meeting article sponsored by the Architectural Preservation District Commission (APDC). The report addressed whether the Brooks House (built in 1835, within the Village Center and Architectural Preservation Districts) could be split into a standalone parcel and sold or leased for restoration. He reported that: ● The parcel could be created through the Approval Not Required process, because the Village Center District has no minimum lot size and requires 75 feet of frontage, and the property has approximately 160 feet on Massachusetts Avenue. ● The principal obstacle is access. The Library Trustees and School Department declined to formalize use of the library and school driveways, respectively. Use of those driveways would also require a zoning variance, because the bylaw requires access from the lot frontage. ● Access from Massachusetts Avenue would require a MassDOT driveway permit, redesign of drainage basins on either side of the house, and relocation of a utility pole and removal of a tree. ● A request for proposals (RFP) should be prepared with Town Counsel to address special conditions protecting the town's interests. Member McQuaid supported pursuing an RFP at no cost to the town but said another curb cut on Massachusetts Avenue was not ideal. Mr. Wallace said a sale was more likely to succeed than a lease, and that a possible "Plan B" was eventual demolition to provide drainage for a future T.C. Passios replacement. Vice-Chair Lehtinen asked about alternatives, including whether an RFP could go through the capital process. The Town Manager noted that tonight's item was a dress rehearsal of the report to Town Meeting and that next steps should be a separate agenda item. Members Alonzo and McLeod voiced concerns about leaving a private parcel surrounded by town property and about losing control of the parcel. Member McLeod suggested a right of first refusal. Chair Emkey noted concerns about parking and about the need for a new driveway. The Board reached consensus that a future agenda item on an RFP, to follow the Special Town Meeting, would be scheduled. Public comment: Anthony Sculimbrene, 5 Valley Road, of the APDC, stated that no municipal use existed for the building and that, if the RFP failed, demolition would likely follow. He suggested an underground stormwater chamber system could resolve access and drainage concerns and called demolition solely to create more lawn "insane." Mali Wagner, 191 White Street, asked that upcoming changes to the surrounding lots be considered as part of next steps. Mr. Wallace also asked members to review the planning board's proposed May 2027 zoning amendments on the Planning Board webpage and to submit comments by the end of November. A public forum is scheduled for December 1st at the Adult Activity Center.  SWTF members present: Chair Tim Wilsmer, Member John Beale, Member Jenny Pewtherer, Member Renee Emkey.  SWTF Members Absent: Member Mark Muzzi. Also present: Jaurice Schwartz, consultant from Weston & Sampson, and Bill Bernard, DPW Director.  Jaurice Schwartz, consultant from Weston & Sampson, presented on funding mechanisms for compliance with the federal MS4 stormwater permit. Key points: ● The town maintains more than 450 outfalls and over 1,000 catch basins, with infrastructure estimated at more than $70 million, and much of it is over 50 years old. ● The MS4 permit's fines for noncompliance range from $5,000 to $50,000 per day. EPA historically works with communities toward compliance. ● DPW Director Bill Bernard reported that annual stormwater spending has been roughly $100,000, against an estimated $480,000 per year needed for compliance. ● A stormwater utility would charge fees based on impervious surface, using an equivalent residential unit (ERU) of 4,200 square feet. The proposed rate has not been finalized by the SWTF but current estimate is $85 per ERU, not to exceed $100 for the first three years, with a flat rate for single- and two-family homes. ● A stormwater utility would carry about 4.5% overhead, credits and abatements of up to 50% for maintained on-site controls, and annual approval of the budget by Town Meeting. Fee collection would begin no earlier than July 2028. ● Under the operating budget alternative, an average single-family property would contribute approximately $109 annually towards stormwater maintenance/MS4 compliance, versus $85 under the utility. ● The November Special Town Meeting article is an advisory, nonbinding straw poll on the preferred funding mechanism. Clerk McLeod asked whether waivers were available. Ms. Schwartz stated that Lunenburg was not known to be eligible. Clerk McLeod voiced concern that a nonbinding question at a Special Town Meeting could mislead voters, citing prior appropriations of roughly $80,000 for stormwater utility work and recent voter rejection of an override. He stated he would not support placing the question on the warrant. Chair Emkey responded that the data work was necessary to evaluate a utility and that the article was intended to gather public input without bias. SWTF member John Beale stated that the central issue was the $460,000 funding need, and that the utility is one vehicle to meet it. Member McQuaid advised the SWTF to prepare a clear answer on how fees could rise and who would approve increases. Member Alonzo expressed strong support, calling a utility the most equitable approach to an existing mandate. Vice-Chair Lehtinen thanked the presenters, recommended a shorter presentation and suggested highlighting grants and public-private partnerships.  Motion by Member Beale to adjourn. Seconded by Member Jenny Pewtherer. Call for vote: Four – aye. Motion passed unanimously (4-0-0).  Renee Emkey re-joined the Select Board meeting and resumed the chairing the meeting.
