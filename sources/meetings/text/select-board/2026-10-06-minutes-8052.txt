Discussion on Future of the Tri-board  Chair Emkey opened a discussion on the future of the Tri-board, noting that the School Committee and Finance Committee had already discussed the topic but the Select Board had not yet weighed in. She asked for the Board's thoughts before circling back with the other chairs. Member McQuaid expressed that while he was not opposed to continuing the Tri-board, he did not see the value in meeting simply for the sake of meeting and felt the town had managed without it before. Clerk McLeod expressed a generally favorable view, noting that keeping lines of communication open between the three bodies is valuable and suggesting that meeting once or twice a year, particularly before budget season, could be beneficial. He acknowledged, however, that if other boards had no interest, that was acceptable. Member Alonzo was candid in his assessment. He stated that while he was not opposed to the Tri-board in principle, if the goal had been to achieve a unified front going into Town Meeting on budget matters, "that failed pretty significantly," as significant division had emerged among the parties. He questioned whether the 
===PAGE 3===
Select Board Meeting Minutes October 6, 2026  3 
structure of the meetings with up to eighteen or twenty participants going around the room was an effective use of time and cautioned that a poorly run Tri-board could "exacerbate existing problems" rather than resolve them. He emphasized that any future meeting must have a clear, defined charge and specific, high-level agenda items rather than serving as a broad check-in. Chair Emkey noted that common themes emerging from the other boards' discussions included a desire for high-level strategy sessions around budget priorities. She also noted that Finance Committee had found value in getting early looks at departmental budgets, and that this practice could continue independent of a formal Tri-board structure. Town Manager Warren-Dyment reflected that one challenge during the prior year was that feedback and direction from Tri-board members came very late in the budget process by which point it was nearly impossible for staff to meaningfully incorporate changes. She indicated that having departments present to the Finance Committee earlier in the season had been identified as a productive improvement that could continue without a full Tri-board. The Board did not take a formal vote but reached a general understanding that any future Tri-board activity should have a well-defined purpose, a focused agenda, and should not repeat the previous model of extensive, broadly attended sessions. Chair Emkey indicated she would circle back with the Finance Committee and School Committee chairs to share the Board's perspective.  Discussion of Additions/Modifications to Bylaws Ch. 12 Boards, Commissions, and Committees Chair Emkey introduced a discussion on Chapter 12 of the Town Bylaws, which covers boards, commissions, and committees. She shared a document identifying four committees whose membership includes Select Board representatives and whose bylaw language could benefit from clarification particularly around whether membership must be a sitting Select Board member or may be a designee, term of membership, and what occurs when a member departs the Board. The four committees defined in the bylaws are: the Americans with Disabilities Committee, the Building Committee, the Capital Planning Committee, and the Finance Committee Appointing Committee (the latter was subsequently clarified to reside in the Town Charter under Section 2-3, not the bylaws). Chair Emkey also identified four additional committees with Select Board representation whose membership and charge are defined only on the town website and not codified in the bylaws: the Cable Advisory Committee, the Economic Development Committee, the Lunenburg Municipal Building Design Committee, and the Stormwater Task Force. Member Alonzo identified the Green Communities Committee as another body that should be added to the list. The Board discussed the appropriate standard language for Select Board membership on other committees, with Member Alonzo advocating that all such provisions should consistently read "member of the Select Board, or its designee." Chair Emkey indicated she had reached out to the Bylaw Review Committee chair to avoid duplicating efforts and was awaiting a response. The Board agreed to move forward with refining the document incorporating Green Communities and any other omitted committees and to bring proposed amendments forward for the spring Town Meeting rather than the November Special Town Meeting. Article X – Middle High School Track and Field Engineering Services ($195,830): This article, submitted by the School Committee, requests funding for engineering services from Gale Engineering covering design through construction management. Town Manager Warren-Dyment noted the quote from December 2025 remains valid. The Board engaged in substantial discussion on whether this article was appropriate for a Special Town Meeting, given that capital projects are typically reviewed through the Capital Planning Committee process. Article X – Nonbinding Advisory Vote on MS4 Stormwater Funding Mechanisms: This article asks Town Meeting voters to express a preference between two mechanisms for funding MS4 stormwater compliance: inclusion in the operating budget, or establishment of a stormwater utility (Enterprise Fund). The vote would be nonbinding and advisory only. Clerk McLeod raised several concerns, including that the article and the process leading up to it may not sufficiently put residents on notice of the full financial implications particularly for businesses, which would 
===PAGE 8===
Select Board Meeting Minutes October 6, 2026  8 
bear a proportionally larger share of a stormwater utility fee. He also objected to language in Option 2 stating that current funding "has historically been insufficient and will likely require a Proposition 2.5 override in the future," characterizing that as editorializing. He suggested the article, in his view, essentially asks voters to choose between absorbing costs in the operating budget or accepting a new utility bill and that the stormwater utility effectively bypasses the protections of Proposition 2.5. Member Alonzo pushed back on those concerns, noting that MS4 compliance is a mandatory federal and state requirement not a discretionary program and that the fundamental question is not whether to comply but how to pay for compliance. He affirmed that a stormwater enterprise fund would distribute costs on a per-property, proportional basis similar to the sewer enterprise fund, and suggested that a nonbinding vote is a reasonable way to get public feedback on the preferred path forward. Chair Emkey explained that the article is intended to help the Stormwater Task Force understand where to focus its efforts, and that a public information session presenting the pros and cons of both mechanisms is planned in addition to the Select Board and Finance Committee presentations. The Board agreed to defer the vote on this article to the October 20th meeting, following the Stormwater Task Force's presentation.
