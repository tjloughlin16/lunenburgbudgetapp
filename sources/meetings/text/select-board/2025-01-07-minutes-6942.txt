Motion passed unanimously (5-0-0). 7:30 PM Municipal Building Design Committee Status Report  Vice-Chair Jeffreys, serving as Chair of the Municipal Building Design Committee, presented an interim status report on the Committee's work with its architectural team. He outlined a three-phase approach to consolidating and modernizing town and school administrative facilities: Phase 1 involves construction of a new Town Hall on the Ritter Memorial building parcel on School Street. Phase 2 involves renovation of the existing Town Hall into a community meeting house and renovation of the Ritter building for school administration offices, including the ACE program. Phase 3, not before the Board this evening, involves repurposing the TC Passeals building for community uses. The Chair described the space programming and adjacency planning undertaken with the architects, noting that the new Town Hall would be connected to the renovated Ritter building and would house municipal offices including the Tax Collector, Town Accountant, Assessors, Town Clerk, IT, and related functions. The Ritter building would accommodate school district administrative offices and the ACE program on the lower level. The existing Town Hall, once vacated, would be renovated as a meeting house with conference rooms, a gallery space, an expanded hearing room, modernized elevator access, and public restrooms, with the exterior appearance largely preserved. The Chair presented architectural renderings and CAD models prepared by the design team, noting the Committee's preference for an exterior design that is "distinctively different" from the existing historic buildings per Department of Interior preservation standards, while reflecting the architectural character of the town center. The Committee favored a design featuring a covered walkway and prominent forward-facing facade along Mass Ave. A covered, solar-paneled walkway from the parking area to the building was also proposed. Member Emkey inquired about parking adequacy and the scale of the proposed footprint. The Chair confirmed that the new Town Hall footprint is approximately 5,000 square feet with the existing Ritter building at 6,500 square feet, and that the architects are confident that expanded parking at 30 School Street, combined with existing nearby public parking, will be sufficient without eliminating all green space from the site. Member Alonzo raised the importance of future-proofing the building, citing the Town's growth rate, and asked whether a historical analysis of staffing levels had been conducted to ensure sufficient space for future needs. The Chair confirmed that the space planning process included increases beyond current square footage for each department and that approximately five to six additional offices could be accommodated without additions to the building. He acknowledged that a historical staffing analysis had not been conducted but indicated the Committee would consider that approach

Greg Roy, citizen-at-large member of the Municipal Building Design Committee, affirmed the quality and rigor of the planning process and noted the positive reception from the architectural team. The total cost estimate remains targeted at approximately $15 million, with a cost assumption of $600 per square foot, though elevator costs and other specialized elements may increase the total. 7:50 PM Meeting with Steve Walker, Chair of Public Access Cable Committee Re: Municipal Building Plan  Steve Walker, Chair of the Public Access Cable (PAC) Committee, accompanied by staff member Jo Toale, presented an overview of PAC's operations, funding structure, and space needs in the context of the Municipal Building Plan. Mr. Walker emphasized that PAC is a town department and should receive equal consideration in the municipal facilities planning process. He noted that PAC is self-funded through franchise fees governed under Section 622 of the Cable Act of 1984, which are restricted for cable-related public, educational, and government services. He reported that the enterprise fund has been invested, generating approximately $36,000 in its first year, and that franchise fees are declining at approximately 3–4% annually. PAC's current studio in the TC Passios building is inadequate and has been treated as a temporary arrangement since the department moved in around 2012. Mr. Walker described PAC's programming activities, including government meeting coverage (approximately 30 different meetings), story time, sports coverage, community events, and productions distributed to over 85 public access stations statewide. He noted PAC's YouTube channel had 1,114 subscribers and over 3,700 views in the prior 28 days. He outlined the space requirements the Committee had refined, totaling approximately 1,425 square feet of dedicated studio space, including a 625-square-foot studio, 200-square-foot control room, two offices at 100 square feet each, and a 200-square-foot equipment and editing room. He acknowledged that corridors, bathrooms, and security provisions were excluded from that figure under the assumption that PAC would be integrated into a larger building. Mr. Walker stressed that a standalone building would require additional elements—corridors, restrooms, mechanical space, parking, and security provisions—and that cost estimates from 2022 placed such a building at approximately $1.5–2 million for the shell alone, not including interior buildout. He noted that PAC's enterprise fund has reserves of approximately $900,000 earmarked for interior buildout, but that operating a standalone facility would strain the fund to the point where staffing would be at risk.

The Chair explained that the Municipal Building Design Committee had placed PAC in Phase 3 of the plan, in part because TC Passios was assumed to remain viable and because the Committee was concerned that incorporating PAC into Phase 1 or 2 might complicate the project's viability at Town Meeting. He noted that the Committee had heard no public support for incorporating PAC into the earlier phases, and that the Ritter parcel does not have sufficient land to accommodate a PAC building while also providing the required handicapped parking and vehicle access. He proposed that if the Board directed the Committee to address PAC in Phase 1 or 2, the most viable site would be 30 School Street, where a standalone white-box building could be constructed separately and voted on independently at Town Meeting. Member Alonzo expressed strong support for accelerating PAC's placement in the plan, citing the deteriorating condition of TC Passios, the essential nature of PAC's services, and the risk of losing equipment and service continuity. He called on anyone with available commercial space to reach out to the town as a potential interim solution. Member Emkey expressed concern about the TC Passios timeline, noting the risk of the boiler failing before a permanent solution is in place, and asked about the feasibility of shared studio space with neighboring communities as an interim measure. Mr. Walker acknowledged that option had been discussed only briefly and expressed concern about being subordinated to larger partner organizations. Clerk Moore inquired about the scope of the Committee's charge regarding 30 School Street and noted that the charge language appeared to already allow the Committee to analyze other town properties. The Chair agreed that the Board's explicit direction to consider 30 School Street would strengthen the Committee's ability to proceed. Greg Roy, Municipal Building Design Committee member, stated that in his view there was no need to keep PAC in Phase 3, that a standalone building presented a natural cost separation that could be presented transparently to Town Meeting, and that the phasing structure was flexible enough to accommodate the change. The Board reached consensus directing the Municipal Building Design Committee to incorporate PAC space into Phase 1 or Phase 2 of the Municipal Building Plan, with 30 School Street identified as the preferred site for a standalone PAC facility, to be developed in coordination with the architectural team. Action Re: Comparison Communities for Wage and Other Studies  Vice-Chair Jeffreys presented a revised list of comparison communities, incorporating data on average single-family tax bills, median family income, and an affordability ratio (average tax bill divided by median family income) for each community. Based on the analysis prepared by town staff, the following removals were proposed: Rutland, due to a median household income of $155,000; Templeton, due to an average tax bill of $4,338; Concord, with a tax bill of $19,104 and an affordability ratio of approximately 9%; and Acton, with a tax bill of $13,992 and an affordability ratio of 9.12%. Lunenburg's affordability ratio is approximately 6.18%. A discussion arose regarding the City of Gardner, which had an affordability ratio of 7.77%—slightly outside the 25% threshold above Lunenburg's ratio. Town staff recommended retaining Gardner given that the town had recently lost a DPW Director and an Assistant Town Accountant to Gardner at higher pay rates, making it a relevant comparator for recruitment and compensation purposes. Motion to accept the revised list of comparable competitive communities, inclusive of the City of Gardner, was made by Vice-Chair Jeffreys and seconded by Member Alonzo. Call for vote: Four – Aye. The motion passed unanimously (4-0-0). 3. Status Update on ARPA Funds  Interim Town Manager Terenzini provided an update on the status of ARPA funds. He reported that following the receipt of tighter-than-anticipated bids on other school projects, no surplus funds remained to cover a $62,000 shortfall on the Turkey Hill Elementary School fire alarm project, plus up to $25,000 in engineering and contingency costs. To address the gap, the Town transferred approximately $20,000 in unused capital funds internally to Turkey Hill and identified an additional $75,000 in unallocated ARPA funds, providing the school department with sufficient funds to execute the contract and approximately $13,000 toward engineering and contingency. To preserve flexibility for the incoming Town Manager's compensation package, the Interim Town Manager elected not to write a continuity plan for previously set-aside ARPA administrative funds, choosing instead to apply those funds to existing administration and finance salaries and freeze the unused balances. This approach reduces audit risk and ensures that sufficient funds can be reversed and redirected upon finalization of the Town Manager's contract. The Interim Town Manager thanked staff, particularly the finance team, for their diligent work in managing the ARPA closeout process. 4.
