PUBLIC COMMENT  Public  Anthony Sculimbrene of 5 Valley Road spoke as a private citizen and noted that his comments had not been vetted by the Architectural Preservation District Commission (APDC), which he chairs, or the Lunenburg Municipal Building Design Committee, of which he is Vice Chair. He stated that the Building Commissioner had classified the structure proposed at 30 School Street for the Lunenburg Food Pantry as a permanent building. He disagreed with that classification based on the Planning Board notice describing a temporary relocation, OpenGov documents describing the structure as a trailer, and the fact that the structure would be leased. He stated that he believed the matter should come before the APDC under Chapter 113 and urged the Town to determine a long-term plan for the Food Pantry before moving locations and suggested a permanent building committee.

John Bowen of 162 Highland Street discussed procedures at the September 3 Special Town Meeting. He stated that Section 81-9 of the Town bylaws requires a two-thirds vote to raise or appropriate money at a Special Town Meeting and argued that transfers should be treated similarly. He described his point of order and concerns regarding the handling of motions, vote counts, and meeting procedure. Discussion on Lunenburg Food Pantry (taken out of order at the discretion of the Board)  Nancy Beach, President of the Lunenburg Food Pantry, and Ann Nelson, Food Pantry Board member, updated the Board on the temporary relocation to 30 School Street. They reported that Dillis & Roy was preparing the site plan application for Planning Board review and that a zoning variance would be required. Powell Stone and Gravel had offered to construct a 40-by-80-foot pad for the proposed 24-by-64-foot mobile unit and the Food Pantry trailer, with Dustin Powell managing the work and Jeff Sullivan serving as general contractor. The Food Pantry also reported on the existing septic system, temporary electrical service, water hookup, parking lot conditions, and ongoing discussions with Triumph regarding the mobile units. The Town Manager stated that DPW and Facilities had already been contacted regarding temporary pothole patching and noted that full parking lot reclamation would be a significant future expense.

 The Town Manager explained that Town Counsel recommended a license agreement rather than a lease because a lease would require Town Meeting approval. A license agreement does not have any interest in the property itself and can be terminated as needed, unlike a lease. The draft license would address the term, insurance, indemnification, and site restoration, among other matters. The Board discussed the need for a term longer than one year and the expectation that the Food Pantry continue working toward a permanent location.  Member Alonzo moved to authorize the Town Manager to proceed with the license agreement with Town Counsel for the property at 30 School Street.  Seconded by Vice Chair Lehtinen. Call for the vote: Five-aye. The motion passed unanimously (5-0-0).  The Town Manager advised that the Select Board had authority to waive certain Building Department fees associated with the project, including building, wiring, plumbing, gas, sheet metal, and technology fees.  Member McQuaid moved to waive the building, wiring, plumbing, gas, sheet metal, and technology fees associated with the Lunenburg Food Pantry project at 30 School Street.  Seconded by Vice Chair Lehtinen. Call for the vote: Five-aye. The motion passed unanimously (5-0-0).  Member Alonzo voiced his support for the Food Pantry and states it was always known to the Town that the Food Pantry’s move to T. C. Passios was temporary. Member Alonzo also stated that a permanent placement needs to be found during their three years at School Street so that there is no scrambling last minute and suggested placing a hard deadline on finding a permanent location. Discussion of Opioid Settlement Funds: Use for AEDs in All Town/School Buildings and Police/Fire Vehicles  Chair Emkey had a conversation with Police Chief Thibodeau and the Chief stated many of the AED’s need to be replaced. The Board discussed potential use of opioid settlement funds to replace Automated External Defibrillators (AEDs) in Town and School buildings and Police and Fire vehicles. The preliminary discussion contemplated a significant number of units and a potentially six-figure total cost. Members supported researching the number, age, and condition of existing AEDs and the cost of replacement. Member Alonzo also suggested reviewing Narcan supplies and expiration dates. Staff was asked to return with additional information for a future vote.   Status of Edits to Policies- Appointment Policy  This policy was amended in the last year, but there were areas that were unclear. UPCOMING MEETINGS/EVENTS SCHEDULE  The Board discussed whether to operate on a true every-other-week meeting schedule or continue reserving Tuesdays and cancel meetings when there was insufficient business. Members noted that time-sensitive appointments, licenses, and other matters can make a strict biweekly schedule difficult. The Board generally favored maintaining Tuesday availability and canceling meetings as appropriate rather than formally adopting a rigid biweekly schedule.  The Board confirmed a meeting for September 29, with a liquor license public hearing and two earth removal renewals anticipated.
