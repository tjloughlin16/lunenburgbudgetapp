Town of Lunenburg Select Board Meeting Minutes Site Visit of Brooks House (1033 Massachusetts Avenue) and T. C. Passios (1025 Massachusetts Avenue) Tuesday, August 11, 2026  Members Present: Chair Renee Emkey, Vice-Chair Brian Lehtinen, Clerk Glenn McLeod, and Member Damon McQuaid  Members Absent:  Member Tom Alonzo Also Present: Jennifer Warren-Dyment, Town Manager; Christopher Ruth, Facilities Director; Anthony Sculimbrene, Laura Brzozoski, and Greg Roy of the Lunenburg Municipal Building Design Committee; Andrew Gillis of the Architectural Preservation District Commission; Kim Duffy of the Master Plan Sterring Committee; Dave and Anne Nelson of the Lunenburg Food Pantry.   CALL TO ORDER Chair Renee Emkey called the meeting to order at 4:00 PM at the Brooks House, 1033 Massachusetts Avenue. Chair Emkey informed attendees that the meeting was being recorded and would be uploaded to YouTube after the meeting. The Chair stated that the purpose of the site visit was to familiarize members with the present condition and layout of the Brooks House and T.C. Passios Elementary School. Discussion was limited to observations and factual information. Any deliberation or action regarding the future of the properties would occur only at a properly posted public meeting.  SCHEDULED MEETING  • Select Board site visit to Brooks House and T.C. Passios No votes or deliberation took place. Facilities Director Christopher Ruth led the tour and answered members' factual questions.  • Brooks House, 1033 Massachusetts Avenue (4:00 PM – 4:15 PM)  Facilities Director Ruth reported that the building has been winterized. Water and gas are off, and electricity is on only for the fire alarms. Staff conduct regular checks. The upper floors are restricted because the fire escapes are not connected. The building was most recently used as a school facilities office and for storage by the Friendly Seniors group. It is currently used only for police K-9 training. The principal obstacles to reuse are: ● Doorway and entrance widths that do not meet ADA requirements, and the fire escape. ● Any use would likely require substantial renovation. ● The building is assumed to be balloon-framed, and hidden structural conditions cannot be known without opening walls. ● The electrical panel is believed to be relatively new, but the lighting circuits are believed to be knob-and-tube. ● The floor framing appears strong, but no engineer has evaluated it. ● The roof is due for replacement and has chimney flashing issues, though there are no active leaks. Anne Nelson of the food pantry said the pantry has not found a new home. Sites considered so far lack water and bathrooms. The pantry needs water, two sinks, and refrigeration, which adds floor load. She said the Brooks House might serve as an emergency short-term space, and that the pantry would expect to contribute financially to a building it occupies.

Facilities Director Ruth said fire safety would likely require blocking off the second floor. A building inspector would need to determine ADA entrance requirements, and a ramp and widened doorways were possibilities. Town Manager Jennifer Warren-Dyment noted that parking would likely require relief from the Planning Board and involve the Library Trustees' control of the lot. Facilities Director Ruth did not consider the building suitable as a permanent home, since the site could affect future plans for T.C. Passios. A temporary solution was possible, and any town project was years away.  • T.C. Passios Elementary School, 1025 Massachusetts Avenue (4:20 PM – 5:16 PM)  Food pantry area and exterior doors. Facilities Director Ruth reported that the exterior door systems are proprietary, and replacement parts are no longer available. The food pantry occupies the cafeteria and kitchen, about 3,500 square feet. Floor tiles contain asbestos, and the kitchen equipment has not been certified in years. Electricity for the pantry area costs roughly $20,000 per year, driven largely by refrigeration and air conditioning. Staining was visible on the ceiling, though leaks there were reported as minor. Ms. Nelson noted that a warehouse space under consideration has no water, bathrooms, or heat.  Boiler room and electrical. Facilities Director Ruth reported the following: ● There are three oversized boilers, left from heating the former high school. One is running and has failed state inspection because of a pressure relief valve. Repair estimates range from $10,000 to $20,000. ● Only one of two hot water tanks is functional. ● The heat exchanger continuously discharges water. ● The control system is obsolete and unable to communicate. ● Asbestos is present on the exhaust piping. ● No drawings exist, and a suspected cross-connection between the heating and domestic water systems was reported. ● The transformers are leaking and failing and need replacement. Any building work would require a service upgrade from Unitil. ● The leaking oil has not been sampled for PCBs. ● There were new roof leaks in the boiler room during the last storm. ● The standby generator works, and the Town has offered it to the school department for the primary school.  Gymnasium and stage/break room. Facilities Director Ruth reported the following: ● The gym has no air conditioning. ● There were new roof leaks during the last major storm, including water down a stairway with peeling lead paint, and a threat to the wood floor. ● HVAC units serving the gym were found running during leaks and were shut off. Their filters are about 23 years old. ● Air quality testing in 2024 found elevated VOCs and lower-than-expected mold. It did not include asbestos. ● The Shapiro Center left in mid-June at the Town's request not to extend, after its classrooms began leaking.  Decommissioned area.

Facilities Director Ruth described the decommissioning of roughly 50 percent of the floor space. The sprinkler and heating systems were drained and electricity was cut. Each ceiling opening costs about $5,000 because drywall ceiling compound contains asbestos, and piping has been cut and cross-connected over the years. Work was performed on a time-and-materials basis because no plans exist, and he agreed to provide the cost. Other conditions reported: ● Severe leaks and mold, including in the 1970s library addition. ● Cracking asbestos floor tile. ● Roof drains with undersized sleeves. ● The EPDM roof, installed in 1986 and reseamed in 2012, is shrinking and failing. ● Former skylights are covered with metal panels. ● Unexplained underground vaults. ● Drinking water signage, because lead has been known to be present in the water since the 1980s.  Records storage. Three classrooms are used to store Town records in water-resistant bins, organized by department. Town Manager Warren-Dyment stated that department heads have been purging records permitted for destruction under retention schedules, and that permanent records must be kept. It was too early to estimate the volume reduction. A storage trailer will hold only election equipment, and no other storage plan has been determined.  Roof, ADA, and cost considerations. Facilities Director Ruth reported that roof work costing over $500,000 triggers ADA improvements. The current estimate to repair the portion from the hallway edge to the decommissioning doors is about $750,000. This excludes asbestos abatement and roof deck replacement. Work above roughly 30 percent of the prorated full and fair cash value (assessed at $5.4 million, about $2 million prorated, or about $600,000) may trigger full ADA upgrades. Members discussed the structural and code implications of alteration, including seismic and wind upgrades. Original drawings are incomplete. Roof costs to date are per-square-foot figures from roofing contractors, with no formal estimate for the figure used at the November 2023 Special Town Meeting. Core samples showed an EPDM roof over built-up tar and gravel, with asbestos only in the perimeter caulking. Facilities Director Ruth is continuing to develop pricing.   ADJOURN  Motion to adjourn at 5:16 PM was made by Chair Emkey and seconded by Vice-Chair Brian Lehtinen. Call for vote: Four - aye. The motion passed unanimously (4-0-0).
