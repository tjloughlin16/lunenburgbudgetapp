PUBLIC COMMENT  Public Comment Tim Murphy, 97 Island Road, Town Moderator, addressed the Board regarding the mailing of postcards notifying residents of the upcoming Special Town Meeting. Mr. Murphy stated that he had sent a message to the Town Manager's office on Monday because he had never received an official postcard notice. He noted that over the weekend he had gathered with five couples, and that four of them had not received a postcard either. He expressed concern that this may be a recurring issue, noting that he had heard similar complaints over the years about residents not receiving their annual town meeting warrants. Mr. Murphy acknowledged that he did not know whether the problem originated with the town or with the post office, but offered that mail delivery problems in Lunenburg had been noted anecdotally in the past. The Town Manager responded, explaining that the postcards are sent via Every Door Direct Mail (EDDM), a bulk mailing system by which the post office is supposed to deliver a piece to every address on a route automatically and therefore no individual addresses are applied. The Town Manager noted that staff had reached out to Mr. Murphy to gather addresses of affected residents so that the information could be provided to the post office. He indicated that the issue appeared to be concentrated around lake-area properties, possibly attributable to a specific carrier, and that further investigation would be needed.

Article 3 — Petitioner Matthew Nazarenko: Middle School and High School Sports Restoration Matthew Nazarenko appeared remotely to present an update on the citizen's petition article he had filed to restore cut sports programs. Chair Emkey noted that the Board had access to Mr. Nazarenko's original presentation, which had been submitted to the Finance Committee. Mr. Nazarenko explained the evolution of the article's dollar amount. The original petition had been filed at $151,000, a figure he derived from documents obtained through a public records request after being unable to get a straight answer from the school department about the cost of restoring middle school sports. He recounted that a group of parents had attempted as far back as April to learn the cost so they could write a check as a donation, but were not provided figures even after a month of waiting. He explained that the figure had since been substantially revised downward. The school department had contacted the transportation vendor (D-Bus) to arrange transportation for high school sports for fall, winter, and spring at a cost of approximately $60,000, which was no longer included in Article 3 as that item was being funded separately. What remained in the article was: ● Restoration of middle school boys' and girls' basketball and outdoor track (for winter and spring seasons, as fall was too late to schedule); ● Restoration of high school women's lacrosse and skiing; ● All associated transportation costs for those programs; ● A credit of $5,541 — an excess amount from Article 1 that the school committee was expected to vote to redirect to Article 3 at their meeting the following night. The resulting total for Article 3 was $30,308. Member Alonzo raised several concerns. First, he noted the dramatic reduction from $151,000 to $30,308 — a decrease of approximately 80 percent — and expressed skepticism about the explanation that the school department simply had a "better idea" of costs now than it did when the original figures were provided. He found it difficult to accept that so large a discrepancy could be

attributed to improved scheduling information alone. Second, he raised the question of a proper funding source, noting that the only new money available was the Winter Recovery Assistance Program (WRAP) funds. Third, he expressed the view that $30,000 should arguably be findable within the school's existing budget from other sources, particularly given that sports are not educational programming. Member McQuaid echoed the frustration about the inability to get precise figures, and questioned whether the $30,308 was a direct cost figure or a net figure that accounted for athletic fee revenue. Mr. Nazarenko clarified that based on his understanding of the most recent School Committee meeting, the article amount represented direct costs only, and that the school department had elected not to factor in revenue from athletic fees citing the negative balance in the athletic revolving account two years prior. Member McQuaid pressed the point, suggesting that athletic fees at the middle school level, approximately $275 to $350 per student, would make many of these programs substantially or even entirely self-funding, and expressed frustration that programs generating net positive revenue were being cut. Mr. Nazarenko agreed with that analysis, noting that middle school track alone had generated approximately $9,000 in net positive revenue in the prior year at the $275 fee level, and that middle school sports as a whole had likely been net positive when viewed in isolation from allocated overhead costs. He added that his analysis did not include allocated costs for the athletic director, trainers, or field rentals, reasoning that those costs were already being borne by the athletic program regardless of whether middle school sports were offered. Member McQuaid stated, "I personally hope we can find a way to help Matt succeed here, because cutting middle school sports is such a… it was a threat to get people to support the override, and it's an unfortunate casualty that shouldn't have been put on the chopping block to begin with." Mr. Nazarenko corrected the framing, noting that middle school sports were never "on the chopping block", they were cut outright before the budget process even reached the stage where items were debated for restoration. That was why the parents got together and asked how much do we write the check for and go fund raise." He further expressed concern about the long-term effect on the athletics pipeline, saying: "If we don't have middle school sports and a pipeline in, I just worry that a lot of kids are going to transfer, and we really don't want that." Member Alonzo also raised a concern about the interaction between Articles 1 and 3, specifically the $5,541 credit. Article 1 — School Department FY27 Budget Supplemental Appropriation from Additional State Aid Chair Emkey turned the Board's attention to Article 1, which sought an appropriation of $418,056 in additional state aid to supplement the FY27 operating budget of the school department. She noted that a spending plan had been presented and voted on by the school committee, and that the Finance Committee had voted to recommend approval of the article as written. Member Alonzo led the primary discussion. He explained that his concern was not with the substance of the spending plan, but with how the appropriation amount of $418,056 had been determined. He stated that he had extensive experience with the cherry sheet process both as a Finance Committee member and as a Select Board member, and that he did not believe the amount as proposed followed proper budgeting convention. Member Alonzo explained that the $418,056 figure represents the total of school-oriented receipt line items on the final conference committee state budget. However, he noted that the cherry sheet also includes education-related offsets specifically, increases to school choice and charter school sending tuitions which came in approximately $15,326 higher. Additionally, the school choice receiving tuition line item is a pass-through that does not need to be appropriated. He stated that those two items together totaled approximately $20,867, which he argued should not be included in the school's appropriation request. The figure he would support appropriating to the school is $397,189 the net education aid available for appropriation. He stated: "The town has never budgeted like that in the past. And those other budget items could go to other items that people in the town do care about as well… We had the town beach memorial, and next year, the town beach is slated to be closed. That's money that could be used too." He added: "I'm 100 percent for the school line item budget from the cherry sheet that changed to be allocated to the school. That is the amount of $397,189. I am totally for that. I am not for $418,056." Chair Emkey confirmed that she had been advocating for the net figure since the early discussions about the Special Town Meeting, and acknowledged going back and forth on whether the approximately $21,000 difference was worth the conflict, particularly in light of the spending plan's positive elements including a new second-grade teacher and an additional reading specialist. She noted that the spending plan would need to be revised if the lower figure were adopted. Vice Chair Lehtinen expressed agreement with Member Alonzo's position, supporting the $397,189 figure. He also expressed frustration with how the broader process had unfolded noting that the school committee had initially requested even less and stated that he hoped leadership could work more cohesively in the future to avoid the need for a special town meeting to resolve such matters. Clerk McLeod agreed with the substance of Member Alonzo's argument, though he noted his concern about the impact the lower figure would have on the school's approved spending plan. He stated it was nonetheless difficult to argue against the principle. Member McQuaid agreed with the net figure, stating: "What's fair is fair. The net number is the real number the gross is an inflated number." He added that multiple town departments have critical needs, and that the schools, while essential, are not the only priority.

Peter Beardmore, Vice Chairman of the Finance Committee, addressed the Board from the floor. He stated that the Finance Committee had voted to support Article 1 as submitted. He did not speak to the gross-versus-net debate as a whole, but addressed the $5,541 question specifically. Member Alonzo stated that the fundamental solution to Lunenburg's structural budget challenges is an override, noting: "This only gets fixed, seriously only gets fixed, with an override vote at some point. Otherwise, it's just gonna be more pulling at a fixed budget with departments that see increasing costs." Clerk McLeod offered that adequate state local aid, if it were provided at appropriate levels, could also go a long way toward resolving the issue, and that the solution was not limited solely to Proposition 2½ overrides. Vote on Winter Recovery Assistance Program (WRAP) FY2026 Snow and Ice Reimbursement  Chair Emkey presented a detailed overview of the Winter Recovery Assistance Program (WRAP) and the decisions before the Board. She shared slides to illustrate the options. Chair Emkey explained that the town's FY26 snow and ice deficit was estimated at approximately $691,000 noting that the figure was still being refined due to the recently settled DPW collective bargaining agreement, which required retroactive calculation of overtime wages. At the May Annual Town Meeting, $520,000 in free cash had been appropriated toward that deficit, leaving a remaining balance of approximately $171,000. The town received $228,154.34 in WRAP funds. The Division of Local Services (DLS) had strongly recommended that the WRAP funds be used first to fully cure the FY26 snow and ice deficit, which would consume approximately $171,000 of the WRAP funds, 
===PAGE 10===
Select Board Meeting Minutes September 1, 2026 Page 10 of 12  
leaving a remaining balance of approximately $57,154.34. Chair Emkey then outlined three options for the remaining balance: ● Option A: Transfer the full remaining balance to FY26 free cash. These funds would not be available until FY26 free cash is certified by the state and would be appropriated by Town Meeting. ● Option B: Establish a special revenue fund (created by Select Board vote) and deposit the remaining balance there. These funds would be available immediately, would still require Town Meeting appropriation, and any balance remaining at the end of FY27 would roll into FY27 free cash. ● Option C: Split the remaining balance between FY26 free cash and a special revenue fund — for example, directing the portion needed for Article 3 into the special revenue fund (for immediate availability) while placing the remainder into free cash. The Board discussed the interplay between the WRAP funds and Article 3. PUBLIC COMMENT**  • PUBLIC Ana Lockwood, 91 Main Street, addressed the Board. She stated that she had gone back and listened to the recording of her comments at the Finance Committee hearing and wished to clarify her remarks in context of Member McQuaid's earlier comments about the $5,000 discussion. She affirmed that she shared the view that every tax dollar must be spent carefully. She sought to provide context, noting that the schools are currently operating with approximately $800,000 less than their level-service budget would require, representing a real reduction in services to students. In that context, she explained, her comment at the Finance Committee was that spending extended meeting time debating $5,000 risks losing sight of the larger problem. She stated: "There's been a lot of conversation about this $5,000, and it feels like we're missing the big point. Students are receiving $800,000 less in service." She acknowledged that the reaction from the community, including parents organizing a citizen's petition and a special town meeting, demonstrated how seriously residents take the issue. She stated she hoped town leadership would focus on solving the larger structural problems.  • TOWN MANAGER Town Manager Warren-Dyment offered no additional comment. • BOARD No comment by the Select Board.
