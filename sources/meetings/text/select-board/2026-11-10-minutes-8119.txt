Town of Lunenburg Select Board Meeting Minutes Town Hall, 17 Main Street and via Zoom Tuesday, November 10, 2026  Members Present: Chair Renee Emkey, Vice-Chair Brian Lehtinen, Clerk Glenn McLeod, Member Damon McQuaid, and Member Tom Alonzo Members Absent:  None Also Present: Jennifer Warren-Dyment, Town Manager  Public Comment Participants are listed in the minutes   CALL TO ORDER AND PLEDGE OF ALLEGIANCE  Chair Renee Emkey called the meeting to order at 7:00 PM and led the Pledge of Allegiance. Chair Emkey informed attendees that the meeting was being recorded and broadcast live and would be uploaded to YouTube after the meeting. She explained that the agenda listed topics that may be discussed, but not all items may be addressed and other items not listed could be brought up as permitted by open meeting law.  ANNOUNCEMENTS  Chair Emkey announced thatSpecial Town Meeting will be held Tuesday, November 17, 2026, at 7:00 PM in the Middle/High School Auditorium.  Vice-Chair Brian Lenthinen announced that the Lunenburg Community Food Pantry would sponsor a holiday concert at City Hall in Leominster on Sunday, December 6th at 2:00 PM. Tickets were $15 in advance, $20 at the door, and free for those under 18, with proceeds supporting the pantry.  Member Tom Alonzo noted the passing of Charles Sanderson, a U.S. Army veteran who recently passed away at the age of 101 and who was a regular at Memorial Day services. He extended condolences to the family.  PUBLIC COMMENT  • PUBLIC Ashley York (70 Pratt Street), Paula Cookson-Luria (63 Pratt Street), Paul Luria (63 Pratt Street), and James Moran (43 Pratt Street) asked the Board to address speeding on Pratt Street. They thanked Officer Ortega for conducting speed studies. ● Westbound study (Oct. 21–25): 2,055 vehicles, an average speed of 21.46 mph, an 85th percentile speed of 29.64 mph, and 12 vehicles over 40 mph. ● Eastbound study (Oct. 27–31): an average speed of 25.19 mph, an 85th percentile speed of 31.75 mph, a high of 59 mph, and 28 vehicles over 40 mph. ● Road conditions: Residents described a straight "pass-through" road with no sidewalks, curbs, or posted speed limit sign. About nine children board the bus at the center of the street, and street lighting is limited. ● Reported incidents: A deer was struck, and one neighbor replaced a mailbox seven times. ● Suggestions made by the residents: A posted speed of 20 mph, speed bumps, and increased enforcement.  Nancy Beach (31 Prospect Street) and Anne Nelson (435 Townsend Harbor Road) spoke for the Lunenburg Community Food Pantry. They stated that the pantry had been asked to vacate

TC Passios by December 1st and that unanticipated costs (the surety bond and structural plans) would deplete their funds. PUBLIC HEARINGS AND SCHEDULED MEETINGS   APPOINTMENTS, RATIFICATIONS, AND RESIGNATIONS  • Ratification of the Town Manager's Appointment of Kat Lemur as Videographer  Town Manager Jennifer Warren-Dyment reported that Ms. Lemur is a student at Mount Wachusett Community College studying film and audio production, with freelance videography experience. The Town Manager commented that the videographer ratified at the last Board meeting, Alex, was learning in the booth that evening.  Motion to ratify the appointment of Kat Lemur as Videographer was made by Member Alonzo and seconded by Vice-Chair Lehtinen. Call for vote: Five - aye. The motion passed unanimously (5-0-0).   TOWN MANAGER REPORT  Town Manager Warren-Dyment reported on the following.  ● Flat Hill Road bridge: Excavation had begun, and water control measures were tentatively scheduled for installation Thursday, depending on water levels. ● Public safety building roof (about $500,000): Five bids were received. The low bidder skipped the mandatory site visit, was bypassed, and filed a bid protest with the Attorney General's office. The project would likely be delayed to spring. ● FY26 closeout and free cash: The Town had not closed out FY26. Free cash would not be certified by the Special Town Meeting, and closeout documents were unlikely to be submitted by then. The Finance Director had about eight pages of reconciling items. The tax rate setting and FY27 budget work required her to step back temporarily, and the Town Manager had contacted last year's consultant for help. ● State election (Nov. 3): This was the first election held outside the TC Passios building. The Town Manager thanked the Town Clerk, registrars, election workers, and Town and school staff.

● Fire Chief Patrick Sullivan retired November 6th after 43 years of service. A swearing-in ceremony for Chief Jones and a pinning ceremony were planned for December. ● Library minimum municipal appropriation: A math error left the town $3,647 below the required amount ($598,960 as calculated). A waiver was submitted to the state explaining the error and the FY27 library supplement at next week’s Special Town Meeting would more than address it. ● Planning Board forum will be held December 1st at 6:00 PM at the Adult Activity Center on zoning amendments they are planning on bringing to the May Annual Town Meeting. The proposed zoning amendments address senior housing, inclusionary housing, and cluster housing, the affordable housing trust fund, and minor site plan review. ● Recruitments: Open positions included a 19-hour data collector, assistant library director interviews, seasonal and permanent heavy equipment operators, and the assistant town manager (to be reviewed by the Personnel Committee Monday). Vacancies existed on several boards and committees.  Member Alonzo thanked poll workers, the Town Clerk, and the Assistant Town Clerk for all of their work during the election. Member Damon McQuaid volunteered to be the Select Board’s member. The Board reviewed Chapters 7 through 12. No votes were taken. Chair Emkey will compile the changes into a new file for the next meeting, when the Board may vote. Changes discussed: ● Chapter 7 (Meetings and Open Meeting Law): Member Alonzo suggested that restating Open Meeting Law definitions risks becoming outdated. The Board consensus was to keep the content as a single-source reference. ● Chapter 8 (Executive Session): No changes. ● Chapter 9 (Posting/Scheduling): The Board agreed to remove the library room-scheduling hyperlink and add the Middle/High School auditorium and collaborative rooms. The introductory line will refer to the "most common meeting room spaces." The appendix reference will change to "B," with a sample Finance Committee agenda added. ● Chapter 10 (Minutes): "Need not be verbatim" will change to "should not be verbatim," and the Chair's added minutes-posting process will be kept. The paragraph on recording meetings will move to the Open Meeting Law chapter. ● Chapter 11 (Conflict of Interest): The sentence on filing written determination requests with the Select Board will be deleted. The first sentence will be clarified so that appointed members go to their appointing authority and elected members go to the State Ethics Commission. ● Chapter 12 (Standards of Conduct): "Well informed" will be hyphenated, and "should not" will change to "must not" regarding purporting to represent the board. Members discussed identifying oneself as a board member when speaking as a private citizen.  OLD BUSINESS  • Special Town Meeting, November 17, 2026  The Board will meet at 6:30 PM in the Middle/High School auditorium on November 17th to vote on deferred articles. ● Article 2 (remaining state aid and reimbursement, about $14,000 directed to the Finance Committee reserve) and Article 3 (prior fiscal year bill) remained deferred. The Finance Committee is scheduled to meet Thursday, November 12th. ● Article 4: The School Committee voted to pass over. ● Article 9 (track and field engineering): The Board remained deferred pending a School Committee meeting Monday. Free cash would not be certified, and the School Committee was considering the stabilization fund (a two-thirds vote threshold, versus a majority for free cash). Board members agreed that the School Committee should make the main motion on its own article. ● Articles 7 and 8: The Board reviewed Town Counsel's motion wording for the two nonbinding articles. ● Motion assignments: Motions would be read in order based on the seating of the Select Board members. Member Glenn McLeod expressed willingness to have another member to make the motion on Article 8. Member Alonzo agreed to move Article 8.
