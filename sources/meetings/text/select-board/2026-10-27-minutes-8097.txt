Clerk Glenn McLeod and Vice-Chair Lehtinen introduced their draft flag policies for discussion. Both drafts rely on a KP Law memo describing two options under the Supreme Court's Shurtleff decision:  • Option A: The Town declares its flagpoles a nonpublic forum reserved for government speech, with only approved flags flown. • Option B: The Town accepts requests from the public while remaining content-neutral. Clerk McLeod's draft is based on KP Law’s Option A and modeled on Wellesley's policy. Vice-Chair Lehtinen's draft adds a request process under which the board may consider written requests at a public meeting, with display not to exceed 30 days, while preserving the principle of government speech. Vice-Chair Lehtinen favored allowing requests to encourage civic participation and recognize community events. Member McQuaid favored the simpler approach, citing practical concerns that any flag decision would prompt controversy. Member Alonzo supported either approach as long as it was legally sound, and said the policy should make clear that all flags flown are the Town's own speech. Town Manager Warren-Dyment read prior guidance from Town Counsel that a flag is government speech when the Board actively decides to fly it, and that flying any flag requested, or allowing public use of the poles, could create a public forum. Clerk McLeod favored the simpler policy as avoiding difficult viewpoint-related decisions. Chair Emkey suggested the flags on the front of Town Hall might serve as an alternative display location and noted the existing policy on half-staff flags should be retained. Members discussed excluding campaign or ballot-question flags. The Board agreed to refine the draft before seeking Town Counsel's review and to consider holding a workshop on the draft at the November 10th meeting. No vote was taken.  • Special Education Reserve Fund Policy  Chair Emkey explained that the Special Education Reserve Fund was created at the fall 2025 Special Town Meeting without funding. Statute caps the fund at 2% of net school spending and requires a majority vote of both the School Committee and the Select Board for withdrawals. Funding sources, withdrawal criteria, and other rules are not defined by law. Some Finance Committee members have said they will not support funding the reserve without a policy. Sample policies from four towns were distributed. Members generally supported having a policy. Members debated whether the Town Manager, a cross-committee working group or the School Committee should prepare the first draft.   Motion to charge the Town Manager with creating the first draft of the Special Education Reserve Fund policy was made by Member McQuaid and seconded by Vice-Chair Lehtinen. Following discussion, in which Member Alonzo stated he would oppose the motion and Vice-Chair Lehtinen said the School Committee should take the lead, the motion was withdrawn.  Chair Emkey will ask the School Committee Chair to consider placing the matter on that committee's agenda, and the Board will revisit the item at an upcoming meeting.
