1. Review Ballot Question - Ms. Speidel explained that the ballot question was modified slightly based on the comments
that were received by both the MSBA and the Bond Counsel. Ms. Speidel read the ballot question as follows:
Shall the Town of Lunenburg be allowed to exempt from the provisions of Proposition two-and-one-half, so called, the
amounts required to pay for the bonds issued in order to construct a new Lunenburg Middle/High School, designed for
approximately 820 students in grades 6-12 to be located at 1079 Massachusetts Avenue, Lunenburg, MA 01462.
Ms. Spiedel explained that by request from the MSBA and the Bond Counsel, the square footage and Parcel ID# were
eliminated from the previous ballot question. The cost of the project is not included due to the fact that this is not done for
any debt exemption, which is specific to State Law.
There will be a separate mailing to residents with all costs of the school building project done by the Political: Action
Committee (PAC).
Mr. Matthews moved to approve the ballot question as printed. Mr. Toale seconded. On vote, motion carried, 4 in favor,
0 opposed
2. Review Warrant Articles on Special Town Meeting Warrant: Ms. Speidel explained the changes which were added
to the warrant as follows:
•
Added were the last days to register to vote for both the Special Town Meeting and Special Election.
• Article 1 has been amended by removing the Parcel ID# and square footage. Both the MSBA and Bond Counsel
have approved the wording of this article.
• Town Counsel has reviewed both Articles 2 & 3 and their opinion was that only Article 3 was necessary: Their
reasons were that Article 2 was a definition of the Zoning Bylaw and this definition is already included in Article 3.
By approving Article 3 it will amend the current Zoning Bylaw to include that definition.
The BOS recommendation for Article 1 will be added to the warrant and the BOS recommendation for Article 3 will be
given at Special Town Meeting.
Mr. Matthews moved to approve the Special Town Meeting warrant as printed. Mr. Ebersole seconded. On vote, motion
carried, 4 in favor, 0 opposed.

PUBLIC COMMENT
Clarence Floyd, 720 Goodrich St., spoke to the BOS concerning questions he had with the proposed new school building
project. He was concerned with the amount of $36.65M as being principle only for the Town's part of this. He asked what
the interest was going to cost the Town for a 30 year term and a 20 year term. He believes that, that would be the true
total of cost. His understanding is that the State will not help the Town with interest. He believes that the information
brochure states incorrectly what the Town's interest cost will be and he would like the Towns people to know what the
cost will be
Mr. Matthews confirmed that the $36M is the principal and we need to add the interest to the total cost for Special Town
Meeting.
Mr. Floyd also mentioned that residents call him for information and asked how many administrators and staff will be
added. He believes that a Technology person will be added this year but that's a separate issue.
Mr. Alonzo replied that the operating cost of the building will not change regardless if the project goes through or not.
Mr. Floyd asked how long after we float the bonds will the State begin to give us monies towards this project.
Mr. Matthews replied that they make payments immediately. We only have to bond for our portion of it, not the States.
Mr. Floyd asked what criteria would the Treasurer and the Selectmen use to select the term.
Mr. Matthews replied that we had that vote last week. We looked at total cost, immediate impact on the tax payers, and
ultimately we decided to borrow over 30 years. This adds more cost in total but alleviates cost immediately to the tax
payers.
Ms. Speidel informed Mr. Floyd that the interest cost over 30 years is approximately $32M which will be added to the
$36M principle for the total cost.
Mr. Floyd mentioned that Question 1 is confusing and that some people may consider this to be a Proposition 2.5
Override and would like clarification that this would be a Debt Exclusion.
Mr. Alonzo replied that it comes under an "umbrella" law called "Proposition 2.5". There are general overrides and this is
not one, this is a debt exclusion that only impacts the tax bill until the bond is paid off and is not permanent.
Mr. Floyd believes that the way it is worded, some people in the Town will think it's a Proposition 2.5 override and will not
vote for it. He believes that it should be better explained to the public that this is a Debt Exclusion
Mr. Matthews replied that this is specific language from the MSBA as to what we have to do. We did want to have more
numbers in the Town Meeting article. At Town Meeting we will have a discussion which will designate the specific amount
and it will be outlined that this is a Debt Exclusion.
Regarding the ballot question, the State sets the wording on that, we tried to adjust it to be more specific, but they set the
language.
Mr. Matthews asked Mr. Floyd if he was supporting the project.
===PAGE 6===
Mr. Floyd replied that he was leaning that way and mentioned that he would rather take his money and spend it on
something worth while then try to resurrect it.
