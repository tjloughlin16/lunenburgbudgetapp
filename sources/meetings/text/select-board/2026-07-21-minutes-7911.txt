SCHEDULED MEETINGS Presentation by Thomas Carey on Proposed Eagle Scout Project of 10x12 Loafing Shed at DPW  Thomas Carey presented a proposal to build a 10-by-12-foot, three-sided shed near the DPW entrance for residents to collect sand and salt during winter. He described the proposed construction and location, noted that corrugated metal roofing and other materials had been donated, and estimated the remaining cost at approximately $1,900. Mr. Carey said the project was ready to proceed pending approval and Scout district review, with a goal of completing it before September. Board members supported the project. Vice-Chair Brian Lehtinen noted residents’ recurring requests for sand and salt and encouraged Mr. Carey to return with photographs when the project was complete. The Chair confirmed that the Board’s approval was needed to use Town property. DPW Director William Bernard said the proposed location was convenient and that the containers currently there would be moved. Director Bernard said the cost could be accommodated within the DPW budget. Clerk Glenn McLeod encouraged Mr. Carey to consider community fundraising, while Mr. Carey clarified that he expected the Town to fund the materials.

Following the appointment, John Bowen, Chair of the Trust Fund Commission, requested a correction to the record of the July 7 joint meeting. He said the Town had received Mr. Howard’s volunteer application on May 27, though the July 7 record stated that no application existed. Mr. Bowen asked that the minutes reflect the application’s receipt and that the Town correct its public-facing application process, including the listed recipients and the boards available on the online form. He also requested shared ground rules for future joint meetings.  Mr. Bowen requested the following: 1. That the minutes of the July 7 joint meeting be corrected or supplemented to reflect that Mr. Howard's application was in the Town's possession as of May 27, 2026. 2. That Mr. Howard's appointment to the Trust Fund Commission be taken up and completed, with Mr. Howard present. 3. That the Town's public-facing application process be corrected so that a citizen following the Town's published instructions actually reaches the person who processes applications. Each of the Town webpages described here can be researched and verified individually. At present the vacancy posting offers an Email Resume link, pre-addressed to Ms. Belliveau, alongside a link to an online Volunteer Form; the Town separately maintains a paper application, which instructs applicants to return it to the Office of the Town Manager but is not linked from the vacancy posting and must be located through the website's search function; and on July 8, Ms. Belliveau advised in writing that volunteer applications should in fact be submitted to Melissa Richard, Executive Assistant. That instruction appears nowhere: not on the Town's website, not on the paper application for new applicants, and not on the Reappointment Application — which itself directs returning volunteers to submit to the Town Manager's office. It was never communicated to the Town's boards and commissions. The correct submission channel was disclosed for the first time only after this application had already gone astray. Of greatest concern, the online Volunteer Form does not list the Trust Fund Commission among its selectable boards at all — nor the Planning Board or School Committee — even though the same page advertises vacancies on all three. Discussion on School Committee Request to Include Article on Warrant for November 2026 Special Town Meeting to Appropriate Additional $350,000 in State Aid to the School Department for the FY27 School Operating Budget.  Chair Emkey presented an overview of the state budget and Cherry Sheet process, explaining that the Town’s annual budget and Town Meeting occurred before the state budget was finalized. She reviewed the timeline and described the additional state aid, the School Committee’s proposed uses, and the steps taken since the state budget was signed on July 9. Chair Emkey said the superintendent’s initial proposal discussed at a July 8 meeting totaled $477,896, exceeding the then-identified $471,121 in unappropriated state aid. The total included education and general-government aid; she noted that the general-government increase was approximately $73,998 and that the Town had not yet discussed how to use that portion. Chair Emkey said the school proposal was revised, and the School Committee approved a $350,000 spending plan on July 15, including reading specialists, a full-time high school assistant principal, additional high school music staffing, and tutoring licenses. She reported that three staffing positions had been posted and could begin before the November Town Meeting using available funding sources. A grant application for tutoring licenses remained pending, and the proposed music staffing increase was not yet being addressed. Chair Emkey said the Town had received a citizen petition for a special Town Meeting and that the Town Clerk had verified its signatures. She planned to consult Town Counsel about legal requirements and said the Select Board would meet July 27 to determine the special Town meeting date and related procedures. She also noted the need for a Finance Committee public hearing and thanked the Town Clerk’s office for managing multiple election and Town Meeting responsibilities. Vice-Chair Lehtinen thanked the Chair for the presentation and those involved in securing state aid, but expressed disappointment that the request had shifted from approximately $418,000 in education funding to $350,000. He said additional education revenue should be directed to students as reasonably possible and called for transparent discussion involving the Select Board, School Committee, and Finance Committee. Clerk McLeod thanked the Chair for clarifying the process and emphasized that the additional aid resulted from a state budget of approximately $63.4 billion. He cautioned that the timing was disruptive to the Town’s budget process and did not represent a broader resolution of state underinvestment in Lunenburg. Chair Emky clarified that the net education-related aid would be less than $418,000 after assessments, with the precise figure expected to be discussed by the Finance Committee. She said official communications and documented votes were important to avoid misunderstandings, and explained that the timing of the state budget’s final approval made earlier scheduling difficult. No action was taken on the School Committee request at this meeting.
