Frank McPartlan of Ducharme & Dillis Civil Design Group, Inc, representing Dan & Jean Proctor on
this project, stated the applicants are requesting an earth removal permit to remove 50,000 cubic yards at
1325 Massachusetts Avenue. The property is east of Townsend Harbor Road. On the north side there is
an existing single family house and 2 existing stables. The existing site is approximately 6 acres with
with wetlands located along the rear property line. We are working west of that in the grassy area with
wooded areas along the perimeter. On the far west side there will be a new row of screening plantings,
probably an arborvitae/evergreen planting. From there we are working east and are proposing removing
a wedge of soil that comes down to a flat area. This is a fairly gentle "mowable" slope for a proposed cut
pitched at 1% towards the wetlands on the west side. Once we get close to the wetlands, we are
proposing a stormwater basin to address runoff created by the project and for any future development.
This basin is designed to handle all rainfall events up to and including the 100-year storm event,
assuming approximately 77,000 square feet impervious on the flat area. The basin provides for
groundwater recharge for all small storm events as well as total suspended solids removal through the
use of four bays at each stormwater discharge. The intent of this basin design is for it to be constructed
during the earth removal process and then not touched again except for maintenance activities. It has
been graded so you can drive down to the basin with light equipment to remove material as needed.
There are three manholes at various points along the basin that provide for future connection.

What is a Financial Forecast?
• A financial planning and policymaking tool that projects revenues and expenditures
conservatively over a multi-year period
• It is a "living document" that is used to evaluate the budget over a five year period and update
forecast with changing financial data
Why Forecast?
• Provides a starting point from which a balanced budget is developed
• Projects likely outcomes based on historical practices
• Allows to look at "what if" scenarios
• Indicates future budget gaps
• Projects revenues and expenditures conservatively over a five year period
• Required by Town's Charter, Section 4-2 (s)
• Bond Rating Agencies look favorably upon communities that annually prepare a forecast
document.
===PAGE 10===
• Determine whether fiscal problems are on the horizon
• Time to plan for change, favorable or unfavorable
• Reinforce the need for long-range considerations during the annual budget process
Forecasting- Key Concepts
• Uses reasonable, conservative assumptions that must be evaluated and updated each year
• Represents the community's projected financial position
• Is a tool to evaluate the impact of various budgeting decisions and policy choices over time
• Is a living document that can be adjusted
• Once you develop baseline projections, the underlying assumptions can be adjusted to address
issues
• A forecasted shortfall needs to be examined and does imply that some action will be taken, or at
least planned, to avoid the financing problem
• Action to balance the budget
• Raise revenue; Reduce expenditures
