  SCHEDULED MEETINGS Presentation by Stephen Wallace on Mid-Year Updates in Land Use The Director of Planning and Economic Development, Stephen Wallace, reported that about two-thirds of his work week is spent managing the Planning Board caseload. He outlined the following: ● Special Town Meeting (anticipated in November): a consolidated housekeeping zoning amendment to correct misused terms, including references to a retail commercial district that no longer exists. ● Annual Town Meeting (May, 2027): four significant zoning amendments are in development with the Planning Board. They cover revising the inclusionary zoning bylaw, a new senior housing bylaw, a minor site plan review provision to streamline permitting for simple changes of use in existing buildings, and revisions to the cluster housing bylaw. Working sessions are planned over the summer, followed by outreach to other boards and public forums. ● Senior housing: none of the Town's parcels are suitable, as a site would need to be about 8 to 20 acres within roughly a mile of town center services. He will examine tax-delinquent parcels. ● Completed work: revised the Planning Board stormwater permit conditions into sequence and updated the fee schedule for the first time in about ten years. ● Housing Choice grant: the Town does not currently qualify because of insufficient housing growth over the last five years. ● Housing production plan: the regional planning commission has applied for a grant to update the plan, with a decision expected in September. ● Developable lands mapping project: a Regional Planning Commission grant will update the year-2000 build-out data. Mr. Wallace noted that commercial and industrial development provides about 8 percent of the Town's tax revenue, below the 10 percent threshold at which a community is considered a bedroom community. ● 925 Mass Ave: Mr. Wallace served on the RFP working group. Town Manager Jennifer Warren-Dyment stated that the RFP was expected to be released the next day at 10:00 AM, or the following Wednesday, pending a final review by Town Counsel. ● Tax title and surplus land: the Town has about six vacant residential lots that could be sold. He suggested Habitat for Humanity as a possible recipient of one lot. Town Manager Warren-Dyment suggested that the Board have Mr. Wallace return after the goals workshop to present options. ● Chapter 53G accounts: about 70 outstanding consulting-engineer fee accounts remain, and about 20 have been closed out with the business office. ● Economic development: Mr. Wallace created a "Doing Business in Lunenburg" web page and a spreadsheet of new businesses. The Economic Development Commission and the North Central Mass Chamber of Commerce are planning ribbon-cutting ceremonies, and Board members were invited to attend.

Presentation by Patrick Slattery and William Tyler of the Historical Society regarding American Revolutionary War Memorial  William Tyler and Ann Nelson, both Historical Society board members (Mr. Slattery was not present), presented a proposed gift to the Town. It is a monument commemorating the more than 300 Lunenburg residents who served in the American Revolution between 1775 and 1783. The names were verified through years of research. Ms. Nelson explained that the Lower Common was a militia training field, created from land gifts by Benjamin Bellows (1771) and Dr. John Taylor, and requested that the monument be placed there. The monument is a granite obelisk approximately 10 feet 4 inches tall, with four bronze plaques bearing about 75 names each. The Society seeks completion toward the end of 2027 ahead of the town's 300th anniversary in 2028. The lead time is about six months, plus installation. It is intended as a gift with no cost or maintenance expense to the Town. Ms. Nelson suggested a site behind the gazebo toward Whiting Street and slightly to the left. She also noted the need to consider traffic safety, mowing, and nearby memorials. Town Manager Warren-Dyment suggested a joint site review with the Society, the DPW Director, and the Facilities Director. The Board viewed the matter as two steps: permission to place the monument on town property, and then its exact location. Chair Emkey and Vice-Chair Brian Lehtinen expressed support but stated that the agenda item was not specific enough to take a vote. Town Manager Warren-Dyment and the Board agreed that permission to place the monument on town property would be placed on a future agenda, with the exact location to be determined later. Vice-Chair Lehtinen suggested community input, such as a listening session at the gazebo, stakes marking proposed sites, or an online survey. No vote was taken.
