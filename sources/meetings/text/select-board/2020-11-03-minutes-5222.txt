The classification hearing is held after the Board of Assessors has determined final values,
classified all properties and reported this information to Dept. of Revenue. These values set the
parameters for the options the municipality may adopt. Municipalities have several options in
distributing the tax levy among taxpayers under property tax classification. Use of these options
results in multiple tax rates for different property classes because they change the components
used to calculate the rate, i.e., the amount of the tax levy being paid by, or the assessed valuation
of, the class. The total tax levy remains the same.
Municipalities must decide whether (1) to tax all classes of property at their full and fair cash
valuation share of the tax levy, which results in a single tax rate, or (2) to reduce the share of the
tax levy paid by the residential and shift those taxes to commercial, industrial and personal
property taxpayers, which results in a split tax rate.
The Select Board must decide the percentages of the tax levy that each class of real property and
personal property will bear. To do so, a residential factor is adopted. The residential factor
by Dept. of Revenue (DOR). The MRF represents the maximum shift allowed in the tax levy for
the year and establishes the parameters for local decision-making.
A residential factor of "1" results in the taxation of all property at the same rate. Each property
class pays its full and fair cash valuation share of the tax levy, e.g., if the value of all residential
properties make up 80 percent of the total assessed valuation, residential taxpayers will pay 80
percent of the tax levy.
A residential factor of less than "1" reduces the share of the tax levy paid by the residential class
and increases the share paid by the CIP classes. The result is two tax rates: one for residential
properties and a second, higher rate for CIP properties. A factor greater than "1" may be adopted,
which would have the opposite effect. The maximum shift in the tax levy allowed for the year is
determined as follows:
Commercial/industrial/personal property taxpayers cannot pay more than 150% of their full and
fair cash value share of the tax levy (single rate share)
Residential taxpayers must pay at least 65% of their full and fair cash value share of the levy.

Board ratify my appointment of Robert Oliva as the Director of the Department of Public
Works. As the Board is aware, after receiving the current DPW Director's written notice on
September 21st that his last day before retirement would be on October 30th, the Assistant
Town Manager/HR Director advertised the position that same week on the town website, the
American Public Works Association, the newspaper and on Indeed.com with a preference
given to applicants that submitted applications by October 14th. We received a total of 7
applications and after pre-screening the applications to determine if they qualified, we
scheduled interviews the week of October 19th with four of the candidates. We ultimately
interviewed two of the four candidates, as two withdrew their applications.
The Assistant Town Manager/HR Director and I were very impressed with how Rob
presented himself during the interview and the level of experience he would bring to
Lunenburg. Rob comes highly qualified with 25 years of experience as a civil engineer and
has worked the last 3 years in municipal government. Rob attended the University of
Massachusetts at Lowell where he received a Bachelor's degree in Civil Engineering. He
then went on to work for two different engineering firms, Hamwey Engineering and David E.
Ross Associates, where he was responsible for all planning and design aspects for site
development projects of various sizes, including local, state, and federal permitting processes,
and was a Senior Project Manager for 8 years that also included consulting for the Lunenburg
Planning Board on various projects. Rob is currently the Assistant Director of the Department
of Public Works for the City of Gardner, where he has worked the last three years. In that
position he is responsible for the day to day operation of the Water and Sewer Divisions and
assists with the management of the Highway, Parks, and Cemetery Divisions under the DPW.
Overall, his decades of civil engineering experience dealing with roads, infrastructure, storm
water, water, sewer, and site plan review, as well as municipal experience on assisting with
managing cemeteries, parks, budgeting, capital planning, and experience working with the
union make him an ideal candidate. Speaking with his references and others that have worked
with Rob, we heard repeated comments about how he is easy to work with, that he is a good
communicator, and he would be an asset to the Town. Given Rob's extensive background in
civil engineering, municipal experience, and personality that would be a good fit to lead the
Department of Public Works, I respectfully request that you ratify my appointment of Robert
Oliva as the next Director of the Department of Public Works.

Governor Baker announced new Orders & Advisories on November 2, 2020:
Each of the following Orders & Advisory will be effective Friday, November 6'at 12:01 AM.
Stay At Home Advisory:
The Administration issued a revised Stay At Home Advisory to ensure residents avoid
unnecessary activities that can lead to increased COVID-19 transmission. The revised Stay
At Home Advisory instructs residents to stay home between 10 PM and 5 AM. The Advisory
allows for activities such as going to work, running critical errands to get groceries and
address health needs, and taking a walk.
Order Requiring Early Closure of Businesses and Activities (COVID-19 Order No. 53):
Governor Baker issued a new executive order that requires the early closure of certain
businesses and activities each night at 9:30 PM. The 9:30 PM closure requirement is
aligned with the Stay At Home Advisory and together the two new initiatives are designed
to further limit activities that could lead to COVID-19 transmission.
Effective November 6, the following businesses and activities must close to the public each
day between the hours of 9:30 PM and 5:00 AM.
•
Restaurants (in-person dining must cease at 9:30 PM, although takeout and
delivery may continue for food and non-alcoholic beverages, but not alcohol
• Liquor stores and other retail establishments that sell alcohol must cease alcohol
sales at 9:30 PM (but may continue to sell other products)
• Adult-use marijuana sales must cease at 9:30 PM (not including medical marijuana)
• Indoor & outdoor events
• Theaters/movie theaters (including drive-in movie theaters), and performance
venues (indoor and outdoor)
• Youth and adult amateur sports activities
• Golf facilities
• Recreational boating and boating businesses
• Outdoor recreational experiences
• Casinos and horse tracks/simulcast facilities
• Driving and flight schools
• Zoos, botanical gardens, wildlife reserves, nature centers
• Close contact personal services (such as hair and nail salons)
• Gyms, Fitness Centers and Health Clubs
• Indoor and outdoor pools

Revised Order on Gatherings (COVID-19 Order No. 54):
Governor Baker also signed an updated order restricting gatherings. The new gatherings
order reduces the gathering size limit for gatherings at private residences: indoor gatherings
at private residences are limited to 10 people and outdoor gatherings at private residences are
limited to 25 people. The limit on gatherings held in public spaces and at event venues (e.g.
wedding venues) remains the same. The new order also requires that all gatherings
(regardless of size or location) must end and disperse by 9:30 PM.
The new gatherings order also requires that organizers of gatherings report known positive
COVID-19 cases to the local health department in that community and requires organizers to
cooperate with contact tracing. The gatherings order authorizes continued enforcement by
local health and police departments and specifies that fines for violating the gathering order
will be $500 for each person above the limit at a particular gathering.
