OLD BUSINESS Town Manager's Annual Evaluation  Chair Emkey explained the annual Town Manager evaluation process for the record, as no current Board member had previously participated in the process, and it had not been completed for three years. Each Board member completes an individual evaluation form covering eight topic areas, meets one-on-one with the Town Manager, and the Town Manager also completes a self-assessment. The individual evaluations are compiled by the Select Board Chair in a manner that protects individual identities, and the resulting composite is presented publicly and becomes part of the public record. Individual evaluations are retained in the Town Manager's personnel file. Chair Emkey noted that due to unusual circumstances this evaluation cycle, the four individual evaluations submitted were sent to labor counsel to prepare the composite, as the prior Chair was unable to complete the task. Chair Emkey then prepared a summary of Labor Counsel's composite for public presentation. Board members were given time to review the document prior to its public reading. Chair Emkey read the compiled evaluation summary, which covered the following eight areas:

● Individual Characteristics and Professionalism: The Town Manager was recognized for integrity, transparency, accountability, and ethical leadership. Communication style was identified as an area for continued development, particularly in high-pressure situations and when addressing varied audiences. ● Problem Solving, Decision Making, and Planning: Identified as one of the Town Manager's strongest areas. The Board recognized her sound judgment, adaptability, and ability to bring timely and thoughtful recommendations forward under significant financial and operational pressures. ● Professional Skills and Knowledge: The Board recognized the Town Manager's strong professional competence across municipal responsibilities, with her legal background noted as a significant asset. Meaningful growth in municipal finance was also acknowledged. ● Organizational Leadership and Personnel Management: The Board recognized progress in collaborative leadership, successful labor negotiations, and steps taken to strengthen accountability. Continued attention to staff morale, onboarding, internal communication, and performance management was identified as an important area for ongoing development. ● Community and Public Relations: The Board recognized the Town Manager's commitment to public engagement and transparency during a particularly difficult year. Continued improvement in resident-centered communication and composure in high-stress public settings was noted as important for strengthening community trust. ● Fiscal Management: Identified as a defining focus of the evaluation period. The Board recognized substantial efforts to stabilize financial operations and improve transparency under difficult circumstances, while emphasizing that long-term financial planning, internal controls, and fiscal sustainability must remain ongoing priorities. ● Relations with Members of the Board: Characterized as a strong professional working relationship marked by openness, preparedness, responsiveness, and trust. The overall assessment in this area was notably positive. ● Reporting: The Board recognized meaningful progress in transparency, particularly regarding the finance department transition and budget development. Continued evolution toward more proactive, resident-friendly, and clearly structured reporting was encouraged. Chair Emkey read the compiled best achievement, noting recognition for the Town Manager's efforts in stabilizing the finance department and improving transparency of financial management and decision-making. Areas identified for continued attention included communication style in high-stress situations, internal processes and policies, formal management practices, and fostering a collaborative and supportive work environment. The general comments noted that the Town Manager performed at a high level under exceptionally difficult circumstances and that her efforts have established the foundation needed for continued organizational improvement. Town Manager Warren-Dyment thanked the Board for their individualized feedback and acknowledged that challenges are likely to continue, expressing her commitment to continued growth and collaboration with the Board.
