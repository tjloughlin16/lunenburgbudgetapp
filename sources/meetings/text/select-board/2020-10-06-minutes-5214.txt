Meeting with Bright Farms to discuss Tax Increment Financing Agreement
Bright Farms representative Sean O'Neill joined the meeting. Ms. Lemieux noted in early July
Mr. O'Neill came before the Board to present the Bright Farms project and proposal, as well
as the concept of a Tax Increment Financing Agreement (TIF) with the Town. They had
submitted a timeline of the process. Tonight's appointment is to follow up on the TIF
agreement. Ms. Lemieux has been working with Principal Assessor Byrne and doing research
as the Town does not have any previous TIF agreements. She had been looking at other town's
TIFs as well as reviewing the benefits for both the Town and Bright Farms. The company is
willing to invest in Lunenburg's economy by adding job opportunities and the added value to
the tax revenues. From Ms. Lemieux's memo dated July 14th.
"Tax Increment Financing (TIF) agreements are meant to provide an incentive for large-scale
transformative investments by businesses and developers. The Town can negotiate a Tax Increment
Financing (TIF) agreement when the benefits of private investment are expected to exceed any
foregone tax revenue. One important feature of these negotiated agreements is that they temporarily
reduce taxes on the improvement to the property resulting from new investment (the "increment").
The Town does not lose existing revenue because it continues to collect taxes on pre-investment
property value. A negotiated and temporary reduction in taxes collected against the incremental value
is intended to encourage investment that may not otherwise occur. After the reduction period, the
Town gains all taxes against the baseline value-and all taxes gained against the increment. "
There are two phases that Bright Farms is proposing; the first phase of the project is an
estimated value of a ten million dollar investment. The proposal is a ten year TIF agreement
where year one would start at a 100% discount and then going down by 10% each year.
By the tenth year the Town would be receiving 90% of the value. Year eleven would be the
full tax assessment.

Mr. Jeffreys reported the TCP Building Design Committee (TCPBDC) met and their focus has
been to meet with community organizations to get a better understanding of what their needs. We
had input from the Boys & Girls Club of Lunenburg, the Turkey Hill Lions Club, the Grange,
individual requests for space for ages from 18 to 55 years old. Requests were for expanded space
