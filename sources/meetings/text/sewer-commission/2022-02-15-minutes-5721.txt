Minutes of 02/15/22 Sewer Commission Special Budget Meeting- via Zoom (Video Conference)
Attendees: Chair- Dave MacDonald, Vice-Chair- John Reynolds, Clerk- Brett Ramsden, Members: Mike Deveikis
and Mike Nault, DPW Director- Rob Oliva, Business Manager-Barb Lefebvre, Minutes Clerk- Jane Ranley
Dave opened the meeting at 5:39 pm. and did a roll call of attendees. The Commission began the meeting by saying
the Pledge of Allegiance.
PUBLIC COMMENT- none
CURRENT BUSINESS -
FY23 Budget discussion-
Revenues - Barb went over the figures for FY23. User charges are $1,022,550.00 which is based on current
customers plus expected new customers and includes the annual irrigation meter fee. Barb included a 3% increase
for the Commission's consideration since there will a deficit. The Commission discussed the increase and whether it
needs to be more than 3%: Barb noted the expenses include several items that they still need to discuss. Barb also
pointed out $20k will be transterred from the Capital Reserve, for infrastructure. Currently, there is $123K in the
Capital Reserve Stabilization Fund. Barb reviewed the other revenue items. New developments were discussed.
Expenses- Salaries includes part time employee of 24hrs/week @ $22/hr. and the Cost of Living (COLA) increase to
be voted on at Town Meeting. Barb noted employee benefits along with liability insurances, office space costs and
DPW director's 15% are paid under Indirect Costs as one lump sum. Purchase of Service- Infrastructure includes
repairs at pump stations, jetting, manhole repairs, stub installations, police details, flow meter equipment lease and
meter program licensing for $64,700. I/I remediation - is at $10,000. Outsourcing - $96,414 - Barb noted the
majority of this expense is for the Year 3 pump station contract with CTE. It also includes generator maintenance
contract, groundskeeping, stub location and potential billing support for future bill mailings. Barb noted CTE had an
increase of $3K for the year 3 contract. She clarified that the FY21 budget was originally $50,000 for infrastructure,
but it was bumped up to cover the $ 68,500 spent. Barb noted that if expenses are more in one category in Purchase
of Service, that they can transfer money if available from the other categories. Engineering services - Barb and
Rob noted this category was increased in FY23 to $30K, to cover the Upland/Eastern Ave. design, pump station
evaluation and ARPA fund projects. Barb stated the actuals for FY22 are $10K for Flow Assessment and $13,200 for
Wright Pierce. Utilities - Barb stated all are modest increases and she confirmed we are getting solar credits in
FY22.
Other Departmental Expenses - Advertising- Dave suggested mailing postcards to new owners on sewer line who
are not hooked up. Rob said most new owners learn about Title V issues and sewer at the sale. Barb noted
advertising is usually for posting hearings. Capital Expenditures - Budgeted at $50K. Rob said that he has not
spent all of the FY22 budgeted amount. Dave suggested budgeting $25K instead of $50K and the Commission
discussed other figures. Brett suggested that if Rob needed more money, they could find the extra. Rob added
anything not spent goes back to Retained Earnings. Barb noted the Budget should be finalized by 3/8 meeting as the
FinCom presentation is 3/24. She will have an updated year-to-date FY22 budget for the next meeting. Leominster
debt service is based on flows. Barb received a figure of $38K from Leominster for FY 22 and bumped it up to $41K
for FY23. Fitchburg debt service is $20,962 which is the same amount transferred in from the Highfields payment
on account until it is used up. Leominster usage ($520K) was bumped up for an increase in customers and is
weather dependent. Fitchburg usage ($220K) was also increased due to more customers and the rate increase.
The Connection fees mirror the predicted 5 Leominster and 1 Fitchburg connections. Indirect costs of $106, 165 is
the estimate from Karen (Town Accountant) which includes health insurance and other benefits. Budgeted totals -
Expenses-$1,319,347.82, Revenues -$1, 133,962.00 and Deficit-$185,385.82. Dave asked Barb for an update on
the current deficit for FY22. Barb replied it is now $216K because alot of the yearly expenses have been entered but
only 50% of the quarterly billing has been received so far. Sewer Betterment account - FY23 Sewer Betterment
Debt Service -we will owe $687,992 and the Anticipated Betterment Revenues are $503,081, which leaves a
budgeted deficit of $184,911 for Debt Service over Betterment Revenue. Barb noted the Debt Service is firm, but
Betterment Revenues fluctuate as properties are sold or new properties come on. She pointed out that the next 2
===PAGE 2===
years will be deficits but in FY25 the debt service drops way down, and they will be looking at a surplus of about
$90K-100K in Betterment Revenues over Debt Service. For FY23, the total combined deficit will be $370,297, which
will hit Retained Earnings. This doesn't include the 3% transfer to Capital Reserve of $34,018 for major expenses.
The balance on the Capital Reserve Fund is about $123K now. Barb added that it is up to the Commission if they
want to do the 3% of revenues transfer that was instituted several years ago. The OPEB (the town's state retirement
funding) transfer is $656.00- same as past years. The net/net FY 23 Retained Earnings deficit would be $404,972.
Dave asked about the owners that are delinquent on their betterments and Barb replied that if they do not pay, it will
goes as a tax lien. As properties sell, betterments have to be paid off in order to purchase the property. Brett asked
for clarification on why FY21 ended in the black even though there was a larger Betterment vs Debt deficit. Barb
replied the Betterment figures are estimates and in FY21, they actually received total betterments of $731,129
instead of the budgeted estimate of $ 610,748. Dave asked to confirm the Retained Earnings balance for the end of
FY21 is $1,828,706 and that they would taking out approximately $400K. Barb confirmed. The Commission
discussed and were okay with the FY23 Budget. John and Mike D. agreed that the next two years will be difficult.
Barb said FY23 and FY24 will be harsh, but relief will come in FY25. The Commission discussed a rate increase of
5% or 10%. John and Dave agreed they will need to go higher than 3%. Rob said that they are not keeping up with
inflation and cost of materials without one. He added the CE contract will end after FY23, and they could get hit
even higher. The Commission discussed rate increases between 5% and 8%. They also discussed looking at yearly
increases, since Fitchburg and Leominster rates go up yearly. Dave suggested putting in rate increases to compare
with the Leominster and Fitchburg increases. Barb noted a 3% increase would add $30,600 and 5% would add
$51,600. The Commission agreed there needs to be an increase and Dave suggested adding a policy for automatic
yearly increases. The Commission continued to discuss the increase, and all agreed with 8%. Mike D. added they
could look at a yearly 2-3% increase if needed. Dave asked Barb to run the numbers for the next meeting at a 6%
and 8% increase. Dave pointed out the biggest cost is Leominster usage, and he would still like to talk with
Leominster. Rob pointed out the rain this year was high, but dry years will be lower. Regarding Revenues, Dave
asked when the last time Connection Fees were raised. Barb replied the Connection fee is $2,250 for a 3 bedroom
and higher for more bedrooms. She noted they would need to show what the costs are in order to increase
Connection Fees and many towns do not have both Connection and Betterment fees. Also Fitchburg and Leominster
have not raised their connection fees to Lunenburg. John noted as a ratepayer, he is ok with 8% increase. Barb will
run the numbers and the Commission will discuss it again on 2/22. Dave noted the Town Manager sent an email
stating the Zoom meetings will continue to July.
