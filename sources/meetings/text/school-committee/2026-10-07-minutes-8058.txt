a. In Person Interview Richard Mathieu, Director of Finance and Operations 
 
The Committee interviewed Richard Mathieu for the Director of Finance and Operations position. Chair 
Brzozoski explained that she and Ms. Russell had participated in the first round of interviews. 
Mr. Mathieu reviewed his municipal and school finance experience, beginning with his work as a town 
accountant in Douglas in 2002 and his move to school finance at Dover-Sherborn in 2005. He described 
experience in both regional and municipal school districts, municipal accounting software, accounts 
payable, and financial systems. He also discussed his leadership and legislative committee involvement in 
professional finance organizations. 
 
Mr. Mathieu stated that he was serving as Assistant Business Manager for Concord Public Schools and the 
Concord-Carlisle Regional School District. He explained that he had taken that position after working in 
Dudley-Charlton during the pandemic and was ready to return to a leadership role. He described Lunenburg 
as a community that valued its schools, hard work, and resourcefulness. 
 
Members questioned Mr. Mathieu about accounting, working within limited resources, changing established 
procedures, internal controls, communication, and grant administration. He emphasized listening and 
understanding existing practices before making changes, obtaining staff support, and implementing 
improvements incrementally. 
 
In discussing cash receipts, Mr. Mathieu stressed the importance of sound internal controls and 
accountability, including numbered tickets to verify sales. He noted that proposed changes should account 
for both operational needs and community acceptance. He also emphasized open communication and shared 
goals when working with the Superintendent, employees, and bargaining units. 
 
Mr. Mathieu described his experience with grant accounting and DESE’s grants management system. When 
asked how he would address an unfamiliar issue, he stated that he would acknowledge the need for 
additional information, research the question, and consult colleagues with relevant experience. 
 
A motion was made and seconded to hire Richard Mathieu as Director of Finance and Operations, 
pending successful contract negotiations. Chair Brzozoski called a roll call vote and declared that the 
motion passed unanimously.

c. Beginning of Year Data Presentation 
Dr. Sarfide presented the spring 2026 MCAS results and fall 2026 beginning-of-year DIBELS assessment 
results. The presentation addressed English language arts, mathematics, science, grade eight civics, and 
early literacy screening. 
Dr. Sarfide described the requirements of the Right to Read Act, including evidence-based curricula and 
screening for reading difficulties in kindergarten through grade three. She reported that the district already 
screened students three times annually and that a recent favorable review of its current HMH Into Reading 
curriculum placed the district in a strong position. She did not anticipate a curriculum replacement at that 
time, subject to further state guidance. 
Members discussed how evidence-based curricula are evaluated, the role of DESE, possible options for 
locally developed curricula, and the financial implications of implementation. Dr. Sarfide explained that 
grant opportunities were available and that the state was developing a curriculum option for districts. 
Dr. Sarfide reported that the district had received a PRISM grant to support implementation of the Boston 
Public Schools Focus on Pre-K curriculum and professional development for preschool teachers. A 
consultant was scheduled to visit on October 21 to assess current practices and assist with implementation 
planning. 
Dr. Sarfide emphasized that DIBELS is a screening tool that identifies risk for reading difficulties and does 
not measure proficiency or diagnose a disability. Approximately 70% of kindergarten students were below 
benchmark at the beginning of the year. The percentages presenting as at risk were approximately 45% in 
grade one, 45% in grade two, 46% in grade three, 37% in grade four, and 41% in grade five. 
She noted encouraging results among students performing above benchmark, including approximately 30% 
of grade three students. Members discussed the contributions of instructional materials and Ignite tutoring. 
Dr. Sarfide stated that information about how families would receive screening results would be included in 
her LiteracyLink newsletter. 
On the grade eight civics assessment, 52% of students demonstrated proficiency, exceeding the state 
average by 12 percentage points. Science proficiency was reported at 45% in grade five and 46% in grade 
eight. Both grades exceeded the state average, with grade eight performing seven percentage points above it. 
Dr. Sarfide identified a goal of 85% proficiency in English language arts, mathematics, and science. She 
reported that 39% of students in grades three through eight demonstrated proficiency in English language 
arts. Grade three proficiency was 45%. Members expressed particular concern about the spring grade five 
results, which showed 21% meeting or exceeding expectations, compared with 38% statewide. Those 
students were now in grade six. 
The Committee discussed the importance of early intervention and the increasing difficulty of addressing 
reading gaps as students progress through school. Dr. Sarfide described efforts to use high-quality

h. Vote on Funding Request for the Special Education Reserve Fund – November 2026 Town Meeting 
 
The Committee discussed a funding request for the special education reserve fund for consideration at the November 
2026 Town Meeting. Members noted that the fund had been established but had not yet been funded.

Discussion addressed the unpredictability and cost of out-of-district placements, the timing of Circuit 
Breaker reimbursements, and the need for a reserve to help address unexpected expenses. Members 
expressed concern that limited staffing and resources within the district could contribute to an increased 
need for out-of-district services. 
The Committee considered amounts of $250,000 and $300,000, the relationship to unspent prior-year school 
funds, applicable funding limits, and whether free cash would be certified in time for the November Town 
Meeting. Members discussed the possibility of addressing funding at a later Town Meeting if certification 
was not completed. 
Using an illustrative average of $150,000 per placement, members noted that 15 placements could cost 
approximately $2.25 million. They emphasized that a $300,000 reserve would cover only a limited portion 
of potential costs and that the district was obligated to provide required services. 
Members also identified special education funding and reimbursement timing as topics to raise with 
legislators during an upcoming visit. 
A motion was made and seconded to request $300,000 in free cash, contingent upon certification for 
the November Town Meeting, as part of the warrant article previously voted by the Committee. Chair 
Brzozoski called a roll call vote and declared that the motion passed unanimously.

i. Discussion of Track & Field (Turf) Future Costs 
The Committee discussed future track and field costs, including drainage, retaining walls, and turf 
replacement. Members expressed concern about the condition of the facilities and the ability to obtain 
timely funding through the Town’s capital planning process. 
Members cited previous delays involving the Primary School driveway, an accessible playground, and other 
school facility needs. They expressed concern that a large athletic facility project could absorb available 
capital funding and delay other necessary school projects. 
A member described an understanding from the existing report that drainage and wall repairs could cost 
approximately $1 million before addressing turf replacement. Members requested a clearer explanation of 
the project’s sequence, prerequisites, and anticipated timeline. 
The Committee requested that Mr. Stevens provide an overview, preferably including a concise written 
timeline, to support discussion at the next meeting. Members also encouraged the public to observe the 
condition of the walls and drainage at the athletic facilities. 
No vote was taken.
