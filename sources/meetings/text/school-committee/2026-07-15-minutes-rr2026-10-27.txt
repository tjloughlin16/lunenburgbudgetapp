Action 
a. Vote on warrant article for a Special Town Meeting for school funding 
The Committee reviewed a proposed warrant article requesting that the Select Board include an article on 
the warrant for the November Special Town Meeting to raise and appropriate an additional $350,000 for the 
FY2027 School Department operating budget. Members also reviewed draft warrant language modeled after 
the language used for the FY2024 budget amendment and discussed whether to include an explanatory 
summary describing the purpose of the request. 
The Committee discussed the enactment of the FY2027 state budget and the impact on the School 
Department's finances. Members explained that the Town's FY2027 operating budget had been developed 
using the Governor's preliminary budget proposal released earlier in the year. Because the final state budget 
was not signed until July, the Town's adopted budget did not reflect the additional education-specific state 
revenue subsequently approved by the Legislature. The Committee noted that the final state budget provided 
approximately $418,056 in additional education-related receipts above the amount originally projected. 
Members further discussed that a significant portion of the additional funding was provided through 
education-specific reimbursement accounts rather than through Chapter 70 aid. It was noted that, unlike 
Chapter 70 funding, some reimbursement accounts can fluctuate from year to year and may not represent 
recurring revenue. The Committee also noted that the FY2027 state budget established a commission to 
review the Chapter 70 funding formula, although recommendations from that commission were not 
expected for several years. 
Discussion focused on the decision to seek $350,000, rather than the full $418,056, through the proposed 
warrant article. Members explained that the proposed amount resulted from discussions with Town officials 
regarding the availability of the additional state revenue and the Town's budget obligations. The Committee 
discussed that the remaining balance reflected other education-related financial considerations, including 
required state assessments and municipal budget planning. 
Members discussed the importance of obtaining the additional funding as early as possible in the fiscal year 
to support staffing and educational services. It was noted that delaying the appropriation could affect the 
timing of hiring and implementation of planned expenditures. 
The Committee also reviewed the process required before the funds could be appropriated, including 
submission of the warrant article to the Select Board, preparation and opening of the Special Town Meeting 
warrant, review by the Finance Committee, Select Board action on the warrant, statutory posting 
requirements, and distribution of public notices before the meeting. Members discussed that the anticipated 
Special Town Meeting would likely occur in November, although the scheduling of the meeting remained 
within the authority of the Select Board. 
Questions were raised regarding whether the Committee would have assurances that the proposed article 
would be placed on the November Special Town Meeting warrant and regarding the risks associated with 
implementing portions of the proposed spending plan before Town Meeting approval. Members 
acknowledged that any appropriation remained subject to approval by the voters at Special Town Meeting 
and discussed the possibility of limiting initial expenditures until after Town Meeting action in order to 
reduce financial risk should the article not be approved.

b. Vote on proposed spending plan for school funding 
Dr. Fortuna presented the proposed spending plan for the use of additional school funding. She stated that, 
even if the District ultimately received the full amount of additional funding requested, it would not be 
sufficient to restore all previously reduced staffing or significantly reduce class sizes. She explained that the 
proposal instead focused on investments that research indicated would have the greatest impact on student 
achievement, noting that dedicated reading intervention services had a greater impact on student outcomes 
than reducing class size alone. She emphasized that the recommendations were intended to maximize the 
educational benefit of the available funding while addressing the District's most pressing instructional 
needs. 
The proposed spending plan included restoring a 1.0 Reading Specialist position at the Primary School 
using projected savings resulting from lower-than-anticipated health insurance costs following recent 
negotiations. Dr. Fortuna explained that this position had been eliminated during the prior budget process 
and that restoring it would strengthen early literacy intervention. She further proposed using approximately 
$103,722 of the anticipated additional state funding to restore a 1.0 Reading Specialist position at Turkey 
Hill Elementary School. She noted that preliminary student data continued to support the need for expanded 
reading intervention services and explained that previous staffing reductions had limited the District's ability 
to provide timely support to struggling readers. The proposal also recommended increasing the currently 
budgeted 0.5 Assistant Principal position at Lunenburg High School to a 1.0 full-time position at an 
estimated cost of $90,450. Dr. Fortuna reported that the District had recently hired a 0.5 Athletic Director 
but had been unable to recruit qualified applicants for the remaining half-time Assistant Principal position. 
She explained that feedback from the District's Culture and Climate Survey indicated that a half-time 
administrative position was insufficient to effectively support the school's operational, safety, and student 
needs, and that converting the position to full time would improve both school administration and student 
support. The proposed spending plan also included continuation of the District's Ignite online tutoring 
program at an estimated cost of $129,450. Dr. Fortuna reported that the program had produced measurable 
improvements in student achievement by providing students with individualized, live online tutoring. She 
explained that the purchase of 52 tutoring seats would allow substantially more than 52 students to 
participate over the course of the school year, as seats became available when individual students met their 
learning goals. 
The final recommendation proposed increasing the current 0.6 High School Music Teacher position to 1.0 
full-time at an estimated cost of $26,370. Following discussions with the High School Principal regarding 
the District's instructional priorities, Dr. Fortuna stated that expanding the position would not only increase 
music elective offerings but would also provide greater flexibility in the master schedule. She explained 
that, because high school teachers were permitted to teach one course outside their certification area, the 
expanded position would allow the District to offer an additional wellness section, reducing scheduling

bottlenecks and providing seniors and other students with increased opportunities to enroll in elective and 
advanced-level coursework. 
Discussion then turned to the financial feasibility of implementing the proposed spending plan. It was 
explained that the Primary School Reading Specialist position could be funded with certainty through 
projected health insurance savings. Additional savings realized through year-end purchasing and continued 
conservative budget management were expected to support funding for the Turkey Hill Reading Specialist 
and the full-time Assistant Principal position. It was noted, however, that there was less certainty regarding 
immediate funding for the Ignite tutoring program and the additional 0.4 Music Teacher position. Because 
both initiatives could be implemented later in the school year, members discussed delaying those 
expenditures until after Town Meeting action if necessary. 
In response to questions from Committee members, additional information was provided regarding the 
impact of previous reductions in reading intervention services. It was explained that, at Turkey Hill 
Elementary School, one educator had been assigned both 0.5 Special Education and 0.5 Reading 
Intervention responsibilities. As special education caseloads increased, the educator's legally mandated 
special education responsibilities limited the amount of time available for reading intervention services. At 
the Primary School, reductions in reading intervention staffing had similarly limited the District's ability to 
provide services to all students requiring support. Restoring dedicated reading specialist positions was 
expected to improve the District's ability to identify and provide targeted interventions for students with the 
greatest literacy needs. 
Committee members expressed support for the proposed spending plan, noting that the recommendations 
were data-driven and focused on directing available funding toward programs and positions expected to 
have the greatest educational impact. Members also discussed the risks associated with hiring staff before 
Town Meeting approval of the additional funding while acknowledging the importance of moving forward 
with critical staffing as early as practicable to support students during the upcoming school year. 
Prior to the vote, members emphasized that the spending plan reflected the Committee's proposed allocation 
of the additional school funding rather than authorization to expend the funds. The plan was intended to 
identify the District's funding priorities should the additional appropriation be approved and was 
acknowledged to remain subject to change based on the outcome of the Special Town Meeting and the 
District's financial circumstances. 
Mr. Sculimbrene moved to approve the proposed spending plan for school funding. Discussion 
following the motion: Ms. Brzozoski emphasized that the spending plan represented the Committee's 
proposed use of the additional funding and reflected how the Committee intended to allocate the 
funds if they became available. She noted that the proposal was intended to communicate those 
priorities publicly. The motion was seconded. By roll call vote, the motion passed unanimously: Ms. 
Brzozoski – AYE; Mr. Sculimbrene – AYE; Ms. Russell – AYE; Ms. Gilman – AYE.

7. Public Comment for issues Discussed during 07.15.2026 meeting 
Anna Lockwood addressed the Committee regarding the proposed use and timing of the additional FY2027 
state education funding. She expressed support for appropriating the full amount of the additional state 
funding for the School Department and questioned delaying consideration until the November Special Town 
Meeting. Ms. Lockwood also commented on the proposed compromise amount of $350,000, the process for 
scheduling a Special Town Meeting, and the role of the Select Board in considering the proposed warrant 
article. She encouraged the Committee to pursue an earlier Special Town Meeting and offered to assist in 
obtaining signatures should a citizen's petition become necessary. 
Chris Sullivan also addressed the Committee, expressing support for holding a Special Town Meeting as 
soon as practicable to allow the School Department to access the additional state education funding earlier 
in the fiscal year. He stated that, if the Legislature intended the funding for schools, it should have been 
made available to the District as soon as possible and encouraged the Committee to pursue an earlier 
appropriation regardless of the proposed compromise amount. 
Following public comment, a Committee member noted recent news regarding additional state 
reimbursement funding for municipalities and indicated that additional information might become available 
in the future.
