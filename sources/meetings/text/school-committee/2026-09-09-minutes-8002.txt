4. Chair’s Report 
 
Chair Brzozoski thanked residents for supporting additional school funding at the September 3 Special 
Town Meeting. She reported that the $418,000 appropriation would directly support students, including 
the addition of a second-grade classroom that reduced class sizes from approximately 31 students to 25. 
Although the appropriation addressed immediate needs, it did not eliminate the difference between the 
district’s funding and its educational needs. She recognized school and Town staff for their work preparing 
for the Special Town Meeting and responding to unexpected sound-system problems. Despite successful 
testing before the meeting, the equipment did not function as anticipated. She commended the staff 
members and student employees who remained calm, located alternative equipment and worked together 
to allow the meeting to continue. 
 
Chair Brzozoski also thanked staff for a successful opening of school despite larger classes, reduced 
resources and increased demands. Committee members expressed appreciation for residents’ support and 
for the parents who advocated for additional athletic funding. Members encouraged continued community 
involvement as the district prepared for the next budget cycle.

8. Superintendent’s Report 
 
Dr. Fortuna thanked the community for approving Articles 1 and 3 at the Special Town Meeting. The 
additional funding would expand student learning supports and maintain athletic programming, including 
transportation for student-athletes. She reiterated the district’s commitment to responsible and transparent 
use of the funds.  She also recognized the staff and student employees who addressed the sound -system 
problems during Town Meeting. She commended their flexibility and cooperation under difficult 
circumstances. 
 
The administrative team’s summer retreat focused on the thoughtful and ethical use of emerging 
technologies, grade-level expectations and instructional rigor, equitable access to learning, and consistent 
expectations for sensory spaces.

Dr. Fortuna reported the launch of two literacy initiatives. The New Teacher Center began a landscape 
analysis through classroom learning walks at Turkey Hill and focus groups with educators and other 
stakeholders. The review would examine instructional materials, professional learning, coaching and the 
conditions supporting collaboration. She explained that the work was intended to evaluate the instructional 
system, rather than individual teachers, and identify strengths, barriers and opportunities for more 
consistent literacy instruction. 
 
The Reading Buddy Program also began with training for 12 community volunteers. Volunteers would 
work individually with third-grade students at Turkey Hill during structured, 20-minute sessions focused 
on oral reading fluency. Tr aining addressed reading strategies, student safety, boundaries and program 
expectations. The program would supplement classroom instruction and literacy services. 
 
Dr. Fortuna reported that the additional second -grade classroom reduced class sizes to approximately 24 
or 25 students. Staff considered student needs and family circumstances when reorganizing classes and 
prepared the classroom for students by Tuesday morning following Town Meeting. 
 
Although the additional funding addressed immediate concerns,  Dr. Fortuna emphasized that the district 
continued to face structural financial challenges. The next budget cycle would require distinguishing 
temporary resources from sustainable investments, protecting essential services and explaining the 
educational c onsequences of funding decisions. She committed to providing understandable financial 
information, explaining significant variances and strengthening the assumptions and systems used in 
budget development.

a. Public Interview of Business Administrator – Derrick Andrews 
 
Dr. Fortuna introduced Derrick Andrews, a candidate for School Business Administrator. She described 
his accounting degree from the University of Massachusetts Amherst, public-sector fiscal and 
procurement experience, grant-administration work, prior small-business ownership and current 
employment as a mathematics teacher. 
 
Dr. Fortuna explained that an administrative screening team had interviewed Mr. Andrews and 
recommended him for the Committee’s consideration. The Committee would conduct the public 
interview that evening and discuss the hiring decision at its next meeting. 
 
Mr. Andrews described his work with the Commonwealth’s Operational Services Division and 
Executive Office of Education. His responsibilities included procurement, requests for proposals, vendor 
evaluation, contract negotiations, reconciliations, encumbrances, fund transfers and year-end financial 
work. He had worked with financial leadership on a budget of approximately $56 million to $60 million. 
His grant experience primarily involved financial oversight and compliance. 
 
Members questioned Mr. Andrews about the district’s repeated turnover in the position and the 
challenge of combining accounting expertise, school operations, public communication and financial 
analysis in one role. Mr. Andrews acknowledged that he had not previously served as a school business 
administrator. He emphasized his accounting and public-sector experience, relationship-building skills 
and interest in supporting students through financial administration.

Mr. Andrews described his role in preparing procurement documents, comparing vendor proposals and 
negotiating costs, deliverables and timelines following the selection of vendors. He acknowledged that 
he had not used Munis but explained that he had successfully learned the Commonwealth’s financial 
system in a previous position. 
 
In discussing year-end closeout, Mr. Andrews described reconciling accounts, reviewing encumbrances, 
coordinating invoices and payroll, identifying potential overruns and working with budget leadership to 
address variances before the fiscal year ended. His approach to budget development would begin with 
actual expenditures, historical trends, recurring obligations and anticipated revenue. He would consult 
stakeholders to distinguish ongoing needs from temporary expenses and present options and tradeoffs to 
decision-makers. 
 
Members asked how Mr. Andrews would recognize when outside assistance was needed. He described 
seeking procurement and legal guidance for a complex project involving construction requirements. He 
also discussed the importance of professional contacts and support from experienced school business 
administrators. 
 
Mr. Andrews described experience using data analysis to support procurement decisions, including the 
Commonwealth’s acquisition of personal protective equipment during the pandemic. He emphasized 
presenting financial information in a form that nonfinancial audiences could understand. He had 
reviewed professional training opportunities through the Massachusetts Association of School Business 
Officials and intended to begin the process toward the necessary qualifications. 
 
Members raised concerns about unexpectedly large unspent balances in recent fiscal years despite 
repeated requests for financial updates. Mr. Andrews stated that he would first examine historical 
records, reconciliation procedures and existing controls to determine the cause. He emphasized the 
distinction between encumbered funds and actual expenditures and the importance of ensuring that 
reports accurately conveyed the district’s financial position. 
 
The Committee also discussed the complexity of the Town’s and district’s charts of accounts and the 
need for coordination between their financial offices. Mr. Andrews emphasized communication, clear 
responsibilities and timely identification of discrepancies. He identified learning local procedures, 
school-specific requirements and stakeholder expectations as priorities during his first 90 days. 
No hiring decision was made.

c. Back to School Updates-Principals 
Primary School 
The primary school principal reported an enrollment of 385 students and a positive opening of school. 
Kindergarten students began on September 1 with first- and second-grade students, while preschool and 
Early Learning Center students began the following week. Staff focused on establishing routines, 
building relationships and supporting students’ sense of belonging. The school completed its first fire 
drill successfully. 
Following Town Meeting, staff opened a fifth second-grade classroom. Teachers and support staff 
prepared student assignments, notified families and set up the classroom during the period between 
Thursday’s vote and Tuesday’s opening. Committee members thanked the staff who came in on their 
day off to complete the work. 
The principal reviewed staffing changes, including the new second-grade teacher, a long-term 
kindergarten substitute, transfers between grades and schools, new paraprofessionals and a transition 
from a paraprofessional position to a preschool teaching role. 
Upcoming events included the school’s September 14 observance of Dot Day, picture days on 
September 15 and 16, and the September 24 open house. Open-house sessions would be staggered to 
help manage parking and traffic. 
Turkey Hill Elementary School 
Mr. McGrath reported a positive start to the year and reviewed administrative and instructional staffing 
changes. These included a new assistant principal and staff serving the Therapeutic Learning Center, 
Achieve classroom, English language learners and fifth grade. 
Enrollment totaled 362 students: 109 in third grade, 115 in fourth grade and 138 in fifth grade. Average 
class sizes were approximately 27 in third grade, 29 in fourth grade and 27 in fifth grade. 
Mr. McGrath emphasized safety preparation and reported a successful fire drill on the second day of 
school. He also described the New Teacher Center’s classroom visits and thanked staff for supporting 
the transition to new building leadership.

Third- and fourth-grade classrooms would be self-contained to reduce transitions and preserve 
instructional time. Mr. McGrath would continue reviewing the schedule and making adjustments to 
support learning within the district’s staffing constraints. 
Middle School and High School 
The middle/high school principal reported a smooth opening and reviewed enrollment, staffing and 
student opportunities. 
Middle school enrollment totaled 387 students, including 112 in sixth grade, 115 in seventh grade and 
160 in eighth grade. Class sizes were approximately 22 in sixth grade, 24 in seventh grade and 26 to 27 
in eighth grade. Some world language classes approached 32 or 33 students following staffing 
reductions. 
Clubs, student government, drama and Science Olympiad were beginning, and the first middle school 
dance was planned for October. Staff were reviewing travel providers for the eighth-grade Washington, 
D.C. trip, including a possible train option at a cost comparable to previous trips. A proposal would be 
brought to the Committee for approval, with members emphasizing the need to provide families with 
early cost information. 
All teaching positions at the middle and high schools had been filled, although some paraprofessional 
vacancies remained. Social workers had been hired for both schools, strengthening mental health and 
social-emotional support. Approximately 15 staff members were new to their buildings or positions. 
High school schedules were being finalized through the add/drop process. Funding approved at Town 
Meeting would support additional course opportunities through a full-time music teacher. The 
administrative team was fully staffed and focused on supporting students, employees and families. 
Members discussed students choosing other districts because of educational opportunities unavailable 
in Lunenburg. They expressed concern about the effect on students, school funding and the broader 
community, and reiterated their commitment to preserving and expanding local opportunities. 
Dr. Fortuna commended the principals and their teams for filling vacancies, preparing classrooms and 
supporting a successful opening of school.

f. Potential Financial Audit 
Dr. Fortuna reported that the Finance Committee would discuss funding a financial audit at its 
meeting the following evening. The Town Manager was requesting the use of Finance Committee 
reserve funds. Town and school representatives had met with one potential firm and planned to meet 
with another. 
Chair Brzozoski stated that an audit would help establish a reliable financial starting point for the 
incoming business administrator and answer questions about prior-year financial reporting. She 
expressed concern about using funds needed for student services to pay for the work and hoped that 
Town funding would be available. 
Members discussed the proposed scope, including the reasons for recurring financial discrepancies, 
the accuracy of athletic financial information, reconciliation procedures and the relationship between 
the Town and school business offices. They emphasized the need to document responsibilities, 
financial procedures and the flow of information between offices. 
Dr. Fortuna reported that one firm anticipated approximately two months to complete the work. 
Members emphasized obtaining findings early enough to inform budget development. 
The Committee discussed the importance of transparency and implementing improvements identified 
through the review. Members also expressed concern that the audit should be used constructively to 
improve financial practices, rather than as a reason to reduce school funding. 
It was clarified that the proposed audit was intended to improve financial procedures and reporting 
and was not based on suspected criminal activity or malfeasance. Chair Brzozoski and Dr. Fortuna 
planned to attend the Finance Committee meeting to discuss the request. 
No vote was taken.

h. SC Goals & Year Review 
The Committee reviewed the three goals adopted on November 5, 2025: the future of Turkey Hill 
Elementary School, the primary school parking lot and playground, and the budget process. 
The Turkey Hill initiative had been deferred while the new superintendent and facilities manager 
became established. Dr. Fortuna reported that Mr. Stevens was gathering information for the next 
Massachusetts School Building Authority Statement of Interest process. 
Funding had been secured for a study of the primary school parking lot and playground, but the 
study and improvements had not yet been completed. Members proposed a more specific goal to 
implement the study and identify the funding needed for subsequent work. 
Members agreed that the budget process had improved through an earlier start, more frequent 
discussion, greater transparency and tri-board participation. They acknowledged that these 
improvements had not resolved the district’s financial challenges. 
For Turkey Hill, members discussed establishing a goal to prepare and submit the Statement of 
Interest. Dr. Fortuna described the anticipated January-to-April application period and offered to 
provide an overview of the process, including Committee and community participation. Members 
recognized that acceptance was uncertain and that a major school project would take several years. 
They also asked that the district consider interim improvements addressing accessibility and building 
conditions while pursuing longer-term options. 
Financial stability was identified as another proposed goal. Members discussed stabilizing the 
business office, improving access to reliable financial information and implementing 
recommendations from the proposed audit. The assistant business administrator position was 
intended to provide continuity and support. 
The Committee discussed continued communication with the Select Board and Finance Committee. 
Chair Brzozoski proposed at least two tri-board meetings, including an early meeting focused on 
community priorities and the relationship between those priorities and budget decisions. Members 
identified joint discussion, public participation and school advocacy as valuable aspects of the prior 
process. Some members questioned the value of repeating detailed departmental presentations 
already provided elsewhere. 
Members also discussed the need for a broader Town strategy to increase revenue and state support. 
Suggestions included inviting legislators to visit the schools, involving teachers and students in 
advocacy, encouraging community-generated ideas and holding an informational event to explain 
school needs and budget choices.
