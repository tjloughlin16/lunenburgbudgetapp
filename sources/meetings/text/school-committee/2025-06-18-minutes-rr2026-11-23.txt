Beardmores.  I recently came across a bit aged and worn Russ Coburn carving of a black capped 
chickadee and I couldn’t resist the urge to buy it and present it to Dr. Burnham this evening, not only 
was this bird made in Lunenburg, like the education of 2500 or so students that were here during her 
tenure, but the black capped chickadee is the state bird of Massachusetts.  This unassuming song bird 
is a year round resident and during the harshest winters with grace, despite its size the chickadee 
demonstrates extraordinary resilience, it can lower its body temperature during cold nights to 
conserve energy.  A testament to its adaptability and strength in the face of adversity.  Much like the 
chickadee, Dr. Burnham has faced challenges head on, always finding a way to persevere, whether 
tackling complex projects, navigating unforeseen obstacles, Covid, or impossible budget scenarios, 
she has shown unwavering persistence, determination, and grace.  The chickadee also possesses 
remarkable intelligence, it catches food in numerous locations, relying on its exceptional memory to 
retrieve its reserves later on.  This foresight and planning mirror Dr. Burnham intellect, strategic 
thinking, wisdom, and accomplishments throughout her career, and perhaps notably the chickadee is 
thick skinned, not in the literal sense, but in its ability to endure and thrive despite its small size. 
Similarly, Dr. Burnham has demonstrated a resilient spirit, handling pressure with poise and 
maintain a positive outlook even in the most challenging of times.  Dr. Burnham and I have not 
always agreed, but she nearly always sought amenable solutions and compromise and always 
reminded me that what we agree on was much greater than what we disagreed on and that we are 
more alike than different.  It has been an honor to serve with her, to collaborate with her, and to learn 
from her both before and during my time on the school committee.  I hope we maintain a friendship 
for years to come. The black cap chickadee carved by Lunenburg’s Russ Coburn is a symbol of the 
enduring qualities that Kate Burnham has brought to our team.  May it serve as a reminder to her, 
perhaps displayed amongst her turtles, of our appreciation of her intellect, resilience, persistence, 
strength, and leadership?  Congratulations and thank you

4. Chair’s Report We were notified last week that there will likely be more funds allocated to the 
school district in the amount of roughly $50,000 and we will generate a plan for how those funds 
will be spent.  C.S. Lewis once said is that integrity is when you do the right thing even if no one is 
watching.  That phrase is Dr. Kate Burnham, over the past 7 years she has worked tirelessly with 
innovation and courage for our children.  The test of every action she has taken in that time is simple 
– Will it make it better for the students?  Her focus on students is relentless, I learned this last year 
after a policy subcommittee meeting, you told me that the thing you were going to do to get into the 
Christmas spirit was to go down to the Primary School and watch dismissal, it was a source of joy 
and happiness for her because as always she is focused on the students.  Being a superintendent can 
be a thankless job, good results are barely acceptable and bad results, even when beyond your 
control are unquestionably your fault.  Through covid, inflation, budget uncertainty, political climate 
dominated by extremists, and classic small town politics, Dr. Burnham, you have led with integrity.  
We are lucky to have had you at the helm.  I know that my children and other children in Lunenburg 
are better for you being here. As you end your education career, it is hard to imagine all of the lives 
you have made better.  The butterfly effect is the idea that small changes can have profound effects 
in the future.  This is what educators do every day, it is what you have done for your students, your 
staff, and teachers and for all of us. In the years I have been on school committee, I have often been 
one of your students, you have taught me a lot about education and you have taught me a lot about 
how to be a leader.  I have learned a great deal about this complex endeavor of public education.  
Thank you Kate, I hope you have a well-deserved and enjoyable retirement.

7. Superintendents Report If a family has made a request for bus service for the next school year, 
they need to make the payment of the bus fee.  Anyone who has not paid the fee is not being passed 
on to Dee bus for a bus route.  We have forms posted on our website for families that believe they 
would qualify for a waived or reduced fee.   We are working on packing up the spaces used by ACE 
and extended day as well as all administration office to move to Turkey Hill.  The movers will move 
the heavy items on June 26th, phones will be changed over on the 26th.  Administration offices 
should be open on June 30th, however, it will take several weeks to unpack.  As far as the move out 
of TCP, the town did not force us to move, earlier in 2024 we had a strategy of options to move from 
TCP due to the deterioration of the building. Based on conversations for programs and rent, it was 
ultimately decided we should move to Turkey Hill, this was supposed to be a temporary move until 
the Ritter Building upgrades were complete.  Business Administrator position has been reposted 
after two applicants declined the position due to salary.  I did speak with Dr. Fortuna and it was 
agreed upon to have a contracted service in the interim, this will allow us to get through end or year 
reports on time.  Summer hours are open Monday – Thursday and remote work on Friday. All 
offices are closed tomorrow for the Juneteenth holiday.  Dr. Burnham shared her statement to the 
district and her experience over the past 38 years in education.
