5. Chair’s Report 
Chair Brzozoski reported that, following the previous School Committee meeting, Ms. Gilman, acting as a 
private citizen and not on behalf of the School Committee, submitted a petition to the Town Clerk calling 
for a Special Town Meeting. The petition triggered the legal process requiring the Select Board to hold a 
Special Town Meeting within 45 days. The additional meeting, separate from the November Special Town 
Meeting, is scheduled for September 3, 2026. The Committee will discuss the meeting and any related 
action it may wish to take later on the agenda. She then provided a detailed presentation on the 
Massachusetts Department of Revenue Cherry Sheet estimates and the additional state funding available for 
FY27. She explained that the Town develops its budget early in the year using the Governor’s initial local 
aid proposal, which generally contains more conservative estimates than the amounts ultimately approved 
through the state budget process. The final state budget resulted in increases in several education-related 
funding categories above the amounts used when the Town budget was developed. She reviewed Chapter 
70, explaining that it establishes a foundation budget and considers both a municipality’s ability to 
contribute toward education and the needs of its students. Lunenburg, like many communities, funds its 
schools above the state-required minimum because the foundation budget does not reflect the actual cost of 
educating students. She also reviewed charter tuition reimbursement, Smart Growth School Cost 
Reimbursement, and School Choice tuition, noting that charter and Smart Growth reimbursements can 
fluctuate significantly from year to year and should not necessarily be viewed as recurring revenue. School 
Choice revenue has generally declined over the past 15 years, although it increased for FY27. 
The final FY27 state budget provides an additional $135,915 in Chapter 70 funding, $116,413 in charter 
tuition reimbursement, and approximately $160,000 in Smart Growth School Cost Reimbursement. The 
overall increase in Cherry Sheet receipts was $491,988. She explained that increases in state aid are not 
automatically available to the School Department and must be appropriated by Town Meeting before they 
can be allocated to a Town department. She also reviewed the assessment side of the Cherry Sheet, 
including the amounts charged to the Town for students attending charter schools and other districts through 
School Choice. Charter school tuition is based on the cost of educating a student in the sending community, 
while state charter tuition reimbursement is intended to offset a portion of that financial impact. For FY27, 
School Choice sending tuition decreased while charter school tuition increased, resulting in an additional 
$15,326 in education-related state assessments. After subtracting those increased assessments from the 
additional Cherry Sheet receipts, the Town will receive a net increase of $476,662 in state aid. She 
expressed concern that charter and School Choice sending costs could increase in the future if families seek 
educational opportunities outside the District, noting both the financial impact on the Town and the broader 
effect on the community when students leave Lunenburg schools. She emphasized the importance of 
providing educational opportunities that encourage families to remain in the District. In closing, she thanked 
the parents, students, community members, and School Committee members who attended state hearings, 
contacted legislators, signed letters, and otherwise advocated for increased education funding, noting that 
those continued advocacy efforts contributed to the additional state funding received for FY27.

Fireworks – Lunenburg 300th Anniversary 
The Committee reviewed a proposal related to the Town of Lunenburg’s 300th Anniversary celebration. It was 
explained that the Town will celebrate its 300th anniversary in 2028 and, consistent with prior anniversary 
celebrations, the 300th Anniversary Committee is planning a “First Knight” celebration on December 31, 2027, to 
formally begin the anniversary year. The evening is expected to include activities in the Town center, including 
possible activities involving the Senior Center and Teen Center, followed by a short fireworks display of 
approximately six to eight minutes. The proposal calls for the fireworks to be launched from the grass football 
practice field near the high school rather than the turf field. The location was selected because it is fenced, can be 
secured from public access and would allow the fireworks to be visible from much of the Town center area. The 
Committee reviewed information regarding the professional fireworks company, insurance requirements and site 
considerations. It was noted that the company, formerly known as Atlas Fireworks had reviewed potential 
locations and determined that other sites, including the area behind Turkey Hill Elementary School, presented 
concerns regarding potential property damage. The Committee discussed possible impacts to the practice field, 
including vehicle access, snow conditions and the potential for damage to school property. It was noted that the 
event will occur during the winter when the ground is expected to be frozen and potentially snow-covered, 
reducing the likelihood of damage to the grass. The fireworks will be conducted by a professional company, and 
the Committee noted that concerns regarding fire or property damage were substantially reduced compared with 
summertime or unauthorized fireworks displays. Parking and access logistics will continue to be coordinated as 
planning progresses. The Committee expressed support for the event as a community celebration marking the 
beginning of Lunenburg’s 300th anniversary year.

FY27 Budget – Additional State Aid 
Ms. Gilman requested that the Committee reconsider the warrant article approved at its previous meeting, 
increasing the FY27 School Department operating budget request from $350,000 to $418,056 and submitting the 
revised article for the September 3, 2026 Special Town Meeting. She noted that the final FY27 state budget 
provides Lunenburg with $418,056 more in education-specific receipts than had been included in the Governor’s 
original proposal. The Committee also discussed the Town’s approximately $476,000 overall net increase in 
state aid. Ms. Gilman noted that the School Department had reduced its FY27 budget by more than $800,000 and 
maintained that the additional education funding should be directed to the schools. She clarified that her citizen 
petition was filed as a private citizen to trigger a Special Town Meeting and did not specify an appropriation 
amount. 
 
Dr. Fortuna identified restoration of a Certified Occupational Therapy Assistant (COTA) position as an 
additional priority that could be funded by increasing the request to $418,056. The position had been left vacant 
through attrition to help balance the FY27 budget, despite increasing numbers of students requiring specialized 
services. She explained that recent state restrictions on the use of cool-down rooms have increased the need for 
individualized sensory and regulation plans, which occupational therapy staff are trained to develop and support 
in classrooms. Members noted that the new requirements were not accompanied by additional state funding. 
Dr. Fortuna explained that adequate specialized staffing helps the District serve students within its schools and 
potentially avoid more restrictive and costly out-of-district placements. Members emphasized that the COTA 
position had remained vacant because of financial constraints, not because the position was unnecessary. 
The Committee discussed the relationship between Ms. Gilman’s citizen petition and a School Committee-
sponsored warrant article. The September 3 warrant remained open until August 7, allowing the Committee to 
amend its previously approved article and submit the revised amount without collecting signatures. Ms. Gilman 
stated that if the Committee sponsored the article, she did not intend to separately seek an appropriation through 
her citizen petition. 
 
Members also discussed whether the additional state aid should be considered recurring revenue, noting that 
overall education-related Cherry Sheet receipts have historically trended upward while individual categories, 
including charter and Smart Growth reimbursements, can fluctuate. Dr. Fortuna acknowledged the challenges of 
using potentially variable revenue for recurring personnel costs. 
 
The Committee discussed directing the additional funding primarily toward intervention and support positions 
rather than immediately restoring classroom teachers. Dr. Fortuna noted the difficulty of hiring teachers after a 
September Town Meeting, the effectiveness of targeted intervention, and the limited class-size reduction that 
could be achieved with the available funding. Members emphasized that classroom teachers remain necessary, 
but the District has been forced to prioritize its most immediate needs because of financial constraints. 
Although members acknowledged that individual warrant articles are not an ideal method of developing a school 
budget, they characterized the circumstances as extraordinary and supported seeking the additional education-
specific state funding. 
 
Ms. Gilman moved to amend the previously approved warrant article to request $418,056 for the FY27 
School Department operating budget and to submit the amended article for the September 3, 2026 Special 
Town Meeting. Mr. Sculimbrene seconded the motion. Following discussion, the motion was restated as: 
To see if the Town will vote to raise and appropriate an additional sum of $418,056 and no cents for the FY27 
operating budget for the School Department, or take any other action relative thereto. Ms. Brzozoski – AYE; 
Mr. Sculimbrene – AYE; Ms. Russell – AYE; Ms. Gilman – AYE. Motion passed unanimously.

Custodial MOU 
Dr. Fortuna reviewed the recently negotiated agreement with the custodial bargaining unit, AFSCME Council 
93. She expressed appreciation for the collaborative manner in which both sides approached negotiations. School 
Committee representatives on the negotiating team were Ms. Brzozoski and Ms. Gilman. The union negotiating 
team included Rich Krafke, Jimmy Gentilia and Tim McKenna. The agreement provides salary increases of 
3.5% in FY27, 2.5% in FY28 and 2.5% in FY29. Two additional salary steps were also added to assist with 
employee retention and recognize longevity in these essential positions. 
 
Additional contractual changes include revisions to Article 5 regarding dues deduction language to more closely 
correspond with AFSCME membership-card language; replacement of references to “working days” with 
“business days” in the grievance and arbitration provisions; and replacement of the American Arbitration 
Association with the Labor Relations Connection for arbitration, formalizing a provision that had previously 
been addressed through a side letter. Language in Article 7 was also revised to clarify that alleged contractual 
violations may proceed through the grievance and arbitration process. 
 
Changes to Article 10 address the use of temporary employees. Individuals outside of the bargaining unit may 
not perform bargaining-unit work on a permanent or indefinite basis. Temporary workers may be used to assist 
with work inside or outside District buildings but may not work for more than six consecutive months or be used 
to diminish bargaining-unit rights or benefits. The District must notify the union of the temporary worker’s start 
date, work location and anticipated length of service. 
 
Article 22 was revised to include a new longevity payment of $500 for employees with 5–9 years of service. 
Existing longevity benefits were changed from percentages to fixed-dollar amounts to make budgeting more 
predictable: $1,000 for 10–14 years; $1,250 for 15–19 years; and $1,750 for 20 or more years of service. The 
clothing allowance was increased to $455. 
 
Dr. Fortuna reported that AFSCME Council 93 had ratified the agreement and that School Committee approval 
was required to complete the process. Committee members complimented both negotiating teams for being 
prepared, professional and collaborative and noted the custodians’ commitment to the District, its facilities and 
students. 
 
Ms. Gilman moved to accept the negotiated changes and approve the custodial agreement with AFSCME 
Council 93. Mr. Sculimbrene seconded the motion. Ms. Brzozoski – AYE; Mr. Sculimbrene – AYE; Ms. 
Russell – AYE; Ms. Gilman – AYE. Motion passed unanimously.
