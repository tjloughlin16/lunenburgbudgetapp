This office received a complaint from Leia Adey, dated October 13, 2016, alleging that 
the Freetown Zoning Board of Appeals (the "Board") violated the Open Meeting Law, G.L. c. 
30A, §§ 18-25. The complaint was originally filed with the Board on September 7, 2016, and, 
after an extension of time was granted, the Board responded by letter dated September 29, 2016. 
In her complaint, Ms. Adey alleges that the Board violated the Open Meeting Law by: (i) 
improperly regulating public participation during a meeting; and (ii) deliberated during a meeting 
in a manner that was inaudible to the public.1 
Following our review, we find that the Board did not violate the Open Meeting Law. In 
reaching a determination, we reviewed the original complaint, the Board's response, and the 
request for further review filed with our office. We also spoke with the complainant by 
telephone on January 11, 2017. 
We find the facts as follows. On August 3, 2016, the Board conducted a public hearing 
concerning property located at 17 Ridge Flill Road in Assonet. During this hearing, the Board 
heard testimony from various stakeholders, including members of the public. During its next 
meeting, on August 31, 2016, the Board met to discuss and vote on the matter based on the

DISCUSSION 
I. The Board Did Not Violate the Open Meeting Law by Limiting Public 
Participation During its August 31. 2016 Meeting. 
The Open Meeting Law states that "no person shall address a meeting of a public body 
without permission of the chair, and all persons shall, at the request of the chair, be silent." G.L. 
c. 3OA, § 20(f). Thus, the law gives the public body chair discretion to determine who may 
speak or otherwise participate during a meeting. See OML 2014-23; OML 2013-64; OML 2012-
59.2 
Here, the complaint alleges that the Board violated the Open Meeting Law by denying 
certain members of the audience the opportunity to speak during the August 31, 2016 meeting, 
while allowing one individual the chance to do so. While we find that it is within the chair's 
discretion to limit participation, and thus the Board did not violate the Open Meeting Law with 
respect to this allegation, we encourage public bodies to allow public participation during 
meetings whenever possible. See OML 2014-129; OML 2012-59; OML 2012-23. 
II. While Portions of the August 31. 2016 Meeting were Difficult to Hear, we find 
No Violation of the Open Meeting Law. 
The Open Meeting Law requires that meetings of a public body be open and accessible to 
the public. See G.L. c. 3OA, § 20(a); OML 2012-49. Access must include the opportunity to be 
physically present as well as to see and hear what is being discussed by the members of the 
public body. See OML 2013-189; OML 2012-66; OML 2012-48. 
The complaint alleges that the Board violated the Open Meeting Law by holding 
discussions during the August 31, 2016 meeting that were inaudible to members of the audience. 
Indeed, because the Board was unaccustomed to holding meetings in a large venue and was 
similarly unaccustomed to using microphones during meetings, it seems that portions of the 
meeting were difficult to hear. When notified that such problems were occurring, the chair made 
an attempt to repeat certain statements and questions. Because of this, we find that any audio 
problems did not rise to the level of an Open Meeting Law violation. See OML 2014-3. 
Nevertheless, we strongly caution the Board to be diligent during future meetings to ensure that 
members of the public present can clearly hear all discussion that takes place.

While not raised in the complaint, we note that in the request for review filed with our 
office the complainant raises questions about the manner in which the Board responded to her 
complaint. We therefore take this opportunity to clarify the proper procedure for responding to 
an Open Meeting Law complaint. Within 14 days of receipt of an Open Meeting Law complaint, 
a public body must review the complaint's allegations; take remedial action, if appropriate; and 
send to the Attorney General a copy of the complaint and a description of any remedial action 
taken. G.L. c. 30A, § 23(b); 940 CMR 29.05(5). The body must review the complaint before 
authorizing legal counsel, or any other individual, to respond on its behalf. 940 CMR 29.05(4); 
OML 2015-24. 
CONCLUSION 
For the reasons stated above, we find that the Board did not violate the Open Meeting 
Law. We now consider the complaint addressed by this determination to be resolved. This 
determination does not address any other complaints that may be pending with our office or the 
Board. Please feel free to contact the Division at (617) 963 - 2540 if you have any questions.
