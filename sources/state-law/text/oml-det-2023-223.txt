 RE: Open Meeting Law Complaint 
 
Dear Attorney Braun: 
 
This office received a complaint from Patrick Higgins on November 23, 2023,
1 alleging 
that the Somerset Zoning Board of Appeals (the “Board”) violated the Open Meeting Law, G.L. 
c. 30A, §§ 18-25.  The complaint was originally filed with the Board on or about October 19, and 
you responded on behalf of the Board by letter dated November 22.  The complaint pertains to 
the Board’s October 12 meeting. 
 
The complaint first alleges that the Board’s Chair did not announce that the meeting was 
being recorded; the complainant contends that the Chair must specifically use the word 
“recorded” in order to comply with the Law’s requirements.  The Open Meeting Law states, 
“[a]fter notifying the chair of the public body, any person may make a video or audio recording 
of an open session of a meeting of a public body, or may transmit the meeting through any 
medium, subject to reasonable requirements of the chair as to the number, placement and 
operation of equipment used so as not to interfere with the conduct of the meeting.  At the 
beginning of the meeting, the chair shall inform other attendees of any recordings.”  G.L. c. 30A, 
§ 20(f).  At the start of the Board’s October 12 meeting, the Chair announced “this meeting is 
being telecast live on SATV Channel 15 or YouTube.  It is also available on the SATV website, 
video of the meeting is available.”
2  This statement clearly informed attendees that the meeting 
was being recorded; any assertion to the contrary is not made in good faith. 
 
Next, the complaint alleges that the Board voted to authorize legal counsel to respond to 
seven prior Open Meeting Law complaints without engaging in a full review of the complaints 
during a meeting.  As we have previously explained to the complainant on numerous occasions,

Finally, the complaint alleges that the Board did not read the complaints aloud during the 
meeting but instead referred to the complaints by number, which the complainant asserts 
prohibited the public from understanding the substance of the complaints.  We find no error in 
the manner in which the Board handled the complaints and note a public body may even choose 
to review Open Meeting Law complaints entirely in executive session.  See, e.g., OML 2023-
109; OML 2019-170; OML 2013-82; OML 2012-119. 
 
For the reasons stated above, we find that the Board did not violate the Open Meeting 
Law as alleged. We now consider the complaint addressed by this determination to be resolved.  
This determination does not address any other complaints that may be pending with our office or 
the Board.  Please feel free to contact the Division at (617) 963-2540 if you have any questions.
