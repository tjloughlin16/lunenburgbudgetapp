This office received a complaint from Jack Arute, dated November 24, 2017, alleging 
that the Hampden Planning Board (the "Board") violated the Open Meeting Law, G.L. c. 30A, 
§§ 18-25. The complaint was originally filed with the Board on November 11, 2017, and, after 
an extension of time was granted by our office, the Board responded by letter dated December 
15, 2017. In his complaint, Mr. Arute alleges that the Board failed to provide meeting minutes 
within the time frame required by law.1 
Following our review, we find the Board violated the Open Meeting Law as alleged in 
the complaint. In reaching a determination, we reviewed the original complaint, the Board's 
response, and the request for further review filed with our office. We reviewed supplementary 
responses from the Board sent by e-mail on April 12 and 25, 2018. We spoke with the 
complainant by telephone on March 27, 2018, and we spoke with the Board's legal counsel by 
telephone on April 17, 2018.2

DISCUSSION 
Under the Open Meeting Law, a public body must "create and maintain accurate minutes 
of all meetings, including executive sessions, setting forth the date, time and place, the members 
present or absent, a summary of the discussions on each subject, a list of documents and other 
exhibits used at the meeting, the decisions made and the actions taken at each meeting, including 
the record of all votes." G.L. c. 30A, § 22(a). The minutes of an open session, if they exist and 
whether approved or in draft form, must be made available upon request by any person within 10 
days. G.L. c. 30A, § 22(c). 
The complaint alleges that the Board failed to provide the meeting minutes requested in 
the October 23, 2017 letter. As an initial matter, we note that the Open Meeting Law requires a 
public body to make meeting minutes availablQ in response to a request, see G.L. c. 3OA, § 22 
(c), but it does not explicitly require bodies to furnish a requestor with printed copies. Here, the 
Board's administrative assistant directed the complainant to the municipal website, which would 
typically suffice to make meetings minutes available to a requestor. However, the Board's 
response came more than 10 days after the request, in violation of the Open Meeting Law. See 
OML 2017-159.3 Further, the complainant claims that none of the requested meeting minutes 
were on the website at the time he received the Board's letter. The Board provided a 
supplementary response in which the Board's administrator attests to the posting dates 
mentioned above. Where, as here, we have conflicting versions of events, we will credit a public 
body's version of events, absent sufficient evidence to the contrary. See OML 2018-51. 
Accordingly, we credit the posting dates provided to us in the Board's supplementary response, 
and we conclude that the majority of the requested minutes were available on the municipal 
website at the time of the Board's November 9, 2017 response.4

CONCLUSION 
For the reasons stated above, we find that the Board violated the Open Meeting Law. We 
order the Board's immediate and future compliance with the Open Meeting Law, and we caution 
that similar future violations may be considered evidence of intent to violate the law. 
We now consider the complaint addressed by this determination to be resolved. This 
determination does not address any other complaints that may be pending with our office or the 
Board. Please feel free to contact the Division at (617) 963 - 2540 if you have any questions.
