This office received two complaints from Amrith Kumar, dated March 16, 2014, alleging 
that the Sterling Board of Selectmen (the "Board") violated the Open Meeting Law, G.L. c. 30A, 
§§ 18-25. The complaints were originally filed with the Board on or about February 2, 2014 and 
February 13, 2014 and you responded, on behalf of the Board, to the original complaints by letter 
dated March 7, 2014.' In his complaints, Mr. Kumar alleges that the Board's procedure for 
responding to his earlier Open Meeting Law complaints violated the Open Meeting Law. 
Specifically, he alleges that the Board failed to acknowledge several earlier complaints before 
responding and failed to post appropriate notice for a topic of deliberation that the Chair 
reasonably anticipated 48 hours in advance. 
In reaching a determination, we reviewed the February 2, 2014 and February 13, 2014 
complaints filed with the Board, the Board's March 7, 2014 response, and the request for further 
review filed with our office on March 16, 2014.2 Additionally, we reviewed the posted agendas 
for the Board's meetings on January 22, 2014; February 12, 2014; and February 26, 2014. We 
also reviewed the minutes of the Board's February 26, 2014 meeting. 
Following our review, we find that the Board violated the Open Meeting Law by 
deliberating on a topic not included in its posted notice which the Chair reasonably anticipated 
discussing 48 hours in advance, and by failing to follow proper procedure in responding to the 
complainant's January 10, 2014 complaint. We do not find these violations intentional, 
however, and we note that the Board took appropriate action to address these violations in

FACTS 
We find the facts as follows. The allegations present here stem from the procedures the 
Board followed in dealing with a previous set of Open Meeting Law complaints against the 
Board. On January 10, 2014, the complainant filed two complaints alleging that the Board 
violated the Open Meeting Law in several ways. Between January 10, 2014 and January 29, 
2014, the Board met only once, on January 22, 2014. The January 10, 2014 complaints were not 
a subject of discussion at that Board meeting. The Board responded to the January 10, 2014 
complaints by letter dated January 29, 2014. On February 2, 2014, the complainant filed a 
complaint alleging that the Board's failure to acknowledge his January 10, 2014 complaints 
during its January 22, 2014 meeting constituted a violation of the Open Meeting Law. 
The Board next met on February 12, 2014. The agenda posted 48 hours in advance of 
this meeting did not include an item pertaining to the Open Meeting Law complaints of January 
10, 2014 or February 2, 2014. However, at 11:02 A.M. on February 11, 2014, the agenda was 
amended to include: 
"7:45 Open Meeting Law Complaint - January 10, 2014 
Discuss and possible vote to ratify response to complaint by Chair and Town 
Counsel 
At the February 12, 2014 meeting, after deliberation, the Board voted to ratify Town 
Counsel's January 29, 2014 response to the January 10, 2014 complaint. The February 13, 2014 
complaint followed, alleging that the Board had failed to post timely notice of a topic that the 
Chair reasonably anticipated 48 hours in advance. 
In its reply to the February 13, 2014 complaint, the Board admitted that the late 
amendment to the agenda for the February 12, 2014 meeting was to correct an inadvertent 
oversight on the part of the Board, rather than to add a topic that the Chair did not reasonably 
anticipate 48 hours in advance. Additionally, in response to the February 2, 2014 complaint, at 
its February 26, 2014 meeting the Board adopted a policy establishing procedures for responding 
to future Open Meeting Law complaints and also reconsidered and ratified its earlier responses to 
the January 10, 2014 complaints. 
DISCUSSION 
The Open Meeting Law requires that public bodies post notice 48 hours in advance of a 
meeting and include, a "listing of topics that the chair reasonably anticipates will be discussed at 
the meeting." G.L. c. 30A, § 20(b). Public bodies are required to list topics in a meeting notice

Here, the complaints allege that the deliberation on the January 10, 2014 Open Meeting 
Law complaints which took place on February 12, 2014 was anticipated by the Chair 48 hours in 
advance, and should therefore have been placed on the initial notice. The Board has admitted as 
much in its response and, to remedy the situation, "discuss [ed] again and revote[d] the 
ratification of the [January 29, 2014] response at this properly posted meeting [on February 26, 
2014]." We therefore find that while the Board violated the Open Meeting Law by initially 
failing to post notice for an anticipated topic of discussion, the Board took appropriate remedial 
action in response to the complaints. 
The complaints also allege that the Board violated the Open Meeting Law by preparing a 
response to the January 10, 2014 complaints without first acknowledging the complaints during a 
meeting. While a public body may authorize an individual such as the Chair or Town Counsel to 
respond on the public body's behalf, the Open Meeting Law and the Attorney General's 
regulations require that the public body first review the Open Meeting Law complaint. See G.L. 
c. 30A, § 23(b); 940 CMR 29.05(5); OML 2014-55. The Board did not conduct such a review 
prior to its January 29, 2014 response. We note, however, that the policy adopted by the Board 
at its February 26, 2014 meeting "concerning the receipt and response to Open Meeting Law 
Complaints" does address this procedural deficiency going forward. We therefore order no 
further action with regard to this allegation. 
For the reasons stated above, we find that the Board violated the Open Meeting Law by 
deliberating on a topic not included in its posted notice that the Chair reasonably anticipated 
discussing 48 hours in advance, and by failing to review the complainant's January 10, 2014 
complaints before responding to them. We order no further relief because we find the Board 
took appropriate remedial action in response to the complaints. We find no other violations of the 
Open Meeting Law. 
We now consider the complaints addressed by this determination closed. This 
determination does not address any other complaints which may be pending before this office or 
the Board. Please feel free to contact our office at (617) 963-2540 if you have any questions 
regarding this letter.
