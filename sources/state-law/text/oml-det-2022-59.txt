This office received a complaint from Christine Cohen on December 8, 2021, that the 
Hanson Zoning Board of Appeals (the “Board”) violated the Open Meeting Law, G.L. c. 30A, §§ 
18-25.1  The complaint was originally filed with the Board on September 20, and you responded, 
on behalf of the Board, by letter dated October 22, pursuant to an extension of time granted by 
this office. The complaint alleges that the Board failed to follow proper procedure in responding 
to a prior Open Meeting Law complaint.   
 
We resolve this complaint by informal action in acc
 ordance with 940 CMR 29.07(2)(a), 
after reviewing the original complaints, the Board’s response to the complaint, and the request 
for further review filed with our office.  We also reviewed the notice for the Board’s August 31 
meeting, and viewed a video recording of that meeting.
2  We find that the Board did not violate 
the Open Meeting Law. 
 
The Open Meeting Law requires that, unless an extension has been granted by the 
Attor
ney General, within 14 business days of receipt of an Open Meeting Law complaint, a 
public body must meet to review the complaint’s allegations; take remedial action, if appropriate; 
and send to the complainant a response and a description of any remedial action taken. 940 CMR 
29.05(5).  The public body must also send the Attorney General a copy of the complaint and a 
description of any remedial action taken.  Id.  The public body may review a complaint during a 
meeting and refer the complaint to a designee to respond, or a designee may receive and draft a

Here, the Board posted n otice for a meeting to be held on August 31, which included the 
topic “Discuss, Consider and Vote on response to Open Meeting Law Complaint against the 
Board of Appeals dated August 16, 2021 filed by Christine Cohen on file with the Town Clerk.”  
During the August 31 meeting, the Board’s Chair confirmed that the other Board members “had 
a chance to look at” the complaint and the response that town counsel had prepared for the 
Board.  The Board then voted unanimously to approve the response that counsel had drafted.  
This procedure, where counsel prepared a response to the complaint in advance of the meeting 
for the Board’s consideration and approval at the meeting, satisfied the Board’s obligations under 
the Open Meeting Law.  Therefore, we find that the Board did not violate the Open Meeting 
Law. 
 
 We now consider the complaint addressed by this determination to be resolved. This
