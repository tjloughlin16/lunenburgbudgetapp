RE: Open Meeting Law Complaint 
Dear Mr. Bullock: 
This office received a complaint from Mr. Thomas Bratko, dated June 24, 2011, alleging 
a violation of the Open Meeting Law, G.L. c. 30A, §§ 18-25. Mr. Bratko originally filed his 
complaint with the Hubbardston Board of Selectmen (the "Board") on June 6, 2011, alleging that 
the Board "met [on May 16, 2011] to discuss the purchase of a parcel of land on High St listed 
on assessors map 5c lot 48 without posting the address or location on there [sic] agenda." Your 
response to that complaint was received by this office on June 14, 2011. 
The meeting notice for the Board's May 16, 2011 meeting included two topics: "Review 
of Special Town Meeting & Annual Town Meeting Warrant Articles" and "Other New 
Business." The minutes of the May 16, 2011 meeting state that the Board considered 24 
different articles, including Article #11, "to see if the Town will purchase, accept by gift or take 
by eminent domain a parcel of land abutting Town Recreation Land for a price not to exceed 
$60,000.00 contingent on the receipt of funding from Community Preservation Committee 
(CPC)." The Board discussed the article, and according to the minutes, the Finance Committee, 
meeting jointly with the Board, recommended the article (though it is unclear from the minutes 
whether a vote took place). 
The Open Meeting Law requires a public body to post a meeting notice 48 hours in 
advance of a meeting and include, a "listing of topics that the chair reasonably anticipates will be 
discussed at the meeting." G.L. c. 30A, § 20(b). Public bodies are required to list topics in a

meeting notice with "sufficient specificity to reasonably advise the public of the issues to be 
discussed at the meeting." 940 CMR 29.03(1)(b). We generally consider a topic to include 
sufficient specificity when a member of the public could read the topic and understand what the 
nature of the public body's discussion will be. See  OML 2012-34.1  
According to the Board's response to the complaint, the Board convened the meeting on 
May 16, 2011 "to review the Warrant for the upcoming Annual Town Meeting" on June 7, 2011. 
The notice for the Board's May 16, 2011 meeting was, therefore, sufficiently specific because it 
informed the public that the Board would be generally reviewing the warrant for the June 7, 2011 
Town Meeting. While we have said in past determinations that, where a public body anticipates 
discussing specific warrant articles, it must list them in its meeting notice, that was not the case 
here. See OML 2011-7. Furthermore, while we have stated in a past decision that, when a 
public body considers action on a specific piece of real property, the Board must list the location 
of the property in the meeting notice, here the Board was not considering action on a piece of 
property, but rather was considering the Town Meeting warrant as a whole. See OML 2010-2. 
The notice topic "Review of Special Town Meeting & Annual Town Meeting Warrant Articles" 
was, therefore, sufficiently specific to reasonably advise the public of the issues to be discussed 
at the meeting. See 940 CMR 29.03(1)(b). As a best practice, we recommend that when 
considering a Town Meeting warrant generally, as was done here, the Board list on its meeting 
notice where members of the public can find the full text of the specific warrant articles ahead of 
the meeting. 
For the reasons stated above, we find that the Board did not violate the Open Meeting 
Law. We now consider this matter closed. If you have any questions regarding this letter, please 
do not hesitate to contact me. 
cc: Thomas Bratko
