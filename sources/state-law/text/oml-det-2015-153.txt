RE: Open Meeting Law Complaint 
Dear Mr. McCormick: 
This office received a complaint from Ronald Beaty on August 17, alleging that the 
Dennis Board of Selectmen (the "Board") violated the Open Meeting Law, G.L. c. 30A, §§ 18-
25.1 Specifically, the complaint alleges that the Board failed to list "open session" in the notice 
for its July 14 meeting, and failed to provide sufficient detail for an executive session topic in the 
notice for that same meeting. The complaint was originally filed with the Board on July 15. The 
Board responded by letter dated July 28. 
Following our review, we resolve this complaint by informal action in accordance with 
940 CMR 29.07(2)(a), and find that the Board did not violate the Open Meeting Law. As we 
have noted in prior determinations resolving complaints filed by Mr. Beaty, a public body does 
not need to list "open session" in its meeting notice, unless executive session is the only topic for 
discussion. See OML 2015-110; OML 2015-105.2 Here, the July 14 meeting notice included 14 
open session topics. The Board also listed "Call to Order" prior to the executive session topic. 
We therefore find that the Board did not violate the Open Meeting Law. Id. 
Mr. Beaty also alleges that the executive session topic was not sufficiently specific. The 
topic was described in the notice for the Board's July 14 meeting as, "Executive Session: 5:45 
PM. M.G.L Ch. 30A § 21A Reason # 6. To discuss the purchase, exchange, lease or value of 
real estate." A public body must identify in its meeting notice the specific property it intends to 
discuss in executive session under G.L. c. 30A, § 21(a)(6), if doing so would not compromise the 
purpose for which the executive session was called. See OML 2015-97; OML 2014-97; OML 
2012-52. Here, the Board believed that identifying the specific property would have
