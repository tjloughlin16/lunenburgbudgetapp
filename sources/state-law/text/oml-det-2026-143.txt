The Open Meeting Law requires that a public body post notice of every meeting at least 
48 hours prior to the meeting, not including Saturdays, Sundays, and legal holidays. G.L. c. 30A, 
§ 20(b). The notice must include “a listing of topics that the chair reasonably anticipates will be 
discussed at the meeting.” Id. A public body may amend a meeting notice less than 48 hours 
prior to the meeting but may not add additional discussion topics if those topics were reasonably 
anticipated more than 48 hours prior to the meeting. See OML 2024-6; OML 2020-29; OML 
2011-11.6 
 
 On March 24 at 3:46 p.m., the Board posted notice for a meeting to be held on March 26 
at 6:30 p.m. The notice did not include a topic related to a Charter Commission. On March 25 at 
12:25 p.m. the Board amended the notice of the March 26 meeting to remove a topic and add the 
topic “Charter Commission.” During the March 26 meeting, under the topic “Charter 
Commission,” the Town Administrator introduced an informational memo the Town received 
from the Collins Center regarding the creation of town charters and charter commissions. The 
Town Administrator shared some of the guidance received by the Collins Center with the Board. 
The Board discussed the requirements of a charter commission, goals for the makeup of a 
commission, and the best ways of generating interest and informing the public that the Board 
was seeking volunteers for a commission.      
 
 In its response to the complaint, the Board asserted that it complied with the notice 
posting requirements of the Open Meeting Law and that it followed “the proper protocols to 
amend the agenda, to provide the Selectboard with correspondence that was recently received” 
related to charter commissions. In further communications with our office, the Chair explained 
that he had expected the topic “Charter Commission” to be included on the original notice, 
posted on March 24, and when he realized that the topic had not been included, he notified the 
Town Administrator and the notice was thereafter amended on March 25.  
 
By discussing a topic that was anticipated 48 hours or more prior to the March 26 
meeting but that was not included on the notice posted 48 hours prior to the meeting, the Board 
violated the Open Meeting Law. We clarify for the Board that meeting notices must include a list 
of those topics anticipated for discussion 48 hours or more prior to the meeting. Meeting notices 
may be amended within 48 hours of a meeting only to include topics that were not reasonably 
anticipated. We order the Board’s immediate and future compliance with the Open Meeting Law 
and caution that similar future violations may be considered evidence of an intent to violate the 
Law.
