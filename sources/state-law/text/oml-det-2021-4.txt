the Malden Community Preservation Committee (the “Committee”) violated the Open Meeting

We appreciate the patience of the parties during our review. Following our review, we 
find that the Committee violated the Open Meeting Law as alleged.5 In reaching this 
determination, we reviewed the original complaint, the Committee’s response to the complaint, 
and the complainant’s request for further review. We also reviewed a copy of Ms. Sullivan’s 
email requesting several sets of meeting minutes from the Committee and the Committee’s 
approved March 27, 2019, meeting minutes. We also reviewed the meeting minutes and a video 
of the Committee’s April 13 meeting in which the committee reviewed the complaint. Finally, 
we emailed Ms. Sullivan on September 23, 2020, and on several occasions in January 2021.  
DISCUSSION 
1. The Committee failed to approve minutes for a March 27, 2019, meeting in a timely 
manner.  
 The Open Meeting Law requires that a public body “create and maintain accurate 
minutes of all meetings, including executive sessions.” G.L. c. 30A, § 22(a). These meeting 
minutes must be reviewed and approved in a timely manner. G.L. c. 30A, § 22(c). The Open 
Meeting Law itself does not define “timely manner.” However, the Attorney General’s Open 
Meeting Law regulations provide that “timely manner” means “within the next three public body 
meetings or within 30 days, whichever is later, unless the public body can show good cause for 
further delay.” 940 CMR 29.11 (2).  
 On March 27, 2019, the Committee met to conduct a public hearing. The Committee did 
not approve meeting minutes for the March 27, 2019, meeting until February 11, 2020, over 10 
months and more than a dozen meetings later. Therefore, we find that the Committee violated the 
Open Meeting Law by failing to approve minutes for its March 27, 2019, meeting in a timely 
manner. 
2. The Committee approved minutes for its March 27, 2019, meeting which were 
inaccurate. 
 The Open Meeting Law requires public bodies to “create and maintain accurate minutes 
of all meetings, including executive sessions, setting forth the date, time and place, the members 
present or absent, a summary of the discussions on each subject, a list of documents and other 
exhibits used at the meeting, the decisions made and the actions taken at each meeting, including 
the record of all votes.” G.L. c. 30A, § 22(a). When reviewing minutes for compliance with the

Open Meeting Law, we look for substantial compliance with the accuracy requirement. See 
OML 2013-64. By substantial compliance, we mean that the minutes should contain enough 
detail and accuracy so that a member of the public who did not attend the meeting could read the 
minutes and have a clear understanding of what occurred. See OML 2019-167. While minutes 
must include a summary of the discussion on each topic, a transcript is not required, and the 
minutes do not need to include every remark or opinion presented. See OML 2018-74. 
 The complaint alleges that the Committee approved meeting minutes of its March 27, 
2019, meeting which were inaccurate. The Committee admits that the attendance list was 
inaccurate in two ways. The original minutes listed Member Molis as attending when in fact he 
did not and Member Tse’s presence was not noted. We therefore find a violation of the Open 
Meeting Law regarding the list of members present and absent. The Committee, in response to 
the instant complaint, voted on April 13 to correct these inaccuracies and add two public 
comments based on member’s recollections of the hearing. As these changes illustrate, the timely 
approval of meeting minutes is essential to ensuring the accuracy of those minutes. 
 In addition, the complaint alleges that the minutes failed to include certain statements 
made by members of the public during the public comment period. As there is no recording of 
the meeting, we are unable to independently ascertain who spoke or what was said during public 
comment. Even if we were able to determine what took place during the public comment portion 
of the March 27, 2019, meeting, a public body is not required to capture every comment made 
during public comment in order to substantially comply with the accuracy requirement of the 
Open Meeting Law. See OML 2019-136. We therefore find no violation regarding the public 
comment portion of the minutes.  
CONCLUSION 
 For the reasons stated above, we find that the Committee 1) failed to approve meeting 
minutes for a March 27, 2019, meeting in a timely manner; and 2) approved meeting minutes for 
its March 27, 2019, meeting which were inaccurate. We order the Committee’s immediate and 
future compliance with the Open Meeting Law and caution the Committee that a determination 
by our Office of a similar violation in the future may be considered evidence of intent to violate 
the Open Meeting Law. As the Committee has already corrected the attendance list contained in 
the minutes of the March 27, 2019, meeting, we order no further relief.  
 We now consider the complaint addressed by this determination to be resolved. This 
determination does not address any other complaints that may be pending with our Office or the 
Committee. Please feel free to contact the Division at (617) 963 - 2540 if you have any 
questions.
