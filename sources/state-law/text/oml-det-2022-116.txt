 RE: Open Meeting Law Complaint 
Dear Mr. Maihos: 
This office received a complaint from Ron Smoller on February 22, 2022, alleging that 
the Peabody Municipal Lighting Commission (the “Commission”) violated the Open Meeting 
Law, G.L. c. 30A, §§ 18-25. The complaint was originally filed with the Commission on January 
19, and you responded on behalf of the Commission by email dated February 23.1, 2 In his 
complaint, Mr. Smoller alleges that the Commission met on January 11 without posting notice 
48 hours in advance. 
Following our review, we find that the Commission violated the Open Meeting Law as 
alleged. In reaching this determination, we reviewed the original complaint, the Commission’s 
response to the complaint, and the complainant’s request for further review. We also reviewed 
the minutes of the Commission’s January 11 meeting.  
DISCUSSION 
 The Open Meeting Law requires that “[e]xcept in an emergency, in addition to any notice 
otherwise required by law, a public body shall post notice of every meeting at least 48 hours 
prior to such meeting excluding Saturdays, Sundays and legal holidays.” G.L. c. 30A, § 20(b). 
The Open Meeting Law defines an emergency as “a sudden, generally unexpected occurrence or 
set of circumstances demanding immediate action.” G.L. c. 30A, § 18; 940 CMR 29.02. The

burden of justifying the need for an emergency meeting falls on the public body. See OML 2020-
123.3 Emergency meetings are reserved for circumstances that are unanticipated and require an 
immediate response, that is, action that could not wait for notice of a meeting to be posted 48 
hours in advance. See OML 2021-119; OML 2017-131; OML 2016-70; OML 2011-45.  
 Here, the Commission concedes that, although copies of the notice for the January 11 
meeting were sent to the Peabody and Lynnfield clerks for posting on January 7, the notices were 
not posted until January 10 due to a snowstorm which reduced staff availability to complete the 
posting of notices. The Commission does not argue that the January 11 meeting met the criteria 
for an emergency meeting, nor do we find that the budget discussion the Commission held on 
January 11 would have qualified as an emergency, as that discussion was not unanticipated. 
Therefore, we find that the Commission violated the Open Meeting Law by failing to post notice 
48 hours in advance of the January 11 meeting.  
CONCLUSION 
 For the reasons stated above, the Commission violated the Open Meeting Law by failing 
to post notice 48 hours in advance for a January 11 meeting. We order the Commission’s 
immediate and future compliance with the Open Meeting Law and caution the Commission that 
a determination by our office of a similar violation in the future may be considered evidence of 
intent to violate the Open Meeting Law.  
 Furthermore, we order all members of the Commission to attend an Open Meeting Law 
training webinar provided by our office within ninety (90) days of the Commission’s receipt of 
this letter.4  
 We now consider the complaint addressed by this determination to be resolved. This 
determination does not address any other complaints that may be pending with our office or the 
Commission. Please feel free to contact the Division at (617) 963 - 2540 if you have any 
questions.
