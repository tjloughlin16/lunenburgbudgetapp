RE: Open Meeting Law Complaints
Dear Attorney Murray:
This office received five complaints from George Harris on July 27; August 1; October 
30; February 5, 2019; and March 7, 2019, alleging that the Wayland Zoning Board of Appeals 
(the “Board”) violated the Open Meeting Law, G.L. c. 30A, §§ 18-25.1 The first complaint was 
originally filed with the Board on or about May 21, and you responded, on behalf of the Board, 
by letter dated June 14, following an extension of time granted by our office. The second 
complaint was originally filed with the Board on or about June 4, and you responded, on behalf 
of the Board, by letter dated June 14. The third complaint was originally filed with the Board on 
or about July 23, and you responded, on behalf of the Board, by letter dated August 24, 
following an extension of time granted by our office. The fourth complaint was originally filed 
with the Board on or about December 10, and you responded, on behalf of the Board, by letter 
dated December 26. The fifth complaint was originally filed with the Board on or about January 
3, 2019, and you responded, on behalf of the Board, by letter dated January 17, 2019. The first 
three complaints allege that the Board failed to timely approve minutes of meetings held between 
December 20, 2017, and June 19. The first, fourth and fifth complaints allege that the 
notices for the Board’s May 8, November 15, and December 11 meetings lacked sufficient detail.
Following our review, we find that the Board violated the Open Meeting Law by failing 
to timely approve meeting minutes. We find that the Board did not violate the Open Meeting 
Law by failing to include sufficient detail in its meeting notices. In reaching this determination, 
we reviewed the original complaints, the Board’s responses to the complaints, and the 
complaints filed with our office requesting further review. We also reviewed the notices and 
open session minutes of the Board’s May 8, June 12, June 19, August 21, November 15, and 
December 11 meetings.

FACTS
We find the facts as follows. The Board duly posted notices for, and held meetings on, 
December 20, 2017; January 23; January 25; January 29; February 13; February 27; March 20; 
March 29; April 10; April 24; April 25; May 8; May 22; June 12; June 19; June 26; June 28; July 
11; July 17; July 24; and August 21.
One topic on the May 8 notice was “Review/Accept Minutes.” During this meeting, the 
Board approved the minutes of its December 20, 2017 meeting.
One topic on the June 12 notice was “Review/Accept Minutes, including but not limited 
to, 11/7/17, 1/25/18, 1/29/18, 2/27/18, 3/20/18, 3/29/18, 4/10/18, 4/24/18.” During this meeting, 
the Board approved the minutes of its November 7, 2017; January 25; January 29; February 27; 
March 20; March 29; April 10; April 24; and April 25 meetings.
One topic on the June 19 notice was “Review/Accept Minutes, including but not limited 
to, 5/8/18 & 5/22/18.” During this meeting, the Board approved the minutes of its May 8 
meeting.
The notices for Board meetings held on June 26, June 28, July 11, July 17, and July 24 
included review and acceptance of minutes as a topic but did not identify specific meeting dates. 
The Board did not discuss or approve any minutes during these meetings.
One topic on the August 21 notice was “Review/Accept Minutes 5/22/18, 6/12/18, 
6/19/18, 6/26/18, 6/28/18, 7/11/18, 7/24/18.” During this meeting, the Board approved the 
minutes of its May 22, June 12, June 19, June 26, June 28, July 11, and July 24 meetings.
The notices for Board meetings held on November 15 and December 11 included review 
and acceptance of minutes as a topic but did not identify specific meeting dates.2 During the 
November 15 meeting, the Board approved the minutes of its October 16 and October 23 
meetings. During the December 11 meeting, the Board approved the minutes of its November 
15, November 27, and December 4 meetings.
DISCUSSION
I. The Board Violated the Open Meeting Law by Failing to Timely Approve
Meeting Minutes.
The Open Meeting Law requires that a public body “create and maintain accurate 
minutes of all meetings, including executive sessions, setting forth the date, time and place, the 
members present or absent, a summary of the discussions on each subject, a list of documents 
and other exhibits used at the meeting, the decisions made and the actions taken at each meeting, 
including the record of all votes.” G.L. c. 30A, § 22(a). The meeting minutes must be reviewed

and approved in a timely manner. G.L. c. 30A, § 22(c). “Timely manner” means “within the 
next three public body meetings or within 30 days, whichever is later, unless the public body can 
show good cause for further delay.” See 940 CMR 29.11; OML 2018-48.3 Whenever possible, 
we recommend that minutes of a meeting be approved at the next meeting. See OML 2018-67; 
OML 2017-133.
The complaints allege that the Board failed to timely approve 11 sets of open session 
minutes. Specifically, the complaints contend that the minutes of Board meetings held on 
December 20, 2017; January 25; January 29; February 27; March 20; March 29; April 10; April 
24; May 22; June 12; and June 19 were not approved in a timely manner because they were not 
approved either within the next three public body meetings or 30 days. Here, the Board posted 
notices for, and held meetings on, December 20, 2017; January 23; January 25; January 29; 
February 13; February 27; March 20; March 29; April 10; April 24; April 25; May 8; May 22; 
June 12; June 19; June 26; June 28; July 11; July 17; July 24.
The Board was required to approve the minutes of its December 20, 2017; January 25, 
January 29, February 27, March 20, and May 22 meetings within the next three public body 
meetings as that was the later of the two time periods. See 940 CMR 29.11; OML 2018-111. 
Since the Board did not approve its December 20, 2017, minutes until May 8, its eleventh 
meeting thereafter; did not approve its January 25 minutes until June 12, its eleventh meeting 
thereafter; did not approve its January 29 minutes until June 12, its tenth meeting thereafter; did 
not approve its February 27 minutes until June 12, its eighth meeting thereafter; did not approve 
its March 20 minutes until June 12, its seventh meeting thereafter; and did not approve its May 
22 minutes until August 21, its eighth meeting thereafter, we find that the Board violated the 
Open Meeting Law by failing to approve these minutes in a timely manner.
The Board was required to approve the minutes of its March 29, April 10, April 24, June 
12 and June 19 meetings within 30 days as that was the later of the two time periods for these 
minutes because the Board met at least three times within 30 days following these meetings. See 
940 CMR 29.11; OML 2018-152. Since the Board did not approve its March 29 minutes until 
75 days thereafter; did not approve its April 10 minutes until 63 days thereafter; did not approve 
its April 24 minutes until 49 days thereafter; did not approve its June 12 minutes until 70 days 
thereafter; and did not approve its June 19 minutes until 63 days thereafter, we find that the 
Board violated the Open Meeting Law by also failing to approve these minutes in a timely 
manner.
The Board suggests that it had good cause for delay because of a staffing shortage and the 
frequency and complexity of the meetings. We understand the demands on a public body with 
limited staff; however, the Board is still obligated to timely approve minutes. See OML 2018- 
111; OML 2017-115. The responsibility for complying with the Open Meeting Law rests with 
public bodies and members of such bodies, not with individual public employees, such as 
administrative staff. See OML 2018-105. If staff resources are not available to create minutes 
for the Board to approve, then Board members must take on this responsibility to ensure 
compliance with the law. See OML 2017-115.

We must determine whether the violation was intentional. See G.L. c. 30A, § 23(c). An 
intentional violation is an “act or omission by a public body or a member thereof, in knowing 
violation of [the Open Meeting Law].” 940 CMR 29.02. An intentional violation may be found 
where the public body acted with deliberate ignorance of the law’s requirement or has previously 
been advised that certain conduct violates the Open Meeting Law. Id. In a prior determination, 
OML 2017-71, the Board was advised that the Open Meeting Law requires that minutes be 
approved in a timely manner. However, the guidance provided to the Board at that time relied 
on the previous version of the Open Meeting Law Regulations which did not define timely 
manner. The revised regulations, which took effect on October 17, 2017, defined “timely 
manner” for the first time. Because the Board was advised of its obligations under the old 
standard, rather than under the more specific current standard, we decline to find an intentional 
violation of the law here.
II. The Board Did Not Violate the Open Meeting Law by Discussing Topics Not
Listed on its Meeting Notice.
The Open Meeting Law requires a public body to post notice of a meeting at least 48 
hours in advance, including a “listing of topics that the chair reasonably anticipates will be 
discussed at the meeting.” G.L. c. 30A, § 20(b). Public bodies are required to list topics on a 
notice with “sufficient specificity to reasonably advise the public of the issues to be discussed at 
the meeting.” 940 CMR 29.03(l)(b); OML 2014-47. We generally consider a topic sufficiently 
specific when a reasonable member of the public could read the topic and understand the 
anticipated nature of the public body’s discussion. See OML 2015-35.
The complaints allege that the May 8, November 15, and December 11 meeting notices 
should have identified the specific meeting minutes to be reviewed and approved. While we 
agree that notices should generally include this detail, the Board contends that the chair did not 
anticipate which specific meeting minutes would be available for review during those meetings. 
Here, the specific minutes that would be reviewed during these three meetings were not known 
to the Board chair until either the day before or the day of the meeting. Because the chair did not 
anticipate which minutes would be ready for review until the meeting, the generic topic,
“Review/Accept Minutes,” was sufficient in this instance and did not violate the Open Meeting 
Law. See OML 2016-103; OML 2015-25. We note that when specific meeting minutes were 
anticipated for review, the Board identified them on its meeting notices.
CONCLUSION
We find that the Board violated the Open Meeting Law by failing to approve minutes in a 
timely manner. We order immediate and future compliance with the law’s requirements, and we 
caution that similar future violations could be considered evidence of intent to violate the law.
We now consider the complaints addressed by this determination to be resolved. This 
determination does not address any other complaints that may be pending with our office or the 
Board. Please feel free to contact our office at (617) 963-2540 if you have any questions 
regarding this letter.
