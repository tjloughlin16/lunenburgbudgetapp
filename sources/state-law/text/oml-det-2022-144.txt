This office received a complaint from Patrick Higgins on May 12, 2022,
1 alleging that 
the Somerset Advisory & Finance Committee (the “Committee”) violated the Open Meeting 
Law, G.L. c. 30A, §§ 18-25. The complaint was originally filed with the Committee on April 12, 
and you responded on behalf of the Committee by letter dated May 6. The complaint alleges that 
the Committee failed to timely approve minutes for open sessions held on February 14 and 
March 16, and executive sessions held on March 9 and March 14.   
 
Following our review, we resolve this complaint by informal action in accordance with 
940 CMR 29.07(2)(a) and find that the Committee violated the Open Meeting Law as alleged in 
part. In reaching this determination we reviewed the original Open Meeting Law complaint; the 
Committee’s response; the request for further review; the notices for Committee meetings held 
on March 16, March 23, March 30, April 6, and April 11; and the Somerset Town Calendar.
2  
 
The Open Meeting Law requires that a public body “create and maintain accurate 
minutes of all meetings, including executive sessions, setting forth the date, time and place, the 
members present or absent, a summary of the discussions on each subject, a list of documents 
and other exhibits used at the meeting, the decisions made and the actions taken at each meeting, 
including the record of all votes.” G.L. c. 30A, § 22(a). Meeting minutes for both open and 
executive sessions must be created and approved in a timely manner. G.L. c. 30A, § 22(c); 940 
CMR 29.11(2). “Timely manner” means “within the next three public body meetings or within 
30 days, whichever is later, unless the public body can show good cause for further delay.” 940

Executive Session Minutes 
 
The complaint alleges that the Committee failed to timely approve minutes for executive 
sessions held on March 9 and March 14. The Committee has explained that it did not hold 
executive sessions on those dates and therefore there are no minutes to approve. After review of 
the Town’s website, which shows no notices posted for meetings of the Committee on those 
dates, whether for open or executive session, and absent sufficient evidence to the contrary, we 
credit the Committee’s account and find no violation.  
 
Minutes of the Committee’s March 16 Meeting 
 
The Committee held a meeting on March 16. The Committee approved the minutes of the 
Marc
h 16 meeting at its April 11 meeting. As explained above, meeting minutes must be 
approved “within the next three public body meetings or within 30 days, whichever is later. . . .” 
940 CMR 29.11 (emphasis added). Because the Committee approved the March 16 meeting 
minutes within 30 days, we find no violation.  
 
Minut
es of the Committee’s February 14 Meeting 
 
The Committee held a meeting on February 14. The Committee then met jointly with the 
Somerset Board of Selectmen (the “Board”) on March 16, March 23, March 30, and April 6. The 
Committee approved the minutes of the February 14 meeting at its April 11 meeting. The April 
11 meeting was the fifth meeting and 56 days after the February 14 meeting; therefore, we find 
that the Committee violated the Open Meeting Law by failing to timely approve the minutes for 
its February 14 meeting.  
 
The Committee acknowledges that the minutes of the February 14 meeting were not 
approved w
ithin 30 days and that it met on March 16, March 23, March 30, and April 6. 
However, the Committee asserts that these meetings should not count for the purpose of 
calculating timely approval of the February 14 minutes because these meetings were held jointly 
with the Board. We disagree. The Open Meeting Law defines “meeting” as “a deliberation by a 
public body with respect to any matter within the body’s jurisdiction.”  G.L. c. 30A, § 18. The 
Open Meeting Law does not include an exception to the definition of “meeting” for meetings 
that are held jointly by two or more public bodies. Furthermore, we have consistently rejected 
the argument that various types of meetings do not count toward timely approval of meeting 
minutes. See OML 2021-177, n. 4 (finding a public body’s joint meeting with other town public 
bodies to be meetings of the public body for the purpose of calculating timely approval of 
meeting minutes); OML 2019-115 (no exception for “single topic” meetings or meetings not 
“normally scheduled”); OML 2021-169.

Alternatively, the Committee asserts that it had good cause for delay in timely approving 
the minutes of the February 14 meeting due to its workload in preparing the FY23 budget and 
Annual Town Meeting warrant. Specifically, the Committee asserts that it met weekly during 
this period and that it was required to “review and pass upon each warrant article involving a 
financial obligation” and to advise on the budget. We find that the Committee has not established 
good cause for delay in approving the minutes of its February 14 meeting. In general, a public 
body’s workload does not constitute good cause for delay in approving meeting minutes. See 
OML 2018-83; OML 2017-61.  
 
Finally, to the extent the complaint asserts that t
 he violation was intentional, we disagree. 
An intentional violation is an “act or omission by a public body or a member thereof, in knowing 
violation of [the Open Meeting Law].” 940 CMR 29.02. An intentional violation may be found 
where the public body acted with deliberate ignorance of the law’s requirements or has 
previously been advised that certain conduct violates the Open Meeting Law. Id. Our office has 
not previously found the Committee in violation for failing to timely approve meeting minutes. 
Furthermore, we find that the Committee sincerely, but mistakenly, believed that its joint 
meetings with the Board did not count for the purpose of calculating timely approval of meeting 
minutes. We therefore decline to find that the violation was intentional. 
 
For the
 reasons stated above, we find that the Committee violated the Open Meeting Law 
by failing to timely approve the minutes of its February 14 meeting. We order the Committee’s 
immediate and future compliance with the Open Meeting Law, and caution that similar future 
violations could be considered evidence of intent to violate the Law. We now consider the 
complaint addressed by this determination to be resolved. This determination does not address 
any other complaints that may be pending with our office or the Committee. Please feel free to 
contact the Division at (617) 963-2540 if you have any questions.
