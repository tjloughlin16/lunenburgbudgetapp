RE: Open Meeting Law Complaint 
Dear Attorney Riley: 
This office received a complaint from Timothy Toomey, dated January 22, 2018, alleging 
that the Rowley Board of Selectmen and the Rowley Board of Water Commissioners 
(collectively "the Boards") violated the Open Meeting Law, G.L. c. 30A, §§ 18-25. The 
complaint was originally filed on December 1, 2017, and the two public bodies responded by a 
joint letter dated December 12, 2017. In the complaint, Mr. Toomey alleges that he was 
unlawfully directed to file an Open Meeting Law complaint in order to obtain the Boards' 
executive session meeting minutes.1 
Following our review, we resolve this complaint by informal action in accordance with 
940 CMR 29.07(2)(a), and we find that the Boards did not violate the Open Meeting Law. On 
some date prior to November 28, 2017, Mr. Toomey sent two requests to the Rowley Town 
Clerk, who is also the town's records access officer. These requests were for the minutes of joint 
meetings held by the Boards in executive session on June 28, 2017 and September 18, 2017. On 
November 28, 2017, the clerk responded stating that, while requests for many public records 
could be made by contacting her, requests for meeting minutes should be directed to the relevant 
public body or bodies. The present complaint followed on December 1, 2017. In the complaint, 
Mr. Toomey characterizes the clerk's response as stating that "there should have been [an] OML 
complaint instead of the records request." This is not an accurate representation of the clerk's 
November 28, 2017 letter. It appears that Mr. Toomey did not make any requests to the Boards 
prior to filing the present complaint. Because Mr. Toomey did not request the minutes from the
