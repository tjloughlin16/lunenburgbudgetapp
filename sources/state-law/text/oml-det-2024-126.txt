 RE: Open Meeting Law Complaint 
 
Dear Attorney Greenspan: 
 
This office received a complaint from Eleanor Axelrod on November 20, 2023, alleging 
that the Northeast Metropolitan Regional Vocational School Building Committee (the 
“Committee”) violated the Open Meeting Law, G.L. c. 30A, §§ 18-25.  The complaint was 
originally filed with the Committee on or about October 19, 2023, and you responded to the 
complaint, on behalf of the Committee, by letter dated December 11, 2023.
1  The complaint 
alleges that the Committee deliberated outside of a properly posted meeting.  
 
Following our review, we find that the Committee did not violate the Open Meeting Law 
in the way alleged.  In reaching this determination, we reviewed the original complaint, the 
Committee’s response to the complaint, and the complaint filed with our office requesting further 
review.  In addition, we reviewed the notices and minutes of Committee meetings held on 
November 29, 2018, January 10, 2019, May 9, 2019, and May 24, 2023, as well as PowerPoint 
presentations made during Committee meetings held on September 28, 2023, and December 7, 
2023. 
 
The Open Meeting Law was enacted “to eliminate much of the secrecy surrounding 
deli
beration and decisions on which public policy is based.”  Ghiglione v. School Board of 
Southbridge, 376 Mass. 70, 72 (1978).  The Open Meeting Law requires that meetings of a

public body be properly noticed and open to members of the public, unless an executive session

onvened.  See G.L. c. 30A, §§ 20(a)-(b), 21.  A “meeting” is defined, in relevant part, as “a 
deliberation by a public body with respect to any matter within the body’s jurisdiction.”  G.L. c. 
30A, § 18.  The law defines “deliberation” as “an oral or written communication through any 
medium, including electronic mail, between or among a quorum of a public body on any public 
business within its jurisdiction.”  Id.  For the purposes of the Open Meeting Law, a “quorum” is 
a simple majority of the members of a public body.  Id.  
 
The Committee is a 21-member public body; thus, 11 members constitute a quorum.  The 
Superintendent of the Northeast Metropolitan Regional Vocational School District is a member 
of the Committee.  The Committee has met approximately 31 times between November 29, 
2018, and December 7, 2023.  During a meeting held on May 9, 2019, the Committee created a 
Selection Committee which was responsible for choosing an Owner’s Project Manager; the 
Project Manager would oversee the entire new school building operation.  The Committee then 
unanimously voted to authorize the Superintendent to execute and deliver the Feasibility Study 
Agreement, and any amendments thereto, as well as to make final, binding decisions, on behalf 
of the Northeast Metropolitan Regional Vocational School District with respect to the proposed 
school building project. 
 
The complaint alleges that the Committee decided certain matters regarding the new 
school
 building outside of a properly posted meeting.  Specifically, the complaint alleges that 
after a Committee meeting held on May 24, 2023, but before the Committee’s next meeting on 
September 28, 2023, “several consequential decisions” were made, and therefore must “have 
been made out of full view of the public and without the involvement of the” Committee.  We 
will not conduct broad audits of a public body based on generalized allegations.  See OML 2016-
57.  Nonetheless, the Committee asserts that any decisions or authorizations were made by the 
Superintendent in accordance with the Committee’s vote during the May 19, 2019, meeting.  
Even though the Superintendent is a member of the Committee, we find no evidence that he 
communicated with a quorum of Committee members outside of a posted meeting about the new 
school building project.  As such, we find no deliberation because there was no communication 
“between or among a quorum” of the Committee.  See OML 2022-45. 
 
To the extent that the complaint may be construed as alleging that the Superintendent 
effectively made a decision that should have been made by the Committee as a whole, this 
allegation goes to the authority of individuals such as the Superintendent versus the Committee 
to take action on particular matters, which is not a matter governed by the Open Meeting Law.  
See OML 2018-144.   
 
Finally, with respect to the concerns raised in the complaint that the Committee did not 
provide documents or answer questions, the Open Meeting Law governs requests for meeting 
minutes only.  See G.L. c. 30A, § 22.  Requests for other documents are governed by the Public 
Records Law.  See G.L. c. 66, § 10.  Therefore, failure to provide documents other than meeting 
minutes concerns a potential violation of the Public Records Law, but does not violate the Open 
Meeting Law.  See OML 2015-17; OML Declination 7-16-20 (Greater Lowell Technical School 
Committee); OML Declination 3-11-15 (Wayland School Committee); OML Declination 8-9-13
