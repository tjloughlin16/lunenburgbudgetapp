 RE: Open Meeting Law Complaint 
 
Dear Chair Cronin: 
 
This office received a complaint from Amy Troup on October 12, 2021,1 alleging that the 
Plympton Board of Health (the “Board”) violated the Open Meeting Law, G.L. c. 30A, §§ 18-25. 
The complaint was originally filed with the Board on July 8, and you responded, on behalf of the 
Board by letter dated July 27. The complaint alleges that the Board failed to provide the 
Complainant with individual notice that she would be discussed during the Board’s June 8 
meeting and that the Board voted to send a letter to the Board of Licensure concerning the 
Complainant.2    
 
Following our review, we resolve this complaint by informal action in accordance with 
940 CMR 29.07(1) and find that the Board did not violate the Open Meeting Law. In reaching 
this determination, we reviewed the original complaint, the Board’s response, the request for 
further review,3 and the notice and minutes for the Board’s June 8 meeting.

The complaint alleges that the Board failed to provide the Complainant with individual 
notice of its June 8 meeting at which the Board discussed the Complainant. The Open Meeting 
Law does not require that a public body provide specific notice to individuals it may discuss 
during a meeting, unless its discussion will occur during executive session pursuant to G.L. c. 
30A, § 21(a)(1). See, e.g., OML 2017-75.4 Rather, the Open Meeting Law requires that public 
bodies post notice 48 hours prior to a meeting—excluding Saturdays, Sundays, and legal 
holidays—and that such notice include, among other things, a list of the topics the chair 
reasonably anticipates will be discussed at the meeting. G.L. c. 30A, § 20(b). Here, the Board 
timely posted notice of its June 8 meeting and the meeting was held entirely in open session. 
Therefore, we find that the Board was not required to provide the Complainant with individual 
notice of the June 8 meeting and did not violate the Open Meeting Law.   
Finally, although not raised in the complaint, we note that the minutes for the June 8 
meeting are insufficiently detailed. The Open Meeting Law requires that a public body “create 
and maintain accurate minutes of all meetings, including executive sessions, setting forth the 
date, time and place, the members present or absent, a summary of the discussions on each 
subject, a list of documents and other exhibits used at the meeting, the decisions made and the 
actions taken at each meeting, including the record of all votes.” G.L. c. 30A, § 22(a). When 
reviewing minutes for compliance with the Open Meeting Law, we look for substantial 
compliance with the accuracy requirement. See OML 2016-105; OML 2013-64. By substantial 
compliance, we mean that the minutes should contain enough detail and accuracy so that a 
member of the public who did not attend the meeting could read the minutes and have a clear 
understanding of what occurred. See OML 2012-106. A statement that a discussion on a 
particular topic took place, without summarizing the discussion, is insufficient. See OML 2020-
149; OML 2020-55; OML 2012-29. Here, the minutes are essentially a spreadsheet of the topics 
discussed and include almost no detail summarizing the discussions held, decisions made, or 
actions taken. We encourage the Board to amend the minutes of its June 8 meeting so that they 
comply with the requirements of the Open Meeting Law.   
 For the reasons stated above, we find that the Board did not violate the Open Meeting 
Law as alleged. We now consider the complaint addressed by this determination to be resolved. 
This determination does not address any other complaints that may be pending with our office or 
the Board. Please feel free to contact the Division at (617) 963-2540 if you have any questions.
