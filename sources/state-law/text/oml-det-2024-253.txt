Following our review, we find that the Commission violated the Open Meeting Law by 
creating insufficient amended minutes for its November 30, 2023, executive session meeting and 
by failing to respond to the First Complaint and Second Complaint. Additionally, we find the 
Commission’s failure to respond to the First Complaint and Second Complaint to constitute 
intentional violations. In reaching this determination, we reviewed the Open Meeting Law 
complaints, communications between our office and the Conservation Director3 and the Chair of 
the Commission, the requests for further review, Open Meeting Law determination OML 2024-
94,4 and the original and amended minutes of the Commission’s November 30, 2023, executive 
session.  
 
Sufficiency of Amended Minutes 
 
First, we address the sufficiency of the amended minutes of the Commission’s November 
30, 2023, executive session. On May 10, 2024, our office issued Open Meeting Law 
determination OML 2024-94, which resolved three Open Meeting Law complaints, filed by the 
Complainant, alleging that the Commission had violated the Open Meeting Law.
5 Relevant here, 
the complaints resolved by OML 2024-94 alleged that the minutes of the Commission’s 
November 30, 2023, executive session were insufficiently detailed. In OML 2024-94 we found 
that the minutes of the November 30, 2023, executive session were insufficient, explaining that 
“[t]he minutes substantively record the Commission’s approximately hourlong discussion in less 
than half a page,” and noting that “[a]lthough the minutes identify some topics that were 
discussed, they fail to sufficiently summarize the discussions such that an individual who did not 
attend the meeting could read the minutes and have a clear understanding of what occurred.” 
OML 2024-94. We ordered the Commission to amend the minutes of its November 30, 2023, 
executive session to include a substantive summary of the discussions that were held, and to 
release the amended minutes to the public subject to any appropriate redactions. Thereafter, the 
Commission amended the minutes of the November 30, 2023, executive session and released the 
amended minutes to the public. Following the release of the amended minutes, the Complainant 
filed the First Complaint alleging that the amended minutes are insufficient. 
 
After reviewing the original minutes of the November 30, 2023, executive session and 
the amended minutes, we find that the amended minutes are insufficient in the same way as the 
original minutes because they continue to fail to substantively summarize the discussions that 
occurred. Indeed, the substantive portion of the amended minutes is shorter than the substantive 
portion of the original minutes and the amended minutes include fewer details than the original 
minutes. For example, in the original minutes there was mention of a comment about the Boy 
Scouts and Eagle Scouts. Rather than include additional detail that would have clarified what 
was discussed with respect to the Boy Scouts and Eagle Scouts, the amended minutes simply 
remove this reference altogether. Additionally, the original minutes included a discussion 
regarding an Open Meeting Law complaint the Commission received that evening; the amended

Failure to Respond to Complaints 
 
Next, we address the Commission’s failure to review and respond to the Open Meeting 
Law c
omplaints at issue here within 14 business days, as is required under the Open Meeting 
Law. See G.L. c. 30A, § 23(b); 940 CMR 29.05. The First Complaint and Second Complaint 
were filed with the Commission on or about July 18 and August 8, 2024, respectively. The 
Commission did not respond to these complaints, as alleged in the Second Complaint and Third 
Complaint, and we therefore find that the Commission violated the Open Meeting Law. See G.L. 
c. 30A, § 23(b); 940 CMR 29.05; OML 2024-94.6 Furthermore, for the reasons discussed below, 
we find these violations to be intentional, as the Complainant alleges. See G.L. c. 30A, § 23(c). 
 
An intentional violation is an “act or omission by a public body or a member thereof, in 
knowing vi
olation of [the Open Meeting Law].” 940 CMR 29.02. An intentional violation may 
be found where the public body acted with deliberate ignorance of the Law’s requirement or has 
previously been advised that certain conduct violates the Open Meeting Law. Id. Evidence of an 
intentional violation includes that a public body was previously advised by the Attorney General 
that the conduct violates the Open Meeting Law. See, e.g., OML 2017-109 (repeated failure to 
respond to complaints within 14 days constitutes intentional violation); OML 2017-159 (repeated 
failure to respond to requests for executive session minutes constitutes intentional violation); 
OML 2019-114 (violation intentional where Board was warned that it must include identity of 
parties about whom it is conducting strategy discussions). 
 
In OM
L 2024-94, issued on May 10, 2024, we advised the Commission that “in 2024 our 
office has issued five determinations that address complaints that the Commission either did not 
respond to at all or did not respond to within the timeframe set forth in the Open Meeting Law.” 
We further noted that the Commission also did not properly respond to the complaints that were 
the subject of OML 2024-94. After explaining the Commission’s obligations under the Law to 
respond to Open Meeting Law complaints, we emphasized that “[t]he Commission’s continued 
failure to comply with the requirement to review and respond to Open Meeting Law complaints 
within 14 business days is concerning and is itself a violation of the Law.” OML 2024-94. 
Finally, we warned the Commission that “a future finding of a violation for failure to timely 
respond to an Open Meeting Law complaint will be considered an intentional violation and may 
result in the imposition of a $1,000 civil penalty per intentional violation.” OML 2024-94.

Despite the clear warning given in OML 2024-94, issued on May 10, 2024, the 
Commission failed to respond to the First Complaint and the Second Complaint, both of which 
were filed with the Commission within the four months following issuance of OML 2024-94. In 
light of the Commission’s clear disregard for our previous warnings and guidance regarding its 
obligation to respond to Open Meeting Law complaints, we find the Commission’s failure to 
respond to the First Complaint and Second Complaint to constitute intentional violations of the 
Open Meeting Law.
7 
 
For the reasons stated above, we find that the Commission violated the Open Meeting 
Law by creating insufficient amended minutes for its November 30, 2023, executive session 
meeting and by failing to respond to the First Complaint and Second Complaint. We find the 
latter violations to be intentional. We order the Commission’s immediate and future compliance 
with the Open Meeting Law.  
 
We additionally order the Commission, within 30 days of the date of this letter, to amend 
the minutes of its November 30, 2023, executive session to substantively and accurately 
summarize the discussions held on each topic and to certify to our office that it has done so. 
Finally, we refer this matter for a hearing pursuant to 940 CMR 29.07(3) and recommend that the 
Commission be assessed a fine of $200 per intentional violation, for a total of $400, pursuant to 
G.L. c. 30A, § 23(c) (“Upon the finding of a violation, the attorney general may issue an order to 
. . . impose a civil penalty upon the public body of not more than $1,000 for each intentional 
violation.”). We invite the Commission to contact this office to discuss the hearing process or a 
resolution of this matter. 
 
This determination does not address any other complaints that may be pending with the 
Commission or with our office. Please feel free to contact our office at (617) 963-2540 if you 
have any questions regarding this letter.
