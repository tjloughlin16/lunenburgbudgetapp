RE: Open Meeting Law Complaints
Dear Ms. Green:
This office received two complaints from George Harris on December 11, 2019, alleging 
that the Wayland Personnel Board (the “Board”) violated the Open Meeting Law, G.L. c. 30A,
§§ 18-25. The first complaint was originally filed with the Board on June 24, 2019, and the 
second complaint was originally filed with the Board on July 16, 2019. You responded to both 
complaints, on behalf of the Board, by separate letters dated November 12, 2019, following an 
extension of time granted by our office and mediation.1 The complaints allege that the Board 
failed to timely approve minutes of meetings held on July 17, 2018; October 30, 2018; December 
13, 2018; January 10, 2019; and February 25, 2019.2
We resolve these matters by informal action, in accordance with 940 CMR 29.07(2)(a), 
after reviewing the original complaints, the Board’s responses to the complaints, the complaints 
filed with our office requesting further review, and the notices and open session minutes of the 
Board’s December 13, 2018; January 10, 2019; July 30, 2019; and August 28, 2019, meetings. 
We find that the Board violated the Open Meeting Law by failing to timely approve minutes 
from its July 17, 2018; January 10, 2019; and February 25, 2019, meetings. We find that the 
Board timely approved minutes from its October 30, 2018, and December 13, 2018, meetings.
The Open Meeting Law requires that a public body “create and maintain accurate 
minutes of all meetings, including executive sessions, setting forth the date, time and place, the 
members present or absent, a summary of the discussions on each subject, a list of documents

and other exhibits used at the meeting, the decisions made and the actions taken at each meeting, 
including the record of all votes.” G.L. c. 30A, § 22(a). The meeting minutes must be reviewed 
and approved in a timely manner. G.L. c. 30A, § 22(c). “Timely manner” means “within the 
next three public body meetings or within 30 days, whichever is later, unless the public body can 
show good cause for further delay.” See 940 CMR 29.11; OML 2018-48.3 Whenever possible, 
we recommend that minutes of a meeting be approved at the next meeting. See OML 2018-67; 
OML 2017-133.
The Board was required to approve the minutes of its July 17, 2018; October 30, 2018; 
December 13, 2018; January 10, 2019; and February 25, 2019, meetings within the next three 
public body meetings as that was the later of the two time periods. See 940 CMR 29.11; OML 
2018-111. Since the Board did not approve its July 17, 2018, minutes until July 30, 2019, its 
tenth meeting thereafter; did not approve its January 10, 2019, minutes until August 28, 2019, its 
sixth meeting thereafter; and did not approve its February 25, 2019, minutes until August 28, 
2019, its fifth meeting thereafter, we find that the Board violated the Open Meeting Law by 
failing to approve these minutes in a timely manner.
The Board approved its October 30, 2018, minutes at its next meeting on December 13,
2018, and approved its December 13, 2018, minutes at its following meeting on January 10,
2019. During both of those meetings, the Board “moved to approve the minutes ... as 
amended” and then unanimously approved each set of minutes. The complaint suggests that 
these minutes “were approved as amended by voice vote, but the corrected draft minutes were 
not reduced to writing” and therefore not “created.” We disagree. The Open Meeting Law 
requires that minutes be created and approved. See G.L. c. 30A, § 22(c); 940 CMR 29.11. Here, 
the Board reviewed draft minutes of the October and December meetings, discussed amendments 
to be made, and then voted to approve the minutes “as amended.” We have not required that a 
public body subsequently review the amended minutes, when it has already identified the 
amendments to be made and voted to approve the minutes with those amendments. Therefore, 
we find that the Board approved its October 30, 2018, and December 13, 2018, meeting minutes 
in a timely manner and thus did not violate the Open Meeting Law. We note that, in response to 
the complaints, the Board approved its October 30, 2018, and December 13, 2018, amended 
minutes during its July 30, 2019, meeting.
We remind the Board that the responsibility for complying with the Open Meeting Law 
rests with public bodies and members of such bodies, not with individual public employees, such 
as administrative staff. See OML 2018-105. We understand the demands on a public body with 
limited staff; however, the Board is still obligated to timely approve minutes. See OML 2018- 
111; OML 2017-115. If staff resources are not available to create minutes for the Board to 
approve, then Board members must take on this responsibility to ensure compliance with the law. 
See OML 2017-115.
For the reasons stated above, we find that the Board violated the Open Meeting Law by 
failing to approve its July 17, 2018; January 10, 2019; and February 25, 2019, minutes in a
