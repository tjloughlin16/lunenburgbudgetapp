 This office received a complaint from Christopher Kelleher on July 3, 2025, alleging that 
the Brookfield Cable Advisory Committee (the “Committee”) violated the Open Meeting Law, 
G.L. c. 30A, §§ 18-25. The complaint was originally filed with the Committee on April 21, 2025, 
and you responded to the complaint, on behalf of the Committee, by letter dated June 12, 2025, 
following an extension of time granted by this office. The complaint alleges that the Committee 
posted a notice with insufficient specificity for a meeting held on March 26, 2025, and failed to 
announce that the March 26, 2025, meeting was being recorded. 
 
 Following our review, we find that the Committee violated the Open Meeting Law in the 
ways alleged. In reaching this determination, we reviewed the original complaint, the 
Committee’s response to the complaint, the complaint filed with our office requesting further 
review, and the notice of the Committee’s March 26, 2025, meeting.  
 
The Open Meeting Law requires that public bodies post notice of every meeting “at least 
48 hours prior to such meeting, excluding Saturdays, Sundays and legal holidays” and that every 
notice include “the date, time and place of such meeting and a listing of topics that the chair 
reasonably anticipates will be discussed at the meeting.” G.L. c. 30A, § 20(b). A public body is 
required to list topics on a notice with “sufficient specificity to reasonably advise the public of 
the issues to be discussed at the meeting.” 940 CMR 29.03(1)(b). We generally consider a topic

sufficiently specific when a reasonable member of the public could read the topic and understand 
the anticipated nature of the public body’s discussion. See OML 2015-35.1  
 
Here, the Committee duly pos ted notice for a meeting to be held on March 26, 2025, at 
7:00 P.M. The notice listed the following eight topics: call to order, introduce Jacob, Contract 
draft #1, recommend edits to go to Charter for Draft #2, Public Hearing dates, Spencer Cable 
Access Channel discussion, survey information, and OML complaint filed January 6, 2025. The 
complaint alleges that the topic “survey information” was insufficiently specific. Generally, 
when reviewing a meeting notice for sufficiency, unless it is clearly insufficient on its face, we 
do not review a meeting notice standing alone. See OML 2015-109; OML 2014-155. Rather, we 
review what was actually discussed at the meeting to determine if the notice was sufficiently 
specific to make the public aware of the discussion topics. We reached out to the Committee on 
September 24, October 10, and October 15, 2025, requesting the minutes of the March 26, 2025, 
meeting but were not provided with the minutes.

A public body need not list each and every detail about a topic, nor anticipate on the 
not
ice the course of deliberation or the outcome of a discussion. See OML 2022-98; OML 2020- 
132; OML 2019-134. However, notice topics must be described with sufficient specificity to 
reasonably advise the public of the issues to be discussed at the meeting so that the public can 
make an informed decision about whether to attend the meeting in order to observe an 
anticipated discussion. See OML 2023-86; OML 2019-95; OML 2018-22; OML 2015-74. The 
Committee maintains that the notice included sufficient specificity because the Committee “has a 
very narrow charge” strictly related to cable television topics and therefore any survey must be 
“a cable television related survey.” We disagree. Although the topic, as listed, may have been 
meaningful to Committee members and regular Committee meeting attendees, we find that it was 
insufficient to inform members of the public unfamiliar with the regular matters before the 
Committee of the anticipated nature of the Committee’s discussions. See OML 2024-32; OML 
2020-164; OML 2013-55. As such, we find that the Committee posted a notice with an 
insufficiently specific topic for the March 26, 2025, meeting. 
 
In a
ddition, the Committee acknowledges, and we find, that the Committee violated the 
Open Meeting Law when the Chair did not announce at the beginning of the March 26, 2025, 
meeting that the meeting was being recorded. The Open Meeting Law requires that “after 
notifying the chair of the public body, any person may make a video or audio recording of an 
open session of a meeting of a public body, or may transmit the meeting through any medium, 
subject to reasonable requirements of the chair as to the number, placement and operation of 
equipment used so as not to interfere with the conduct of the meeting. At the beginning of the

For the
 reasons stated above, we find that the Committee violated the Open Meeting Law 
by posting a notice with insufficient specificity for the March 26, 2025, meeting, and failing to 
announce that the meeting was being recorded. We order the Committee’s immediate and future 
compliance with the Open Meeting Law, and caution that future similar violations may be 
considered evidence of intent to violate the Law. 
 
We now consider the complaint addressed by this determination to be resolved. This 
determination does not address any other complaints that may be pending with our office or the
