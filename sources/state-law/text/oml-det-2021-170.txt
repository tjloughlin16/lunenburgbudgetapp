 RE: Open Meeting Law Complaint 
Dear Attorney Corbo: 
This office received a complaint from Judith Ahern on September 21, 2021, alleging that 
the Wellfleet Select Board (the “Board”) violated the Open Meeting Law, G.L. c. 30A, §§ 18-25. 
The complaint was originally filed with the Board on or around July 8, and you responded on 
behalf of the Board by letter dated July 28.
1 In her complaint, Ms. Ahern alleges that the Board 
posted an insufficient notice for its June 22 meeting because the notice incorrectly identified an 
aquaculture license to be discussed as “01-02 Extension” rather than merely “01-02,” and the file 
number “01-02 Extension” already referred to a different matter. 
Following our review, we resolve this complaint by informal action in accordance with 
940 CMR 29.07(2)(a) and find that the Board did not violate the Open Meeting Law.  In 
reaching this determination, we reviewed the complaint filed with the Board, the Board’s 
response, and the complaint filed with our office for review, as well as the notice and meeting 
packet for the Board’s June 22 meeting. 
On June 17, the Board duly posted notice for a meeting to be held on June 22.  Relevant 
here, the notice included the heading “III. Public Hearings” and beneath that the topic “A. 
Aquaculture Grant Extension for Shea Murphy—Shellfish Constable, Nancy Civetta.”  Along 
with the two-page meeting notice, the Board also posted an 89-page meeting packet, which 
included additional information and documents pertaining to the topics listed on the meeting 
notice.  The packet included an “Agenda Action Request” form which identified a proposed 
motion to “approve approximately 1.8 acres as grant extensions 01-02 Extension 2007 – B and

2007 – C for Shea Murphy.”  The packet also included a letter in support from the Shellfish 
Constable, which similarly referred to an application for a shellfish grant license extension from 
Shea Murphy as “01-02 Ext.”  
The Open Meeting Law requires that public bodies post notice 48 hours in advance of a 
meeting and include a “listing of topics that the chair reasonably anticipates will be discussed at 
the meeting.”  G.L. c. 30A, § 20(b).  Public bodies are required to list topics in a meeting notice 
with “sufficient specificity to reasonably advise the public of the issues to be discussed at the 
meeting.”  940 CMR 29.03(l)(b).  We generally consider a topic to include sufficient specificity 
when a reasonable member of the public could read the topic and understand the anticipated 
nature of the public body's discussion.  See OML 2018-7; OML 2015-35.
 2  The law does not 
require the meeting notice to list all issues tangential to the topic.  See OML 2016-120.  
Furthermore, even for public bodies that use a website as their official notice posting location 
and therefore may post supporting documentation alongside the meeting notice, a meeting 
notices should include on the notice itself the level of specificity that the Open Meeting Law 
requires; we do not review the contents of the meeting packet. See OML 2020-31 (finding that 
the Wellfleet Select Board violated the Open Meeting Law where required information was 
contained within a meeting packet but was not included on the notice itself); OML 2020-21. 
Here, we find that the notice for the Board’s June 22 meeting was sufficiently specific, 
and accurate, where it described the topic to be discussed as “Aquaculture Grant Extension for 
Shea Murphy—Shellfish Constable, Nancy Civetta.”  Therefore, we find that the Board did not 
violate the Open Meeting Law.  We do not review whether additional materials contained within 
a meeting packet may have included an inaccurate or unclear application number. 
 We now consider the complaint addressed by this determination to be resolved.  This 
determination does not address any other complaints that may be pending with our office or the 
Board.  Please feel free to contact the Division at (617) 963 - 2540 if you have any questions.
