FACTS 
 
We find the facts as follows.  During a meeting of the Mohawk Trail Regional School 
Committee (the “Committee”) held on August 10, 2022, the Committee discussed the assessed 
cost for each student in the Town of Heath who attends the Mohawk Trail Regional School 
District (the “District”).  The Business Administrator reported that the Town of Heath owes the 
District $44,000.  After discussing the efforts made by the District to resolve the assessment cost 
matter, one Committee member stated that if the District needs to file a lawsuit, he would “be the 
person to make the motion.”   
 
On October 12, 2022, Special Town Legal Counsel Robert Kerwin sent an email to 
Attorney Russell Dupere, legal counsel for the District, requesting clarification with respect to 
the District’s student cost assessment to the Town of Heath.  By email dated October 17, 2022, 
Attorney Dupere explained that the regional agreement “provides for the assessment 
methodology” and if the District cannot resolve the assessment issue with the Town of Heath, the 
District plans “on filing suit to recover the monies.” 
 
On October 28, 2022, at 1:37 P.M., the Board posted notice of a meeting to be held on 
November 2, 2022, at 6:00 P.M.  The notice listed an executive session pursuant to Purpose 3 
“[t]o discuss strategy with respect to collective bargaining or litigation if an open meeting may 
have a detrimental effect on the bargaining or litigating position of the public body and the chair 
so declares.  For deliberation and strategy involving dispute over the operating assessment 
process between the Mohawk Trail Regional School District and the Town of Heath.” 
 
The November 2, 2022, meeting was held as planned and the Board convened in open 
session.  Board member Susan Lively than moved to enter executive session to “discuss strategy 
with respect to collective bargaining or litigation and the chairman declared that an open meeting 
might have a detrimental effect on the bargaining or litigating position of the public body, for 
deliberation and strategy involving dispute over the operating assessment process between the 
Mohawk Trail Regional School District and the Town of Heath.”  The Board approved a 
unanimous vote by roll call to convene in executive session and announced that it would not 
return to open session.  The Board has not publicly released the minutes of the executive session; 
therefore, we do not recount their content in detail here.  However, after our in camera review of 
the minutes, we note that the Board discussed strategy with respect to potential litigation against 
the Town of Heath for its decision to question and withhold payment of the student cost 
assessment fees.   
 
The Board held a meeting on November 29, 2022.  During the meeting, the Board 
allowed a public comment period and heard from two individuals, one of whom was the 
complainant.  The minutes summarize the complainant’s comments as follows: “Jesse Wiegand 
said he had called the Attorney General’s office and spoke to an attorney about the Open

DISCUSSION 
 
I. We Decline to Review the Complaint Alleging that the Board Created 
Insufficiently Detailed Meeting Minutes as It was not Timely Filed.   
 
The second complaint alleges that the Board created insufficiently detailed minutes of the 
meeting held on November 29, 2022.
  Complaints alleging violations of the Open Meeting Law 
must be filed with the public body within 30 days of the alleged violation.  G.L. c. 30A, § 23(b).  
If the alleged violation could not reasonably have been known at the time it occurred, then the 
complaint must be filed within 30 days of the date it should reasonably have been discovered.  
940 CMR 29.05(3).  The complainant asserts that the sufficiency of the minutes was not 
discoverable until the minutes were posted to the municipal website on January 3, 2023.  We 
disagree.  The Open Meeting Law does not require that public bodies post their minutes to a 
website or that minutes be submitted to the Town Clerk, but merely that meeting minutes be 
made available within 10 days of a request.  See G.L. c. 30A, § 22(c); OML 2013-103.2  Hence, 
complaints regarding the sufficiency or accuracy of open session minutes must be filed within 30 
days of when the approved minutes could have been reviewed by a member of the public.  See 
OML 2015-26; OML Declination 5-14-12 (Sheffield Planning Board).  Here, the minutes of the 
November 29, 2022, meeting were approved on December 13, 2022, and could have been 
requested at that time.  Because the complaint here was filed on February 2, 2023, we find the 
complaint to be untimely and decline to review it.  See OML Declination 10-21-20 
(Massachusetts Maritime Academy Board of Trustees); OML Declination 7-2-18 (Pembroke 
Planning Board); OML Declination 1-13-14 (Ashfield Select Board).   
 
II. We Find that the Board Convened in Executive Session for a Proper Purpose. 
 
The first complaint challenges the propriety of the Board’s November 2, 2022, executive 
session discussions.  The Open Meeting Law was enacted “to eliminate much of the secrecy 
surrounding deliberations and decisions on which public policy is based.”  Ghiglione v. School 
Committee of Southbridge, 376 Mass. 70, 72 (1978).  The Law requires that all meetings of a 
public body be conducted in an open session, unless an executive session is convened.  G.L. c. 
30A, §§ 20(a), 21(a).  A public body may enter an executive, or closed, session for any of the ten 
purposes enumerated in the Open Meeting Law provided that it has first convened in an open 
session stating all subjects that may be revealed without compromising the purpose for which the 
executive session was called, that a majority of members of the body have voted to go into 
executive session, that the vote of each member is recorded by roll call and entered into the 
minutes, and the chair has publicly announced whether the open session will reconvene at the 
conclusion of the executive session.  G.L. c. 30A, §§ 21(a), (b); see also OML 2014-94.

One permissible reason to convene in executive session is “to discuss strategy with 
respect to collective bargaining or litigation if an open meeting may have a detrimental effect on 
the bargaining or litigating position of the public body and the chair so declares.”  G.L. c. 30A, § 
21(a)(3) (“Purpose 3”).  This purpose offers the narrow opportunity to discuss strategy with 
respect to litigation that is pending or clearly and imminently threatened or otherwise 
demonstrably likely; the mere possibility of litigation is not sufficient to invoke Purpose 3.  See 
Doherty v. School Committee of Boston, 386 Mass. 643, 648 (1982); Perryman v. School 
Committee of Boston, 17 Mass. App. Ct. 346, 352 (1983); OML 2012-05.  When convening in 
executive session pursuant to Purpose 3, a public body should identify the litigation matter to be 
discussed, if doing so will not compromise the lawful purpose for secrecy.  See OML 2016-12; 
OML 2013-97.   
 
During the meeting held on November 2, 2022, the Board announced in open session that 
it would convene in executive session pursuant to Purpose 3 and announced the specific matter it 
would discuss, namely the “dispute over the operating assessment process between the Mohawk 
Trail Regional School District and the Town of Heath.”  The Board argues that its discussions in 
executive session were proper under Purpose 3 because the Town “had received multiple direct 
threats of litigation that were reasonably likely to lead to litigation.”  Looking at all that 
transpired prior to the November 2, 2022, meeting, it was reasonable for the Board and its legal 
counsel to view litigation in response to the Town’s ongoing dispute of the student cost 
assessment fees as clearly and imminently threatened and to discuss the potential litigation 
consequences of that dispute.
3  See OML 2012-5 (concluding that a public body’s executive 
session discussion was proper where the public body’s attorney advised the public body about 
the potential litigation consequence of its decision because, in the attorney’s judgment, a real 
threat of litigation existed).  In addition, after reviewing the executive session minutes, we are 
satisfied that the discussions here were limited to matters appropriate for executive session under 
Purpose 3, that is matters of litigation strategy.  As such, we find that the Board properly met in 
executive session on November 2, 2022.  We remind the Board that a public body’s discussions 
with its counsel do not automatically fall under Purpose 3 or any other executive session 
purpose.  See Plymouth Dist. Atty v. Selectmen of Middleborough, 395 Mass. 629 (1985); OML

CONCLUSION 
 
We find that the Board did not violate the Open Meeting Law by meeting in executive 
session on November 2, 2022, to discuss litigation strategy.  We now consider the complaints 
addressed by this determination to be resolved.  This determination does not address any other 
complaints that may be pending with our office or the Board.  Please feel free to contact our 
office at (617) 963-2540 if you have any questions regarding this letter.
