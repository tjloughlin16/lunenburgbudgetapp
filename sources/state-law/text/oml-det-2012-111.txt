RE: Open Meeting Law Complaint 
Dear Attorney Lane: 
This office reviewed two complaints from Mr. Christian Schiavone, on behalf of the 
Patriot Ledger, dated December 20, 2011, alleging violations of the Open Meeting Law, G.L. c. 
30A, §§ 18-25, by the Weymouth School Committee ("School Committee") and the Weymouth 
Superintendent Search Committee ("Search Committee"). The first complaint alleges that during 
a September 8, 2011 School Committee meeting, the School Committee chair moved to consider 
a topic that had not been listed in the School Committee's meeting notice, although he 
reasonably anticipated discussing the topic more than 48 hours before the meeting. The 
complaint additionally alleges that some members of the School Committee may have known 
about or discussed the topic outside of a posted meeting. The second complaint alleges that the 
Search Committee failed to keep sufficient minutes of its meetings, specifically for its August 2, 
2011 and August 3, 2011 meetings. The first complaint was filed with the School Committee on 
September 15, 2011, and the School Committee responded to the complaint by letter dated 
December 12, 2011. The second complaint was filed with the.  Search Committee on September 
14, 2011. The Search Committee responded to the complaint in a letter dated December 7, 2011. 
With respect to the first complaint, we find that the School Committee did not violate the 
Open Meeting Law because the Chair did not reasonably anticipate discussing the topic more 
than 48 hours in advance of the School Committee's September 8, 2011 meeting. In reaching 
this determination, we reviewed the September 15, 2011 complaint; the School Committee's 
December 12, 2011 response; and the December 20, 2011 complaint. We reviewed the notice 
and minutes of the School Committee's September 8, 2011 meeting, and the minutes of the 
School Committee's December 8, 2011 meeting. Finally, we spoke with School Committee

FACTS 
Weymouth School Committee 
The School Committee met in open session on August 25, 2011. During that meeting, 
School Committee member Gerald Murphy made public comments regarding the superintendent 
search process. About one hour before the School Committee's meeting on September 8, 2011, 
School Committee Chair Sean Guilfoyle spoke with Mr. Murphy by telephone and asked him to 
apologize for the comments he made at the August 25, 2011 meeting. Mr. Murphy did not 
apologize. 
The School Committee then convened its September 8, 2011 meeting. At the end of 
regular business, under a section titled "New Business," Chair Guilfoyle turned the chair over to 
another member of the School Committee, and then read the following motion: "We, as members 
of the Weymouth School Committee, go on record disassociating the Committee from remarks 
made by a member about the veracity of another member at the meeting of August 25, 2011 and, 
that such remarks are ill advised, lack civility and adversely impact the decorum, efficiency and 
mission of the Weymouth School Committee." The Motion passed without discussion, with Mr. 
Murphy casting the lone dissenting vote. Chair Guilfoyle explained to our office during a 
telephone interview that he did not anticipate discussing the motion at the September 8, 2011 
until after he had spoken with Mr. Murphy an hour prior to the meeting. 
Chair Guilfoyle asserts that he did not speak to any other member of the School 
Committee regarding his motion prior to the September 8, 2011 meeting. He acknowledges that 
several School Committee members spoke with him individually about Mr. Murphy's August 25, 
2011 comments following the meeting, but he had no conversations regarding a specific course 
of action nor developed a plan to respond. Chair Guilfoyle explained to our office that he first 
considered drafting his motion on September 8, 2011, and began writing it midday. He decided 
to introduce the motion after speaking to Mr. Murphy over the telephone about an hour before 
the meeting. 
Weymouth Superintendent Search Committee 
The Search Committee consisted of 11 members who met on June 27, 2011; July 25, 
2011; August 2, 2011 and August 3, 2011. The complaint specifically alleges that the minutes 
from the Search Committee's August 2, 2011 and August 3, 2011 meetings failed to meet the 
Open Meeting Law's requirements. The draft minutes for the August 2, 2011 meeting list the 
members who were present, and state: "Each member picked out 2 questions each to be asked

DISCUSSION 
The Open Meeting Law requires that meetings of a public body be properly noticed and 
open to members of the public, unless an executive session is convened. See G.L. c. 30A, §§ 
20(a)—(b), 21. A meeting notice must be posted 48 hours before a meeting, not including 
Saturdays, Sundays and legal holidays, and "shall contain the date, time and place of such 
meeting and a listing of topics that the chair reasonably anticipates will be discussed at the 
meeting." G.L. c. 30A, §20(b). 
A public body subject to the Open Meeting Law must "create and maintain accurate 
minutes of all meetings, including executive sessions, setting forth the date, time and place, the 
members present or absent, a summary of the discussions on each subject, a list of documents 
and other exhibits used at the meeting, the decisions made and the actions taken at each meeting, 
including the record of all votes." G.L. c. 30A, § 22(a). Minutes must be "created and approved 
in a timely manner." G.L. c. 30A, § 22(c). 
1. The School Committee did Not Violate the Open Meeting Law by Considering a 
Motion that Had Not Been Listed in the Notice for its September 8, 2011 Meeting.  
The first complaint alleges that during a September 8, 2011 School Committee meeting, 
the School Committee Chair moved to consider a topic that had not been listed in the School 
Committee's meeting notice, although he reasonably anticipated discussing the topic more than 
48 hours before the meeting. A meeting notice must be posted 48 hours before a meeting and 
contain "the date, time and place of such meeting and a listing of topics that the chair reasonably 
anticipates will be discussed at the meeting." G.L. c. 30A, § 20(b). Here, the Chair spoke with a 
School Committee member an hour before the School Committee's September 8, 2011 meeting, 
seeking an apology. After that conversation, he decided to introduce a motion under the "New 
Business" section of the meeting. The Chair did not reasonably anticipate this motion 48 hours 
before the meeting, and therefore it was not unlawful to consider the motion, even though it had 
not been listed in a meeting notice. However, our office continues to encourage public bodies to 
postpone to a subsequent meeting consideration of any topic that was not listed in a meeting 
notice so that members of the public may be given advance notice of agenda items. 
We also find no evidence that Chair Guilfoyle discussed the motion with other members

2. The Search Committee did Not Violate the Open Meeting Law by Creating 
Insufficiently Detailed Draft Minutes.  
The second complaint alleges that the Search Committee failed to keep sufficient minutes 
for its August 2, 2011 and August 3, 2011 meetings. The draft minutes for the Search 
Committee's August 2, 2011 and August 3, 2011 meeting minutes do not satisfy the Open 
Meeting Law's requirements for complete minutes. However, we note that these are draft 
minutes, and had not been approved as the final minutes by the Search Committee at the time the 
complaint was filed. Therefore, we cannot find a violation of the Open Meeting Law. The draft 
minutes in their current form are insufficient, and the Search Committee must amend them 
before approving them in order to comply with the law. Minutes must include a "summary of 
the discussions on each subject, a list of documents and other exhibits used at the meeting, the 
decisions made and the actions taken at each meeting, including the record of all votes." G.L. c. 
30A, §22(a). These draft minutes fail to include any summary of the questions posed to the 
candidates or the answers the candidates provided in response. For the August 3, 2011 meeting, 
the minutes fail to provide any summary of the discussion that led the Search Committee to 
choose a final candidate to recommend to the School Committee.' We expect that these minutes 
will be completed with detailed summaries before their final approval. 
We also note that minutes must be "created and approved in a timely manner." G.L. c. 
30A, 22(c). The meetings took place in early August 2011. The minutes of these meetings 
should be amended and approved by the Search Committee, or if it has been disbanded, its parent 
public body, the School Committee. 
CONCLUSION 
We find that neither the School Committee nor the Search Committee violated the Open 
Meeting Law. We remind the School Committee that a best practice is to postpone consideration 
of items that were not listed in a meeting notice. We remind the Search Committee that meeting 
minutes must include a detailed summary of the decision of each topic, and that final minutes 
must be created and approved in a timely manner. The minutes of the Search Committee's 
August 2, 2011 and August 3, 2011 meetings should be reviewed, amended, and approved in 
their final form within thirty (30) days of receipt of this letter, if they have not already been 
We note that the August 2, 2011 and August 3, 2011 meetings occurred in open session. A preliminary screening 
committee may not meet in executive session to choose a single finalist. A preliminary screening committee 
meeting in executive session must choose multiple finalists, who will then be reviewed in open session, either by the 
preliminary screening committee or the parent public body. See G.L. c. 30A, § 21(a)(8); Gerstein v.  
Superintendent Search Screening Committee, 405 Mass. 465, 471-73 (1989). Here, the Search Committee chose a 
single finalist after interviewing candidates in open session.
