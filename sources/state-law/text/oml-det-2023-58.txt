 RE: Open Meeting Law Complaint 
 
Dear Chair Carvalho: 
 
This office received a complaint from Patrick Higgins on October 7, 2022,
1 alleging that 
the Swansea Board of Health (the “Board”) violated the Open Meeting Law, G.L. c. 30A, §§ 18-
25. The complaint was originally filed with the Board on or about October 3, and the Board 
responded on October 7. The complaint alleges that after initially responding to a request for 
meeting minutes explaining that a set of minutes did not yet exist, the Board failed to later 
provide those minutes once they did exist.   
 
Following our review, we find that the Board did not violate the Open Meeting Law. In 
reaching this determination we reviewed the original Open Meeting Law complaint, the Board’s 
response, and the request for further review. We also reviewed the original request for meeting 
minutes and the response thereto.  
 
On September 14 the Complainant sent a request for meeting minutes via email to the 
Board. Specifically, the Complainant requested “copies of all minutes of the Swansea Board of 
Health meetings held in 2022, including September 14, 2022 . . . .” The Town’s Health Agent 
responded to the request by email on September 15 providing several sets of minutes and 
explaining that the request for the September 14 minutes could not be “completed . . . as the 
meeting was last night and the minutes do not exist yet.”  
 
On October 3, the Complainant filed the present complaint alleging that, based on the 
notice for the Board’s October 5 meeting, it was clear that the September 14 meeting minutes 
had since been drafted but had not been provided to the Complainant. In response to the 
complaint, the Board provided a copy of the September 14 meeting minutes to the Complainant.

The Open Meeting Law requires that “[m]inutes of an open session, if they exist and 
whether approved or in draft form, shall be made available upon request by any person within 10 
days.” G.L. c. 30A, § 22(c) (emphasis added). Under the Open Meeting Law, a public body has 
10 calendar days from the date a request is received to provide a response. A public body is only 
required to produce meeting minutes, whether in draft or approved form, which exist at the time 
of the request. See OML 2022-77; OML 2021-139; OML 2021-134; OML 2018-117.
2 If minutes 
do not exist at the time of a request, the public body is still required to respond to the request 
within 10 calendar days and must explain that the minutes do not exist in either approved or draft 
form. See OML 2021-139; OML 2019-65. 
 
The complaint asserts that the Board had an ongoing obligation to provide the September 
14 meeting minutes to the Complainant within 10 days of the minutes’ creation. We disagree. 
The Board had an obligation to respond to the September 14 request for minutes within 10 days 
by providing any minutes that existed, whether in draft or final form, and explaining whether any 
requested minutes did not exist. That is exactly what the Board did.  
 
For the reasons stated above, we find that Board did not violate the Open Meeting Law. 
We now consider the complaint addressed by this determination to be resolved. This 
determination does not address any other complaints that may be pending with our office or the 
Board. Please feel free to contact the Division at (617) 963-2540 if you have any questions.
