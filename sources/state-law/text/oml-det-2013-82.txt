Our office received two complaints filed by Todd Wallack, on behalf of the Boston 
Globe, dated January 23, 2013 and February 25, 2013, respectively, alleging that the Board of 
Registration in Pharmacy (the "Board") violated the Open Meeting Law, G.L. c. 30A, §§ 18-

originally filed with the Board on December 19, 2012 and January 4, 2013, and your  
responded on behalf of the Board to the complaints on or about January 10 and February 12, 
2013, respectively.2  
We appreciate the patience and cooperation of the parties while we reviewed this 
matter. Following our review, we find that that the Board's executive session discussions on 
December 20, 2012 and January 8, 2013 were appropriate under the Open Meeting Law; 
however, however we find that the Board violated the Open Meeting Law by failing to list a 
topic with sufficient specificity in its meeting notice and by failing to follow required 
procedures for entering into executive session. Nevertheless, we find that the Board took 
appropriate remedial action to correct those errors.

DISCUSSION 
The Open Meeting Law was enacted "to eliminate much of the secrecy surrounding 
deliberations and decisions on which public policy is based." Ghiglione v. School Committee  
of Southbridge,  376 Mass. 70, 72 (1978). The law therefore requires that meetings of a public 
body be properly noticed and open to members of the public, unless an executive session is 
convened. See G.L. c. 30A, §§ 20(a)—(b), 21. A public body may enter executive—or 
closed—session for any of ten purposes. G.L. c. 30A, § 21(a). 
i. 	 The Board's Discussion Regarding Open Meeting Law Complaints Was Appropriate 
for Executive Session Under Purpose 1 of the Open Meeting Law.  
One permissible reason for executive session is Purpose 1, "[t]o discuss the reputation, 
character, physical condition or mental health, rather than professional competence, of an 
individual, or to discuss the discipline or dismissal of, or complaints or charges brought 
against, a public officer, employee, staff member or individual." G.L. c. 30A, § 21(a)(1). Our 
office has stated in past determinations that public bodies may meet in executive session 
under Purpose 1 to review Open Meeting Law complaints against the body. OML 2012-119; 
OML 2011-6. The Board relied upon our guidance in OML 2011-6 when deciding to enter 
executive session on December 20, 2012 and January 8, 2013 to discuss the Open Meeting 
Law complaints filed against the Board. 
The complainant asserts that Purpose 1 is narrowly drawn to permit discussion of a 
complaint against an individual, only, and not against a Board as a whole. However, we have 
determined that such a complaint is by its nature "brought against, a public officer," namely 
the members of the public body. OML 2012-119. Purpose 1 does not limit the number of 
individuals against whom a complaint may be filed and/or discussed in executive session; 
rather, it permits a public body to discuss such a complaint behind closed doors provided it 
affords certain rights to the individual or individuals. Here, a majority of the Board voted to 
enter into executive session to discuss the complaints and was present for the closed door 
discussion. Accordingly, we fmd that the Board did not violate the Open Meeting Law by 
entering into executive session to discuss the Open Meeting Law complaints.3

ii. The Board Failed to Comply With Notice Posting Requirements under the Open 
Meeting Law.  
The Open Meeting Law requires that public bodies post notice 48 hours in advance of 
a meeting and include "the date, time and place of such meeting and a listing of topics that the 
chair reasonably anticipates will be discussed at the meeting." G.L. c. 30A, § 20(b). Public 
bodies must list topics in a meeting notice with "sufficient specificity to reasonably advise the 
public of the issues to be discussed at the meeting." 940 CMR 29.03(1)(b). We generally 
consider a topic to include sufficient specificity when a reasonable member of the public 
could read the topic and understand the anticipated nature of the public body's discussion. 
See OML 2011-44. 
The original notice for the December 20, 2012 meeting should have included the name 
of the person who filed the Open Meeting Law complaint, if doing so would not have 
compromised the purpose for the executive session. See 940 CMR 29.03(1)(b); OML 2011-
54. However, the Board recognized this deficiency before the meeting occurred and took 
appropriate remedial steps to correct the violation by revising its agenda to include additional 
details about the complaints, including the complainant's name. Moreover, the Board appears 
to have learned from its error, as it properly posted notice of its January 8, 2013 meeting, 
including the requisite detail concerning its anticipated executive session to discuss an Open 
Meeting Law complaint. 
iii. The Board Failed to Comply with Certain Procedural Requirements Before Convening 
in Executive Session.  
Although not raised by the complainant, we note that during its December 20, 2012 
meeting, the Board failed to follow certain procedural steps required before convening an 
executive session under the Open Meeting Law. A public body may enter executive, or 
closed, session provided that it has first convened in an open session, that a majority of 
members of the body have voted to go into executive session, and that the vote of each 
member is recorded by roll call and entered into the minutes. G.L. c. 30A, § 21(b). Before 
entering the executive session, the chair must state the purpose for the executive session, 
stating all subjects that may be revealed without compromising the purpose for which the 
executive session was called, and the chair must publicly announce whether the open session 
will reconvene at the conclusion of the executive session. Id. 
According to the meeting minutes, although the Board convened in open session on 
December 20, 2012 and voted to enter into executive session by roll call prior to convening in 
executive session, President DeVita failed to state the purpose for the executive session and 
whether the Board would reconvene in open session following the closed session. However, 
we acknowledge that the Board properly followed these requisite steps during its subsequent 
meeting on January 8, 2013.

CONCLUSION 
For the reasons stated above, we find that the Board's executive session discussions 
were appropriate under the Open Meeting Law; however we find that the Board violated the 
Open Meeting Law by failing to list a topic with sufficient specificity in its meeting notice 
and by failing to follow required procedures for entering into executive session. We order 
immediate and future compliance with the Open Meeting Law, and caution the Board that a 
determination by our office of similar violations in the future may be considered evidence of 
intent to violate the Open Meeting Law. 
We now consider the complaint addressed by this determination to be resolved. This 
determination does not address any other complaints which may be pending with our office or 
the Board. Please feel free to contact our office at (617) 963-2540 if you have any questions 
or believe any facts in this letter to be inaccurate.
