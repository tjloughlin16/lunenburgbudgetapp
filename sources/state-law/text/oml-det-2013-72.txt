RE: Open Meeting Law Complaint 
Dear Mr. Briggs: 
This office received a complaint from Ms. G. Lillian Whitney dated July 17, 2012, 
alleging that the Ashby Board of Selectmen (the "Board") violated the Open Meeting Law, G.L. 
c. 30A, §§ 18-25. Specifically, the complaint alleges that the Board met on April 18, 2012 
without properly posting notice. The complaint was filed with the Board on May 9, 2012. The 
Board responded to the complaint by letter dated June 20, 2012.1  
Following our review, we find that the Board violated the Open Meeting Law by meeting 
on April 18, 2012 at a time other than that stated in the meeting notice. In reaching this 
determination, we reviewed the May 9, 2012 complaint; the Board's June 20, 2012 response to 
that complaint; and the July 17, 2012 complaint filed with our office. Additionally, we reviewed 
the notice for the Board's 2:00 PM, April 18, 2012 meeting. We also reviewed a letter from 
Town Administrator Douglas Briggs,2  addressed to our office, dated August 7, 2012. Finally, we 
reviewed the open session minutes for the Board's 6:35 PM, April 18, 2012 meeting. 
FACTS 
The Board is a three-member public body. The Board planned to meet at 2:00 PM on 
April 18, 2012. The notice for that meeting, posted on April 12, 2012, listed several topics, 
including an executive session to discuss the "Reputation, Character, Physical Condition or 
Mental Health, rather than Professional Competence of an Individual," and "Request for 
Selectmen's Approval and Signature on 2 One Day Liquor Licenses for the Fitchburg Rod and 
Gun Club." At 2:00 PM on April 18, 2012, only the Board Chair arrived for the meeting. Town 
Administrator Briggs was informed that a second Board member would not be available until

3:00 PM. The Board Chair could not wait until 3:00 PM. However, the Chair planned to return 
at 6:30 PM for a question and answer session with Town residents concerning regionalization. 
The Chair decided to postpone the meeting to discuss the two liquor licenses until 6:30 PM.

The Board convened a meeting at 6:35 PM that evening, and a quorum of the Board was 
present. During the meeting, the Board discussed and approved two one-day liquor licenses for 
the Fitchburg Rod and Club for events on April 21, 2012 and April 28, 2012. The Board did not 
enter into executive session. The Board did not post notice for a meeting at this time. 
DISCUSSION 
The Open Meeting Law requires that a public body post a notice at least 48 hours before 
a meeting, not including Saturdays, Sundays, and legal holidays, and include in the notice "a 
listing of topics that the chair reasonably anticipates will be discussed at the meeting." G.L. c. 
30A, § 20(b). In an emergency, a public body shall post notice as soon as reasonably possible 
prior to such meeting. Id. An "emergency" is defined as "a sudden, generally unexpected 
occurrence or set of circumstances demanding immediate action." G.L. c. 30A, § 18. 
The Board posted notice for a meeting scheduled for 2:00 PM on April 18, 2012. 
Because the Board could not reach a quorum at 2:00 PM, the Chair postponed the meeting to that 
evening at 6:30 PM. The Board did not post a separate notice 48 hours in advance for a meeting 
at 6:30 PM. Although a public body may delay starting a posted meeting by a short period of 
time to enable tardy members to arrive, if the meeting is significantly postponed, or rescheduled, 
the body must post notice 48 hours in advance of the new meeting. See G.L. c. 30A, § 20(b). 
Here, the postponement was lengthy enough that we find the 6:30 PM meeting was a new 
meeting requiring separate notice. Because the Board did not post notice for a meeting at 6:30 
PM, meeting at that time was improper. Id. 
Although not explicitly argued by the Board, Town Administrator Briggs' August 7, 
2012 letter to our office suggests the Board may have viewed this as an emergency meeting 
because "[o]ne of the liquor licenses was for an event happening that Saturday and could not 
wait." In an emergency, a public body does not need to post notice 48 hours before the meeting, 
but shall post notice as soon as reasonably possible. See G.L. c. 30A, §§ 18, 20(b). While the 
liquor license requests may have demanded immediate action because the first event was only 
three days away, the requests were not "a sudden, generally unexpected occurrence or set of 
circumstances..." G.L. c. 30A, § 18; OML 2012-7. 3  The Board anticipated discussing the liquor 
license requests as early as April 12, 2012, when it posted notice for the April 18, 2012, 2:00 PM 
meeting and listed the license requests as a topic for discussion. That the Board was not able to

CONCLUSION 
For the reasons stated above, we find that the Board violated the Open Meeting Law by 
meeting on April 18, 2012 at a time other than that stated in the meeting notice. We order 
immediate and future compliance with the law, and caution that Board that a finding of a similar 
future violation may be considered evidence of an intentional violation of the law.

parties during this investigation. If you have any questions regarding this letter, or believe any of
