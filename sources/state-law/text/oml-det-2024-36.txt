RE: Open Meeting Law Complaint  
 
Dear Attorney Mello:  
 
This office received a complaint from Sean McNamara on January 9, 2024, alleging that 
the Attleboro Planning Board (the “Board”) violated the Open Meeting Law, G.L. c. 30A, §§ 18-
25.  Mr. McNamara filed the complaint with the Board on December 6, 2023 and you responded 
on behalf of the Board by letter dated December 20.1  The complaint alleges that minutes of the 
Board’s October 16 meeting were not created and approved in a timely manner.2    
 
Following our review, we find that the Board did not violate the Open Meeting Law in 
the ways alleged.  In reaching this determination, we reviewed the original complaint, the 
Board’s response to the complaint and the complainant’s request for further review.  We also 
reviewed the minutes of the Board’s October 16 and December 4 meetings, and reviewed the list 
of meetings available on the Board’s website.
3 
 
FACTS  
We find the facts as follows.   The Board held meetings on October 16, November 6, 
November 20, and December 4.  The Board approved minutes of the October 16 meeting at its 
December 4 meeting.

DISCUSSION  
 
The Open Meeting Law was enacted “to eliminate much of the secrecy surrounding the 
delibe
rations and decisions on which public policy is based.” Ghiglione v. School Board of 
Southbridge, 376 Mass. 70, 72 (1978).  The Open Meeting Law requires public bodies to create 
and approve meeting minutes in a timely manner.  G.L. c. 30A, § 22(c).  “Timely manner” 
means “within the next three public body meetings or within 30 days, whichever is later, unless 
the public body can show good cause for further delay.”  940 CMR 29.11; see OML 2018-48.  
Whenever possible, we recommend that minutes of a meeting be approved at the next meeting.  
See OML 2018-67; OML 2017-133.  
 
The complainant alleges that as of December 5, minutes of the October 16 meeting were 
not av
ailable on the city website.  To the extent that the complainant argues that the subject 
minutes should have been posted to a municipal website, we note that the Open Meeting Law 
does not require public bodies to post meeting minutes.  See OML 2023-179; OML 2015-173; 
OML 2013-117.  Rather, the Law requires that “[t]he minutes of an open session, if they exist 
and whether approved or in draft form, shall be made available upon request by any person 
within 10 days.”  G.L. c. 30A, § 22(c).  The complainant does not allege that he requested, and 
was not provided with, minutes of the October 16 meeting.  Therefore, this determination 
addresses only the failure to approve minutes timely and not any allegation that the Board failed 
to respond to a request for minutes. 
 
The mi
nutes of the October 16 meeting were approved on December 4, the third meeting 
after October 16.  Therefore, they were created and approved in a timely manner, in accordance 
with the Open Meeting Law.   
 
CONCLUSION 
  
For the reasons stated above, we find that the Board did not violate the Open Meeting 
Law in the ways alleged.  We now consider the complaint addressed by this determination to be 
resolved.  This determination does not address any other complaints that may be pending with 
our office or the Board.  Please feel free to contact the Division at (617) 963 - 2540 if you have 
any questions.
