Dear Attorney Goldberg: 
This office received an Open Meeting Law complaint from Bernard Brahm on April 30, 
2012, alleging that the Provincetown Historical District Commission (the "Commission") 
violated the Open Meeting Law, G.L. c. 30A, §§ 18-25. The complaint specifically alleges that 
the Commission held an executive session meeting on January 4, 2012, to "discuss behind closed 
doors two Open Meeting Law complaints....claiming that these complaints represents 'litigation' 
and they had to 'discuss strategy'... A complaint under the Open Meeting Law originates at the 
town level and is absolutely not litigation" (emphasis in original). The complaint was originally 
filed with the Commission on February 1, 2011. Following extensions granted by our office, the 
Commission responded by letter, through counsel, on March 23, 2012. 
Following our review, we find that Commission did not violate the Open Meeting Law 
by meeting in executive session on January 4, 2012. In reaching this determination, we reviewed 
the February 1, 2012 complaint filed with the Commission; the Commission's March 23, 2012 
response; and the April 30, 2012 complaint. We also reviewed the notice and minutes of the 
Commission's January 4, 2012 open and executive session meetings. 
FACTS 
On January 4, 2012, the Provincetown Historical District Commission met to discuss, 
among other topics, two Open meeting Law complaints filed with the Commission by Mr. 
Bernard Brahm, the complainant in the present complaint. During the meeting, the Commission 
voted unanimously to enter into executive session "in accordance with G.L. c. 30A, §21(a)(3) to 
discuss strategy with respect to" the two Open Meeting Law complaints.

DISCUSSION 
A public body may enter executive session for any of ten enumerated purposes. G.L. c. 
30A, § 21(a). Among the ten purposes is Purpose 3, which states that a public body may enter 
into executive session to "discuss strategy with respect to collective bargaining or litigation if an 
open meeting may have a detrimental effect on the bargaining or litigating position of the public 
body and the chair so declares." G.L. c. 30A, § 21(a)(3). Also among the ten purposes is 
Purpose 1, "to discuss the discipline or dismissal of, or complaints or charges brought against, 
a public officer, employee, staff member or individual." Id. at § 21(a)(1). 
A public body may enter executive session under Purpose 1 to discuss an Open Meeting 
Law complaint. See OML 2011-6.1  Such a complaint is by its nature a complaint "brought 
against, a public officer," namely the members of the public body. Here, the Commission 
appropriately entered into executive session to discuss two Open Meeting Law complaints. The 
Commission cited Purpose 3, to discuss strategy with respect to ligation, to justify its executive 
session. The Commission should have cited Purpose 1. An Open Meeting Law complaint, when 
filed with a public body, is not litigation. Although the Commission cited the wrong purpose for 
entering into executive session, it was legally entitled to discuss the Open Meeting Law 
complaints behind closed doors. 
CONCLUSION 
For the reasons stated above, we find that the Commission did not violate the Open 
Meeting Law.
