RE:  Open Meeting Law Complaint 
 
Dear Attorney Mooney: 
 
 This office received a complaint from Bruce Friedman on November 7, 2024, alleging 
that the Malden Licensing Board (the “Board”) violated the Open Meeting Law, G.L. c. 30A, §§ 
18-25.
1  The complaint was originally filed with the Board on October 24, and you responded, 
on behalf of the Board, by letter dated November 6.  The complaint alleges that the Board failed 
to timely respond to a request for meeting minutes.  
 
Following review, we resolve this complaint by informal action, in accordance with 940 
CMR 29.07(2)(a).2  The Board acknowledges, and we find, that the Board violated the Open 
Meeting Law by failing to respond timely to a request for minutes.3  While not claiming that it is 
excused from a violation, the Board has explained that the failure to respond to the complainant’s 
request for minutes was due a change of personnel and a misunderstanding that the request had 
already been addressed.   
 
We must determine whether the Board’s violation was, as the complainant urges, 
intentional.  See G.L. c. 30A, § 23(c).  The complainant cites our prior determination, OML

2024-202 in support of his argument that the Board has been warned previously about the need 
to respond to a request for minutes.  However, that determination was issued on November 1.  
Therefore, at the time of the instant complaint, October 24, the Board had not yet been warned 
against the violation found here.  In light of the circumstances set out in the Board’s response, 
we decline to find that the violation was intentional. 
 
Finally, as previously explained to the complainant in OML 2024-202 and OML 2024-
146, the Open Meeting Law does not govern a public body’s response to a request for records 
other than meeting minutes.  See G.L. c. 30A, §§ 22(c), (e); OML 2024-202; OML 2024-146. 4  
Therefore, failure to timely respond to requests for documents other than minutes concerns a 
potential violation of the Public Records Law, rather than the Open Meeting Law, and we thus 
decline to review this allegation.   
 
We order the Board’s immediate and future compliance with the Open Meeting Law.  We 
now consider the complaint addressed by this determination to be resolved.  This determination 
does not address any other complaints that may be pending with our office or the Board.  Please 
feel free to contact the Division at (617) 963 - 2540 if you have any questions.
