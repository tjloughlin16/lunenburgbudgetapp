This office received a complaint from Gerrit Blauvelt on February 17, 2022, alleging that 
the North Adams Airport Commission (the “Commission”) violated the Open Meeting Law, 
G.L. c. 30A, §§ 18-25.  The complaint was originally filed with the Commission on December 
30, 2021, and Commission Chair Dan Caplinger responded to the complaint, on behalf of the 
Commission, by letter dated January 18, 2022.  The complaint alleges that the Commission 
failed to timely respond to a request for meeting minutes.   
 
We appreciate the patience of the parties while we reviewed this matter.  We resolve this 
matter
 by informal action, in accordance with 940 CMR 29.07(2)(a), after reviewing the 
original complaint, the Commission’s response to the complaint, and the complaint filed with our 
office requesting further review.  We find that the Commission violated the Open Meeting Law 
in the way alleged. 
 
The Open Meeting Law requires that a public body “create and maintain accurate 
minut
es of all meetings, including executive sessions.”  G.L. c. 30A, § 22(a).  The minutes of an 
open session, if they exist and whether approved or in draft form, shall be made available upon 
request by any person within 10 days.”  G.L. c. 30A, § 22(c).  If such minutes do not yet exist at 
the time of a request, the public body is still required to respond to the request within 10 days 
with an explanation of whether the minutes do or do not exist in either approved or draft form.

See OML 2018-98; OML 2017-50; OML 2016-71; OML 2015-173.1  We note that a request for 
meeting minutes that is directed to a public body will trigger the Open Meeting Law.  On the 
other hand, a request for minutes that is directed to a municipal employee, such as the Town 
Clerk, will likely trigger the Public Records Law.  See OML 2019-126; OML 2019-68.   
 
On December 2, 2021, the complainant sent an email to Commission Chair Dan 
Caplinger requesting “any meeting minutes from 1995, 1998-2002, and 2016 that the North 
Adams Airport Commission has.”
2  The Commission did not respond to this request for minutes 
within the timeframe required by the Open Meeting Law.3  The complainant thereafter filed the  
Open Meeting Law complaint at issue here regarding the Commission’s failure to respond to his 
request for meeting minutes.  Because the Commission failed to provide a response within 10 
days of the request for minutes, we find that it violated the Open Meeting Law by not timely 
responding to a request for minutes.  On February 23, 2022, the Town Administrator provided to 
the complainant copies of minutes of Commission meetings held between 1982 and 2001, as 
well as minutes of meetings held in 2016.  On March 7, 2022, the Town Administrator provided 
copies of minutes of Commission meetings held in 2002 and 2003.  Since the Commission has 
provided to the complainant copies of requested meeting minutes, we order no further remedial 
action.
4 
 
For the reasons stated above, we find that the Commission violated the Open Meeting 
Law by failing to timely respond to a request for meeting minutes.  We order immediate and 
future compliance with the law’s requirements, and we caution that similar future violations 
could be considered evidence of intent to violate the law.
