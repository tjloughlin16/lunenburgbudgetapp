RE: Open Meeting Law Complaint 
Dear Attorney Cruise: 
This office received a complaint from John Alcaidinho, dated August 28, 2012, alleging 
that the Dartmouth Fire District One Prudential Committee (the "Committee") violated the Open 
Meeting Law, G.L. c. 30A, §§ 18-25. The complaint was originally filed with the Committee on 
or about July 16, 2012, and the Committee responded to the original complaint by letter dated 
August 23, 2012. In his complaint, Mr. Alcaidinho alleges that the Committee failed to respond 
to his June 19, 2012 request for meeting minutes within ten days.' 
We reviewed the July 16, 2012 complaint, which included the June 19, 2012 and July 16, 
2012 letters from the complainant to the Committee; your August 23, 2012 response on behalf of 
the Committee; the August 28, 2012 complaint filed with our office requesting further review; 
your September 5, 2012 letter to the complainant; and your March 3, 2013 letter to our office. 
Finally, we reviewed the notice and minutes for the Board's June 13, 2012 meeting.2  
We appreciate the patience and cooperation of the parties while we reviewed this matter. 
Following our review, we have decided to resolve this complaint by informal action,  in 
accordance with 940 CMR 29.07(2)(a). We find that the Committee violated the Open Meeting 
Law by failing to provide meeting minutes within ten (10) days of a request. See G.L. c. 30A, §

22(c). However, we do not find that the violation was intentional, as it was not clear from the 
complainant's request which minutes were being requested. 
In a June 19, 2012 request to the Committee, the complainant stated that the Committee 
received a letter from "a New Bedford resident requesting my past service agreement and 
separation agreement," thus he requested "a copy of that letter and a copy of the return address 
on the envelope, a copy of your boards [sic] open session minutes and the session meeting 
tapes."3  In a July 16, 2012 letter to the Chair of the Committee, which was attached to the 
complaint filed with the Committee, the complainant states, "[t]he June 19, 2012 letter clearly 
was referring to your boards [sic] June 13, 2012 [meeting] just by its content and the basis of my 
request." Despite this clarification, the Committee did not provide the June 13, 2012 meeting 
minutes to the complainant until September 5, 2012.4  The Committee has not provided an 
explanation for the delay. 
The Committee clearly failed to provide meeting minutes within ten days of a request. 
However, after the complainant clarified in his Open Meeting Law complaint which minutes he 
was seeking, the Committee did ultimately provide the requested minutes. Although we do not 
find that the Committee's violation was intentional, we note that when a request for minutes is 
unclear, public bodies should contact the requestor promptly for clarification of the request. 
OML 2012-86. 
Finally, while not raised in the complaint, we note that the discussion topics listed in the 
notice for the Committee's June 13, 2012 meeting were not sufficiently specific. See OML 
2012-34.5  We remind the Committee of its obligation to list topics in a meeting notice with 
"sufficient specificity to reasonably advise the public of the issues to be discussed at the 
meeting." 940 CMR 29.03(1)(b). We generally consider a topic to include sufficient specificity 
when a reasonable member of the public could read the topic and understand the anticipated 
nature of the public body's discussion. See OML 2011-44. Topics such as "Discuss old 
business" and "To discuss 3 letters" do not offer the public any understanding of the anticipated 
topics to be discussed. In recent correspondence with our office, Attorney Anthony Savastano 
assured us that he has advised the Committee's new chair of the Law's requirements in this 
regard. 
For the reasons stated above, we find the Committee violated the Open Meeting Law. 
We order immediate and future compliance with the Law, and caution that future similar 
violations may be considered evidence of intent to violate the Law. 940 CMR 29.02. We also 
order the Committee to review the Open Meeting Law Guide, available at the Attorney General's
