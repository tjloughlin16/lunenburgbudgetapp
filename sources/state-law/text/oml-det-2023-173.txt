This office received a complaint from Patrick Higgins on July 18, 2023, alleging that the 
Swansea Free Public Library Board of Trustees (the “Board”) violated the Open Meeting Law, 
G.L. c. 30A, §§ 18-25.
1  The complaint was originally filed with the Board on June 23, and you2 
responded to the complaint, on behalf of the Board, by letter dated July 18, following an 
extension granted by our office.  The complaint alleges that the Board improperly amended its 
notice by adding a topic within 48 hours of its June 22 meeting and providing insufficient 
specificity with respect to that additional topic.   
 
We resolve this matter by informal action, in ac
 cordance with 940 CMR 29.07(2)(a), 
after reviewing the original complaint, the Board’s response to the complaint, the complaint filed 
with our office requesting further review, and the notice, amended notice and minutes of the 
Board meeting held on June 22.  We find that the Board did not violate the Open Meeting Law in 
the ways alleged. 
 
The O
pen Meeting Law requires that public bodies post notice of every meeting “at least 
48 hours prior to such meeting, excluding Saturdays, Sundays and legal holidays.”  G.L. c. 30A, 
§ 20(b).  Every notice must include “the date, time and place of such meeting and a listing of 
topics that the chair reasonably anticipates will be discussed at the meeting.”  G.L. c. 30A,          
§ 20(b).  Public bodies are required to list topics on a notice with “sufficient specificity to

reasonably advise the public of the issues to be discussed at the meeting.”  940 CMR 29.03(1) 
(b).  We
 generally consider a topic sufficiently specific when a reasonable member of the public 
could read the topic and understand the anticipated nature of the public body’s discussion.  See 
OML 2015-35; OML 2012-71.3    
 
On June 20 at 8:12 A.M., the Board posted notice of a meeting to be held on June 22 at 
6:00 P.M.  The notice included nine topics.  On June 22 at 4:07 P.M., the Board posted an 
amended notice for the June 22 meeting and listed one additional topic, “VOTE to authorize the 
use of up to $10,000 in MEG funds for payroll and expenses for the remainder of FY23.”  
During the June 22 meeting, the Board voted unanimously to authorize the use of up to $10,000 
in Municipal Equalization Grant (MEG) funds for payroll and expenses for the remainder of 
fiscal year 2023.  
 
The complaint alleges that the Board violated the Open Meeting Law by discussing a 
topic, a
uthorizing the use of funds for payroll and expenses, during its June 22 meeting that was 
not listed on the original notice but rather was added within 48 hours of the meeting.  A public 
body may amend a meeting notice within 48 hours of a meeting to include an unanticipated topic 
and then discuss that topic during the meeting.  See OML 2019-131.  Here, the Board amended 
the June 22 meeting notice approximately 2 hours prior to the meeting to include the discussion 
of the authorization of funds.  Board Chair Angela Dolan stated that she did not anticipate the 
need to discuss authorization of Municipal Equalization Grant funds until three hours before the 
meeting when library staff contacted her and explained the need to utilize the grant funds.  See 
OML 2017-2; OML 2013-87.  We find that the Board did not violate the Open Meeting Law by 
amending the June 22 meeting notice to include this unanticipated topic.  See OML 2020-53; 
OML 2017-2; OML 2013-87.  In fact, although the Board could have discussed the unanticipated 
topic without adding it to the meeting notice at all, the Board followed our recommended best 
practice of amending its meeting notice to include topics that arise fewer than 48 hours before a 
meeting, so that members of the public are given advance notice of agenda items.  See OML 
2015-111; OML 2015-76. 
 
The complaint also alleges that the Board provided insufficient specificity on the 
amende
d meeting notice by using the acronym “MEG” which is not widely understood by the 
public.  The Attorney General’s Office discourages public bodies from using abbreviations or 
acronyms in meeting notices if they are not commonly understood.  See OML 2016-5 (finding 
that the acronym “PILOT” is not one that members of the public are likely to understand); Cf. 
OML 2015-172 (concluding that “FAA” is a widely understood acronym).  While we agree that 
the acronym “MEG” is not one that members of the public are likely to understand, we find that 
the additional detail on the amended notice that accompanied this acronym was sufficient to 
reasonably advise the public of the issue to be discussed at the meeting.  See G.L. c. 30A, § 
20(b); see also OML 2013-152.  Specifically, the notice included the particular amount of funds 
to be authorized as well as the exact use for the funds.  As such, we find that the notice was 
sufficiently specific to inform the public of the Board’s anticipated discussion during the June 22 
meeting.
