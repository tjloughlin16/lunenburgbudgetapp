Dear Mr. Meehan: 
 
This office received two complaints from Maryjane Churchville on December 4, 2025,  
alleging that the Townsend Selectboard (the “Board”) violated the Open Meeting Law, G.L. c. 
30A, §§ 18-25.  Both complaints were originally filed with the Board on October 24, 2025, and 
former Town Administrator Nelson Mui responded to the complaints, on behalf of the Board, by 
separate letters dated November 13, 2025.  The complaints allege that the notices of Board 
meetings held on September 25 and October 7, 2025, were insufficiently specific. 
 
We appreciate the patience of the parties while we reviewed these matters.  Following 
our review, we find that the Board did not violate the Open Meeting Law in the ways alleged.  In 
reaching this determination, we reviewed the original complaints, the Board’s responses to the 
complaints, and the complaints filed with our office requesting further review.  In addition, we 
reviewed the notices and minutes of the Board meetings held on September 25 and October 7, 
2025, as well as video recordings of those two meetings.

FACTS 
 
We find the facts as follows.  On September 19, 2025, at 9:54 A.M., the Board posted 
notice of a meeting to be held on September 25, 2025, at 4:30 P.M.  The notice listed seven main 
topic headings, under which more specific topics were listed.  One main topic was listed as 
“Meeting Business” and included 14 specific item descriptions that further advised the public of 
the topics to be discussed, one of which was “Approve and sign outside of a meeting all 2026 
renewals for Common Victualler licenses contingent upon receipt of required documentation and 
payment.”  Another main topic was “Work Session” and included five specific item descriptions 
that further advised the public of the topics to be discussed, one of which was “Reports from 
Board liaisons.”  This topic, “Reports from Board liaisons,” also included three additional item 
descriptions - “General Government,” “Elected Boards” and “Public Safety.”  
 
The September 25, 2025, meeting was held as planned.  During the meeting, the Board 
did not discuss any specific licenses that were up for renewal or approve any specific licenses.

Board member Valerie Goodrich suggested that the Conversation Agent attend a board meeting

DISCUSSION 
 
The Open Meeting Law requires that public bodies must post notice of each meeting “at 
least 48 hours prior to such meeting” and that every notice include “the date, time and place of 
such meeting and a listing of topics that the chair reasonably anticipates will be discussed at the 
meeting.”  G.L. c. 30A, § 20(b).  The list of topics shall have “sufficient specificity to reasonably 
advise the public of the issues to be discussed at the meeting.”  940 CMR 29.03(l)(b).  We 
generally consider a topic to include sufficient specificity when a reasonable member of the 
public could read the topic and understand the anticipated nature of the public body’s discussion.  
See OML 2015-35; OML 2012-71; OML 2011-44.
4  When reviewing a meeting notice for 
sufficiency, unless it is clearly insufficient on its face, we do not review a meeting notice 
standing alone.  See OML 2015-109; OML 2014-155.  Rather, we review what was actually 
discussed at the meeting to determine if the notice was sufficiently detailed to make the public 
aware of the discussion topics.  See OML 2015-101; OML 2015-21.   
 
The complaints allege that the Board posted insufficient notices for the meetings held on 
September 25 and October 7, 2025, where the notices included broad topics without including 
additional item descriptions that further advised the public of the topics to be discussed.  
Specifically, the complaints allege that the Board did not include on the September 25, 2025, 
meeting notice the specific businesses seeking renewal of common victualler licenses under the 
header “Approve and sign outside of a meeting all 2026 renewals for Common Victualler 
licenses contingent upon receipt of required documentation and payment.”  Here, the Board did 
not actually discuss any licenses, nor did it approve or deny any licenses.  Rather, it simply 
moved to sign license renewals outside of a meeting.  When a public body does not actually 
deliberate on a noticed topic, the public body cannot violate the Open Meeting Law with respect 
to that topic, even if it would have been insufficiently detailed had the public body proceeded

with its deliberation.  See OML 2015-63; OML 2014-81.  Although we find no violation with 
respect to this topic, we remind the Board that, to be sufficiently specific, a meeting notice must 
identify the specific applicants or license holders that will be considered during the meeting, as 
well as the specific type of license renewal to be reviewed.  See OML 2020-112; OML 2020-20; 
OML 2017-33; OML 2016-90.  That is, where a topic requires a public body’s approval or other 
specific decision, such as annual license renewals or appointment of candidates to public bodies, 
the specific applicants or candidates should be listed on the notice itself; it is not sufficient to 
reference a separate list that is available elsewhere.  See OML 2024-236; OML 2022-68; OML 
2020-20; OML 2019-22.  We understand that the Board has since included the specific 
businesses seeking licenses on its notices and we commend the Board for committing to 
including the additional specificity on meeting notices going forward. 
 
With respect to the topic “Reports from Board liaisons” that was listed on both the 
September 25 and October 7, 2025, meeting notices, we find that during this part of the meeting 
Board members generally notified residents of upcoming events, reported on the budget 
discussions of the school and finance committees, recognized town employees, and suggested 
topics for future meetings.  Such announcements are generally spontaneous and not pieces of 
business that Board members anticipate discussing or otherwise within the Board’s jurisdiction.  
See G.L. c. 30A, § 20(b) (notices must contain “a listing of topics that the chair reasonably 
anticipates will be discussed at the meeting.”).  As such, we find that these types of specific 
announcements do not need to be listed on the meeting notice.  See OML 2017-33.  We note 
that, during the September 25, 2025, meeting, Chair Shifrin briefed the Board on a Board of 
Health meeting during which the Board of Health discussed the solid waste program.  
Specifically, Chair Shifrin highlighted the revenue and expenses generated by the program and 
stated that she had requested specific data on waste tonnage.  The Board briefly discussed the 
expenditures and whether to hold a joint meeting with the Board of Health.  Although these 
discussions amounted to more than simple announcements, we do not find a violation where the 
notice of the September 25, 2025, meeting also included the following topic “Update on Solid 
Waste Contract – enterprise fund accounting; contract for consideration for pickup starting 9/1.”  
In fact, when the Board reached this topic during the September 25, 2025, meeting, Chair Shifrin 
mentioned that she had updates but would provide them during that part of the meeting reserved 
for “Reports from Board liaisons.”  Once a meeting begins, the public body controls its own 
agenda.  Accordingly, the Law does not prohibit a public body from removing, postponing, or 
declining to discuss a topic listed on a meeting notice or taking topics in a different order than 
listed on the notice.  See OML 2021-201. 
 
Finally, with respect to the allegation that the topic “Town Administrator Updates and 
Reports,” was not sufficiently specific on the October 7, 2025, meeting notice, we do not find a 
violation.  Here, the Town Administrator informed the Board of various projects and activities 
that had occurred in Town, as well as meetings or conferences he attended.  As such, the reports 
simply provided status updates to the Board and were generally not pieces of business that Board 
members anticipated discussing such that they needed to be listed on the notice.  See OML 2019-
83; OML 2011-23; see also G.L. c. 30A, § 20(b).  Therefore, we find that the notice was 
sufficient with respect to this topic where the Board did not deliberate on the matters presented 
by the Town Administrator.  See OML 2022-111 (finding no violation where topic “Council on 
Aging Director's report” was simply a time to notify Council members and residents of

upcoming events, and that announcements were generally not pieces of business that Council 
members anticipated discussing); OML 2021-43 (finding no violation with respect to the 
sufficiency of the notice where the chair read the town administrator's memo aloud and “[n]o 
Board member, including the Chair, shared any comments or asked any questions about the 
memo....”); OML 2019-94 (finding no violation when a meeting notice included the topic 
“Discussion of Legal Settlements” without identifying any particular legal matters, but the 
committee merely heard a report from the superintendent and did not engage in any discussion). 
 
Nonetheless, we encourage the Chair to solicit a more detailed set of topics from the 
Town Administrator for the meeting notice when the Chair anticipates that he will provide a 
report.  See OML 2018-33 (finding no violation where the public body engaged in deliberation 
regarding a matter that arose during the town administrator’s report to the Board, listed on the 
notice merely as “Report from  Departments,” but encouraging the chair to solicit a more 
detailed set of topics in the future); OML 2012-19 (“best practice dictates that the Chair make 
reasonable efforts to ascertain what the Superintendent plans to discuss and include all matters 
that are reasonably anticipated in the meeting notice”).  Moreover, we remind the Board that 
where the chair of a public body has designated some other individual to create meeting notices, 
we look to what the chair and the other individual responsible for creating the meeting notice 
reasonably anticipated, within 48 hours, would be discussed at the meeting.  See OML 2022-167; 
OML 2021-146. 
 
CONCLUSION 
  
For the reasons stated above, we find that the Board did not violate the Open Meeting 
Law.  We now consider the complaints addressed by this determination to be resolved.  This 
determination does not address any other complaints that may be pending with the Board or with 
our office.  Please feel free to contact our office at (617) 963-2540 if you have any questions 
regarding this letter.
