RE: Open Meeting Law Complaint 
Dear Chair Kahn: 
This office received a complaint from Stephen Curley, dated June 26, 2012, alleging that 
the Wellfleet Historic Review Board (the "Board") violated the Open Meeting Law, G.L. c. 30A, 
§§ 18-25. The complaint was originally filed with the Board on March 26, 2012 and, following 
an extension granted by our office, former Board Chair John Smith responded to the complaint 
on behalf of the Board by letter dated May 24, 2012. In his complaint, Mr. Curley alleges that 1) 
the "Board met and decided on action without posting;" 2) the "Board fails to keep up to date 
minutes of meetings and decisions," specifically for the Board's July 16, 2011 meeting; and 3) 
the "[B]oard met to discuss actions against a homeowner without giving [the] homeowner 
adequate notice in order to attend." 1  
In reaching a determination, we reviewed Mr. Curley's March 26, 2012 complaint filed 
with the Board; the Board's May 24, 2012 response; and Mr. Curley's complaint filed with this 
office on June 26, 2012. We also reviewed the minutes of the Board's meetings on March 2, 
2012; March 23, 2012 and April 5, 2012; a letter written by Mr. Curley to the Wellfleet Board of 
Selectmen, dated April 26, 2012; a letter from the Wellfleet Town Clerk/Treasurer, dated March 
1, 2013, provided to our office by the Assistant Town Administrator; and a letter from the 
Assistant Town Administrator to our office, dated March 1, 2013, sent in response to our request 
for the Board's meeting notices and minutes. 
Following our review, we find that the Board violated the Open Meeting Law by failing 
to create and approve minutes for its July 16, 2011 meeting. However, we find that the Board 
did not violate the Open Meeting Law by not providing individual notice to homeowners that 
their property would be discussed in an open session meeting. We are unable to determine 
whether the Board complied with notice posting requirements for its March 23, 2012 meeting, as 
we were not able to review the meeting notice. 
Mr. Curley also alleges that "Board members [discussed] actions without due process." This determination 
concerns only compliance with the Open Meeting Law, G.L. c. 30A, §§ 18-25, thus we decline to review allegations 
of violation of any other laws.

FACTS  
Based upon our review of the material listed above, the facts are as follows. 
The Board's March 23, 2012 meeting minutes indicate that the Board conducted a 
hearing on July 16, 2011. The Wellfleet Town Clerk's office does not have any record of a 
notice posted for a meeting of the Board on July 16, 2011, and neither the Board nor the Town 
Clerk was able to provide us with minutes for a meeting of the Board on that date. Due to the 
absence of records, it is unclear exactly what happened during the July 16, 2011 meeting. 
However, according to the Board's March 23, 2012 meeting minutes, the Board reached a 
consensus at the July 16, 2011 meeting regarding the historical and architectural significance of 
certain properties. During the March 23, 2012 meeting, the Board voted that the properties 
reviewed during the July 16, 2011 hearing be deemed historically and architecturally significant. 
In its May 24, 2012 response to the complaint, the Board identifies one of the properties affected 
by this decision as a home owned by Jeremy Wise and Jan Klausner Wise (hereinafter the "Wise 
Family") located at 445 King Phillip Road (hereinafter the "Wise House"). 
Mr. Curley, the complainant, was hired by the Wise Family to perform construction work 
at the Wise House. Work began in February 2012. On or about February 21, 2012, the Wellfleet 
Assistant Building Inspector issued a "stop work order" for the work at the Wise House. Mr. 
Curley alleges that two members of the Board (Steve Durkee and Olga Kahn2) accompanied the 
Assistant Building Inspector to the Wise House on February 21, 2012 and informed him that he 
needed approval from the Board before any work could be done. Mr. Curley alleges that Mr. 
Durkee stated that the Board had designated the Wise House historically significant following a 
hearing held the previous summer. 
On the morning of March 2, 2012, the Wise Family learned that the Board would be 
meeting that afternoon regarding the Wise House. According to the Clerk's office, notice for the 
March 2, 2012 meeting was posted on February 29, 2012. The Clerk's office does not retain 
meeting notices after meetings occur, however, thus it was unable to provide our office with a 
copy to review. The Wise Family was unable to attend the March 2, 2012 meeting, but had 
attorney Ben Zehnder attend and represent them. According to the minutes, "[t]he meeting had 
been called to discuss issues with regard to the recent demolition and ongoing work at [the Wise 
House]." The minutes reflect that the Board discussed the following topics: "Existing & Future 
Measures in Place to Protect Historic Structures;" "The Wise [House];" "The Historic Review 
Process;" and "Educating the Building Department." 
The Board met again on March 23, 2012. According to the Clerk's office, notice for the 
March 23, 2012 meeting was posted on March 20, 2012. Again, as the Clerk's office does not 
retain the meeting notices, it was unable to provide our office with a copy to review. The 
minutes reflect that the meeting was called to order at 3:05 PM and adjourned at 4:30 PM. The 
Board discussed two topics: approval of the March 2, 2012 minutes and "Determination of 
Historical Significance of the Modern Houses." For the latter topic, the minutes state that "[t]o 
formalize the consensus at the end of the hearing on the modern houses (July 16, 2011), Durkee 
moved that properties reviewed at the hearing be deemed historically and architecturally 
significant. Smith second and the motion carried unanimously." The minutes do not indicate

DISCUSSION 
In his complaint, Mr. Curley alleges that 1) the "Board met and decided on action without 
posting;" 2) the "Board fail[ed] to keep ... minutes of its July 16, 2011 meeting[] and decisions 
made at that meeting;" and 3) the "[B]oard met to discuss actions against a homeowner without 
giving [the] homeowner adequate notice in order to attend." We will address each of Mr. 
Curley's allegations in turn. 
I. 	 We are not Able to Determine Whether the Board Posted Notice for its March 23, 2012  
Meeting, but Even if it did not, the Board Took Appropriate Remedial Action in 
Response to the Complaint.  
The Open Meeting Law requires that, except in emergencies, public bodies shall "in 
addition to any notice otherwise required by law...post notice of every meeting at least 48 hours 
prior to such meeting, excluding Saturdays, Sundays and legal holidays," and that the "[n]otice 
shall be printed in a legible, easily understandable format and shall contain the date, time and 
place of such meeting and a listing of topics that the chair reasonably anticipates will be 
discussed at the meeting." G.L. c. 30A, § 20(b). 
Mr. Curley alleges that the "Board met and decided on action without posting," but his 
complaint does not allege specific meetings for which the Board failed to post notice. In its 
response to the complaint, the Board assumed the complaint referred to its March 23, 2012 
meeting. Although the Clerk's office recorded that notice for this meeting was posted on March 
20, 2013, the Clerk's office does not retain actual notices after the meetings occur, thus we were 
unable to determine whether the notice complied with the Open Meeting Law.3  However, in its 
response to the complaint, the Board "acknowledg[ed] and apologized] for its failure to follow 
the requirements of the Open Meeting Law for posting of the agenda ...." Furthermore, the 
Board declared during its April 5, 2012 meeting that its March 23, 2012 action, deeming the 
Wise House historically and architecturally significant, was null and void. Therefore, even if the 
notice was not properly posted, the Board took appropriate remedial action in response to the 
complaint by nullifying the action taken during the March 23, 2013 meeting.

II. The Board Failed to Create and Approve Minutes for its July 16, 2011 Meeting.  
The Open Meeting Law requires that "[m]inutes of all open sessions shall be created and 
approved in a timely manner." G.L. c. 30A, § 22(c); OML 2012-30. 4  According to the minutes 
from the Board's March 23, 2012 meeting, at a July 16, 2011 meeting, following a hearing, the 
Board concluded that certain properties reviewed were historically and architecturally 
significant. 5  No minutes exist for the July 16, 2011 meeting, however. 6  We therefore find that 
the Board violated the Open Meeting Law, and order the Board, to the best of its ability, to create 
and approve minutes for its July 16, 2011 meeting. 
III. The Board did not Violate the Open Meeting Law by Failing to Notify Homeowners that 
their Property Would be Discussed in Open Session.  
Mr. Curley alleges that the "[B]oard met to discuss actions against a homeowner without 
giving [the] homeowner adequate notice in order to attend." The date that this alleged violation 
occurred is unclear from the complaint.7  The Board, in its response, stated that it "fail[ed] to 
follow the requirements of the Open Meeting Law for . . . notice to the affected landowner." 
While we commend the Board's willingness to accept responsibility for any procedural 
deficiency that may have occurred, we note that, with the exception of certain executive session 
discussions, the Open Meeting Law does not require that public bodies provide notice of 
meetings to specific groups or individuals, such as affected property owners. See OML 2013-41; 
OML 2013-24; OML 2011-41. Mr. Curley does not allege that the Board discussed the Wise 
House or any other properties in executive session. Thus, the Open Meeting Law did not require 
the Board to provide the Wise Family with individual notice of its meetings, even when property 
owned by the Wise Family was being discussed. We do not address whether any other law 
required such notice. 
IV. We Decline to Review the Allegation that the Board Convened an Unposted Meeting on 
February 21, 2012.  
In a letter sent to the Wellfleet Board of Selectmen, Mr. Curley alleges that on February 
21, 2012, two members of the Board accompanied the Assistant Building Inspector to the Wise 
House and informed Mr. Curley that he needed approval from the Board before any work could

It is unclear whether Mr. Curley intended to raise this allegation is his complaint filed 
with the Board. Mr. Curley did list, on the Open Meeting Law complaint form filed with the

of the events of February 21, 2012 in his initial complaint. Therefore, as this allegation was not 
explicitly raised in the initial complaint, the Board did not have the opportunity to formally 
respond. 
Furthermore, the Open Meeting Law requires that a complaint be filed with the public 
body within 30 days of the alleged violation. G.L. c. 30A, § 23(b). If the alleged violation could 
not reasonably have been discovered at the time it occurred, then the complaint must be filed 
with the public body within 30 days of when it reasonably could have been discovered. 940 
CMR 29.05(3). As Mr. Curley was present during the alleged meeting on February 21, 2012, 
the alleged violation could have reasonably been discovered on February 21, 2012. Mr. Curley's 
complaint was filed with the Board on March 26, 2012, 33 days after the alleged violation took 
place. Thus, even if this allegation had been raised in the initial complaint, it was untimely. We 
therefore decline to review this this allegation. See 940 CMR 29.05(1), (3); OML 2012-114; 
OML 2012-102; OML Declination 5-6-13 (Worthington Board of Selectmen). 
Although we decline to review this allegation, we wish to provide guidance on the issues 
raised by the complaint. A "meeting" is "a deliberation by a public body with respect to any 
matter within the body's jurisdiction." G.L. c. 30A, § 18. A "deliberation" is, in relevant part, 
"an oral or written communication through any medium, including electronic mail, between or 
among a quorum of a public body on any public business within its jurisdiction." Id. A 
"quorum" is "a simple majority of the members of the public body, unless otherwise provided in 
a general or special law, executive order or other authorizing provision." Id. For a three-
member public body, a quorum is two members. Therefore, two members of a three-member 
public body must not communicate outside of a properly posted public meeting regarding public 
business within the body's jurisdiction. See OML 2011-38. 
CONCLUSION 
We find that the Board violated the Open Meeting Law by failing to create and approve 
minutes for its July 16, 2011 meeting. We find that the Board did not violate the Open Meeting 
Law by failing to provide the Wise Family with individual notice that the Board planned to 
discuss in open session property owned by the Wise Family. We are unable to determine if the 
Board complied with notice posting requirement for its March 23, 2012 meeting, as we were not 
able to review the meeting notice. 
We order the Board's immediate and future compliance with the Open Meeting Law. 
Additionally, we order the Board, to the best of its ability, to create and approve complete and 
accurate minutes for the Board's July 16, 2011 meeting. This should occur within sixty (60)
