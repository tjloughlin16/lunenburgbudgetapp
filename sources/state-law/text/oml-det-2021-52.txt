 RE: Open Meeting Law Complaint 
Dear Attorney Barnes: 
This office received a complaint from James Fitzgerald on October 13, 2020, alleging 
that the Holyoke City Council (the “Council”) violated the Open Meeting Law, G.L. c. 30A, §§ 
18-25. The complaint was originally filed with the Council on August 28, and you responded on 
behalf of the Council by letter dated December 21.1, 2 In his complaint, Mr. Fitzgerald alleges 
that the Council improperly discussed a topic at an August 4 meeting which was not included on 
the meeting notice.3 
 We resolve this complaint by informal action in accordance with 940 CMR 29.07(2)(a), 
and find that the Council violated the Open Meeting Law as alleged. The Open Meeting Law 
requires that a public body post notice of every meeting, and include a listing of topics that the 
chair reasonably anticipates will be discussed at the meeting. G.L. c. 30A, § 20(b); 940

30A, § 23(b); 940 CMR 29.05(5). 
3 Mr. Fitzgerald also alleges that the Council took up this topic when it was not included on the meeting notice in 
order to prevent him from being able to speak on the topic. We note that the Open Meeting Law does not require 
that a public body allow public participation during its meetings. G.L. c. 30A § 20(f). The Open Meeting Law 
permits the chair of a public body to decide whether to allow for public participation, and if so, to set limitations on 
participation. See OML 2017-189; OML 2014-23; OML 2012-23. We do, however, encourage public bodies to 
allow for as much public participation as time and circumstances permit. See OML 2015-12; OML 2014-129. As 
public participation is discretionary, this allegation would not constitute a violation of G.L. c. 30A, §§ 18-25. 
Accordingly, we decline to review it. See OML Declination 1-25-16 (Nahant Housing Authority Board of 
Commissioners).

CMR29.03(1)(b). A public body may discuss a topic not listed on a meeting notice if the topic 
was not reasonably anticipated 48 hours in advance of the meeting. See OML 2017-2; OML 
2013-87.4 Nevertheless, we strongly encourage public bodies to postpone discussion of topics 
that were not listed on the meeting notice until the topic may be included on a meeting notice, 
unless the circumstances demand immediate action by the public body. See OML 2020-164. 
 At an August 4 meeting, the Council voted 11-1 to deny a citizen’s petition to rezone a 
parcel of Business-Highway land. This topic was anticipated by the Chair more than 48 hours in 
advance of the meeting, as the Ordinance Committee voted on June 9 to recommend a denial of 
the petition to the full Council. The Council concedes that this recommendation should have 
been included on the meeting notice for the August 4 meeting. We agree, and find that the 
Council violated the Open Meeting Law by discussing an anticipated topic which was not 
included on the meeting notice.  
 For the reasons stated above, we find the Council violated the Open Meeting Law as 
alleged. We order the Council’s immediate and future compliance with the Open Meeting Law 
and caution the Council that a determination by our office of a similar violation in the future may 
be considered evidence of intent to violate the Open Meeting Law. Additionally, we order the 
Council, within 30 days of receipt of this determination, to take steps to remedy the violation by 
repeating its discussions regarding the specific petition, including ratifying any votes taken, 
during a properly posted future meeting. 
 We now consider the complaint addressed by this determination to be resolved. This 
determination does not address any other complaints that may be pending with our office or the 
Council. Please feel free to contact the Division at (617) 963 - 2540 if you have any questions.
