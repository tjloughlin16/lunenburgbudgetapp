This office received a complaint from Jacquelyn Wehtje on February 11, 2025, alleging 
that the Fitchburg Agricultural Commission (the “Commission”) violated the Open Meeting 
Law, G.L. c. 30A, §§ 18-25.  The complaint was originally filed with the Commission on 
January 13, 2025, and you responded, on behalf of the Commission, by letter dated February 11, 
2025, following an extension of time granted by this office.  The complaint alleges that the 
Commission convened in executive session for an improper purpose during a meeting held on

Following our review, we find that the Commission violated the Open Meeting Law in 
the way alleged.  In reaching this determination, we reviewed the original complaint, the 
Commission’s response to the complaint, and the complaint filed with our office requesting 
further review.  In addition, we received the notice and open and executive session minutes of 
the Commission meeting held on December 3, 2024, as well as a video recording of the open 
session portion of that meeting.

FACTS 
 
 We find the facts as follows.  On November 27, 2024, at 11:14 A.M., the Commission 
posted notice of a meeting to be held on December 3, 2024, at 6:30 P.M.  The notice listed five 
topics, including an executive session “in accordance with G.L. c. 30A, § 21(6) to consider the 
purchase, exchange, lease or value of real property if the Chair declares that an open meeting 
may have a detrimental effect on the negotiating position of the public body and also in 
accordance with G.L. c. 30A, § 21(7) to comply with, or act under the authority of, any general 
or special law or federal grant-in-aid requirements.”   
 
 The December 3, 2024, meeting was held as planned.  The Commission first convened in 
open session and received a presentation from Growing Places on its efforts to develop food 
security and the local food access network.  The Commission then reviewed and unanimously 
voted to accept minutes of meetings held on October 1 and November 19, 2024, as well as a 
proposed schedule for 2025 Commission meetings.  Commission Chair Evan Halstead 
announced that the Commission would convene in executive session pursuant to Purposes 6 and 
7, read the language of Purposes 6 and 7 as was listed on the notice, and announced that the 
Commission would not reconvene in open session at the conclusion of the executive session.  
The Commission then unanimously voted by roll call to convene in executive session. 
 
The Commission has not publicly released the minutes of the December 3, 2024, 
executive session; therefore, we do not recount their content in detail here.  However, based on 
our in camera review of the minutes, during the executive session meeting held on December 3, 
2024, the Commission reviewed two sets of executive sessions minutes and received advice from 
legal counsel on the necessity of subcommittees. 
 
DISCUSSION 
 
The Open Meeting Law was enacted “to eliminate much of the secrecy surrounding 
deli
beration and decisions on which public policy is based.”  Ghiglione v. School Committee of 
Southbridge, 376 Mass. 70, 72 (1978).  The Law requires that public bodies post notice of every 
meeting “at least 48 hours prior to such meeting, excluding Saturdays, Sundays and legal 
holidays” and that every notice include “the date, time and place of such meeting and a listing of 
topics that the chair reasonably anticipates will be discussed at the meeting.”  G.L. c. 30A, § 
20(b).  Public bodies are required to list topics on a notice with “sufficient specificity to 
reasonably advise the public of the issues to be discussed at the meeting.”  940 CMR 29.03(1) 
(b).  We generally consider a topic sufficiently specific when a reasonable member of the public 
could read the topic and understand the anticipated nature of the public body’s discussion.  See 
OML 2015-35.2

In addition, the O pen Meeting Law requires that all meetings of a public body be 
conducted in an open session, with some exceptions.  G.L. c. 30A, §§ 20(a), 21(a).  A public 
body may enter an executive, or closed, session for any of the ten purposes enumerated in the 
Open Meeting Law provided that it has first convened in an open session, that a majority of 
members of the body have voted to go into executive session, that the vote of each member is 
recorded by roll call and entered into the minutes, and the chair has publicly announced whether 
the open session will reconvene at the conclusion of the executive session.  G.L. c. 30A, §§ 
21(a), (b); see also OML 2014-94.   
 
One permissible reason to convene in executive session is “to comply with, or act under 
the authority of, any general or special law or federal grant-in-aid requirements.”  G.L. c. 30A, § 
21(a)(7) (“Purpose 7”).  A public body entering executive session under Purpose 7 must state the 
specific law that requires the public body to keep its deliberations confidential unless doing so 
would compromise the purpose for the closed door session.  See District Attorney for N. Dist. v. 
School Comm. of Wayland, 455 Mass. 561, 569 (explaining proper use of Purpose 7 to enter 
executive session requires enumeration of enabling law).  In particular, a public body may 
convene in executive session to review and approve executive session minutes under either the 
original purpose for the executive session, or under Purpose 7 and citing to the Open Meeting 
Law, G.L. c.30A, §§ 22(f), (g). 
 
The complaint alleges that the Comm
 ission convened in executive session for an 
improper purpose when it discussed certain matters with its legal counsel during the December 3, 
2024, meeting.  Here, the notice of the December 3, 2024, meeting included two executive 
session purposes, Purpose 6 and Purpose 7, but did not include any further detail that would have 
“reasonably advise[d] the public of the issues to be discussed at the meeting.”  See 940 CMR 
29.03(l)(b).  We note that the Commission’s discussions with legal counsel did not pertain to the 
purchase, exchange, lease or value of real property.  Rather, the Commission’s discussions with 
legal counsel pertained to the necessity of subcommittees.  A public body’s discussions with 
legal counsel do not automatically fall within Purpose 7 or any other executive session purpose.  
See OML 2015-14; OML 2014-42; OML 2012-55.  Indeed, the protection of the attorney-client 
privilege is not itself a permissible purpose for meeting in executive session.  Instead, a public 
body may only enter executive session for one of the ten enumerated purposes set out in General 
Laws chapter 30A, section 21(a).  The Supreme Judicial Court has unequivocally held that the 
Open Meeting Law constitutes “a statutory public waiver of any possible privilege of the public 
client in meetings of governmental bodies except in the narrow circumstances stated in the 
statute.”  District Atty. for the Plymouth Dist. v. Bd. of Selectmen of Middleborough, 395 Mass. 
629, 634 (1985); accord Revere Retirement Bd. v. Attorney General, 93 Mass. App. Ct. 1117 
(2018) (Rule 1:28 Decision) (Further Appellate Review denied Sep. 13, 2018).  The protection of 
the attorney-client privilege is not a valid basis for holding a discussion in executive session that 
does not otherwise fall within one of the ten enumerated statutory purposes for executive session.  
See OML 2020-120; OML 2020-50.  We find here that the Commission’s discussions with legal 
counsel were not appropriate for executive session.  
 
The C
ommission’s other discussions during the executive session - reviewing two sets of 
executive session minutes and discussing whether the purpose for the executive sessions had 
concluded such that the minutes could be released – were appropriate for executive session under 
Purpose 7.  We have consistently explained that a public body may convene in executive session

reviewed or to Purpose 7, referencing the Open Meeting Law, G.L. c. 30A, §§ 22(f), (g).  See 
OML 2024-204; OML 2020-120; OML 2019-81; 2013-199.  The Commission explains that it 
included Purpose 6 on the notice as that was the original purpose for one of the executive

Although we find that the Commission’s review of the two sets of executive session 
minute
s was an appropriate discussion in executive session, we find that the executive session 
topic as listed on the meeting notice contained insufficient detail.  First, the notice did not 
identify the statute, i.e. the Open Meeting Law, that required its deliberations be kept 
confidential under Purpose 7, either in the meeting notice or in the announcement in open 
session.  See OML 2015-55.  Second, the notice did not identify that the Commission would 
review and approve executive session minutes nor did the notice specifically list the meeting 
dates of the minutes it planned to review for public disclosure.  See OML 2024-259.  As such, 
we find that the topic did not comply with the Open Meeting Law as it did not sufficiently 
inform the public that the Commission would be reviewing executive session minutes in 
executive session.  See 2013-199. 
 
CONCLUSION 
 
We find that the Commission vio lated the Open Meeting Law by discussing a matter in 
executive session during a meeting held on December 3, 2024, that was not appropriate for 
executive session.  Moreover, we find that the notice of the December 3, 2024, meeting was 
insufficiently specific.  We order immediate and future compliance with the law’s requirements, 
and we caution that similar future violations could be considered evidence of intent to violate the 
law.  In addition, we order the Commission, within 30 days of the date of this letter, to release 
the executive session minutes of the meeting held on December 3, 2024, in their entirety and to 
notify our office that it has done so.  The Commission may not redact or withhold the minutes in 
any way.  See G.L c. 30A, § 22(f) (the minutes of any executive session may be withheld from 
disclosure to the public as long as publication may defeat the lawful purposes of the executive 
session; provided, however, that the executive session was held in compliance with the Open 
Meeting Law); OML 2020-63; OML 2019-164; OML 2017-23; OML 2015-120.
