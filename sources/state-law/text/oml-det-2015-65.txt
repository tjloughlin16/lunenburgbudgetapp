This office received a complaint from Susan Rufo on March 4, 2015, alleging that the 
Boston Zoning Board of Appeals (the "Board") violated the Open Meeting Law, G.L. c. 30A, §§ 
18-25. Specifically, the complaint alleges that the Board failed to provide meeting minutes 
following a request and that the Board failed to create minutes for its June 5, 2012; September 
11, 2012; and January 22, 2013 meetings. The complaint was originally filed with the Board on 
February 6, 2015. Lhe Board's Executive Secretary, Derric Small,1 responded by letter dated 
March 25, 2015.2 
Following our review, we find that the Board violated the Open Meeting Law as alleged. 
In reaching this determination, we reviewed the complaint filed with the Board, the Board's 
response, and the complaint filed with our office. We also spoke with Mr. Small by telephone on 
May 4, 2015.

DISCUSSION 
1. The Board Failed to Create and Approve Minutes for Three Meetings. 
The Open Meeting Law requires a public body to "create and maintain accurate minutes 
of all meetings, including executive sessions, setting forth the date, time and place, the members 
present or absent, a summary of the discussions on each subject, a list of documents and other 
exhibits used at the meeting, the decisions made and the actions taken at each meeting, including 
the record of all votes." G.L. c. 30A, § 22(a). Minutes of all open sessions shall be created and 
approved in a timely manner. G.L. c. 30A, § 22(c). 
The Board violated the Open Meeting Law by failing to create meeting minutes for its 
June 5, 2012; September 11, 2012; and January 22, 2013 meetings. While we are advised that 
the Board creates written decisions of its hearings, the Open Meeting Law requires that minutes 
contain specific information, including a summary of the Board's discussions of each subject. 
G.L. c. 30A, § 22(a). The Board's written decisions do not satisfy the requirements for meeting 
minutes; thus we find that the Board violated the Open Meeting Law by not creating and 
approving minutes. 
Public bodies that conduct administrative hearings, such as the Board, may adopt 
individual or collective written decisions as minutes of a meeting, provided they include all 
required information as outlined in G.L. c. 30A, § 22(a). We understand that the Board plans to 
take this approach in the future. We invite the Board to contact our office if it has questions 
about how to comply with this aspect of the Open Meeting Law. 
Finally, we note that it appears that the Board has failed to create minutes that meet the 
requirements of the Open Meeting Law for any of its meetings. We strongly encourage the 
Board to create minutes for all of its meetings dating back to July 1, 2010, if it has not done so 
already.4

2. The Board Failed to Respond to Ms. Rufo's Request within the Timeframe Require by 
Law. 
The minutes of an open session, if they exist and whether approved or in draft form, shall 
be made available upon request by any person within 10 days. G.L. c. 30A, § 22(c). Ms. Rufo 
requested the minutes of three open session meetings of the Board on September 25, 2014. The 
Board did not respond to her request for minutes, despite her repeated requests, until it responded 
to her Open Meeting Law complaint on March 25, 2015, six months later. It is clear, therefore, 
that the Board violated the Open Meeting Law by failing to respond to a request for minutes 
within 10 days. G.L. c. BOA, § 22(c). 
CONCLUSION 
For the reasons stated above, we find that the Board violated the Open Meeting Law by 
failing to create and approve minutes for three meetings and by failing to provide minutes in 
response to a request. We order the Board's immediate and future compliance with the Open 
Meeting Law and caution the Board that a future similar violation may be considered evidence of 
an intentional violation of the Open Meeting Law. Additionally, we order the Board to create 
and approve minutes of its June 5, 2012; September 11, 2012; and January 22, 2013 meetings. 
This must occur within thirty (30) days of receipt of this letter. 
We now consider this matter closed. Please be advised that this letter does not resolve 
any other complaints that may be pending with this office or with the Board. Please feel free to 
contact our office at 617-963-2540 if you have any questions regarding this letter.
