RE: Open Meeting Law Complaint — Rehoboth Board of Selectmen 
Dear Mr. Ritter: 
This office received two complaints from Christopher Morra, on or about March 6, 2012, 
alleging that the Rehoboth Board of Selectmen (the "Board") violated the Open Meeting Law, 
G.L. c. 30A, §§ 18-25. The complaints allege that during the Board's December 19, 2011 
meeting, Selectman Joseph Tito "brought forward discussion to have streets accepted by the 
Town of Rehoboth. The matter was not on the agenda," and that Board Chair Michael Costello 
"brought forward discussion to have [Mr. Morra] removed as a member of the Rehoboth Finance 
Committee (Fin.Comm.) The matter had not been set out on the agenda, and [Mr. Morra] was 
given no notice the matter would be discussed." The complaints were filed with the Board on or 
about December 28, 2011. The Board responded to the complaints in two letters dated January 
24, 2012 
We appreciate the patience of the parties as we considered this matter. Following our 
review, we find that the Board did not violate the Open Meeting Law by discussing topics that 
were not listed in the notice for its December 19, 2011 meeting. In reaching this determination, 
we reviewed the two December 28, 2011 complaints filed with the Board; the Board's January 
24, 2012 responses; and the March 6, 2012 complaints filed with our office. We also reviewed 
the notices and minutes of the Board's December 19, 2011 and January 3, 2012 meetings. 
Finally, we reviewed correspondence between the Board and Mr. Morra dated December 22, 
2011; December 28, 2011; January 4, 2012; January 11, 2012; and January 27, 2012. 
FACTS 
At the time the complaints were filed, the Board consisted of three members: Chair 
Michael Costello, Joseph Tito, and Donald Leffort. On December 15, 2011, the Board posted

notice for its December 19, 2011 meeting. The notice included 19 topics, including "Other 
Business," which the notice described as "[a]ny other business that comes before Board." 
During the "Other Business" portion of the Board's December 19, 2011 meeting, Selectman 
Joseph Tito made a motion to "instruct Town Counsel to file at the Deeds the appropriate 
paperwork and titles for recent street acceptances approved at the November 7th  Special Town 
Meeting." The motion passed by a vote of 2-1. 
Later during the same meeting, under the topic of "Board of Selectmen Reports," Chair 
Costello reported that he attended a recent Finance Committee meeting and "was very concerned 
about the behavior of member Chris Mona. This individual has been involved with three (3) law 
suits against the Town one resulting in over a million dollar settlement which the town's 
insurance carrier had to pay." Rehoboth Board of Selectmen Meeting Minutes, December 19, 
2011. Chair Costello then made a motion to remove Mr. Mona from the Finance Committee and 
to rescind his appointment to that Committee. The motion passed by a vote of 2-1. 
On January 3, 2012, the Board met in open session. Under "New Business: Action Item 
#6: Discussion re. Finance Committee Appointment," the Board revisited its December 19, 2011 
vote to remove Mr. Mona from the Finance Committee. The minutes state that "[Selectman] 
Tito stated that he believed the previous vote of the Board to rescind the appointment of 
Christopher Mona to the Finance Committee was premature and that C. Morra should be invited 
to meet with the Board to discuss his appointment to the Finance Committee." The Board voted 
2-0-1 to "rescind the vote to remove C. Mona from the Finance Committee and in order to 
protect the Town against litigation the proper procedure must be followed." 
DISCUSSION 
The Open Meeting Law was enacted "to eliminate much of the secrecy surrounding 
deliberations and decisions on which public policy is based." Ghiglione v. School Committee of 
Southbridge, 376 Mass. 70, 72 (1978). The law requires that meetings of a public body be 
properly noticed and open to members of the public, unless an executive session is convened. 
See G.L. c. 30A, §§ 20(a)—(b), 21. Notice of every meeting must be posted "at least 48 hours 
prior to such meeting, excluding Saturdays, Sundays and legal holidays," and every notice shall 
include "the date, time and place of such meeting and a listing of topics that the chair reasonably 
anticipates will be discussed at the meeting." G.L. c. 30A, § 20(b). 
During the Board's December 19, 2011 meeting, Selectman Tito made a motion during 
the "Other Business" portion of the meeting to "instruct Town Counsel to file at the Deeds the 
appropriate paperwork and titles for recent street acceptances approved at the November 7th 
Special Town Meeting." The Board writes in its January 24, 2012 letter that "the Chair did not 
reasonably anticipate that the subject matter would be brought up by a member of the Board. 
Had the Chair known this was going to be brought up by, it would have been listed on the 
meeting notice." We credit that Board's assertion that the Chair did not reasonably anticipate the 
topic and the subsequent vote. A Chair is not required to list a topic in a notice that the he or she 
did not reasonably anticipate 48 hours before a meeting. 
The second vote taken at the December 19, 2011 meeting was proposed by the Chair 
during "Selectmen Reports." The Chair stated that he "was very concerned about the behavior of

member Chris Mona. This individual has been involved with three (3) law suits against the 
Town one resulting in over a million dollar settlement which the town's insurance carrier had to 
pay." The Chair then called a vote to remove Mr. Mona from his position with the Finance 
Committee. In the Board's January 24, 2012 letter in response to this complaint, the Board 
asserts that "the Chair did not reasonably anticipate that the subject would be brought up at the 
meeting when the meeting notice was being prepared. It was only very shortly prior to the 
meeting that the Chair considered bringing up this matter." We credit the Chair's assertion, but 
note that even if the Chair did anticipate discussion of this topic at least 48 hours' before its 
December 19, 2011 meeting, any violation of the Open Meeting Law was remedied when the 
Board rescinded its action against Mr. Mona during its January 3, 2012 meeting and placed the 
topic for discussion on subsequent meeting notices. 
We encourage the Board to postpone discussion of similar unanticipated topics in the 
future. When reasonable, the Board should wait to discuss items not included in the meeting 
notice until those topics can be listed in a meeting notice 48 hours prior to the meeting, with 
enough specificity to advise the public and Board members of the issues to be discussed during 
the meeting. See OML 2012-85; OML 2011-53. 1  
CONCLUSION 
For the reasons stated above, we find that the Board did not violate the Open Meeting 
Law.
