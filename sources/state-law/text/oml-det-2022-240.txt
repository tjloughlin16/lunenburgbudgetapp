This office received a complaint from Kimberly Wetherell on July 26, 2022, alleging that 
the Peru Select Board (the “Board”) violated the Open Meeting Law, G.L. c. 30A, §§ 18-25.  
The complaint was originally filed with the Board on or around June 21, 2022, and you 
responded on behalf of the board by letter dated June 29, 2022.
1  The complaint alleges that the 
Board failed to timely approve minutes of meetings held on September 20, September 27, and 
October 4, 2021. 
 
Following our review, we find that the Board violated the Open Meeting Law by failing 
to timely approve the meeting minutes at issue.  In reaching this determination we reviewed the 
original complaint, the Town Administrator’s

The Open Meeting Law requires that public bodies “create and maintain accurate minutes 
of all meetings.”  G.L. c. 30A, §22(a).  These minutes must be reviewed and approved by the 
public body in a timely manner.  G.L. c. 30A, §22(c).  “Timely manner” means “within the next 
three public body meetings or within 30 days, whichever is later, unless the public body can 
show good cause for further delay.”  940 CMR 29.11; see OML 2018-48.

In it
s response, the Board does not directly address the allegation that it failed to timely 
approve the September 20, September 27, and October 4, 2021, meeting minutes.  Based on our 
review of the Board’s meeting minutes, which generally are quite detailed and clearly identify 
which prior meeting minutes were reviewed and either tabled or approved at each meeting, we 
conclude that the Board’s October 4, 2021, meeting minutes were approved on November 29, 
2021, which was the Board’s fifth meeting and more than 30 days thereafter.  The Board’s 
September 13, 20, and 27, 2021, minutes were considered for review and approval at the Board’s 
November 15, 2021, meeting, but only the September 13, 2021, minutes were approved on that 
date.  Moreover, even if the September 20 and 27 minutes had been approved on November 15, 
2021, the approval would have been untimely as November 15, 2021, was more than three 
meetings and 30 days after the Board’s September 20 and 27, 2021, meetings.  We do not find a 
notation in meeting minutes indicating when the September 20 or 27 minutes were approved nor 
are these minutes dated, but the September 20, 2021, minutes are stamped as received by the 
Town Clerk on November 16, 2021, and the September 27, 2021, minutes are stamped as 
received by the Town Clerk on July 18, 2022; most other meeting minutes are stamped by the 
Town Clerk within a day of approval.  Furthermore, the Town Administrator provided draft 
minutes of the September 27 meeting to the Complainant in June 2022, thus suggesting that the 
minutes were not yet approved by that time.  Therefore, we conclude that the Board failed to 
timely approve all three sets of meeting minutes at issue in this complaint. 
 
For the reasons stated above, we find that the Board violated the Open Meeting Law by 
failing to timely approve meeting minutes.  We order the Board’s immediate and future 
compliance with the Open Meeting Law, and caution that a finding of a similar future violation 
may be considered evidence of an intent to violate the Law.
