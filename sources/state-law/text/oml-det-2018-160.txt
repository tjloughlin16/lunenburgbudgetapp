Dear Attorney Feodoroff:
This office received a complaint from Susan Spencer on August 23, 2018, alleging 
that the Uxbridge Board of Selectmen (the “Board”) violated the Open Meeting Law, G.L. c. 
30A, §§ 18-25. The complaint was originally filed with the Board on or about July 11,2018, 
and you responded to the complaint, on behalf of the Board, in a letter dated July 30, 2018. In 
her complaint, Ms. Spencer alleges that the Board convened an executive session on July 3, 
2018 for an improper purpose.
Following our review, we find that the Board did not violate the Open Meeting Law.
In reaching a determination, we reviewed the original complaint, the Board’s response to the 
complaint, and the complaint filed with our office requesting further review. We also 
reviewed the notice and open session minutes of the Board’s July 3, 2018 meeting.1 Finally, 
we spoke with you by telephone on December 6, 2018.

FACTS
We find the facts as follows. On May 22, the Board met with the Town Manager in 
executive session and took a vote to issue a preliminary resolution of removal. Following this 
meeting, counsel for the Town Manager sent a letter to the Board on June 4, 2018 regarding 
the Board’s action. In that letter, counsel stated:
By this declaration my client expressly reserves all her rights. Further, this request for 
hearing is filed without prejudice to or waiver of any such rights and nothing in this 
letter shall be construed in any manner to limit my client’s right to contest any actions 
of the Uxbridge Board of Selectmen taken on May 22, 2018 and/or thereafter, 
pursuant to the Uxbridge Charter; the Massachusetts Open Meeting Law; or the 
Massachusetts Commission Against Discrimination; and/or in any court of competent 
jurisdiction for violation of the Massachusetts Civil Rights Statutes and/or the 14th 
Amendment of the U.S. Constitution.
Upon receiving that letter and an Open Meeting Law complaint filed by the Town 
Manager, the Board convened in executive session under Purpose 3 on July 3 to discuss two 
related topics: (1) “Potential Litigation of Personnel Matters-Town Manager;” and (2) “Open 
Meeting Law Complaint dated June 8, 2018 filed by Angeline Ellison [the Town Manager].” 
During the executive session, the Board discussed the potential litigation consequences of the 
Board’s action, including potential risks and claims under the statutory provisions cited in 
counsel’s letter to the Board.
DISCUSSION
The Open Meeting Law was enacted “to eliminate much of the secrecy surrounding 
deliberation and decisions on which public policy is based.” Ghiglione v. School Board of 
Southbridge. 376 Mass. 70, 72 (1978). A public body may convene an executive session for 
any of ten purposes enumerated in the Open Meeting Law. See G.L. c. 30A, § 21(a). One 
permissible reason is “to discuss strategy with respect to collective bargaining or litigation if 
an open meeting may have a detrimental effect on the collective bargaining or litigating 
position of the public body and the chair so declares.” G.L. c. 30A, § 21(a)(3) (“Purpose 3”). 
The mere possibility of litigation is not sufficient to invoke executive session Purpose 3. See 
OML 2012-5; OML 2011-30.2 Litigation must be pending or clearly and imminently 
threatened or otherwise demonstrably likely. See Powers v. Freetown-Lakeville Reg’l Sch. 
Dist. Comm.. 392 Mass. 656, 659 (1984); OML 2017-121; OML 2012-43.
Here, the complaint alleges that the Board convened in executive session for an 
improper purpose because there was no litigation, threatened or otherwise. Based on our 
conversation with Board counsel, we conclude that it was appropriate for the Board to 
convene in executive session in response to a perceived threat of litigation by the Town 
Manager following the Board’s vote to issue a preliminary resolution of removal. See G.L. c.

30A, § 21(a)(3); OML 2017-176. The June 4, 2018 letter from the Town Manager’s attorney 
warned the Board that any decision to terminate her could result in a lawsuit. A reasonable 
person would read such a letter as threatening litigation. The Board thus convened in 
executive session to discuss the various potential litigation consequences should the Board 
proceed with its planned course of action. We therefore find that the Board properly met in 
executive session under Purpose 3 to discuss its litigation strategy with respect to this matter. 
See G.L. e. 30A, § 21(a)(3).
CONCLUSION
For the reasons stated above, we find that the Board did not violate the Open Meeting 
Law. We now consider the complaint addressed by this determination to be resolved. This 
determination does not address any other complaints that may be pending with our office or 
the Board. Please feel free to contact our office at (617) 963-2540 if you have any questions 
regarding this letter.
