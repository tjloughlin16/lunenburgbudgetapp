RE: Open Meeting Law Complaint  
 
Dear Attorney O’Keefe:  
 
This office received a complaint from Raquel Russell on March 6, 2026, alleging that the 
Falmouth School Committee (the “Committee”) violated the Open Meeting Law, G.L. c. 30A, §§ 
18-25.  The complaint originally was filed with the Committee on January 14, 2026.  You 
responded on behalf of the Committee by letter dated February 13, 2026.  The complaint alleges 
that the Committee: (1) took action without a lawful vote or authorization; and (2) deprived the 
public of its right to observe the decision-making process. 
 
We appreciate the patience of the parties while we reviewed this matter.  Following our 
review, we find that to the extent that the Committee violated the Open Meeting Law, that 
violation was the subject of our prior determination, OML 2026-2.1  In reaching this 
determination, we reviewed the complaint, the Committee’s response to the complaint, and the 
complainant’s request for further review.  We also reviewed additional documents forwarded to 
our office by the complainant, including minutes of the executive session the Committee held on 
May 27, 2025.  Finally, we communicated with the complainant via email. 
 
FACTS 
 
This is the second complaint that Raquel Russell has filed with our office relative to the 
Committee’s approval in 2025 of a six-year contract with the Falmouth Superintendent of 
Schools at a salary of $213,000 (the “Contract”).  We incorporate by reference the facts set forth 
in determination OML 2026-2.

Briefly, the chronology of the contract approval is as follows: 
 
May 13, 2025:  The Committee met in executive session and voted in favor of the

In OML 2026-2, we found that the Committee violated the Open Meeting Law when it 
signed the Contract prior to voting to approve it in open session.2  That determination was issued 
on January 2, 2026.  On January 30, 2026, the Committee released several sets of executive 
session minutes, including minutes of an executive session held on May 27, 2025.  Minutes of 
the May 27 executive session state that “[Committee Member] Kelly Welch shared draft notes 
for the Superintendent’s contract.  These notes include additions and deletions to the existing 
contract.  DISCUSSION.” 
 
DISCUSSION 
 
I. Allegations relative to approval of the Contract were resolved in OML 2026-2. 
 
The complainant maintains that the instant complaint is not a request for reconsideration 
of our findings in OML 2026-2 but instead alleges additional violations on the part of the 
Committee.  In support of this position, the complainant notes that minutes of the May 27, 2025, 
executive session were not available at the time that our office issued OML 2026-2 and that 
those minutes demonstrate that the Committee continued to discuss and finalize the Contract 
after it had already been executed.  According to the complainant, “[t]his confirms that the 
contract was not complete or approved at the time of execution and further demonstrates that no 
authorization to execute existed on May 20, 2025.” 
 
The allegation that the Committee did not follow the Open Meeting Law in its approval 
of the Contract was resolved in OML 2026-2.  We found in OML 2026-2 that the Committee 
violated the Open Meeting Law when it approved the Contract in executive session; this finding 
disposes of both allegations of the present complaint.  That the Committee continued to discuss, 
and seemingly revise, the Contract after it was executed does not form the basis of an additional 
violation.  If anything, the minutes of the May 27 executive session simply provide further 
support for the allegations that were the subject of OML 2026-2.  As to the allegation that the 
minutes of the May 27, 2025, executive session demonstrate that the Chair did not have authority 
to execute the Contract on May 20, 2025, we note that the Open Meeting Law does not govern 
the delegation of authority to execute a contract.

Committee violated the Open Meeting Law by meeting in executive session on May 27 to

II. We decline to review allegations arising out of meetings held after the filing of 
the complaint. 
 
As noted above, the complaint before us originally was filed with the Committee on 
January 14, 2026.  In subsequent communications with our office, the complainant alleges that 
the Committee violated the Open Meeting Law at meetings held after she filed this complaint 
and / or that minutes of these subsequent meetings4 provide further support of existing 
allegations.  We decline to review allegations that were not raised in the complaint filed with the 
public body.  See OML 2026-144 (declining to review allegation of insufficiency of notice 
where allegation was raised in complaint but details in support of allegation were provided after 
public body responded to complaint); OML 2026-111 (declining to review allegation of 
insufficiently detailed minutes first raised in request for review).  See also OML Declination 8-
06-25 (Middlefield Select Board); OML 2023-89.  If any such allegations would still be timely, 
the complainant may raise them in a separate complaint.5   
 
CONCLUSION 
 
For the reasons stated above, we find that the Committee did not violate the Open 
Meeting Law relative to execution of the Contract in any way other than what was already found 
in OML 2026-2.  We order no further remedial action by the Committee. 
 
We now consider the complaint addressed by this determination to be resolved.  This 
determination does not address any other complaints that may be pending with our office or the 
Committee.  Please feel free to contact the Division at (617) 963 - 2540 if you have any
