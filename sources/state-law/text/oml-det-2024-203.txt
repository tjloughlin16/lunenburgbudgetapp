 RE: Open Meeting Law Complaint 
 
Dear Mr. Burris: 
 
This office received a complaint from Patrick Higgins on September 1, 2024, alleging 
that the Westport Planning Board (the “Board”) violated the Open Meeting Law, G.L. c. 30A, §§ 
18-25.  The complaint was originally filed with the Board on or about July 18, 2024, and Board 
Chair James Whitin responded to the complaint, on behalf of the Board, by letter dated August 
14, 2024.
1  The complaint alleges that the Board created inaccurate minutes of the meeting held 
on June 18, 2024, by not including a list of documents or other exhibits used during the meeting. 
 
We resolve this matter by informal action, in accordance with 940 CMR 29.07(2)(a), 
after reviewing the original complaint, the Board’s response to the complaint, the complaint filed 
with our office requesting further review, and the minutes of the Board meeting held on June 18, 
2024.  We find that the Board violated the Open Meeting Law in the way alleged. 
 
The Open Meeting Law requires that a public body “create and maintain accurate 
minutes of all meetings, including executive sessions, setting forth the date, time and place, the 
members present or absent, a summary of the discussions on each subject, a list of documents 
and other exhibits used at the meeting, the decisions made and the actions taken at each meeting,

including the record of all votes.”  G.L. c. 30A, § 22(a).  The Board acknowledges, and we agree, 
that a list of documents or other exhibits used during the June 18, 2024, meeting was missing 
from the minutes.  Any documents that are physically present, verbally identified, and discussed 
during a meeting must be separately listed in the minutes.  See OML 2019-163; OML 2014-12; 
OML 2012-42.2  It is not enough that those documents are simply referenced in the body of the 
minutes or that the minutes include a digital link to the documents.  See OML 2019-29; OML 
2018-70.  The Board asserts that all required information will be included in minutes “going 
forward” and we commend the Board’s commitment to ensure future compliance.   
 
For the reasons stated above, we find that the Board violated the Open Meeting Law by 
creating insufficient minutes of the meeting held on June 18, 2024.  We order the Board to 
amend the minutes of the June 18, 2024, meeting in accordance with the guidance provided 
above, within 30 days of the date of this letter.  See 940 C.M.R. 29.07(4).  We also order the 
Board’s immediate and future compliance with the law’s requirements, and we caution that 
similar future violations could be considered evidence of intent to violate the law.   
 
We now consider the complaint addressed by this determination to be resolved.  This 
determination does not address any other complaints that may be pending with the Subcommittee 
or with our office.  Please feel free to contact our office at (617) 963-2540 if you have any 
questions regarding this letter.
