 RE: Open Meeting Law Complaint 
 
Dear Chair Swett: 
 
This office received a complaint from Nora Bicki (the “Complainant”) on June 1, 2026, 
alleging that the Wareham School Committee (the “Committee”) violated the Open Meeting 
Law, G.L. c. 30A, §§ 18-25. The complaint was originally filed with the Committee on April 13
1 
and you responded, on behalf of the Committee, by letter dated May 8.2 The complaint alleges 
that the Committee violated the Open Meeting Law by failing to timely approve and post 
minutes of meetings held between May 2025 and April 2026. 
 
Following our review, we find that the Committee violated the Open Meeting Law by 
failing to timely approve minutes for meetings held on January 15, February 5, February 26, 
March 5, and March 19. For the reasons explained below, we decline to review the remainder of 
the allegations raised in the complaint. In reaching this determination, we reviewed the Open 
Meeting Law complaint, the request for further review, and the Committee’s response to the 
complaint. We also reviewed relevant minutes of Committee meetings held in 2026.3

The Open Meeting Law requires that a public body create and maintain accurate minutes 
of all meetings. G.L. c. 30A, § 22(a). The Open Meeting Law further requires that open session 
minutes “be created and approved in a timely manner.” Id. at § 22(c). “Timely manner” means 
“within the next three public body meetings or within 30 days, whichever is later, unless the 
public body can show good cause for further delay.” See 940 CMR 29.11; OML 2018-48.4  
Whenever possible, we recommend that minutes of a meeting be approved at the next meeting.  
See OML 2018-67; OML 2017-133.  
 
The complaint alleges that the Committee failed to timely approve minutes of meetings 
held between May 2025 and April 2026. We decline to review whether all minutes of Committee 
meetings held during the eleven-month period between May 2025 and April 2026 have been 
timely approved as our office will not conduct broad audits of public bodies based on 
generalized allegations. See OML 2013-180; OML 2012-106. Nonetheless, we have reviewed 
this issue with respect to Committee meetings held in 2026 prior to April 13, the day the 
complaint was filed with the Committee. 
 
Here, the Committee held meetings on January 15, February 5, February 26, March 5, 
March 19 and April 2. The Committee approved the minutes of the January 15 and February 5 
meetings during a meeting held on May 7, which was the seventh and sixth meeting thereafter, 
respectively.
5 The Committee approved the minutes of the February 26, March 5, March 19, and 
April 2 during a meeting held on May 21, the sixth, fifth, fourth, and third subsequent meeting, 
respectively.6 We find that the Committee failed to timely approve the minutes of the meetings 
held on January 15, February 5, February 26, March 5, and March 19 as the minutes were not 
approved within the later of the next three meetings or 30 days following each meeting. We find 
that the minutes of the April 2 meeting were approved in a timely manner where the minutes 
were approved within three meetings as required by the Open Meeting Law. To the extent there 
are other meeting minutes that the Committee has not yet approved, we remind the Committee of 
its obligation to approve those minutes in a timely manner as well.
7 
 
Finally, the complaint alleges that the Committee violated the Town Charter by failing to 
post meeting minutes. The Division of Open Government’s review concerns compliance with the 
Open Meeting Law, G.L. c. 30A, §§ 18-25. The Division of Open Government does not interpret 
municipal charters or bylaws nor does it review allegations that a Committee violated the town 
charter. See OML Declination 10-3-2024 (Wareham Select Committee); OML 2013-41; OML 
2012-47. In addition, the Open Meeting Law does not require that public bodies post their 
minutes to a website or that minutes be submitted to the Town Clerk, but merely that meeting 
minutes be made available within 10 days of a request. See G.L. c. 30A, § 22(c); OML 2013-
103. Therefore, even if true, this allegation would not constitute a violation of the Open Meeting 
Law and we decline to review it.

For the reasons stated above, we find that the Committee violated the Open Meeting Law 
by failing to timely approve minutes of meetings held on January 15, February 5, February 26, 
March 5, and March 19. We order immediate and future compliance with the law’s requirements, 
and we caution that similar future violations could be considered evidence of intent to violate the 
law.  
 
We now consider the complaint addressed by this determination to be resolved.  This 
determination does not address any other complaints that may be pending with our office or the 
Committee.  Please feel free to contact our office at (617) 963-2540 if you have any questions 
regarding this letter.
