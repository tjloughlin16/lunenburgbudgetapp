 RE: Open Meeting Law Complaint 
 
Dear Attorney Assad: 
 
This office received three complaints from Collin Dias, one on June 6, 2025, and two on 
July 21, 2025,  alleging that the Fall River School Committee (the “School Committee”) and the 
Fall River Public Schools Superintendent of Schools Search Committee (the “Search 
Committee”) violated the Open Meeting Law, G.L. c. 30A, §§ 18-25.  In the first complaint, 
originally filed with the Search Committee on May 6, 2025, Mr. Dias alleges that the Search 
Committee failed to respond within the time allowed to a request for minutes he sent on April 25, 
2025.  You responded to the first complaint on May 23, 2025.  The other two complaints were 
originally filed (one with the Search Committee and the other with the School Committee) on 
May 23, 2025.  In these two complaints, Mr. Dias alleges that the School Committee failed to 
timely respond to a request made to the School Committee for minutes of Search Committee 
meetings and that minutes of Search Committee meetings produced in response to his requests 
were improperly redacted and were not sufficiently detailed.  You initially responded to these 
two later complaints on June 18, 2025 and supplemented the responses on June 25, 2025.     
 
Following our review, we find that the School Committee violated the Open Meeting 
Law by failing to respond to a request for minutes within the time allowed under the Law.  We 
decline to review the remaining allegations of the complaints.  In reaching this determination, we 
reviewed the Open Meeting Law complaints, the School Committee’s responses and the requests 
for further review.  We also reviewed minutes of the meetings the Search Committee held on 
April 24, 2024, May 2, 2024 (redacted), May 7, 2024 (redacted), May 9, 2024, and May 13, 
2024.  Finally, we communicated via email with you and with the complainant.

FACTS 
 
We find the facts as follows.  At all relevant times, the complainant was a member of the 
School Committee but not the Search Committee.  The Search Committee met in April and May 
2024 to screen candidates for the position of Superintendent of Schools.  On May 13, 2024, it 
voted to recommend four candidates to the School Committee for the latter’s consideration.  On 
August 22, 2024, members of the School Committee, including the complainant, were sent 
redacted minutes of all meetings held by the Search Committee. 
 
On April 25, 2025, the complainant directed a request to the Search Committee for 
minutes of open and executive sessions it held in 2024.  On May 6, 2025, the complainant 
directed a request to the School Committee for the same Search Committee minutes.  On May 
23, 2025, in response to the complainant’s request, the School Committee sent the complainant 
minutes of executive sessions the Search Committee held on April 24, 2024, May 2, 2024, May 
7, 2024, May 9, 2024, and May 13, 2024, some of which contained redactions. 
 
DISCUSSION 
 
I. The School Committee violated the Open Meeting Law when it failed to respond 
to a request for minutes of the Search Committee within ten days. 
 
The Open Meeting Law requires that “[t]he minutes of an open session, if they exist and 
whether approved or in draft form, ... be made available upon request by any person within 10 
days.”  G.L. c. 30A, § 22(c).  The Open Meeting Law also provides that “[u]pon request by any 
person to inspect or copy the minutes of an executive session or any portion thereof, the body 
shall respond to the request within 10 days following receipt and shall release any [non-exempt] 
minutes ....”  G.L. c. 30A, § 22(g)(2).  If the public body has recently performed a review of the 
requested executive session minutes, then it should provide any minutes it determined should be 
released and explain that the remaining minutes continue to be withheld.  See OML 2024-168; 
OML 2013-105; OML 2013-99.1  Where a Search Committee has been disbanded prior to 
service of a request for its minutes, we have required the parent body to respond to the request 
within the time allowed under the Open Meeting Law.  See OML 2024-168 (finding that a 
school committee failed to timely respond to a request made to the school committee for, among 
other things, minutes of the school committee’s superintendent search committee); see also, 
OML 2016-96 (ordering parent public body to approve the minutes of its subcommittee); OML 
2017-34 (“where a parent public body has sufficient connection to or control over a subordinate 
public body, such as a subcommittee or a preliminary screening committee, we conclude that it is 
a permissible practice for one public body to approve the minutes of another public body.”). 
 
There is no dispute that the School Committee did not respond to the complainant’s 
request for minutes within ten days.  The School Committee argues, however, that the 
complainant had already received the minutes on August 22, 2024, relieving it of the obligation 
to respond within the time allowed under the Law.  We disagree.  The requirements of the Open 
Meeting Law remained even though the complainant received copies of the minutes previously

II. We decline to review the allegation that minutes were improperly redacted. 
 
Mr. Dias complains that when he finally received the minutes, they were improperly 
redacted.  When the purpose for which a valid executive session was held has been served, the 
minutes, preparatory materials and documents and exhibits of the session must be disclosed 
unless the attorney-client privilege or one or more exemptions under the Public Records Law, 
G.L. c. 4, § 7, apply to withhold these records, or any portion thereof, from disclosure.  G.L. c. 
30A, § 22(f).  Even when the Open Meeting Law no longer provides a basis for withholding 
executive session minutes, the Public Records Law may authorize a public body to withhold or 
redact the minutes at issue.  See OML 2021-143; OML 2019-143.  We do not make a 
determination whether redactions made under the Public Records Law were proper.  We refer the 
complainant to the Supervisor of Records in the Office of the Secretary of the Commonwealth 
for a determination as to whether the minutes may continue to be redacted pursuant to a Public 
Records Law exemption.  See OML 2015-170. 
 
Regardless of the requirements of the Public Records Law, the complainant argues that 
the Open Meeting Law prohibits redaction of the minutes because the Search Committee failed 
to follow procedure when entering executive session.  According to Mr. Dias, “[n]o valid 
executive [s]ession for all of the 2024 [Search Committee] meetings were held in accordance 
with the Open Meeting Law” because the Search Committee failed to announce whether it would 
reconvene in open session following executive session, and because it failed to make a 
declaration that a discussion in open session would have a detrimental effect in obtaining 
qualified applicants.

Even if the propriety of the redactions were within our purview, the complainant first received 
redacted copies of these Search Committee minutes on August 22, 2024 and any allegation 
arising out of the redactions were discoverable at that time.  Because these complaints were filed 
on May 23, 2025, they are untimely as to any allegation about the redactions. 
 
Although we decline to review this allegation, we take this opportunity to clarify the 
impact of a failure to make required announcements, e.g. whether the public body will reconvene 
in open session, prior to entering executive session.  It is true that when a public body discusses a 
topic that is not appropriate for executive session, minutes of the executive session may not be 
withheld or redacted.  See OML 2019-150; OML 2018-49; OML 2014-69; OML 2012-71.  
Failure to follow procedure when entering executive session for a proper purpose does not 
invariably result in the invalidation of the executive session, however.  See, e.g., OML 2025-165; 
OML 2017-69.   
 
III. We decline to review the allegation that minutes of the Search Committee’s 
executive sessions were not sufficiently detailed. 
 
Public bodies are required to “create and maintain accurate minutes of all meetings, 
including executive sessions.”  G.L. c. 30A, § 22(a); 940 CMR 29.11(1).  The minutes must 
include “the date, time and place, the members present or absent, a summary of the discussions 
on each subject, a list of documents and other exhibits used at the meeting, the decisions made 
and the actions taken at each meeting, including the record of all votes.”  G.L c. 30A, § 22(a).  
Where a public body has conducted interviews, minutes must summarize the substance of the 
interviews, including a summary of the questions asked and the responses given.  See OML 
2024-25; OML 2023-198; OML 2022-17; OML 2015-49.  A document is “used” at a meeting 
when, at a minimum, it is physically present, verbally identified, and its contents are discussed.  
See OML 2025-210; OML 2024-115; OML 2017-169.   
 
Mr. Dias alleges that minutes received on May 23 in response to his request were 
insufficient because they did not include summaries of interviews of lists of documents used. 
However, the complainant first received copies of these Search Committee minutes on August 
22, 2024.  Any deficiencies in the minutes were discoverable at that time.  Because these 
complaints were filed on May 23, 2025, they are untimely as to this allegation and we decline to 
review them. 
 
CONCLUSION 
 
For the reasons stated above, we find that the School Committee violated the Open 
Meeting Law by failing to respond to a request for minutes within the time allowed under the 
Law.  We order immediate and future compliance with the Open Meeting Law.
