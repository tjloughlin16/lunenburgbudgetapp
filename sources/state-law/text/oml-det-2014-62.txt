RE: Open Meeting Law Complaint 
Dear Attorney Kelly: 
This office received a complaint from Barbara Trainor-Tessier dated March 11, 2014, 
alleging that the Cotuit Board of Fire Commissioners (the Board) violated the Open Meeting 
Law, G.L. c. 30A, §§ 18-25. The complaint was originally filed with the Board on or about 
February 20, 2014, and Board Chair Ronald Mycock responded to the complaint by letter dated 
February 26, 2014.' The complaint alleges that the minutes from the November 21, 2013 and 
December 19, 2013 Board meetings are inaccurate and lack sufficient detail." 
We reviewed the February 20, 2014 complaint; Chair Mycock's response; and the March 
11, 2014 complaint filed with our office, requesting further review. Following our review, we 
find that the Board did not violate the Open Meeting Law as alleged, however the Board violated 
the Open Meeting Law by failing to approve its November 21, 2013 meeting minutes in a timely 
manner. We do not find this violation to be intentional, however, and we commend the Board 
for taking appropriate remedial action in response to the complaint.

FACTS 
We find the facts as follows. The Board posted a draft version of the minutes from its 
November 21, 2013 meeting to the Cotuit Fire District website on December 31, 2013. In 
January 2014, Ms. Trainor-Tessier obtained copies of certain meeting minutes, including the 
November 21, 2013 draft meeting minutes. Generally, the Board meets on a monthly basis and 
approves the minutes from the Board's prior meeting at that time. Here, however, the minutes 
were not ready in time for approval at its next meeting, held in December 2013. It was not until 
after the filing of this complaint, and upon further inquiry by our office in April 2014, that the 
Board realized the November 21, 2013 meeting minutes had not been approved. Accordingly, 
the Board approved the minutes at its May 22, 2014 meeting. 
DISCUSSION 
The Open Meeting Law requires that a public body "create and maintain accurate 
minutes of all meetings, including executive sessions, setting forth the date, time and place, the 
members present or absent, a summary of the discussions on each subject, a list of documents 
and other exhibits used at the meeting, the decisions made and the actions taken at each meeting, 
including the record of all votes." G.L. c. 30A, § 22(a). The minutes should include sufficient 
detail and accuracy so that a member of the public who did not attend the meeting could read the 
minutes and have a clear understanding of what actually occurred. OML 2013-157; OML 2013-
16.3 
While a draft version of the Board's November 21, 2013 meeting minutes was posted on 
the Cotuit Fire District website on December 31, 2013, those minutes had not been approved as 
of the filing of this complaint. A public body may revise draft minutes at any point prior to their 
approval to correct inaccuracies or add detail. Accordingly, because these minutes remained in 
draft form at the time of this complaint, we find that the Board did not violate the Open Meeting 
Law by creating inaccurate or insufficiently detailed minutes. See OML 2014-13; OML 2014-7. 
However, we find that the Board failed to approve its November 21, 2013 meeting 
minutes in a timely manner because it did not approve those minutes until six months after that 
meeting. Meeting minutes must be reviewed and approved in a timely manner. G.L. c. 30A, § 
22(c). Although the Open Meeting Law does not define "timely manner," we recommend that 
minutes of a meeting be approved at the next meeting, whenever possible. OML 2013-31; OML 
2012-91. While the Board generally follows this recommendation, it appears that the Board 
inadvertently overlooked the approval of this particular set of minutes. We therefore do not find 
this violation to be intentional, and we commend the Board for approving the minutes upon 
learning of this oversight. We find that this was appropriate remedial action, and order no 
additional relief.4

CONCLUSION 
For the reasons stated above, we find that the Board violated the Open Meeting Law by 
failing to approve its November 21, 2013 meeting minutes in a timely manner. Because the 
Board took appropriate remedial action, we order no additional relief. 
We now consider the complaint addressed by this determination to be resolved. This 
determination does not address any other complaints that may be pending with our office or the 
Board. Please feel free to contact our office at (617) 963-2540 if you have any questions.
