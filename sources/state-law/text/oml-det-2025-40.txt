RE: Open Meeting Law Complaint  
 
Dear Attorney Castro:  
 
This office received a complaint from Anthony Fyden on January 9, 2025, alleging that 
the Hadley Select Board (the “Board”) violated the Open Meeting Law, G.L. c. 30A, §§ 18-25.  
The complaint was originally filed with the Board on December 4, 2024, and you responded on 
behalf of the Board by letter dated December 23.1  The complainant alleges that Article 11.2 of 
the Town Meeting Warrant mischaracterized a solar engineering design plan as a “Solar Project 
Study.”  He further alleges that notice of the Board’s November 6 meeting was insufficiently 
specific due to this alleged mischaracterization.     
 
Following our review, we find that the Board did not violate the Open Meeting Law as 
alleged.  In reaching this determination, we reviewed the original complaint, the Board’s 
response to the complaint, notice and minutes of the November 6 meeting, and the complainant’s 
request for further review. 
  
FACTS 
  
We find the facts as follows.  The Board posted notice of a meeting to be held on 
November 6.  The notice included as a topic “Special Town Meeting Public Forum” with the 
notation “The Select Board will present the Special Town Meeting Warrant to the public.”  Both 
the full Special Town Meeting Warrant and a power point slide show were available via links 
within the notice.  The meeting took place on November 6, as scheduled.  The Board presented

DISCUSSION 
  
I. We decline to review allegations regarding administration of the Special Town 
Meeting or regarding the sufficiency of the Special Town Meeting Warrant. 
 
The Division of Open Government’s review concerns compliance with the Open Meeting 
Law, G.L. c. 30A, §§ 18-25.  The Open Meeting Law does not govern procedures for Town 
Meeting.  The Open Meeting Law’s definition of “meeting” specifically excludes “a session of a 
town meeting convened under section 9 of chapter 39 which would include the attendance by a 
quorum of a public body at any such session.”  The Attorney General has interpreted the 
exemption in the definition of “meeting” to mean that the Open Meeting Law does not reach any 
aspect of Town Meeting.  See OML Declination 3-20-25 (Swansea Town Moderator); OML 
Declination 7-10-23 (West Boylston Semi-Annual Town Meeting); OML Declination 8-1-22 
(Adams Fire District).2  Town Meetings, as well as the preparation and issuance of the Warrant, 
are subject to other legal requirements.  See, e.g., G.L. c. 39, §§ 9, 10.  Because the allegations 
regarding the Special Town Meeting and its Warrant, even if true, would not constitute violations 
of the Open Meeting Law, we decline to review these allegations.  We offer no opinion on 
whether any actions taken by the Town or the Select Board in relation to the Special Town 
Meeting could be a violation of any other law, regulation, or local rule outside the scope of the 
Division’s review.   
 
II. Notice of the November 6 Board Meeting was sufficiently specific. 
 
Pursuant to the Open Meeting Law, “[e]xcept in an emergency ... a public body shall post 
notice of every meeting at least 48 hours prior to such meeting, excluding Saturdays, Sundays 
and legal holidays.”  G.L. c. 30A, § 20(b).  The notice must be “printed in a legible, easily 
understandable format and shall contain the date, time and place of such meeting and a listing of 
topics that the chair reasonably anticipates will be discussed at the meeting.”  Id.   
 
We generally consider a topic to be sufficiently specific when a reasonable member of 
the public could read the topic and understand the anticipated nature of the public body’s 
discussion.  See OML 2023-199; OML 2023-3; OML 2019-134.  The Open Meeting Law 
requires that the notice describe topics with sufficient specificity so that the public can make an 
informed decision whether to attend the meeting in order to observe the discussion regarding a 
topic of interest.  See OML 2023-199; OML 2023-86; OML 2019-95.  When reviewing a 
meeting notice for sufficiency, unless it is clearly insufficient on its face, we do not review a 
meeting notice standing alone.  See OML 2024-155; OML 2021-89; OML 2015-101.  Rather, we 
review what was actually discussed at the meeting to determine if the notice was sufficiently 
specific to make the public aware of the discussion topics.  See OML 2024-155; OML 2017-141; 
OML 2015-101.

To the extent that the complaint alleges that notice of the Board’s meeting on November 
6 was not sufficiently specific because it listed as a topic “Special Town Meeting Public Forum” 
without reference to Article 11.2 (“Solar Project Study”), we find no violation of the Open 
Meeting Law.  Where a public body anticipates limiting its discussion to specific Town Meeting 
warrant articles, it must identify those articles in its meeting notice.  See OML 2024-64; OML 
2013-8; OML 2011-7.  However, when a public body anticipates it will review all articles of a 
Town Meeting Warrant, it is not required to identify all warrant articles on its 
meeting notice.  See OML 2024-74; OML 2024-64; OML 2017-185; OML 2012-83.  Notice of 
the Board’s November 6 meeting indicated that the Board intended to review all articles of the 
Town Meeting Warrant.  At the November 6 meeting, it did just that.  Because the Board’s 
review was of the warrant as a whole, we find that the notice of the November 6 meeting was 
sufficiently specific, and the Board did not violate the Open Meeting Law.  To the extent that the 
complaint alleges that notice of the Board’s November 6 meeting did not adequately describe 
what was to be voted on at Town Meeting, we decline to review that allegation for the reasons 
set forth in I, above. 
 
Although we find no violation, we note that the format of the Board’s meeting notice 
contained superfluous language and multiple fonts, making it more difficult to read.  For 
example, each topic repeated “Meeting: Nov 6, 2024 – Select Board Meeting Agenda” and 
included a “Category,” which repeated the large font topic heading, and “Type” such as “action.”  
We recommend that the Board consider adopting a more streamlined format which meets the 
requirements of the Open Meeting Law without containing unnecessary or confusing verbiage.  
Additionally, the Board is reminded that “[t]he date and time that a meeting notice is posted shall 
be conspicuously recorded thereon or therewith.”  940 CMR 29.03(1)(d). 
 
CONCLUSION 
  
For the reasons stated above, we find that the Board did not violate the Open Meeting 
Law as alleged.  We now consider the complaint addressed by this determination to be resolved.  
This determination does not address any other complaints that may be pending with our office or 
the Board.  Please feel free to contact the Division at (617) 963 - 2540 if you have any questions.
