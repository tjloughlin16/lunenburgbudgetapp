 RE:        Open Meeting Law Complaint 
 
Dear Attorney Feher: 
 
This office received a complaint from Patrick Higgins on March 8, 2022, alleging that the 
Somerset Board of Assessors (the “Board”) violated the Open Meeting Law, G.L. c. 30A, §§ 18-
25.  The complaint was originally filed with the Board on or about December 16, 2021, and you 
responded to the complaint, on behalf of the Board, by letter dated December 23, 2021.  The 
complaint alleges that the Board violated the Open Meeting Law by failing to include sufficient 
detail on the notice for the Board meeting held on December 8, 2021.   
 
We appreciate the patience of the parties while we reviewed this matter.  We resolve this 
matter by informal action, in accordance with 940 CMR 29.07(2)(a), after reviewing the 
original complaint, the Board’s response to the complaint, the complaint filed with our office 
requesting further review, and the notice and minutes of the Board meeting held on December 8, 
2021, as well as the minutes and video recording of the meeting of the Board of Selectmen held 
on December 8, 2021.
1  We find that the Board violated the Open Meeting Law by not including 
sufficient detail with respect to one of the topics listed on the notice. 
 
The Open Meeting Law requires that public bodies post notice of every meeting “at least 
48 hours prior to such meeting” and that every notice include “the date, time and place of such 
meeting and a listing of topics that the chair reasonably anticipates will be discussed at the 
meeting.”  G.L. c. 30A, § 20(b).  Public bodies are required to list topics on a notice with

notice for sufficiency, unless it is clearly insufficient on its face, we do not review a meeting 
notice standing alone.  See OML 2015-109; OML 2014-155.  Rather, we review what was 
actually discussed at the meeting to determine if the notice was sufficiently detailed to make the 
public aware of the discussion topics. 
 
On December 3, 2021, at 3 P.M., the Board posted notice of a meeting to be held on 
December 8, 2021, at 3:30 P.M.  The notice listed five topics, including “Review/sign routine 
bills if necessary,” “Review sign monthly reports,” and “Discuss/review scenario work sheet.”  
On December 6, 2021, at 2 P.M., the Board of Selectmen posted notice of a meeting to be held 
on December 8, 2021, at 5 P.M.  The notice listed 13 topics, including “Tax Classification with 
Board of Assessors.” 
 
The December 8, 2021, meeting was held as planned and the Board briefly reviewed and 
signed two bills and four motor vehicle excise reports.  The Board then discussed tax 
classification rates for residential and commercial real estate.  Immediately following the Board 
meeting, the Chair of the Board presented the tax classification rates at the Board of Selectmen 
meeting, which began after the conclusion of the Board meeting at 5 P.M. on December 8, 2021.  
 
We find that the Board violated the Open Meeting Law by failing to include on the notice 
any specific details regarding the “scenario worksheet” that was discussed during the December 
8, 2021, meeting.  See OML 2021-36; OML 2018-96; OML 2017-33.  The Board maintains that 
the Chair did not reasonably anticipate “the specific nature of the information and discussion 
regarding the scenario worksheet” because the worksheet was to be “presented by the Town 
Accountant.”  Although we have previously found that a notice did not lack specificity when it 
included as a topic a report or presentation by somebody who is not a member of the public body 
(such as “Administrator’s Report” or “Report from Department Heads”), and a public body need 
not list each and every detail about a topic to comply with the Law’s specificity requirement, we 
find here that the Chair must have anticipated at a minimum that the Town Accountant would 
present the tax rate classifications where the Chair was due to present such tax classifications to 
the Board of Selectmen at their meeting, which directly followed the Board meeting.  See OML 
2019-83; OML 2018-33; OML 2012-19 (best practice dictates that public body chairs solicit 
from department heads a more detailed set of topics and include on the meeting notice additional 
specificity regarding the precise matters to be discussed); see also OML 2021-114; OML 2018-
22; OML 2016-171 (notice need not include every detail about an anticipated discussion).  
Moreover, the notice for the Board of Selectmen meeting included the specific topic of “Tax 
Classification with Board of Assessors” and was posted 48 hours prior to the Board meeting.  As 
such, we find that the Board’s notice should have included additional details about the 
worksheet, including, at the very least, that the Town Accountant would be the individual 
presenting the worksheet.  This additional detail would have provided members of the public

With respect to the other two topics relating to bills and monthly reports listed on the 
Board’s notice, we have found that payroll approval and approval of expenses are standard 
administrative tasks that municipal governments engage in.  Therefore, descriptions such as 
“review and approve” payroll or vendor warrants are sufficiently detailed to reasonably inform 
the public of what will occur at the meeting where the nature of these matters do not require 
discussion and are perfunctory administrative tasks.  See OML 2019-69.   
 
For the reasons stated above, we find that the Board violated the Open Meeting Law by 
failing to list on the notice for its December 8, 2021, meeting sufficient detail.  We order the 
Board’s immediate and future compliance with the Open Meeting Law, and caution that future 
similar violations may be considered evidence of intent to violate the law. 
 
We now consider the complaint addressed by this determination to be resolved.  This
