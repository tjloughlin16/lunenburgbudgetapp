RE: Open Meeting Law Complaint 
Dear Attorney Pucci: 
This office received two complaints on March 11, 2014, one from Eileen Murphy and 
another from Maureen Murphy (the "complainants"), alleging that the Nantucket Historic 
District Commission (the "Commission") violated the Open Meeting Law, G.L. c. 30A, §§ 18-
25.' Both complaints allege that the Commission failed to properly notice its February 6, 2014 
meeting and failed to create minutes of that meeting. The complaints were filed with the 
Commission on or about March 8, 2014. The Commission responded to both complaints in a 
letter from counsel dated March 26, 2014. 
In reaching a determination, we reviewed both March 8, 2014 complaints filed with the 
Commission; the March 11, 2014 complaints filed with our office; and the Commission's March 
26, 2014 response. Additionally, we reviewed the notices for the Commission's March 11, 2014 
and March 13, 2014 meetings; the minutes of the Commission's February 18, 2014 and March 
13, 2014 meetings; and email correspondence between Commission Chair Linda Williams, 
Commission members, the complainants, and the Town Clerk's Office, provided to us by the 
Town Clerk's Office and the complainants. Finally, we spoke via telephone with Chair Williams 
and the Nantucket Deputy Director of Planning, Leslie Snell, on June 4, 2014. 
Following our review, we find that the Commission violated the Open Meeting Law by 
failing to create minutes of its February 6, 2014 meeting and by failing to post notice for that 
meeting. We do not find this was an intentional violation, and we commend the Commission for 
taking some remedial action upon learning of its notice posting error.

FACTS 
On January 31, 2014, James Grieder, the Commission's Assistant Administrator, emailed 
notices for the Commission's Tuesday, February 4, 2014 and Thursday, February 6, 2014 
meetings to the Town Clerk's Office for posting. Upon receiving the notices, a member of the 
Town Clerk's staff asked Mr. Grieder if the most recent notice was the one the Commission 
wanted posted. Mr. Grieder replied that he believed so. The clerk, understanding that Mr. 
Grieder was requesting that only the February 4, 2013 notice be posted, then posted only that 
notice and sent the Commission a confirmation that the meeting had been posted. 
On February 6, 2014, the Commission held a meeting. Because no notice had been 
posted for this meeting, the Town Minutes Taker was unaware of the meeting and did not attend. 
As it was not unusual for the Town Minutes Taker to miss meetings, the Commission members 
did not infer any lack of notice from her absence. No Commission member took minutes of the 
meeting, though the meeting was video recorded. On February 7, 2014, the Town Minutes Taker 
informed the Commission that no notice had been posted for the Commission's February 6, 2014 
meeting. 
During the next meeting of the Commission, on February 18, 2014, the Commission 
attempted to remedy the lack of notice for its February 6, 2014 meeting by reaffirming the 
decisions made and votes taken during that meeting. 
DISCUSSION 
1. The Commission Failed to Post Notice for its February 6, 2014 meeting. 
The Open Meeting Law requires a public body to post notice at least 48 hours in advance 
of a meeting. G.L. c. 30A, § 20(b). For meetings of a local public body, the notice must be 
"filed with the municipal clerk and posted in a manner conspicuously visible to the public at all 
hours in or on the municipal building in which the clerk's office is located." G.L. c. 30A, § 
20(c). 
Due to a misunderstanding, no notice was posted for the Commission's February 6, 2014 
meeting. While this was a violation of the Open Meeting Law, we do not find that this violation 
was intentional. Notice was timely submitted to the Town Clerk, but was not posted due to a 
miscommunication. The Commission did not discover the error until after the meeting had 
occurred. Upon discovering the error, the Commission reaffirmed all decisions made and votes 
taken during the un-posted meeting at a subsequent meeting, held on February 18, 2014. While 
we commend the Commission for attempting to remedy this violation, we nevertheless find that 
the action taken at the February 18, 2014 meeting was not wholly sufficient to address the 
violation. See Pearson v. Board of Selectmen of Longmeadow, 49 Mass. App. Ct. 119, 125 
(2000), citing Tolar v. School Bd. of Liberty County, 398 So.2d 427, 429 (Fla. 1981) (In order to 
cure a violation, the public body must take an independent, deliberative action, and not merely 
engage in a ceremonial acceptance or perfunctory ratification of a secret decision).

2. The Commission Failed to Create Minutes of its February 6. 2014 Meeting. 
The Open Meeting Law requires that a public body "create and maintain accurate 
minutes of all meetings, including executive sessions, setting forth the date, time and place, the 
members present or absent, a summary of the discussions on each subject, a list of documents 
and other exhibits used at the meeting, the decisions made and the actions taken at each meeting, 
including the record of all votes." G.L. c. 30A, § 22(a). 
The Commission has acknowledged that it failed to create minutes for its February 6, 
2014 meeting, and thus violated the law. We remind the Commission that the Open Meeting 
Law requires public bodies to create minutes for all meetings. G.L. c. 30A, § 22(a). Minutes 
must include sufficient detail and accuracy so that a member of the public who did not attend the 
meeting could read the minutes and have a clear understanding of what happened. See OML 
2014-54; OML 2013-157.2 
We find that the Commission violated the Open Meeting Law by both failing to post 
notice for its February 6, 2014 meeting and by failing to create minutes for that meeting. We 
order the Commission's immediate and future compliance with the Open Meeting Law. 
Additionally, we order the Commission to create and approve minutes of its February 6, 2014 
meeting. The Commission must provide our office with a copy of the final, approved minutes 
within thirty (30) days after receipt of this letter. 
We now consider the complaints addressed by this determination to be resolved. This 
determination does not address any other complaints that may be pending with the Commission 
or with our office. Please feel free to contact our office at (617) 963-2540 if you have any 
questions regarding this letter.
