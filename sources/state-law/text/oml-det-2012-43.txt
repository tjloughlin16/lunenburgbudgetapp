This office received an Open Meeting Law complaint from Gary Lopez on December 2, 
2011, alleging that the Barnstable Conservation Commission (the "Commission") violated the 
Open Meeting Law, G.L. c. 30A, §§ 18-25. The complaint specifically alleges that the 
Commission's "July 19, 2011 executive session was unlawful," and that the "resolve approved in 
the secret session that was approved without public comment and public debate on September 
27, 2011 was equally unlawful." The complaint was filed with the Commission on October 20, 
2011. The Commission responded to the complaint in a letter dated November 16, 2011. 
We find that the Commission did not violate the Open Meeting Law by entering into 
executive session on July 19, 2011, or by approving a resolution on September 27, 2011 that was 
drafted and considered by the Commission during its July 19, 2011 executive session. We do 
find, however, that the minutes of the Commission's July 19, 2011 executive session do not 
comply with the Open Meeting Law's requirement that minutes contain a summary of the 
discussions on each subject. 
In reaching our determination, we reviewed the October 20, 2011 complaint filed with 
the Commission; the Commission's November 16, 2011 response; and the December 2, 2011 
complaint filed with our office. We also reviewed the open and executive session minutes from 
the Commission's July 19, 2011 meeting, and the open session minutes of the Commission's 
September 27, 2011 meeting. Additionally, we reviewed letters, provided by the Commission, 
from Mr. Patrick Page to the Commission and to the Town Attorney's Office, dated January 11, 
2011; February 25, 2011; March 22, 2011; August 4, 2011; and September 15, 2011. We also 
spoke with you by telephone on December 28, 2011 regarding the July 19, 2011 executive

FACTS 
The Conservation Commission was approached several times by Barnstable resident 
Patrick Page, who asked the Commission to take action with respect to decisions it had made 
regarding his property. Mr. Page attended several meetings, including one on March 1, 2011 
during which Mr. Page threatened to sue the Commission and its members. Additionally, Mr. 
Page sent the Town of Barnstable letters on January 11, 2011, February 25, 2011, and March 22, 
2011 threatening to sue the Town and seeking $30,000,000 in damages. In these letters, Mr. 
Page offered to settle his claims for $3,000,000. He also called the Town Attorney's Office 
numerous times demanding payment of the proposed settlement amount. 
Although Mr. Page had no application before the Commission, the Commission 
Chairman decided to take action in an effort to help Mr. Page move forward with projects on his 
property. However, because of Mr. Page's consistent threat of litigation and demand for 
settlement, the Commission felt it needed to meet with the Town Attorney prior to taking any 
action with respect to Mr. Page's property. The Commission convened in open session on July 
19, 2011 and voted to enter executive session "to discuss pending litigation," under a topic listed 
in the meeting notice as "litigation strategy." The Commission met with the Town Attorney to 
discuss the legal consequences of a specific resolution proposed by the Chairman. The 
Commission crafted specific language in consultation with counsel, and voted to accept the 
language to bring to a public vote during its August 30, 2011 meeting. Due to a scheduling 
conflict, the vote was moved to the Commission's September 27, 2011 meeting. 
Mr. Page was notified by letter, dated September 15, 2011, that the Commission would 
be considering the resolution at its next meeting. The Commission convened an open meeting on 
September 27, 2011 and took up the resolution as the first item of business. The Chairman 
explained that the Commission had met with the Town Attorney on July 19, 2011 to consider the 
legal consequences of the resolution. The Chairman then read the resolution aloud, and it was 
adopted unanimously by the Commission without discussion. 
The resolution stated: 
Resolved, that the Conservation Commission finds that changed drainage conditions at 21 
Buttonwood Lane, West Barnstable, MA, support the wetland boundaries of the property 
and are consistent with the soil analysis conducted on or about January 15, 2006, and 
shown as the consultant's wetland boundary on the January 23, 2006 site plan, and 
adequately indicates the current wetland boundary for this particular property. 
Following the vote, the Commission opened the floor for comment and allowed Mr. Page 
and his wife to address the Commission for five minutes.

DISCUSSION 
The Open Meeting Law was enacted "to eliminate much of the secrecy surrounding 
deliberations and decisions on which public policy is based." Ghiglione v. School Committee of 
Southbridge,  376 Mass. 70, 72 (1978). Accordingly, the law requires that the "all meetings of a 
public body shall be open to the public," subject to limited exceptions for executive sessions. 
G.L. c. 30A, §§ 20 and 21. One purpose for meeting in executive session is to "discuss strategy 
with respect to collective bargaining or litigation if an open meeting may have a detrimental 
effect on the bargaining or litigating position of the public body and the chair so declares." G.L. 
c. 30A, § 21(a)(3). The mere possibility of litigation is not sufficient to invoke executive session 
Purpose 3. See OML 2012-5; OML 2011-30. 1  Litigation must be pending or clearly and 
imminently threatened or otherwise demonstrably likely. See Perryman v School Committee of 
Boston,  17 Mass. App. Ct. 346, 352 (1983); see also  Attorney General's Open Meeting Law 
Guide, February 10, 2012, at page 8. 
Here, the Commission entered into executive session on July 19, 2011 under Purpose 3 
because litigation was clearly threatened by Mr. Page on numerous occasions, both in person and 
in writing. During the executive session, because it knew that any action on the subject of Mr. 
Page's threatened litigation could have legal implications, the Commission consulted with 
counsel as it crafted the specific language that it intended to vote upon in open session. It was 
appropriate for the Commission to meet in executive session to receive legal advice from the 
Town Attorney as to the litigation consequences of certain actions the Commission proposed 
taking with respect to Mr. Page's property. Furthermore, it was permissible for the Commission 
to vote on the resolution language in executive session. The vote was to bring the resolution up 
for a vote at an open session meeting; the resolution itself did not take effect until the 
Commission voted on it in open session on September 27, 2011. 
While we do not find that the Commission violated the Open Meeting Law by 
considering language of a resolution in executive session and then voting to approve the 
resolution in open session, we are concerned by the lack of public discussion by the Commission 
members prior to the Commission adopting the resolution. In the interest of transparency, the 
Commission should have explained the context of the resolution and the process followed by the 
Commission prior to voting to approve it. Furthermore, we find that the July 19, 2011 executive 
session minutes are incomplete. Public bodies must "create and maintain accurate minutes of all 
meetings, including executive sessions, setting forth the date, time and place, the members 
present or absent, a summary of the discussions on each subject, a list of documents and other 
exhibits used at the meeting, the decisions made and the actions taken at each meeting, including 
the record of all votes." G.L. c. 30A, § 22(a). The July 19, 2011 minutes do not include a 
sufficiently detailed summary of the discussion that led to the language adopted by the 
Commission. Since the public is unable to witness deliberations in executive session, it is 
particularly important that a sufficient written record of the discussion exist. 
Open Meeting Law Determinations may be found at the Attorney General's website,

CONCLUSION 
We find that the Commission did not violate the Open Meeting Law by meeting in 
executive session on July 19, 2011 to discuss litigation strategy with respect to the resolution that 
was subsequently approved on September 27, 2011. However, we find that the minutes of the 
July 19, 2011 executive session are incomplete, and order the Commission to revise the minutes 
within 30 days of receipt of this letter to include a summary of the Commission's discussion. 
We appreciate the patience and cooperation of the parties during this investigation, and 
now consider this matter closed. This deteimination does not resolve any other complaints 
against public bodies in Barnstable that may be pending with our office. Please contact me if 
you have any questions regarding this letter.
