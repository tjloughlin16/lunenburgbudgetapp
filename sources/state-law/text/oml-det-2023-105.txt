 RE: Open Meeting Law Complaint 
 
Dear Ms. Cofske: 
 
This office received a complaint from Michael Doyle on March 13, 2023, alleging that 
the Brimfield Select Board (the “Board”) violated the Open Meeting Law, G.L. c. 30A, §§ 
18-25. The complaint was originally filed with the Board on January 4, 2023, and you responded 
to the complaint, on behalf of the Board, by letter dated January 19, 2023. The complaint alleges 
that the Board violated the Open Meeting Law by discussing and voting on a topic that was not 
included on the notice for the Board meeting held on December 19, 2022.
1 
 
We resolve this matter by informal action, in accordance with 940 CMR 29.07(2)(a), 
after reviewing the original complaint, the Board’s response to the complaint, the complaint filed 
with our office requesting further review, and the notices and minutes of the Board meetings held 
on December 19 and January 3, 2023. We find that the Board did not violate the Open Meeting 
Law in the way alleged. 
 
On December 15 at 4:00 P.M., the Board posted notice of a meeting to be held on 
December 19 at 6:00 P.M. This notice included several topics, including: “Appointment(s)/ 
Resignation(s)” under which were listed the names of individuals to be appointed as the Police 
Department Clerk, a member of the Police Chief Search Committee, and the Chief Procurement 
Officer.  
 
On December 19, Stephen Phifer hand-delivered his letter of interest to be appointed to 
the Building Committee, for which the Board was seeking applicants. Later that day, the Board

posted an amended notice on the bulletin board outside Town Hall, which is the official posting 
location for all public bodies in the Town of Brimfield. The amended notice added “Building 
Committee – Stephen Phifer” to the “Appointment(s)/Resignation(s)” topic heading. During the 
Board’s December 19 meeting, the Board voted unanimously to appoint Stephen Phifer to the 
Building Committee. 
 
The complaint alleges that the Board improperly discussed the appointment of and voted 
to appoint Mr. Phifer to the Building Committee during the December 19 meeting where that 
topic was not listed on the notice. We agree that the topic was not specifically listed on the 
original notice but was discussed during that part of the meeting under “Appointment(s)/ 
Resignation(s).” However, we do not find a violation of the Open Meeting Law because a public 
body may discuss a topic not listed on a meeting notice if the topic was not anticipated by the 
chair 48 hours in advance of the meeting. See OML 2017-2; OML 2013-87.
2 Here, the Board 
states that Mr. Phifer delivered his letter of interest to the Board on December 19, the day of the 
meeting. As such, the topic was not anticipated by the Chair 48 hours before the meeting. We 
find that the Board did not violate the Open Meeting Law by discussing the appointment of and 
voting to appoint Mr. Phifer to the Building Committee during the December 19 meeting. See 
OML 2017-2; OML 2013-87. In fact, although the Board could have discussed the unanticipated 
topic without adding it to the meeting notice at all, the Board followed our recommended 
practice of amending its meeting notice to include the appointment of Mr. Phifer, so that 
members of the public were given advance notice of the item to be discussed.

For the reasons stated above, we find that the Board did not violate the Open Meeting 
Law. We now consider the complaint addressed by this determination to be resolved. This 
determination does not address any other complaints that may be pending with the Board or with 
our office. Please feel free to contact our office at (617) 963-2540 if you have any questions 
regarding this letter.
