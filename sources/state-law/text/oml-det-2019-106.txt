RE: Open Meeting Law Complaint
Dear Attorney Basu:
This office received a complaint from Jeff Brown on March 11 alleging that the 
Gloucester Dogtown Advisory Committee (the “Committee”) violated the Open Meeting Law, 
G.L. c. 30A, §§ 18-25.1 The complaint was originally filed with the Committee on February 5, 
and you responded, on behalf of the Committee, by letter dated February 25. The complaint 
alleges that the Committee failed to timely respond to a request for open session minutes.
We resolve this complaint by informal action in accordance with 940 CMR 29.07(2)(a), 
after reviewing the original complaint, the Committee’s response to the complaint, the complaint 
filed with our office requesting further review, and the minutes of Committee meetings held on 
January 16 and February 27. Finally, we spoke with the complainant by telephone on March 18 
and March 25. We find that the Committee violated the Open Meeting Law as alleged.
The Open Meeting Law requires that “[mjinutes of all open sessions shall be created and 
approved in a timely manner. The minutes of an open session, if they exist and whether 
approved or in draft form, shall be made available upon request by any person within 10 days.” 
G.L. c. 30A, § 22 (c).
In an email to Committee member Cindy Dunn on January 22, the complainant requested 
from the Committee minutes of past meetings, including minutes of its December 19, 2018, 
meeting. Ms. Dunn responded to the request the next day stating that “I will post the minutes

after they approved, [sic] Should be after the Feb 20th meeting.” On February 4, having 
received no further response from the Committee, the complainant filed an Open Meeting Law 
complaint regarding the Committee’s failure to respond to his request for the December 19,
2018, open session minutes. The Committee provided the complainant with a copy of draft open 
session minutes on February 19.
Here, the complainant requested copies of draft minutes on January 22, but minutes were 
not provided until February 19. We acknowledge that the Committee initially responded to the 
complainant’s request within 10 days. However, the Open Meeting Law requires the Committee 
to provide open session minutes, whether approved or in draft form, within 10 calendar days of a 
request. See G.L. c. 30A, § 22 (c); OML 2017-50; OML 2015-17.2 The Committee has not 
asserted that the December 19, 2018, meeting minutes did not exist at the time of the request. 
Therefore, because the Committee did not provide minutes within 10 days, we find that it 
violated the Open Meeting Law.
We order the Committee’s immediate and future compliance with the law’s requirements 
and we caution that similar future violations could be considered evidence of intent to violate the 
law. We now consider the complaint addressed by this determination to be resolved. This 
determination does not address any other complaints that may be pending with the Committee or 
with our office. Please feel free to contact our office at (617) 963-2540 if you have any 
questions regarding this letter.
