This determination addresses two complaints from Steven Fitzgerald alleging that the 
Everett School Committee (the “Committee”) violated the Open Meeting Law, G.L. c. 30A, 
§§ 18-25.  The first complaint is dated July 21, 2022, and alleges that the Committee failed to 
comply with a prior order from our office.  The second complaint is dated August 16, 2022, and 
alleges that the Committee failed to respond to the first complaint.
1   
 
After review, we find that the Committee violated the Open Meeting Law by failing to 
notify this office that it had complied with an order that was issued in resolution of a prior 
complaint. 
 
On March 1, 2022, this office issued an Open Meeting Law determination, OML 2022-
34, finding that the Committee violated the Open Meeting Law.  Specifically, in OML 2022-34 
we found that the minutes of the Committee’s July 19, 2021, meeting failed to include a list of 
documents the Committee had used during that meeting, as is required by the Open Meeting 
Law.  We “order[ed] the Committee to amend the minutes of the July 19 meeting to include a list 
of documents used at the meeting and to certify to this office within thirty (30) days of the date 
of this letter that it has done so.”  OML 2022-34.  The Open Meeting Law regulations also 
require that “[a] public body subject to an order of the Attorney General following a written

determination issued pursuant to 940 CMR 29.07 shall notify the Attorney General in writing of 
its compliance with the order within 30 days of receipt of the order, unless otherwise indicated 
by the order itself.” 
 
The Committee did not notify the Attorney General, within 30 days of March 1, 2022, 
that it had complied with the order in OML 2022-34.  On June 2, 2022, our office contacted the 
Committee regarding the matter and did not receive a response.  On June 22, 2022, our office 
contacted the Committee again.  On July 20, 2022, the Committee responded and notified our 
office that it had amended the meeting minutes at issue and posted the amended minutes to its 
website.  Although the Committee did not specify the date it amended the meeting minutes, we 
understand the minutes were amended to add the list of documents on or very shortly before July 
20, 2022. 
 
We are satisfied that the Committee has now complied with our order to amend the 
minutes of the July 19, 2021, meeting, to add a list of documents used during that meeting.  
However, because the Committee did not notify the Attorney General that it had done so within 
30 days of the date of the order (and indeed did not amend the minutes within 30 days of the date 
of the order), we find the Committee violated the Open Meeting Law as alleged. 
 
Next, the second complaint alleges that the Committee failed to respond to the first 
complaint.  We decline to review this complaint.  On February 18, 2022, our office notified Mr. 
Fitzgerald, as well as the City of Everett, that because of the extraordinarily high volume of 
Open Meeting Law complaints that Mr. Fitzgerald had filed with various public bodies in Everett 
(approximately 200 separate complaints as of that time), a large portion of which were meritless 
or not filed in good faith, our office would decline to review complaints alleging that public 
bodies in Everett have failed to review or respond to prior complaints.  Instead, we explained, 
our office would contact the applicable public body for any needed information in the course of 
our review of properly filed Open Meeting Law complaints. 
 
 We now consider the complaints addressed by this determination to be resolved. This 
determination does not address any other complaints that may be pending with our office or the 
Committee.  Please feel free to contact the Division at (617) 963-2540 if you have any questions.
