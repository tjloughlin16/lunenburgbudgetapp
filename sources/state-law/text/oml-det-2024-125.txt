 RE: Open Meeting Law Complaint 
Dear Robert Alford: 
This office received a complaint from Patrick Higgins on April 24, 2024, alleging that the 
Swansea Advisory and Finance Committee (the “Committee”) violated the Open Meeting Law, 
G.L. c. 30A, §§ 18-25.  The complaint was originally filed with the Committee on or about April 
3, 2024, and you responded to the complaint, on behalf of the Committee, by letter dated April 
23, 2024.  The complaint alleges that the Committee failed to timely respond to a request for 
meeting minutes. 
 
Following our review, we find that the Committee did not violate the Open Meeting Law 
in the way alleged.  In reaching this determination, we reviewed the original complaint, the 
Committee’s response to the complaint, the complaint filed with our office requesting further 
review, and the Town of Swansea website. 
 
The Open Meeting Law requires that a public body “create and maintain accurate 
minutes of all meetings, including executive sessions.”  G.L. c. 30A, § 22(a).  The minutes of an 
open session, if they exist and whether approved or in draft form, shall be made available upon 
request by any person within 10 days.”  G.L. c. 30A, § 22(c).  If such minutes do not yet exist at 
the time of a request, the public body is still required to respond to the request within 10 days 
with an explanation of whether the minutes do or do not exist in either approved or draft form. 
See OML 2018-98; OML 2017-50; OML 2016-71; OML 2015-173.

On March 28, 2024, Patrick Higgins sent an email to you requesting the minutes of the 
March 27, 2024, meeting.  On April 3, 2024, you responded and advised that the minutes would 
be posted to the town’s website on either April 4 or April 5, 2024.  Mr. Higgins then filed the 
Open Meeting Law complaint at issue here on April 3, 2024.   
 
A public body must make minutes of an open session available within 10 days of a 
request, if they exist.  However, the Open Meeting Law does not specify in what format minutes 
must be made available.  Therefore, directing a requester to a website where the requested 
minutes are posted and accessible complies with the requirement to make the minutes 
“available.”  See OML 2024-24.  Here, the Committee responded to the request within six days 
and advised that minutes would be available within the 10 day timeframe.  Because the 
complaint was filed less than 10 days after the request was made, we do not review whether the 
requested minutes were actually made available within 10 days, and we find that the Committee 
did not violate the Open Meeting Law.  See OML 2017- 55 (“Because the action proposed by the 
Board had not yet occurred when the complainant filed his complaint . . ., we find no violation of 
the law.”) 
 
For the reasons stated above, we find that the Committee did not violate the Open 
Meeting Law.  We now consider the complaint addressed by this determination to be resolved.  
This determination does not address any other complaints that may be pending with our office or 
the Committee.  Please feel free to contact our office at (617) 963-2540 if you have any 
questions regarding this letter.
