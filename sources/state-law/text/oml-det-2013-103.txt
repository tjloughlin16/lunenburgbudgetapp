RE: Open Meeting Law Complaint 
Dear Attorney Engman: 
Our office received a complaint filed by Todd Wallack, on behalf of the Boston Globe, dated 
February 25, 2013, alleging that the Board of Registration in Pharmacy (the "Board") violated the 
Open Meeting Law, G.L. c. 30A, §§ 18-25. Specifically, the complaint alleges that the Board failed 
to post minutes on its website for meetings held on August 12, 2008; September 30, 2008; October 
7, 2008; October 18, 2011; November 15, 2011; and December 13, 2011. The complaint was 
originally filed with the Board on January 4, 2013, and you1 responded on behalf of the Board to the 
complaint on or about February 12, 2013. 
We appreciate the patience and cooperation of the parties while we reviewed this matter. 
Following our review, we find that the Board did not violate the Open Meeting Law by failing to 
post its meeting minutes on its website. In reaching this determination, we reviewed the January 4, 
2013 complaint filed with the Board; the Board's February 12, 2013 response; and the February 25, 
2013 complaint filed with our office. Finally, we spoke by telephone with Heather Engman, 
Counsel to the Board. 
FACTS 
Based upon our review of the material listed above, the facts are as follows. In his original 
complaint to the Board, Mr. Wallack alleged that the Board had recently posted meeting minutes to 
its website, but failed to post certain other meeting minutes. In its response to this complaint, the 
Board stated that it was not required to post meeting minutes on its website. Upon receiving the 
Board's response, Mr. Wallack clarified in an email to the Board on February 25, 2013 that he 
sought copies of the relevant meeting minutes, or if they did not exist, copies of recordings or notes 
from those proceedings. On March 18, 2013, a staff member from the Department of Public

DISCUSSION 
The Open Meeting Law requires that the "[m]inutes of all open sessions shall be created and 
approved in a timely manner"4 and "[t]he minutes of an open session, if they exist and whether 
approved or in draft form, shall be made available upon request by any person within 10 days." 
G.L. c. 30A, § 22(a), (c). The Open Meeting Law does not require that public bodies post their 
meeting minutes to a website. Accordingly, the Board did not violate the Open Meeting Law by 
failing to post its meeting minutes on its website. We note, however, that the Board has chosen to 
post its meeting minutes on its website5 and has notified the complainant that minutes from its 
meetings are available at that location. 
CONCLUSION 
For the reasons stated above, we find that the Board did not violate the Open Meeting Law 
by failing to post its meeting minutes on its website.
