This office received a complaint from Alan Dias on January 11, 2023, alleging that the 
Hanson Board of Health (the “Board”) violated the Open Meeting Law, G.L. c. 30A, §§ 18-25.
1 
The complaint was originally filed with the Board on October 26, 2022, and Attorney Alexander 
Castro responded on behalf of the Board by letter dated November 16, 2022.2 The complaint 
alleges that the Board violated the Open Meeting Law when it discussed a proposed septic plan 
for property located at 83 Arlene Street in Hanson during its October 18 meeting without having 
included that topic on the meeting notice.
3 
 
Following our review, we find that the Board violated the Open Meeting Law as alleged. 
In reaching this determination, we reviewed the Open Meeting Law complaint, the Board’s 
response, the request for further review, and additional information provided by the Complainant 
including the notice for the October 18 meeting and various communications exchanged with the 
Board and its Administrative Assistant regarding the septic plan at issue here. We also reviewed 
an audio recording of the Board’s October 18 meeting.4

The Open Meeting Law requires a public body to post notice 48 hours in advance of a 
meeting and to include on that notice a “listing of topics that the chair reasonably anticipates will 
be discussed at the meeting.” G.L. c. 30A, § 20(b). Where the chair delegates to another person 
responsibility for creating the meeting notice, we look to what that person anticipated would be 
discussed 48 hours prior the meeting. See OML 2020-80; OML 2015-190.
5 A public body may 
discuss a truly unanticipated topic at a meeting even if that topic was not included on the meeting 
notice, although the Attorney General strongly recommends that public bodies, as a matter of 
best practice, table discussion of topics that might be of significant interest to the public so that 
the topics can be included on the notice for a future meeting. See OML 2019-131; OML 2018-
119. When reviewing whether a topic was anticipated, we look to whether it was reasonably 
anticipated for discussion at the particular meeting at issue. See OML 2020-55; OML 2018-159; 
OML 2017-2.  
 
The Com
plaint alleges that the Board discussed the proposed septic plan for 83 Arlene 
Street during its October 18 meeting without having included that topic on the meeting notice.6 
The Board asserts that it “simply had no reason to anticipate that [the] septic plans would be 
reviewed by its engineer in time for its October 18, 2022 meeting and, as such, did not include 
them in the original [notice].” After reviewing correspondence from the Board’s Administrative 
Assistant to the Complainant and the owners of 83 Arlene Street, as well as information provided 
in the Board’s response, we find that, although the Board may not have known definitively that 
review of the septic plan would be completed in time for discussion at the October 18 meeting, it 
was reasonable to anticipate that the plan would be discussed at the October 18 meeting. 
Therefore, we find that the Board violated the Open Meeting Law by discussing a reasonably 
anticipated topic without that topic having been included on the meeting notice. See OML 2022-
203; OML 2016-101; OML 2014- 117.  
 
We orde
r the Board’s immediate and future compliance with the Open Meeting Law, and 
caution that similar future violations could be considered evidence of intent to violate the Law. 
Because the Complainant received notice via email on October 17 that the septic plan would be 
discussed at the October 18 meeting, the Complainant attended the October 18 meeting and 
presented the septic plan on behalf of his client, and the Board voted favorably to approve the 
septic plan, we order no further remedial action.
