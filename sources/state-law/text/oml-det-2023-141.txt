 RE:        Open Meeting Law Complaint 
 
Dear Attorney Corbo: 
 
This office received a complaint from Patrick Higgins on June 12, 2023, alleging that the 
Middleborough Zoning Board of Appeals (the “Board”) violated the Open Meeting Law, G.L. c. 
30A, §§ 18-25.
1  The complaint was originally filed with the Board on or about May 23, and you 
responded, on behalf of the Board, by letter dated June 12.  The complaint alleges that the Board 
violated the Open Meeting Law by failing to announce that the meeting held on April 27 was 
being recorded. 
 
We resolve this matter by informal action, in accordance with 940 CMR 29.07(2)(a), 
after reviewing the original complaint, the Board’s response to the complaint, the complaint filed 
with our office requesting further review, and the notice, minutes and video recording of the 
Board meeting held on April 27.
2  We find that the Board violated the Open Meeting Law in the 
way alleged.     
 
The Open Meeting Law requires that “[a]fter notifying the chair of the public body, any 
person may make a video or audio recording of an open session of a meeting of a public body, or 
may transmit the meeting through any medium, subject to reasonable requirements of the chair 
as to the number, placement and operation of equipment used so as not to interfere with the

conduct of the meeting.  At the beginning of the meeting, the chair shall inform other attendees 
of any recordings.”  G.L. c. 30A, § 20(f).  The requirement that the chair inform attendees of any 
recording includes any recording made by members of the public body itself, including those 
made for public broadcasting or administrative purposes, such as assisting in the drafting of 
meeting minutes.  See OML 2019-129; OML 2016-155; OML 2013-136.   
 
The Board meeting held on April 27 was recorded and broadcast by Middleborough 
Cable Community Access Media.  In its response, the Board does not dispute that the Chair did 
not make an announcement regarding the recording.  However, the Board asserts that there were 
no attendees other than the Board members present at the start of the meeting.  If there were no 
other attendees present, there was no obligation to make a statement that the meeting was being 
recorded.  See OML 2019-80.  We find here though that there were other attendees at the April 
27 meeting such that the Chair was required to make an announcement that the meeting was 
being recorded.  See OML 2023-111; OML 2012-24.  After reviewing a video recording of the 
April 27 meeting, we note that all five Board members were seated at a table in the designated 
meeting location and that two additional individuals were also seated with the Board at that table.  
While we recognize that these two individuals were either employed by the Board or a member 
of another public body in Middleborough, they are technically not Board members and thus 
would be considered “other attendees” for purposes of the Open Meeting Law.  Therefore, the 
Chair was required to inform these attendees that the meeting was being recorded.  While the 
failure to inform these individuals that the April 27 meeting was being recorded likely did not 
compromise the Open Meeting Law’s overarching goal of enhanced government transparency, 
we nonetheless are constrained to find that the Board violated the law but find such violation was 
de minimus.  See OML 2019-34; OML 2013-187. 
 
For the reasons stated above, we find that the Board violated the Open Meeting Law by  
failing to announce that the meeting held on April 27 was being recorded.  We order the Board’s 
immediate and future compliance with the law’s requirements, and we caution that similar future 
violations could be considered evidence of intent to violate the law.   
 
We now consider the complaint addressed by this determination to be resolved.  This 
determination does not address any other complaints that may be pending with our office or the 
Board.  Please feel free to contact our office at (617) 963-2540 if you have any questions 
regarding this letter.
