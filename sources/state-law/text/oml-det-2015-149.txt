This office received a complaint from Attorney Norman Kaplan, dated April 7, 2015, 
alleging that the Boston Fair Housing Commission (the "Commission") violated the Open 
Meeting Law, G.L. c. 30A, §§ 18-25. Specifically, the complaint alleges that the Commission 
failed to make meeting minutes available in response to Attorney Kaplan's request. The 
complaint was originally filed with the Commission on January 12, 2015. The Commission 
responded by letter dated February 27, 2015, following an extension of time granted by our 
office. 
We appreciate the patience and cooperation of the parties while we reviewed this matter. 
Following our review, we find that the Commission violated the Open Meeting Law as alleged. 
In reaching this determination, we reviewed the complaint filed with the Commission, the 
Commission's response, and the complaint filed with our office. Additionally, we spoke by 
telephone with Mr. Kaplan on May 6, 2015, and with Commission staff members Dion Irish and 
Vera Schneider on May 21, 2015. We reviewed letters from Mr. Kaplan addressed to our office,

FACTS 
The Commission is a public body charged with enforcing the City of Boston's fair 
housing laws. On November 10, 2014, Mr. Kaplan sent a letter to the Commission requesting 
copies of "all Commission meeting notices, meeting agendas and meeting minutes for the 
following periods: 
1. Aug. 1,2007 to Dec. 31,2007 
2. Jan. 1, 2008 to Dec. 31, 2008 
3. Jan. 1, 2009 to Dec. 31, 2009 
4. Jan. 1, 2010 to Dec. 31, 2010 
5. Jan. 1,2011 to Dec. 31,2011 
6. Jan. 1, 2012 to Dec. 31, 2012 
7. Jan. 1,2013 to Dec. 31,2013 
8. Oct. 1, 2014 to Oct. 31, 2014." (Emphasis omitted). 
The Commission responded to Mr. Kaplan's request in a November 14, 2014 letter, 
providing documents responsive to certain date ranges, and asserting that there were no 
responsive documents with respect to the other date ranges. Mr. Kaplan wrote the Commission 
on November 20, 2014 requesting an explanation as to why the Commission claimed no meeting 
minutes existed for certain date ranges. In a January 2, 2015 letter to the Commission, Mr. 
Kaplan highlighted 31 months between July 2010 and October 2014 for which he believed 
meeting minutes were missing. The Commission replied to Mr. Kaplan's letter on January 15, 
2015, explaining that it did not hold regular meetings until August 2012 and that some of its 
meetings were closed to the public as quasi-judicial meetings. 
On January 12, 2015, Mr. Kaplan filed the present complaint. In the complaint, in 
addition to the missing 31 months of meeting minutes, Mr. Kaplan identified nine meetings for 
which the Commission had posted a meeting notice but had not made meeting minutes available 
in response to his request.2 On June 19, 2015, Attorney Driscoll provided our office with an 
accounting of the nine dates. For three of the dates, although notice had been posted, the 
meeting was cancelled and never took place, therefore no minutes were produced.3 For two 
meeting dates. May 19, 2011 and November 13, 2012, the Commission was unable to locate 
either evidence of cancellation of the meeting or the meeting minutes. For four of the dates, the 
Commission located meeting minutes and provided them to our office, though it has not yet 
provided them to Mr. Kaplan.4 
The Commission also met in its quasi-judicial capacity on at least 16 dates between July 
2010 and May 2015.5 For these meetings, the Commission did not post notice, record minutes, 
or open the meeting to the public.

DISCUSSION 
1. The Commission Failed to Make All Responsive Meeting Minutes Available to Mr. 
Kaplan within 10 Days of his Request. 
The Open Meeting Law requires a public body to "create and maintain accurate minutes 
of all meetings, including executive sessions." G.L. c. 30A, § 22(a); OML 2013-47; OML 2013-
16; OML 2012-29.6 Minutes of all open sessions shall be created and approved in a timely 
manner. G.L. c. 30A, § 22(c). The minutes of an open session, if they exist and whether 
approved or in draft form, shall be made available upon request by any person within 10 days. 
Id 
All of the meetings at issue here were held in open session. Thus, the Commission had 
an obligation to make those minutes available to Mr. Kaplan within 10 days of his request. 
While the Commission responded to Mr. Kaplan's request within 10 days, it violated the Open 
Meeting Law by failing to make the minutes of at least four meetings available to him within that 
same time period. G.L. c. 30A, § 22(c). It also failed to create minutes for two meetings, place 
on May 19, 2011 and November 13, 2012. Because the Commission posted notice for these two 
meetings and has not provided evidence showing that the meetings were cancelled, we presume 
they occurred, and therefore the Commission should have created and retained minutes. 
With respect to the 31 months identified during the period between July 2010 and 
October 2014 for which Mr. Kaplan asserts minutes are missing, the Commission states that, 
prior to August 2012, the Commission did not meet on a monthly basis, and only met a few times 
year. Therefore, the Commission does not believe there were meetings during the months at 
issue for which the Commission would have been required to create minutes. In the absence of 
evidence to the contrary, we credit the Commission's version of events and find no violation

Commission held in its quasi-judicial capacity, no minutes are produced because quasi-judicial 
meetings are exempt from the Open Meeting Law. The Open Meeting defines a "meeting" as, "a 
deliberation by a public body with respect to any matter within the body's jurisdiction; provided,

however, "meeting" shall not include... a meeting of a quasi-judicial board or commission held 
for the sole purpose of making a decision required in an adjudicatory proceeding brought before 
it. G.L. c. 30A, § 18. We note, however, that the law's quasi-judicial exception applies only to 
certain state "quasi-judicial" bodies whose adjudicatory proceedings are specifically subject to 
G.L. c. 30A. This conclusion is based on the plain language of the definitions included in the 
Open Meeting Law, G.L. c. 30A, § 18, read in harmony with the definition section found at G.L. 
c. 30A, § 1. That section defines "adjudicatory proceeding" as "a proceeding before an agency 
in which the legal rights, duties or privileges of specifically named persons are required by 
constitutional right or by any provision of the General Laws to be determined after opportunity 
for an agency hearing." G.L. c. 30A, § 1(1). Massachusetts General Laws Chapter 30A, § 1(2), 
defines "agency" as: 
[A]ny department, board, commission, division or authority of the state government or 
subdivision of any of the foregoing, or official of the state government, authorized by law 
to make regulations or to conduct adjudicatory proceedings, but does not include the 
following: the legislative and judicial departments; the governor and council; military or 
naval boards, commissions or officials; the department of correction; the department of 
youth services; the parole board; the division of dispute resolution of the division of 
industrial accidents; the personnel administrator; the civil service commission; and the 
appellate tax board. 
This definition makes clear that an agency must be a part of state government. While the 
Commission derives some authority from state law (see Chapter 37 of the Acts of 1994), the 
Commission was created by the City of Boston, is overseen by the City of Boston, and its 
jurisdiction is entirely local. It is therefore a local public body. The Commission argues that it 
nevertheless qualifies as an agency under Chapter 3OA, § 1, because it is substantially equivalent 
to the federal Department of Housing and Urban Development and the Massachusetts 
Commission Against Discrimination in the way it handles fair housing claims. The 
Commission's final orders are appealable to Suffolk Superior Court or the Boston Housing Court 
under G.L. c. 30A, § 14. Finally, the Commission is concerned that if the quasi-judicial 
exception does not apply, that would force claimants to litigate their fair housing claims in a 
public forum, potentially revealing an individual's familial, financial, religious, or other personal 
status. 
We understand the Commission's concerns; however, we find that the Commission is a 
municipal—not state—public body, and cannot avail itself of the Open Meeting Law's quasi-
judicial exception. See G.L. c. 30A, § 18. We note that, even though the quasi-judicial 
exception does not apply, the Commission may still proceed in confidential sessions in some 
cases. A public body may enter into executive session to discuss "complaints or charges brought 
against, a public officer, employee, staff member or individual." G.L. c. 30A, § 21(a)(1).8 The 
Commission may also be able to enter into executive session "to comply with, or act under the

CONCLUSION 
We find that the Commission violated the Open Meeting Law by failing to make meeting 
minutes available within 10 days of a request. We order the Commission to take the following 
remedial action: 
1. Immediate and future compliance with the Open Meeting Law. We caution the 
Commission that a similar future violation of the Law may be considered evidence of an 
intentional violation of the Law. 
2. Create, to the best of its ability, minutes for the Commission meetings held on May 19, 
2011 and November 13, 2012. This must occur within thirty (30) days of receipt of this 
order. 
3. Provide Mr. Kaplan with the four sets of meeting minutes it provided our office on June 
19, 2015.9 It must do this within ten (10) days receipt of this letter. 
4. Create, to the best of its ability, minutes for the 16 quasi-judicial meetings held between 
July 2010 and the present.10 
5. Make copies of the minutes of the 16 quasi-judicial meetings available to Mr. Kaplan. 
The Commission must also provide this office with copies of the minutes of the 16 quasi-
judicial meetings to ensure its compliance with this order. This must occur within thirty 
(30) days of receipt of this order. 
We now consider this matter closed. Please be advised that this letter does not resolve 
any other complaints that may be pending with this office or with the Commission. Please feel 
free to contact our office at 617-963-2540 if you have any questions regarding this letter.
