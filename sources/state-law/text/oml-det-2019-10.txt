RE: Open Meeting Law Complaints
Dear Mr. Jaillet:
This office received two complaints from Nada Milosavljevic-Fabrizio on March 19, 
2018, alleging that the Westwood Board of Selectmen (the “Board”) violated the Open 
Meeting Law, G.L. c. 30A, §§ 18-25. The complaints were originally filed with the Board on 
or about January 9, 2018, and you responded, on behalf of the Board, in a letter dated January 
23, 2018. In her complaints, Ms. Milosavljevic-Fabrizio alleges that the Board’s December 
18, 2017 and January 8, 2018 meeting notices failed to identify the property that was the 
subject of an executive session.
Following our review, we find that the Board violated the Open Meeting Law by 
failing to include sufficient detail about an executive session topic on its December 18, 2017 
meeting notice. In reaching a determination, we reviewed the original complaint, the Board’s 
response to the complaint, and the complaint filed with our office requesting further review. 
We also reviewed the notices and open and executive sessions minutes of the Board’s 
December 18, 2017 and January 8, 2018 meetings. Finally, we communicated by telephone 
and email on January 3 and February 5, 2019.
FACTS
We find the facts as follows. On December 18, 2017 and January 8, 2018, the Board 
convened in executive session pursuant to G.L. c. 30A, § 21(a)(6) (“Purpose 6”) to discuss 
strategy with respect to negotiating the purchase, exchange, lease or value of real property. 
The executive session discussions were unrelated to each other, although in both instances the 
Board did not publicly disclose the properties at issue, on advice of its counsel. The 
December 18, 2017 meeting notice listed the executive session topic as, “potential

sale/transfer/lease of real property along Gay Street.” The January 8, 2018 meeting notice 
listed the executive session topic as, “the potential sale of real property.”
DISCUSSION
The Open Meeting Law requires that all meetings of a public body be conducted in an 
open session, with some exceptions. G.L. c. 30A, §§ 20(a), 21(a). One permissible reason to 
convene in executive session is “[t]o consider the purchase, exchange, lease or value of real 
property if the chair declares that an open meeting may have a detrimental effect on the 
negotiating position of the public body.” Id. at § 21(a)(6) (“Purpose 6”).
The Open Meeting Law requires that public bodies post notice of each meeting and 
include in that notice a “listings of topics that the chair reasonably anticipates will be 
discussed at the meeting.” G.L. c. 30A, § 20(b). Public bodies must list topics for discussion 
with “sufficient specificity to reasonably advise the public of the issues to be discussed at the 
meeting.” 940 CMR 29.03(l)(b). Executive session topics must be described, both in the 
meeting notice and in an announcement during open session, in as much detail as possible 
without compromising the purpose for which the executive session was called. See District 
Attorney for N. Dist. v. Sch. Comm. Of Wayland. 455 Mass. 561, 567 (“[a] precise statement 
of the reason for convening in executive session is necessary under the open meeting law 
because that is the only notification given to the public that the school committee would 
conduct business in private, and the only way the public would know if the reason for doing 
so was proper or improper.”); see G.L. c. 30A, §§ 20(b), 21(b)(3); 940 CMR 29.03(l)(b).
The complaint alleges that the meeting notices failed to provide sufficient information 
about what properties the Board would discuss in executive session. When noticing an 
executive session pursuant to Purpose 6, a public body must identify the property to be 
discussed if doing so would not compromise the purpose for which the executive session was 
called. See OML 2016-5; OML 2014-136; OML 2013-97.1 According to the Board, the 
December 18, 2017 executive session concerned the discussion of the sale of “a portion of the 
Hanlon School parcel.” If the location had been disclosed, the Board contends, the public 
may have confused the discussion with a project at the school itself. The lack of information 
about the executive session on its notice, however, did not alleviate any confusion the public 
may have had about the anticipated discussion. More importantly, the Board’s reason for 
withholding the information from the public had nothing to do with protecting the Board’s 
negotiating position. For these reasons, we find that the Board violated the Open Meeting 
Law by failing to identify the property on its meeting notice. Due to the insufficiency of this 
topic, the Board may not rely on the Open Meeting Law to withhold the executive session 
minutes. See G.L. c. 30A, § 22(f). The Board may, however, continue to withhold the 
minutes, in whole or in part, if an exemption to the Public Records Law or the attorney-client 
privilege applies. Id

In contrast, the Board did not violate the law by omitting the property address from the 
notice for its January 8, 2018, executive session. That session concerned an ongoing 
discussion of negotiating the sale of Town-owned property for a proposed development in 
Town. Purpose 6 is intended to preserve confidentiality in negotiating the value of the 
property to be purchased, exchanged or leased to avoid putting the public body at a 
disadvantage in its negotiations for the property. See District Attorney for the Plymouth Dist. 
v. Selectmen of Middleborough, 395 Mass. 629, 631 (1985). Here, the Board was justifiably 
concerned that disclosing the subject of the executive session would affect the ongoing 
negotiations with the developer for a variety of strategic reasons. Accordingly, the Board did 
not violate the Open Meeting Law by omitting identifying information regarding the 
development project and/or properties under consideration in this instance. See OML 2016- 
50; OML 2016-5.
CONCLUSION
For the reasons stated above, we find that the Board violated the Open Meeting Law 
by failing to include sufficient detail about an executive session topic on its December 18, 
2017 meeting notice. We order the Board’s immediate and future compliance with the law’s 
requirements, and we caution that similar future violations could be considered evidence of 
intent to violate the law. Additionally, we order the Board to publicly release its December 
18, 2017 executive session meeting minutes in accordance with the above guidance.
We now consider the complaints addressed by this determination to be resolved. This 
determination does not address any other complaints that may be pending with our office or 
the Board. Please feel free to contact our office at (617) 963-2540 if you have any questions 
regarding this letter.
