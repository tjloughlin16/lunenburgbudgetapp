RE: Open Meeting Law Complaint 
Dear Mr. Chelgren: 
This office received a complaint from Ms. Michelle Bailey on August 9, 2011, alleging 
that the Hamilton-Wenham Capital Management Committee (the "Committee") violated the 
Open Meeting Law, G.L. c. 30A, §§ 18-25. Specifically, the complaint alleges that the 
Committee posted notice in Wenham fewer than 48 hours before its June 15, 2011 meeting, and 
never posted notice in Hamilton, as was required by G.L. c. 30A, § 20. The complaint was 
originally filed with the Committee on July 5, 2011, and the Committee's response was received 
by this office on July 20, 2011. 
We reviewed the July 5, 2011 complaint filed with the Wenham Town Clerk; the 
Committee's July 20, 2011 response; and the August 9, 2011 complaint and attached letter filed 
with this office. Following our review, we find that the Committee violated the Open Meeting 
Law by failing to post notice of its June 15, 2011 meeting at least 48 hours in advance in 
Wenham, and by failing to post any notice in Hamilton.

FACTS 
Based upon our review of the material listed above, the facts are as follows. The 
Hamilton-Wenham Capital Management Committee is a board composed of members from the 
towns of Hamilton and Wenham, with jurisdiction covering both towns. The Committee held a 
meeting on June 15, 2011. According to Wenham Town Administrator Jeff A. Chelgren,1  who 
responded to the complaint on behalf of the Committee, the Committee's notice for its June 15, 
2011 meeting was "posted 90 minutes short of the required 48 hours." The Town Administrator 
also admits that there was "a failure to transmit the notice to the Hamilton Town Clerk" for 
posting. The Town Administrator also noted that the Committee is an advisory committee 
lacking direct authority, and there was "no action taken on any issues or recommendations" at 
the meeting on June 15, 2011. 
DISCUSSION 
The Open Meeting Law requires that a public body "post notice of every meeting at least 
48 hours prior to such meeting, excluding Saturdays, Sundays and legal holidays." G.L. c. 30A, 
§ 20(b). The law further requires that "for meetings of a regional or district public body, notice 
shall be filed and posted in each city or town within the region or district in a manner prescribed 
for local public bodies." Id. § 20(c). For meetings of local public bodies, "notice shall be filed 
with the municipal clerk and posted in a manner conspicuously visible to the public at all hours 
in or on the municipal building in which the clerk's office is located." Icl. 
The Committee posted notice for its June 15, 2011 meeting in Wenham fewer than 48 
hours before the meeting. The Town Administrator admits that notice was "posted 90 minutes 
short of the required 48 hours." This is a violation of G.L. c. 30A, § 20(b). Furthermore, notice 
for the June 15, 2011 Committee meeting was never posted in Hamilton. As a regional public 
body, the Committee is required to post notice in both Hamilton and Wenham. The lack of 
posted notice in Hamilton also constituted a violation of G.L. c. 30A, § 20(c). 
The Committee acknowledged these violations in the July 20, 2011 response letter sent 
by the Town Administrator. The Town Administrator also noted that the Committee is an 
advisory committee and that there was "no action taken on any issues or recommendations" at 
the June 15, 2011 meeting. However, that does not excuse the Committee from posting notice in 
compliance with G.L. c. 30A, § 20. A meeting occurs whenever there is "a deliberation by a 
public body with respect to any matter within the body's jurisdiction," not just when the body 
takes action or reaches a decision on matters under discussion. See G.L. c. 30A, § 18. Posted 
notice was therefore required for the Committee's June 15, 2011 meeting. See id. at § 20. 
CONCLUSION 
We fmd that the Committee violated the Open Meeting Law by failing to post proper 
notice for its June 15, 2011 meeting. We order immediate and future compliance with the Open 
Meeting Law, and caution that future similar violations may be considered evidence of intent to 
violate the Law.
