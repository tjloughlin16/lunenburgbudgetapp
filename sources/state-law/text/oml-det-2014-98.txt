RE: Open Meeting Law Complaint 
Dear Mr. Fraine, 
This office received two complaints from Douglas Maclachlan (the "Complainant"), on 
June 13 and June 16, alleging that the Bellingham Board of Selectmen (the "Board") violated the 
Open Meeting Law, G.L. c. 30A, §§18-25.' The first complaint alleges that the Town 
Administrator intentionally misled the public when answering a Board member's question during 
the Board's March 3 meeting. The second complaint alleges that the Board's Chair prevented 
the complainant from reading an email aloud during the Board's March 17 meeting. The 
complaints were originally filed with the Board on March 26. The Board did not respond to the 
first complaint, finding it lacked an Open Meeting Law issue. The Board responded to the 
second complaint on March 27. For the reasons described below, we decline to review the first 
complaint and find no violation with respect to the second. 
First, the allegations in the June 13 complaint regarding the Town Administrator," even if 
taken as true, would not constitute a violation of the Open Meeting Law. The Town 
Administrator is an individual public employee who is not a member of a public body, thus he 
was not required to comply with the Open Meeting Law and could not have violated it. See 
OML Declination 8-27-12. We therefore decline to review the June 13 complaint. 
Second, the June 16 complaint alleges that the Board's Chair prevented the complainant 
from reading an email aloud during a meeting. Again, even if taken as true, the facts alleged

would not constitute a violation of the Open Meeting Law. The Open Meeting Law does not 
require a public body to discuss any topic. OML 2013-64; OML 2012-23. The Open Meeting 
Law just requires that a public body post a timely notice listing the topics the chair reasonably 
anticipates will be discussed, and that such discussions take place during meetings. See G.L. c. 
3OA, §§ 20(a), (b); id. Additionally, the Open Meeting Law does not require that a public body 
allow public participation. See OML 2013-64; OML 2012-23. The law states that "[n]o person 
shall address a meeting of a public body without permission of the chair, all persons shall, at the 
request for the chair, be silent." G.L. c. 30A, § 20(f). Therefore, the Chair had discretion to 
decide who could speak during the Board's March 17 meeting and, even if he did prevent the 
complainant from reading aloud an email during the meeting, did not violate the law. See id.; 
OML 2012-23. 
We now consider this matter closed. Please contact our office at (617) 963-2540 if you 
have any questions regarding this letter.
