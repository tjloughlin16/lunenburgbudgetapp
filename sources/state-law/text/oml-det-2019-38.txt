RE: Open Meeting Law Complaint
Dear Mr. Rees:
This office received a complaint from Karen Vilandry on January 28, 2019, alleging that 
the Fairhaven Marijuana Advisory Committee (the “Committee”) violated the Open Meeting 
Law, G.L. c. 30A, §§ 18-25. The complaint was originally filed with the Committee on 
November 28, 2018; and you responded, on behalf of the Committee, by letter dated March 18, 
2019.1 The complaint alleges that the Committee failed to create minutes of its meetings and 
submit those minutes to the Town Clerk.
We resolve this complaint by informal action in accordance with 940 CMR 29.07(2)(a), 
after reviewing the original complaint, the Committee’s response to the complaint, and the 
complaint filed with our office requesting further review. We find that the Committee violated 
the Open Meeting Law by failing to create minutes of its meetings.
The Open Meeting Law requires that a public body “create and maintain accurate 
minutes of all meetings, including executive sessions, setting forth the date, time and place, the 
members present or absent, a summary of the discussions on each subject, a list of documents 
and other exhibits used at the meeting, the decisions made and the actions taken at each meeting, 
including the record of all votes.” G.L. c. 30A, § 22(a).

The Committee was created by the Board of Selectmen (“Board”) on October 30, 2017, 
to advise the Board on the regulation of recreational marijuana establishments. The Committee 
met only four times: on January 3, January 30, April 4, and June 6, 2018. The complaint alleges 
that minutes of those four meetings were not created and submitted to the Town Clerk. Here, the 
Committee created minutes of its January 3 and April 4, 2018, meetings but not of its January 30 
and June 6, 2018, meetings. The Committee violated the Open Meeting Law by failing to create 
and maintain minutes of the meetings held on January 30 and June 6, 2018.
As for the allegation that the Committee failed to submit its meeting minutes to the Town 
Clerk, the Open Meeting Law does not require that public bodies post or submit minutes to the 
Town Clerk, but merely that meeting minutes be made available within 10 days of a request. See 
G.L. c. 30A, § 22(c); OML 2013-103.2 Therefore, we do not find a violation on this allegation.
We order the Committee’s immediate and future compliance with the law’s requirements, 
and we caution that similar future violations could be considered evidence of intent to violate the 
law. We also order the Committee to create minutes for its January 30, 2018, and June 6, 2018, 
meetings to the best of its ability, through whatever means are available, including the memories 
and individual notations of the attendees. Compliance with this order should occur within sixty 
(60) days of receipt of this letter, and the Committee should certify compliance with this order to 
our office in writing, pursuant to 940 CMR 29.07(4).
We now consider the complaint addressed by this determination to be resolved. This 
determination does not address any other complaints that may be pending with the Committee or 
with our office. Please feel free to contact our office at (617) 963-2540 if you have any 
questions regarding this letter.
