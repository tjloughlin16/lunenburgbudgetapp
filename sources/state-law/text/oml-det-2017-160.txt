FACTS 
We find the facts as follows. The Trust is a five-member public body charged with 
assisting the Ashland Board of Selectmen in the development of affordable housing opportunities 
for seniors and individuals of low and moderate income. The Trust held meetings on the 
following dates: September 20, October 24, and November 1, 2016. The Trust created minutes 
of these meetings. 
On November 20, 2016, the complainant filed an Open Meeting Law with the Trust 
alleging violations related to a meeting of the Trust held on September 20, 2016. The Trust did 
not meet to review the merits of this complaint, and legal counsel for the Trust responded by 
letter dated November 29, 2016. 
DISCUSSION 
I. The Trust Created Minutes of its October 24 and November 1. 2016 Meetings. 
The Open Meeting Law requires public bodies, such as the Trust, to create and maintain 
accurate minutes of all meetings, "setting forth the date, time and place, the members present or 
absent, a summary of the discussions on each subject, a list of documents and other exhibits used 
at the meeting, the decisions made and the actions taken at each meeting, including the record of 
all votes." G.L. c, 30A, § 22(a). Minutes must be created and approved in a timely manner. 
G.L. c. 3OA, § 22(c). 
The complaint alleges that the Trust failed to create minutes for its meetings held on 
October 24, and November 1,17, and 22, 2016.4 Because the Trust has provided our office with 
copies of these minutes, we find that the Trust did not violate the Open Meeting Law with 
respect to this allegation.5 While not raised in the complaint, we take this opportunity to remind 
the Commission that minutes of all meetings, whether open session or executive session, must 
contain a summary of the discussion of each topic, including sufficient detail for a reasonable 
member of the public who did not attend the meeting to read them and have a clear 
understanding of what occurred. See OML 2014-116.

II. The Trust Failed to Follow Proper Procedure in Responding to an Open Meeting 
Law Complaint. 
When an individual files an Open Meeting Law complaint with a public body, the chair 
"shall disseminate copies of the complaint to the members of the public body," and "the public 
body shall review the complaint's allegations." 940 CMR 29.05(3), (5). The chair is required to 
disseminate copies and the public body must meet to review the complaint and formulate a 
response, or must meet to delegate that authority. See OML 2012-90. 
The complaint alleges that the Trust failed to convene a meeting before responding to the 
Open Meeting Law complaint filed on November 20, 2016. Indeed, it appears that the Trust did 
not convene a meeting before its legal counsel sent a response to the complaint on November 29, 
2016. This violated the Open Meeting Law. See OML 2017-6. While we have consistently 
interpreted the law to require that a public body meet to review a complaint, we note that the 
Attorney General's amended Open Meeting Law regulations that took effect on October 6, 2017 
explicitly state this requirement. See 940 CMR 29.05(5). 
CONCLUSION 
For the reasons stated above, we find that the Trust violated the Open Meeting Law. We 
order the Trust's immediate and future compliance with the law, and we caution that similar 
future violations may be considered evidence of intent to violate the law. 
We now consider the complaint addressed by this determination to be resolved. This 
determination does not address any other complaints that may be pending with our office or the 
Trust. Please feel free to contact the Division at (617) 963 - 2540 if you have any questions.
