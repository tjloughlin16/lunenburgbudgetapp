 RE: Open Meeting Law Complaint 
Dear Attorney Dupere: 
This office received a complaint from Craig Swinson on April 21, 2021, alleging that the 
Richmond School Committee (the “Committee”) violated the Open Meeting Law, G.L. c. 30A, 
§§ 18-25. The complaint was originally filed with the Committee on March 5, and you 
responded on behalf of the Committee by letter dated March 17.1 In his complaint, Mr. Swinson 
alleges that the Committee failed to make available to Mr. Swinson draft meeting minutes of a 
February 9 meeting within 10 calendar days of Mr. Swinson’s request.2, 3 
 
1 Unless otherwise specified, all dates refer to 2021. 
2 To the extent that Mr. Swinson alleges that the Committee did not allow public participation during the February 9 
meeting, we decline to review this allegation. See OML Declination 1-25-16 (Nahant Housing Authority Board of 
Commissioners). Even if true, this allegation would not constitute a violation of G.L. c. 30A, §§ 18-25. The Open 
Meeting Law does not require that a public body allow public participation during its meetings, but rather provides 
that “[n]o person shall address a meeting of a public body without permission of the chair, and all persons shall, at 
the request of the chair, be silent.” G.L. c. 30A § 20(f). The Open Meeting Law, therefore, permits the chair of a 
public body to decide whether to allow for public participation, and if so, to set limitations on participation. See 
OML 2017-189; OML 2014-23; OML 2012-23. We do, however, encourage public bodies to allow for as much 
public participation as time and circumstances permit. See OML 2015-12; OML 2014-129; OML 2012-59. 
3 To the extent that Mr. Swinson alleges that the Committee failed to produce the recording of the February 9

Law governs requests for meeting minutes, only. See G.L. c. 30A, § 22. Requests for other documents, including 
recordings of meetings, are governed by the Public Records Law. See G.L. c. 66, § 10. Because this allegation

 We resolve this complaint by informal action in accordance with 940 CMR 29.07(2)(a), 
and find that the Committee did not violate the Open Meeting Law. In reaching this 
determination, we reviewed the original complaint, the Committee’s response to the complaint, 
and the complainant’s request for further review. 
 The Open Meeting Law requires that “[m]inutes of an open session, if they exist and 
whether approved or in draft form, shall be made available upon request by any person within 10 
days.” G.L. c. 30A, § 22(c). Under the Open Meeting Law, a public body has 10 calendar days 
from the date a request is received to provide a response. A public body is only required to 
produce any meeting minutes, whether in draft or approved form, which existed at the time of a 
request. Handwritten notes, depending on the condition they are in, may be considered a draft of 
the minutes that need to be produced, and “materials used in the preparation of minutes” are 
public records. See OML 2018-117; OML 2013-131; OML 2013-64; OML 2013-31.4 However, 
a public body is not required to produce meeting minutes in response to a request that do not 
already exist. See OML 2020-151. If minutes do not yet exist at the time of a request, the public 
body is still required to respond to the request within 10 calendar days with an explanation of 
whether the minutes do or do not exist in either approved or draft form. See OML 2021-46.  
  On February 8, Mr. Swinson requested the draft minutes from the Committee’s February 
9 meeting. On February 18, the Committee’s counsel, Kimberly Roche, sent Mr. Swinson a 
response noting that the requested meeting minutes had not yet been drafted, but offering that 
“such drafted minutes will be sent to you[r] attention upon their completion.” As Mr. Swinson 
request meeting minutes for a meeting which did not occur until the next day, we find that the 
Open Meeting Law would not have required the Committee to produce any draft minutes in 
response to this request, as none existed on the date of the request, February 8. We commend the 
Committee for offering to voluntarily provide the meeting minutes to Mr. Swinson upon their 
completion, instead of requiring Mr. Swinson to make a follow-up request once the meeting 
minutes were in existence. The Committee sent the requested meeting minutes to Mr. Swinson 
shortly after they were created on March 5.  
 For the reasons stated above, we find that the Committee did not violate the Open 
Meeting Law. We now consider the complaint addressed by this determination to be resolved. 
This determination does not address any other complaints that may be pending with our office or 
the Committee. Please feel free to contact the Division at (617) 963 - 2540 if you have any 
questions.
