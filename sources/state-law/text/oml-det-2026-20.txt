This office received a complaint from Patrick Higgins on August 12, 2025, and a second 
complaint on August 15, 2025, alleging that the Lincoln Select Board (the “Board”) violated the 
Open Meeting Law, G.L. c. 30A, §§ 18-25.  The first complaint was originally filed with the 
Board on or about May 12, 2025, the second complaint was originally filed with the Board on or 
about July 19, 2025, and you responded to both complaints, on behalf of the Board, by letter 
dated August 5, 2025.  The first complaint alleges that the Board posted a notice with insufficient 
specificity for a meeting held on April 14, 2025.
1  The second complaint alleges that the Board 
failed to timely respond to the first Open Meeting Law complaint. 
 
Following our review, we find that the Board violated the Open Meeting Law in the ways 
alleged.  In reaching this determination, we reviewed the original complaints, the Board’s 
responses to the complaints, and the complaints filed with our office requesting further review.  
In addition, we reviewed the notice, minutes and video recording of the Board meeting held on 
April 14, 2025.

The Open Meeting Law requires that, except in an emergency, “a public body shall post 
notice of every meeting at least 48 hours prior to such meeting, excluding Saturdays, Sundays 
and legal holidays.”  G.L. c. 30A, § 20(b).  The notice must be printed in a legible, easily 
understandable format and must contain the date, time and place of the meeting, as well as a 
listing of topics that the chair reasonably anticipates will be discussed.  Id.  The list of topics 
shall have “sufficient specificity to reasonably advise the public of the issues to be discussed at 
the meeting.”  940 CMR 29.03(l)(b).  We generally consider a topic to be sufficiently specific 
when a reasonable member of the public could read the topic and understand the anticipated 
nature of the public body’s discussion.  See OML 2019-70; OML 2015-35; OML 2013-39.
3   
 
Here, the notice of the April 14, 2025, meeting included the following topic: “Annual 
Vote to Reappoint Board/Committee Members.”  The notice did not include the names of any 
individuals to be reappointed.  During the meeting, the Board approved the reappointments of 
numerous public body members that it is required to approve on an annual basis.    
 
The first complaint alleges that the Board’s notice of the April 14, 2025, meeting lacked 
sufficient specificity because it failed to list the names of the candidates for reappointment.
4  We 
have consistently stated that the names of applicants for appointment or employment which are 
known 48 hours in advance of a meeting should be included in a meeting notice. See OML 2022-
217; 2019-24; OML 2016-150; OML 2013-163.  The Board admits, and we find, that the notice 
for the meeting held on April 14, 2025, should have included the names of the individuals to be 
considered by the Board.  Accordingly, the Board violated the Open Meeting Law by failing to 
include this information in the notice.  We commend the Board for its commitment to including 
this additional specificity in meeting notices going forward and take this opportunity to clarify 
that the list of candidates for appointment or reappointment must be included as part of the notice 
document itself, even if it is several pages long; it is not sufficient to reference a separate list that 
is available elsewhere.  See OML 2022-68; OML 2020-21; OML 2019-22.  In the event of a 
similar situation, the Board may include as part of the notice document a list of each public body 
for which an appointment would be made, along with the names of the candidates for 
consideration.  See OML 2024-236. 
 
The second complaint alleges that the Board failed to timely respond to the first Open 
Meeting Law complaint, which was filed on May 12, 2025.  The Open Meeting Law requires 
that within 14 days of receipt of a complaint, the public body review the complaint’s allegations; 
take remedial action, if appropriate; and send to the complainant a response and a description of 
any remedial action taken.  G.L. c. 30A, § 23(b); 940 CMR 29.05(5).  The public body shall 
simultaneously send to the Attorney General a copy of the complaint and a description of any 
remedial action taken.  940 CMR 29.05(5).  Upon the filing of an Open Meeting Law complaint 
with a public body, the chair “shall disseminate copies of the complaint to the members of the

public body,” and “the public body shall review the complaint’s allegations.”  940 CMR 
29.05(3), (5).  Thus, a public body must meet to review the complaint and formulate a response, 
or meet to delegate that authority, and respond to the complaint within 14 business days.  See 
G.L. c. 30A, § 23(b); OML 2022-43; OML 2017-69; OML 2012-90.   
 
By letter dated August 5, 2025, the Board responded to both Open Meeting Law 
complaints.  While the Board timely responded to the second complaint, which was filed on or 
about July 19, 2025, we find that it did not respond to the first complaint within the time frame 
required by the Open Meeting Law, that is within 14 business days.  As such, we find that the 
Board violated the Open Meeting Law.  We remind the Board that the Open Meeting Law and 
Regulations require that any response be sent to both the complainant and the Attorney General.  
See G.L. c. 30A, § 23(b); 940 CMR 29.05(5).   
 
For the reasons stated above, we find that the Board violated the Open Meeting Law by 
posting an insufficiently specific notice for the April 14, 2025, meeting that failed to list the 
names of candidates for reappointment, and by failing to respond to an Open Meeting Law 
complaint within 14 business days.  We order the Board’s immediate and future compliance with 
the law’s requirements, and we caution that similar future violations could be considered 
evidence of intent to violate the law. 
 
We now consider the complaints addressed by this determination to be resolved.  This 
determination does not address any other complaints that may be pending with our office or the 
Board.  Please feel free to contact our office at (617) 963-2540 if you have any questions 
regarding this letter.
