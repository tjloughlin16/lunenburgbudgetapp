RE: Open Meeting Law Complaint 
Dear Attorney Petrini: 
This office received a complaint from Ms. Deborah Butler, dated March 23, 2015, 
alleging that the Framingham Board of Selectmen (the "Board") violated the Open Meeting Law, 
G.L. c. 30A, §§ 18-25. The complaint was originally filed with the Board on February 17, 2015, 
and the Board responded to the original complaint by letter dated March 5, 2015. In her 
complaint, Ms. Butler alleges that the Board entered executive session for improper purposes. 
Following our review, we find that the Board did not violate the Open Meeting Law. In 
reaching this determination, we received the complaint; the Board's response; and the request for 
further review filed with our office. We also reviewed, in camera, the minutes of an executive 
session held by the Board on January 20, 2015. 
FACTS 
We find the facts as follows. On January 14, 2015, the Chair of the Framingham Town 
Meeting Standing Committee on Rules sent a public records request to the Board seeking the 
release of three written legal opinions prepared by Town Counsel. The Board held a meeting on 
January 20, 2015. The notice for this meeting listed three anticipated executive session topics: 
(i) to discuss strategy with respect to pending litigation; (ii) to discuss the potential release of 
past executive session minutes; and (iii) to meet with Town Counsel to discuss the potential 
release of three written legal opinions.1 Following this meeting, the Board sent to the Town 
Meeting Committee Chair three "executive summaries" encapsulating the written legal opinions. 
1 The minutes of the January 20, 2015 executive session have not been released, and we therefore do not recount 
their contents.

DISCUSSION 
The Open Meeting Law requires that meetings of a public body be properly noticed and 
open to members of the public, unless a proper executive session is convened. See G.L. c. 30A, 
§§ 20(a)-(b), 21. A public body may enter executive session only for one of the ten purposes 
enumerated in the law. See G.L. c. 30A, § 21(a). Purpose 7 permits a public body to enter into 
executive session to "comply with, or act under the authority of, any general or special law or 
federal grant-in-aid requirements." G.L. c. 30A, § 21(a)(7). A public body may invoke Purpose 
7 only where a general or special law or federal grant-in-aid requirement requires that the public 
body act in a confidential manner. See Dist. Attorney for the N. Dist. v. Sch. Comm. of 
Wavland. 455 Mass. 561, 569 (2009); OML 2014-97; OML 2013-23.2 
At issue here are two of the three topics discussed by the Board in executive session on 
January 20, 2015.3 The complaint first alleges that the Board improperly discussed the release of 
written legal opinions in executive session. In response, the Board claims that this discussion 
was appropriate for executive session under Purpose 7, to comply with the attorney client-
privilege, as articulated in town bylaws and Suffolk Construction v. DCAM. 449 Mass. 444 
(2007). A public body may enter executive session under Purpose 7 to "act under the authority" 
of the Public Records Law if it is discussing whether to release a document that it may lawfully 
withhold. See OML 2012-104. Here, the Board is the holder of attorney-client privilege and is 
the only body authorized to waive the privilege. See Suffolk Construction. 449 Mass. at 450-
451. The discussion of whether to waive such privilege is the type of discussion that must take 
place at a meeting. See G.L. c. 30A, §18 (defining "Deliberation"). Our review of the January 
20, 2015 executive session minutes shows that the Board discussed Ms. Butler's public records 
request for the written legal opinions and whether to waive its attorney-client privilege with 
respect to those opinions. Because the Board was acting under the authority of the Public 
Records Law, we find no Open Meeting Law violation with respect to this discussion. See G.L. 
c. 3OA, § 21(a)(7); OML 2015-55; OML 2012-104. 
The complaint also contends that the Board's discussion of the potential release of 
executive session minutes was not a proper subject for an executive session. The Open Meeting 
Law permits a public body to convene in executive session in order to review past executive 
session minutes and deliberate on their potential release. See OML 2013-199. Accordingly, we 
find that the Board did not violate the Open Meeting Law by discussing this topic in executive 
session. 
CONCLUSION 
For the reasons stated above, we find that the Board did not violate the Open Meeting 
Law. We now consider the complaint addressed by this determination to be resolved. This
