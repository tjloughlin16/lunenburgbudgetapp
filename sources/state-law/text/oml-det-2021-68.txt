 RE: Open Meeting Law Complaint 
 
 
Dear Mr. Farias: 
 
This office received a complaint from Patrick Higgins on January 6, 2021, alleging that 
the New Bedford City Council’s Committee on Finance (the “Committee”), and specifically its 
Chair Joseph Lopes, violated the Open Meeting Law, G.L. c. 30A, §§ 18-25. The complaint was 
originally filed with the Committee on or about November 19, 2020. You responded on behalf of 
the Committee by letters dated January 7 and February 23, 2021.1 The complaint alleges that the 
Committee violated the Open Meeting Law by failing to take all votes by roll call at the 
Committee’s November 18, 2020, meeting.2        
 
Following our review, we resolve this complaint by informal action in accordance with 
940 CMR 29.07(2)(a). We find, and the Committee acknowledges, that the Committee violated 
the Open Meeting Law by failing to take all votes by roll call during the November 18, 2020, 
meeting at which all members participated remotely, as required by 940 CMR 29.10(7)(c). See, 
 
1 We note that in addition to failing to timely respond to the Open Meeting Law complaint, it appears from the 
response letters that the Committee may not have met to review the Open Meeting Law complaint and to vote to 
respond or to delegate authority to respond to the complaint. We remind the Committee of its obligation to comply 
with all provisions of the Open Meeting Law, including the obligation to meet to review a complaint and formulate a 
response, or delegate that authority, and respond to the complaint within 14 business days. See G.L. c. 30A, § 23(b); 
OML 2017-69; OML 2012-90.   
2 The complaint also alleges that the Committee violated the Law by failing to announce the name of each member

our office, the Complainant withdrew this portion of the complaint, and we do not address it.
