RE: Open Meeting Law Complaint 
Dear Attorney Randazzo; 
This office received a complaint from Judith Stainbrook, dated January 20, 2016, alleging 
that the Sandwich Historic District Committee (the "Committee") violated the Open Meeting 
Law, G.L. c. 30A, §§ 18-25. The complaint was originally filed with the Committee on 
December 10, 2015, and the Committee responded by letter dated January 15, 2016. In her 
complaint, Ms. Stainbrook alleges that the Committee posted an insufficiently specific meeting 
notice. 
Following our review, we find that the Committee did not violate the Open Meeting Law. 
In reaching this determination, we reviewed the original complaint; the Committee's response; 
and the request for further review filed with our office. We also reviewed the notice and minutes 
of a Committee meeting held on December 9, 2015. 
We find the facts as follows. The Committee is a five-member public body. Among the 
Committee's duties is to act on applications for certificates of appropriateness concerning 
construction or alternations made to property. The Committee posted notice of a meeting to be 
held on December 9, 2015. The notice listed, among several others, the following three 
certificates of appropriateness for consideration: 
15-176D Justice Resource Institute 
Demolish garage at 209 and 0 Route 6A, located at Map 67, Lot 13 & 52 
15-177D Justice Resource Institute 
Demolish existing addition at 209 and 0 Route 6A, located at Map 67, Lot 13 & 52

15-178 Justice Resource Institute 
New building added to existing at 209 and 0 Route 6A, located at Map 67, Lot 13 & 52 
The Committee held the December 9, 2015 meeting as planned. During the discussion of 
the above-referenced property, the applicant offered "a brief explanation of the owner's proposed 
usage, which is a school." Ultimately, the Committee voted to continue the discussion of these 
three agenda items. 
The Open Meeting Law requires that public bodies post notice of each meeting "at least 
48 hours prior to such meeting, excluding Saturdays, Sundays and legal holidays" and that every 
notice must include "the date, time and place of such meeting and listings of topics that the chair 
reasonably anticipates will be discussed at the meeting." G.L. c. 30A, § 20(b). Public bodies 
must list topics for discussion with "sufficient specificity to reasonably advise the public of the 
issues to be discussed at the meeting." 940 CMR 29.03(l)(b). 
Here, the complaint alleges that the notice of the December 9, 2015 provides insufficient 
notice regarding the anticipated nature of the discussion because it failed to state that the 
applicant intended to construct a school on the property at issue. We find that the December 9, 
2015 meeting notice met the minimum standards required by the Open Meeting Law. See G.L. 
c. 30A, § 20(b); 940 CMR 29.03(l)(b). The notice identified the property under consideration by 
address and specified the proposed action to be taken. We agree with the complainant that the 
inclusion of information regarding the proposed school would have been helpful for members of 
the public. However, we find that the Committee did not violate the Open Meeting Law with 
respect to its December 9, 2015 meeting notice. See OML 2015-115. 
For the reasons stated above, we find that the Committee did not violate the Open 
Meeting Law. We now consider the complaint addressed by this determination to be resolved. 
This determination does not address any other complaints that may be pending with our office or 
the Committee. Please feel free to contact the Division at (617) 963 - 2540 if you have any 
questions.
