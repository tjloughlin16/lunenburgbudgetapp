FACTS 
 
 We find the facts as follows.  The Council duly posted notice of a meeting to be held on 
June 15, 2026, at 7:30 P.M.  The notice listed 15 main topic headings, one of which was “Public 
Hearings.”  The notice included the following specific item description that further advised the 
public of the nature of the matter to be discussed under this heading: “11843 – Betty Spring 
Road – National Grid to relocate 1 Jointly Owned Pole on Betty Spring Road beginning at a 
point approximately 10 feet south of the centerline of the intersection of Betty Spring Road and 
Linus Allain Ave. Relocate Polie #94-50 on Betty Spring Road 10 feet to the sidewalk at 
42.567436-71.952130.”  One other main topic heading listed on the notice was “Petitions, 
Applications, Communications, Etc.”  The notice included the following specific item 
description that further advised the public of the nature of the matter to be discussed under this 
heading: “11852 – Election of City Clerk. (Appeared before the Special Search Committee for 
the City Clerk 5/7/2026 More Time; 6/8/2026 Referred to City Council).” 
 
 The June 15, 2026, meeting was held as planned.  Council President George Tyros 
opened the public hearing regarding National Grid’s petition to relocate utility infrastructure on 
Betty Spring Road.  A representative of National Grid advised the Council that the petition had 
been revised that morning for installation of a new utility pole, rather than relocation of an 
existing pole, which would support the Advanced Metering Infrastructure equipment for the 
City's upgraded electric meter system.  A representative of Control Point Technologies further 
explained that the proposed pole would be installed between two existing poles to support the 
communications equipment associated with the new meter system.  No member of the public 
spoke in favor or against the petition and President Tyros closed the public hearing.  The Council 
took no further action regarding the petition. 
 
Councillor Judy Mack then presented a report of the Special Search Committee for City 
Clerk, explaining that three members of the Council—President Tyros, Councillor Elizabeth 
Kazinskas, and herself—were members of that Committee.  Councillor Mack stated that the 
Special Search Committee had revised the job descriptions for the City Clerk and Assistant City 
Clerk positions, and then reviewed 49 applications and interviewed three finalists for the position 
of City Clerk.  The Special Search Committee recommended that the Council appoint Jennifer 
Pelavin as City Clerk.  Councillor Mack then nominated Jennifer Pelavin for the position of City 
Clerk for a three-year term beginning July 6, 2026, and the Council unanimously voted by roll 
call to appoint Jennifer Pelavin as City Clerk.  
 
DISCUSSION 
 
The Open Meeting Law was enacted “to eliminate much of the secrecy surrounding 
deliberations and decisions on which public policy is based.”  Ghiglione v. School Committee of 
Southbridge, 376 Mass. 70, 72 (1978).  The Law requires that meetings of a public body be 
properly noticed and open to members of the public, unless an executive session is convened.  
See G.L. c. 30A, §§ 20(a)-(b), 21.  A public body must post notice of every meeting at least 48 
hours in advance, and include the date, time and place of the meeting, as well as “a listing of 
topics that the chair reasonably anticipates will be discussed at the meeting.”  G.L. c. 30A, 
§ 20(b).  The list of topics shall have sufficient specificity to reasonably advise the public of the

issues to be discussed at the meeting.  940 CMR 29.03(l)(b).  A public body need not list each 
and every detail about a topic, nor anticipate on the notice the course of deliberation or the 
outcome of a discussion.  See OML 2022-98; OML 2020-132; OML 2019-134.
3  However, 
notice topics must be described with sufficient specificity to reasonably advise the public of the 
issues to be discussed at the meeting so that the public can make an informed decision about 
whether to attend the meeting in order to observe an anticipated discussion.  See OML 2023-86; 
OML 2019-95; OML 2018-22; OML 2015-74. 
 
The complaints allege that the notice for the Council’s June 15, 2026, meeting was 
insufficiently specific with respect to two topics.  The first complaint alleges that the “Public 
Hearing” topic was insufficient as it “failed to provide the correct information as to what work 
National Grid was planning to do.”  Here, the notice indicated that National Grid was planning to 
relocate a pole on Betty Spring Road.  However, during the meeting, a representative from 
National Grid informed the Council that it planned to install a new pole rather than relocate a 
pole.  We find that the notice was sufficient where the Council only anticipated discussion of a 
pole relocation and was not aware that National Grid intended to install a new pole until the day 
of the meeting.  As such, we do not find a violation of the Open Meeting Law.  See OML 2017-
64. 
 
The second complaint alleges that the notice did not list the name of the candidate to be 
appointed as City Clerk.  When a public body anticipates making appointments at a meeting, the 
meeting notice should include specific details about what appointments will be made and the 
names of anticipated appointees.  See OML 2019-128; OML 2017-177; OML 2017-148; OML 
2016-167.  We find here that the Council anticipated appointing a specifically identified person, 
Jennifer Pelavin, to the position of City Clerk and thus should have included her name on the 
meeting notice.  Therefore, we find that the topic, “Election of City Clerk,” was insufficiently 
specific where it did not list the name of the applicant to be considered and appointed.  See 2015-
196; OML 2014-155; OML 2011-11.  Furthermore, we remind the Council that the specific 
information must be included on or with the notice itself; it is not acceptable for a meeting notice 
to refer members of the public to another location to find the required information or to reference 
a separate list that is available elsewhere.  See OML 2024-236; OML 2020-20; OML 2019-22; 
see also Town of Swansea v. Healey, 2018 WL 8692757, at *3 (Suffolk Sup. Ct. October 29, 
2018).   
 
CONCLUSION 
 
For the reasons stated above, we find that the Council violated the Open Meeting Law by 
failing to list on the notice of the June 15, 2026, meeting the name of the candidate to be 
appointed as City Clerk.  We order the Council’s immediate and future compliance with the 
Open Meeting Law and caution that a finding of a similar violation in the future may be 
considered evidence of intent to violate the Law.
