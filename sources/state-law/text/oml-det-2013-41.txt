This office received a complaint from Monique Manna, dated December 11, 2011, 
alleging that the Town of Southbridge Board of Health (the "Board") violated the Open Meeting 
Law, G.L. c. 30A, §§ 18-25. The complaint, originally filed with the Board on or about 
September 8, 2011, alleges that the Board held a meeting without a quorum present, discussed a 
topic not listed on its meeting notice 48 hours in advance of the meeting, and failed to notify a 
new Board member of that meeting. Ms. Manna requests that the Board make a public apology 
for its actions, attend an Open Meeting Law training session, and elect a new Board chair. The 
Board responded to the original complaint by letter dated November 9, 2011. 
In reaching our determination, we reviewed Ms. Manna's original September 8, 2011 
complaint to the Board; the Board's September 20, 2011 letter requesting additional information 
from Ms. Manna; Ms. Manna's October 4, 2011 letter to the Board providing the requested

FACTS 
Based upon our review of the material listed above, the facts are as follows. 
The Southbridge Department of Public Works ("DPW") initially alerted the Board about 
using chloramines in the Town's public water supply on July 13, 2010, and sent notification to 
the Health Director, Mr. James J. Morin, via email. At the Board's January 20, 2011 meeting, 
Acting DPW Director Hamer Clarke presented the DPW's plan to start the "roll out" of 
chloramines into the Town's water supply "next month," and on March 24, 2011 new DPW 
Director Thomas Daley again discussed chloramines with the Board. The DPW conducted a 
public meeting on chloramines, which the Board attended on May 12, 2011, and on June 30, 
2011 it issued a news release about chloramines. 
Ann Fenwick-Beinema was appointed to the Board of Health in the Summer of 2011. At 
the Town Council's August 8, 2011 meeting, at which Ms. Fenwick-Beinema was present, the 
Council voted to appoint Mr. Robert Checkosky to the Board for a three-year term. The Board's 
composition following his appointment then included Mr. Rinaldo Bernadone, Ms. Fenwick-
Beinema, and Mr. Checkosky. The next topic the Town Council discussed was former Council 
Chair Lazo's order at the July 19, 2010 Council meeting nullifying an expansion of the Board 
from three members to five members. The Council passed a motion to rescind the order and 
expand the Board from three members to five members.

DISCUSSION 
The complaint alleges multiple violations of the Open Meeting Law stemming from the 
Board's August 18, 2011 meeting. We address these issues in turn. 
1. 	 The Board did not Violate the Open Meeting Law by Discussing a Topic that was  
not Listed in the Notice Posted 48 Hours in Advance of the Board's August 18,  
2011 Meeting.  
The Open Meeting Law was enacted "to eliminate much of the secrecy surrounding 
deliberations and decisions on which public policy is based." Ghiglione v. School Committee of 
Southbridge, 376 Mass. 70, 72 (1978). Except in an emergency, a public body must post notice 
of every meeting at least 48 hours prior to such meeting in an easily understandable format 
containing the date, time, and place of the meeting and a list of topics that the chair reasonably 
anticipates will be discussed. G.L. c. 30A, § 21(b). The Law defines "emergency" as "a sudden, 
generally unexpected occurrence or set of circumstances demanding immediate action." G.L. c. 
30A, § 18. Additionally, public bodies must list topics in a meeting notice with "sufficient 
specificity to reasonably advise the public of the issues to be discussed at the meeting." 940 
CMR 29.03(1)(b). 
The Board posted notice for its August 18, 2011 meeting on August 11, 2011 and listed, 
among other topics, "Welcome Board Member, Robert Checkosky[,]" "Letter to DPW 
[Department of Public Works] re: Chloramines in Spanish[,]" and "Re-organization[.]" It is 
unclear whether the posted discussion topic "Chloramines in Spanish" referred to the DPW's 
chloramine roll out, or to some other aspect of the Chloramines issue. 
During the Board's August 18, 2011 meeting, the Board voted to suspend the use of 
chloramines. Although we were not able to obtain a copy of the minutes of that meeting, and no 
audio or video recording exists, an August 22, 2011 Southbridge Evening News article stated that 
the Board took an "emergency vote" on the issue. Because notice was posted for the August 18, 
2011 meeting at least 48 hours in advance and listed the date, time, and place of the meeting, the 
August 18, 2011 meeting was not an emergency meeting. See G.L. c. 30A, § 18. Furthermore, 
the DPW informed the Board more than a year before this meeting that it planned to gradually 
roll out chloramines in the public water supply. However, it is not clear from the material we 
reviewed that the Chair reasonably anticipated this particular discussion about chloramines 48

2. The Board did not Violate the Open Meeting Law by Failing to Notify a New 
Board Member of the Meeting.  
On August 11, 2011, the Board posted notice for its August 18, 2011 meeting. The 
complainant alleges that the Board failed to notify Mr. Checkosky, a new Board member, of that 
meeting. The Open Meeting Law's notice posting requirements do not require public bodies to 
provide additional notice of meetings to specific individuals or groups of individuals. See OML 
2011-41. Because the Board is not required to notify individual Board members of a meeting in 
addition to a timely posted meeting notice, we find that the Board did not violate the Open 
Meeting Law. We do not address whether any other law may require such notice. However, we 
strongly encourage chairs of public bodies to ensure that all members are aware of meeting times 
and locations. 
3. The Board Failed to Create and Approve Minutes of its August 18, 2011 Meeting 
in a Timely Manner.  
Although not raised in the complaint, we note that the Board failed to create and approve 
the minutes of its August 18, 2011 meeting in a timely manner. The Open Meeting Law requires 
that the "[m]inutes of all open sessions shall be created and approved in a timely manner." G.L. 
c. 30A, § 22(c). This responsibility falls upon the public body. We understand that the Board 
made efforts to obtain the August 18, 2011 draft meeting minutes created by Ms. Fenwick-
Beinema, but was ultimately unable to do so. Because the responsibility for creating approving 
minutes remains with the Board, it should have created and approved a new version of the 
minutes in a timely manner.

CONCLUSION 
For the reasons stated above, we find that the Board did not violate the Open Meeting 
Law. Although the Board did not violate the Law as alleged by the complainant, it did fail to 
create and approve minutes from its August 18, 2011 meeting in a timely manner. We therefore 
order the Board to create and approve minutes for this meeting, to the best of its ability, within 
sixty (60) days of receipt of this letter. 
We now consider the complaint addressed by this determination to be resolved. This 
determination does not address any other complaints which may be pending with our office or 
the Board. Please feel free to contact me if you have any questions or believe any facts in this 
letter to be inaccurate.
