 RE: Open Meeting Law Complaint 
Dear Attorney Callanan: 
This office received a complaint from Gerard Mackin on April 22, 2020, alleging that the 
Weymouth School Committee (the “Committee”) violated the Open Meeting Law, G.L. c. 30A, 
§§ 18-25. The complaint was originally filed with the Committee on February 10.1 Although the 
Committee requested, and was granted, two extensions of time from our office, the Committee 
did not respond to the complaint.2 In his complaint, Mr. Mackin alleges that the Committee 
added an item to its agenda within 48 hours before the meeting.  
We resolve this complaint by informal action in accordance with 940 CMR 29.07(2)(a), 
after reviewing the complaint, the Committee’s meeting notice website, and the complainant’s 
request for further review. Following our review, we find that the Committee did not violate the 
Open Meeting Law. 
The Open Meeting Law requires that public bodies post notice of each meeting “at least 
48 hours prior to such meeting, excluding Saturdays, Sundays and legal holidays” and that every 
notice include “the date, time and place of such meeting and a listing of topics that the chair 
reasonably anticipates will be discussed at the meeting.” G.L. c. 30A, § 20(b). A public body 
may amend the notice within 48 hours of a meeting if a previously unanticipated topic comes to 
the Chair’s attention. See 940 CMR 29.03(1)(d); OML 2020-29; OML 2019-131.3

On February 4 at 5:09 PM, the Committee posted a meeting notice for its February 6 
meeting to take place at 5:30 PM. On February 5, the Committee received an Open Meeting Law 
complaint from Mr. Mackin. On February 5 at 5:13 PM, the Committee posted an amended 
notice which added one item: “Refer OML of February 5, 2020 filed by Gerard Mackin against 
Weymouth School Committee, Chair Belmarsh to Town Solicitor Callanan.”  
 The complaint alleges that the Committee violated the Open Meeting Law by adding the 
topic of Mr. Mackin’s February 5 complaint to its meeting notice within 48 hours of the 
February 6 meeting. A public body may amend a meeting notice within 48 hours of a meeting to 
include an unanticipated topic and then discuss that topic during the meeting. See OML 2019-
131. Here, the topic was not anticipated by the chair 48 hours in advance of the meeting, as Mr. 
Mackin did not file his complaint until the day before the meeting was scheduled to take place. 
As such, the chair could not have reasonably anticipated the topic 48 hours prior to the 
meeting. See OML 2017-2; OML 2013-87. We find that the Committee did not violate the Open 
Meeting Law by amending the notice to include this unanticipated topic. In fact, although the 
Committee could have discussed the unanticipated topic without adding it to the meeting notice 
at all, the Committee followed our recommended practice of amending its meeting notice to 
include a topic that arose fewer than 48 hours before a meeting, so that members of the public 
are given advance notice of items to be discussed. See OML 2015-111; OML 2015-76. 
 For the reasons stated above, w e find that the Committee did not violate the Open 
Meeting Law. We now consider the complaint addressed by this determination to be resolved. 
This determination does not address any other complaints that may be pending with our office or 
the Committee. Please feel free to contact the Division at (617) 963 - 2540 if you have any 
questions.
