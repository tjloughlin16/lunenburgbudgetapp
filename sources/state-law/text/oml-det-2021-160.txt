The Open Meeting Law was enacted “to eliminate much of the secrecy surrounding 
deliberations and decisions on which public policy is based.”  Ghiglione v. School Council of 
Southbridge, 376 Mass. 70, 72 (1978).  The law requires that meetings of a public body be 
properly noticed and open to members of the public, unless an executive session is convened.  
See G.L. c. 30A, §§ 20(a)-(b), 21.  Generally, access to a meeting must include the opportunity 
to be physically present, as well as to see and hear what is being discussed by the members of the 
public body.  See OML 2017-135; OML 2015-38; OML 2014-3; OML 2013-189.3  Public bodies 
are permitted to meet entirely remotely, provided that the public body provides adequate, 
alternative means of public access to the deliberations of the public body while those 
deliberations are occurring.  See St. 2021, c. 20, § 20 (Act Extending Certain COVID-19 
Measures Adopted During the State of Emergency); Governor’s Order Suspending Certain 
Provisions of the Open Meeting Law, G. L. c. 30A, § 20 (Mar. 12, 2020). 
 
The complaints allege that the Council meetings held on October 26, 2020, and February 
3, 2021, were inaccessible because the public was unable to hear the discussions that occurred.  
Both the October and February meetings were held remotely via Zoom.  After reviewing a video 
recording of the February 3, 2021, meeting, we note that the video cut out many times during the 
one hour and forty-five-minute meeting.  The interruptions, which lasted only a few seconds, 
occurred within the first hour of the meeting; no interruptions occurred during the remaining 40 
minutes of the meeting.  Despite the interruptions that occurred during the February 3, 2021, 
meeting, we find that the audio was otherwise clear and members of the public could still 
meaningfully follow the proceedings as they occurred.  As such, we find the meeting was 
accessible and the Council did not violate the Open Meeting Law.  See OML 2018-20.    
 
We were unable to review a video recording of the October 26, 2020, meeting.4  
However, Mr. Magnan acknowledged that the Council experienced some technical difficulties 
with remote meetings last year and that the voices of Council members faded in and out and 
could be difficult to hear at times.  Nevertheless, without a meaningful way to review the 
October 26, 2020, meeting, we are unable to make a finding as to whether this particular meeting 
was accessible.  See OML 2021-147.  We understand that the Council has since upgraded the 
sound system within the Council Chambers and purchased new microphones which has reduced 
audio issues.   
 
For the reasons stated above, we find that the Council did not violate the Open Meeting 
Law.  We now consider the complaints addressed by this determination to be resolved.  This 
determination does not address any other complaints that may be pending with the Council or 
with our office.  Please feel free to contact our office at (617) 963-2540 if you have any 
questions regarding this letter.
