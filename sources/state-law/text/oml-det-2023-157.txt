 RE: Open Meeting Law Complaint 
 
Dear Attorney Shove: 
 
 This office received a complaint from Walter Hamilton on April 17, 2023, alleging that 
the South Hadley Electric Light Department Board of Commissioners (the “Board”) violated the 
Open Meeting Law, G.L. c. 30A, §§ 18-25.  The complaint was originally filed with the Board 
on March 16, 2023, and you responded, on behalf of the Board, by letter dated March 31, 2023.  
The complaint alleges that the minutes of the executive session meeting held on December 20, 
2022, are insufficient. 
 
We resolve this matter by informal action, in ac
 cordance with 940 CMR 29.07(2)(a), 
after reviewing the original complaint, the Board’s response to the complaint, the complaint filed 
with our office requesting further review, and the notice and executive session minutes of the 
Board meeting held on December 20, 2022.  In addition, we communicated with you on 
September 7, 2023.  Following our review, we find that the Board violated the Open Meeting 
Law in the way alleged.   
 
The O
pen Meeting Law requires that a public body “create and maintain accurate 
minutes of all meetings, including executive sessions, setting forth the date, time and place, the 
members present or absent, a summary of the discussions on each subject, a list of documents 
and other exhibits used at the meeting, the decisions made and the actions taken at each meeting, 
including the record of all votes.”  G.L. c. 30A, § 22(a).  When reviewing minutes for 
compliance with the Open Meeting Law, we look for substantial compliance with the accuracy

requirement.  See OML 2017-104; OML 2016-105; OML 2013-64.1  By substantial compliance, 
we mean that the minutes should contain enough detail and accuracy so that a member of the 
public who did not attend the meeting could read the minutes and have a clear understanding of 
what occurred.  See OML 2019-163; OML 2012-106.  Although a transcript of the discussions is 
not required, and the minutes need not include every remark or opinion presented, minutes must 
include a substantive summary of the discussion on each topic.  See OML 2020-18.  Minutes of 
executive sessions must include the same level of detail as is required for open sessions.  See 
G.L. c. 30A, § 22(a); OML 2020-53. 
 
The B
oard convened in executive session on December 20, 2022, “to discuss contract 
negotiations for nonunion staff.”2  During the meeting, which lasted approximately 40 minutes, 
the Board discussed a contract amendment and possible salary increase for the Light 
Department’s General Manager.  However, the minutes of the Board’s executive session include 
little more than a statement that the Board engaged in a discussion about the contract 
amendment, without any summary of the actual discussions.  The Open Meeting Law requires 
that meeting minutes include more than a statement that a public body held a discussion about a 
specified topic; the Law requires that the minutes summarize the substance of the public body’s 
discussion.  See OML 2020-53; OML 2019-167; OML 2018-8.  Although the Board may have 
been concerned with the sensitive nature of certain matters that were discussed in executive 
session, creating insufficiently detailed meeting minutes is not a permissible solution.

We order the Board, within 30 days of the date of this letter, to amend the minutes of its 
December
 20, 2022, executive session to accurately summarize its discussion of the Manager’s 
contract and to certify to this office within thirty (30) days of the date of this letter that it has 
done so.  See 940 C.M.R. 29.07(4).  If, thereafter, the Board determines that its amended minutes 
may be released to the public subject to redactions, it may make such redactions.  If Mr. 
Hamilton disputes the validity of any redactions made, he may file an appeal with the Supervisor 
of Records in the Secretary of the Commonwealth’s Office. 
 
For the
 reasons stated above, we find that the Board violated the Open Meeting Law by 
creating insufficiently detailed minutes of the executive session meeting held on December 20, 
2022.  In addition to our order that the Board amend the minutes of the December 20, 2022, 
meeting, we order the Board’s immediate and future compliance with the law’s requirements, 
and we caution that similar future violations could be considered evidence of intent to violate the 
law did not violate the Open Meeting Law.   
 
We now consider the complaint addressed by this determination to be resolved.  This 
determination does not address any other complaints that may be pending with the Board or with 
our office.  Please feel free to contact our office at (617) 963-2540 if you have any questions 
regarding this letter.
