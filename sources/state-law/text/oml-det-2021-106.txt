 RE: Open Meeting Law Complaint 
 
Dear Attorney Mead: 
 
This office received a complaint from Allen Waller on February 12, 2021, alleging that 
the Marblehead Board of Health (the “Board”) violated the Open Meeting Law, G.L. c. 30A,    
§§ 18-25.  The complaint was originally filed with the Board on January 6, 2021, and you 
responded, on behalf of the Board, by letter dated February 23, 2021.1  The complaint alleges 
that the Board failed to maintain minutes of an executive session meeting held on January 15, 
2019. 
 
We resolve this matter by informal action, in accordance with 940 CMR 29.07(2)(a), 
after reviewing the original complaint, the Board’s response to the complaint, the complaint filed 
with our office requesting further review, and the open and executive session minutes of the 
Board meeting held on January 15, 2019.  Following our review, we find that the Board did not 
violate the Open Meeting Law as alleged. 
 
The Open Meeting Law requires that a public body “create and maintain accurate 
minutes of all meetings, including executive sessions, setting forth the date, time and place, the 
members present or absent, a summary of the discussions on each subject, a list of documents 
and other exhibits used at the meeting, the decisions made and the actions taken at each meeting,

compliance with the Open Meeting Law, we look for substantial compliance with the accuracy 
requirement.  See OML 2016-105; OML 2013-64.2  By substantial compliance, we mean that the 
minutes should contain enough detail and accuracy so that a member of the public who did not 
attend the meeting could read the minutes and have a clear understanding of what occurred.  See 
OML 2012-106.   
 
 On December 1, 2020, the complainant requested copies of executive session minutes of 
several Board meetings.  In response to the request, the Board provided copies of minutes of a 
January 15, 2019, meeting.  The minutes included the date, time and place of the meeting, the 
members who were present and absent, and the announcement and unanimous vote by roll call to 
convene in executive session “under G.L. c. 30A, § 21(a)(3) for the purpose of discussing 
strategy with respect to litigation namely Maverick Construction Management Services, Inc. vs. 
Town of Marblehead” and “under G.L. c. 30A, § 21(a)(1) to discuss” two open meeting law 
complaints filed by the complainant.  The minutes did not include any details or summaries of 
the discussions that took place regarding the executive session topics.   
 
In determination OML 2019-64, this office resolved a complaint filed by the complainant 
regarding the sufficiency of the notice for the Board’s January 15, 2019, meeting.  In that 
determination, we noted that “during the executive session, the Board discussed pending 
litigation involving a dispute with an engineer and contractor hired to do work on a landfill in 
town.  The Board also discussed two open meeting law complaints filed by the complainant and 
unanimously voted to have town legal counsel respond to the complaints on behalf of the 
Board.”  Because the minutes of the January 15, 2019, meeting provided to the complainant in 
response to his December 1, 2020, request did not contain any reference to discussions involving 
pending litigation or previous open meeting law complaints, the complainant filed the complaint 
at issue here asserting that the Board failed to maintain sufficiently detailed executive session 
minutes.    
    
 We agree that the minutes provided to the complainant in response to his December 
request did not contain any details or summaries of the discussions that took place during the 
January 15, 2019, executive session meeting.  However, the minutes provided to the complainant 
on February 23, 2021, in response to his Open Meeting Law complaint did provide such detail.  
We note that the executive session minutes of the January 15, 2019, meeting provided to the 
complainant in February are the exact same minutes that were provided to this office in 2019.  It 
appears that in response to his December request, the Board erroneously provided the 
complainant with a copy of the minutes of the open session portion of the January 15, 2019, 
meeting, rather than the executive session minutes that he had requested.  We find that the 
minutes of the January 15, 2019, executive session meeting are sufficiently detailed and 
therefore the Board did not violate the Open Meeting Law. 
 
For the reasons stated above, we find that the Board did not violate the Open Meeting 
Law.  We now consider the complaint addressed by this determination to be resolved.  This
