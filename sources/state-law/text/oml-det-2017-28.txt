This office received a complaint from John Anzivino, dated December 7, 2016, alleging 
that the Stoughton Board of Selectmen (the "Board") violated the Open Meeting Law, G.L. c. 
3OA, §§ 18-25. The complaint was originally filed with the Board on October 20, 2016, and the 
Board's administrative assistant responded on behalf of the Board by letter dated November 2, 
2016. The Board then sent a supplementary response by letter dated January 27, 2017. In his 
complaint, Mr. Anzivino alleges that the Board discussed an anticipated topic that was not listed 
on a meeting notice. 
Following our review, we resolve this matter by informal action, in accordance with 940 
CMR 29.07(2)(a). We find that the Board did not violate the Open Meeting Law. 
The complaint concerns a Board meeting held on October 18, 2016. Prior to this 
meeting, the Board chair was aware that a representative from Sonic Drive-In would be present 
at the meeting to request a common victualler license. However, the topic was not listed on the 
October 18, 2016 meeting notice. During the meeting, before the matter of the license was 
substantively discussed, it became clear that the topic had been omitted from the meeting notice 
in error. The Board members discussed with the Sonic Drive-In representative whether the 
discussion could be rescheduled for an upcoming meeting. It was agreed that the matter would 
be postponed until the Board's October 24, 2016 meeting. The notice of the Board's October 24, 
2016 meeting included the Sonic Drive-In matter, and the Board discussed the license 
application during that meeting, ultimately voting to grant it. A meeting notice must include 
those topics for discussion the chair reasonably anticipates at least 48 hours prior to the meeting. 
G.L. c. 30A, § 20(b). While the complaint is correct in stating that the Board discussed the Sonic 
Drive-in application during its October 18, 2016 meeting inasmuch as it discussed rescheduling 
discussion to a later meeting, discussions of scheduling or procedural information are exempt 
from the Open Meeting Law's definition of "deliberation." G.L. c. 30A, § 18. We therefore find

While not raised, it appears that the Board's administrative assistant responded to the 
present complaint before the Board reviewed it. Within 14 days of receipt of an Open Meeting 
Law complaint, a public body must review the complaint's allegations; take remedial action, if 
appropriate; and send to the Attorney General a copy of the complaint and a description of any 
remedial action taken. G.L. c. 30A, § 23(b); 940 CMR 29.05(5). The body must review the 
complaint before authorizing legal counsel, or any other individual, to respond on its behalf. 940 
CMR 29.05(4); OML 2015-24, We take this opportunity to remind the Board of its obligations 
under the Open Meeting Law, 
We now consider the complaint addressed by this determination to be resolved. This 
determination does not address any other complaints that may be pending with our office or the 
Board, Please feel free to contact the Division at (617) 963 - 2540 if you have any questions.
