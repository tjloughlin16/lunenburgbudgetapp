RE: Open Meeting Law Complaint 
Dear Attorney Riley: 
 
This office received a complaint from Susan and Robert Butler on March 18, 2020, 
alleging that the Sheffield Board of Selectmen (the “Board”) violated the Open Meeting Law, 
G.L. c. 30A, §§ 18-25.  The complaint was originally filed with the Board on January 17, 2020, 
and you responded, on behalf of the Board, by letter dated January 30, 2020.
1  The complaint 
alleges that the Board’s November 5, 2019, meeting minutes are insufficiently detailed.   
 
We resolve this matter by informal action, in accordance with 940 CMR 29.07(2)(a), 
after reviewing the original complaint, the Board’s response to the complaint, the complaint filed 
with our office requesting further review, and the open session minutes of the Board’s November 
5, 2019, and December 9, 2019, meetings.  We also spoke with Ms. Butler via telephone on July 
1, 2020.  We find that the Board violated the Open Meeting Law in the way alleged.   
 
The Open Meeting Law requires that a public body “create and maintain accurate 
minutes of all meetings, including executive sessions, setting forth the date, time and place, the 
members present or absent, a summary of the discussions on each subject, a list of documents

including the record of all votes.”  G.L. c. 30A, § 22(a).  When reviewing minutes for 
compliance with the Open Meeting Law, we look for substantial compliance with the accuracy 
requirement.  See OML 2016-105; OML 2013-64.2  By substantial compliance, we mean that the 
minutes should contain enough detail and accuracy so that a member of the public who did not 
attend the meeting could read the minutes and have a clear understanding of what occurred.  See 
OML 2012-106.  While minutes must include a summary of the discussion on each topic, a 
transcript is not required, and the minutes do not need to include every remark or opinion 
presented.  See OML 2012-29; OML 2011-55.   
 
The complaint alleges that the November 5, 2019, meeting minutes failed to include an 
accurate summary of 56 topics that the Board discussed during the meeting.  Here, the Board 
held a retreat meeting in Connecticut on November 5, 2019, that lasted approximately five hours.  
The meeting minutes state, in pertinent part, the following: 1) that “[d]iscussion ensued 
regarding the following” with 20 topics then listed; 2) that Town Administrator Rhonda 
LaBombard “discussed the following matters with the Board” with eight topics then listed; 3) the 
“goals for FY2020” of each of the three Board members with a list of three to seven topics under 
the name of each Board member; 4) a discussion of the revolving fund for Fire Department 
increases, stipends, budgeting and increasing pay for training; 5) that “there will be increases in 
the budget for FY2021. Discussion ensued on the following” with four topics then listed; and 6) 
“outstanding issues that are currently with Town Counsel.”  Not included in the minutes are any 
details or summaries of the discussions that took place regarding any of the above topics or 
issues.  The Open Meeting Law requires that meeting minutes include more than a statement that 
a public body held a discussion about a specified topic; the law requires that the minutes 
summarize the discussions that were held.  See OML 2019-167; OML 2018-8.  We therefore 
find that the Board violated the Open Meeting Law by failing to draft and approve accurate 
minutes for its November 5, 2019, meeting.  We order the Board to revise the November 5, 2019, 
meeting minutes to include a summary of each discussion, with sufficient detail and accuracy so 
that a member of the public who did not attend the meeting could read the minutes and have a 
clear understanding of what occurred. 
 
We take this opportunity to advise the Board of the Attorney General’s recommendations 
as to best practices for drafting meeting minutes.  In particular, when summarizing discussions, 
we encourage public bodies to include dissenting or minority opinions whenever possible.  
Where significant debate on a matter occurs and it is clear that one or more members of the body 
and/or the public disagree with the decision ultimately taken by the public body, public bodies 
should note the opposing viewpoints in the meeting minutes.  While it is not necessary to record 
the comments of every speaker, and we acknowledge the difficulty of doing so where there are 
many speakers whose remarks may even overlap, if a particular individual speaks at some length 
or is the only one to offer an argument for or against a proposal, that person and his or her 
comments should be identified in the minutes.  See OML 2014-108; OML 2013-47; OML 2012-
29.

Finally, although not raised in the complaint, we note our concern with the Board’s 
failure to timely respond to Ms. Butler’s request for the November 5, 2019, meeting minutes.3  
Here, Ms. Butler submitted a written request for the meeting minutes on December 17, 2019, but 
the Board did not respond until December 30, 2019 – 13 days later.  The minutes of an open 
session, if they exist and whether approved or in draft form, shall be made available upon request 
by any person within 10 days.  G.L. c. 30A, § 22(c).  If minutes do not yet exist at the time of a 
request, the public body is still required to respond to the request within 10 days with an 
explanation of whether the minutes do or do not exist in either approved or draft form.  See OML 
2017-50; OML 2016-71; OML 2015-173.  We remind the Board of its obligations under the law. 
 
 For the reasons stated above, we find that the Board violated the Open Meeting Law by 
failing to include sufficient detail in the minutes of its November 5, 2019, meeting.  We order the 
Board to revise those meeting minutes and to certify to this office within sixty (60) days of the 
date of this letter that it has done so.  See 940 C.M.R. 29.07(4).  We also order the Board’s 
immediate and future compliance with the law’s requirements, and we caution that similar future 
violations could be considered evidence of intent to violate the law.   
 
We now consider the complaint addressed by this determination to be resolved.  This 
determination does not address any other complaints that may be pending with the Board or with 
our office.  Please feel free to contact our office at (617) 963-2540 if you have any questions 
regarding this letter.
