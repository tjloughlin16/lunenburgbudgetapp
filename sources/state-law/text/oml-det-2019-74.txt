RE: Open Meeting Law Complaint
Dear Mr. Fair:
This office received a complaint from George Harris on May 31, alleging that the 
Wayland Conservation Commission (the “Commission”) violated the Open Meeting Law, G.L. 
c. 30A, §§ 18-25.1 The complaint was originally filed with the Commission on April 22, and the 
Commission responded by email dated April 23. In his complaint, Mr. Harris alleges that the 
Commission violated the Open Meeting Law by failing to timely notify this office of having 
received a complaint and by failing to provide this office with a copy of that complaint.
We resolve this complaint by informal action, in accordance with 940 CMR 29.07(2)(a), 
after reviewing the original complaint, the Commission’s response to the complaint, and the 
complaint filed with our office requesting further review.
The Open Meeting Law requires that a public body “shall, within 14 business days of 
receipt of a complaint, send a copy of the complaint to the attorney general and notify the 
attorney general of any remedial action taken.” G.L. c. 30A, § 23(b); see also 940 CMR 
29.05(5).
On March 5, Mr. Harris filed a complaint with the Commission alleging that the 
Commission entered into executive session for an improper purpose during its February 7 
meeting. Although the Commission’s response letter was dated March 25, a copy of that 
response was not provided to the complainant or to this office until April 3, 21 business days 
after the Commission received the complaint. The Commission also did not include a copy of 
the complaint with its response to this office. We find that the Commission violated the Open 
Meeting Law by failing to timely notify this office of its response to the March 5 complaint and

by failing to provide this office with a copy of that complaint within 14 business days. See OML 
2013-176; OML 2013-124. While we recognize that the Commission’s short delay in sending its 
response likely did not compromise the Open Meeting Law’s overarching goal of enhanced 
government transparency, we nonetheless are constrained to find that the Commission violated 
the law.
We order the Commission’s immediate and future compliance with the law’s 
requirements, and we caution that similar future violations could be considered evidence of 
intent to violate the law. We now consider the complaint addressed by this determination to be 
resolved. This determination does not address any other complaints that may be pending with 
the Commission or with our office. Please feel free to contact the Division at (617) 963-2540 if 
you have any questions.
