 RE: Open Meeting Law Complaint 
 
Dear Attorney Mejia: 
 
This determination addresses a complaint filed by Steven Fitzgerald alleging that the 
Everett Zoning Board of Appeals (the “Board”) violated the Open Meeting Law, G.L. c. 30A, 
§§ 18-25.1 The complaint was originally filed with the Board on or about June 21, 2021.2 It does 
not appear that the Board responded to the complaint. The complaint alleges that the Board failed 
to meet and review an Open Meeting Law complaint filed with the Board on June 14. 
 
Following our review, we resolve this complaint by informal action in accordance with 
940 CMR 29.07(2)(a), and find that the Board violated the Open Meeting Law as alleged. In 
reaching this determination we reviewed the original Open Meeting Law complaint and the 
notice and minutes of the Board’s June 21 meeting. Additionally, we communicated with the 
City Solicitor3 via email.  
 
The Open Meeting Law requires that within 14 business days of receipt of a complaint, 
the public body review the complaint’s allegations; take remedial action, if appropriate; and send 
to the complainant a response and a description of any remedial action taken. G.L. c. 30A, §

23(b); 940 CMR 29.05(5). The public body shall simultaneously notify the Attorney General that 
it has sent such materials to the complainant and shall provide the Attorney General with a copy 
of the complaint, the response, and a description of any remedial action taken. Id. Thus, a public 
body must meet to review the complaint and formulate a response, or meet to delegate that 
authority, and respond to the complaint within 14 business days. See G.L. c. 30A, § 23(b); OML 
2017-69; OML 2012-90.4  
 
Proper procedure requires that a public body review a complaint before authorizing an 
individual, such as legal counsel, to respond on its behalf. See OML 2017-132; OML 2012- 95; 
OML 2011-6. A public body may review the complaint at a meeting and then authorize an 
individual to prepare and send the response, or the individual may receive the complaint and 
draft a response for the public body to review at a meeting, but the public body may not remove 
itself entirely from the process. See OML 2020-164. Once a public body has reviewed a 
complaint during a meeting, its decision to simply refer the complaint, rather than discuss its 
substance, is the public body’s prerogative. See OML 2019-40; OML 2017-96.  
 
Here, a complaint was filed with the Board on June 14, and the City Solicitor responded 
to the complaint by letter dated June 21. We find that the Board did not review the complaint and 
authorize a response at any point between June 14 and June 21. Therefore, we find that the Board 
violated the Open Meeting Law by failing to meet to review the complaint prior to sending the 
June 21 response. See OML 2021-185.  
 
We order the Board’s immediate and future compliance with the law’s requirements, and 
we caution that similar future violations could be considered evidence of intent to violate the 
law. We now consider the complaint addressed by this determination to be resolved. This 
determination does not address any other complaints that may be pending with the Board or with 
our office. Please feel free to contact our office at (617) 963-2540 if you have any questions 
regarding this letter.
