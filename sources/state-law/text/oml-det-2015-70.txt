RE: Open Meeting Law Complaint 
Dear Attorney Riley: 
This office received a complaint from Sean Murphy, dated March 9, 2015, alleging that 
the Hinsdale Board of Selectmen (the "Board") violated the Open Meeting Law, G.L. c. 30A, §§ 
18-25. The complaint specifically alleges that following a December 2, 2014 Board meeting. 
Selectman Bruce Marshall unlawfully provided confidential information to a group of 
individuals who had attended the meeting. The complaint was originally filed with the Board on 
February 13, 2015, and Selectman Marshall responded on behalf of the Board by letter dated 
February 26, 2015. 
Following our review, we resolve this complaint by informal action in accordance with 
940 CMR 29.07(2)(a), and find that the Board did not violate the Open Meeting Law. The 
complaint alleges that following the Board's December 2, 2014 executive session meeting. 
Selectman Marshall unlawfully shared his notes from the meeting and divulged confidential 
information to a group of individuals who were gathered outside the meeting. The information 
Selectman Marshall allegedly provided to the group included information regarding Mr. 
Murphy's application for employment with the Town, which was considered by the Board in 
executive session.1 The Open Meeting Law allows public bodies to discuss certain topics behind 
closed doors if the body believes confidentiality is required. G.L. c. BOA, § 21. However, the 
law does not require that public body members keep topics discussed in executive session 
confidential nor does it impose penalties for members' failure to do so. See id; OML 2014-91; 
1 The minutes of the Board's December 2, 2014 executive session do not identify which of the ten permissible 
purposes for executive session the Board invoked to justify the executive session, as required by G.L. c. 30A, § 
21(b)(3). We remind the Board that its executive session discussion must fit within one or more of the ten 
enumerated executive session purposes outlined in the Open Meeting Law. See G.L. c. 30A, § 21(a).

OML 2013-200.2 In Mr. Murphy's March 9, 2015 letter to our office he asserts that G.L. c. 
30A, § 22(e) required confidentiality of the documents at issue here. As this section concerns 
only documents used at an open session meeting, we find it is inapplicable to the present 
situation, which concerns documents allegedly used during an executive session discussion. See 
G.L. c. BOA, 22(e). 
For these reasons, we find that, even if taken as true, the complainant's allegations do not 
constitute a violation of the Open Meeting Law. We express no opinion as to whether other 
laws, including those enforced by the State Ethics Commission, may require public body 
members to maintain the confidentiality of such sessions.
