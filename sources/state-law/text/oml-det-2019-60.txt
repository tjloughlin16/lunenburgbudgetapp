RE: Open  Meeting Law Complaint
Dear Attorney Coughlin:
This office received a complaint from Patrick Higgins, on October 29, 2018, alleging that 
the Fall River Redevelopment Authority Preliminary Screening Committee (the “Committee”) 
violated the Open Meeting Law, G.L. c. 30A, §§ 18-25. The complaint was originally filed with 
the Committee on September 18, 2018, and the Committee responded by letter dated October 17, 
2018. In his complaint, Mr. Higgins alleges that the Committee did not post notice for its August 
30, 2018, meeting.
Following our review, we resolve this complaint by informal action in accordance with 
940 CMR 29.07(2)(a) and find that the Committee violated the Open Meeting Law by failing to 
post notice for its August 30, 2018, meeting. In reaching a determination, we reviewed the 
original complaint, the Committee’s response and the request for further review.
The Open Meeting Law requires that all meetings of a public body be noticed and open to 
the public. G.L. c. 30A, § 20(a), (b). Mr. Higgins alleges, and the Committee concedes, that no 
notice was posted for its first meeting on August 30, 2018. The Committee explains that this 
was an oversight since the newly-formed Committee had not yet chosen a Chair. The Committee 
held a duly noticed meeting on September 19, 2018, and, after a restatement of its prior 
deliberations, voted to ratify the prior deliberations and votes taken at the August 30, 2018, 
meeting.
The goal of this office in enforcing the Open Meeting Law is achieving compliance with 
that law. See OML 2015-118. Where a body acknowledges it failed to fully comply with the 
law and has already taken remedial action to rectify a violation and ensure compliance going 
forward, we may take these efforts into account when deciding whether further relief is 
warranted. Accordingly, we find that the Committee has taken adequate remedial measures and 
no further relief is required. Furthermore, although we acknowledge the role of the complainant
