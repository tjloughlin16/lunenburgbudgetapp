the Fall River Zoning Board of Appeals (the "Board") violated the Open Meeting Law, G.L. c.

DISCUSSION 
The Open Meeting Law requires that a public body "create and maintain accurate 
minutes of all meetings, including executive sessions, setting forth the date, time and place, the 
members present or absent, a summary of the discussions on each subject, a list of documents 
and other exhibits used at the meeting, the decisions made and the actions taken at each meeting, 
including the record of all votes." G.L. c. 30A, § 22(a). The meeting minutes must be reviewed 
and approved in a timely manner. G.L. c. 30A, § 22(c). The Open Meeting Law itself does not 
define "timely manner." However, the Attorney General's Open Meeting Law regulations 
provide that "timely manner" means "within the next three public body meetings or within 30 
days, whichever is later, unless the public body can show good cause for further delay." 940 
CMR 29.11(2). 
The complaint alleges that the Board failed to approve meeting minutes within the time 
frame required by law. The Board approved the October 19, 2017, minutes on January 18, 2018. 
As the Board met only once between these two dates, the approval of the minutes occurred 
within the three meetings allowed for by the Open Meeting Law, and was thus timely. With 
respect to the February 15, March 15, and April 19, 2018 meeting minutes, the Board also did 
not violate the Open Meeting Law. See id. The Board approved these three sets of minutes 
during the May 17, 2018 meeting. Therefore, these minutes were timely approved within three 
meetings, as required. 
With respect to the November 16, 2017, and January 18, 2018, meeting minutes, 
however, the Board violated the Open Meeting Law by failing to timely approve the minutes. 
See id. In order to have been timely, the Board was required to approve these minutes by no 
later than its February 15 and March 15, 2018, meetings, respectively. The Board's legal 
counsel argues that due to staff absences, the Board had good cause as to the delay. We 
understand the demands on a Board with limited staff; however the Board is still obligated to 
timely approve minutes. See OML 2017-115.4 If staff resources are not available to create 
minutes for the Board to approve, then Board members must take on this responsibility to ensure 
compliance with the law. Id. Accordingly, we find that the Board failed to approve minutes of 
its November 16, 2017, and January 18, 2018, meetings in a timely manner. 
CONCLUSION 
For the reasons stated above, we find that the Board violated the Open Meeting Law with 
respect to the timeliness of approval of minutes of meetings held on November 16, 2017, and 
January 18, 2018. However, we find no violation with respect to the other meeting minutes 
referenced in the complaint. We order the Board's immediate and future compliance with the 
law's requirements, and we caution that similar future violations could be considered evidence of
