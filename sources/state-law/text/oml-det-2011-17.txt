On December 6, 2010, this office received a complaint filed by Ms. Mary Arata alleging 
violations of the Open Meeting Law, G.L. c. 30A, §§ 18-25, by the Harvard School Committee 
("Committee"). Specifically, the complaint alleges that the Committee has not released the 
executive session minutes from meetings of the Committee that occurred on May 28, 2010, June 
29, 2010, July 19, 2010 and August 30, 2010. The complaint requests a determination from this 
office that the purpose for which the Committee entered executive session on the stated dates has 
concluded and, accordingly, an order releasing the minutes of the executive sessions and the 
documents used by the Committee during those sessions. The complaint was originally filed with 
the Committee on October 10, 2010. The Committee's response to the complaint was received 
by this office on November 9, 2010. 
Our office reviewed the initial complaint; the Board's response; the public meeting

Attorneys Michael Long and Joshua Coleman, as well as conversations with counsel to the 
Committee Naomi Stonberg and you, as Committee Chair. We appreciate the cooperation of the

DISCUSSION 
The complainant alleges that the Committee violated the Open Meeting Law, G.L. c. 30A, §§ 
18-25, by failing to release the minutes of executive sessions convened by the Committee on 
May 28th, June 29th, July 19th  and August 30th  . The complainant seeks the "immediate release 
[of] the Harvard School Committee executive session minutes including but not limited to the 
meetings of May 10, May 28, June 29, July 19, and August 30, 2010, as well as any other 
executive session minutes of the board the pertain to this matter along with all supporting 
documentation." We address the release of the executive session minutes and the release of 
other documentation in turn. 
1. Release of Executive Session Minutes 
Public bodies may enter executive session for one or more purposes delineated in the Open 
Meeting Law. See G.L. c. 30A, § 21(a). Although a public body has discretion to enter 
executive session, it bears the burden of demonstrating the need for the session. See District  
Attorney for the Northwestern District v. Board of Selectmen of Sunderland, 11 Mass. App. Ct. 
663, 666 (1981). Furthermore, the public body is required to follow the procedure for entering 
executive session set forth in the law. See G.L. c. 30A, § 21(b). It is also required to maintain 
accurate minutes of the executive session: 
The minutes of any executive session, the notes, recordings or other 
materials used in the preparation of such minutes and all documents 
and exhibits used at the session, may be withheld from disclosure to

G.L. c. 30A, § 22(f). 
The public body also has discretion to determine when the purpose for entering executive 
session is concluded. See G.L. c. 30A, § 21(a); see also Foudy v. Amherst-Pelham Regional  
School Committee, 402 Mass. 179, 182 (1988).  However, the public body bears the burden of 
justifying continued nondisclosure of executive session minutes. Foudy,  402 Mass. at 184. 
Once the public body has determined that the purpose for which it entered executive session has 
concluded, the minutes may no longer remain private because, "it is essential to the democratic 
form of government that the public have broad access to the decisions made by its elected 
officials and to the way in which the decisions are reached." Id. 
Here, the Harvard School Committee acted with an abundance of caution to safeguard the 
rights of Dr. Jefferson while also conducting the business that was appropriately within its 
jurisdiction. Specifically, the Committee was tasked with determining whether and what 
disciplinary action was appropriate in the aftermath of a highly publicized matter that, by all 
accounts, caused friction within the community and, in some instances, was personally painful to 
members of the Committee. Prior to reaching a deteimination, the Conimittee sought counsel 
and deliberated over the course of seven meetings, several of which were attended by Counsel 
for the Superintendent and, in some cases, the Superintendent himself The question before us is 
whether the purpose for which the Committee entered executive session during the meetings in 
question has concluded. We find that it has. 
As stated above, publiC bodies are authorized to enter executive session for one or more 
of ten purposes delineated in the Open Meeting Law, G.L. c. 30A, § 21(a). The statute states that 
the first purpose for which a public body may enter executive session is, "[t]o discuss the 
reputation, character, physical condition or mental health, rather than professional competence, 
of an individual, or to discuss the discipline or dismissal of...a public officer, employee, staff 
member or individual." G.L. c. 30A, § 21(a)(1).2  
Before proceeding to our determination, we are obligated to point out that during the May 
28th  meeting, the Committee was not as conscientious about following the requirements of the 
Open Meeting Law for convening an executive session as it appears to have been during 
subsequent meetings. On May 28th, the Committee convened in executive session, but failed to 
state an appropriate purpose for entering executive session. The minutes reflect that following a 
discussion about the general purpose for the meeting, executive session was convened upon a 
"[m]otion to go into Executive Session by Pat Wenger; Piali De seconded the motion," followed 
by the roll call. However, at no time prior to taking the roll call vote to enter executive session 
and beginning discussions in earnest did the Committee cite one of the purposes allowed under

2. Release of Documents Used by the Committee 
The Open Meeting Law that went into effect on July 1, 2010 requires that the minutes of 
open and executive sessions of meetings created and maintained by public bodies include, "a list 
of documents and other exhibits used at the meeting." G.L. c. 30A, § 22(a).3  We interpret 
documents "used" at a meeting to include those documents physically present at the meeting and 
referred to by members of the public body in the context of its deliberations. Documents used by 
individuals other than members of the public body, and which are not in the custody of the public 
body, are necessarily excluded from those documents "used" by the public body. The law states 
explicitly that, "Mocuments and other exhibits used by the body at an open or executive session 
shall, along with the minutes, be part of the official record of the session." G.L. c. 30A, § 22(d). 
It further provides that all documents used by a public body at an executive session may be 
withheld under the Public Records Law, "as long as publication may defeat the lawful purposes 
of the executive session, but no longer." G.L. c. 30A, § 22(f). However, once the purpose for an 
executive session is concluded, "the minutes, preparatory materials and documents and exhibits 
of the session shall be disclosed unless the attorney-client privilege or 1 or more of the 
exemptions" to the Public Records Law applies to withhold the record in whole or in part. Id. 
Here, the Committee minutes from the executive sessions convened on July 12th, July 
19th, August 30th  and September 8th  do not include a list of documents and other exhibits used at 
the meeting as required by the Open Meeting Law. See G.L. c. 30A, § 22(a). The failure to 
include a list of the documents used by the Committee during these executive sessions is a 
violation of the Open.Meeting Law. After reviewing the minutes of these executive sessions, we 
have determined that the minutes suggest the following documents were "used at the meeting" of 
the Committee: 
July 12th: 	 None 
July 19th: 	 None 
August 30th: A letter concerning an Order of Impoundment submitted by Counsel for 
the Superintendent; a copy of the main motion, as amended, of the 
disciplinary action fmally adopted by the Committee 
September 8th: All draft public statements reviewed by the members of the Committee

The Open Meeting Law provides that all documents used by a public body at an 
executive session may be withheld under the Public Records Law, "as long as publication may 
defeat the lawful purposes of the executive session, but no longer." G.L. c. 30A, § 22(f). The 
statute further states, "[w]hen the purpose for which a valid executive session was held has been 
served, the...documents and exhibits of the session shall be disclosed unless the attorney-client 
privilege or 1 or more of the exemptions under [the Public Records Law] apply...." The purpose 
for which the Comniittee entered executive session concluded on September 13, 2010, as 
determined above. Accordingly, consistent with the requirements of the Open Meeting Law, we 
order that the following documents "used at the meeting[s]" of the Committee be made available 
to the public, subject to G.L. c. 30A, § 22(f): 1) the Jefferson writing distributed to Committee 
members by School Counsel Naomi Stonberg on May 10, 2010; 2) the letter concerning an Order 
of Impoundment submitted by Counsel for the Superintendent to the Committee on August 30, 
2010; 3) the main motion, as amended, of the disciplinary action adopted by the Committee on 
August 30, 2010; and 4) all draft public statements reviewed by the members of the Committee 
on September 8, 2010. 
We note that the release of the executive session minutes and the documents listed above 
will be the first time that the public is made fully aware of what transpired during the executive 
sessions and what documents were used by the Committee. This further illustrates the need for 
transparency reflected in this determination and order.
