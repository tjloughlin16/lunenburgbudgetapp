RE: Open Meeting Law Complaint 
Dear Attorney Heim: 
This office received a complaint from Christopher Loreti, dated August 11, 2015, 
alleging that the Arlington Redevelopment Board (the "Board") violated the Open Meeting Law, 
G.L. c. 3OA, §§ 18-25. The complaint was originally filed with the Board on May 19, 2015, and 
the Board responded by letter dated June 9, 2015. In his complaint, Mr. Loreti alleges that the 
Board: (i) failed to maintain accurate minutes of several meetings; and (ii) recorded a meeting 
without first informing members of the public. 
Following our review, we find that the allegations concerning inaccuracies in meeting 
minutes are untimely. Furthermore, we find that, while the Board did not inform members of the 
public that certain meetings were being recorded, this failure to do so did not violate the Open 
Meeting Law because the secretary making the recordings did not notify the chair that she was 
doing so. In reaching a determination, we reviewed the original complaint; the Board's 
response; and the request for further review filed with our office. 
We find the facts as follows. The Board held a meeting on November 3, 2014. The 
minutes of this meeting were approved on November 17, 2014. The Board held another meeting 
on May 13, 2015. During this second meeting, the Board Secretary informed the complainant 
that three meetings— held on September 22, 2014; January 5, 2015; and February 23, 2015— 
had been audio recorded. This recording was done to assist the Secretary in the preparation of 
meeting minutes. It was not the Secretary's practice to inform the Board or its chair when she 
was recording meetings, and she did not inform the chair that she was doing so on the three dates 
at issue. No statement was made during any of these three meetings informing members of the 
public that the meetings were being recorded.

DISCUSSION 
I. The Allegation Concerning Alleged Inaccuracies in Meeting Minutes is Untimely. 
The Open Meeting Law requires that complaints be filed with the public body within 30 
days of an alleged violation. G.L. c. 30A, § 23(b); 940 CMR 29.05(1). If the alleged violation 
could not have been known at the time it occurred, then the complaint must be filed with the 
public body within 30 days of the date on which the alleged violation could reasonably have 
been discovered. 940 CMR 29.05(3). A complaint regarding the sufficiency or accuracy of open 
session meeting minutes must be filed within 30 days of the date on which those minutes were 
approved. See OML 2015-26. 
Here, the complaint alleges that the minutes of the November 3, 2014 meeting omit a 
discussion that took place during the meeting. These minutes were approved by the Board 
during an open session meeting on November 17, 2014. Therefore, in order to be timely, any 
complaint regarding their sufficiency or accuracy should have been filed with the Board no later 
than December 17, 2014. See OML Declination 5-4-2012 (Sheffield Planning Board). Because 
the complaint was not filed until May 19, 2015, we find that this allegation is untimely, and we 
decline to review it. 
II. Because the Secretary did not Notify the Board that she was Recording the 
Meetings at Issue, the Board did not Violate the Open Meeting Law by Failing to 
Notify Members of the Public that the Meetings at Issue were being Recorded. 
The Open Meeting Law requires that "[a] Iter notifying the chair of the public body, any 
person may make a video or audio recording of an open session of a meeting of a public body, or 
may transmit the meeting through any medium, subject to the reasonable requirements of the 
chair as to the number, placement and operation of equipment so as not to interfere with the 
conduct of the meeting." G.L. c. 30A, § 20(f). The law requires the chair to inform attendees of 
any recording. Id. The requirement that the chair inform attendees of any recording includes 
any recording made by the public body itself, including those made for public broadcasting or 
administrative purposes, such as assisting in the drafting of meeting minutes. See OML 2013-
136; OML 2012-24. 
Here, the Board Secretary recorded the meetings held on September 22, 2014; January 5, 
2015; and February 23, 2015 for the purpose of assisting her in the creation of meeting minutes. 
No statement was made informing members of the public that this recording was taking place. 
However, because the Secretary did not inform the chair that she was making these recordings, 
the chair was not obligated to inform the public. See OML 2014-8 (the chair must inform 
members of the public of any recording of which he or she is aware). Accordingly, we find that 
the Board did not violate the Open Meeting Law with respect to these meetings.1

CONCLUSION 
For the reasons stated above, we find that the Board did not violate the Open Meeting 
Law. We now consider the complaint addressed by this determination to be resolved. This 
determination does not address any other complaints that may be pending with our office or the 
Board. Please feel free to contact the Division at (617) 963 - 2540 if you have any questions.
