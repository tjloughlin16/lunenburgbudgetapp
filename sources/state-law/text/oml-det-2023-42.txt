This office received a complaint from Allen Waller on March 8, 2023, alleging that the 
Marblehead Select Board (the “Board”), violated the Open Meeting Law, G.L. c. 30A, §§ 18-25.  
The complaint was originally filed with the Board on February 1, 2023, and you responded, on 
behalf of the Board, by letter dated February 14, 2023.  The complaint alleges that the Board 
failed to timely approve minutes of 2019 meetings of the Complete Streets Committee (the 
“Committee”) and the minutes are inaccurate or incomplete.       
 
Following our review, we find that the Board did not violate the Open Meeting Law.  In 
reaching this determination, we reviewed the Open Meeting Law complaint, the Board’s 
response, and the request for further review.  We also reviewed notices and minutes for meetings 
of the Complete Streets Committee held on January 15, February 19, March 20, and November 
19, 2019. 
 
FACTS 
 
We find the facts as follows.  The Complete Streets Committee was an ad hoc committee 
in the Town of Marblehead created to oversee and implement the Complete Streets Policy.  The 
Committee held meetings on December 11, 2018, and January 15, February 19, March 20, and 
November 11, 2019.  The Committee has held no meetings since 2019 and seems to have

DISCUSSION 
 
The Open Meeting Law requires that a public body “create and maintain accurate 
minut
es of all meetings, including executive sessions, setting forth the date, time and place, the 
members present or absent, a summary of the discussions on each subject, a list of documents 
and other exhibits used at the meeting, the decisions made and the actions taken at each meeting, 
including the record of all votes.”  G.L. c. 30A, § 22(a).  All open and executive session meeting 
minutes must be reviewed and approved in a timely manner.  G.L. c. 30A, § 22(c).  “Timely 
manner” means “within the next three public body meetings or within 30 days, whichever is 
later, unless the public body can show good cause for further delay.”  See 940 CMR 29.11; OML 
2018-48.1  Whenever possible, we recommend that minutes of a meeting be approved at the next 
meeting.  See OML 2018-67; OML 2017-133.  
 
The complaint alleges that the Board violated the Open Meeting Law when it approved 
minutes of Committee meetings held on February 19, March 20, and November 11, 2019, over 
three years after the meetings took place, and the minutes were insufficient.
2  We disagree.  As a 
preliminary matter, there has been no suggestion that the Committee was a subcommittee of the 
Board nor that the Board was obligated to approve the Committee’s meeting minutes.  Although 
we acknowledge that the Committee seems to have disbanded over three years ago and therefore 
filing an Open Meeting Law complaint with the Committee directly may not have been feasible, 
we find no basis for finding a violation by the Board relating to its approval of Committee 
meeting minutes.  See OML 2021-99 (declining to review allegations that a finance committee 
violated the Open Meeting Law when complaint was filed only with select board); OML 
Declination 9-7-21 (Seekonk Board of Selectmen) (declining to review complaint about 
sufficiency of minutes of a building committee when complaint was filed only with board of 
selectmen and building committee was not a subcommittee of board of selectmen). 
 
Furthermore, as the February 19, March 20, and November 11, 2019, meetings were the 
Committe
e’s final three meetings, we decline to find that the minutes were not timely approved 
in any event.  The Committee was a separate public body from the Board for purposes of 
complying with the Open Meeting Law.  Therefore, the minutes of the Committee’s meetings 
were required to be approved within the later of the next 30 days or three meetings of the 
Committee.  See OML 2020-19 (minutes of a subcommittee meeting were timely approved by

consent of the body (approved unless there is an objection), or approval by a parent public body

of approval of minutes does not impact the standard for timely approval of minutes, which 
remains “within the next three public body meetings or within 30 days, whichever is later.”  940 
CMR 29.11; OML 2022-85.   
 
CONCLUSION 
 
For the reasons stated above, we find that the Board did not violate the Open Meeting 
Law.  We now consider the complaint addressed by this determination to be resolved. This 
determination does not address any other complaints which may be pending with our office or 
the Committee.  Please feel free to contact our office at (617) 963-2540 if you have any 
questions.
