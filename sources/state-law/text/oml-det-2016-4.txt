This office received a complaint from Patrick Higgins, dated December 16, 2015, 
alleging that the Westfield Housing Authority (the "Authority") violated the Open Meeting Law, 
G.L. c. 30A, §§ 18-25. The complaint was originally filed with the Authority on November 12, 
2015, and Authority Executive Director Daniel Kelly responded to the original complaint by e-
mail dated December 11, 2015.1 In his complaint, Mr. Higgins alleges that the Authority posted 
a meeting notice that was insufficiently specific. 
Following our review, we find that the Authority violated the Open Meeting Law as 
alleged. Furthermore, we find that the Authority did not respond to Mr. Higgins' complaint in 
the manner required by the Open Meeting Law. In reaching a determination, we reviewed the 
original complaint; the Authority's response; and the request for further review filed with our 
office. We also reviewed the notice and minutes of the Authority's November 10, 2015 meeting. 
Finally, we spoke with Executive Director Kelly by telephone on January 5, 2016. 
FACTS 
We find the facts as follows. The Authority posted notice of a meeting to be held on 
November 10, 2015. The notice lists, among other anticipated discussion topics, "Committee 
Reports (Treasurer, Accounting and Quarterly) concerning all projects;" and "New Business— 
CFA Amendments." The acronym "CFA" was intended to describe a "Contract for Financial 
Assistance." 
The Authority held its November 10, 2015 meeting as planned. During the meeting, the 
Authority voted to ratify eight reports: a Treasurer's report; a Consolidated Expenditure report; 
and reports on six distinct projects which were listed in the meeting minutes as follows; 400-C,

DISCUSSION 
Public bodies must post notice of every meeting. G.L, c. 30A, § 20(b). The notice must 
be printed in a legible, easily understandable format and must contain the date, time and place of 
the meeting, as well as a listing of topics that the chair reasonably anticipates will be discussed. 
Id. The listing of topics "shall have sufficient specificity to reasonably advise the public of the 
issues to be discussed at the meeting." 940 CMR 29.03(l)(b); OML 2011-15.2 
Here, the complaint points to two sections of the meeting notice alleged to be 
insufficiently specific—the reference to "all projects" and the use of the acronym "CFA." 
Because the Contract for Financial Assistance was not actually discussed during the November 
10, 2015 meeting, the Open Meeting Law was not violated with respect to the sufficiency of the 
meeting notice. See OML 2015-37. We note, however, that the Attorney General's Office 
generally discourages the use of abbreviations or acronyms on meeting notices, see OML 2014-
116, particularly where the acronym used is not widely understood by members of the public. 
See OML 2015-197. 
The use of the broad term "all projects" on the meeting notice presents a different 
question. This vague phrasing used on the notice did not give members of the public a 
reasonable idea of which projects would be discussed during the meeting at issue. See OML 
2013-46. Because the Authority was voting to ratify specific reports, it should have listed out on 
the meeting notice the reports it expected to discuss. See OML 2011-11. This lack of specificity 
violated the Open Meeting Law. G.L. c. 30A, § 20(b); 940 CMR 29.03(l)(b). 
As a final matter, we note that the Authority did not respond to the complaint in the 
manner required by the Open Meeting Law. When an individual files an Open Meeting Law 
complaint with a public body, the chair "shall disseminate copies of the complaint to the 
members of the public body," and "the public body shall review the complaint's allegations." 
940 CMR 29.05(3), (5). Thus, the public body must meet to review the complaint and formulate 
a response, or meet to delegate that authority. See OML 2012-90. Here, Executive Director 
Kelly replied to the complaint by e-mail without the Authority meeting to review it. This, too, 
violated the Open Meeting Law. See OML 2015-94.

CONCLUSION 
For the reasons stated above, we find that the Authority violated the Open Meeting Law. 
We order the Authority's immediate and future compliance with the Open Meeting Law, and we 
caution that similar future violations may be considered evidence of intent to violate the law. 
We now consider the complaint addressed by this determination to be resolved. This 
determination does not address any other complaints that may be pending with our office or the 
Authority. Please feel free to contact the Division at (617) 963 - 2540 if you have any questions.
