RE: Open Meeting Law Complaint 
Dear Mr. O'Regan; 
This office received a complaint from Dr. Erdem Ural on March 14, 2018, alleging 
that the Stoughton Board of Selectmen (the "Board") violated the Open Meeting Law, G.L. c. 
30A, §§ 18-25. The complaint was originally filed with the Board on or about January 9, 
2018, and you responded to the complaint, on behalf of the Board, in a letter dated January 
30, 2018. In his complaint. Dr. Ural alleges that the Board failed to make its December 11, 
2017 meeting notice available to the public at all hours of the day at least 48 hours before the 
meeting. 
Following our review, we find that the Board violated the Open Meeting Law in the 
manner alleged. In reaching a determination, we reviewed the original complaint, the Board's 
response to the complaint, and the complaint filed with our office requesting further review. 
We also reviewed the Board's December 11, 2017 meeting notice. Finally, we spoke with the 
Town Clerk on April 5, 2018. 
We find the facts as follows. On December 7, 2017, the Board posted notice of a 
meeting scheduled for December 11, 2017.1 The Town's official notice posting method is a 
bulletin board inside Town Hall, which is only available to the public when the Town Hall is 
unlocked. In response to this complaint, the Town Clerk began posting a copy of each 
meeting notice on the exterior door of Town Hall in addition to its official posting location 
inside Town Hall.

DISCUSSION 
The Open Meeting Law requires a public body to post notice of a meeting at least 
48 hours in advance of a meeting. G.L. c. 30A, § 20(b). The municipal clerk must post notice 
of meetings in a manner conspicuously visible to the public at all hours in or on the municipal 
building in which the clerk's office is located. G.L. c. 30A, § 20(c). Alternatively, the Chief 
Executive Officer of the municipality may authorize the adoption of the municipal website as 
the official method of posting notice. 940 CMR 29.03(2)(1). 
The complaint alleges that the posting location for the Board's December 11, 2017 
meeting notice was not available to the public at all hours of the day. We agree and find that 
the Town's notice posting location does not comply with the law's requirement that a public 
body post notice of a meeting at least 48 hours in advance of a meeting in a manner 
conspicuously visible to the public at all hours in or on the municipal building in which the 
clerk's office is located, or to a website, if adopted in accordance with the regulations. G.L. 
c. 3OA, §§ 20(b), (c); 940 CMR 29.03. Accordingly, we conclude that the Board violated the 
Open Meeting Law by posting its December 11, 2017 meeting notice in a location that was 
not available to the public at all hours of the day. See OML 2017-99.3 
We commend the Board's attempt to correct this error for its future meeting notices by 
posting a copy of the meeting notices in the exterior entrance to Town Hall. However, we 
understand that the notices posted on the exterior door are reduced in size, thereby reducing 
the font size. We remind the Board that "notices shall be printed in a legible, easily 
understandable format and shall contain the date, time and place of such meeting and a listing 
of topics that the chair reasonably anticipates will be discussed at the meeting." G.L. c. 3 OA, 
§ 20(b); 940 CMR 29.03(b). We encourage the Board to post meeting notices that are legible 
to the public by using at least a 12-point standard font. See OML 2016-133. If it is unfeasible 
to post all of the notices on the exterior door in at least 12-point font, we strongly recommend 
that the Town consider adopting the municipal website as its official notice posting location. 
See 940 CMR 29.03(2)(1). 
CONCLUSION 
For the reasons stated above, we find that the Board violated the Open Meeting Law 
by failing to make its December 11, 2017 meeting notice available to the public at all hours of 
the day. We order the Board's immediate and future compliance with the law's requirements, 
and we caution that similar future violations could be considered evidence of intent to violate 
the law.
