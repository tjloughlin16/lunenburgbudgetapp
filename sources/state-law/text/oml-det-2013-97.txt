This office received a complaint from Nancy White, Editor of the Scituate Mariner, dated 
June 21, 2012, alleging that the Scituate Board of Selectmen (the "Board") violated the Open 
Meeting Law, G.L. c. 30A, §§ 18-25. Ms. White originally filed her complaint with the Board 
on May 3, 2012, and the Board responded by letter dated May 23, 2012. In her complaint, Ms. 
White alleges that: 1) the Board improperly entered into executive session to discuss the 
professional competence of an individual; 2) the Board failed to state the purpose for entering 
into executive session with sufficient specificity on six different occasions; 3) the Board failed to 
list documents used during an open session in the meeting minutes; and 4) the Board voted on 
employment contracts in executive session, and failed to approve those contracts in open session. 
In reaching our determination, we reviewed Ms. White's original May 3, 2012 complaint; 
the Board's May 23, 2012 response; Ms. White's complaint filed with our office, dated June 21, 
2012; and the Board's response to our office, dated August 30, 2012. We also reviewed the 
meeting notices, agendas, open session minutes, and executive session minutes1 for the Board's 
November 1, 2011; November 15, 2011; November 29, 2011; December 6, 2011; December 10, 
2011; December 20, 2011; and April 5, 2012 meetings. Finally, we reviewed the Town 
Administrator's employment contract and addenda, and video recordings of the open session 
meetings held on November 1, 2011; November 15, 2011; November 29, 2011; December 6, 
2011; and December 20,2011.

We appreciate the patience of the parties while we reviewed this matter. Following our 
review, we find that the Board violated the Open Meeting Law by failing to state with sufficient 
specificity the purpose for multiple executive sessions. We also find that the Board violated the 
Open Meeting Law by failing to list in its minutes all documents used during an open session 
meeting, and by failing to approve certain Town officials' employment contracts in open session. 
However, we find that the Board did not improperly discuss the professional competence of an 
individual in executive session. 
FACTS 
Based upon our review of the material listed above, the facts are as follows. The Board 
posted notice on October 27, 2011 for its November 1, 2011 meeting, and listed as an agenda 
item the "Consideration/Purchase/Exchange/Lease/Value of Real Property & Labor Negotiations 
(Executive Sessions)." During the executive session, the Board voted to exercise its options 
under the Scituate Boatworks lease, and was informed by Town Administrator Patricia Vinchesi, 
that "police [contract] negotiations" continued even after "marathon negotiations over the past 
four days." 
On November 9, 2011, the Board posted notice for its November 15, 2011 meeting, and 
posted a revised notice the following morning. The agenda included the topic "Union & Non-
Union Collective Bargaining (Executive Sessions)." During the November 15, 2011 meeting, 
the Board entered into executive session and discussed Town Treasurer/Collector Jane Lepardo's 
salary and her request to increase it based upon her tenure and a benchmark analysis with other 
towns. The Board also discussed recent changes in health insurance offered to municipal 
employees and retirees, and unanimously voted to ratify the memorandum of agreement between 
the Town of Scituate and the International Brotherhood of Police Officers, Local 461. 
The Board posted notice on November 23, 2011 for its November 29, 2011 meeting and 
included on its agenda "Labor Negotiations (Non Union) Executive Session." During the 
executive session, the Board discussed increasing Ms. Lepardo's and Town Accountant Mary 
Gallagher's salaries, and unanimously voted to increase both, effective January 1, 2012. The 
Board also reviewed Ms. Vinchesi's proposal to renew her employment agreement for a five-
year term, from fiscal year 2013 through fiscal year 2017, and to approve an addendum for fiscal 
year 2012 increasing her salary. Ms. Vinchesi asked the Board to "consider fairness, equity and 
performance . . . [and] the size of the town compared with other communities, staff support and 
number of Selectmen served." The Board voted unanimously to renew Ms. Vinchesi's 
employment agreement as outlined in her proposal, effective January 1, 2012, and to approve the 
addendum increasing her salary for fiscal year 2012.

as an item for discussion the "Consideration/Purchase/Exchange/Lease/Value of Real Property & 
Labor Negotiations (Non-Union) (Executive Sessions)." During the executive session, the Board 
discussed the process for evaluating and selling parcels of Town-owned property following

DISCUSSION 
The complaint alleges that the Board improperly entered into executive session to 
discuss the professional competence of an individual; the Board failed to state the purpose for 
entering into executive sessions with sufficient specificity on six different occasions; the Board 
failed to list documents used during an open session meeting in the meeting minutes; and the 
Board failed to approve the Town Administrator's, Treasurer/Tax Collector's, and Town 
Accountant's employment contracts in open session. We consider each issue in turn. 
1. The Board did not Discuss the Professional Competence of the Town Administrator 
in Executive Session. 
The complaint alleges that the Board improperly entered into executive session to discuss 
the professional competence of Town Administrator Patricia Vinchesi. Meetings shall be open 
to members of the public, unless an executive session is convened. See G.L. c. 30A, §§ 20(a), 
21(a). The Law permits public bodies to enter executive session for any of ten enumerated 
purposes, and the public body may only address a narrow range of subjects related to the cited 
purpose(s) when in executive session. G.L. c. 3OA, § 21; see District Attorney for the 
Northwestern District v. Selectmen of Sunderland. 11 Mass. App. Ct. 663, 666 (1981). Under 
Purpose 2, a Board may enter into executive session "[t]o conduct strategy sessions in 
3 
===PAGE 4===
preparation for negotiations with nonunion personnel or to conduct collective bargaining 
sessions or contract negotiations with nonunion personnel." G.L. c. 30A, § 21(a)(2). Another 
purpose, Purpose 1, permits the Board to enter executive session "[t]o discuss the reputation, 
character, physical condition or mental health," of an individual, but prohibits a public body 
from discussing in executive session the "professional competence" of an individual. G.L. c. 
3OA, § 21(a)(1). 
The Board entered into executive session on November 29, 2011, to discuss "Labor 
Negotiations (Non Union)" which falls under Purpose 2. During the executive session, the 
Board discussed Town Administrator Vinchesi's proposal to renew her employment contract for 
a five-year term, and her proposed addendum for fiscal year 2012 to increase her salary. In her 
proposal, Ms. Vinchesi specifically requested the Board to "consider fairness, equity and 
performance" in its deliberations. We do not believe, as the complainant alleges, that the Board 
conducted a substantive discussion of Ms. Vinchesi's professional competence during its 
November 29, 2011 session. Because the topic of Ms. Vinchesi's proposed contract renewal and 
addendum was appropriate for executive session under Purpose 2, we find that the Board did not 
violate the Open Meeting Law. 
2. The Board Violated the Open Meeting Law by Failing to State the Purpose for 
Entering into Executive Session with Sufficient Specificity on Six Different 
Occasions. 
The Open Meeting Law was enacted "to eliminate much of the secrecy surrounding 
deliberations and decisions on which public policy is based." Ghiglione v. School Committee of 
Southbridge, 376 Mass. 70, 72 (1978). The law requires that meetings of a public body be 
properly noticed, at least 48 hours prior to the meeting, in a "legible, easily understandable 
format and shall contain the date, time and place of such meeting and a listing of topics that the 
chair reasonably anticipates will be discussed at the meeting." G.L. c. 30A, § 20(b). The listing 
of topics "shall have sufficient specificity to reasonably advise the public of the issues to be 
discussed at the meeting." 940 CMR 29.03(b). Furthermore, before entering into executive 
session, the chair must state the purpose for the executive session, stating all subjects that may be 
revealed without compromising the purpose for which the executive session was called. G.L. c. 
30A,§ 21(b). 
The Board listed the following executive session topics in its meeting notices: 
November 1,2011 "Consideration/Purchasc/Exchange/Lease/Value of Real Property 
& Labor Negotiations (Executive Sessions)" 
November 15, 2011 "Union & Non-Union Collective Bargaining (Executive Session)" 
November 29, 2011 "Labor Negotiations (Non Union) Executive Session" 
December 6,2011 "Consideration/Purchase/Exchange/Lease/Value of Real Property 
& Labor Negotiations (Non Union) (Executive Sessions)"

December 10, 2011 "Executive Session[:] Collective bargaining" 
December 20, 2011 "Labor Negotiations (Non-Union) (Executive Session)" 
Under the Open Meeting Law, "a precise statement of the reason for convening in 
executive session is necessary . . . because that is the only notification given to the public that a 
[public body] would conduct business in private, and the only way the public would know if the 
reason for doing so was proper or improper." District Attorney for the Northern District v. 
School Committee of Wayland. 455 Mass. 561, 567 (2009); OML 2011-30.3 This level of detail 
is required in both the meeting notice and prior to convening an executive session. Thus, with 
regard to union and non-union contract negotiations, the Board should have identified on the 
meeting notice the bargaining unit or individual that was the subject of the executive session, and 
the Board Chair should have publicly identified the bargaining unit or individual prior to 
convening the executive session. See OML 2012-39; OML 2011-56; OML 2011-15. For 
example, the Board could have listed on its November 29, 2011 meeting notice that the Board 
would convene in executive session for "Purpose 2: strategy session in preparation for contract 
negotiations with Town Administrator Patricia Vinchesi, Town Treasurer/Collector Jane 
Lepardo, and Town Accountant Mary Gallagher." 
With regard to its discussions of the purchase, exchange, lease or value of real property, 
when entering executive session for this purpose, a public body must publicly identify the 
property if doing so would not compromise the purpose for which the executive session was 
called. See OML 2012-52. A specific address is always preferable, but if unavailable, a public 
body may list bordering streets, intersections, landmarks or other identifying information. See 
id.; OML 2010-2. The Board did not list any identifying information for the properties discussed 
on either November 1, 2011 or December 6, 2011. Executive session minutes from December 6, 
2011 show that the Board discussed parcels of "Town owned property" currently under review as 
potential parcels to be sold by a public, sealed bid process. The Board also cited Purpose 6 for its 
executive session on November 1, 2011. Because the Board has not fully released the minutes 
pertaining to its Purpose 6 discussions, we do not recount their full detail here, however the 
Board should have identified the specific properties it planned to discuss if doing so would not 
have a detrimental effect on the Board's negotiating position.4 
Because the Board did not describe executive session topics with sufficient specificity on 
the meeting notices, and because the Chair failed to specify all topics that may be revealed 
without compromising the purpose for which the executive session was called prior to entering 
executive session, the Board violated the Open Meeting Law.

3. The Board Violated the Open Meeting Law by Failing to List in its Meeting Minutes 
the Documents Used During its December 10. 2011 Open Session. 
The Law requires that a public body include in the minutes of its meetings a list of 
documents and other exhibits used at the meeting. G.L. c. 30A, § 22(a). The December 10, 
2011 meeting minutes state that "[Executive Assistant to the Board Kim] Donovan presented the 
Board with a variety of previously approved items by the Board for signature." The minutes do 
not list what "approved items" the Board signed, nor what other exhibits the Board used during 
their discussions. Review of Ms. Vinchesi's Employment Addendum increasing her fiscal year 
2012 salary shows that the Board signed it on December 10, 2011. The Board violated the Open 
Meeting Law by failing to list the documents and exhibits used during the December 10, 2011 
meeting in its minutes. 
4. The Board Violated the Open Meeting Law by Failing to Approve the Town 
Administrator's. Town Treasurer/Collector's, and Town Accountant's Contracts in 
Open Session. 
The Open Meeting Law allows a public body to conduct contract negotiations with non-
union personnel in executive session, but it does not authorize a public body to take a final yote 
to execute a non-union personnel contract in executive session. OML 2011-28; see OML 2011-
56; OML 2011-15. While a public body may agree on terms with individual non-union 
personnel in executive session, the final vote to execute such agreements must be taken by the 
public body in open session. See OML 2011-56; OML 2011-28; OML 2011-15. During the 
Board's November 29, 2011 executive session, the Board voted to increase Ms. Lepardo's salary 
as the Town Treasurer/Collector; to increase Ms. Gallagher's salary as Town Accountant; and to 
approve Ms. Vinchesi's proposal to extend her contract as Town Administrator through fiscal 
year 2017 and to increase her salary. The Board did not approve the contracts in open session 
until April 5, 2012. The Board violated the Open Meeting Law by failing to vote on these three 
contracts in open session before executing the agreements. 
The Board writes in its May 23, 2012 response that on April 2, 2012, "in the course of 
reviewing The Beacon, the Massachusetts Municipal Associations monthly publication, the 
Town Administrator" learned of our determination OML 2011-56, issued on December 27, 2011. 
In that determination, we noted that "the Open Meeting Law does not permit the Board to 
approve a contract behind closed doors where the public cannot witness the agreement." The 
Town Administrator contacted Town Counsel the same day she learned of this determination, 
and the Board promptly posted notice on April 3, 2012 for the next open meeting on April 5, 
2012 to approve the contracts in open session. 
We acknowledge that the Board relied on the advice of counsel with regard to voting on 
contracts in executive session. Therefore, even though the interpretation stated in OML 2011-56 
had actually been published in Division decisions as early as July 2011, we do not find an 
intentional violation of the Law. 5

CONCLUSION 
For the reasons stated above, we find that the Board violated the Open Meeting Law by 
failing to state with sufficient specificity the purpose for multiple executive sessions; by failing 
to list in its minutes documents used during open session; and by failing to approve multiple 
Town officials' employment contracts in open session.6 We find that the Board did not discuss 
the professional competence of an individual in executive session. 
We order immediate and future compliance with the Open Meeting Law, and caution the 
Board that a determination of future similar violations may be considered evidence of an 
intentional violation of the Law. In addition, we order the Board to review the Attorney 
General's Open Meeting Law Guide, available at the Attorney General's website, 
www.mass.gov/ago/openmeeting. All members must certify to our office within thirty (30) days 
after receiving this determination that they have reviewed the Guide. 
We now consider the complaints addressed by this determination to be resolved. This 
determination does not address any other complaints which may be pending with our office or 
the Board. Please feel free to contact our office at (617) 963-2540 if you have any questions.
