RE: Open Meeting Law Complaint
Dear Attorney St. John:
This office received a complaint from Gary Trudeau on January 24, 2020, alleging that 
the Cheshire Board of Selectmen (the “Board”) violated the Open Meeting Law, G.L. c. 30A, §§ 
18-25. The complaint was originally filed with the Board on November 27, 2019, and you 
responded on behalf of the Board by letter dated December 20, 2019. In his complaint, Mr. 
Trudeau alleges that a self-appointed group consisting of one Board member, the Cheshire Town 
Administrator, and the Cheshire Highway Superintendent improperly, and without authority, 
interviewed candidates for a highway equipment operator/general laborer position.1
We resolve this complaint by informal action in accordance with 940 CMR 29.07(2)(a), 
and find no violation of the Open Meeting Law by the Board, as only one member of the Board 
was part of the informal group, and therefore at no time did a quorum of the Board deliberate 
outside of a meeting or otherwise violate the Open Meeting Law.
Additionally, we find that the Board did not create the group as a subcommittee. The 
Open Meeting Law applies only to a public body, which is defined, in relevant part, as “a 
multiple member board, commission, committee or subcommittee within the executive or 
legislative branch or within any county, district, city, region or town, however created, elected, 
appointed or otherwise constituted, established to serve a public purpose[.]” G.L. c. 30A, § 18. A 
“subcommittee” is defined as a multiple-member body created to advise or make 
recommendations to a public body. G.L. c. 30A, § 18.

Although the Board considered creating a hiring subcommittee at its October 15, 2019, 
meeting, the Board did not take any formal action to create a subcommittee. The three members 
of the informal group seem to have taken it upon themselves to review and assess job applicants 
for the highway equipment operator/general laborer position, including conducting interviews of 
possible applicants on October 22, 2019, at 4 p.m. At the Board’s next meeting, at 6:30 p.m. on 
October 22, 2019, other Board members expressed concern that the Board did not know 
interviews had already taken place that day. Despite expressing concerns about the hiring 
process, the Board appointed the candidate recommended by the informal group on October 29, 
2019. Since the Board had no role in creating or structuring the informal group, we find that no 
subcommittee was created. See OML 2017-111} Accordingly, we find that the Board did not 
violate the Open Meeting Law.
Finally, we remind the Board of its obligation to review Open Meeting Law complaints 
and respond to the complainant, and to the Attorney General, within 14-business days of receipt 
of the complaint. See G.L. c. 30A, § 23(b); 940 CMR 29.05(5). The Open Meeting Law requires 
that, within 14 business days after receiving an Open Meeting Law complaint, a public body 
shall review the complaint’s allegations; take remedial action, if appropriate; and send to the 
Attorney General’s Office a copy of the complaint and a description of any remedial action 
taken. G.L. c. 30A, § 23(b); 940 CMR 29.05(5). The body must convene a meeting in order to 
review the complaint before sending a response or authorizing its legal counsel or another 
representative to send a response. See OML 2012-90; OML 2011-6.
We now consider the complaint addressed by this determination to be resolved. This 
determination does not address any other complaints that may be pending with our office or the 
Board. Please feel free to contact the Division at (617) 963 - 2540 if you have any questions.
