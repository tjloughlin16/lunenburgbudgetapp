Dear Mr. Metcalf: 
 
This office received a complaint from Patrick McPhee on April 6, 2023, alleging that the 
Ashby Board of Health (the “Board”) violated the Open Meeting Law, G.L. c. 30A, §§ 18-25.  
The complaint was originally filed with the Board on or about January 3, 2023; and you 
responded, on behalf of the Board, by letter dated March 20, 2023.
1  The complaint alleges that 
the Board failed to create sufficient minutes and timely approve minutes of six meetings held 
between July 21, 2022 and December 15, 2022, and that the Board did not post notice or create 
minutes for a meeting held “at the fire dump.” 
 
We resolve this matter by informal action, in ac cordance with 940 CMR 29.07(2)(a), 
after reviewing the original complaint, the Board’s response to the complaint, the Board’s 
supplemental response to the complaint dated April 21, 2023, the complaint filed with our office 
requesting further review, and the minutes of the Board meetings held on July 21, 2022, August 
25, 2022, October 13, 2022, November 10, 2022, and December 15, 2022.  In addition, we spoke 
with the complainant by telephone on May 24, 2023, and September 19, 2023.  We find that the 
Board did not violate the Open Meeting Law in the ways alleged.

The Open Meeting Law requires that a public body “create and maintain accurate 
minutes of all meetings, including executive sessions, setting forth the date, time and place, the 
members present or absent, a summary of the discussions on each subject, a list of documents 
and other exhibits used at the meeting, the decisions made and the actions taken at each meeting, 
including the record of all votes.”  G.L. c. 30A, § 22(a).  The meeting minutes must be reviewed 
and approved in a timely manner.  G.L. c. 30A, § 22(c).  “Timely manner” means “within the 
next three public body meetings or within 30 days, whichever is later, unless the public body can 
show good cause for further delay.”  See 940 CMR 29.11; OML 2018-48.

The complaint alleges that the Board failed to timely approve the minutes of meetings 
held on J
uly 21, 2022, August 25, 2022, September 22, 2022, October 13, 2022, November 10, 
2022, and December 15, 2022.  We find that the Board meeting held on September 22, 2022, was 
cancelled.  Since there was no meeting, the Board was not required to create minutes of this 
meeting.  With respect to the remaining five meetings, the Board approved each set of minutes at 
the next meeting.  As such, we find that the Board approved all five sets of minutes in a timely 
manner and therefore did not violate the Open Meeting Law as all five sets of minutes were 
approved within the later of the next three Board meetings or 30 days following each Board 
meeting.  See OML 2023-4.  We note that the Open Meeting Law does not require that public 
bodies post their minutes to a website or that minutes be submitted to the Town Clerk, but rather 
that meeting minutes be made available within 10 days of a request.  See G.L. c. 30A, § 22(c); 
OML 2013-103.3   
 
The complaint also alleges tha
 t the Board created insufficiently detailed minutes for the 
meetings held on July 21, 2022, August 25, 2022, October 13, 2022, November 10, 2022, and 
December 15, 2022, by not including a list of documents used during each meeting, as required 
by the Open Meeting Law.  The minutes of each meeting contain the date of the meeting, the 
members who were present, a summary of the discussions, and the decisions made and actions 
taken, including a record of all votes.  Although not specifically raised in the complaint, we note 
that the minutes of each meeting do not include the time or place of the meeting.  Meeting 
minutes must include the location of the meeting, as well as the actual scheduled time of each 
meeting and not just the time that the chair opened the meeting.  See OML 2019-163; OML 
2019-29.  We encourage the Board to amend the five sets of minutes to include this information.   
 
With respect to the specific allegation that the minutes do not include a list of documents, 
we acknowledge that each of the five sets of minutes do not contain such a list.  The Open 
Meeting Law does not define what it means for a document to be used at a meeting but, at a 
minimum, it is clear that where a document is physically present, verbally identified, and the 
contents are discussed by the members of a public body during an open meeting, it has been

Finally, the complaint alleges that the Board failed to post notice for a Board meeting that 
occur
red “at the fire dump” and another meeting that potentially occurred at “Ayer court.”  The 
Open Meeting Law requires that, except in an emergency, “a public body shall post notice of 
every meeting at least 48 hours prior to such meeting, excluding Saturdays, Sundays and legal 
holidays.”  G.L. c. 30A, § 20(b).  The notice must be printed in a legible, easily understandable 
format and must contain the date, time and place of the meeting, as well as a listing of topics that 
the chair reasonably anticipates will be discussed.  Id.  After speaking with the complainant, he 
informed our office that the meeting “at the fire dump” occurred in August 2022 and the meeting 
at “Ayer court” occurred in December 2022.  The Board denies that any such meetings occurred.     
Here, we find insufficient evidence to conclude that a meeting, as defined by the Open Meeting 
Law, occurred.

In his request for further review, the complainant suggests that the Board failed to 
res
pond to a request for meeting minutes.  We generally decline to review an allegation that was 
not included within the original complaint to the Board because the Board has not had an 
opportunity to respond to it.  See G.L. c. 30A, § 23(b); 940 CMR 29.05(3).  We find that this 
allegation was not clearly raised in the original complaint and thus we do not review its merits.5  
However, we take seriously the allegations raised by the complainant, and take this opportunity 
to remind the Board that a request for meeting minutes that is directed to a public body will fall 
under the Open Meeting Law and requires a response in 10 calendar days.  See G.L. c. 30A, §§ 
22(c), (g); OML 2019-163; OML 2019-20; OML 2017-50.  On the other hand, a request for 
minutes that is directed to a clerk or other municipal employee as Records Access Officer will 
likely fall under the Public Records Law and requires a response in 10 business days.  See G.L. 
c. 66, § 10; OML 2019-126; OML 2019-68.
