Re: Open Meeting Law Complaint 
Dear Mr. Metters: 
This office received a complaint from Gloria Freeman, dated March 4, 2012, alleging 
violations of the Open Meeting Law, G.L. c. 30A, §§ 18-25, by the Chatham Planning Board (the 
"Board"). The complaint was originally filed with the Board on February 23, 2012, and the 
Board responded by letter dated February 29, 2012. The complaint alleges that the Board failed 
to adequately inform the public of a topic for discussion at its January 24, 2012 meeting. The 
complaint also alleges that on January 24, 2012, the Board held an improper hearing to amend a 
special permit, and that notice for this hearing was insufficient. The Division of Open 
Government only enforces and interprets the Open Meeting Law. The Open Meeting Law does 
not govern when a public hearing must be held or the specific notice requirements for public 
hearings, therefore we decline to review the latter allegations. 
Following our review, we fmd that the Board did not violate the Open Meeting Law by 
not indicating on the notice for its January 24, 2012 meeting that it would vote to reopen a public 
hearing. In reaching this determination, we reviewed the February 23, 2012 and March 4, 2012 
complaints; the Board's February 29, 2012 response; the meeting notice for the Board's January 
24, 2012 meeting; the minutes of the Board's January 24, 2012 meeting; and a video recording of 
the Board's January 24, 2012 meeting. 
FACTS 
On December 13, 2011, the Board held a public hearing regarding an application from 
the Sage Group, LLC to amend its special permit. During that hearing, the Board voted 4-3 in

The Chair1  started the discussion by providing background information on the issue and 
indicated that the Board would vote to reopen the hearing. The Board voted unanimously to 
reopen the hearing. The Board then heard testimony from the property owner and members of 
the public. At the conclusion of the hearing, the Board again voted 4-3 to amend the special 
permit. Because a supermajority vote was required, the amendment to the permit was not 
granted. 
DISCUSSION 
The Open Meeting Law states that "in addition to any notice otherwise required by law, a 
public body shall post notice of every meeting at least 48 hours prior to such meeting, excluding 
Saturdays, Sundays and legal holidays." G.L. c. 30A, § 20(b). Notices must "contain the date, 
time and place of such meeting and a listing of topics that the chair reasonably anticipates will be 
discussed at the meeting." Id. The Attorney General's Open Meeting Law regulations state that 
"[Ole list of topics shall have sufficient specificity to reasonably advise the public of the issues 
to be discussed at the meeting." 940 CMR 29.03(1)(b). We generally consider a topic to include 
sufficient specificity when a reasonable member of the public could read the topic and 
understand what the nature of the public body's discussion will be. See OML 2011-7. 2  
The complainant states that the Board's meeting notice was insufficient because it did not 
indicate that the Board would vote to reopen the public hearing. The Board's notice specifically 
stated that it would be considering an amendment to a special permit, and specifically identified 
the applicant and the property address. A reasonable member of the public could infer from this 
language that the Board would need to vote to reopen the hearing before holding this discussion. 
$ee OML 2011-15. We therefore find that the Board's notice was sufficiently specific. 3
