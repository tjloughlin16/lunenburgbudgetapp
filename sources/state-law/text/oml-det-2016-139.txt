This office received a complaint from Jeanne Holmes on July 28, 2016 alleging that the 
Brockton Planning Board (the "Board") violated the Open Meeting Law, G.L. c. 30A, §§ 18-25. 
The complaint was originally filed with the Board on or about May 18, 2016 and you responded, 
on behalf of the Board, by letter dated June 13, 2016. In her complaint, Ms. Holmes alleges that 
the Board failed to provide her with the April 13, 2015 meeting minutes upon request. In 
addition, Ms. Holmes alleges that the Board's April 26, 2016 meeting minutes are inaccurate and 
that the Board failed to timely approve its April 5, 2016 meeting minutes. 
Following our review, we find that the Board did not violate the Open Meeting Law. In 
reaching this determination, we reviewed the original complaint, the Board's response to the 
complaint, and the complaint filed with our office requesting further review. We also reviewed 
the complainant's request for minutes and the Board's response. Finally, we reviewed the 
minutes from the Board's April 5, 2016 and April 26, 2016 meetings. 
FACTS 
We find the facts as follows. During the meetings at issue in this complaint, the Board 
was a nine-member public body, thus five members constituted a quorum.1 The Board has a 
practice of posting approved minutes on the City website. On May 4, 2016 the complainant 
submitted a request by email to Robert May of the Brockton Planning Department for copies of 
Board minutes of various dates, including the minutes at issue in her complaint—the April 13, 
2015; April 5, 2016; and April 26, 2016 meeting minutes. Mr. May responded by email the next 
day, indicating that the April 5, 2016 meeting minutes were not available because the meeting 
was "voided due to procedural error" and the April 26, 2016 meeting minutes were "currently 
being processed." The Board "voided" the actions taken during its April 5, 2016 meeting due to

DISCUSSION 
The Open Meeting Law was enacted "to eliminate much of the secrecy surrounding 
deliberation and decisions on which public policy is based." Ghiglione v. School Board of 
Southbridge. 376 Mass, 70, 72 (1978). In general, meetings of a public body must be noticed 
and open to the public. G.L. c. 3OA, § 20(a), (b). A meeting is "a deliberation by a public body 
with respect to any matter within the body's jurisdiction." G.L. c. 3OA, § 18. A deliberation is 
"an oral or written communication through any medium, including electronic mail, between or 
among a quorum of a public body on any public business within its jurisdiction." Id. A 
"quorum" is, generally, a simple majority of the members of a public body. IcL 
Public bodies must create and maintain minutes of all meetings "setting forth the date, 
time and place, the members present or absent, a summary of the discussions on each subject, a 
list of documents and other exhibits used at the meeting, the decisions made and the actions 
taken at each meeting, including the record of all votes." G.L. c. 30A, § 22(a). Minutes of open 
session meetings must be created and approved in a timely manner. See OML 2012-91.4 While 
the Open Meeting Law does not define "timely manner," we recommend that minutes be 
approved at the next meeting, whenever possible. Id. The law also requires that the "minutes of 
an open session, if they exist and whether approved or in draft form, shall be made available 
upon request by any person within 10 days." G.L. c. 30A, § 22(c).

minutes existed at that time because less than a quorum of the nine-member Board attended the 
scheduled meetings on those days. Where less than a quorum is present, even with the intention 
of holding a meeting as defined under the law, it is not a meeting for which minutes must be 
created. See OML 2015-114. In such instances, we encourage public bodies to postpone any 
discussion until a future posted meeting where a quorum is present and can deliberate. 
Accordingly, we find no violation in this regard. 
The complaint also alleges that the April 26, 2016 minutes do not include certain 
statements made by the Board chair regarding an individual "allegedly harassing board 
members" that he allegedly described as "very sick" and "needed to get help." When reviewing 
meeting minutes, we look for substantial compliance with the accuracy requirement. See OML 
2016-105. The minutes should contain enough detail and accuracy so that a member of the 
public who did not attend the meeting could read the minutes and have a clear understanding of 
what occurred. See id. While the minutes may not reflect every statement made regarding the 
topic, the final minutes, approved after this complaint was filed, sufficiently summarized the 
discussion regarding "potential harassment" of Board members in substantial compliance with 
the requirements of the Open Meeting Law. See OML 2013-64. Accordingly, we find that the 
Board did not violate the law. 
CONCLUSION 
For the reasons stated above, we find that the Board did not violate the Open Meeting 
Law. We now consider the complaint addressed by this determination to be resolved. This 
determination does not address any other complaints that may be pending with our office or the 
Board. Please feel free to contact our office at (617) 963-2540 if you have any questions 
regarding this letter.
