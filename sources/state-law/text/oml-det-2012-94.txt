RE: Open Meeting Law Complaint 
Dear Attorney McCarron: 
Our office reviewed two complaints filed by Mr. Thomas Atwood, dated August 18, 2011 
and November 23, 2011, alleging violations of the Open Meeting Law, G.L. c. 30A, §§ 18-25, by 
the West Newbury Board of Selectmen (the "Board"). The first complaint alleges that at the 
Board's May 18, 2011 meeting, the Board "held discussions 'off the record' in Executive 
Session regarding a legal matter involving a former employee," and requests that the minutes of 
the May 18, 2011 meeting, and the May 25, 2011 meeting during which the minutes of the May 
18, 2011 meeting were discussed and approved, be amended to more accurately reflect the 
discussion that occurred. The first complaint was originally filed with the Board on July 18, 
2011. We received the Board's response to that complaint dated July 29, 2011. The second 
complaint alleges that the minutes of the Board's May 25, 2011 executive session were 
insufficiently detailed, and only included "two sentences for a 15-minutes discussion." That 
complaint was filed with the Board on October 5, 2011. We received the Board's response to 
that complaint on October 26, 2011. 
The May 18, 2011 minutes were approved by the Board on May 25, 2011. The Open 
Meeting Law states that a complaint must be filed with the chair of the public body within 30 
days of the alleged violation. See G.L. c. 30A, § 23(b). The Attorney General's Open Meeting 
Law regulations state that "if the alleged violation of [the Open Meeting Law] could not 
reasonably have been known at the time it occurred, then [the complaint must be filed] within 30 
days of the date it should reasonably have been discovered." 940 CMR 29.05(3). Mr. Atwood 
was a member of the Board and participated in the review of the May 18, 2011 executive session 
===PAGE 2===
minutes, thus he was aware of the alleged deficiencies in those minutes at the time they were 
approved. However, Mr. Atwood did not file his complaint with the Board until July 18, 2011 — 
54 days later. Therefore, we decline to investigate the allegations related to the May 18, 2011 
meeting because it was not timely filed with the Board. 
The first complaint also alleges that the minutes of the May 25, 2011 meeting did not 
adequately reflect the discussion of the May 18, 2011 minutes that occurred on that date. The 
Board reviewed draft minutes of its May 25, 2011 meeting during its June 29, 2011 meeting, and 
again during its July 13, 2011 meeting. According to the complaint, the second draft of the 
minutes "included additional sentences but still lacked the broad philosophical discussion that 
occurred about the minutes." The Board approved those minutes at its September 21, 2011 
meeting, and the discussion on that date prompted the second complaint. The second complaint 
alleges that the minutes are "missing entire topics discussed that evening related to the minutes 
of May 18, 2011, which are important to the events discussed as well as future possible actions 
related to currently pending legal actions." 
A public body must create and maintain accurate minutes of all meetings, including 
executive sessions. G.L. c. 30A, § 22(a). The minutes must include the meeting "date, time and 
place, the members present or absent, a summary of the discussions on each subject, a list of 
documents and other exhibits used at the meeting, the decisions made and the actions taken at 
each meeting, including the record of all votes." Id. While the minutes must include a summary 
of the discussions on each subject, a transcript is not required. OML 2012-29; OML 2011-55. 1  
When reviewing meeting minutes for compliance with the requirement to create and maintain 
accurate meeting minutes, we look for substantial compliance with the accuracy requirement. Id. 
By substantial compliance, we mean that the minutes should contain enough detail and accuracy 
so that a member of the public who did not attend the meeting could read the minutes and have a 
clear understanding of what occurred. Id. 
After reviewing both the approved minutes of the Board's May 25, 2011 executive 
session, as well as the handwritten contemporaneous notes of the Board's secretary, we find that 
the Board's May 25, 2011 executive session minutes substantially complied with the requirement 
that it provide an accurate and sufficiently detailed summary of the discussion that occurred. 
Because the minutes have not yet been publicly released, we do not recount their content here. 
We note, however, that while the minutes do not cover every remark or opinion presented at the 
meeting, and could have included more detail regarding the Board's discussion of the May 18, 
2011 executive session minutes, the Board included the minimum that the law requires. 
Although the content of meeting minutes beyond those minimum requirements is left to the 
discretion of the public body, we encourage public bodies to include additional detail when 
possible.
