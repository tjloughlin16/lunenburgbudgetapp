Dear Chair Jozsef: 
This office received an Open Meeting Law complaint from Janine Savoy, dated June 26, 
2012, alleging that the Middlefield Board of Assessors (the "Board") violated the Open Meeting 
Law, G.L. c. 30A, §§ 18-25. The complaint alleges that the Board prohibited Ms. Savoy from 
audio recording its open meeting on May 26, 2012, and that the Board failed to post notice for 
that meeting. The complaint was filed with the Board on or about June 8, 2012. The Board 
responded to the complaint by a letter dated June 15, 2012. 
Following our review, we find that the Board violated the Open Meeting Law by failing 
to post a proper notice for its May 26, 2012 meeting and by discouraging Ms. Savoy from 
recording its May 26, 2012 meeting. In reaching this determination, we reviewed the June 8, 
2012 complaint; the Board's June 15, 2012 response; and the June 26, 2012 complaint filed with 
our office. We also reviewed the minutes of the Board's May 26, 2012 meeting and a screen 
capture of the Town of Middlefield's website calendar page, depicting the notice for the Board's 
May 26, 2012 meeting. 
FACTS 
The Board posted a notice for its May 26, 2012 meeting on the Town of Middlefield's 
website. The date and time of posting are not recorded on or with the notice, though Chair Gita 
Jozsef2  states in her June 15, 2012 response to the complaint that the notice was posted to the 
website more than 48 hours before the Board's May 26, 2012 meeting. 3  The notice was not 
posted at any other location.

At the beginning of Board's May 26, 2012 meeting, Board member Janine Savoy stated 
that she wanted to audio record the meeting. Board member Laura Lafreniere stated that she was 
uncomfortable with Ms. Savoy recording the meeting. The Board voted 2-1 to "request her not 
to tape the meeting." Ms. Savoy complied with the request. 
DISCUSSION 
The complaint alleges that the Board failed to post notice of its May 26, 2012 meeting. 
The Open Meeting Law requires that "[e]xcept in an emergency, in addition to any notice 
otherwise required by law, a public body shall post notice of every meeting at least 48 hours 
prior to such meeting, excluding Saturdays, Sundays and legal holidays," and that notice for a 
local public body "shall be filed with the municipal clerk and posted in a manner conspicuously 
visible to the public at all hours in or on the municipal building in which the clerk's office is 
located." G.L. c. 30A, §§ 20(b), (c). The Attorney General's regulations provide that 
municipalities may choose an alternate notice posting method, including posting to a municipal 
website. 940 CMR 29.03(2)(b)(a). However, before adopting an alternative notice posting 
method, the municipality "shall file with the Attorney General written notice of adoption of the 
alternative method, including the website address where applicable." 940 CMR 29.03(1)(c). 
Our office has not received a written notice of adoption of an alternative notice posting method 
for the Town of Middlefield. 
Here, the Board posted the notice for its May 26, 2012 meeting on the municipal website. 
Because Middlefield has not notified the Attorney General that it has adopted an alternative 
notice posting method, the Board should have posted its notice "in or on the municipal building 
in which the clerk's office is located." G.L. c. 30A, § 20(c); 940 CMR 29.03(1)(c). In her June 
15, 2012 letter in response to the complaint, Chair Gita Jozsef wrote that "we will post all future 
meetings on the town hall bulletin board" as well as the municipal website. We are satisfied that 
the Board is aware of its obligations and has committed to compliance with the Open Meeting 
Law in the future. We remind the Board that notices posted to the Town Hall bulletin board 
must be visible to the public at all hours. G.L. c. 30A, § 20(c). 
The complaint also alleges that the Board unlawfully prohibited Ms. Savoy from audio-
recording its May 26, 2012 meeting. The Open Meeting Law states that "[a]fter notifying the 
chair of the public body, any person may make a video or audio recording of an open session of a 
meeting of a public body, or may transmit the meeting through any medium." G.L. c. 30A, § 
20(e). Therefore, any individual—including members of public bodies—may record an open 
session meeting of a public body. See OML 2012-24. 4  
After Ms. Savoy stated her desire to record at the beginning of the Board's May 26, 2012 
meeting, the Board voted 2-1 to "request her not to tape the meeting." Ms. Savoy and the Board 
disagree as to whether or not that vote was a mere request that she not record the meeting, or an 
order that she not record, but in either event she complied and did not tape the meeting. In the 
response to this complaint, Chair Jozsef writes that "[Board member] Laura [Lafreniere] and I 
are fully aware that any person has a right to record a public meeting." We note that the right to

record a meeting also carries with it an obligation that the public body refrain from discouraging 
those who wish to exercise that right. While the chair may impose reasonable restrictions on the 
"number, placement, and operation of equipment used so as not to interfere with the conduct of 
the meeting," because the Law unequivocally states that "any person may make a video or audio 
recording of an open session meeting of a public body," it is improper for a public body to 
discourage an individual who wishes to record a meeting from doing so. See G.L. c. 30A, § 
20(e). We therefore find that the Board violated the Open Meeting Law. 
CONCLUSION 
We find that the Board violated the Open Meeting Law by failing to post proper notice 
for its May 26, 2012 meeting, and by discouraging an individual from recording an open meeting 
after she notified the Chair of her desire to do so. We order the Board's immediate and future 
compliance with the Open Meeting Law, and caution that similar future violations may be 
considered evidence of an intentional violation of the law.
