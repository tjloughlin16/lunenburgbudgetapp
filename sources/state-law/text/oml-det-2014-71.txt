RE: Open Meeting Law Complaint 
This office received a complaint from Bruce and Angela Stetson dated May 12, 2014, 
alleging that the Hinsdale Police Chief Search Committee (the Committee) violated the Open 
Meeting Law, G.L. c. 30A, §§ 18-25. The complaint was originally filed with the Committee on 
or about March 10, 2014, and you responded to the complaint on behalf of the Committee by 
letter dated March 25, 2014.' In their complaint, the Stetsons allege that the Committee failed to 
include a reasonably anticipated topic in the notice for its March 7, 2014 meeting. 
Following our review, we find that the Committee violated the Open Meeting Law by 
failing to include a topic reasonably anticipated by the Chair in the notice for its March 7, 2014 
meeting.2 In reaching this determination, we reviewed the March 10, 2014 complaint filed with 
the Committee; the Committee's March 25, 2014 response; and the May 12, 2014 complaint 
filed with our office, requesting further review. We also reviewed a draft timeline distributed to 
the Committee members during the March 7, 2014 meeting, and the notice for and minutes of the 
Committee's March 7, 2014 meeting. Finally, we spoke by telephone with Committee Chair 
Ray Bolduc on June 2, 2014 and June 3, 2014.

FACTS 
We find the facts as follows. On Monday, March 3, 2014, the Committee posted notice 
for a meeting to be held on Friday, March 7,2014. The meeting notice included the following 
topics for discussion: "New Business: a. Organizational meeting-structure and moving forward; 
b. Establish schedule." According to Chair Bolduc, he and several other Committee members 
had served on a previous police chief search committee and wanted to bring their prior 
experience to this Committee. Therefore, for the Committee's first meeting, he decided to 
provide the members with an example of an advertisement that the previous search committee 
used to solicit applicants.3 During the March 7, 2014 meeting. Chair Bolduc distributed a packet 
of documents to the Committee, which included this sample advertisement as well as a draft 
timeline for the Committee to complete its mission. The first item on the draft timeline was 
publication of an advertisement on March 10, 2014. The Committee reviewed the sample 
advertisement and discussed possible media sites on which to post its own advertisement. The 
Massachusetts Chiefs of Police Association was discussed as one of these potential posting sites. 
The Committee decided that the language of the advertisement would be discussed and finalized 
at the Committee's next meeting. 
DISCUSSION 
The Open Meeting Law requires that public bodies post notice 48 hours in advance of a 
meeting and include, a "listing of topics that the chair reasonably anticipates will be discussed at 
the meeting." G.L. c. 30A, § 20(b). Public bodies are required to list topics in a meeting notice 
with "sufficient specificity to reasonably advise the public of the issues to be discussed at the 
meeting." 940 CMR 29.03; OML 2012-34; OML 2014-47.4 We generally consider a topic to 
include sufficient specificity when a reasonable member of the public could read the topic and 
understand the anticipated nature of the public body's discussion. See OML 2014-58; OML 
2013-157. 
As part of the Committee's first organizational meeting. Chair Bolduc distributed a 
packet that included a draft timeline and a copy of a previous advertisement for a full-time police 
chief. The Committee reviewed and discussed both documents. Although the notice did not 
specifically state that the Committee would discuss the timeline for the police chief search, it 
could be reasonably inferred from the topic "Establish Schedule" that the Committee could 
discuss such a document. Chief Bolduc contends that the discussion of the advertisement was 
also reasonably inferable from the notice because it was part of the Committee's overall 
organizational process and fell under the topic "Organizational meeting-structure and moving 
forward." We disagree. Members of the public could not reasonably infer from this broad topic 
that the discussion would include a proposed advertisement for the vacant position. See OML 
2013-71. Chair Bolduc should, therefore, have listed this anticipated item as either a separate 
discussion topic or as a sub-topic on the notice. We note that while the Committee's discussion

CONCLUSION 
We find that the Committee violated the Open Meeting by failing to include a topic that 
was reasonably anticipated by the Chair in the notice for its March 7, 2014 meeting. As of June 
4, 2014, this Committee has been disbanded. We therefore order that, should the Committee be 
reconstituted, it shall comply with the Open Meeting Law, and caution that similar future 
conduct may be considered evidence of intent to violate the law. 
We now consider this matter closed. This determination does not address any other 
complaints that may be pending with our office or the Committee. Please feel free to contact our 
office at (617) 963-2540 if you have any questions.
