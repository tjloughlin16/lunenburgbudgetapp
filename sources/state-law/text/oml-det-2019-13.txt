RE: Open Meeting Law Complaint
Dear Attorney Mead:
This office received a complaint from Allen Waller on January 24, 2019 alleging that the 
Marblehead Board of Selectmen (the “Board”) violated the Open Meeting Law, G.L. c. 30 A, §§
18-25. The complaint was originally filed with the Board on November 30, 2018, and you 
responded, on behalf of the Board, by letter dated January 15, 2019, following an extension of 
time granted by our office. The complaint alleges that, because the Board failed to discuss a 
litigation matter during numerous meetings held between December 2017 and November 2018, it 
therefore must have deliberated on such matter outside of a properly posted meeting.1
We resolve this matter by informal action, in accordance with 940 CMR 29.07(2)(a), 
after reviewing the original complaint, the Board’s response to the complaint, the complaint filed 
with our office requesting further review, and the notices and minutes of Board meetings held on 
October 30, 2018; November 14, 2018; and November 20, 2018. We also spoke with the 
complainant and Attorney Sarah Bellino, Board legal counsel, by telephone on February 5, 2019. 
Following our review, we find that the Board did not violate the Open Meeting Law as alleged.
The Open Meeting Law was enacted “to eliminate much of the secrecy surrounding 
deliberation and decisions on which public policy is based.” Ghiglione v. School Board of 
Southbridge, 376 Mass. 70, 72 (1978). The Open Meeting Law requires that meetings of a pubic 
body be properly noticed and open to members of the public, unless an executive session is 
convened. See G.L. c. 30A, §§ 20(a)-(b), 21. A “meeting” is defined, in relevant part, as “a

deliberation by a public body with respect to any matter within the body’s jurisdiction.” G.L. c. 
30A, § 18. The law defines “deliberation” as “an oral or written communication through any 
medium, including electronic mail, between or among a quorum of a public body on any public 
business within its jurisdiction.” Id. For the purposes of the Open Meeting Law, a “quorum” is 
a simple majority of the members of a public body. Id.
The complaint alleges that the Board did not discuss a specific litigation matter involving 
a landfill during its open session meetings and therefore must have secretly deliberated about the 
matter. The litigation matter was not listed as a topic on the October 30, 2018, November 14, 
2018, or November 20, 2018 notices and the Board did not discuss the litigation matter during 
those meetings. The Open Meeting Law does not require a public body to discuss any topic. See 
OML 2014-98; OML 2013-64; OML 2012-23.2 Moreover, the Open Meeting Law imposes no 
obligation on chairs of public bodies to place items on a meeting agenda, unless the chair 
anticipates discussing a particular topic. See OML 2015-73.
The Board denies that it discussed the litigation matter outside of a properly posted 
meeting. Absent sufficient evidence to the contrary, we credit the Board’s account of the facts 
and find that it did not have any private discussions outside of a noticed meeting. See OML 
2014-122. As the Board did not deliberate outside of a properly posted meeting, we find no 
violation of the Open Meeting Law.
For the reasons stated above, we find that the Board did not violate the Open Meeting 
Law. We now consider the complaint addressed by this determination to be resolved. This 
determination does not address any other complaints that may be pending with our office or the 
Board. Please feel free to contact our office at (617) 963-2540 if you have any questions 
regarding this letter.
