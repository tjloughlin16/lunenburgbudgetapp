 RE: Open Meeting Law Complaint 
Dear Ms. Kadar-Hull: 
This Office received a complaint from Steven Baker on February 5, 2021, alleging that 
the Groveland Board of Health (the “Board”) violated the Open Meeting Law, G.L. c. 30A, §§ 
18-25. The complaint was originally filed with the Board on January 6, and the Board responded 
by letter dated January 11.1 In his complaint, Mr. Baker alleges that the Board failed to timely 
approve meeting minutes for all meetings since late March 2020.2 Mr. Baker also alleges that the 
Board did not timely respond to his request for meeting minutes under the Open Meeting Law.  
Following our review, we resolve this complaint by informal action in accordance with 
940 CMR 29.07(2)(a) and find that the Board violated the Open Meeting Law by failing to 
approve meeting minutes in a timely manner. In reaching this determination, we reviewed the 
original complaint, the Board’s response to the complaint, and the complainant’s request for 
further review. We also reviewed the Board’s website and corresponded by email with you, the 
Groveland Town Clerk, and the Board’s Administrative Assistant in February 2021.  
DISCUSSION 
 The Open Meeting Law requires that a public body “create and maintain accurate 
minutes of all meetings, including executive sessions, setting forth the date, time and place, the 
members present or absent, a summary of the discussions on each subject, a list of documents

and other exhibits used at the meeting, the decisions made and the actions taken at each meeting, 
including the record of all votes.” G.L. c. 30A, § 22(a). The meeting minutes must be reviewed 
and approved in a timely manner. G.L. c. 30A, § 22(c). “Timely manner” means “within the next 
three public body meetings or within 30 days, whichever is later, unless the public body can 
show good cause for further delay.” See 940 CMR 29.11; OML 2018-48.3 Whenever possible, 
we recommend that minutes of a meeting be approved at the next meeting. See OML 2018-67; 
OML 2017-133.  
  Mr.  Baker alleges that the Board did not timely approve meeting minutes for the 
following 2020 meetings: March 30, April 6, April 30, May 14, August 10, September 2, 
November 2, November 16, December 11, December 17, December 23. The Board’s website 
makes clear that the Board subsequently met on December 31, January 7, February 1, and 
February 10. In its response, the Board concedes that some of its meeting minutes were not 
timely approved and pledges that the Board’s part-time administrative assistant will work to 
complete the outstanding minutes “as soon as possible in these unprecedented times.” We 
understand the demands on a public body with limited staff; however, the Board is still obligated 
to timely approve minutes. See OML 2019-21; OML 2018-111; OML 2017-115. The 
responsibility for complying with the Open Meeting Law rests with public bodies and members 
of such bodies, not with individual public employees, such as administrative staff. See OML 
2019-21; OML 2018-105. If staff resources are not available to create minutes for the Board to 
approve in a timely manner, then Board members must undertake this responsibility themselves 
to ensure compliance with the Law. See 2019-21; OML 2017-115. When Mr. Baker filed his 
complaint on January 6, more than 30 days and three meetings had elapsed since the Board’s 
March 30, April 6, April 30, May 14, August 10, September 2, November 2, and November 16 
meetings, and we therefore find that the Board failed to timely approve minutes for any of those 
meetings that had not yet been approved by January 6.  
 The Open Meeting Law also requires that a public body respond to a request for minutes 
of both open and executive sessions within 10 days of the request. G.L. c. 30A, § 22(c), (g)(2). A 
public body must provide open session minutes, if they exist and whether approved or in draft 
form, within those 10 days. G.L. c. 30A, § 22(c). Executive session minutes, however, may be 
withheld from disclosure to the public as long as publication may defeat the lawful purposes of 
the executive session, but no longer. G.L. c. 30A, § 22(f). If a public body has determined, prior 
to the request, that the requested executive session minutes may be released, the body must 
respond to the requester directly, notifying him or her as to whether those minutes are available. 
See OML 2020-101. If, however, the public body has not conducted a review of the minutes to 
determine whether continued nondisclosure is warranted, the body must perform such a review 
and release the minutes, if appropriate, no later than its next meeting or within 30 days, 
whichever occurs first. G.L. c. 30A, § 22(g)(2). In such circumstances, the public body must still 
respond to the requestor within 10 days, notifying him or her that it is conducting this review. 
See OML 2020-16; OML 2015-53.

assistant requesting “under the state open meeting law” that all meeting minutes for the Board’s 
2020 meetings be either posted online or sent to him. Then, having received no response from

CONCLUSION 
 For the reasons stated above, the Board violated the Open Meeting Law by failing to 
approve meeting minutes in a timely manner. We order the Board’s immediate and future 
compliance with the Open Meeting Law and caution the Board that a determination by our 
Office of a similar violation in the future may be considered evidence of intent to violate the 
Open Meeting Law.  
 We also order the Board, if it has not already done so, to create and approve meeting 
minutes for the following 2020 meetings within 60 days of receipt of this determination: March 
30, April 6, April 30, May 14, August 10, September 2, November 2, November 16, December 
11, December 17, and December 23. Finally, we order the Board to provide to Mr. Baker, within 
65 days of receipt of this determination, copies of any of these eleven sets of meeting minutes 
which the Board has not already provided to him. 
 We now consider the complaint addressed by this determination to be resolved. This 
determination does not address any other complaints that may be pending with our Office or the 
Board. Please feel free to contact the Division at (617) 963 - 2540 if you have any questions.
