This office received a complaint from Patrick Higgins on January 16, 2026, alleging that
the Sharon Cultural Council (the "Council") violated the Open Meeting Law, G.L. c. 30A, §§ 18-
25. The complaint was originally filed with the Council on or about December 23, 2025, and
you responded, on behalf of the Council, by email on January 16, 2026.! The complaint alleges
that the Council violated the Open Meeting Law by failing to include on the notice the specific
grant applications it planned to discuss during a meeting held on December 8, 2025.
We appreciate the patience of the parties while we reviewed this matter. We resolve this
matter by informal action, in accordance with 940 CMR 29.07(2)(a), after reviewing the
original complaint, the Council's response to the complaint, the complaint filed with our office
requesting further review, and the notice and minutes of the Council meeting held on December
8, 2025. We find that the Council violated the Open Meeting Law in the way alleged.
The Open Meeting Law requires that public bodies post notice of every meeting "at least
48 hours prior to such meeting" and that every notice include "the date, time and place of such
meeting and a listing of topics that the chair reasonably anticipates will be discussed at the

Here, notice for the Council’s December 8, 2025, meeting stated the following:  
 
Mass Cultural Council Grantmaking Panel 
 
Voting Process, Session 2: 
 
1. Round 1: We will announce the application and requested amount, then hold 
an initial Yes/No vote with a show of hands. 
2. Deliberation: If there is not a unanimous decision, we will briefly discuss the 
application. One person from each side of the vote will share their perspective, 
starting with the side with less votes. 
3. Round 2: We will re-vote, and the majority vote will determine the final Yes 
or No outcome. 
4. Award Amount: For approved applications, we will then signal award 
amounts through hand vote and take an average of the proposed amounts to 
decide our final funding amount.  
 
We find that the Council violated the Open Meeting Law by failing to include on the 
notice a list of the specific grant applicants that the chair anticipated discussing.  See OML 2021-
36; OML 2018-96; OML 2017-33.  During the meeting, the Council reviewed 56 grant 
applications and awarded over $13,000 to 41 applicants.  We have previously explained that a 
notice must include details about each grant application, including the identity of the applicant, 
and/or the address that is the subject of an application to be considered by the public body.  See 
OML 2021-57; OML 2021-36.  This level of detail is intended to provide members of the public 
with sufficient information to understand what business is being transacted by the public body 
and the opportunity to attend and observe deliberations in which they are interested.  See OML 
2016-167; OML 2011-11.  Furthermore, we remind the Council that the specific information 
must be included on or with the notice itself; it is not acceptable for a meeting notice to refer 
members of the public to another location, such as a Town Clerk’s office, to find the required 
information.  See OML 2019-22; Town of Swansea v. Healey, 2018 WL 8692757, at *3 (Suffolk 
Sup. Ct. October 29, 2018).   
 
Although not raised in the complaint, we note our concern that the Council did not appear 
to vote by roll call during the December 8, 2025, meeting.  The December 8, 2025, meeting was 
held via Zoom and all Council members participated remotely.  All votes taken during a meeting 
where Council members participate remotely must be by roll call vote.  See 940 CMR 
29.10(7)(c); OML 2016-132; OML 2015-145.  Moreover, we note that the Open Meeting Law

requires that a public body create and maintain accurate minutes of all meetings, and that
minutes must include, among other things, "the record of all votes." G.L. c. 30A, § 22(a).
Because the Open Meeting Law requires a public body to create accurate minutes, all votes taken
during a meeting must be accurately recorded. Thus, to be accurate, minutes must document a
roll call vote by listing the names of each public body member and how each voted. Even
unanimous votes should be recorded by roll call in the minutes. See OML 2015-131; OMI
2013-195. We remind the Council of its obligations under the Open Meeting Law.
For the reasons stated above, we find that the Council violated the Open Meeting Law by
failing to list on the notice for its December 8, 2025, meeting the specific grant applicants it
planned to discuss. We order the Council's immediate and future compliance with the Open
Meeting Law, and caution that future similar violations may be considered evidence of intent to
violate the law.
We now consider the complaint addressed by this determination to be resolved. This
determination does not address any other complaints that may be pending with our office or the
Council. Please feel free to contact our office at (617) 963-2540 if you have any questions
regarding this letter.
