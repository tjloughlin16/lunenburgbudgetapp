 RE: Open Meeting Law Complaint 
 
Dear Attorney Goldberg: 
 
This office received a complaint from Michael Hennessey on August 25, 2023, alleging 
that the Bellingham Charter Review Committee (the “Committee”) violated the Open Meeting 
Law, G.L. c. 30A, §§ 18-25.1  The complaint was originally filed with the Committee on or 
around July 26 and you responded to the complaint, on behalf of the Committee, on August 15.  
The complaint alleges that the Committee failed to timely approve minutes of meetings held 
since February 2022.   
 
Following our review, we find that the Committee violated the Open Meeting Law as 
alleged in the complaint.  In reaching this determination, we reviewed the original complaint, the 
Committee’s response to the complaint, and the complainant’s request for further review.  In 
addition, we reviewed minutes of Committee meetings held on June 16, July 12, August 9, and 
August 23. 
 
The Open Meeting Law requires that a public body “create and maintain accurate 
minutes of all meetings, including executive sessions, setting forth the date, time and place, the 
members present or absent, a summary of the discussions on each subject, a list of documents 
and other exhibits used at the meeting, the decisions made and the actions taken at each meeting, 
including the record of all votes.”  G.L. c. 30A, § 22(a).  The meeting minutes must be reviewed 
and approved in a timely manner.  G.L. c. 30A, § 22(c).  “Timely manner” means “within the 
next three public body meetings or within 30 days, whichever is later, unless the public body can

show good cause for further delay.”  See 940 CMR 29.11; OML 2018-48.2  Whenever possible, 
we recommend that minutes of a meeting be approved at the next meeting.  See OML 2018-67; 
OML 2017-133.  
 
The Committee does not  dispute that it failed to timely approve minutes of meetings as 
alleged in the complaint.  Instead, the Committee asserts that the Open Meeting Law complaint 
was not filed with the Committee in a timely manner and therefore the Attorney General should 
decline to review it.  We disagree.  Complaints alleging violations of the Open Meeting Law 
generally must be filed with the public body within 30 days of the alleged violation.  G.L. c. 
30A, § 23; 940 CMR 29.05(4).  The Open Meeting Law imposes a continuing obligation to 
maintain minutes of all meetings.  See G.L. c. 30A, § 22(a); OML 2020-69 (reviewing a 
complaint regarding a committee’s failure to approve meeting minutes for a three-year period); 
OML 2019-133; OML 2018-115 (reviewing a complaint regarding the failure to create and 
maintain meeting minutes for meetings held four years prior); OML 2013-45.  Therefore, the 
alleged violation continued each day until the minutes were approved, and the complaint was 
timely filed where the minutes still had not been approved as of the date the complaint was filed.  
The minutes of the Committee’s August 30 meeting document that on that date the Committee 
approved minutes of six meetings held in 2021, eight meetings held in 2022, and four meetings 
held in 2023.  The Committee’s approval of all of the 2021 and 2022 meetings, as well as the 
May 10, 2023, meeting, was untimely.
3  Therefore, the Committee violated the Open Meeting 
Law as alleged in the complaint. 
 
To the extent the complaint separately alleg
 es that the Committee violated the Open 
Meeting Law by failing to post meeting minutes online, we note that the Open Meeting Law 
does not require a public body to post minutes to a website, but rather to provide meeting 
minutes upon request within 10 days, if the minutes exist, whether approved or still in draft form.  
See G.L. c. 30A, § 22(c). Therefore, the lack of minutes posted to a website does not necessarily 
indicate that the minutes were not timely approved and does not violate the Open Meeting Law.  
See OML 2022-16; OML 2020-19; OML 2018-107. 
 
For the reasons stated above, we find that the Committee violated the Open Meeting Law 
as alleged.  We order the Board’s immediate and future compliance with the with the Open 
Meeting Law and caution the Board that a determination by our office of a similar violation in 
the future may be considered evidence of intent to violate the Open Meeting Law.  The Board 
has since approved all the meeting minutes at issue and posted the minutes online, and therefore 
we order no further remedial action.
