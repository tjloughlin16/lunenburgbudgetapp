This office received three complaints from Terry Kinnas (the complainant), one dated 
December 30, 2013 and two dated January 16, 2014, alleging that the Pittsfield School Building 
Needs Commission (the Commission) and its Design Subcommittee (the Subcommittee) violated 
the Open Meeting Law, G.L. c. 30A, §§ 18-25. The complaint against the Commission was 
originally filed on October 22, 2013, and the Commission responded by letter dated October 31, 
2013. The complaints against the Subcommittee were originally filed with the Subcommittee on 
October 15, 2013 and October 22, 2013, and the Subcommittee responded by letters dated 
October 23, 2013 and October 31, 2013, respectively. The complaints allege that the 
Subcommittee failed to properly post notice at least 48 hours before a meeting held on October 
4, 2013, and that the location of two meetings held on October 21, 2013 lacked adequate signage. 
We reviewed the October 14, 2013 and October 22, 2013 complaints; your October 23, 
2013 and October 31, 2013 responses on behalf of the Commission and the Subcommittee; and 
the December 30, 2013 and January 16, 2014 complaints filed with our office, requesting further 
review. We also reviewed the notice and open session minutes for the October 4, 2013 meeting 
as well as the notice and open session minutes for the October 21, 2013 meetings. Finally, we 
spoke by telephone with the complainant, with Superintendent Jason McCandless, and with 
Pittsfield City Clerk Linda Tyre in January 2014.1 
Following our review, we are unable to determine whether the Commission failed to post 
notice at least 48 hours in advance of its October 4, 2013 meeting, however we find that the

FACTS 
The Commission submitted a notice to the City of Pittsfield City Clerk on October 2, 
2013 at 10:54 A.M. for a meeting to be held on October 4, 2013 at 11 A.M. The usual practice 
of the Clerk's office upon receiving such notice is to print, date stamp, and physically post the 
notice at City Hall before scanning the notice to the City's website and an electronic billboard. 
The City has not adopted an alternative posting method pursuant to 940 CMR 29.03(2)(b). At 
times, a delay occurs between the physical posting and the scan. The notice was posted to the 
City's website at 11:57 A.M. on October 2, 2013. 
At the Commission's October 4, 2013 meeting, the Commission created the Design 
Subcommittee by introducing its members. The Design Subcommittee reviewed the design 
process with representatives of a private firm and approved the design process during the same 
meeting. The October 15, 2013 complaint alleging that the notice for this meeting had not been 
timely posted followed. 
In response to that complaint, the Commission posted notice and an agenda well in 
excess of the 48 hour minimum for two meetings held on October 21, 2013. The agenda 
included the room location. During the first meeting, in an effort to address any deficiency in the 
notice for its October 4, 2013 meeting, the Commission repeated the discussion held during that 
meeting, following the same agenda. Immediately following this remedial meeting focusing on 
the Design Subcommittee, the Commission held its own meeting. 
The complainant attended both October 21, 2013 meetings. His October 22, 2013 
complaints allege that the Subcommittee and the Commission failed to provide adequate signage 
to direct the public from the parking lot to the doors unlocked for public access and then to the 
room in the building where the meetings occurred. In response to the October 22, 2013 
complaints, the Commission and Subcommittee purchased signage to direct members of the 
public to unlocked doors and then to the meeting location. 
DISCUSSION 
I. We are Unable to Determine Whether The Commission Posted Notice Forty-
Eight Hours Before its October 4, 2013 Meeting 
The Open Meeting Law requires that, except in an emergency, "a public body shall post 
notice of every meeting at least 48 hours prior to such meeting, excluding Saturdays, Sundays 
and legal holidays." G.L. c. 30A, § 20(b). For local public bodies, notice shall be filed with the 
municipal clerk and posted in a manner conspicuously visible to the public at all hours in or on

the municipal building in which the clerk's office is located. Id. at § 20(c). A municipality may 
designate a website as an alternative notice posting location for all local public bodies within the 
municipality. 940 CMR 29.03(2)(b)(a). 
Here, the City has not adopted an alternative notice posting method, thus the official 
posting method remains the default physical posting on a municipal building. The notice for the 
Commission's October 4, 2013 meeting was received by the City Clerk little more than 48 hours 
in advance of that meeting. The usual practice of the Clerk's office is to post the notice 
physically with little delay after receipt. Although it is clear that the notice was not posted 
electronically until approximately 47 hours in advance of the meeting, we are unable to 
determine when it was physically posted. 
Thus, we are unable to determine if the Commission violated the Open Meeting Law by 
failing to post notice within 48 hours of a meeting. We note, however, that the Commission took 
appropriate action in response to the complaint by properly posting notice for a subsequent 
meeting at which it deliberated on the same agenda items. Accordingly, we order no further 
action in response to the October 15, 2013 complaint. 
II. The Public Had Sufficient Access to and Notice of the October 21, 2013 
Meetings' Location. 
The Open Meeting Law states that all meetings of a public body must be open to the 
public. G.L. c. 30A, § 20(a). For a meeting to be truly "open to the public," it must be held at a 
location that is accessible to persons with disabilities and open for the duration of the meeting. 
See id.; see also OML 2012-49; OML 2012-46. The October 22, 2013 complaints allege that no 
signs were posted at the building in which the October 21, 2013 meetings took place for the 
purpose of directing the public to the proper room. Additionally, the complaint alleges some 
doors to the building were locked. 
The room location of both October 21, 2013 meetings was displayed on each meeting's 
agenda. See G.L. c. 30A, § 20(b). Furthermore, the complainant was able to gain admittance to 
the meeting room location on the day of the meeting. We therefore find that these meetings were 
open and no violation of the Open Meeting Law occurred. We commend the Commission and 
Subcommittee for taking the proactive steps to purchase and post appropriate signage to reduce 
any confusion of those seeking to attend future meetings of those bodies. 
CONCLUSION 
We find that the Commission did not violate the Open Meeting Law with respect to its 
October 21, 2013 meetings. We are unable to determine whether the Commission failed to post 
notice at least 48 hours in advance of its October 4, 2013 meeting, but find that the Commission 
took appropriate action in response to the complaint. 
We now consider the complaints addressed by this determination resolved. This 
determination does not address any other complaints that may be pending with our office, the
