RE: Open Meeting Law Complaint 
Dear Attorney Feodoroff: 
This office received a complaint from Stephen Morgan on September 8, alleging that 
the Ashland Board of Selectmen (the "Board") violated the Open Meeting Law, G.L. c. 3OA, 
§§ 18-25.1 Lhe complaint was originally filed with the Board on or about July 30, and you 
responded, on behalf of the Board, by letter dated September 8. In his complaint, Mr. Morgan 
alleges that the Board improperly deliberated by email. 
Following our review, we resolve this matter by informal action, in accordance with 
940 CMR 29.07(2)(a), and find that the Board violated the Open Meeting Law. 
Lhe complaint alleges that, on July 28, Board member Carl Hakansson responded to 
an email from the complainant to the Board regarding the Charter Review Committee. 
Similar to OML 2017-140, we conclude that Mr. Hakansson's email concerned a matter 
within the Board's jurisdiction, in violation of the Open Meeting Law. However, we 
acknowledge that, in both instances, the Board members were attempting to respond to the 
complainant, who had copied the entire Board, and did not necessarily intend to communicate 
to their fellow Board members. Emails sent to a quorum of a public body inviting a response 
have the potential to result in an inadvertent reply to all recipients expressing a matter within 
the public body's jurisdiction. Because of this risk, we continue to recommend that public 
bodies avoid communicating with each other over email except for distributing meeting 
agendas, scheduling meetings, or distributing documents to be discussed at meetings, 
provided that no opinions are expressed. See OML 2013-133; OML 2013-29.2
