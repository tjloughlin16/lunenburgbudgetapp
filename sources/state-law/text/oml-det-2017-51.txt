RE: Open Meeting Law Complaint 
Dear Chairman Cullett: 
This office received a complaint from Robin Wadsworth, dated March 14, 2017, alleging 
that the Peru Board of Selectmen (the "Board") violated the Open Meeting Law, G.L. c. 30A, §§ 
18-25. The complaint was originally filed with the Board on January 30, 2017, and the Board 
responded by letter dated February 6, 2017. In her complaint, Ms. Wadsworth alleges that the 
Board deliberated on a topic that was not listed on the meeting notice. 
Following our review, we find that the Board did not violate the Open Meeting Law. In 
reaching a determination, we reviewed the original complaint filed with the Board, the Board's 
response, and the request for further review filed with our office. We reviewed all 
documentation provided with these filings, including the minutes of Board meetings held on 
December 5, 2016 and January 9, 2017. 
The complaint concerns the Board's discussion of software used by the Peru Board of 
Assessors. During a Board meeting held on December 5, 2016, the chair stated that he had 
received a letter from the Massachusetts Office of Information Technology ("Mass IT") 
requesting an opinion from the Board concerning the software currently in use by the Peru Board 
of Assessors.1 Following this meeting, the Board chair conferred with the Peru Town Assessor, 
who recommended that the Board take no action on Mass IT's letter at that time. 
On January 9, 2017, Mass IT contacted the Peru Town Administrator to say that a 
response was required on the December 2016 letter. The Town Administrator then contacted the 
Board chair to relay this conversation. The Board had a meeting scheduled for that evening.

During the January 9, 2017 meeting, the Town Assessor was present and the Board discussed the 
software issue. This discussion was not among the topics listed on the meeting notice. 
DISCUSSION 
Under the Open Meeting Law, a public body must post notice of a meeting at least 48 
hours in advance, including a "listing of topics that the chair reasonably anticipates will be 
discussed at the meeting." G.L. c. 30A, § 20(b). Public bodies are required to list topics on a 
notice with "sufficient specificity to reasonably advise the public of the issues to be discussed at 
the meeting." 940 CMR 29.03(1 )(b); OML 2014-47.2 While public bodies may discuss a topic 
not listed on a meeting notice if the topic was not anticipated by the chair 48 hours in advance of 
the meeting, we encourage public bodies to postpone discussion of such topics until proper 
notice can be given to the public. See OML 2013-87; OML 2013-13. 
Here, the complaint alleges that the Board violated the Open Meeting Law by failing to 
list the discussion of the software issue on the January 9, 2017 meeting notice. It is clear from 
the Board's response that the chair anticipated discussing the software issue at some future 
meeting, however the Board's response states that he did not anticipate discussing the matter at 
the January 9, 2017 meeting until he was contacted by the Town Administrator that day. See 
OML 2017-1 (public body need not list a topic that the chair did not anticipate would be 
discussed at a specific meeting). Absent sufficient evidence to the contrary, we credit the public 
body's account of the facts. See OML 2013-174; OML 2011-44. Accordingly, we find that the 
chair did not anticipate discussing the topic at issue during the January 9, 2017 meeting. While 
the complaint argues that the Board could have delayed discussions until a later meeting when 
proper notice could be given, we note that a public body does not violate the Open Meeting Law 
by discussing an unanticipated topic during a meeting. We therefore find no violation of the 
Open Meeting Law with respect to this allegation. 
For the reasons stated above, we find that the Board did not violate the Open Meeting 
Law. We now consider the complaint addressed by this determination to be resolved. This 
determination does not address any other complaints that may be pending with our office or the 
Board. Please feel free to contact the Division at (617) 963 - 2540 if you have any questions.
