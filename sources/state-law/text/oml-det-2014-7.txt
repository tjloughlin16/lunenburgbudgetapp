RE: Open Meeting Law Complaint 
Dear Attorney Goldberg: 
This office received a complaint from Lauren Walsh, dated November 29, 2013, alleging 
that the Seekonk Animal Shelter Building Committee (the "Committee") and the Seekonk Board 
of Selectmen (the "Board") violated the Open Meeting Law, G.L. c. 30A, §§ 18-25. The 
complaint was originally filed with the Board on or about September 18, 2013, and you 
responded, on behalf of the Board and the Committee, to the original complaint by letter dated 
October 25, 2013. In her complaint, Ms. Walsh alleges that the Committee failed to approve 
sufficiently detailed minutes for 17 meetings held between January 10, 2012 and May 16, 2013.1 
The Open Meeting Law states that "prior to the filing of a complaint with the attorney 
general, the complainant shall file a written complaint with the public body, setting forth the 
circumstances which constitute the alleged violation; provided, however, that such complaint 
shall be filed within 30 days of the date of the alleged violation." G.L. c. 30A, § 23(b). If the 
alleged violation could not reasonably have been discovered at the time it occurred, the 
complaint must be filed with the public body within 30 days of when it reasonably could have 
been discovered. 940 CMR 29.05(3). 
We find the allegations regarding 16 of the 17 sets of meeting minutes untimely, and 
decline to review them. These minutes record meetings held between January 2012 and May 7,

With respect to the minutes of the Committee's May 16, 2013 meeting, at the time this

However, we find that the Committee did violate the law by failing to approve the final minutes 
in a timely manner. Minutes of all open session meetings shall be created and approved in a 
timely manner. G.L. c. 30A, § 22(c). Although the Open Meeting Law does not define "timely 
manner," we recommend that minutes of a meeting be approved at the next meeting, whenever 
possible. See OML 2013-155; OML 2013-31. The May 16, 2013 minutes were approved on 
September 24, 2013, more than four months after the meeting. Therefore, we find that the 
minutes were not approved in a timely manner. We order the Committee's immediate and future 
compliance with the Open Meeting Law.
