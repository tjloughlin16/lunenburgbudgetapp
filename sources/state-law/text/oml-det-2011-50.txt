RE: Open Meeting Law Complaint/B.Trainor-Tessier 
Dear Mr. Mycock: 
This office received a complaint filed by Ms. Barbara Trainor-Tessier ("Complainant") 
on September 22, 2010 alleging violations of the Open Meeting Law, G.L. c. 39, § 23B1  by the 
Cotuit Board of Fire Commissioners ("Board"). Specifically, the Complainant alleges that 
between September 25, 2007 and April 22, 2010 the Board failed to maintain minutes of its 
executive sessions. The complaint was filed with the Board on August 19, 2010. Responses to 
the complaint were received on or about August 25, 2010 and September 14, 2010. A complaint 
was filed with our office on September 20, 2010. 
The Attorney General assumed responsibility for enforcement of the Open Meeting Law, 
G.L. c. 30A, §§ 18-25, on July 1, 2010. As you are aware, prior to that date, the District 
Attorneys enforced the law. As part of our statutory obligations, we will review and potentially 
investigate complaints of alleged violations that occurred after July 1, 2010. However, we have 
no plans to re-open complaints that were reviewed by the District Attorneys before July 1, 2010. 
It is our understanding that an Open Meeting Law complaint based on the same facts was 
investigated and closed by the Cape and Islands District Attorney in June 2010. During that 
investigation, it was determined that the Board failed to maintain records of executive sessions. 
To resolve the matter, the Board was reminded of its obligation to maintain records of executive 
sessions under the law. Additionally, the Board was required to undergo training on the

requirements of the Open Meeting Law, including the requirements imposed by the revisions to 
the Open Meeting Law that went into effect on July 1, 2010, and encompassing the specific 
requirements for maintaining records of executive sessions. 
On June 17, 2010, Mr. Mark H. Boudreau, attorney for the Board, conducted a training 
based on materials discussed with and provided to him by the District Attorney. Following the 
training, the District Attorney's office indicated that it was satisfied with the steps taken by the 
Board and considered the case closed. Our office confirmed this through conversations with 
Cape and Islands Assistant District Attorney Thomas Shack and by review of an affidavit signed 
by Attorney Boudreau. 
The District Attorney's office found that the Board failed to maintain adequate records of 
its executive session minutes, in violation of G.L. c. 39, § 23B, and the Board subsequently 
acknowledged that fact and underwent training intended to ensure that it understands its 
responsibilities and what is required of it under the law. The Complainant has every right to 
demand that the Board comply with the law going forward but, for the reasons stated above, we 
decline to reconsider this previously resolved complaint. We do, however, remind the Board of 
the requirement in G.L. c. 30A, § 22 that public bodies create and maintain accurate minutes of 
all meetings, including executive sessions, and caution that future action by the Board that 
violates the law in a way that previous orders have notified it is illegal may be found to be an 
intentional violation. 
We appreciate your patience as we sought to confirm details of the earlier resolution. 
Having confirmed that information, we are now satisfied that the Board has been informed of its 
responsibilities under the law going forward and we consider this matter resolved. Please do not 
hesitate to contact our office with any questions at (617) 963-2540.
