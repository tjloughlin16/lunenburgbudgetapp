Re; Open Meeting Law Complaint 
Dear Attorney Cormier: 
This office received a complaint from William Plummer, dated July 25, 2013, alleging 
that the Concord-Carlisle Regional School Committee (the "Regional School Committee") and 
the Concord School Committee (the "School Committee") (collectively "the Committees") 
violated the Open Meeting Law, G.L. c. 30A, §§ 18-25. The complaint was originally filed with 
the Committees on or about June 14, 2013, and the Committees responded to the original 
complaint by letter dated July 3, 2013. The complaint alleges that the Committees illegally 
deliberated outside of posted meetings to elect their chairs. 
Following our review, we find that the Committees did not violate the Open Meeting 
Law. In reaching this determination, we reviewed the June 14, 2013 complaint; the Committees' 
July 3, 2013 response; and the July 25, 2013 complaint filed with our office. Additionally, we 
reviewed emails provided by Mr. Plummer, sent by members of the Committees, dated May 14, 
2013 and May 15, 2013. We reviewed written statements from Regional School Committee 
Chair Louis Salemy, dated September 4, 2013, and School Committee Chair Pamela Gannon, 
dated September 4, 2013, provided to our office in response to our inquiry. Finally, we reviewed 
the minutes of the School Committee's May 14, 2013 meeting and the minutes of the Regional 
School Committee's May 28, 2013 meeting.

FACTS 
We find the facts to be as follows. The Town of Concord held an election on March 19, 
2013. Following the Town election, both the School Committee and the Regional School 
Committee held meetings during which new officers were elected to those bodies. According to 
the Committees, for at least the past 10 years, the practice of these bodies has been to elect the 
previous year's vice chair as the next chair, except where the chair has remained in that role for 
successive years. 
During its May 14, 2013 open meeting, the School Committee elected Pamela Gannon to 
serve as chair. Ms. Gannon had served as vice-chair the previous year. During its May 28, 2013 
open meeting, the Regional School Committee elected Louis Salemy to serve as chair. Mr. 
Salemy had served as vice-chair the previous year. 
Prior to these meetings, on May 13, 2013, Mr. Salemy sent an email to several 
individuals responding to a citizen's concern regarding the legal services budget. In that email, 
he identifies himself as "the incoming Chair of the Concord Carlisle Regional District," and 
writes that he has spoken with Pam Gannon, "incoming Chair for Concord Public Schools." Both 
Mr. Salemy and Ms. Gannon denied, in signed statements submitted to this office, that any 
deliberation with respect to their election as chair took place other than at the open meetings 
discussed. 
DISCUSSION 
The Open Meeting Law defines "deliberation," in relevant part, as an "oral or written 
communication through any medium, including electronic mail, between or among a quorum of 
a public body on any public business within its jurisdiction." G.L. c. 30A, § 18. All deliberation 
must occur during a meeting. G.L. c. 30A, §§ 18, 20(a). 
Mr. Salemy identified himself as the incoming Chair of the Regional School Committee, 
and Ms. Gannon as the incoming Chair of the School Committee, in his May 13, 2013 email. 
Mr. Plummer alleges that this, coupled with the fact that there was little public discussion of the 
issue during meetings of the Committees, demonstrates that the two bodies must have chosen 
their leadership outside of public view. In its formal response to the complaint and in signed 
statements by the two chairs, the Committees deny any unlawful deliberation, and explain that 
Mr. Salemy's May 13, 2013 email "reflects each Committee's past practice of electing its Vice 
Chair to the office of Chair." The Committees further note that the lack of discussion during the 
officer elections was "consistent with past practice" of merely elevating the prior vice chairs to 
chair, "a longstanding and eminently practical tradition." In the absence of any evidence to the 
contrary, we accept the Committees' explanation, and find no violation of the Open Meeting 
Law.
