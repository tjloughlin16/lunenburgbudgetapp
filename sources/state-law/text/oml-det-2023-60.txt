This office received a complaint from Meredith Kilpatrick on October 11, 2022, alleging 
that the Mashpee Select Board (the “Board”) violated the Open Meeting Law, G.L. c. 30A, §§ 
18-25.  The complaint was originally filed with the Board on September 13, 2022, and you 
responded to the complaint, on behalf of the Board, by letter dated October 3, 2022.  The 
complaint alleges that the Board allowed a quorum of Planning Board members to speak during 
a Board meeting held on September 12, 2022.  
 
We resolve this matter by 
 informal action, in accordance with 940 CMR 29.07(2)(a), 
after reviewing the original complaint, the Board’s response to the complaint, the complaint filed 
with our office requesting further review, and the notice, minutes and video recording of the 
Board meeting held on September 12, 2022.
1  We find that the Board did not violate the Open 
Meeting Law in the way alleged. 
 
On September 12, 2022, the Board held a meeting and discussed several town meeting 
warrant articles.  During a discussion of Article 10, the Board heard from the Chair and Vice 
Chair of the Planning Board who explained that the new article would repeal and replace the 
current floodplain zoning bylaw.  Specifically, the proposed new bylaw would prohibit residents 
who live in the floodplain from using fill, such as sand or rock, to raise or replace an existing 
home or construct a new home.  During the presentation by the Planning Board Chair and Vice 
Chair, a third member of the Planning Board spoke about the proposed new article.  The Town 
Manager then noted that a quorum of Planning Board members was present; the third member

 Here, the complaint alleges that Board Chair David Weeden violated the Open Meeting 
Law
 by “intentionally, knowingly allowed and encouraged the deliberation of Planning Board 
members.”  The Open Meeting Law provides that “[n]o person shall address a meeting of a 
public body without permission of the chair, and all persons shall, at the request of the chair, be 
silent.”  G.L. c. 30A § 20(f); see also OML 2021-27; OML 2020-161; OML 2017-189; OML 
2014-23; OML 2012-78.3  As such, the law gives the chair of the public body discretion to 
decide who may speak or otherwise participate during a meeting.  See OML 2017-189; OML 
2014-23; OML 2012-78.  Thus, we find that Chair Weeden did not violate the Open Meeting 
Law by allowing three members of the Planning Board to speak at the September 12, 2022, 
meeting.   
 
We decline to review whether the Planning Board may have violated the Open Meeting 
Law by de
liberating among a quorum outside of a properly posted meeting because the 
complaint at issue here was only filed with the Board.  See G.L. c. 30A § 23(b) (individuals who 
allege a violation of the Open Meeting Law must first file a complaint with the public body 
alleged to have violated the Law).  We note that a quorum of one public body may be in the 
same place at the same time, including attending the meeting of another public body, without 
posting their own meeting notice, provided they do not deliberate.  See OML 2015-38.   
 
For the reasons stated above, we find that the Board did not violate the Open Meeting 
Law.  We now consider the complaint addressed by this determination to be resolved.  This 
determination does not address any other complaints that may be pending with our office or the 
Board.  Please feel free to contact our office at (617) 963-2540 if you have any questions 
regarding this letter.
