This office received a complaint from Tanya Polak on September 20, 2022, alleging that 
the Blackstone Conservation Commission (the “Commission”) violated the Open Meeting Law, 
G.L. c. 30A,  §§ 18-25.  The complaint was originally filed with the Commission on or about 
August 3, 2022, and you responded to the complaint, on behalf of the Commission, by email on 
January 31, 2023.
1  The  complaint alleges that the Commission discussed a topic, 
reorganization, that was not listed on the notice of a meeting held on July 18, 2022.   
 
We resolve this matter by informal action, in ac
 cordance with 940 CMR 29.07(2)(a), 
after reviewing the original complaint, the Commission’s response to the complaint, the 
complaint filed with our office requesting further review, and the notices, minutes and video 
recordings of the Commission meetings held on July 18, 2022, and August 15, 2022.2  We find 
that the Commission violated the Open Meeting Law in the way alleged.

The Open Meeting Law requires that, except in an emergency, “a public body shall post 
notic
e of every meeting at least 48 hours prior to such meeting, excluding Saturdays, Sundays 
and legal holidays.”  G.L. c. 30A, § 20(b).  The notice must be printed in a legible, easily 
understandable format and must contain the date, time and place of the meeting, as well as a 
listing of topics that the chair reasonably anticipates will be discussed.  Id.  The list of topics 
shall have “sufficient specificity to reasonably advise the public of the issues to be discussed at 
the meeting.”  940 CMR 29.03(l)(b).  We generally consider a topic to be sufficiently specific 
when a reasonable member of the public could read the topic and understand the anticipated 
nature of the public body’s discussion.  See OML 2015-35; OML 2012-71; OML 2011-44.
3   
 
On July 14, 2022, at 12:47 P.M., the Commission posted notice for a meeting to be held 
on July 18, 2022, at 7:00 P.M.  The notice listed ten topics including Roll Call – Attendance, 
New Business, Old Business and several specific substantive topics .   
 
The July 18, 2022, meeting was held as planned.  At the beginning of the meeting, after 
taking attendance, Commission Vice Chair Julie Wingate announced that the Commission 
needed to reorganize because there was currently no chair.
4  The Commission unanimously 
voted for Jim Pitler as Chair and Ryan Casey as Vice Chair.  The Commission then discussed the 
remaining noticed topics. 
 
The Commission admits that the topic of reorganization “was inadvertently left off the 
agenda.”  As such, we find that the Commission violated the Open Meeting Law by not 
specifically including on the notice a topic to discuss reorganization.  We commend the 
Commission for taking prompt remedial action by including the specific topic of reorganization 
on the notice of the Commission’s next meeting held on August 15, 2022.  During that meeting, 
the Commission revoted to reorganize and again appointed Jim Pitler as Chair and Ryan Casey 
as Vice Chair.   
 
Finally, although not specifically ra
 ised in the complaint, we remind the Commission that 
boilerplate topics, such as “Old Business” or “New Business,” are too vague to offer the public 
an understanding of the anticipated topics to be discussed at that particular meeting; if the chair 
anticipates discussing a topic under “Old Business” or “New Business” more than 48 hours prior 
to the meeting, then that specific topic must be listed in the meeting notice.  G.L. c. 30A, 20(b); 
see OML 2022-234; OML 2021-51; OML 2016-45; OML 2013-14.  
 
For the reasons stated above, we find that the Commission violated the Open Meeting 
Law by failing to include a topic regarding reorganization on its notice for the July 18, 2022, 
meeting.  We order immediate and future compliance with the law’s requirements, and we 
caution that similar future violations could be considered evidence of intent to violate the law.  
 
We now consider the complaint addressed by this determination to be resolved.  This
