Re: Open Meeting Law Complaint 
Dear Attorney Riley: 
This office received an Open Meeting Law complaint from Michael Young on March 10, 
2014, alleging that the Winchendon Board of Selectmen (the "Board") violated the Open 
Meeting Law, G.L. c. 30A, §§ 18-25. The complaint specifically alleges that the Board 
deliberated outside of a meeting to approve a letter to the Inspector General's Office, and that the 
Board failed to include an anticipated topic in the notice for its January 13, 2014 meeting. The 
complaint was filed with the Board on or about January 28, 2014. The Board responded to the 
complaint in a letter dated February 18, 2014.1 
Following our review, we find that the Board did not violate the Open Meeting Law. In 
reaching this determination, we reviewed the January 28, 2014 complaint filed with the Board; 
the Board's February 18, 2014 response; and the March 10, 2014 complaint filed with our office. 
Additionally, we reviewed emails between the Town Manager and Board members regarding the 
Inspector General's letter, sent between December 27, 2013 and January II, 2014.

FACTS 
On January 16, 2013, the Board issued a Request for Proposals ("RFP") pursuant to G.L. 
c. 306, § 16, for acquisition of a suitable site for a proposed police station. The Town received 
two responses to the RFP, and both were publicly opened on March 4, 2013. The Police Station 
RFP02013-04 Review Committee (the "Committee") declared a winning bidder in an open 
meeting on April 25, 2013. The Committee selected Winchendon Court, LLC's proposal as the 
winning bid. The Board and Winchendon Court, LLC signed a Purchase and Sale Agreement on 
August 29, 2013. 
On December 24, 2013, the Office of the Inspector General sent the Town a letter raising 
concerns about the legality of its selection process and its ultimate selection of the property for 
purchase. On that day. Town Counsel notified Town Manager James Kreidler that she had 
received the letter and that she would prepare a response. On December 27, 2013, Mr. Kreidler 
emailed a copy of the Inspector General's letter to each of the Board members. In that email, he 
advised the Board members not to provide any "written commentary" in response his email. On 
December 31, 2013, Town Counsel emailed Mr. Kreidler a draft letter responding to the 
Inspector General. That same day, Mr. Kreidler forwarded Town Counsel's email to the Board 
members. Mr. Kreidler then sent Town Counsel his own comments on the letter. None of the 
Board members provided comments on the letter. 
On Thursday, January 9, 2014, the Board posted notice for a Monday, January 13, 2014 
meeting. The notice did not include a topic regarding the Inspector General's letter because 
Town Counsel was still considering Mr. Kreidler's comments at the time, and had not yet 
produced a revised draft response for the Board to review. On Friday, January 10, 2014, Town 
Counsel sent Mr. Kreidler a revised draft response to the Inspector General's letter. On 
Saturday, January 11, 2014, Mr. Kreidler prepared the packet for the Board's January 13, 2014 
meeting, which included the revised letter from Town Counsel. 
During the Board's January 13, 2014 meeting, the Board discussed the Inspector 
General's letter, and approved the response letter drafted by Town Counsel. The Board members 
present then signed the letter. 
DISCUSSION 
1. The Board did not Violate the Open Meeting Law by Failing to List a Topic in the Notice 
for its January 13, 2014 Meeting. 
The Open Meeting Law requires that, except in an emergency, "a public body shall post 
notice of every meeting at least 48 hours prior to such meeting, excluding Saturdays, Sundays 
and legal holidays." G.L. c. 30A, § 20(b). The notice shall "contain the date, time and place of 
such meeting and a listing of topics that the chair reasonably anticipates will be discussed at the 
meeting." Id.

The Chair did not know at the time notice was posted for the Board's January 13, 2014 
meeting whether Town Counsel would have a draft response to the Inspector General's 
December 24, 2013 letter prepared for the Board to review and approve at that meeting. Town 
Counsel was still considering Mr. Kreidler's comments at the time, and had not yet produced a 
revised draft response for the Board to review. Therefore, we find that the Chair did not 
reasonably anticipate discussing the draft letter prepared by Town Counsel more than 48 hours, 
not including Saturday and Sunday, prior to the Board's January 13, 2014 meeting. 
Although the Board was legally permitted to discuss the draft letter during its January 13, 
2014 meeting, we encourage the Board to postpone discussion of unanticipated topics whenever 
possible. When reasonable, the Board should wait to discuss items not included in the meeting 
notice until those topics can be listed in a meeting notice 48 hours prior to the meeting, with 
enough specificity to advise the public and Board members of the issues to be discussed during 
the meeting. See OML 2013-25; OML 2012-85; OML 2011-53. 
2. The Board did not Deliberate Outside of a Meeting Regarding its Response to the 
Inspector General's Letter. 
The Open Meeting Law defines "Deliberation" as "an oral or written communication 
through any medium, including electronic mail, between or among a quorum of a public body on 
any public business within its jurisdiction." G.L. c. 30A, § 18. All deliberation must occur 
during a noticed meeting. G.L. c. 30A, §§ 18, 20(b). A "Quorum" is a "simple majority of the 
members of the public body, unless otherwise provided in a general or special law, executive 
order or other authorizing provision." G.L. c. 30A, § 18. Because the Board consists of five 
members, three constitutes a quorum. 
The complaint alleges that the Board deliberated outside of a noticed meeting some time 
prior to its January 13, 2014 meeting, to discuss its response to the Inspector General's 
December 24, 2013 letter. After reviewing the Board's response and the emails sent between 
Mr. Kreidler and the Board, we do not find evidence that the Board members deliberated outside 
of a noticed meeting. When the Town received the Inspector General's December 24, 2013 
letter, the Town Manager forwarded it to Town Counsel to prepare a response. The Town 
Manager kept the Board members abreast of the letter and the Town Counsel's effort to prepare a 
response. When the response was prepared, the Town Manager sent it to the Board members. 
The Board members then discussed and approved the response letter during the Board's January 
13, 2014 meeting. A quorum of the Board's members did not communicate regarding the 
response letter at any time prior to the January 13, 2013 meeting. Therefore we find no violation 
of the law. 
CONCLUSION 
For the reasons stated above, we find that the Board did not violate the Open Meeting 
Law. We now consider this matter closed. Please contact our office at (617) 963-2540 if you
