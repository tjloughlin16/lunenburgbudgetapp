 RE:  Open Meeting Law Complaint 
 
Dear Mr. Wright: 
 
This office received a complaint from Owen Smith on May 13, 2024, alleging that the 
Newburyport Licenses and Permits Committee (the “Committee”) violated the Open Meeting 
Law, G.L. c. 30A, §§ 18-25.  The complaint was originally filed with the Committee on April 22, 
2024, and City Clerk Richard Jones responded, on behalf of the Committee, by email dated June  
21, 2024.
1  The complaint alleges that the Committee failed to timely respond to a request for 
meeting minutes.   
 
We resolve this matter by informal action, in accordance with 940 CMR 29.07(2)(a), 
after reviewing the original complaint, the Committee’s response to the complaint, and the 
complaint filed with our office requesting further review.  We find that the Committee violated 
the Open Meeting Law in the way alleged. 
 
 
1 We remind the Committee that the Open Meeting Law requires that within 14 business days of receipt of a 
complaint, the public body must meet to review the complaint’s allegations; take remedial action, if appropriate;

a copy of the complaint and a description of any remedial action taken.  G.L. c. 30A, § 23(b); 940 CMR 29.05(5).  
While a public body may delegate another individual to respond to a complaint, proper procedure requires that a 
public body review a complaint before authorizing such other individual to respond on its behalf.  See OML 2017-
132; OML 2012- 95; OML 2011-6.

The Open Meeting Law requires that a public body “create and maintain accurate 
minutes of all meetings, including executive sessions.”  G.L. c. 30A, § 22(a).  The minutes of an 
open session, if they exist and whether approved or in draft form, shall be made available upon 
request by any person within 10 days.”  G.L. c. 30A, § 22(c).  If such minutes do not yet exist at 
the time of a request, the public body is still required to respond to the request within 10 days 
with an explanation of whether the minutes do or do not exist in either approved or draft form.  
See OML 2018-98; OML 2017-50; OML 2016-71; OML 2015-173.2 
 
On April 11, 2024, the complainant sent an email to Committee Chair Mark Wright 
requesting copies of minutes of all Committee meetings held in 2024.  The complainant received 
no response from the Committee.  On April 22, 2024, the complainant filed with the Committee, 
as well as with City Clerk Richard Jones, the Open Meeting Law complaint at issue here 
regarding the Committee’s failure to provide meeting minutes.  Two hours later, Mr. Jones 
responded to the complainant and directed him to the municipal website where meeting minutes 
were available.  A public body must make minutes of an open session available within 10 days of 
a request, if they exist.  However, the Open Meeting Law does not specify in what format 
minutes must be made available.  Therefore, directing a requester to a website where the 
requested minutes are posted and accessible complies with the requirement to make the minutes 
“available.”  See OML 2024-24; OML 2022-205.  Here, however, neither the Committee nor 
anyone acting on its behalf responded to the request within 10 days.3  Therefore, we find that the 
Committee violated the Open Meeting Law by not properly responding to a request for meeting 
minutes.  We order no further remedial action as the Committee has since posted all requested 
minutes to its website.   
 
For the reasons stated above, we find that the Committee violated the Open Meeting Law 
by failing to timely respond to a request for meeting minutes.  We order immediate and future 
compliance with the law’s requirements, and we caution that similar future violations could be 
considered evidence of intent to violate the law.   
  
 
2 Open Meeting Law determinations may be found at the Attorney General’s website, https://www.mass.gov/the-

3 We note that a request for meeting minutes may fall under the Public Records Law or the Open Meeting Law, 
depending on how the request is made, and the requirements for responding differ.  A request for minutes that is 
directed to a clerk or other municipal employee as Records Access Officer will likely fall under the Public Records 
Law and requires a response in 10 business days.  See G.L. c. 66, § 10; OML 2019-126; OML 2019-68.  A request 
for meeting minutes made directly to the public body falls under the Open Meeting Law and requires a response 
within 10 calendar days.  See G.L. c. 30A, § 22(g)(2); OML 2019-125.
