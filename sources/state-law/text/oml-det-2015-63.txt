RE: Open Meeting Law Complaint 
Dear Attorney Fried: 
This office received a complaint from Mr. Michael Goodwin, dated February 16, 2015, 
alleging that the Hopkinton Board of Selectmen (the "Board") violated the Open Meeting Law, 
G.L. c. 30A, §§ 18-25. The complaint was originally filed with the Board on December 19, 
2014, and the Board responded to the original complaint by letter dated January 12, 2015. In his 
complaint, Mr. Goodwin alleges that the Board: (i) improperly convened an executive session 
pursuant to G.L. c. 30A, § 21(a)(2); and (ii) created insufficiently specific meeting agendas on 
three occasions. 
Following our review, we find that the Board violated the Open Meeting Law by failing 
to provide sufficient detail in several meeting notices regarding its negotiations with nonunion 
personnel and discussion of ongoing litigation matters. We also find that, on one occasion, the 
Board improperly discussed compensation policy in executive session. In reaching a 
determination, we reviewed the original complaint; the Board's response; and the request for 
further review filed with our office. We also reviewed minutes of open session meetings the 
Board held on October 21, 2014; November 18, 2014; December 2, 2014; December 16, 2014; 
and January 6, 2015. Finally, we reviewed, in camera, draft minutes of an executive session 
held by the Board on November 18, 2014. 
FACTS 
We find the facts as follows. The Board is a five-member public body that meets at 
regular intervals to discuss and act on matters of relevance to the Town of Hopkinton. The 
Board held a meeting on October 21, 2014. The notice listed an anticipated executive session 
"[t]o conduct contract negotiations with nonunion personnel." At the meeting, the Board entered 
executive session and discussed the contracts of several nonunion employees. 
The Board held another meeting on November 18, 2014. The notice for this meeting 
stated, in part, that the Board anticipated convening in executive session "[t]o conduct contract

negotiations with nonunion personnel." The notice also listed an anticipated executive session 
"[t]o discuss strategy with respect to litigation, because an open meeting may have a detrimental 
effect on the litigation position of the board." Similar language appeared in the notices for the 
Board's December 2 and December 16, 2014 meetings. The name of the litigation matter at 
issue was not listed on any of these three notices. The Board convened in executive session on 
November 18 and December 16, 2014, and discussed pending litigation on both dates. The 
Board entered executive session during the December 2, 2014 meeting, but it did not discuss 
pending litigation. 
The Board held a meeting on January 6, 2015. During this meeting, the Board voted to 
amend its October 21, 2014 agenda and open session minutes to indicate that the Board met in 
executive session to discuss Fire Chief Kenneth Clark. Also during the January 6, 2015 meeting, 
the Board voted to amend the November 18 and December 16, 2014 agendas and open session 
minutes to indicate that the Board met in executive session to discuss a pending lawsuit 
captioned Doe v. Town of Hopkinton. 
DISCUSSION 
I. The Board Violated the Open Meeting Law by Providing Insufficient Information 
Regarding Anticipated Executive Sessions. 
The Open Meeting Law requires that all meetings of a public body be conducted in an 
open session, with some exceptions. G.L. c. 30A, §§ 20(a), 21(a). Public bodies may enter a 
closed, executive session for any of ten enumerated purposes, provided that the chair of the 
public body first announces in open session the purpose for the executive session, "stating all 
subjects that may be revealed without compromising the purpose for which the executive session 
was called." G.L. c. 30A, §§ 21(a), 21(b)(3). One permissible reason to convene in executive 
session is "[t]o conduct strategy sessions in preparation for negotiations with nonunion personnel 
or to conduct collective bargaining sessions or contract negotiations with nonunion personnel." 
G.L. c. 3OA, § 21(a)(2) ("Purpose 2"). Another permissible reason for entering executive session 
is "[t]o discuss strategy with respect to collective bargaining or litigation if an open meeting may 
have a detrimental effect on the bargaining or litigating position of the public body and the chair 
so declares." G.L. c. 30A, § 21(a)(3) ("Purpose 3"). 
Public bodies must identify in the notice for a meeting the specific executive session 
purpose they intend to invoke to justify an executive session, and must provide as much detail as 
possible to inform the public of the nature of the executive session discussion without 
compromising the confidentiality of the executive session. See OML 2011-9. Thus, with respect 
to Purpose 2 and Purpose 3, we have said that unless disclosure will defeat the purpose for the 
executive session, public bodies should state in their notice the name of the specific nonunion 
personnel with whom they will be negotiating, or the name of the litigation matter that will be 
discussed. See OML 2014-145; OML 2013-97. 
Here, the October 21, 2014 meeting agenda should have contained Chief Clark's name. 
Likewise, the November 18 and December 16, 2014 notices should have stated that the Board 
planned to discuss the Doe v. Town of Hopkinton lawsuit. The Board's failure to include this 
detail violated the Open Meeting Law, because inclusion of this information would not have 
compromised the purpose for secrecy. See OML 2013-139. We note that, although the topic

was noticed, the Board did not actually discuss any litigation matters in executive session during 
its December 2, 2014 meeting. When a public body does not actually engage in executive 
session deliberation, the Open Meeting Law is not violated with respect to the sufficiency of the 
meeting notice. See OML 2014-81. 
We note with approval the Board's efforts to address these violations after receiving the 
complaint, however these actions were not sufficient to address the deficiency in the notices. 
Amending meeting notices after the fact does not achieve the Open Meeting Law's purpose of 
notifying the public as to what topics a public body plans to discuss. See OML 2013-23; OML 
2012-68. 
II. On One Occasion, the Board Discussed an Impermissible Topic in Executive 
Session. 
While a public body may discuss the contract of a specific nonunion employee or a 
collective bargaining agreement in executive session, general compensation policy must be 
discussed in open session. See OML 2014-30; OML 2013-91. Here, while the meeting agenda 
stated that the executive session was convened, in part, "[t]o conduct contract negotiations with 
nonunion personnel," our review of the as-yet-unreleased November 18, 2014 executive session 
minutes shows that the Board used this executive session to discuss a more wide-ranging strategy 
that would reach beyond any one employee or bargaining unit. We have said in the past that 
such deliberations are akin to a budget discussion, and must therefore take place in open session. 
See OML 2014-30. We therefore find that the Board violated the Open Meeting Law by holding 
this discussion in executive session. 
CONCLUSION 
For the reasons stated above, we find that the Board violated the Open Meeting Law. We 
order immediate and future compliance with the Open Meeting Law, and we caution that future 
similar violations may be considered evidence of intent to violate the law. Furthermore, because 
a discussion took place during the November 18, 2014 executive session that lacked a proper 
purpose, we order the Board to release these executive session minutes to the public within thirty 
(30) days of receipt of this letter. The Board may not redact or withhold these minutes or any 
portion thereof based on privilege or any exemption under the Public Records Law. See G.L. c. 
4, § 7, cl. 26. 
We now consider the complaint addressed by this determination to be resolved. This 
determination does not address any other complaints that may be pending with our office or the 
Board. Please feel free to contact the Division at (617) 963 - 2540 if you have any questions.
