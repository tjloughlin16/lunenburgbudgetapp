RE: Open Meeting Law Complaint 
Dear Attorney Mead: 
This office received an Open Meeting Law complaint from Leslie Githens dated January 
30, 2012, alleging that the Ashland Board of Selectmen (the "Board") violated the Open Meeting 
Law, G.L. c. 30A, §§ 18-25. The complaint specifically alleges that the Board "decided at a 
meeting under the guise of a retreat to vote down multiple articles on the Special Town Meeting 
(STM) Warrant. This was not an item for discussion on their agenda." The complaint was 
originally field with the Board on November 8, 2011. The Board responded through its counsel, 
by letter dated November 17, 2011. 
We appreciate the patience and cooperation of the parties during our investigation. 
Following our review, we find that the Board did not violate the Open Meeting Law. In reaching 
this determination we reviewed the November 8, 2011 complaint; the Board's November 17, 
2011 response; and the January 30, 2012 complaint filed with our office. We also reviewed the 
notices and minutes of the Board's November 5, 2011 and November 10, 2011 meetings. 
FACTS 
On October 25, 2011, the Ashland Board of Health voted to place two articles on the 
Special Town Meeting Warrant for November 30, 2011. The articles were forwarded to Town 
Counsel and the Town Administrator for review on or about November 2, 2011. On November 
2, 2011, the Board posted notice for its November 5, 2011 meeting. The notice included four 
topics: "Discussion Planning/Goals Past and Future;" "Board Communications;" "Any General 
Govt concerns;" and "Board reports." The notice included a statement that "[t]his is a public 
meeting and all are welcome, but is a board retreat and not necessary for any town employees to 
===PAGE 2===
attend." 
According to the Board's November 17, 2011 response to the complaint, at the time the 
agenda was posted, the Chair was not aware that the Town Manager intended to request that the 
Board review the Warrant for the November 30, 2011 Town Meeting at its November 5, 2011 
retreat. 
The Board met for its retreat on November 5, 2011. The minutes state that during the 
meeting, "[Town Administrator John] Petrin passed out draft copies of the warrant for the Board 
to review, as well as the financial recommendations." After some discussion, the Board 
"removed the articles for scenic road changes and the Board of Health requested articles, and 
signed the warrant." 
The Board held a meeting the next week, on November 10, 2011. During the meeting, 
the complainant met with the Board to discuss the two Board of Health articles that the Board 
had removed from the warrant. Later in the meeting, the Board voted unanimously to approve a 
new Warrant, which did not include the two Board of Health articles. 
DISCUSSION 
The Open Meeting Law requires public bodies to post notice in advance of every 
meeting. "Except in an emergency, in addition to any notice otherwise required by law, a public 
body shall post notice of every meeting at least 48 hours prior to such meeting, excluding 
Saturdays, Sundays and legal holidays.... Notice shall be printed in a legible, easily 
understandable format and shall contain the date, time and place of such meeting and a listing of 
topics that the chair reasonably anticipates will be discussed at the meeting." G.L. c. 30A, § 
20(b). The Attorney General's regulations require further that the "list of topics shall have 
sufficient specificity to reasonably advise the public of the issues to be discussed at the meeting." 
940 CMR 29.03(1)(b). 
The Board posted notice on November 2, 2011 for its November 5, 2011 retreat. When 
the notice was posted, the Chair was not aware that the Town Administrator would introduce the 
Warrant for the Board's consideration during the retreat. The law requires that meeting notices 
include only those topics "the chair reasonably anticipates will be discussed at the meeting." 
G.L. c. 30A, § 20(b). Here, the Chair did not reasonably anticipate the discussion more than 48 
hours before the meeting, and therefore was not required to list the topic in the meeting notice. 
Although, it was lawful for the Board to consider the Town Meeting Warrant at the November 5, 
2011 meeting, our office strongly encourages public bodies to postpone discussion and action on 
any topic that was not listed on a meeting notice, particularly where the topic may be of 
significant interest to the public. See OML 2012-19; OML 2012-3; OML 2011-32. 
CONCLUSION 
We find that the Board did not violate the Open Meeting Law by discussing topics at its 
November 5, 2011 meeting that were not listed in the meeting notice because they were not 
reasonably anticipated by the Chair more than 48 hours before the meeting,
