 RE: Open Meeting Law Complaint 
 
Dear Chair Makepeace O’Neal: 
 
This office received a complaint from Amanda Alix on June 15, 2026,
1 alleging that the 
Shutesbury Select Board (the “Board”) violated the Open Meeting Law, G.L. c. 30A, §§ 18-25. 
The complaint was originally filed with the Board on or about May 11, and you responded on 
behalf of the Board by letter on May 18. The complaint alleges that the Board reviewed 
executive session minutes at a meeting it held on April 13 but failed to announce the result of 
that review at its next meeting, held on April 14. 
 
Following our review, we find that the Board violated the Open Meeting Law in the way 
alleged. In reaching this determination, we reviewed the Open Meeting Law complaint, the 
Board’s response, the request for further review, and the minutes of meetings the Board held on 
April 13 and April 14. 
 
FACTS 
 
We find the facts to be as follows. On September 15, 2025, the Board met in executive 
session to discuss settlement of potential claims involving former Shutesbury Police Chief 
Kristen Burgess.

DISCUSSION 
 
The Open Meeting Law was enacted “to eliminate much of the secrecy surrounding the 
deliberations and decisions on which public policy is based.” Ghiglione v. Sch. Comm. of 
Southbridge, 376 Mass. 70, 72 (1978). The Open Meeting Law permits public bodies to meet in 
executive session for one of ten enumerated purposes, and requires public bodies to create and 
maintain minutes of those executive sessions. G.L. c. 30A, §§ 21(a), 22(a). The law further 
mandates that a public body “or its chair or designee, shall, at reasonable intervals, review the 
minutes of executive sessions to determine if the provisions of [G.L. c. 30A, § 22] warrant 
continued non-disclosure.” G.L. c. 30A, § 22(g)(1). After the review, “such determination shall 
be announced at the body’s next meeting and such announcement shall be included in the 
minutes of that meeting.” Id.; see also OML 2022-155; OML 2015-62.
3 
 
 The Board met on April 13 to conduct a review of executive session minutes, as required 
by the Open Meeting Law. G.L. c. 30A, § 22(g)(1). The Board was required to announce the 
result of the April 13 review at its next meeting, held on April 14. Id. Because it failed to do so, 
we find the Board violated the Open Meeting Law. As an apparent defense to its failure to 
announce the results of the April 13 review, the Board asserts that the September 15, 2025, 
executive session minutes have been released to the complainant and posted on the Town 
website. While we commend the Board on its release of the September 15, 2025, executive 
session minutes, this action does not negate the Board’s obligations under the Open Meeting 
Law to announce the result of the executive session minutes review. The Open Meeting Law’s 
requirement to announce the result of the review in open session and record the announcement in 
the minutes is not merely a procedural formality; rather, it serves to create a permanent record of 
the review and, if the minutes were released, to notify the public of the availability of the 
executive session minutes. 
 
 Although not raised in the complaint, we take this opportunity to remind the Board of its 
obligations under the Open Meeting Law to routinely review executive session minutes. A public 
body’s obligation to review executive session minutes for possible release is ongoing. See G.L. 
c. 30A, § 22(g)(1); OML 2025-206. If a public body reviews executive session minutes and 
determines that the purpose for the executive session remains such that the minutes should 
continue to be withheld from the public, it must continue to review those same executive session 
minutes at reasonable intervals until it determines that the reason for secrecy has expired. See

OML 2022-155; OML 2021-129. Although “reasonable interval” is not defined by the Open 
Meeting Law, our office has found a quarterly review or a review every six months to be 
appropriate. See OML 2025-63; OML 2020-101; OML 2017-104. Almost seven months passed 
between the date of the September 15, 2025, executive session and the date of what we 
understand to be the first review of those minutes, April 13. 
 
CONCLUSION 
 
For the reasons stated above, we find that the Board violated the Open Meeting Law 
when it failed to announce at its next meeting the result of its April 13 review of minutes of an 
executive session held on September 15, 2025. We order the Board’s immediate and future 
compliance with the Open Meeting Law, and caution that similar future violations could be 
considered evidence of intent to violate the Law. We now consider the complaint addressed by 
this determination to be resolved. This determination does not address any other complaints that 
may be pending with the Board or with our office. Please feel free to contact our office at (617) 
963-2540 if you have any questions regarding this letter.
