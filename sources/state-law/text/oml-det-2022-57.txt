 RE: Open Meeting Law Complaint 
Dear Chair Reardon: 
 
This office received a complaint from Thomas Polito on March 10, alleging that the 
Norfolk County Advisory Board (the “Board”) violated the Open Meeting Law, G.L. c. 30A, 
§§ 18-25.  The complaint was originally filed with the Board on or around January 15, 2022, and 
you responded, on behalf of the Board, by letter dated February 14, 2022.  In his complaint, Mr. 
Polito alleges that the Board failed to timely approve minutes of its June 10, 2020, meeting.   
 
Following our review, we find that the Board violated the Open Meeting Law as alleged 
but had already taken corrective action by approving the meeting minutes in question before this 
complaint was filed.  We resolve this complaint by informal action in accordance with 940 
CMR 29.07(1), after reviewing the original complaint, the Board’s response to the complaint, the 
Complainant’s request for further review, and a further response from the Board.  
 
The Open Meeting Law requires that public bodies “create and maintain accurate minutes 
of all meetings.”  G.L. c. 30A, §22(a).  These minutes must be reviewed and approved by the 
public body in a timely manner.  G.L. c. 30A, §22(c).  “Timely manner” means “within the next 
three public body meetings or within 30 days, whichever is later, unless the public body can 
show good cause for further delay.”  940 CMR 29.11; see OML 2018-48.1  Whenever possible, 
we recommend that minutes of a meeting be approved at the next meeting.  See OML 2018-67; 
OML 2017-133.  
  
Here, during a meeting on December 15, 2021, the Board approved minutes of its June 
10, 2020, meeting.  The Complainant, a member of the Board, was present at the December 15

meeting where the Board voted to approve the minutes in question.  The Complainant 
acknowledges that the Board has approved minutes of all other meetings in accordance with the 
Open Meeting Law.  The Board asserts that the failure to approve minutes of the June 10, 2020, 
meeting until December 15, 2021, was an oversight which went unnoticed by the entire Board, 
including the Complainant.  Therefore, we find, and the Board acknowledges, that the minutes of 
the June 10, 2020, meeting were not timely approved, in violation of the Open Meeting Law.  
We acknowledge that the Board had already taken corrective action by approving the meeting 
minutes in question before this complaint was filed, and therefore we order no further remedial 
action.   
 
Finally, although the Complainant attributes the oversight in approving the minutes of the 
June 10, 2020, meeting directly to the Chair and the County Director, we note that the obligation 
for approving minutes in a timely manner rests with the public body as a whole and not any one 
individual public body member. 
 
 We now consider the complaint addressed by this determination to be resolved. This 
determination does not address any other complaints that may be pending with our office or the 
Board. Please feel free to contact the Division at (617) 963-2540 if you have any questions.
