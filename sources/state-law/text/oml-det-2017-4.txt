RE: Open Meeting Law Complaint 
Dear Attorney Greenspan: 
This office received a complaint from Scott Lake, dated October 20, 2016, alleging that 
the Dartmouth Select Board (the "Board") violated the Open Meeting Law, G.L. c. 30A, §§ 18-
25. Lhe complaint was originally filed with the Board on July 29, 2016, and the Board 
responded by letter dated August 12, 2016. In his complaint, Mr. Lake alleges that the Board 
improperly entered executive session to review an Open Meeting Law complaint.1 
Following our review, we resolve this matter by informal action, in accordance with 940 
CMR 29.07(2)(a) and find that the Board did not violate the Open Meeting Law. The 
complainant filed an Open Meeting Law complaint with the Board on July 5, 2016. On July 21, 
2016, the Board posted notice of a meeting to be held on July 25, 2016. The notice listed an 
anticipated executive session described as follows; "Pursuant to G.L. c. 30A section (7) to 
discuss a complaint relating to Open Meeting Law."2 Indeed, the Board met in executive session 
during its July 25, 2016 meeting to discuss the complaint filed on July 5, 2016. The complaint 
alleges that the Board lacked a proper purpose for convening in executive session during its July 
25, 2016 meeting. However, we have stated in past determinations that a public body may meet 
in executive session to review Open Meeting Law complaints. See OML 2015-105; OML 2013-
82; OML 2012-119. Accordingly, we find that the Board did not violate the Open Meeting Law 
with respect to this allegation.
