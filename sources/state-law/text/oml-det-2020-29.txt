RE: Open Meeting Law Complaint
Dear Attorney Howayeck:
This office received a complaint from Collin Dias on August 28, 2019, alleging that the 
Fall River Board of Park Commissioners (the “Board”) violated the Open Meeting Law, G.L. c. 
30A, §§ 18-25.1 The complaint was originally filed with the Board on June 28, and you 
responded, on behalf of the Board, by letter dated February 3, 2020.2 The complaint alleges that 
the Board improperly amended its notice by adding two new topics within 48 hours of its June 5 
meeting.
We resolve this complaint by informal action in accordance with 940 CMR 29.07(2)(a), 
after reviewing the original complaint, the Board’s response to the complaint, the complaint filed 
with our office requesting further review, and the notice and open session minutes of the Board’s 
June 5 meeting. We find that the Board did not violate the Open Meeting Law.
The Open Meeting Law requires that public bodies post notice of each meeting “at least 
48 hours prior to such meeting, excluding Saturdays, Sundays and legal holidays” and that every 
notice include “the date, time and place of such meeting and a listing of topics that the chair 
reasonably anticipates will be discussed at the meeting.” G.L. c. 30A, § 20(b). A public body 
may amend the notice within 48 hours of a meeting if a previously unanticipated topic comes to

topics for discussion with “sufficient specificity to reasonably advise the public of the issues to 
be discussed at the meeting.” 940 CMR 29.03(l)(b). We generally consider a topic to include 
sufficient specificity when a reasonable member of the public could read the topic and 
understand the anticipated nature of the public body’s discussion. See OML 2015-35; OML

On May 29 at 1:28 P.M., the Board posted notice of a meeting to be held on June 5 at 
5:15 P.M. The notice listed eight topics, including New Business, and then listed sixteen sub­
headings under this general topic heading. On the morning of the June 5 meeting, the Board 
amended its notice to include two additional topics under New Business - “Agenda & Minutes - 
Board Clerk” and “Fonseca School-No. Quarry St.-Ballfield Maintenance-(School Dept. 
Property).”4 During the meeting, the Board discussed the role of the Parks Manager and her 
additional responsibilities as “Board Clerk.” The Board also discussed the fields at the Fonseca 
School, determined that the fields were school property, and thus it was the responsibility of the 
school department to maintain the fields, not the park department.
The complaint alleges that the Board violated the Open Meeting Law by discussing these 
two topics where the topics were not listed on the original notice but added within 48 hours of 
the June 5 meeting. A public body may amend a meeting notice within 48 hours of a meeting to 
include an unanticipated topic and then discuss that topic during the meeting. See OML 2019- 
131. Here, both topics were not anticipated by the chair 48 hours in advance of the meeting; 
rather, the chair was notified and became aware of the topics within the two days prior to the 
June 5 meeting. As such, the chair could not have reasonably anticipated their discussion 48 
hours prior to the meeting. See OML 2017-2; OML 2013-87. We find that the Board did not 
violate the Open Meeting Law by amending the notice to include these two unanticipated topics. 
In fact, although the Board could have discussed the unanticipated topics without adding them to 
the meeting notice at all, the Board followed our recommended best practice of amending its 
meeting notice to include topics that arise fewer than 48 hours before a meeting, so that members 
of the public are given advance notice of agenda items. See OML 2015-111; OML 2015-76.
For the reasons stated above, we find that the Board did not violate the Open Meeting 
Law. We now consider the complaint addressed by this determination to be resolved. This 
determination does not address any other complaints that may be pending with our office or the 
Board. Please feel free to contact our office at (617) 963-2540 if you have any questions 
regarding this letter.
