RE: Open Meeting Law Complaint
Dear Mr. McAuliffe:
This office received a complaint from Patrick Higgins, on July 19, 2019, alleging that the 
Swansea Board of Selectmen (the “Board”) violated the Open Meeting Law, G.L. c. 30A, §§18- 
25. The complaint was originally filed with the Board on June 19, 2019, and you responded on 
behalf of the Board by letter dated July 10.' In his complaint, Mr. Higgins alleges that the Board 
violated the Open Meeting Law by appointing a member of the Board to Selectmen to the Board 
of Health without listing him on the meeting notice as a candidate being considered for 
appointment.
We resolve this complaint by informal action in accordance with 940 CMR 29.07(2)(a), 
and find that the Board did not violate the Open Meeting Law as alleged. The Attorney General’s 
Open Meeting Law regulations require notices to contain “a listing of topics that the chair 
reasonably anticipates will be discussed at the meeting. The list of topics shall have sufficient 
specificity to reasonably advise the public of the issues to be discussed at the meeting.” See 940 
CMR29.03(l)(b). When a public body anticipates making appointments at a meeting, the 
meeting notice should include specific details about what appointments will be made and the 
names of anticipated appointees. See OML 2017-177; OML 2017-148; OML 2016-167.1 2
Here, the Board of Selectmen’s notice for its June 18 meeting listed anticipated 
appointments to dozens of different Swansea public bodies and positions. Next to the listing for 
the appointment of Arlan Thomas to the Board of Health, the meeting notice indicated “TBD.” 
The Board explained that Chairman Kitchin anticipated asking the Board to switch Mr. Thomas

Board appointed Mr. Thomas to an alternate position, his former voting member position became
