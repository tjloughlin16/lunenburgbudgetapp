Re; Open Meeting Law Complaint 
Dear Attorney Riley: 
This office received a complaint from Vincent Currier, dated August 31, 2013, alleging 
that the Provincetown Board of Selectmen (the "Board") violated the Open Meeting Law, G.L. c. 
30A, §§ 18-25. The complaint specifically alleges that the Board discussed a topic during its 
July 8, 2013 meeting that the Chair reasonably anticipated 48 hours prior to the meeting, but 
failed to list in the meeting notice. The complaint was originally filed with the Board on or 
about August 3, 2013. We received your response on behalf of the Board in a letter dated 
August 14, 2013. Finally, we spoke with Mr. Currier by telephone on September 24, 2013. 
Following our review, we find that the Board did not violate the Open Meeting Law. In 
reaching this determination, we reviewed the August 3, 2013 complaint filed with the Board; the 
Board's August 14, 2013 response; and the August 31, 2013 complaint filed with our office. 
Additionally, we reviewed the notice and minutes of the Board's July 8, 2013 meeting. 
FACTS 
We find the facts to be as follows. Selectman Thomas Donegan emailed Board Chair 
Austin Knight ahead of the Board's July 8, 2013 meeting to request that a topic regarding the 
Chief of Police be added to the agenda. The Chair declined to add the topic. The notice for the 
Board's July 8, 2013 meeting included ten topics, but did not include a topic regarding the Chief 
of Police. Topic #3 on the agenda was listed as "Selectmen's Statements - Initial comments 
from the Selectmen. Discussion dependent - Votes may be taken." 
During the "Selectmen's Statements" portion of the July 8, 2013 meeting. Selectman Erik 
Yingling made a statement regarding the Chief of Police, and Selectman Donegan also made a

DISCUSSION 
The Open Meeting Law requires that a public body post notice of each meeting 48 hours 
in advance of the meeting. G.L. c. 30A, § 20(b). Such notice must contain "the date, time and 
place of such meeting and a listing of topics that the chair reasonably anticipates will be 
discussed at the meeting." Id. A public body may discuss topics not listed on the meeting 
notice, so long as the topics were not reasonably anticipated by the chair 48 hours before the 
meeting. See OML 2011-13.1 The Open Meeting Law does not require that the chair of a public 
body guess what will occur during a meeting, but the chair does need to list those topics that he 
or she reasonably anticipates will be discussed. See OML 2013-61; OML 2013-22; OML 2011-
36; OML 2011-23; OML 2011-13. 
Here, the Chair did not anticipate a discussion on the topic of the Police Chief because he 
specifically denied a request to add the topic to the meeting's agenda. The Chair could not have 
reasonably anticipated that a different selectman would raise the topic during the "Selectman's 
Statements" portion of the meeting. Furthermore, that the Chair did not participate in the 
discussion further suggests that he was not trying to frustrate the notice requirement by leaving a 
topic off the notice that he actually intended to discuss. Accordingly, we find that the Board did 
not violate the Open Meeting Law by discussing a topic unanticipated by the Chair.2 See OML 
2011-13. 
While a public body may consider a topic at a meeting that was not listed in the notice if it was 
not reasonably anticipated at least 48 hours before the meeting, we strongly encourage public 
bodies to postpone discussion of topics that were not listed in the meeting notice until the topic 
may be included in a meeting notice, unless the circumstances demand immediate action by the 
public body. See OML 2011-23. 
CONCLUSION 
For the reasons stated above, we find that the Board did not violate the Open Meeting 
Law. 
We now consider this matter closed. Please be advised that closing this complaint does 
not resolve any other complaints that are currently pending with our office, the Town, or the 
Board. Please feel free to contact our office at 617-963-2540 if you have any questions 
regarding this letter.
