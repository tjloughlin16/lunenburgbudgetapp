RE: Open Meeting Law Complaint 
Dear Attorney Riley: 
This office received two complaints from Gary Allard, dated January 4, 2014, alleging 
that the Sturbridge Board of Health (the "Board") violated the Open Meeting Law, G.L. c. 30A, 
§§ 18-25. The first complaint alleges that the Board failed to properly notice its October 7, 2013 
meeting and failed to provide Mr. Allard with minutes and a video recording of that meeting 
following his request. The complaint was filed with the Board on or about October 25, 2013. 
The second complaint alleges that the Board has failed to approve any of its meeting minutes 
since December 17, 2012. This complaint was filed with the Board on or about October 31, 
2013. The Board responded to both complaints in a letter from counsel dated November 6, 2013. 
Following our review, we find that the Board properly noticed its October 7, 2013 
meeting. We are unable to resolve the allegation that the Board failed to provide Mr. Allard with 
the minutes of its October 7, 2013 meeting within 10 days of his specific request for those 
minutes. We find that the Board failed to approve many of its 2013 meeting minutes in a timely 
manner. Finally, we find that the Board violated the Open Meeting Law by failing to respond to 
Mr. Allard's request for 10 months of meeting minutes. In reaching this determination, we 
reviewed the October 25, 2013 and October 31, 2013 complaints filed with the Board; the

FACTS 
The Board posted notice for its Monday, October 7, 2013 meeting on Thursday, October 
3, 2013. That notice included several topics for discussion. The notice did not include a topic 
regarding Mr. Allard or his property. 
On October 7, 2013, the Board received a memorandum from the Office of the 
Conservation Commission, stating that the Commission had discussed health and safety concerns 
regarding the culvert obstruction on Mr. Allard's property during the Commission's Thursday, 
October 3, 2013 meeting. On October 7, 2013, the Board also received a complaint from an 
abutter to Mr. Allard's property regarding the status of a beaver dam and the surrounding area. 
In light of these letters, during the Board's October 7, 2013 meeting, the Board discussed Mr. 
Allard's property. Following this discussion, the Board voted to renew its previous order issued 
to Mr. Allard on March 21, 2012. 
The Board approved the minutes of its October 7, 2013 meeting on November 4, 2013. 
Mr. Allard states that he requested the Board's October 7, 2013 minutes when he visited the 
Public Health Office on October 21, 2013. The Board denies that it received this specific 
request. Mr. Allard further states that on October 21, 2013, he requested all of the Board's 
meeting minutes from the past 10 months. The Board claims that when the Public Health Agent 
asked him to identify the meeting dates he wanted, he refused to respond. The Board writes in 
its November 6, 2013 response to the complaint that Mr. Allard "declined to make a clear 
request." 
For its meetings that took place between December 2012 and October 2013, the Board 
took an average of 133 days to approve its meeting minutes. The minutes for the Board meetings 
held between July 2013 and September 2013 remained unapproved until May 22, 2014. 
DISCUSSION 
1. The Board did Not Violate the Open Meeting Law Because the Chair did not Reasonably 
Anticipate Discussing Mr. Allard's Property more than 48 Hours Prior to its October 7, 
2013 Meeting. 
The Open Meeting Law requires that public bodies post notice 48 hours in advance of a 
meeting, not including Saturdays, Sundays, and legal holidays, and include, a "listing of topics 
that the chair reasonably anticipates will be discussed at the meeting." G.L. c. 30A, § 20(b).

Public bodies are required to list topics in a meeting notice with "sufficient specificity to 
reasonably advise the public of the issues to be discussed at the meeting." 940 CMR 
29.03(l)(b); OML 2012-34.3 
The Board posted notice for its Monday, October 7, 2013 meeting on Thursday, October 
3, 2013. After the notice was posted, but before the meeting occurred, the Board received 
correspondence regarding Mr. Allard's property. Because the Board chair did not reasonably 
anticipate discussing these matters 48 hours before the meeting, it was not a violation of the law 
for the Board to discuss Mr. Allard's property, even though it was not listed in the meeting 
notice. See OML 2013-39. Although a public body may consider an unanticipated topic that 
was not listed in the meeting notice, the Attorney General strongly encourages public bodies to 
postpone discussion and action on topics that are controversial or may be of particular interest to 
the public if those topics were not listed in the meeting notice. See OML 2012-19.4 Finally, we 
note that the Open Meeting Law does not require notice to an individual that a public body may 
discuss a topic involving that individual, unless the public body intends to enter executive 
session under G.L. c. 30A, § 21(a)(1). OML 2013-41; OML 2013-24. 
2. The Board Failed to Approve Minutes in a Timely Manner and Failed to Provide Mr. 
Allard with the Meeting Minutes he Requested. 
The Board is required to "create and maintain accurate minutes of all meetings, including 
executive sessions, setting forth the date, time, and place, the members present or absent, a 
summary of the discussions on each subject, a list of documents and other exhibits used at the 
meetings, the decisions made and the actions taken at each meeting, including the record of all 
votes." G.L. c. 30A, § 22(a). The "[mjinutes of all open sessions shall be created and approved 
in a timely manner" and "shall be made available upon request by any person within 10 days." 
G.L. c. 30A, § 22(c). The Law does not provide a definition of "timely manner," but we 
recommend that minutes be approved at the next meeting, whenever possible. See OML 2013-
122; OML 2012-91. 
Mr. Allard states that he requested the Board's October 7, 2013 minutes when he visited 
the Board office on October 21, 2013. The Board denies that it received any such request. 
Because Mr. Allard did not make his request in writing, we cannot verify whether the Board 
received his request, thus we cannot resolve this allegation. We remind the Board that it must 
provide minutes of an open meeting within 10 days of a request, whether in draft or final form. 
G.L. c. 30A, § 22(c). 
Mr. Allard's complaint also alleges that the Board failed to approve minutes in the 
period between December 17, 2012 and October 21, 2013. His basis for the allegation is that the

Board did not provide him with minutes from that time period in response to his request, nor has 
the Board posted all the minutes from that time period to its website. The Board denies this 
allegation, writing in response to the complaint that "[w]hile the Board's minutes that are posted 
to the Town's website are not up to date, there are numerous minutes that have been approved by 
the Board since December and are available to the public." However, the accounting provided 
by the Board after a request from this office demonstrates that few of its meeting minutes from 
that period were approved in a timely manner. On average, the Board took 133 days to approve 
its meeting minutes. The minutes for six meetings from 2013 remained unapproved until May 
22, 2014. We therefore find that the Board failed to approve meeting minutes in a timely 
manner. 
With regard to Mr. Allard's request for these minutes, the Board states that when Mr. 
Allard visited the Public Health Office on October 21, 2013, the Public Health Agent asked him 
to identify the meetings dates he wanted, but he refused to respond. The Board writes in its 
November 6, 2013 response to the complaint that Mr. Allard "declined to make a clear request." 
Again, because Mr. Allard did not make his request in writing, we cannot verify the exact nature 
of his request. However, we credit Mr. Allard's statement to our office that he requested minutes 
from the past 10 months of meetings, as of October 31, 2013. That request encompasses 
approximately 20 sets of meeting minutes. Mr. Allard is not required to specify the dates of the 
meetings, so long as the date range he requested is clear. The Board should, therefore, have 
provided Mr. Allard with the minutes of the Board's past 10 months of meetings within 10 days 
of his October 21, 2013 request. G.L. c. 30A, § 22(c); OML 2013-18. 
We find lhal the Board failed to provide Mr. Allard meeting minutes within 10 days of 
his request, and failed to approve meeting minutes in a timely manner. We order the Board's 
immediate and future compliance with the Open Meeting Law. We understand that as of the 
Board's May 22, 2014 meeting, the Board has approved the outstanding minutes of its 2013 
meetings. If it has not done so already, the Board should provide Mr. Allard with copies of all of 
the minutes he requested, as identified in this letter. 
We now consider this matter closed. If you have any questions regarding this letter, 
please do not hesitate to contact our office at (617) 963-2540.
