RE: Open Meeting Law Complaint 
Dear Attorney Mead: 
This office received a complaint from Stephen Morgan on September 28, alleging that 
the Ashland Board of Selectmen (the "Board") violated the Open Meeting Law, G.L. c. 3OA, 
§§ 18-25.' The complaint was originally filed with the Board on or about August 16, and you 
responded, on behalf of the Board, by letter dated September 12.2 In his complaint, Mr. 
Morgan alleges that the Board failed to timely review and release executive session minutes 
for which the purpose for secrecy had expired. 
Following our review, we resolve this matter by informal action, in accordance with 
940 CMR 29.07(2)(a), and find that the Board did not violate the Open Meeting Law. 
The complaint concerns the Board's failure to timely review and release the executive 
session minutes from the following meetings: April 6; April 20; May 4; May 11; May 16; 
and July 12. At the time of its response, the Board asserted that the purposes for the executive 
sessions were ongoing and thus, the minutes at issue remained confidential. The executive 
sessions concerned negotiations relating to collective bargaining contracts and the purchase of 
real property, as well as an ongoing litigation matter. Executive session minutes may be 
withheld from disclosure to the public "as long as publication may defeat the lawful purposes 
of the executive session, but no longer." G.L. c. 3 OA, § 22(f). When the purpose for a valid 
executive session has been served, the minutes and any documents or exhibits used at the 
session must be disclosed, unless the attorney-client privilege or an exemption to the Public 
Records Law applies. Id. The burden of justifying continued nondisclosure of executive

Because the executive session minutes concerned ongoing matters at the time this 
complaint was filed, we find that the Board did not violate the Open Meeting Law by 
withholding them from public disclosure. See G.L. c. 30A, § 22(f); OML 2017-53; OML 
2014-28.3 Further, we note that the Board has since reviewed and released the executive 
session minutes to the public on a rolling basis.4 We commend the Board for its attention to 
the law's requirement that executive session minutes must be periodically reviewed and then 
released upon the expiration of the executive session purpose. 
We now consider the complaint addressed by this determination to be resolved.
