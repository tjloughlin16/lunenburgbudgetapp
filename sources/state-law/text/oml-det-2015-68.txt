RE: Open Meeting Law Complaint 
Dear Mr. Ference: 
This office received a complaint from Mr. Brian Johnson, dated March 20, 2015, alleging 
that the Holland Board of Health (the "Board") violated the Open Meeting Law, G.L. c. 30A, §§ 
18-25. The complaint was originally filed with the Board on January 24, 2015, and the Board 
responded to the original complaint by letter dated February 4, 2015.1 In his complaint, Mr. 
Johnson alleges that the Board deliberated outside of an open meeting. 
Following our review, we fmd that the Board did not violate the Open Meeting Law. In 
reaching a determination, we reviewed the complaint; the Board's response; and the request for 
further review filed with our office. We also reviewed several documents provided to us by 
Board member David Kowalski. 
We fmd the facts as follows. The Board is a three-member public body. This matter 
arises from two variances sought by Mr. Johnson for projects on his property. On October 31, 
2014, the Holland Zoning Board of Appeals (the "ZBA") sent a memorandum to several other 
town bodies, including the Board, seeking "questions, comments, or recommendation[s]" 
regarding Mr. Johnson's projects. On November 28, 2014, Board member Dana Manning sent 
an e-mail to the ZBA. This e-mail, which was not sent to any person or entity other than the 
ZBA, states several objections to Mr. Johnson's application. The e-mail is signed, "Dana 
Manning, Board of Health."

The ZBA held a public hearing on December 23, 2014 concerning Mr. Johnson's request 
for the variances. Ms. Manning attended this meeting in case questions were asked concerning 
her e-mail, but ultimately she did not speak. The December 23, 2014 hearing was continued 
until January 13, 2015. During the January 13, 2015 public hearing, a ZBA member made 
reference to Ms. Manning's November 28, 2014 e-mail. This was the first time Mr. Johnson 
learned that the e-mail had been sent. To date, Mr. Johnson has not received a final 
determination on his variance requests. 
The Open Meeting Law requires that all meetings of a public body be properly noticed 
and open to members of the public, unless an executive sessions is convened. See G.L. c. 3 OA, 
§§ 20(a)-(b), 21. A "meeting" is defined, in relevant part, as "a deliberation by a public body 
with respect to any matter within the body's jurisdiction." G.L. c. 30A, § 18. The law defines 
"deliberation" as "an oral or written communication through any medium, including electronic 
mail, between or among a quorum of a public body on any public business within its 
jurisdiction." Id 
Here, the complaint alleges that Ms. Manning's November 28, 2014 e-mail violated the 
Open Meeting Law. Ms. Manning stated to our office that she prepared the e-mail without input 
from her fellow Board members. Even though she signed the e-mail, "Dana Manning, Board of 
Health," she did not send the e-mail on behalf of the entire Board. Rather, Ms. Manning was 
voicing her individual opinions. Indeed, Ms. Manning used the first person throughout her e-
mail. In the absence of any evidence to the contrary, we credit Ms. Manning's statement that her 
email did not reflect discussion between or among a quorum of the Board members, and we find 
that the Board did not deliberate outside of a meeting and therefore did not violate the Open 
Meeting Law. See G.L. c. 30A, § 18; OML 2013-133; OML 2011-40; OML 2011-52. 
For the reasons stated above, we find that the Board did not violate the Open Meeting 
Law. We now consider the complaint addressed by this determination to be resolved. This 
determination does not address any other complaints that may be pending with our office or the 
Board. Please feel free to contact the Division at (617) 963 - 2540 if you have any questions.
