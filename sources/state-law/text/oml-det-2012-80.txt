RE: Open Meeting Law Complaint — Medford Housing Authority 
Dear Attorney Driscoll: 
The Attorney General's Office received a complaint from Bonnie Curran dated March 
12, 2012, alleging that the Medford Housing Authority Board of Commissioners (the "Board") 
violated the Open Meeting Law, G.L. c. 30A, §§ 18-25. Specifically, the complaint alleges that 
on June 15, 2011, "there were written communication[s] between [Authority Director Robert 
Covelle and Board members Michael Luongo and Sylviajean Baumeister] involving a matter 
within the jurisdiction of the Medford Housing Authority." The complaint was filed with the 
Board on or about February 2, 2012, and the Board responded by letter dated February 20, 2012. 
After reviewing the complaint and the Board's response, we have decided to resolve this 
complaint by informal action,  in accordance with 940 CMR 29.07(2)(a). We find that the 
Board did not violate the Open Meeting Law because the email at issue did not constitute 
deliberation outside of an open meeting. 
The Open Meeting Law requires that any deliberation between and among Board 
members take place during a properly noticed open meeting. G.L. c. 30A, §§ 18, 20. 
"Deliberation" is defined as "an oral or written communication through any medium, including 
electronic mail, between or among a quorum of a public body on any public business within its 
jurisdiction." G.L. c. 30A, § 18. "Quorum" is defined as "a simple majority of the members of 
the public body, unless otherwise provided in a general or special law, executive order or other 
authorizing provision." Id. 
The Board is a five-member public body, thus three members constitute a quorum. 
Authority Director Robert Covelle, who is not a member of the Board, sent an email on June 15, 
===PAGE 2===
2011 to two Board members regarding Ms. Curran, an employee of the Authority. Because this 
email did not involve communication "between or among a quorum of a public body," it did not 
violate the Open Meeting Law. See OML 2011-40. 1  
Furthermore, in reviewing this complaint, we requested from the Authority copies of any 
and all written correspondence, including email, that was sent or received by members of the 
Board between June 1, 2011 and June 30, 2011 and related to Ms. Curran. The above-mentioned 
email was the only correspondence sent in response to this request, and the Board's February 20, 
2012 letter to our office asserts that no deliberation by a quorum of the Board occurred on this 
topic. We have not been presented with any evidence to the contrary. 
For the reasons stated above, we find no violation of the Open Meeting Law and we now 
consider this matter closed. This determination does not address any other complaints which 
may be pending with our office or the Authority. Please feel free to contact me if you have any 
questions or believe any facts in this letter to be inaccurate.
