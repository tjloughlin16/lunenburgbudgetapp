 RE: Open Meeting Law Complaint 
 
Dear Ms. Pacheco: 
 
This office received a complaint from Patrick Higgins on March 2, 2023, alleging that the 
Dartmouth Board of Assessors (the “Board”) violated the Open Meeting Law, G.L. c. 30A, §§ 
18-25. The complaint was originally filed with the Board on February 9, 2023, and Board Chair 
Robert Michaud responded to the complaint, on behalf of the Board, by letter dated February 17, 
2023.
1 2 The complaint alleges that the Board failed to create accurate minutes of the meeting 
held on January 19. 
 
We resolve this matter by informal action, in accordance with 940 CMR 29.07(2)(a), 
after reviewing the original complaint, the Board’s response to the complaint, the complaint filed 
with our office requesting further review, and the notice and minutes of the Board meeting held 
on January 19.  We find that the Board violated the Open Meeting Law in the way alleged. 
 
The Open Meeting Law requires that a public body “create and maintain accurate 
minutes of all meetings, including executive sessions, setting forth the date, time and place, the 
members present or absent, a summary of the discussions on each subject, a list of documents

The complaint alleges that the January 19 minutes do not include a list of documents and 
other exhibits used at the meeting, the location of the meeting, a record of all votes, or the 
purpose for convening in executive session. The Board admits, and we agree, that this 
information was missing from the minutes. The Board asserts that all required information will 
be included in minutes “going forward” and we commend the Board’s commitment to ensure 
future compliance. Nonetheless, we find that the Board violated the Open Meeting Law by 
failing to create sufficient and accurate minutes of the meeting held on January 19. 
 
We remind the Board that any documents that are physically present, verbally identified, 
and discussed during a meeting must be separately listed in the minutes. See OML 2019-163; 
OML 2014-12; OML 2012-42. It is not enough that those documents are simply referenced in the 
body of the minutes or that the minutes include a digital link to the documents. See OML 2019-
29; OML 2018-70. Further, prior to entering executive session, a public body must convene in 
open session. G.L. c. 30A, § 22(b)(1). A majority of members of the body must vote to go into 
executive session and the vote of each member must be recorded by roll call and entered into the 
minutes; the chair must state the purpose for the executive session, stating all subjects that may 
be revealed without compromising the purpose for which the executive session was called; and 
the chair must state whether the open session will reconvene at the conclusion of the executive 
session. G.L. c. 30A, §§ 22(b)(2)-(4).  
 
For the reasons stated above, we find that the Board violated the Open Meeting Law by 
creating insufficient minutes of the meeting held on January 19. We order the Board to amend 
the minutes of the January 19 in compliance with the guidance provided above and to certify to 
our office within 60 days that it has done so. See 940 C.M.R. 29.07(4). We also order the 
Board’s immediate and future compliance with the law’s requirements, and we caution that 
similar future violations could be considered evidence of intent to violate the law.
