 RE: Open Meeting Law Complaints 
 
Dear Attorney Castro: 
 
This office received a complaint from Stephen Morgan on June 28, 2021, and a second 
complaint on July 26, 2021, alleging that the Ashland Select Board (the “Board”) violated the 
Open Meeting Law, G.L. c. 30A, §§ 18-25.  The first complaint was originally filed with the 
Board on or about February 2, 2021, the second complaint was originally filed with the Board on 
or about June 28, 2021, and you responded to both complaints, on behalf of the Board, by 
separate letters dated July 26, 2021.  The first complaint alleges that the Board failed to timely 
approve and provide minutes of meetings held between the years 2016 to 2020.  The second 
complaint alleges that the Board failed to timely respond to the first Open Meeting Law 
complaint.  
 
We resolve these matters by informal action, in accordance with 940 CMR 29.07(2)(a), 
after reviewing the original complaint, the Board’s response to the complaint, and the complaint 
filed with our office requesting further review.  In addition, we communicated with the 
complainant on July 22, 2021, and with you on February 10, 2022.  We find that the Board 
violated the Open Meeting Law by failing to timely approve minutes for two meetings held in 
2020 and failing to respond to an Open Meeting Law complaint within the timeline established 
in the law.  We decline to review the remaining allegations relating to the timely approval of 
minutes from the years 2016 to 2019 for the reasons discussed below.   
 
The Open Meeting Law requires that a public body “create and maintain accurate 
minutes of all meetings, including executive sessions, setting forth the date, time and place, the 
members present or absent, a summary of the discussions on each subject, a list of documents

and other exhibits used at the meeting, the decisions made and the actions taken at each meeting, 
including the record of all votes.”  G.L. c. 30A, § 22(a).  All open and executive session meeting 
minutes must be reviewed and approved in a timely manner.  G.L. c. 30A, § 22(c).  “Timely 
manner” means “within the next three public body meetings or within 30 days, whichever is 
later, unless the public body can show good cause for further delay.”  See 940 CMR 29.11; OML 
2018-48.1  Whenever possible, we recommend that minutes of a meeting be approved at the next 
meeting.  See OML 2018-67; OML 2017-133.  
 
Executive session minutes may be withheld from disclosure to the public “as long as 
publication may defeat the lawful purposes of the executive session, but no longer.”  G.L. c. 
30A, § 22(f).  When the purpose for a valid executive session has been served, the minutes and 
any documents or exhibits used at the session must be disclosed unless the attorney-client 
privilege or an exemption to the public records law applies to withhold them, in whole or in part, 
from disclosure.  See id.  Public bodies have an obligation to review the minutes of executive 
sessions at reasonable intervals to determine if continued non-disclosure of minutes is warranted, 
and to announce that determination at the next meeting following its review.  See G.L. c. 30A, § 
22(g)(1); OML 2015-94; OML 2013-56.  Although the Open Meeting Law does not define 
“reasonable intervals,” we have found that quarterly reviews or reviews every six months satisfy 
the law.  See OML 2017-104; OML 2015-166; OML 2013-180.   
 
The first complaint alleges that the Board failed to timely approve and provide 67 sets of 
minutes for meetings held between January 14, 2016, and November 18, 2020.  We decline to 
review whether all 67 sets of meeting minutes have been timely approved as our office will not 
conduct broad audits of public bodies based on generalized allegations.  See OML 2013-180; 
OML 2012-106.  Nonetheless, we note the Board has posted—and therefore presumably 
approved—the majority of the relevant meeting minutes to its website.  In fact, for the year 2016, 
the Board has failed to post only nine sets of open session meeting minutes and one set of 
executive session meeting minutes out of 39 meetings; for the year 2017, the Board has failed to 
post only two sets of open session meeting minutes and one set of executive session meeting 
minutes out of 34 meetings;2 for the year 2018, the Board has failed to post only two sets of open 
session meeting minutes and one set of executive session meeting minutes out of 35 meetings; 
for the year 2019, the Board has failed to post only four sets of executive session meeting 
minutes out of 35 meetings; and for the year, 2020, the Board has failed to post only one set of 
open session meeting minutes and one set of executive session meeting minutes out of 35 
meetings.   
 
Moreover, the Open Meeting Law does not require a public body to post minutes to a 
website, but rather to provide meeting minutes upon request within 10 days, if the minutes exist.  
See G.L. c. 30A, § 22(c).  Therefore, the lack of minutes posted to a website for a given meeting 
does not necessarily indicate that the minutes were not timely approved.  See OML 2022-16; 
OML 2020-19; OML 2018-107.  However, we remind the Board of its obligations under the

Open Meeting Law to create and approve meeting minutes, whether for open or executive 
session, in a timely manner.  G.L. c. 30A, § 22(c).  We encourage the Board to post to the 
municipal website those open session minutes that are missing for the years 2016 to 2019, and to 
post any executive session minutes when publication would no longer defeat the lawful purpose 
for the executive session.    
 
Although we decline to formally review precisely when the Board approved all 67 sets of 
meeting minutes at issue and whether the approval of each was timely, we have reviewed this 
issue with respect to the minutes of the 2020 meetings that are not posted to the Town website.  
We find that the Board failed to timely approve minutes of the June 29, 2020, and December 9, 
2020, meetings, as the minutes were not approved within the later of the next three meetings or 
30 days following each meeting.  See OML 2022-16; OML 2021-155.  As such, we find that the 
Board violated the Open Meeting Law by failing to approve these minutes in a timely manner. 
 
Finally, the Board has assured our office that it reviews executive session minutes at 
regular intervals to determine whether publication would defeat the lawful purpose for the 
executive session.  Specifically, the Board states that minutes pertaining to pending litigation are 
reviewed every other week, while executive session minutes involving other issues are reviewed 
quarterly to determine whether continued non-disclosure is warranted.    
 
The second complaint concerns the Board’s failure to respond to the first Open Meeting 
Law complaint in the timeframe required by the law.  The Open Meeting Law requires that 
within 14 days of receipt of a complaint, the public body review the complaint’s allegations; take 
remedial action, if appropriate; and send to the Attorney General a copy of the complaint and a 
description of any remedial action taken.  G.L. c. 30A, § 23(b); 940 CMR 29.05(5).  A public 
body must meet to review the complaint and formulate a response, or meet to delegate that 
authority, and respond to the complaint within 14 business days.  See G.L. c. 30A, § 23(b); OML 
2017-69; OML 2012-90.  
 
Here, the Board admits that the complaint was “overlooked” and therefore it did not 
respond until approximately six months after the complaint was filed.  Because the Board failed 
to respond to an Open Meeting Law complaint within 14 business days, we find that it violated 
the Open Meeting Law.    
 
For the reasons stated above, we find that the Board violated the Open Meeting Law by 
failing to timely approve meeting minutes from 2020 and failing to timely respond to an Open 
Meeting Law complaint.  We order immediate and future compliance with the law’s 
requirements, and we caution that similar future violations could be considered evidence of 
intent to violate the law.   
 
We now consider the complaints addressed by this determination to be resolved.  This 
determination does not address any other complaints that may be pending with our office or the 
Board.  Please feel free to contact our office at (617) 963-2540 if you have any questions 
regarding this letter.
