RE: Open Meeting Law Complaint  
 
Dear Chair Chafee:  
 
This office received two complaints from Bruce Clarke on October 24, 2025,1 alleging 
that the Brookfield Select Board (the “Board”) violated the Open Meeting Law, G.L. c. 30A, §§ 
18-25. Mr. Clarke originally filed the complaints with the Board on September 26, and you 
responded on behalf of the Board via email on October 15. The first complaint alleges that notice 
of a meeting the Board held on September 16 was not sufficiently specific. The second complaint 
alleges that the Board made disparaging remarks about a member of the Town Hall Improvement 
Committee (“the Committee”) at the September 16 meeting. 
 
Following our review, we find the Board did not violate the Open Meeting Law as 
alleged in the first complaint. We decline to review the allegations raised in the second 
complaint. In reaching this determination, we reviewed the original complaints relating to the 
September 16 meeting, the Board’s response to the complaints, the complainant’s request for 
further review, and subsequent email communications sent to our office. We also reviewed the 
meeting notice and minutes for the September 16 meeting, and a video recording of the meeting.

 We find the facts as follows. On  July 22, the Board posted notice of a meeting to be held 
on September 16. The meeting notice, as amended on September 11, included the topic “Town 
Hall Improvement Resignations: Al Jones; William Simpson; Cathy Larocca.” The meeting took 
place as scheduled with Board Chair Richard Chaffee, Board member Beth Coughlin and Board 
member Sarah Campbell in attendance.  
 
Approximately forty-two minutes into the September 16 meeting, the Board reached the 
topic of the resignations from the Committee. The Board accepted the resignations of Al Jones, 
William Simpson, and Cathy Larocca. Board member Coughlin acknowledged the Committee’s 
work, commenting that it improved the town hall within budget constraints, maintained good 
communication and negotiated priorities collaboratively. A discussion followed on these points, 
with Chair Chaffee arguing that the Committee did not adequately follow its charge and did not 
properly report to the Board. Chair Chaffee expressed concern about the Committee’s priorities 
and the condition of the windows in the town hall. Chair Chaffee was specifically critical of 
Committee member Al Jones. 
 
DISCUSSION 
 
I. Notice of the September 16 meeting was sufficiently specific. 
 
 A public body must post notice of every me eting at least 48 hours in advance, not 
including Saturdays, Sundays, and legal holidays. G.L. c. 30A, § 20(b). Notices must include “a 
listing of topics that the chair reasonably anticipates will be discussed at the meeting.” Id. The 
list of topics must have sufficient specificity to reasonably advise the public of the issues to be 
discussed at the meeting. 940 CMR 29.03(l)(b). 
 
We generally consider a topic to be sufficiently specific when a reasonable member of 
the public could read the topic and understand the anticipated nature of the public body’s 
discussion. See OML 2023-199; OML 2019-134; OML 2015-35.3 The Open Meeting Law 
requires that the notice describe topics with sufficient specificity so that the public can make an 
informed decision whether to attend the meeting to observe the discussion regarding a topic of 
interest. See OML 2023-86; OML 2019-95; OML 2018-22. 
 
The chair of a public body is not required to anticipate the course of deliberations, and 
the Open Meeting Law does not require the meeting notice to list all tangential issues related to a 
particular topic. See OML 2021-39; OML 2019-102; OML 2016-120. The Open Meeting Law 
does not require the chair to guess what will occur at a meeting. See OML 2024-262; OML 
2020-55; OML 2018-134. Where a public body’s discussion naturally flows from its 
consideration of the posted agenda topic, the notice is sufficient to comply with the requirements 
of the Open Meeting Law. See OML 2021-39; OML 2019-70; OML 2018-14.  
 
We find that the Board’s discussion of the accomplishments and shortcomings of the 
Committee and its members naturally flowed from a topic listed on the meeting notice, to wit,

acceptance of resignations from the Committee. In response to the resignations, Member 
Coughlin took a moment to recognize the Committee’s accomplishments. Chair Chaffee then 
responded to Member Coughlin’s comments by questioning the priorities, transparency, and 
effectiveness of the Committee, and of one Committee member in particular. The recognition of 
Committee members’ contributions, the criticism, and the broader discussion about improvement 
projects within the Committee’s charge naturally flowed from the noticed topic. Therefore, we 
find no violation with respect to the allegation that the notice for the Board’s September 16 
meeting was insufficiently specific.  
 
II. We decline to review allegations that the Chair made disparaging remarks about 
Al Jones. 
  
The Division of Open Government’s review concerns compliance with the Open Meeting 
Law, G.L. c. 30A, §§ 18-25, only. The second complaint alleges that Chair Chaffee engaged in a 
“personal, demoralizing, verbal attack” on resigning Committee member Al Jones during the 
Board’s September 16 meeting. While the Division discourages uncivil and unprofessional 
behavior, an allegation of unprofessional conduct does not constitute a violation of the Open 
Meeting Law. See OML 2024-88; OML 2020-36; OML 2019-166; OML Declination 6-28-16 
(Kingston Board of Selectmen). Moreover, the Open Meeting Law generally does not govern the 
content of a public body’s discussions. See OML 2018-43, n. 3; OML 2020-167. Because an 
allegation of a personal attack, even if true, would not constitute a violation of the Open Meeting 
Law, we decline to review it.4

CONCLUSION 
 
For the reasons stated above, we find that the Board did not violate the Open Meeting 
Law as alleged. We now consider the complaints addressed by this determination to be resolved. 
This determination does not address any other complaints that may be pending with our office or 
the Board. Please feel free to contact the Division at (617) 963-2540 if you have any questions.
