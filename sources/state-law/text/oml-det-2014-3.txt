RE: Open Meeting Law Complaint 
Dear Mr. Turner: 
This office received two complaints alleging that the Egremont Board of Selectmen (the 
"Board") violated the Open Meeting Law, G.L. c. 30A, §§ 18-25. The first complaint was filed 
by William Weigle, dated September 4, 2013, and the second complaint was filed by Kevin 
Zurrin, dated September 18, 2013. Specifically, the complaints allege that during the Board's 
July 22, 2013 meeting, the Board members "spoke to each other in low voices so that many of 
the attendees could not hear." Additionally, the complaints allege that the Board failed to 
provide a large enough meeting room to accommodate the overflow crowd. Mr. Weigle's 
complaint was filed with the Board on or about August 19, 2013, and the Board responded to the 
complaint by letter dated August 26, 2013. Mr. Zurrin's complaint was filed with the Board on 
or about August 20, 2013, and the Board responded by letter dated August 26, 2013. 
Following our review, we find that the Board did not violate the Open Meeting Law. In 
reaching this determination, we reviewed the August 19, 2013 and August 20, 2013 complaints 
filed with the Board; the Board's August 26, 2013 responses to the two complaints; and the 
September 4, 2013 and September 18, 2013 complaints filed with our office. Additionally, we 
reviewed the minutes and an audio recording, provided by the Board, of the Board's July 22, 
2013 meeting. Finally, we reviewed a video recording of the Board's July 22, 2012 meeting, 
provided by Mr. Weigle.

FACTS 
We find the facts to be as follows. The Board convened a special meeting on July 22, 
2013 to consider the employment status of the Town's Police Chief. Approximately 60 residents 
attended the meeting which was, at times, contentious. During the meeting, there was one 
microphone placed at the center of the head table where the three Board members were seated. 
At several points during the meeting, members of the public requested that Board members 
speak up or speak into the microphone. Because of the poor quality of the recordings, it is not 
clear from the audio or video whether the Board members spoke loudly enough for everyone in 
the room to hear. However, it does not appear from the recordings that the Board members 
intentionally tried to speak softly or keep the conversation out of earshot of the audience. 
At one point during the meeting, several audience members expressed anger at the Board 
because they were not able to hear the discussion between the Board members and the Police 
Chief and the Police Chiefs attorney. The Board members did repeat themselves, and explained 
to the audience what was happening when audience members requested clarification. At times, 
the noise coming from the audience drowned out the voices of the Board members. 
The video shows a crowded room with many people standing. It also shows folded chairs 
in the comer. While there are people standing at the edge of the room, the video does not show 
anyone outside the room. 
DISCUSSION 
The Open Meeting Law requires that meetings of a public body be open and accessible to 
the public. See G.L. c. 30A, § 20(a); OML 2012-49.1 Access must include the opportunity to be 
physically present as well as to see and hear what is being discussed by the members of the 
public body. OML 2013-189, OML 2012-66; OML 2012-48. 
Because of the placement and poor quality of the recording devices, we are unable to 
ascertain how loud or clear the Board members were when they spoke during the Board's July 
22, 2013 meeting. However, the audio and video recordings do not demonstrate that the Board 
intentionally conducted its deliberations in an inaudible manner, as the complaints contend. 
Indeed, Board members even repeated themselves at times so that attendees could hear. We 
therefore find that the Board did not violate the Open Meeting Law by intentionally speaking 
inaudibly. 
With regard to the complainant's second allegation, that the meeting room was not 
accessible because it could not accommodate the crowd that wished to attend the July 22, 2013 
meeting, we do not find sufficient evidence to support finding a violation of the Open Meeting 
Law. The video recording shows a crowded room with many attendees standing around the 
edges, but does not clearly demonstrate that the meeting was inaccessible to members of the 
public. Although we do not find a violation, we remind the Board that public bodies must make 
reasonable accommodations for such occurrences as overflow crowds, and ensure that meetings 
are held at times and in places that do not discourage public attendance. OML 2012-66. We
