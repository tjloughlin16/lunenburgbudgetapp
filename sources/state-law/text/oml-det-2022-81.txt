RE: Open Meeting Law Complaint 
Dear Attorney Collins: 
This office received a complaint from Robyn Chrabascz on December 29, 2021, alleging 
that the Wales Select Board (the “Board”) violated the Open Meeting Law, G.L. c. 30A, §§ 18-
25. The complaint was originally filed with the Board on or about November 23, 2021, and you
responded to the complaint, on behalf of the Board, by letter dated December 14, 2021.  The
complaint alleges that the Board failed to timely approve and post minutes of meetings held
between the years 2019 to 2021.
We resolve this matter by informal action, in accordance with 940 CMR 29.07(2)(a), 
after reviewing the original complaint, the Board’s response to the complaint, and the complaint 
filed with our office requesting further review.  For the reasons discussed below, we decline to 
review precisely when the Board approved minutes of meetings held during the years 2019 to 
2021 but find that the Board violated the Open Meeting Law by generally failing to timely 
approve meeting minutes in 2021.   
The Open Meeting Law requires that a public body “create and maintain accurate 
minutes of all meetings, including executive sessions, setting forth the date, time and place, the 
members present or absent, a summary of the discussions on each subject, a list of documents 
and other exhibits used at the meeting, the decisions made and the actions taken at each meeting, 
including the record of all votes.”  G.L. c. 30A, § 22(a).  All open and executive session meeting 
minutes must be reviewed and approved in a timely manner.  G.L. c. 30A, § 22(c).  “Timely 
manner” means “within the next three public body meetings or within 30 days, whichever is

later, unless the public body can show good cause for further delay.”  See 940 CMR 29.11; OML 
2018-48.1  Whenever possible, we recommend that minutes of a meeting be approved at the next 
meeting.  See OML 2018-67; OML 2017-133.  
 
The complaint alleges that the Board failed to timely approve and post minutes for 
meetings held during the years 2019 to 2021.  The Open Meeting Law does not require a public 
body to post minutes to a website, but rather to provide meeting minutes upon request within 10 
days, if the minutes exist.2  See G.L. c. 30A, § 22(c).  Therefore, the lack of minutes posted to a 
website for a given meeting does not necessarily indicate that the minutes were not timely 
approved.  See OML 2022-16; OML 2020-19; OML 2018-107.  However, we note that the 
Board has not posted the majority of the relevant meeting minutes to its website.  In fact, for the 
year 2019, the Board held 32 meetings and has posted 22 sets of meeting minutes; for the year 
2020, the Board held 30 meetings and has only posted 14 sets of meeting minutes; and for the 
year 2021, the Board held 25 meetings and has only posted 11 sets of meeting minutes.  We note 
that the Board has acknowledged the backlog of meeting minutes and has hired additional staff 
to assist with the creation of minutes.    
 
We decline to review precisely when the Board approved meeting minutes from all three 
years and whether approval of each set of minutes was timely as our office will not conduct 
broad audits of public bodies based on generalized allegations.  See OML 2013-180; OML 2012-
106.  However, we have reviewed the minutes posted to the website for meetings held in 2021 
and find that during March 22, 2021, and April 12, 2021, meetings, the Board approved minutes 
of meetings held in April, May and September of 2020; and during January 11, 2021, and a 
January 26, 2021, meetings, the Board approved minutes of meetings held in July, September 
and October of 2019.  During the other seven meetings for which minutes are posted, the Board 
did not approve any meeting minutes.  Because these minutes show that minutes of numerous 
prior meetings were not approved within the later of the next three meetings or 30 days following 
each meeting, we find that the Board violated the Open Meeting Law by failing to approve 
minutes in a timely manner.  See OML 2022-43; OML 2022-16; OML 2021-155. 
 
For the reasons stated above, we find that the Board violated the Open Meeting Law by 
failing to timely approve meeting minutes.  We order immediate and future compliance with the 
law’s requirements, and we caution that similar future violations could be considered evidence of 
intent to violate the law.  We further order the Board to create and approve all outstanding 
meeting minutes from the years 2019 to 2021 within ninety (90) days of the Board’s receipt of 
this letter, and to certify to our office in writing that it has done so. 
 
We now consider the complaint addressed by this determination to be resolved.  This 
determination does not address any other complaints that may be pending with our office or the
