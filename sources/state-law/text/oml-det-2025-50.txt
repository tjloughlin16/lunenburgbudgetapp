 RE: Open Meeting Law Complaints 
 
Dear Attorney Siegel: 
 
This office received two complaints from Patrick McPhee on July 12, 2024, alleging that 
the Ashby Selectboard (the “Board”) violated the Open Meeting Law, G.L. c. 30A, §§ 18-25.  
The first complaint was originally filed with the Board on or around April 30, and you 
responded, on behalf of the Board, by letter dated May 20.1  The first complaint alleges that the 
Board did not respond to a request for meeting minutes within 10 days.  The second complaint 
was originally filed with the Board on or around May 24, and Town Administrator Mary 
Daughraty responded, on behalf of the Board, by letter dated June 13.  The second complaint 
restates the allegations of the first complaint and also alleges that the Board did not provide the 
requested minutes within 30 days.   
 
Following our review, we find that the Board violated the Open Meeting Law by not 
properly responding to a request for meeting minutes.  In reaching this determination, we 
reviewed the original complaints, the Board’s responses to the complaints, and the complainant’s 
requests for further review of the complaints.  We also reviewed correspondence from the 
Board’s legal counsel dated May 31, 2024, purportedly responding to a public records request 
from the complainant, as well as additional correspondence between the complainant, the Town, 
and our office.

The Open Meeting Law requires that a public body “create and maintain accurate 
minutes of all meetings, including executive sessions.”  G.L. c. 30A, § 22(a).  The minutes of an 
open session, if they exist and whether approved or in draft form, shall be made available upon 
request by any person within 10 days.  G.L. c. 30A, § 22(c).  If minutes do not yet exist at the 
time of a request, the public body is still required to respond to the request within 10 days with 
an explanation of whether the minutes do or do not exist in either approved or draft form.  See 
OML 2017-50; OML 2016-71; OML 2015-173. 
 
Executive session minutes may be withheld from disclosure to the public “as long as 
publication may defeat the lawful purposes of the executive session, but no longer.”  G.L. c. 
30A, § 22(f).  When the purpose for a valid executive session has been served, the minutes and 
any documents or exhibits used at the session must be disclosed unless the attorney-client 
privilege or an exemption to the public records law applies to withhold them, in whole or in part, 
from disclosure.  See id.  Public bodies have an obligation to review the minutes of executive 
sessions at reasonable intervals to determine if continued non-disclosure of minutes is warranted, 
and to announce that determination at the next meeting following its review.  G.L. c. 30A, 
§ 22(g)(1); see OML 2019- 35; OML 2015-94; OML 2013-56.  In addition, upon request by any 
person to inspect or copy the minutes of an executive session or any portion thereof, the body 
must respond to the request within 10 days following receipt and release any such minutes where 
publication would not defeat the lawful purposes of the executive session; provided, however, 
that if the body has not performed a review, the public body shall perform the review and release 
the non-exempt minutes, or any portion thereof, not later than the body’s next meeting or 30 
days, whichever first occurs.  G.L. c. 30A, § 22(g)(2); OML 2012-106; OML 2012-91; OML 
2012-86.  In such circumstances, the public body must still respond to the requester within ten 
days, notifying them that the body is conducting a review of the requested executive session 
minutes.  See OML 2013-99. 
 
On April 14, the complainant sent a written request to the Board for the minutes of the 
Board’s February 1 meeting.  The entire substantive portion of the February 1 meeting was held 
in executive session.  The Board did not respond to the request within 10 days.  On May 8, the 
Board reviewed and approved draft minutes of the February 1 meeting.  According to the 
Board’s attorney, on May 30, the Board “reviewed the approved minutes for purposes of 
determining if the minutes may be released to the public in accordance with the Massachusetts 
Open Meeting Law.”  By letter dated May 31, the Board’s attorney sent a letter to the 
complainant stating that she was writing “in response to the request for public records you 
submitted to the Town’s Board of Selectmen, dated 4/14/24.”  The letter explained that after 
reviewing the requested meeting minutes, the minutes were determined to be protected by the 
attorney-client privilege and not subject to disclosure.  The minutes have since been released to 
the public with redactions. 
 
As a preliminary matter, we note that the Board improperly treated the complainant’s 
April 14 request for meeting minutes as a request made under the Public Records Law.  Requests 
for meeting minutes may fall under the Public Records Law or the Open Meeting Law, 
depending on how the request is made, and the requirements for responding differ.  A request for 
minutes that is directed to a clerk or other municipal employee as Records Access Officer will 
likely fall under the Public Records Law and requires a response in 10 business days.  See G.L.

c. 66, § 10; OML 2019-126; OML 2019-68.  A request for meeting minutes made directly to the 
public body falls under the Open Meeting Law and requires a response within 10 calendar days.  
See G.L. c. 30A, § 22(g)(2); OML 2019-125.  As explained above, the Open Meeting Law also 
imposes additional requirements when responding to a request for executive session minutes. 
 
The April 14 request for minutes of the Board’s February 1 meeting was directed to the 
Board, and therefore the request fell under the Open Meeting Law and required a response within 
10 calendar days, even if only to inform the requestor that it would be reviewing the executive 
session minutes to determine whether they could be released.  See G.L. c. 30A, §§ 22(c), (g); 
OML 2024-166; OML 2019-163.  By failing to provide any response within 10 calendar days, 
the Board violated the Open Meeting Law.  Furthermore, the Open Meeting Law required the 
Board to review the executive session minutes to determine whether they could be released no 
later than the Board’s next meeting or within 30 days of the request, whichever first occurred.  
See G.L. c. 30A, § 22(g)(2); OML 2024-213; OML 2013-165.   The Board did not review the 
minutes to determine whether they could be released to the public until May 30, which was its 
fifth meeting and more than 30 days following the request, and therefore violated the Open 
Meeting Law in this additional respect.   
 
On September 5, we issued a determination letter to the Board finding that the Board 
similarly violated the Open Meeting Law by failing to properly respond to a prior request for 
meeting minutes from the same requestor.  See OML 2024-166.  Because the request at issue in 
this letter predated that determination, and the Board did not yet have the benefit of our 
guidance, we do not find that the violations described herein were intentional.  The Board has 
now twice been warned and advised of its obligations, and we caution the Board that a finding of 
a similar violation in the future may be considered evidence of intent to violate the Open 
Meeting Law and may result in a civil penalty of up to $1000 per violation.  See G.L. c. 30A, § 
23(c)(4); 940 CMR 29.02 (“Evidence of an intentional violation of [the Open Meeting Law] 
shall include … that the public body … was previously informed by receipt of a decision from a 
court of competent jurisdiction or advised by the Attorney General, … that the conduct violates 
[the Open Meeting Law].”).

For the reasons stated above, we find that the Board violated the Open Meeting Law by 
failing to properly respond to a request for executive session meeting minutes.  We order the 
Board’s immediate and future compliance with the law’s requirements.  We now consider the 
complaints addressed by this determination to be resolved.  This determination does not address 
any other complaints that may be pending with our office or the Board.  Please feel free to 
contact our office at (617) 963-2540 if you have any questions regarding this letter.
