FACTS
We find the facts as follows. The Board is a five-member public body. In 2013, the 
Town of Stoughton adopted the use of remote participation for meetings of its public bodies in 
accordance with the Attorney General’s regulations.
The Board duly posted notices for, and held meetings on, November 20, 2018; December 
4, 2018; December 11, 2018; December 18, 2018; January 2; January 8; January 15; and January 
29.
One topic on the January 2 notice was “Consent Agenda,” which included the approval of 
five sets of minutes of meetings held between November 13, 2018, and December 18, 2018. One 
topic on the January 8 and January 14 notices was “Consent Agenda,” which included the 
approval of seven sets of minutes of meetings held between October 15, 2018 and January 2.
The Board did not approve any minutes during its January 2, January 8 or January 14 meetings.
The Board duly posted notice of a meeting to be held on January 15 at 7:00 P.M. The 
notice listed seven topics, including Citizens Comment, Town Manager Reports, Selectmen’s 
Comments, Malcolm & Parson Project Update, FY20 Proposed Operating Budget, Consent 
Agenda, and Executive Session. Several of these topic headings included specific item 
descriptions that further advised the public of the topics to be discussed. Specifically, the 
Consent Agenda topic included the approval of seven sets of minutes for meetings held between 
October 15, 2018, and January 2; and the Executive Session topic included discussion of three 
properties under Purpose 6 and the approval of five sets of executive session minutes of meetings 
held between November 13, 2018, and January 2.
The January 15 meeting was held as planned. Four Board members were physically 
present at the meeting and one member, Richard Hill, participated remotely. All votes taken 
during the open portion of the meeting, including the vote to convene in executive session, were 
taken by voice vote and not conducted by roll call vote.
During the consent agenda, the Board discussed the approval of meeting minutes from 
October 15, 2018; November 13, 2018; November 20, 2018; December 4, 2018; December 11, 
2018; December 18, 2018; and January 2. Although the minutes indicate that the Board 
approved these seven sets of minutes, the Board actually only approved the meeting minutes of 
October 15, 2018; November 13, 2018; December 4, 2018; and December 11, 2018. The 
meeting minutes of November 20, 2018; December 18, 2018; and January 2 were not yet 
complete and ready for approval.4

DISCUSSION
The Open Meeting Law was enacted “to eliminate much of the secrecy surrounding 
deliberations and decisions on which public policy is based.” Ghiglione v. School Committee of 
Southbridge, 376 Mass. 70, 72 (1978). The Open Meeting Law requires that meetings of a 
public body be properly noticed and open to members of the public, unless an executive session 
is convened. See G.L. c. 30A, §§ 20(a)-(b), 21. The Open Meeting Law gives the Attorney 
General the power to authorize, by regulation or letter ruling, “remote participation by members 
of a public body not present at the meeting location.” G.L. c. 30A, § 20(d). Pursuant to this 
authority, the Attorney General promulgated regulations, 940 CMR 29.10, which govern public 
bodies’ use of remote participation.
1) The Board Failed to Conduct All Votes by Roll Call.
The complaint alleges that the votes taken by the Board during the January 15 meeting 
were not by roll call. The Open Meeting Law regulations require that “[a]ll votes taken during 
any meeting in which a member participates remotely must be by roll call vote.” 940 CMR 
29.10(7)(c). During the January 15 meeting, one Board member participated remotely.
However, votes taken during this meeting were not conducted by roll call vote. The Board 
acknowledged that the votes were not roll call votes and we, thus, find that the Board violated 
the Open Meeting Law. See OML 2016-132; OML 2015-145.5 In response to the complaint, the 
Board ratified its January 15 votes by roll call during its January 29 meeting. We commend the 
Board for taking this remedial action.
The complaint further alleges that the remote participant did not announce that no other 
person was present or able to hear the executive session discussion prior to convening in 
executive session. The regulations state that a “member participating remotely may participate 
in an executive session, but shall state at the start of any such session that no other person is 
present and/or able to hear the discussion at the remote location.” 940 CMR 29.10(7)(d). This 
statement does not have to occur in open session, but rather at the beginning of the executive 
session. See id. As such, we find that the Board did not violate the Open Meeting Law when 
Mr. Hill failed to state in open session that no other person was present or able to hear the 
executive session discussions. Furthermore, although neither the Open Meeting Law nor the 
regulations expressly require that this declaration made at the start of the executive session be

2) The Board Failed to Timely Approve Open Session Meeting Minutes of November 20,
2018, and December 18, 2018.
The Open Meeting Law requires that a public body “create and maintain accurate 
minutes of all meetings, including executive sessions, setting forth the date, time and place, the 
members present or absent, a summary of the discussions on each subject, a list of documents 
and other exhibits used at the meeting, the decisions made and the actions taken at each meeting, 
including the record of all votes.” G.L. c. 30A, § 22(a). The meeting minutes must be reviewed 
and approved in a timely manner. G.L. c. 30A, § 22(c); 940 CMR 29.11. “Timely manner” 
means “within the next three public body meetings or within 30 days, whichever is later, unless 
the public body can show good cause for further delay.” 940 CMR 29.11; See OML 2018-48. 
Whenever possible, we recommend that minutes of a meeting be approved at the next meeting. 
See OML 2018-67; OML 2017-133.
The complaint alleges that the Board failed to timely approve open session minutes of 
meetings held on November 20, 2018; December 18, 2018; and January 2. The Board was 
required to approve these minutes within 30 days as that was the later of the two time periods for 
these minutes because the Board met at least three times within 30 days following each of these 
meetings. See 940 CMR 29.11; OML 2018-152. The Board did not approve any minutes during 
its January 2, January 8 or January 14 meetings. During the January 15 meeting, the Board 
approved minutes of its October 15, 2018; November 13, 2018; December 4, 2018; and 
December 11, 2018, meetings. We find that the Board violated the Open Meeting Law by failing 
to approve its November 20, 2018, meeting minutes by December 20, 2018; and by failing to 
approve its December 18, 2018, meeting minutes by January 17. We find that the Board timely 
approved its January 2 meeting minutes during its January 29 meeting and thus did not violate 
the Open Meeting Law as to those minutes.
CONCLUSION
For the reasons stated above, we find that the Board violated the Open Meeting Law by 
failing to conduct all votes by roll call during its January 29 meeting and by failing to timely 
approve the November 20, 2018, and December 18, 2018, open session meeting minutes. We 
order the Board’s immediate and future compliance with the law’s requirements, and we caution 
that similar future violations could be considered evidence of intent to violate the law. We 
further order the Board to approve the November 20, 2018, and December 18, 2018, meeting 
minutes at its next meeting.
We now consider the complaint addressed by this determination to be resolved. This 
determination does not address any other complaints that may be pending with our office or the 
Board. Please feel free to contact the Division at (617) 963-2540 if you have any questions.
