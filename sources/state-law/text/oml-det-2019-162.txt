RE: Open  Meeting Law Complaint
Dear Attorney Callanan:
This office received a complaint from Gerard Mackin on June 4, 2019, alleging that the 
Weymouth School Committee (the “Committee”) violated the Open Meeting Law, G.L. c. 30A, 
§§ 18-25.1 The complaint was originally filed with the Committee on May 4, and you responded 
to the complaint, on behalf of the Committee, by letter dated May 24.2 The complaint alleges 
that the Committee discussed a topic that was not listed on its April 25 meeting notice.
We resolve this complaint by informal action in accordance with 940 CMR 29.07(2)(a), 
after reviewing the original complaint, the Committee’s response to the complaint, the complaint 
filed with our office requesting further review, the notice and open session minutes of the 
Committee’s April 25 meeting, and a video recording of the April 25 meeting.3 We find that the 
Committee did not violate the Open Meeting Law.
The Open Meeting Law requires that, except in an emergency, “a public body shall post 
notice of every meeting at least 48 hours prior to such meeting, excluding Saturdays, Sundays 
and legal holidays.” G.L. c. 30A, § 20(b). The notice must be printed in a legible, easily 
understandable format and must contain the date, time and place of the meeting, as well as a

shall have “sufficient specificity to reasonably advise the public of the issues to be discussed at 
the meeting.” 940 CMR 29.03(l)(b). We generally consider a topic to be sufficiently specific 
when a reasonable member of the public could read the topic and understand the anticipated 
nature of the public body’s discussion. See OML 2015-35.4
The complaint alleges that the Committee discussed a topic, the performance of the prior 
athletic director, during its April 25 meeting that was not listed on the notice. The notice for the 
April 25 meeting included a presentation from the interim athletic director. We find that the 
Committee’s discussion with the interim athletic director at the conclusion of his presentation 
and members’ comments that information in the presentation was not provided to them in the 
past naturally flowed from its consideration of the posted agenda topic, such that the notice was 
sufficient to inform the public of the discussion that occurred. See OML 2016-150; OML 2014- 
132; OML 2013-145. The Open Meeting Law does not require a public body to anticipate the 
course of deliberation or the outcome of a discussion, only that it identify the discussion topic 
with sufficient specificity to reasonably advise the public of the issues to be discussed at the 
meeting. See G.L. c. 30A, § 20(b); 940 CMR 29.03(1)0); OML 2014-127; OML 2014-58; OML 
2014-38.
For the reasons stated above, we find that the Committee did not violate the Open 
Meeting Law. We now consider the complaint addressed by this determination to be resolved. 
This determination does not address any other complaints that may be pending with our office or 
the Committee. Please feel free to contact our office at (617) 963-2540 if you have any 
questions regarding this letter.
