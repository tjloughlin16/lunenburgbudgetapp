This office received a complaint from Mark Peterson on May 19, 2025, alleging that the 
Bridgewater Conservation Commission (the “Commission”) violated the Open Meeting Law, 
G.L. c. 30A, §§ 18-25. The complaint was originally filed with the Commission on or about 
January 28, 2025, and you responded on behalf of the Commission by email dated February 28, 
2025.
 The complaint alleges that the minutes of the meeting held on June 13, 2024, are 
insufficiently specific.  
 
Following our review, we find that the Commission violated the Open Meeting Law in 
the way alleged. In reaching this determination, we reviewed the Open Meeting Law complaint, 
the Commission’s response, the request for further review, and the notice, minutes and video 
recording of the meeting held on June 13, 2024.1 
 
FACTS 
 
We find the facts to be as follows. On June 13, 2024, the Commission held a meeting 
which included a public hearing during which it discussed five different notices of intent. One 
notice of intent related to 180 Broad Street. During the meeting, the Commission explained to 
the applicant of the 180 Broad Street project that the Commission was unable to accept the third-

DISCUSSION 
 
The Open Meeting Law requires that a public body “create and maintain accurate 
minutes of all meetings, including executive sessions, setting forth the date, time and place, the 
members present or absent, a summary of the discussions on each subject, a list of documents 
and other exhibits used at the meeting, the decisions made, and the actions taken at each meeting, 
including the record of all votes.” G.L. c. 30A, § 22(a). When reviewing meeting minutes for 
compliance with the requirement to create and maintain accurate meeting minutes, we look for 
substantial compliance with the accuracy requirement. See OML 2011-55.
3 By substantial 
compliance, we mean that the minutes should contain enough detail and accuracy so that a 
member of the public who did not attend the meeting could read the minutes and have a clear 
understanding of what occurred. See OML 2025-58; 2012-106. While the minutes must include a 
summary of the discussions on each subject, a transcript is not required. See OML 2012-29. 
 
The complaint alleges that the minutes of the meeting held on June 13, 2024, do not 
incl
ude a summary of the discussions that occurred with respect to the third-party peer review 
process for the 180 Broad Street project. We agree. Here, the Commission engaged in a 10-
minute discussion about the third-party review process, and the applicant raised concerns about 
the costs for obtaining another peer review report as well as the process put in place by the 
Commission. However, the minutes do not include any reference to this discussion. Rather, the 
minutes simply reflect that the hearing for the 180 Broad Street project was continued to the 
Commission’s next meeting on June 27, 2024. The Open Meeting Law requires that meeting 
minutes include more than a statement that a public body held a discussion about a specified 
topic; the law requires that the minutes summarize the discussions that were held. See OML 
2019-167; OML 2018-8. In addition, although the minutes do not need to cover every remark or 
opinion presented at the meeting, the minutes should include dissenting or minority opinions

CONCLUSION 
 
For the reasons stated above, we find that the Commission violated the Open Meeting 
Law by cre
ating insufficiently detailed minutes of the meeting held on June 13, 2024. We order 
the Commission to amend the minutes of the June 13, 2024, meeting in conformance with this 
letter and to certify to this office within 30 days of the date of this letter that it has done so. See 
940 CMR 29.07(4). We also order the Commission’s immediate and future compliance with the 
Open Meeting Law and caution that future similar violations may be considered evidence of 
intent to violate the law.
