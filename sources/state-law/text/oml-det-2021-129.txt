 RE: Open Meeting Law Complaint 
Dear Attorney Sousa: 
This office received a complaint from Patrick Higgins on October 20, 2020, alleging that 
the Fall River Housing Authority Board of Commissioners (the “Board”) violated the Open 
Meeting Law, G.L. c. 30A, §§ 18-25. The complaint was originally filed with the Board on 
September 19, 2020, and you responded on behalf of the Board by letter dated July 29, 2021.1 
The complaint originally included nine allegations, generally relating to the specificity of a 
notice for a September 19, 2020, meeting and the Board’s failure to review and release to the 
public minutes of executive session meetings held in 2019 and 2020.  
In response to the complaint, the Board’s General Counsel undertook a review of all of 
the outstanding executive session minutes, shared a spreadsheet with the results of that review 
with Mr. Higgins and our office, and reviewed the Open Meeting Law with the Board regarding 
the sufficiency of meeting notices.2 Mr. Higgins seeks further review of the Board’s decision not 
to release to the public five sets of executive session minutes from meetings held in 2020, and 
eleven sets of executive session minutes from meetings held in 2019. For the following reasons, 
we find no violation with respect to the allegation Mr. Higgins requested us to review.  
Executive session minutes may be withheld from disclosure to the public “as long as 
publication may defeat the lawful purposes of the executive session, but no longer.” G.L. c. 30A,

§ 22(f). When the purpose for a valid executive session has been served, the minutes and any 
documents or exhibits used at the session must be disclosed unless the attorney-client privilege 
or an exemption to the Public Records Law applies to withhold them, in whole or in part, from 
disclosure. See id. The burden of justifying continued nondisclosure of executive session minutes 
lies with the public body. Foudy v. Amherst-Pelham Regional School Committee, 402 Mass. 
179, 184 (1988); OML 2014-138.3 Nonetheless, Open Meeting Law complaints must allege 
violations with a degree of specificity; our office will not conduct broad audits of public bodies 
based on generalized allegations. See OML Declination 4-22-15 (Natick Economic Development 
Advisory Committee); OML Declination 3-20-12 (Wilmington Board of Assessors). 
Although Mr. Higgins’s complaint alleges that the Board is violating the Open Meeting 
Law by continuing to withhold 16 sets of executive session minutes from 2019 and 2020, the 
complaint does not specify why the withholding of those minutes is believed to be improper. The 
Board’s meeting notices provide information about the subject of the executive sessions 
sufficient to file a complaint with specific allegations. For example, the Board specified on the 
notice for its August 10, 2020, meeting that the Board would meet in executive session “pursuant 
to M.G.L. c. 30A, Sec. 21(a)(3) to discuss strategy with respect to collective bargaining 
(AFSCME Council 93, Local 1990).” The complaint does not allege that the purpose for this 
executive session has been served, for example, because the negotiations have concluded. 
Instead, the complaint contains a general allegation concerning all of the Board’s sixteen sets of 
2019-2020 executive session meeting minutes, covering dozens of topics, because many of the 
executive sessions covered multiple topics. Although the public body ultimately bears the burden 
of justifying the continued disclosure of executive session minutes when challenged, the 
complaint nonetheless must identify the basis for the alleged violation with a degree of 
specificity. Accordingly, we decline to conduct a broad review of the minutes of 16 separate 
executive session meetings based on a generalized allegation that all of the minutes are being 
wrongfully withheld.   
Although not an issue on which Mr. Higgins requested review, we take this opportunity 
to remind the Board of the Open Meeting Law’s requirements regarding the periodic review of 
executive session minutes. The Board concedes that it did not previously have a practice of 
periodically reviewing all executive session minutes to determine whether the minutes could be 
released to the public. A public body or its chair or designee must, at reasonable intervals, review 
the minutes of executive sessions to determine if continued nondisclosure of those minutes is 
warranted. G.L. c. 30A, § 22(g)(1). Although “reasonable interval” is not defined by the law, our 
office has found a quarterly review or a review every six months to be appropriate. See OML 
2019-45; OML 2017-104; OML 2015-166; OML 2013-180. In its response, the Board states that 
“[t]he [Board’s] General Counsel has commenced and adopted the practice of an annual review 
of all executive session minutes… [which will be] reported to the [Board] accordingly.” A public 
body’s obligation to review executive session minutes for possible release is ongoing. If a public 
body reviews executive session minutes and determines that the purpose for the executive 
session remains such that the minutes should continue to be withheld from the public, it must 
continue to review those same executive session minutes at reasonable intervals until it 
determines that the reason for secrecy has expired. See OML 2019-133.

Finally, we remind that Board that at the meeting following each periodic review, the 
public body must announce which executive session minutes will be released and which will 
continue to be withheld at the next meeting following its review, and such announcement shall 
be included in the minutes. G.L. c. 30A, § 22(g)(1); OML 2019-3; OML 2015-94. 
 
 For the reasons stated above, we find no violation with respect to the allegation Mr. 
Higgins requested us to review, although we note, and the Board acknowledges, that the Board 
failed to periodically review its executive minutes as required by the Open Meeting Law. We 
now consider the complaint addressed by this determination to be resolved. This determination 
does not address any other complaints that may be pending with our office or the Board. Please 
feel free to contact the Division at (617) 963 - 2540 if you have any questions.
