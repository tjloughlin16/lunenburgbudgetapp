RE: Open Meeting Law Complaint
Dear Attorney Murphy:
This office received a complaint from Brooks Ames, on November 12, 2019, alleging 
that the Brookline Select Board (the “Board”) violated the Open Meeting Law, G.L. c. 30A, §§ 
18-25. The complaint was originally filed with the Board on October 2, and the Board responded 
by letter dated October 16.1 In his complaint, Mr. Ames alleges that the Board improperly took a 
vote on whether to file an appeal during an executive session.2
We resolve this complaint by informal action in accordance with 940 CMR 29.07(2)(a), 
and find that the Board did not violate the Open Meeting Law as alleged. A public body may 
enter executive session “[t]o discuss strategy with respect to collective bargaining or litigation if 
an open meeting may have a detrimental effect on the bargaining or litigating position of the 
public body and the chair so declares.” G.L. c. 30A, § 21(a)(3); OML 2019-164.3 Because there 
are circumstances in which both the bargaining and litigating positions of a public body may be 
detrimentally affected by voting on a final collective bargaining agreement or litigation decision 
in open session, final approval may occur in executive session if the chair makes the appropriate 
declaration that such circumstances are present. See OML 2013-84; Doherty v. School 
Committee of Boston. 386 Mass. 643 (1982) (holding that closed door votes are permissible 
where a body meets in executive session to protect its litigating position).

Here, the Board met in executive session under Purpose 3 on September 3, 10, and 17 to 
discuss litigation strategy related to Gerald Alston’s reinstatement as a Brookline Firefighter by 
the Civil Service Commission.4 We have reviewed, in camera, the executive session minutes of 
these meetings. The minutes have not been released to the public, and therefore we do not 
recount their content in detail here. However, we are satisfied that the Board’s discussions 
related to the litigation matter involving Mr. Alston and included a vote authorizing an appeal of 
the Civil Service Commission’s decision to the Appeals Court. Additionally, according to the 
Board’s meeting minutes for September 3, 10 and 17, the Chair declared each time before 
entering executive session that discussing the matter in open session would be detrimental to the 
Board’s litigating position. Therefore, we find no violation of the Open Meeting Law as the 
Board’s vote to file the appeal was not required to take place in open session.
We now consider the complaint addressed by this determination to be resolved. This 
determination does not address any other complaints that may be pending with our office or the 
Board. Please feel free to contact the Division at (617) 963 - 2540 if you have any questions.
