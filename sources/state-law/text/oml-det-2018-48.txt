This office received a complaint from Patrick Higgins, dated March 2, 2018, alleging that 
the Swansea Waterfront Revitalization Committee (the "Committee") violated the Open Meeting 
Law, G.L. c. 30A, §§ 18-25. The complaint was originally filed with the Committee on 
November 30 or December I,1 and the Board responded by letter dated December 27.2 In his 
complaint, Mr. Higgins alleges that the Committee failed to approve four sets of meeting minutes 
in a timely manner and that the Committee failed to implement the Open Meeting Law 
regulations that went into effect on October 6. 
Following our review, we find that the Committee violated the Open Meeting Law by not 
approving its April 18 and October 4 meeting minutes in a timely manner. In reaching this 
determination, we reviewed the original complaint, the Committee's response to the complaint, 
and the complaint filed with our office requesting further review. We also reviewed open 
session minutes from the Committee's April 18, 2017; November 29, 2017; and January 17, 
2018 meetings; and email correspondence from the Chair of the Committee to our office. 
Finally, we spoke with you by telephone on March 9, 2018. 
We find the facts as follows. The Committee met seven times in 2017: January 26, April 
18, August 9, August 30, October 4, November 29, and December 13. The Committee met twice 
in 2018; on January 17, 2018 and February 1, 2018.

The Committee posted notice for a November 29 meeting on November 21. The fifth 
topic on the notice for its November 29 meeting indicated that the Committee would review four 
sets of meeting minutes: April 18, August 9, October 4, and October 13. According to the chair 
and the November 29 minutes, of the four sets of minutes listed on the November 29 notice for 
approval, the Committee only approved the August 9 minutes during the November 29 meeting. 
The October 13 minutes were included in the notice in error. The Committee did not meet on 
October 13. Instead, the Committee had intended to include the August 30 meeting minutes in 
the list of minutes to be approved. The Committee did not approve the April 18 or October 4 
minutes at the November 29 meeting. 
In response to the complaint's allegations that the Committee failed to timely approve 
meeting minutes, the Town Administrator explained that a transcriptionist had previously 
recorded the Committee's minutes but had resigned recently. Since the transcriptionist's 
resignation, the Committee members had taken turns recording the minutes. The Town 
Administrator had volunteered to record the April 18 minutes and had misfiled his handwritten 
notes. At the time of the Town Administrator's response, the April 18 minutes were complete 
and ready for acceptance. The response did not address the other sets of minutes on the meeting 
agenda for the November 29 meeting. 
The Committee approved the April 18, 2017 minutes at its January 17, 2018 meeting. 
The October 4 minutes have not yet been approved but the Committee plans to approve them at 
its next meeting in April 2018. 
DISCUSSION 
The Open Meeting Law requires that a public body "create and maintain accurate 
minutes of all meetings, including executive sessions, setting forth the date, time and place, the 
members present or absent, a summary of the discussions on each subject, a list of documents 
and other exhibits used at the meeting, the decisions made and the actions taken at each meeting, 
including the record of all votes." G.L. c. 3 OA, § 22(a). The meeting minutes must be reviewed 
and approved in a timely manner. G.L. c. 30A, § 22(c). The Open Meeting Law does not define 
"timely manner." However, prior to the Attorney General promulgating updated Open Meeting 
Law regulations on October 6, our office recommended that minutes of a meeting be approved at 
the next meeting, whenever possible. See OML 2017-133; OML 2014-15; OML 2012-91.3 For 
bodies that meet regularly, such as weekly or bi-monthly, we had concluded prior to October 6 
that a delay of two to three months in approving minutes does not comply with the law. See 
OML 2017-33; OML 2016-62; OML 2013-31. According to the revised regulations, as of 
October 6, "timely manner" means "within the next three public body meetings or within 30 
days, whichever is later, unless the public body can show good cause for further delay." See 940 
CMR 29.11. 
The complaint alleges that four sets of Committee meeting minutes were not approved in 
a timely manner: April 18, August 9, October 4, and October 13. We conclude that since the 
August 9 and August 30 meeting minutes were approved within three public body meetings, they

were approved in a timely manner. Because the Committee did not meet on October 13, it was 
not required to approve minutes from a meeting that did not exist. The Committee approved the 
April 18 minutes at the sixth subsequent meeting, without showing cause for delay. Therefore, 
the Committee's delay in approving the April 18 minutes did not comply with our standard of 
timely approval prior to October 6. Because the Committee has not yet approved its October 4 
meeting minutes, the Committee has failed to approve these minutes within a timely manner. 
More than 30 days have passed since the October 4 meeting and the Committee has met four 
times since October 4, so the Committee has had sufficient time and four opportunities to 
approve this set of minutes but has failed to do so. 
We therefore find that the Committee violated the Open Meeting Law by not timely 
approving the minutes from meetings held on April 18 and October 4. However, we commend 
the Committee for taking remedial action in response to the complaint by promptly approving the 
April 18 minutes and by planning to approve the October 4 minutes at its next meeting. 
CONCLUSION 
We find that the Committee violated the Open Meeting Law by not approving its April 
18 and October 4 meeting minutes in a timely manner. We find that the Committee did not 
violate the Open Meeting Law where it approved its August 9 and August 30 meeting minutes in 
a timely manner. We order the Committee's immediate and future compliance with the law's 
requirements, and we caution that similar future violations could be considered evidence of 
intent to violate the law. We encourage the Committee members to review the Open Meeting 
Law regulations, 940 CMR 29.01-29.11, to ensure they follow the most current requirements.4 
We now consider the complaints addressed by this determination to be resolved. This 
determination does not address any other complaints that may be pending with the Committee or 
with our office. Please feel free to contact our office at (617) 963-2540 if you have any 
questions regarding this letter.
