RE: Open Meeting Law Complaint  
 
Dear Attorney Mello:  
 
This office received a complaint from Sean McNamara on November 21, 2023, alleging 
that the Attleboro Planning Board (the “Board”) violated the Open Meeting Law, G.L. c. 30A, §§ 
18-25.  The original complaint was filed with the Board on October 31, 2023, and you 
responded, on behalf of the Board, by letter dated November 20, 2023.  The complaint alleges 
that the Board failed to timely approve minutes of the meeting held on September 11, 2023. 
 
Following our review, we find that the Board failed to timely approve the minutes of the 
September 11, 2023, meeting and that this was an intentional violation of the Open Meeting 
Law.  In reaching this determination, we reviewed the original complaint, the Board’s response 
to the complaint, the complainant’s request for further review, and the minutes of the Board 
meetings held on September 11, 2023, and November 6, 2023, meetings.  In addition, we 
communicated with the complainant on February 23, 2024.  
 
The Board held meetings on September 11, 2023, September 25, 2023, October 2, 2023, 
and October 16, 2023.  On October 17, 2023, the complainant sent an email to Board Chair 
Shannon Bénay requesting a copy of the minutes of the Board meeting held on September 11, 
2023.  That same day, Chair Bénay responded advising the complainant that the Board planned 
to review and approve the September 11, 2023, minutes at its next meeting on November 6, 
2023, and would post the minutes to the municipal website once approved.  During the meeting 
held on November 6, 2023, the Board approved minutes of the meetings held on September 11,

The Open Meeting Law requires that a public body “create and maintain accurate 
minutes of all meetings, including executive sessions, setting forth the date, time and place, the 
members present or absent, a summary of the discussions on each subject, a list of documents 
and other exhibits used at the meeting, the decisions made, and the actions taken at each meeting, 
including the record of all votes.” G.L. c. 30A, § 22(a).  Open session minutes must be created 
and approved in a timely manner.  G.L. c. 30A, § 22(c).  “Timely manner” means “within the 
next three public body meetings or within 30 days, whichever is later, unless the public body can 
show good cause for further delay.”  940 CMR 29.11; see OML 2018-48.  Whenever possible, 
we recommend that minutes of a meeting be approved at the next meeting.  See OML 2018-67; 
OML 2017-133.1   
 
Here, Chair Bénay timely responded to the complainant’s request for a copy of the 
September 11, 2023, minutes, and advised that the minutes would be approved by the Board at 
its following meeting on November 6, 2023.  We remind the Board that open session minutes do 
not need to be approved before such minutes can be provided in response to a request.  See G.L. 
c. 30A, § 22(c) (“minutes of an open session, if they exist and whether approved or in draft form, 
shall be made available upon request by any person within 10 days.”) (emphasis added).   
 
We do find that the Board failed to timely approve the minutes of the September 11, 
2023, meeting however, where the minutes were approved on November 6, 2023, which was the 
fourth meeting thereafter.  We understand that support staff for the Board departed in early 
September and the Board had to assume additional administrative tasks.  In general, staffing 
shortages of the type that all governmental bodies with limited resources experience, or periodic 
staff vacancies, do not constitute good cause for failing to approve minutes in a timely manner.  
It is the responsibility of the public body and its members to take steps to ensure that staff have 
adequate assistance to complete the minutes in a timely manner, including having members of 
the public body assist in the creation of minutes if necessary.  See OML 2021-177; OML 2018-
105; OML 2015-86.  Ultimately, the responsibility for complying with the Open Meeting Law’s 
requirement that meeting minutes be created and approved in a timely manner rests with public 
bodies and members of such bodies, not with individual public employees, such as 
administrative staff.  See OML 2023-113; OML 2018-105.  Therefore, we find that the Board 
failed to timely approve the minutes of the meeting held on September 11, 2023.  
 
We must determine whether this violation was, as the complainant urges, an intentional 
one.  See G.L. c. 30A, § 23(c).  An intentional violation is an “act or omission by a public body 
or a member thereof, in knowing violation of [the Open Meeting Law].”  940 CMR 29.02.  An 
intentional violation may be found where the public body acted with deliberate ignorance of the 
law’s requirement or has previously been advised that certain conduct violates the Open Meeting 
Law.  Id.  The Board was advised by the Attorney General last year, on March 9, 2023, of its 
obligation to review and approve minutes in a timely manner and that timely manner means

within the next three public body meetings or within 30 days, whichever is later.  See OML 
2023-30.  Furthermore, we specifically advised the Board that similar future violations could be 
considered evidence of intent to violate the law.  Because the Board had previously been advised 
that the failure to approve minutes within the next three meetings or 30 days, whichever is 
longer, violates the Open Meeting Law, we find that this violation is intentional.  See G.L. c. 
30A, § 18; 940 CMR 29.02; OML 2013-63.  However, we decline to recommend the imposition 
of a civil penalty in this instance where the minutes at issue were approved before the complaint 
was filed with our office for review.  See G.L. c. 30A, § 23(c); OML 2022-184; OML 2018-108; 
OML 2015-122.  
 
For the reasons stated above, we find that the Board’s failure to timely approve the  
minutes of the September 11, 2023, meeting was an intentional violation of the law.  We order 
the Board’s immediate and future compliance with the law’s requirements and caution the Board 
that a future finding of an intentional violation of the Open Meeting Law may result in a civil 
penalty of up to $1,000 per violation.   
 
We now consider the complaint addressed by this determination to be resolved.  This 
determination does not address any other complaints that may be pending with our office or the 
Board.  Please feel free to contact the Division at (617) 963-2540 if you have any questions.
