 RE: Open Meeting Law Complaint 
Dear Chair Daniels-Demers: 
 
This office received a complaint from Casey Fleming on March 14, 2022, alleging that 
the Oxford Board of Selectmen (the “Board”) violated the Open Meeting Law, G.L. c. 30A, 
§§ 18-25.  The complaint was originally filed with the Board on or around December 14, 2021, 
and you responded, on behalf of the Board, by letter dated December 22, 2021.  In his complaint, 
Mr. Fleming alleges that the Board failed to timely approve minutes of ten meetings that took 
place between March and October of 2021.   
 
Following our review, we find that the Board violated the Open Meeting Law as alleged.  
We resolve this complaint by informal action in accordance with 940 CMR 29.07(1), after 
reviewing the original complaint, the Board’s response to the complaint, and the Complainant’s 
request for further review.  We also corresponded by e-mail with the Board’s Chair as well as 
with the Complainant.  
 
The Open Meeting Law requires that public bodies “create and maintain accurate minutes 
of all meetings.”  G.L. c. 30A, §22(a).  These minutes must be reviewed and approved by the 
public body in a timely manner.  G.L. c. 30A, §22(c).  “Timely manner” means “within the next 
three public body meetings or within 30 days, whichever is later, unless the public body can 
show good cause for further delay.”  940 CMR 29.11; see OML 2018-48.1  Whenever possible, 
we recommend that minutes of a meeting be approved at the next meeting.  See OML 2018-67; 
OML 2017-133.

The Board acknowledges that it failed to timely approve the meeting minutes at issue.  In 
response to the complaint, the Board approved minutes of six meetings that took place during 
March, April, May, and June 2021 during a meeting held on March 22, 2022.  The Board then 
approved the remainder of the outstanding meeting minutes during a meeting held on April 12, 
2022.  The Board has committed to approving its meeting minutes in a timely manner going 
forward, and as of the date of this letter is current in its approval of meeting minutes.     
 
For the reasons stated above, we find that the Board violated the Open Meeting Law by 
failing to timely approve meeting minutes.  We order the Board’s immediate and future 
compliance with the Open Meeting Law, and caution that a finding of a similar future violation 
may be considered evidence of an intent to violate the Law. 
 
 We now consider the complaint addressed by this determination to be resolved.  This 
determination does not address any other complaints that may be pending with our office or the 
Board.  Please feel free to contact the Division at (617) 963-2540 if you have any questions.
