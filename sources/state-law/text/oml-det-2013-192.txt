RE: Open Meeting Law Complaint 
Dear Attorney Goldberg, 
This office received a complaint from Bernard Brahm on November 25, 2013, alleging 
that the Provincetown Board of Selectmen (the Board) violated the Open Meeting Law, G.L. 
c. 30A, §§ 18-25. The complaint was originally filed with the Board on September 19, 2013, and 
the Board responded by letter dated October 1, 2013. The complaint specifically alleges that the 
Board failed to respond in a timely manner to two complaints filed by the complainant on August 
12, 2013. 
Following our review, we have decided to resolve this complaint by informal action, in 
accordance with 940 CMR 29.07(2)(a). We find that the Board violated the Open Meeting Law 
by failing to respond within 14 business-days of receipt of the August 12, 2013 complaints. See 
G.L. c. 30A, § 23(b). We order immediate and future compliance with the Open Meeting Law, 
and caution that future similar violations may be considered evidence of intent to violate the law. 
While we find that the Board violated the law by failing to respond to the underlying complaints 
in a timely manner, we order no additional relief because the Board has responded to the 
underlying complaints and acknowledged its error in the October 1, 2013 letter. 
While we acknowledge the challenges volunteer bodies such as this sometimes face, we 
remind the Board that the Law requires that it meet certain obligations. Public bodies must 
respond to Open Meeting Law complaints within fourteen business days of receipt, send a copy 
of the complaint to our office and notify us of any remedial action taken, unless our office grants 
an extension. See G.L. c. 30A, § 23(b); 940 CMR 29.05(5). Extensions may be granted for 
good cause shown. 940 CMR 29.05(5)(b). We encourage public bodies to reach out to our office 
to determine whether an extension may be warranted.
