RE: Open Meeting Law Complaint 
Dear Mr. Brady: 
This office received a complaint from Robert Osterberg, dated May 23, 2013, alleging 
that the Orleans Board of Assessors (the "Board") violated the Open Meeting Law, G.L. c. 30A, 
§§ 18-25. Specifically, the complaint alleges that the Board improperly denied inspection of its 
January 8, 2013; January 15, 2013; January 22, 2013; and January 29, 2013 executive session 
meeting minutes. Mr. Osterberg filed his complaint with the Board on March 11, 2013. The 
Board responded to the complaint by letter on March 20, 2013. 
In reaching our determination, we reviewed the complaint filed with the Board on March 
11, 2013; the Board's March 20, 2013 response; the May 23, 2013 complaint filed with our 
office; and the redacted and un-redacted minutes for the Board's executive sessions held on 
January 8, 2013; January 15, 2013; January 22, 2013; and January 29, 2013. 
Following our review, we find that the Board did not violate the Open Meeting Law 
because publication of the redacted portions of the minutes would defeat the lawful purpose of 
those executive sessions. 
FACTS 
On January 8, 2013; January 15, 2013; January 22, 2013; and January 29, 2013, the 
Board met in executive session under Purpose 7, citing G.L. c. 59, § 60,1 to discuss tax 
abatement applications. On February 20, 2013, the complainant requested the un-redacted

minutes for each of these sessions. The Board requested an extension from the complainant to 
produce the minutes until March 8, 2013. On March 11, 2013, the complainant filed his Open 
Meeting Law complaint with the Board. The Board publicly released the minutes of the 
executive sessions on March 20, 2013, but certain information regarding the content of 
abatement applications was redacted. For example, discussions about topics unique to the 
applicant's property were redacted, while the amount of the abatement granted, voting results, 
and documents used by the Board in considering the application were published. 
DISCUSSION 
The facts and allegations in this complaint are similar to those raised by Mr. Osterberg in 
the complaint resolved by OML 2013-96, issued July 15, 2013.3 In accordance with our finding 
in that determination, we find that that the Board did not violate the Open Meeting Law. Release 
of the redacted portions of the minutes at issue would allow a reader to infer information from 
certain abatement applications that is protected by statute from public disclosure. See G.L. c. 59, 
§ 60. Therefore, we find that the Board did not violate the Open Meeting Law by withholding 
portions of its executive session minutes. See G.L. c. 30A, § 22(f). 
CONCLUSION 
We find that the Board did not violate the Open Meeting Law because publication of the 
redacted portions of the minutes for its executive sessions held on January 8, 2013; January 15, 
2013; January 22, 2013; and January 29, 2013 would defeat the lawful purpose of those 
executive sessions. We now consider this matter closed. If you have any questions regarding 
this determination, please contact our office at (617) 963-2540.
