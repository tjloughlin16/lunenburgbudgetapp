This office received two complaints from Robert Penta and Cheryl Rodriguez, dated 
January 30, 2018 and January 31, 2018, respectively, alleging that the Medford Zoning Board of 
Appeals (the "Board") violated the Open Meeting Law, G.L. c. 30A, §§ 18-25. The complaints 
were originally filed with the Board on October 30, and the Board responded to both complaints 
in a single letter dated November 20.1,2 In their complaints, Mr. Penta and Ms. Rodriguez allege 
that the Board violated the Open Meeting Law during an October 24 hearing by failing to 
accommodate a large crowd, limiting public participation, and prematurely adjourned the 
hearing. The complainants also allege violations of zoning laws at the October 24 hearing. 
Additionally, Mr. Penta and Ms. Rodriguez allege that the Board provided insufficient notice for 
a September 26 hearing, and that the Board limited attendance at the hearing by scheduling it at 
the same time as a Medford City Council meeting. 
Following our review, we find that the Board did not violate the Open Meeting Law. We

FACTS 
The complaints primarily concern the second hearing on a proposal for a residential 
development located at 87 Medford Street in Medford and 519 Broadway in Somerville. The 
Board had previously held a public hearing on September 26 from 7:00PM to approximately 
midnight. The Board continued the matter to a hearing on October 24 to allow the City of 
Somerville's Planning Director to review the proposal and offer comment. The October 24 
hearing was initially held in Medford City Hall Room 201. Since there were multiple hearings 
listed on the notice for the Board's meeting, there was a large crowd in attendance. The 
complainants indicate that Ms. Rodriguez requested that the Board relocate the hearing to the 
City Council Chambers to accommodate the large crowd, and that the Chair initially denied the 
request. The complainants state that when the Chair was asked a second time, he agreed to move 
the hearing. The Board then relocated the hearing to the City Council Chambers. 
During the October 24 hearing, the Board agreed to allow comments from four Medford 
City Councilors and a Somerville Alderman. Subsequently, Mr. Penta asked the Chair to open 
the floor to public comment, and the Chair explained that public comment about the residential 
development proposal had concluded at the end of the September 26 hearing. Mr. Penta then 
began to address the Board, at which point the Chair indicated he would call the police to remove 
Mr. Penta if he did not "stand down." When other individuals stood up to address the Board as 
well, the Chair adjourned the hearing. 
DISCUSSION 
1. The Board Took Reasonable Steps to Accommodate the Crowd. 
Public bodies must make reasonable accommodations for overflow crowds. See OML 
2015-92; OML 2012-66. The notice for October 24 indicated the hearing would be held in 
Medford City Hall Room 201, the routine meeting location for the Board. Despite initial 
hesitation, the Chair agreed to relocate the hearing in order to accommodate the crowd. 
Therefore, we conclude that, by moving the hearing to the largest space available, the Board 
complied with the Open Meeting Law. See OML 2015-92. 
2. The Board did not Violate the Open Meeting Law by Limiting Public Comment at the 
October 24 Hearing or by Adjourning the Hearing Early. 
The complainants allege that the Board violated the Open Meeting Law by limiting 
public comment at the October 24 hearing. The Open Meeting Law requires that all meetings of 
public bodies be conducted in open session, with some exceptions. G.L. c. 30A, §§ 20(a), 21(a). 
The Open Meeting Law states that "no person shall address a meeting of a public body without 
permission of the chair, and all persons shall, at the request of the chair, be silent." G.L. c. 30A, 
§ 20(g). The law therefore gives discretion to the chair of the public body to determine who may 
speak or otherwise participate at a meeting. See OML 2014-23; OML 2013-64; OML 2012-59. 
Thus, the Chair had the authority to limit public comment to certain topics and specific public

public bodies to allow public participation during meetings whenever feasible. See OML 2015-

The complainants also allege that the Board violated the Open Meeting Law when the 
Chair warned he would call the police if Mr. Penta continued to speak during the hearing. The 
Open Meeting Law states that "[n]o person shall disrupt the proceedings of a meeting of a public 
body. If, after clear warning from the chair, a person continues to disrupt the proceedings, the 
chair may order the person to withdraw from the meeting and if the person does not withdraw, 
the chair may authorize a constable or other officer to remove the person from the meeting." 
G.L. c. 3OA, § 20(g). In this instance, the Chair clearly warned Mr. Penta. 
Furthermore, the law does not prohibit a public body from removing, postponing, or 
declining to discuss a topic listed on a meeting notice. See OML 2015-174; OML 2012-23. The 
Board was not required to discuss the items listed on the hearing notice, or to permit the 
complainant to speak. Consequently, we find that the Council did not violate the Open Meeting 
Law by adjourning the hearing when it chose to do so. 
3. We Decline to Review Whether or not the Board Complied with Zoning Laws. 
Ms. Rodriguez alleges that the Board violated G.L. c. 40A §10, relevant to variances, 
which is a matter outside the scope of the Open Meeting Law. Therefore, we decline to review 
this allegation. See OML 9-4-12; OML 5-21-12. 
4. We Decline to Review Allegations Regarding the Board's September 26 Hearing. 
The complainants each allege that the Board violated the Open Meeting Law by 
scheduling the September 26 hearing at the same time as a Medford City Council meeting. In 
addition, Ms. Rodriguez alleges that neighbors residing in proximity to the property were not 
notified in writing of the September 26 hearing. 
Complaints alleging violations of the Open Meeting Law must be filed with the public 
body within 30 days of the alleged violation. G.L. c. 3 OA, § 23(b). If the alleged violation could 
not reasonably have been known at the time it occurred, then the complaint must be filed within 
30 days of the date it should reasonably have been discovered. 940 CMR 29.05(3). When an 
alleged violation occurs during an open meeting, the alleged violation is reasonably discoverable 
at the time it occurs. See OML Declination 7-18-17 (Wellesley School Committee); OML 2014-
85; OML 2012-52. Here, any complaint concerning the September 26 hearing should have been 
filed with the Committee within thirty days of the hearing, or by October 26. G.L. c. 30A, § 
23(b). Because this complaint was filed with the Board on October 30, we find that this specific 
allegation is untimely and we decline to review it. See OML Declination 2-1-17 (West 
Bridgewater Board of Water Commissioners); OML Declination 11-21-16 (Middleton Planning 
Board); 12-15-14 (Ware Conservation Commission). Additionally, we decline to review these 
allegations because they fall outside the scope of the Open Meeting Law.

CONCLUSION 
For the reasons stated above, we find that the Board did not violate the Open Meeting 
Law. We now consider the complaints addressed by this determination to be resolved. This 
determination does not address any other complaints that may be pending with our office or the
