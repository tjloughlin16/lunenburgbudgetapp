 RE: Open Meeting Law Complaint 
 
Dear Attorney Rozak: 
 
This office received a complaint from Dylan Lambert on October 18, 2021, alleging that 
the Leicester School Committee (the “Committee”) violated the Open Meeting Law, G.L. c. 
30A, §§ 18-25.1  The complaint was originally filed with the Committee on September 16, and 
you responded, on behalf of the Committee, by letter dated September 30.  The complaint alleges 
that the Committee did not allow public participation during a meeting held on September 15. 
   
We resolve this matter by informal action, in accordance with 940 CMR 29.07(2)(a), 
after reviewing the original complaint, the Commission’s response to the complaint, the 
complaint filed with our office requesting further review, and the notice and minutes of the 
meeting held on September 15.  In addition, we spoke with the complainant on March 1, 2022, 
and with you on March 25, 2022.  We find that the Committee did not violate the Open Meeting 
Law in the way alleged. 
 
The Open Meeting Law does not require that a public body allow public participation in 
its meetings, but rather provides that “[n]o person shall address a meeting of a public body 
without permission of the chair, and all persons shall, at the request of the chair, be silent.”  G.L. 
c. 30A § 20(f); see also OML 2021-27; OML 2020-161; OML 2017-189; OML 2014-23; OML 
2012-78.2  We do, however, encourage public bodies to allow for as much public participation as 
time and circumstances permit.  See OML 2015-12; OML 2014-129; OML 2012-59.

The Committee held a meeting on September 15 during which it approved several sets of 
open and executive session meeting minutes; received reports from the Superintendent and 
Student Liaison; reviewed the Town Meeting vote to purchase Becker College; appointed two 
Committee members to the position of Vice Chair and Clerk; approved a Technology 
Coordinator position; discussed pay rates for substitute teachers, the fiscal year 2022 expense 
report, and budget transfers; appointed a school physician; and approved several fundraisers, a 
change to the student handbook and disposal of school furniture.  The Committee then accepted 
comments from the public.  However, when the complainant began to speak about the hiring 
process for an Assistant Superintendent, the Chair stated that topic was not on the notice and 
“will not be recognized for discussion.” 
 
The Open Meeting Law permits the Chair of the Committee to establish parameters for 
public participation.  See OML 2013-2; OML 2012-23. Therefore, because it is within the 
Chair’s discretion to limit public participation during a meeting, the Chair did not violate 
the Open Meeting Law by preventing the complainant from addressing the Committee during the 
September 15 meeting.  See OML 2020-114; OML 2017-189; OML 2014-23; OML 2012-78.  
 
 For the reasons stated above, we find that the Committee did not violate the Open 
Meeting Law.  We now consider the complaint addressed by this determination to be resolved. 
This determination does not address any other complaints that may be pending with our office or 
the Committee.  Please feel free to contact the Division at (617) 963-2540 if you have any 
questions.
