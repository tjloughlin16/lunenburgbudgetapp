This office received a complaint from Andrew Spath on August 27, 2025, alleging that 
the Pembroke Historical Commission (the “Commission”) violated the Open Meeting Law, G.L. 
c. 30A, §§ 18-25.  The complaint was originally filed with the Commission on June 30, 2025, 
and you responded to the complaint, on behalf of the Commission, by letter dated July 21, 2025.  
The complaint alleges that the Commission failed to create and maintain minutes of meetings 
held on August 20, 2020, and September 17, 2020.
1   
 
Following our review, we find that the Commission violated the Open Meeting Law in 
the way alleged.  In reaching this determination, we reviewed the original complaint, the

The Open Meeting Law requires that a public body “create and maintain accurate 
minutes of all meetings, including executive sessions, setting forth the date, time and place, the 
members present or absent, a summary of the discussions on each subject, a list of documents 
and other exhibits used at the meeting, the decisions made and the actions taken at each meeting, 
including the record of all votes.”  G.L. c. 30A, § 22(a).  Minutes of all open sessions must be 
created and approved in a timely manner.  G.L. c. 30A, § 22(c).  “Timely manner” means 
“within the next three public body meetings or within 30 days, whichever is later, unless the 
public body can show good cause for further delay.”  See 940 CMR 29.11; OML 2018-48.
2  
Whenever possible, we recommend that minutes of a meeting be approved at the next meeting.  
See OML 2018-67; OML 2017-133.  
 
The Commission acknowledges, and we find, that it failed to create and timely approve 
minut
es of meetings held on August 20 and September 17, 2020, and thus violated the Open 
Meeting Law.  We understand that the Commission has located draft minutes of the August 20, 
2020, meeting and posted such minutes to the municipal website.  We commend the Commission 
for taking such remedial action.  However, to the extent the Commission has not approved the 
minutes of the August 20, 2020, meeting, we order the Commission to do so within 30 days of 
the date of this letter.  Public body members who did not attend a meeting can approve minutes 
of that meeting; members do not need to attend a meeting to approve the minutes of that meeting.  
See OML 2024-233.  In addition, we remind the Commission that the Open Meeting Law does 
not govern the method for approving meeting minutes and public bodies may choose the method 
for approval.  See OML 2020-81; OML 2019-107; OML 2019-9.  Acceptable methods include, 
but are not limited to, approval by majority vote of the public body during a meeting, approval 
by the public body chair alone or by another designated individual, approval by consent of the 
body (approved unless there is an objection), or approval by a parent public body on behalf of a 
subcommittee.  See OML 2024-25; OML 2019-107; OML 2019-25; OML 2017-34. 
 
With respect to the minutes of the September 17, 2020, meeting, we understand that the 
Comm
ission is unable to locate any draft minutes or video recording of such meeting.  In past 
determinations, when a public body has failed to create minutes of meetings, we have ordered 
that public body to create minutes for all previous meetings to the best of its ability, through 
whatever means are available, including the memories and individual notations of the attendees.  
See OML 2020-160; OML 2018-23; OML 2017-120.  We acknowledge the difficulty in doing so 
here where the September meeting occurred five years ago, and the Commission is currently 
comprised of mostly different members than those who served previously.  Nevertheless, we 
order the Commission to create minutes for the September 17, 2020, meeting using any means 
available to it.  See OML 2021-107 (ordering public body to create meeting minutes for a period 
of 30 years to the extent possible); OML 2020-53 (noting that, although “time ha[d] passed since 
[the] meeting occurred, the Board should add the requisite level of detail to the minutes to the 
best of its ability”); OML 2020-69 (ordering a school committee to create and approve minutes 
for meetings of a subcommittee held over a three-year period).  Although we order the

 reasons stated above, we find that the Commission violated the Open Meeting 
Law by failing to create and maintain meeting minutes.  We order immediate and future 
compliance with the law’s requirements, and we caution that similar future violations could be 
considered evidence of intent to violate the law.  In addition, we order the Commission to create
