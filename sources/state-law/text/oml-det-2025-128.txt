Fall River School Committee (the “Committee”) violated the Open Meeting Law, G.L. c. 30A,

Committee’s response to the complaints, and the complaint filed with our office requesting

10 and June 9, as well as video recordings of the Committee meetings held on May 12 and June

FACTS 
 
We find the facts as follows.  The Committee posted notices for and held meetings on  
March 3, March 10, March 11, March 18, March 19, April 17, April 29, May 12, June 2, and 
June 9.   
 
During a meeting held on May 12, the Committee approved minutes of meetings held on 
March 3 and March 10.  During a meeting held on June 9, the Committee approved minutes of  
meetings held on March 18, March 19, April 8, April 17, April 29, and May 12.    
 
DISCUSSION 
 
The Open Meeting Law requires that a public body “create and maintain accurate 
minutes of all meetings, including executive sessions, setting forth the date, time and place, the 
members present or absent, a summary of the discussions on each subject, a list of documents 
and other exhibits used at the meeting, the decisions made and the actions taken at each meeting, 
including the record of all votes.”  G.L. c. 30A, § 22(a).  The meeting minutes must be reviewed 
and approved in a timely manner.  G.L. c. 30A, § 22(c).  “Timely manner” means “within the 
next three public body meetings or within 30 days, whichever is later, unless the public body can 
show good cause for further delay.”  See 940 CMR 29.11; OML 2018-48.

we recommend that minutes of a meeting be approved at the next meeting.  See OML 2018-67;

The complaints allege that the minutes of meetings held on March 3, March 10, and 
Mar
ch 18 were not approved in a timely manner.  Here, the Committee was required to approve 
the minutes of the meetings held on March 3 and March 10 within 30 days because the 
Committee met at least three times within 30 days following these two meetings; the Committee 
was required to approve the minutes of its March 18 meeting within the next three public body 
meetings as that was the later of the two time periods.  See 940 CMR 29.11; OML 2020-30; 
OML 2018-152; OML 2018-111.  We find that the minutes of the meetings held on March 3 and 
March 10 were not approved in a timely manner where the Committee did not approve the 
minutes until its May 12 meeting, which was between 64 and 71 days thereafter.  Further, we 
find that the minutes of the March 18 meeting were also not approved in a timely manner where 
the Committee did not approve the minutes until its June 9 meeting, which was the sixth meeting 
thereafter.  As such, we find that the Committee violated the Open Meeting Law by failing to 
approve minutes in the timeframe required by the law. 
 
The Committee does not deny that these three sets of minutes were not approved in a 
tim
ely manner but asserts that it had good cause for delay in approving these minutes due to the 
“high volume of meetings within a condensed timeframe.”  We remind the Committee that we 
have consistently explained that neither the Open Meeting Law nor the regulations make any 
distinction between “regular” meetings and other types of meetings for purposes of timely 
approval of minutes.  See OML 2023-129 (no exception for joint meetings with other public 
bodies); OML 2019-115 (no exception for “single topic” meetings or meetings not “normally

In addition, we note that a public body is not required to approve the minutes during a 
meet
ing.  Indeed, we have consistently explained that the Open Meeting Law does not govern 
the method for approving meeting minutes and public bodies may choose the method for 
approval.  See OML 2020-81; OML 2019-107; OML 2019-9.  Acceptable methods include, but 
are not limited to, approval by majority vote of the public body during a meeting, approval by 
the public body chair alone or by another designated individual, approval by consent of the body 
(approved unless there is an objection), or approval by a parent public body on behalf of a 
subcommittee.  See OML 2024-25; OML 2019-107; OML 2019-25; OML 2017-34.   
  
Finally, we warn the Committee that in 2019 and 2022, our office found that the 
Committee
 violated the Open Meeting Law by failing to approve meeting minutes in a timely 
manner.  See OML 2022-184; OML 2019-107.  An intentional violation of the law may be found 
where the public body acted with deliberate ignorance of the law’s requirement or has previously 
been advised that certain conduct violates the Open Meeting Law.  See 940 CMR 29.02.     
 
CONCLUSION 
 
We find that the Committee violated the Open Meeting Law by failing to timely approve 
meeting minutes.  We order immediate and future compliance with the law’s requirements, and 
we caution that similar future violations could be considered evidence of intent to violate the 
law.  Future intentional violations for failure to timely approve meeting minutes may result in a 
recommendation of a civil penalty of up to $1,000 per violation. 
 
We now consider the complaints addressed by this determination to be resolved.  This 
determination does not address any other complaints that may be pending with our office or the 
Committee.  Please feel free to contact our office at (617) 963-2540 if you have any questions
