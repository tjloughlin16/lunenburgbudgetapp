RE: Open Meeting Law Complaint 
Dear Attorney Witten: 
This office received an Open Meeting Law complaint from Robert McNamara, dated July 
11, 2012, alleging that the Marion Zoning Board of Appeals (the "Board") violated the Open 
Meeting Law, G.L. c. 30A, §§ 18-25. The complaint specifically alleges that 1) the Board 
"engaged in ex parte communications with each other regarding the [complainant's] Application, 
both prior to, and after, the May 10, 2012 open meeting;" and 2) the Board "failed to properly 
post notice that it would be deliberating regarding, or voting upon, the Application at its May 10, 
2012 meeting." The complaint was filed with the Board on or about June 8, 2012. The Board 
responded to the complaints by letter dated June 18, 2012. 
Following our review, we find that the Board did not violate the Open Meeting Law. In 
reaching this determination, we reviewed the June 8, 2012 complaint filed with the Board; the 
Board's June 18, 2012 response; and the July 11, 2012 complaint filed with our office. 
Additionally, we reviewed the notice and minutes of the Board's May 10, 2012 meeting. 
Finally, we interviewed by telephone Board members Thomas Cooper and Robert Wedge on 
July 21, 2012, and Assistant Town Clerk Michelle Bissonnette on August 22, 2012. 
FACTS 
Robert McNamara filed an Application for Hearing with the five-member Zoning Board 
of Appeals on March 20, 2012, in which he petitioned the Board to grant him a special permit to 
raze and reconstruct his property in Marion. Shortly thereafter, Board member Thomas Cooper 
visited the property and spoke with Mr. McNamara. There is disagreement between the parties 
as to what occurred during this discussion. Mr. McNamara alleges that Mr. Cooper told him the 
proposed reconstruction was too big, that Board Chair Robert Wedge and the other Board 
members had concerns regarding the application, and that the Board almost always follows Chair 
Wedge's lead. In his interview with our office, Mr. Cooper denied that he made any of these

statements, and denied that he spoke outside of an open meeting with any other Board members 
regarding Mr. McNamara's application. We also interviewed Chair Wedge, and he denied

The Board held a public hearing on Mr. McNamara's application on April 19, 2012, but 
did not take any action. Mr. McNamara states that he was subsequently told by the clerk for the 
Board that the Board would consider his application at its meeting on May 17, 2012. 
The Board posted notice for a May 10, 2012 meeting on May 8, 2012. The notice was

website included notice of a Board meeting at 7:30 PM on May 10, 2012 in the Conference 
Room at Town House. The agenda attached to the calendar entry included a topic listed as 
"Pending Cases," and under that heading, "Case #657 McNamara, 19 Shawondasse Road 
(Special permit request taken under advisement on 4/19/12)." During the meeting, the Board 
discussed Mr. McNamara's application and voted four to zero to deny the special permit, with 
Chair Wedge abstaining because he was not present during the April 19, 2012 hearing. 
Mr. McNamara was not present during the May 10, 2012 meeting. He received 
notification of the Board's decision regarding his application by letter on May 22, 2012. 
DISCUSSION 
1. We Do Not Find Sufficient Evidence that the Board Deliberated Regarding Mr.  
McNamara's Application Outside of a Noticed Meeting. 
In his complaint, Mr. McNamara alleges that the Board discussed his Application for 
Hearing outside of a meeting. The Open Meeting Law requires that a public body's deliberations 
occur during a meeting. See G.L. c. 30A, § 18. A "deliberation" is defined, in relevant part, as 
"an oral or written communication through any medium, including electronic mail, between or 
among a quorum of a public body on any public business within its jurisdiction." Id. A 
"quorum" is defined as "a simple majority of the members of the public body, unless otherwise 
provided in a general or special law, executive order or other authorizing provision." Id. The 
Board consists of five members; therefore three members constitute a quorum. 
Mr. McNamara claims that Board member Thomas Cooper told him that the Board 
discussed Mr. McNamara's application, and that the Board decided that the proposed 
reconstruction was too big. Additionally, Mr. McNamara claims that Mr. Cooper told him that 
Board Chair Robert Wedge and the other Board members had concerns regarding the 
application, and that the Board almost always follows Chair Wedge's lead. These statements, if 
made, suggest conduct that would be improper deliberation outside of an open meeting. 
However, Mr. Cooper denies making these statements, and he and Chair Wedge both deny 
communicating with any other Board members regarding Mr. McNamara's application, outside 
of a noticed Board meeting. In the absence of contemporaneous evidence supporting Mr. 
McNamara's version of events, we do not find sufficient evidence that the Board violated the

2. The Board Posted a Complete and Timely Notice for its May 10, 2012 Meeting. 
In his complaint, Mr. McNamara also alleges that "the Board failed to properly post 
notice that it would be deliberating regarding, or voting upon, the Application at its May 10, 
2012 meeting." The Open Meeting Law requires that public bodies post notice 48 hours in 
advance of a meeting and include the "date, time and place of such meeting and a listing of 
topics that the chair reasonably anticipates will be discussed at the meeting." G.L. c. 30A, § 
20(b). For local public bodies, the notice must be posted "in a manner conspicuously visible to 
the public at all hours in or on the municipal building in which the clerk's office is located." 
G.L. c. 30A, § 20(c). The Attorney General's regulations provide that municipalities may 
choose to designate an alternative notice posting method, however, including posting notice to a 
website. 940 CMR 29.03(2)(b). A municipality that chooses an alternative method to post its 
meeting notices must notify the Attorney General of the method and the posting location. 940 
CMR 29.03(1)(c). The Town of Marion notified our office on October 22, 2010 that its meeting 
notice posting location would be its website, www.marionma.gov. 
The Board submitted the notice for its May 10, 2012 meeting to the Town Clerk's office 
on May 8, 2012. The Clerk's office stamped the agenda at 12:19 PM. According to Assistant 
Town Clerk Michelle Bissonnette, the Clerk's office routinely uploads meeting notices to the 
Town's online calendar within minutes of stamping a notice. The calendar page for May 10, 
2012 lists a Board meeting for 7:30 PM, and lists the location as "Conference Room, Town 
House." A link on the page opens the agenda, which includes "Case #657 McNamara, 19 
Shawondasse Road (Special permit request taken under advisement on 4/19/12)." After 
speaking with Assistant Town Clerk Bissonnette, we believe that the notice was timely posted to 
the website, and included all of the elements required by G.L. c. 30A, § 20(b).3  
CONCLUSION 
For the reasons stated above, we find that the Board did not violate the Open Meeting 
Law. We appreciate the patience of the parties while we reviewed this complaint, and now 
consider this matter closed.
