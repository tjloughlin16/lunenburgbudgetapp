DISCUSSIO N 
1. We Decline to Review A lleg atio ns o f Violations that Occurred Prior to July L 2010. 
Our o ffic e assumed authority for the interpretation and enforcement of the Open Meetin g 
Law, a s it applies to local pub lic bodies, on July 1, 2010. See G .L. c. 3 0A, § 23(a); see also G .L. 
c. 3 0A, §§ 18-25, gen erally. Prior to Ju ly 1, 2 010, the law gov erning meetin gs of local pub lic 
bodies, like the Board, was G.L. c. 39, § 23B (now repealed), and the District Attorneys were 
respon sible for its enforcem ent. One of the practic al effects of th is c hange is that our office has 
authority to enfo rce the Open Meetin g L aw, G.L . c. 30 A, §§ 18-25, only with regard to 
violations that o ccu rred o n or after July 1, 20 10. See O ML Declinatio n 6-3-11. Therefore, we 
will not review alleged violations that o ccurred prior to July 1, 2010, ev en though they m ay have 
been discovered only recently. 
Wh ile we do not review Mr. Yo ung's alle gations that the Boa rd violated the Open 
Meetin g Law by conducting employee performance evaluations and app roving non -union 
person nel con tracts in exec utive session during its February 10, 200 9; August 11, 2009; January 
12, 2010 meetin gs, we note that the current O pen Meetin g Law requires that pub lic bod ies 
conduc t employee evaluations and vote to approve non-union personnel con tracts in o pen 
session. See G.L. c. 3 0A, §§ 21(a)(1), 21(a)(2); OM L 2011-24; OM L 2 011 -56. Furtherm ore, 
alth oug h we decline to review these allegations, three or more registered voters may pursue this 
matter in Superior Court. See G.L. c. 30A, § 23(f).

2. The Boa rd Condu cted Discussions in Execu tive Session that w ere not Appropriate for

The O pen Me etin g L aw permits p ublic bod ies to e nter execu tive session to conduc t

preparation for neg otiations w ith non union personnel or to conduct collective bargaining 
sessions or contract neg otiations w ith non union perso nnel." G.L. c. 30 A, § 21(a)(2) ("P urp ose 
2"). Another purpose fo r executiv e session is to "discuss strategy with respect to collective 
bargaining or litigatio n if an open m eeting may have a detrimental effect on the bargaining or 
litigating position of th e pub lic body and the chair so declares." G.L . c. BOA, § 21(a)(3) 
("P urpose 3"). 
Throu gho ut 2 010 and 2011, the Board app ears to have regularly held discussions in 
execu tive session on top ics that were not app ropriate for exe cutive session. The B oard

exa mple, the Board entered into executive session on September 14, 201 0 and, am ongst other

3. The B oard Failed to Provide the Pub lic with S ufficient Detail abo ut the Purpose for its 
Execu tive Session s. 
Before en tering into execu tive session, th e Chair of a pub lic body must "state the purpose 
for the execu tive se ssion, statin g all subjects that m ay be revealed without comprom isin g the 
purpose for w hich the exe cutive session was called." G.L . c. 30A , § 21(b)(3). This inform atio n 
should also be included in the meeting notice and recorded in the m eeting minutes. See id.; G.L. 
c. 30A, § 22(a); 94 0 C MR 29 .03(l)( b). For many of the exe cutive sessions we reviewed, the 
min utes list the purpose for the exe cutive s ession only as "dis cussing strategic initiatives and 
other legal iss ues." Th is statement is inadeq uate to info rm the public of th e purpose fo r the 
execu tive session. First, there is no exec utive session purpose that covers general "strateg ic 
initiatives." See G . L. c. 3OA, § 21(a). Secon d, "other legal issues" d oes not provide su fficient 
detail about top ics that m ay be a pprop riate for exe cutive session und er Purpo se 3, which allo ws a 
public bod y to "disc uss strategy with respect to c ollective bargaining or litigation if an o pen 
meeting m ay hav e a detrimental effect on the bargaining or litigatin g position of the public body 
and the chair so d eclar es." G.L. c. 30A, § 21(a)(3). The Board should hav e publicly state d and 
recorded in the minutes the specific litigation matter that it planned to discuss, if doing so would 
not have compromised the purpose fo r the execu tive session. See G.L. c. 3OA, § 21(b)(3). 
Additionally, we note that in several exe cutive sessions, the Board discussed negotiatio ns 
with a union. While this is appropriate for execu tive session under Purpose 3, the Board failed to 
list in the minutes that it was m eeting in exec utive session to negotiate a union con tract, and 
failed to pub licly identify the union w ith which it w as negotia ting. See G .L. c. 30A , §§ 21(a)(3), 
21(b)(3). For all of the meetin gs where the Board discussed health insurance neg otia tions with 
an employee union, the B oard should hav e publicly stated the statutory purpo se and identified 
the union with which it w as negotia ting before entering into exe cutive session. 
4. The B oard Failed to Approve a N on-Union Person nel Contra ct in Open Session. 
A public body must v ote in o pen session to approve the final terms of a n on-union 
personnel agreem ent before executing the contract. G.L . c. 30A , § 21(a)(2); OML 2011-56 ; 
OML 2 011-4 4. While a public body may agree on terms with individua l non -union personnel in 
executive session, the final vote to app rove or exec ute such agreements m ust be taken by the 
public body in open session. See O ML 201 1-56; OM L 2 011 -44; O ML 2011-28 . The Board 
entered into exec utive session on January 11, 2011 for the purpose of "d isc ussin g contract and 
legal iss ues." In the exe cutive session, the Board voted to give M r. G ranahan a 5% raise 
effective January 1, 2011. The vote passed four to one , an d w as not conducted by roll-call vote. 
The Board also agreed, without a vote, to c ompen sate Mr. Granahan in line with a new ly 
negotiated health insurance ag reement for union employees. Any cha nge to a non-union 
personnel con tract, suc h as an increase in salary, must a lso be ap proved in open session. See 
G.L. c. 30A, § 21(a)(2); O ML 201 1-56; OM L 201 1-44. Bec ause the Board failed to take these 
votes in open s ession , it violated th e O pen Meetin g Law. Additionally , the B oard violate d the 
Open Me etin g L aw by failing to record these votes as roll-call votes. See G .L. c. 3 0A, § 2 2(b ). 
While the Attorney General has authority to nullify in whole or in part any actio n taken 
imp roperly in execu tive session, we understand that the General Manag er is no longer employed 
by the D epa rtm ent, thus we decline to invalid ate the votes on his c ompensa tion. See G.L. c. 
3OA, § 23(c)(3).

5. The B oard Failed to Release its Execu tive Session Minutes After the Purpose for Non-
Disclosu re had Expired. 
A public body sub ject to the Open Meeting Law must create and appro ve accurate 
minutes of all meetings, including exe cutive sessions, in a timely manner. G.L. c. 3 OA, §§ 22(a), 
22(c). The public bod y, or its chair or designee, shall, at reasonable intervals, review the min utes 
of executive session s to determ ine if continued non-disclosure is warranted. G.L . c. 30A, § 
22(g)(1). It does not appear that the Board too k any action to review the minutes of its executive 
sessions to determ ine if th e executive session purpos es h ad exp ired u ntil it co ndu cted a mass 
review of execu tive session minutes in March 201 2. Public bodies must periodically review 
minutes of all execu tive sessions that occurred after July 1, 2010 and , at the very least, review 
such m inutes any tim e there is reason to believe the executive session p urpose has expired (such 
as upon resolution of a legal matter, or conclusion of a hiring search). Here, it appe ars that the 
execu tive session purposes requiring con fidentiality with respect to non-u nion personn el 
con tracts, as well as some union neg otia tions and litigation matters, m ay hav e expired. The 
Board therefore violate d the Open Meeting Law by waiting until March 201 2 to review and 
pub licly release minutes that no lo nger required confidentiality. 
CO NCLUSIO N 
We find that th e Board v iola ted the Open Meetin g Law by conducting discussions in

personnel contracts in open session; and failing to periodically rev iew and publicly release 
certain executive session min utes. 
We order the Boa rd's imm ediate and future com plia nce with the Open Meeting Law , and 
caution that fu ture similar violations may be c onsidered intentional v iolations of the Law.
