RE: Open Meeting Law Complaint 
Dear Attorney Mullen: 
This office received a complaint from Mr. Daniel Lieber dated September 19, 2012, 
alleging that the Wakefield Board of Selectmen (the "Board") violated the Open Meeting Law, 
G.L. c. 30A, §§ 18-25. The complaint was originally filed with the Board on or about July 24, 
2012, and you responded, on behalf of the Board, to the original complaint by letter dated July 
27, 2012.1  In his complaint, Mr. Lieber alleges that the Board discussed a topic during its June 
25, 2012 meeting that was not listed on its meeting notice. 
We reviewed the July 24, 2012 complaint; your July 27, 2012 response to the complaint; 
the September 19, 2012 complaint filed with our office, requesting further review; and your letter 
to our office dated October 15, 2012.2  We also reviewed the notice and minutes for the June 25, 
2012 meeting. Finally, we spoke with Chair Betsy Sheeran by telephone. 
Following our review, we find that the Board did not violate the Open Meeting Law by 
discussing a topic during its meeting that was not included in the meeting notice because the 
Chair did not reasonably anticipate that discussion on this topic would occur. 
In his September 19, 2012 complaint filed with our office, Mr. Lieber additionally alleges that the Board did not 
review, discuss or respond to this complaint. While we decline to review the additional allegation because it is 
beyond the scope of the original complaint filed with the public body, we remind the Board that, while a public body 
may authorize an individual such as the Town Administrator or Town Counsel to respond on the public body's 
behalf, the Open Meeting Law and the Attorney General's regulations require that the Board first review the Open 
Meeting Law complaint. See  G.L. c. 30A, § 23(b); 940 CMR 29.05(5).

FACTS 
Based upon our review of the material listed above, the facts are as follows. The Board 
voted to install "No Dogs Allowed" signs around the World War II Memorial in Town during its 
May 30, 2012 meeting. During its next meeting, on June 11, 2012, several residents asked the 
Board to reconsider the vote, thus Chair Betsy Sheeran asked Town Administrator Stephen Maio 
to look into the matter and report back to the Board at some point in the future. On Tuesday 
June 19, 2012, the Board posted notice of its upcoming meeting, which was scheduled for 
Monday, June 25, 2012. 
On either June 23, 2012 or June 24, 2012,3  Phyllis Hull, who was chair of the committee 
that renovated the Memorial, approached Mr. Maio to inform him that she planned to attend the 
next Board meeting to discuss dog restrictions at the site. Ms. Hull and Mr. Maio agreed on a 
resolution to the issue, which Mr. Maio then brought before the Board during its June 25, 2012 
open meeting. The Board voted at that meeting to approve the proposed signs and a fence. 
During a telephone conversation with this office, Chair Sheeran stated she did not anticipate 
discussion of this topic until Mr. Maio brought it up at the meeting. 
DISCUSSION 
The Open Meeting Law requires that a public body "post notice of every meeting at least 
48 hours prior to such meeting, excluding Saturdays, Sundays and legal holidays," and include in 
that notice "the date, time and place of such meeting and a listing of topics that the chair 
reasonably anticipates will be discussed at the meeting." G.L. c. 30A, § 20(b). A public body 
may consider a topic at a meeting that was not listed in the meeting notice if it is a topic that the 
chair did not reasonably anticipate at least 48 hours before a meeting. See  OML 2011-36; OML 
2011-23; OML 2011-13.4  However, public bodies are encouraged not to discuss or vote on 
items that arise within that 48-hour period or during a meeting if the topic can be postponed until 
a later date and added to a properly posted meeting notice. OML 2011-13. 
Since the discussion between Mr. Maio and Ms. Hull occurred over the weekend before 
the Monday meeting, Chair Sheeran could not have reasonably anticipated, at least 48 hours 
before the meeting, excluding Saturdays and Sundays, that this would be a topic of discussion. 
Accordingly, the Board was not required to list the topic in the meeting notice. See  G.L. c. 30A, 
§ 20(b); OML 2011-36; OML 2011-23; OML 2011-13. We therefore find that the Board did not 
violate the Open Meeting Law by discussing the matter during its June 25, 2012 meeting.

CONCLUSION 
For the reasons stated above, we find that the Board did not violate the Open Meeting 
Law. We now consider the complaint addressed by this determination to be resolved. This 
determination does not address any other complaints that may be pending with our office or the 
Board. Please feel free to contact our office at (617) 963-2540 if you have any questions or 
believe any facts in this letter to be inaccurate.
