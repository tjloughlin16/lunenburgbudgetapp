Dear Ms. VanderClock: 
This office received a complaint from Harold Meyers on November 4, 2015, alleging 
that the Weston Housing Production Plan Steering Committee (the Committee) violated the 
Open Meeting Law, G.L. c. 30A, §§ 18-25. The complaint was originally filed with the 
Committee on or about October 6, 2015 and you responded, on behalf of the Committee, to 
the complaint by letter dated November 2, 2015.1 The complaint alleges that the Committee 
failed to "document attendance, subjects acting upon, and votes and other official actions" 
from Committee meetings held on April 21, 2015; April 27, 2015; June 18, 2015; September 
19, 2015; and September 24, 2015, and that the Committee failed to post minutes of these 
same meetings to the Town website. Additionally, the complaint alleges that the Committee 
failed to post to the Town website "agenda or minutes for [meetings held in] July and August 
and perhaps other meetings." 
Following our review, we find that the Committee violated the Open Meeting Law by 
failing to create and approve certain meeting minutes in a timely manner. The Committee did 
not violate the law in the other ways alleged. In reaching a determination, we reviewed the 
original complaint, the Committee's response to the complaint, and the complaint filed with 
our office requesting further review. We also spoke by telephone with Ms. VanderClock in 
November and December 2015.

FACTS 
We find the facts as follows. The Town of Weston posts to its website all meeting 
notices, as well as all minutes that have been approved by its public bodies. 
Meeting Notices 
On June 15, 2015 the Town Clerk posted notices of Committee meetings to be heid on 
the following dates: July 2, 201 5; July 16, 2015; July 30, 2015; August 13, 2015; and August 
27, 2015, On July 15, 2015, the Town Clerk posted notice of a July 25, 2015 Committee 
meeting. 
Meeting Minutes 
The Committee has acknowledged that, at the time of the filing of this complaint, it 
had not yet created minutes for its meetings held on July 2, 2015 and August 13, 2015. 
Additionally, the Committee had draft minutes for the other meetings at issue (June 18, 2015; 
July 16, 2015; July 25, 2015; September 19, 2015; and September 24, 2015), although it had 
not yet approved them. The Committee contends that this was due to the resignation of its 
minutes-taker. 
The Committee's April 21, 2015 and April 27, 2015 meeting minutes were approved 
on May 7, 2015; the July 30, 2015 meeting minutes were approved on August 13, 2015; and 
the August 27, 2015 meeting minutes were approved on September 10, 2015. On November 
19, 2015 the Committee approved minutes from meetings held on July 2, 2015; July 16, 2015; 
July 25, 2015; September 19, 2015; and September 24, 2015. As of the date of this letter, the 
Committee still had not approved its June 18, 2015 and August 13, 2015 minutes; however it 
posted a draft version of the August 13, 2015 meeting minutes to the Town website. The 
Committee was disbanded in December 2015. 
DISCUSSION 
The Open Meeting Law requires public bodies to "create and maintain accurate 
minutes of all meetings, including executive sessions." G.L. c. 30A, § 22(a). These meeting 
minutes must be reviewed and approved in a timely manner. G.L. c. 30A, § 22(c). Although 
the Open Meeting Law does not define "timely manner," we recommend that minutes of a 
meeting be approved at the next meeting, whenever possible. See OML 2014-15; OML 2012-
91.3 The Law does not require that minutes be published to a website, however. See G.L. c. 
30A, § 22; OML 2013-117.

Meeting Notices 
First, the complaint alleges that the Committee failed to post notice of its meetings 
held in July and August 2015. The Law requires a public body to post notice 48 hours in 
advance of a meeting. G.L. c. 30A, § 20(b). We find that the Committee complied with the 
Law by posting notice of these meetings at least 48 hours in advance of each meeting. G.L. c. 
30A, § 20(b). Accordingly, we find no violation with respect to this allegation. 
Meeting Minutes 
Second, the complaint alleges that the Committee failed to create and post to the Town 
website minutes of certain meetings held in 2015.4 Although the Open Meeting Law does not 
require it, the Town has a policy of posting all approved minutes to the Town website. At the 
time this complaint was filed, the Committee had drafted minutes of all these meeting dates, 
except for the meetings held on July 2, 2015 and August 13, 2015, which were not drafted 
until after the filing of this complaint. Accordingly, we find that the Committee violated the 
Open Meeting Law by failing to create minutes from its July 2, 2015 and August 13, 2015 
meetings in a timely manner. See OML 2013-37. 
The remaining minutes at issue did exist at the time the complaint was filed, but had 
not been posted to the Town website because they were not yet approved. The Committee 
timely approved its April 21, 2015; April 27, 2015; July 30, 2015; and August 27, 2015 
meeting minutes by approving them at the next meeting. However, the Committee did not 
approve minutes from meetings held on July 2, 2015; July 16, 2015; July 25, 2015; September 
19, 2015; and September 24, 2015 until several months later, on November 19, 2015. Finally, 
the Committee has still not approved minutes from its June 18, 2015 and August 13, 2015 
meetings. We therefore find that the Committee also violated the Open Meeting Law by 
failing to timely approve minutes from these Committee meetings. We order the Committee 
to approve the June 18, 2015 and August 13, 2015 meeting minutes within thirty (30) days of 
receipt of this letter. Because the Committee was recently disbanded, the parent body that 
created the Committee may approve these minutes on its behalf. 
While minutes must be created and approved in a timely manner, the Law does not 
require that minutes be published to a website. See G.L. c. 3OA, § 22; OML 2013-117. 
Therefore, the Committee did not violate the Open Meeting Law by failing to post its minutes, 
whether in draft or final form, to the Town website. 
CONCLUSION 
For the reasons stated above, we find that the Committee violated the Open Meeting 
Law by failing to create and approve certain meeting minutes in a timely manner. The 
Committee did not violate the law in the other ways alleged. We order immediate and future 
compliance with the Open Meeting Law, and caution that future similar conduct may be
