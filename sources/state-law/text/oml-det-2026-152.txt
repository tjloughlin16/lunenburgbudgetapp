DISCUSSION 
 
The Open Meeting Law requires a public body to “create and maintain accurate minutes 
of all meetings, including executive sessions.” G.L. c. 30A, § 22(a). Meeting minutes must set 
forth “the date, time and place, the members present or absent, a summary of the discussions on 
each subject, a list of documents and other exhibits used at the meeting, the decisions made and 
the actions taken at each meeting, including the record of all votes.” Id. When reviewing minutes 
for compliance with the Open Meeting Law, we look for substantial compliance with the 
accuracy requirement. See OML 2017-104; OML 2013-64.
5 By substantial compliance, we mean 
that the minutes should contain enough detail and accuracy so that a member of the public who 
did not attend the meeting could read the minutes and have a clear understanding of what 
occurred. See OML 2019-145; OML 2012-106. Although a transcript of the discussions is not 
required, and the minutes need not include every remark or opinion presented, minutes must 
include a substantive summary of the discussion on each topic. See OML 2020-18; OML 2012-
29; OML 2011-55. Minutes must include more than a statement that a public body held a 
discussion about a specified topic; the Law requires that the minutes summarize the substance of 
the discussion. See OML 2023-11 (finding that “[s]imply attaching a report does not meet the 
Board’s obligation to summarize the discussion in its minutes”); OML 2020-53 (finding 
insufficient minutes that “merely consist of a list of motions made, votes taken, and, at some 
points, a mention that a discussion occurred, without summarizing the discussion”); OML 2019-
167; OML 2018-8. 
 
When summarizing discussions, we encourage public bodies to include dissenting or 
minority opinions whenever possible. Where significant debate on a matter occurs and one or 
more members of the body or the public disagree with the decision ultimately taken by the public 
body, public bodies should note the opposing viewpoints in the meeting minutes. Additionally, 
while it is not necessary to record the comments of every speaker, and we acknowledge the 
difficulty of doing so at times, if a particular individual speaks at some length or is the only one 
to offer an argument for or against a proposal, that person and their comments should be 
identified in the minutes. See OML 2019-167; OML 2012-29. 
 
After reviewing the minutes and video recordings of the Board’s meetings held on June 
3, June 17, and July 1, we find the minutes for each meeting insufficient. Although the Board’s 
meetings primarily consist of motions being read into the record and voted on with little to no 
discussion, at each meeting there were instances when discussions occurred or presentations 
were made. However, the minutes tend not to summarize these discussions and presentations and 
instead simply note that a discussion or a presentation occurred. This is insufficient to comply 
with the requirements of the Open Meeting Law. We find it particularly concerning that the 
minutes of the June 17 meeting do not include Board member Sarah Corbitt’s explanation of 
why she voted against approval of a business’s license during the Board’s previous meeting and 
that the minutes of the July 1 meeting do not include Ms. Corbitt’s comment that she was voting 
against approval of the June 17 minutes because they did not include her comment.

With respect to the allegation that the minutes at issue do not include a list of documents 
and exhibits used at the meetings, we find no violation where each set of minutes includes a list 
of documents purportedly used at the meeting and the complaint does not identify any particular 
document alleged to have been used but not listed.  
 
CONCLUSION 
 
For the reasons stated above, we find that the Board violated the Open Meeting Law by 
creating and approving insufficient minutes for its meetings held on June 3, June 17, and July 1. 
We order the Board’s immediate and future compliance with the Open Meeting Law and caution 
that similar future violations may be considered evidence of an intent to violate the Law. 
Furthermore, we order the Board, within 60 days of the date of this letter, to amend the minutes 
of its meetings held on June 3, June 17, and July 1 to include sufficient detail in compliance with 
the requirements described above. 
 
We now consider the complaint addressed by this determination to be resolved. This 
determination does not address any other complaints that may be pending with the Board or with 
our office. Please feel free to contact our office at (617) 963-2540 if you have any questions 
regarding this letter.
