This office received a complaint, dated May 3, 2012, from Katina Caraganis of the 
Sentinel and Enterprise, alleging a violation of the Open Meeting Law, G.L. c. 30A, §§ 18-25, by 
the Lunenburg Board of Selectmen (the "Board") and the Lunenburg Conservation Commission 
(the "Commission"). The complaint alleges that the Board failed to post notice of its April 17, 
2012 meeting, and the Commission failed to post notice of its April 18, 2012 meeting, 48 hours 
in advance of each meeting in a manner visible to the public at all hours. The complaint was 
originally filed with the Board and the Commission on or around April 24, 2012. We understand 
that you responded directly to Ms. Caraganis, on behalf of the Board and the Commission, in a 
letter dated April 29, 2012. However, you did not forward a copy of your response to our office, 
as required by the Open Meeting Law.' See G.L. c. 30A, § 23(b). 
We reviewed the April 24, 2012 and May 3, 2012 complaints; the April 29, 2012 
response; the notice for the Board's April 17, 2012 meeting, and the notice for the Commission's 
April 18, 2012 meeting. We also conducted telephone interviews with the complainant on June 
26, 2012; with Town Clerk Kathryn Herrick on June 27, 2012; and with Town Manager Kerry 
Speidel2  on July 2, 2012. Because we find no evidence that the Board or the Commission failed 
to properly post notice of their meetings, we do not find that the Board or the Commission 
violated the Open Meeting Law.

FACTS 
The Town of Lunenburg posts notices for meetings of its public bodies outside of Town 
Hall in a stand-alone glass case. The Town also posts notices inside Town Hall, and on its 
website. 
Notices for the Board's April 17, 2012 meeting and the Commission's April 18, 2012 
meeting were submitted to the Town Clerk for posting on the afternoon of Thursday, April 12, 
2012. Both notices were date stamped by the Town Clerk on that date. It is standard practice for 
the Town Clerk to post notices inside Town Hall as soon as they are received, and to post the 
notices on the bulletin board outside of Town Hall by the end of the same day. Typically, the 
Town Clerk employs the help of another individual to hold open the glass case as she posts 
notices on the outdoor bulletin board. 
On Friday, April 13, 2012 at 5:00 p.m., the complainant went to the Town Hall to view 
the notices for the upcoming week's meetings. According to the complainant, at that time there 
were no meeting notices for the Board's April 17, 2012 meeting or the Commission's April 18, 
2012 meeting posted on the bulletin board outside of Town Hall. The complainant stated that 
she was unable to enter Town Hall to check whether the notices were posted inside because it 
had closed at 12:00 p.m. that day. 
The complainant contacted the Town Manager to alert him that the notices were not 
posted. In response, the Town Manager contacted the Town Clerk to inquire whether the notices 
had been posted. The Town Clerk did not personally recall when the notices were posted, but 
spoke with the individual who assisted her with posting the meeting notices. That individual 
confirmed that notice for the Board's April 17, 2012 meeting and notice for the Commission's 
April 18, 2012 meeting were posted on Thursday, April 12, 2012. Confident that the notices 
were timely posted, the Town Manager then advised the Board and the Commission to hold their 
meetings that week. 
DISCUSSION 
The Open Meeting Law states that "[e]xcept in an emergency.  . . . a public body shall post 
notice of every meeting at least 48 hours prior to such meeting, excluding Saturdays, Sundays 
and legal holidays." G.L. c. 30A, § 20(b). A local public body must file notice of its meetings 
with the municipal clerk, and such notice must be "posted in a manner conspicuously visible to 
the public at all hours in or on the municipal building in which the clerk's office is located." 
G.L. c. 30A, § 20(c). As an alternative, a municipality may adopt its website as its primary 
meeting notice posting method. 940 CMR 29.03(2)(b). Municipalities are required to notify the 
Attorney General of the adoption of such alternative posting method, including the website 
address. 940 CMR 29.03(1)(c). 
The Town of Lunenburg has not notified this office of the adoption of an alternative 
meeting notice posting method. Additionally, Monday, April 16, 2012 was Patriot's Day, a legal 
holiday in Massachusetts. Therefore, to be considered timely posted, the Board's April 17, 2012 
meeting had to be posted on the bulletin board outside of Town Hall by Thursday, April 12, 2012

at 7:00 p.m., and the Commission's April 18, 2012 meeting had to be posted in the same manner 
by Friday, April 13, 2012 at 7:00 p.m. 
There is disagreement between the parties about when the notices for the Board and 
Commission meetings were posted on the bulletin board outside of Town Hall. The notices were 
received and date stamped by the Town Clerk on April 12, 2012 and, according to the Town 
Manager, it was standard practice at that time for the Town Clerk to post the notices on the 
outside bulletin board the day they were received. While the Town Clerk did not specifically 
recall when the notices were posted, the individual who assisted her with posting the notices 
recalled posting them on Thursday, April 12, 2012. Because the complainant has not presented 
our office with any evidence to support her competing assertion that the notices were not 
properly posted, we credit the Town's assertion that the standard procedure was followed, and 
that notices for the Board's April 17, 2012 meeting and the Commission's April 18, 2012 
meeting were properly posted. 
CONCLUSION 
Because we find no evidence that the Board or the Commission failed to properly post 
notice of their meetings, we do not find that the Board or the Commission violated the Open 
Meeting Law. We now consider this matter closed. Please contact our office at 617-963-2540 if 
you have any questions regarding this letter.
