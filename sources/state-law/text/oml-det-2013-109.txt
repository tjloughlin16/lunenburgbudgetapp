RE: Open Meeting Law Complaint 
Dear Attorney Morrison; 
This office received an Open Meeting Law complaint from Mr. Patrick Galvin, dated 
October 16, 2012, alleging that the Boston Redevelopment Authority Board of Directors (the 
"BRA") violated the Open Meeting Law, G.L. c. 30A, §§ 18-25. The complaint alleges that the 
BRA failed to give proper notice of a July 12, 2012 meeting and failed to properly open and 
close the meeting.1 The complaint was filed with the BRA on or about August 10, 2012. The 
BRA responded by letter dated September 7, 2012, following an extension granted by our office. 
Following our review, we find that the BRA did not violate the Open Meeting Law. In 
reaching that determination, we reviewed the August 10, 2012 complaint filed with the BRA; the 
BRA's September 7, 2012 response; and the October 16, 2012 complaint filed with our office. 
Additionally, we reviewed the notice and minutes of the BRA's July 12, 2012 meeting. 
FACTS 
On Tuesday, July 10, 2012, at 3:49 PM, the City Clerk posted notice to the City of 
Boston's website for a 5:30 PM meeting on Thursday, July 12, 2012. The time and date stamped 
notice was also posted to the bulletin board on the first floor of City Hall at the Congress Street 
entrance, which is accessible at all hours. The notice listed 23 topics for discussion, including

DISCUSSION 
The Open Meeting Law, G.L. c. 30A, § 20(b), states in relevant part that, "[ejxcept in an 
emergency, in addition to any notice otherwise required by law, a public body shall post notice 
of every meeting at least 48 hours prior to such meeting, excluding Saturdays, Sundays and legal 
holidays... Notice shall be printed in a legible, easily understandable format and shall contain the 
date, time and place of such meeting and a listing of topics that the chair reasonably anticipates 
will be discussed at the meeting." Notices for meetings of local public bodies, such as the BRA, 
must be filed with the municipal clerk and posted in a manner conspicuously visible to the public 
at all hours in or on the municipal building in which the clerk's office is located. G.L. c. 3OA, § 
20(c). Municipalities may also adopt their municipal website as an alternative posting method. 
940 CMR 29.03. 
The complaint alleges that the BRA failed to give proper notice of its July 12, 2012 
meeting. The BRA filed notice with the City Clerk for its Thursday, July 12, 2012, 5:30 PM 
meeting, and the City Clerk posted the notice to the City of Boston's website on Tuesday, July 
10, 2012, at 3:49 PM, more than 48 hours before the meeting. The time and date stamped notice 
was also posted to the bulletin board on the first floor of City Hall at the Congress Street 
entrance, which is accessible at all hours. The notice listed 23 topics for discussion, including a 
detailed topic regarding the issue that appears to be the subject of this complaint. Therefore, we 
find that the BRA properly posted its meeting notice and did not violate the Open Meeting Law. 
The complaint also alleges that the BRA failed to properly open and close the meeting. 
The minutes from the meeting state that the BRA Chair opened the meeting on July 12, 2012, 
and the BRA voted to adjourn that evening. The complainant does not specify why he believes 
this action was insufficient. Additionally, the Open Meeting Law does not require that public 
bodies follow any specific procedure to open or close a meeting. We therefore find no violation 
of the Open Meeting Law. 
CONCLUSION 
For the reasons stated above, we find that the BRA did not violate the Open Meeting 
Law.
