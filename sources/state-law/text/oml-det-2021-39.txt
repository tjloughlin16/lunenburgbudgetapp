 RE: Open Meeting Law Complaint 
Dear Attorney Howayeck: 
This Office received a complaint from Collin Dias on September 21, 2020, alleging that 
the Fall River City Council Subcommittee of Health and Environmental Affairs (the 
“Subcommittee”) violated the Open Meeting Law, G.L. c. 30A, §§ 18-25. The complaint was 
originally filed with the Subcommittee on August 20, and the Subcommittee responded by letter 
dated September 9.1 In his complaint, Mr. Dias alleges that the Subcommittee failed to provide 
sufficient detail on the meeting notice for an August 19 meeting and failed to properly announce 
that the August 19 meeting was being recorded. 
Following our review, we resolve this complaint by informal action in accordance with 
940 C.M.R. 29.07(2)(a) and find that the Subcommittee did not violate the Open Meeting Law as 
alleged. The Attorney General’s Open Meeting Law regulations require notices to contain “a 
listing of topics that the chair reasonably anticipates will be discussed at the meeting. The list of 
topics shall have sufficient specificity to reasonably advise the public of the issues to be 
discussed at the meeting.” See 940 CMR29.03(1)(b). We generally consider a topic to include 
sufficient specificity when a reasonable member of the public could read the topic and 
understand the anticipated nature of the public body’s discussion. See OML 2019-70; OML 
2019-77.2

Here, on the agenda for an August 19 meeting, the Subcommittee listed an agenda item 
“Resolution – Discuss strategies to decrease solid waste and recycling costs (referred 2-25-20).” 
Mr. Dias alleges that the Subcommittee discussed many subtopics under this topic, such as a 
composting program, transfer station, energy recovery, etc. Although a public body is required to 
post a notice that includes a “listing of topics that the chair reasonably anticipates will be 
discussed at the meeting,” the Chair is not required to anticipate the course of deliberations, and 
the Law does not require the meeting notice to list all tangential issues related to a particular 
topic. See OML 2019-102; OML 2016-120. Where a public body’s discussion, including a vote, 
naturally flows from its consideration of the posted agenda topic, the notice is sufficient to 
comply with the requirements of the Open Meeting Law. See OML 2019-70; OML 2018-14. We 
find that the posted topic, “Discuss strategies to decrease solid waste and recycling costs,” 
adequately encompassed the Subcommittee’s discussion of particular strategies to decrease solid 
waste and recycling costs, and therefore the Subcommittee did not violate the Open Meeting 
Law.  
 Finally, Mr. Dias also alleges that the Subcommittee did not make an adequate 
announcement that the August 19 meeting was being recorded.3 This allegation is nearly 
identical to one made by Mr. Dias in a complaint against the Fall River Conservation 
Commission, which we addressed in OML 2020-159. For the same reasons expressed therein, we 
also find no violation here.  
 For the reasons stated above, the Subcommittee did not violate the Open Meeting Law. 
We now consider the complaint addressed by this determination to be resolved. This 
determination does not address any other complaints that may be pending with our Office or the 
Subcommittee. Please feel free to contact the Division at (617) 963 - 2540 if you have any 
questions.
