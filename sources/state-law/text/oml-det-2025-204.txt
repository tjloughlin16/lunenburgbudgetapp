RE:  Open Meeting Law Complaint 
 
Dear Mr. Hanson: 
 
 This office received a complaint from Mark Mullins on September 2, 2025, alleging that 
the Bedford Energy and Sustainability Committee (the “Committee”) violated the Open Meeting 
Law, G.L. c. 30A, §§ 18-25.  The complaint was originally filed with the Committee on June 22, 
2025.
1  The Committee did not respond to the complaint.2  The complaint alleges that the 
Committee “excluded [certain documents] from the official record” of four open session 
meetings held between September 17, 2024, and March 17.   
 
Following our review, we find that the Committee did not violate the Open Meeting Law 
in the way alleged.  In reaching this determination, we reviewed the original complaint, the 
complaint filed with our office requesting further review, and the notice and minutes of 
Committee meetings held on September 17, 2024, January 23, February 27, March 17, and April 
17.   
 
The Open Meeting Law requires that public bodies “create and maintain accurate minutes 
of all meetings, including executive sessions, setting forth the date, time and place, the members 
present or absent, a summary of the discussions on each subject, a list of documents and other

During meetings held on September 17, 2024, January 23, February 27, and March 17, 
the Committee discussed a “Decarbonization Roadmap,” which established guidelines for the 
electrification and improved efficiency of Town buildings and vehicles.  On May 27, the 
complainant emailed the Chair of the Committee4 requesting, via the Public Records Law, 
“agendas” and “meeting documents” from Committee meetings held on September 17, 2024, 
January 23, February 27, and March 17.  The Committee provided some of the requested 
documents but did not provide the Decarbonization Roadmap discussed during the four 
meetings.   
 
To the extent the complaint alleges that the minutes of the meetings held on September 
17, 2024, Janua
ry 23, February 27, and March 17, fail to include a list of documents used during 
the four meetings, we find this allegation to be untimely.  Complaints alleging violations of the 
Open Meeting Law must be filed with the public body within 30 days of the alleged violation.  
G.L. c. 30A, § 23(b).  If the alleged violation could not reasonably have been known at the time 
it occurred, then the complaint must be filed within 30 days of the date it should reasonably have 
been discovered.  940 CMR 29.05(3).  When a public body approves meeting minutes in open 
session, any complaints regarding the sufficiency or accuracy of those minutes must be filed 
within 30 days of the meeting during which they were approved.  See OML Declination 9-4-12 
(Manchester-by-the-Sea Zoning Board of Appeals); OML Declination 5-14-12 (Sheffield 
Planning Board).   
 
Here, the Committee approved the September 17, 2024, meeting minutes during an open 
mee
ting held on October 17, 2024; approved the January 23 meeting minutes during an open 
meeting held on February 27; and approved the February 27 and March 17 meeting minutes 
during an open meeting held on April 17.  Therefore, any complaint with respect to the 
sufficiency of minutes should have been filed within 30 days of the date that the Committee 
approved each set of minutes.  See OML Declination 10-21-20 (Massachusetts Maritime 
Academy Board of Trustees); OML Declination 7-2-18 (Pembroke Planning Board); OML 
Declination 1-13-14 (Ashfield Select Board).  Where the complaint was filed on June 22, which 
was more than 30 days after the Committee approved each set of minutes in open session, we 
find this allegation to be untimely and decline to review it.  See OML Declination 10-21-20 
(Massachusetts Maritime Academy Board of Trustees); OML Declination 7-2-18 (Pembroke 
Planning Board); OML Declination 1-13-14 (Ashfield Select Board).

To the extent the complaint alleges that the Committee failed to post the Decarbonization 
Roadmap or other documents used during the meetings held on September 17, 2024, January 23, 
February 27, and March 17, there is no requirement in the Open Meeting Law that documents be 
posted to a website prior to or after a meeting.
5  See OML 2021-32.  Instead, documents used by 
a public body during an open session meeting must be provided in response to a public records 
request as documents and exhibits used during an open session meeting “shall be public records 
in their entirety.”  G.L. c. 30A, § 22(e).  However, the Open Meeting Law does not govern a 
public body’s response to a request for records other than meeting minutes.  See G.L. c. 30A, § 
22(c) (minutes of an open session, if they exist and whether approved or in draft form, shall be 
made available upon request by any person within 10 days); see also OML Declination 8-19-13 
(Southwick Board of Selectmen).  Failure to timely respond to requests for documents other than 
minutes concerns a potential violation of the Public Records Law, rather than the Open Meeting 
Law.  The Division of Open Government’s review concerns compliance with the Open Meeting 
Law only, G.L. c. 30A, §§ 18-25.  Moreover, although documents and other exhibits, such as 
photographs, recordings or maps, used by the body at an open session shall, along with the 
minutes, be part of the official record of the session, such documents and exhibits do not need to 
be attached to or physically stored with the minutes.  See G.L. c. 30A, § 22(d); see also OML 
2022-172; OML 2017-43; OML 2012-59.  As such, we find that the Committee did not violate 
the Open Meeting Law in this regard.
