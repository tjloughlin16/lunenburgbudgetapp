RE: Open Meeting Law Complaint 
Dear Mr. Murray: 
This office received a complaint from Thomas Bratko, dated June 22, 2011, alleging that 
the Hubbardston Capital Improvement Planning Committee (the "Committee") violated the Open 
Meeting Law, G.L. c. 30A, §§ 18-25. Specifically, the Complainant alleges that, following a 
request, the Committee failed to provide him with minutes for meetings that occurred between 
August 2010 and April 20, 2011. The complaint was originally filed with the Committee on or 
about May 24, 2011, and the Committee responded to the original complaint by letter dated July 
5, 2011. On July 29, 2011, the Complainant stated that he was not satisfied with the meeting 
minutes provided to him by the Committee and requested our office's review. 
We appreciate the parties' patience and cooperation while we reviewed this complaint. In 
reaching a determination, we reviewed the May 24, 2011 complaint and the Committee's July 5, 
2011 response. The Committee's response included copies of the draft minutes for the February 
18, 2010 meeting, as well as the draft minutes for the February 20, 2010; March 18, 2010; March 
25, 2010; April 5, 2010; April 6, 2010; May 20, 2010; January 26, 2011; March 8, 2011; and 
March 22, 2011 meetings. 
Following our review, we find that the Committee violated the Open Meeting Law by 
failing to provide meeting minutes to the Complainant within 10 days of request. 
FACTS 
Based upon our review of the material listed above, the facts are as follows. On April 20, 
2011, the Complainant requested access to or copies of the Committee's minutes for the 
meetings that occurred between August 2010 and April 20, 2011. On April 21, 2011, the Town 
===PAGE 2===
Accountant, a member of the Committee, replied to the Complainant's request by indicating that 
she would provide a draft copy of the meeting minutes within the next ten days. 
On May 24, 2011, the Complainant filed an Open Meeting Complaint Form with the 
Committee alleging that he still had not received the requested meeting minutes. He asked that 
the Committee "supply the CIPC meeting minutes that have been requested" and that the "CIPC 
meeting minutes be made available [to the] public in a timely manner." Having still not received 
an answer to his complaint, on June 22, 2011, the Complainant filed a complaint with our office. 
Subsequently, our office sent a letter to the Chairs  of the Committee explaining that the 
Committee has a legal obligation to respond to an Open Meeting Law complaint within 14 
business days from the date the complaint is filed, which in this case passed on June 13, 2011. 
On July 27, 2011, our office received copies of the Committee's draft meeting minutes, 
which were provided to Complainant on July 5, 2011. The minutes for each meeting contain the 
date of the meeting, the time, the members present, a summary of the discussion during meeting, 
and the votes taken at the meeting. The minutes do not include the place of each meeting, the 
members absent, or a list of documents and exhibits used at the meeting. 
On July 29, 2011, our office spoke with Complainant, who stated he was not satisfied 
with the meeting minutes and requested further review. 
DISCUSSION 
As a public body, the Committee is required to "create and maintain accurate minutes of 
all meetings, including executive sessions, setting forth the date, time, and place, the members 
present or absent, a summary of the discussions on each subject, a list of documents and other 
exhibits used at the meetings, the decisions made and the actions taken at each meeting, 
including the record of all votes." G.L. c. 30A, § 22(a). The "[m]inutes of all open sessions 
shall be created and approved in a timely manner" and "shall be made available upon request by 
any person within 10 days." G.L. c. 30A, § 22(c). 
Here, the Complainant requested meeting minutes on April 21, 2011. The Committee did 
not provide him with the draft minutes until July 5, 2011, well past the 10 day deadline for 
complying with such a request. See G.L. c. 30A, § 22(c). The Committee did not offer an 
explanation for the delay. 
In addition, we note that the Committee failed to respond to the complaint until July 5, 
2011-29 business days after it was filed. We acknowledge, however, that this may have been 
due in part to a procedural error. The Complainant filed his complaint with the Town 
Accountant and the Hubbardston Town Clerk on May 24, 2011. Under the Open Meeting Law, 
complaints must be filed with the chair of the public body as well as the municipal clerk. 940 
CMR 29.05(3). Although the Complainant correctly filed his complaint with the Hubbardston 
Town Clerk, he failed to file his complaint with the Acting Chair, submitting it instead to the

Town Accountant. While the Town Accountant is an active member of the Committee, we 
recognize that this error may have contributed to the Committee's delayed response. 2  
As a final matter, we note that the minutes of the Committee meetings that were 
ultimately provided to the Complainant did not include certain required information. While they 
provide the "date," "time," the "members present," a "summary of the discussions on each 
subject," and the "decisions made and actions taken at each meeting, including a record of all 
votes," they do not include the meeting "place," the "members absent," and a "list of documents 
and other exhibits used at the meeting." See G.L. c. 30A, § 22(a). We recognize that the 
minutes submitted to the complainant were draft minutes, however, and remind the Committee 
that, if it has not already done so, the Committee should supplement the minutes with the missing 
information before they are approved as final minutes. 
CONCLUSION 
We find that the Committee failed to provide meeting minutes within 10 days of request, 
as required by the Open Meeting Law. We order the Committee's immediate and future 
compliance with the law, and caution that future similar violations may be considered evidence 
of an intentional violation. 
We now consider the complaint addressed by this determination to be resolved. Please 
feel free to contact me if you have any questions or believe any facts in this letter to be 
inaccurate.
