This office received a complaint from John Connolly on February 7, 2022, alleging that 
the Somerville Traffic Commission (the “Commission”) violated the Open Meeting Law, G.L. c. 
30A, §§ 18-25. The complaint was originally filed with the Commission on November 23, 2021, 
and you responded on behalf of the Commission by letter dated December 14, 2021.
1 The 
complaint alleges that the Commission posted an insufficiently specific notice for its November 
18 meeting.  
 
Following our review, we resolve this complaint by informal action in accordance with 
940 CMR 29.07(2)(a) and find that the Commission did not violate the Open Meeting Law. In 
reaching this determination we reviewed the original Open Meeting Law complaint, the 
Commission’s response, the request for further review, the meeting notices and minutes for the 
Commission’s July 15 and November 18 meetings, and a video recording of the Commission’s 
November 18 meeting.

 On July 15 the Commission held a meeting during which it discussed and approved 
regulatory changes related to a “College Ave Mobility Improvements Project” (the “Project”). 
The complainant, along with other community members, filed a petition with the Somerville 
Traffic Board (the “Traffic Board”) raising several concerns regarding the Commission’s 
consideration of the Project on July 15, including that votes were not taken by roll call and that 
there was “poor notice of the meeting.” The petition requested that the Traffic Board hold a 
public hearing regarding the Commission’s actions taken on July 15 with respect to the Project.  
 
 The Commission subsequently posted notice for a meeting to be held on November 18. 
The notice included the topic “Request for the Traffic Commission to reaffirm and ratify its 
approval of all items on the July 15, 2021, agenda with a roll call vote.” During the portion of the 
November 18 meeting concerning this item, Commission Chair Jill Lathan announced that the 
Project was subject to a pending appeal before the Traffic Board, that a public hearing regarding 
the appeal would be scheduled for December, and that the November 18 discussion did not 
concern the appeal. The Chair then called for public comment. There was none. Commission 
Member Thomas Graney then moved to “reaffirm and ratify the Traffic Commission’s approval 
of all items on the July 15, 2021, agenda with a roll call vote.” The motion was seconded. The 
Chair then called for questions or comments from the Commission. There were none. The 
Commission then voted, by roll call, to reaffirm and ratify the Commission’s approval of all 
items on the July 15 notice. Thereafter, the Commission turned to the next notice topic. 
 
We understand the complaint to allege that the notice for the Committee’s November 18 
meeting was insufficiently specific with respect to the topic “Request for the Traffic Commission 
to reaffirm and ratify its approval of all items on the July 15, 2021, agenda with a roll call vote,” 
because that item did “not specify[] in sufficient detail the items to be voted on that were 
originally listed on the July 15 Traffic Commission agenda.” 
 
 First, to the extent the complaint raises allegations about the July 15 meeting, we decline to 
review these allegations because they are untimely. To be considered timely, an Open Meeting 
Law complaint must be filed with the public body within 30 days of the alleged violation, or, if 
the alleged violation could not reasonably have been known at the time it occurred, then within 
30 days of the date it should have been discovered. G.L. c. 30A, § 23(b); 940 CMR 29.05(4). 
When an alleged violation occurs during an open meeting, the alleged violation is reasonably 
discoverable at the time it occurs. See OML 2022-111; OML 2014-85; OML 2012-52.
3 Here, the 
Commission meeting held on July 15 took place entirely in open session. To be timely, therefore, 
a complaint concerning the July 15 meeting should have been filed within 30 days of that 
meeting. See OML Declination 2-1-17 (West Bridgewater Water Commission); OML 
Declination 11-21-16 (Middleton Planning Board). Therefore, our review is limited to the 
allegation raised concerning the notice for the Commission’s November 18 meeting. 
 
The Ope
n Meeting Law requires that public bodies post notice of every meeting “at least 
48 hours prior to such meeting, excluding Saturdays, Sundays and legal holidays” and that every 
notice include “the date, time and place of such meeting and a listing of topics that the chair 
reasonably anticipates will be discussed at the meeting.” G.L. c. 30A, § 20(b). A public body is

required to list topics on a notice with “sufficient specificity to reasonably advise the public of 
the issues to be discussed at the meeting.” 940 CMR 29.03(1)(b). We generally consider a topic 
sufficiently specific when a reasonable member of the public could read the topic and understand 
the anticipated nature of the public body’s discussion. See OML 2021-22; OML 2015-35. When 
reviewing a meeting notice for sufficiency, unless it is clearly insufficient on its face, we do not 
review a meeting notice standing alone. See OML 2015-101; OML 2021-116. Rather, we review 
what was actually discussed at the meeting to determine if the notice was sufficiently detailed to 
make the public aware of the discussion topics. See OML 2020-137.  
 
 The complaint asserts that the notice for the November 18 meeting should have included 
details identifying the items on the notice for the July 15 meeting. We disagree. We find that the 
topic “Request for the Traffic Commission to reaffirm and ratify its approval of all items on the 
July 15, 2021, agenda with a roll call vote” was sufficiently specific to inform the public of the 
anticipated discussion where the Commission reaffirmed and ratified, by roll call vote, its 
approval of all items on the July 15 meeting notice.
 
 
For the reasons stated above, we find that the Commission did not violate the Open 
Meeting Law. We now consider the complaint addressed by this determination to be resolved. 
This determination does not address any other complaints that may be pending with our office or 
the Commission. Please feel free to contact the Division at (617) 963-2540 if you have any 
questions.
