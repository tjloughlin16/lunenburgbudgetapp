 RE: Open Meeting Law Complaints 
 
Dear Chair Gniadek and Attorney Goodman: 
 
This office received complaints from Richard Bell on October 24, 2024, and June 10, 
2025, alleging that the Richmond Budget Advisory Committee and the Finance Committee 
(collectively, the “Committees”1) violated the Open Meeting Law, G.L. c. 30A, §§ 18-25.  The 
first complaint was originally filed with the Committees on or about July 26, 2024, and Robert 
Gniadek responded, on behalf of the Committees, by letter dated August 26, 2024.2 The second 
complaint was filed with the Committees on or about March 19, 2025, and Elisabeth Goodman 
responded, on behalf of the Committees, by letter dated March 31, 2025. The complaints allege 
that the Committees failed to respond to requests for meeting minutes, failed to timely approve 
thirteen sets of meeting minutes for meetings held in 2023, 2024, and 2025, and posted notices 
with insufficient detail for meetings held in 2023, 2024, and 2025.

Following our review, we find that the Committees violated the Open Meeting Law in the 
ways alleged. In reaching this determination, we reviewed the original complaints, the 
Committees’ responses to the complaints, the complaints filed with our office requesting further 
review, and the meeting minutes for the Committees posted on the Town of Richmond Website.
3  
 
The Open Meeting Law requires that a public body “create and maintain accurate 
minutes of all meetings, including executive sessions, setting forth the date, time and place, the 
members present or absent, a summary of the discussions on each subject, a list of documents 
and other exhibits used at the meeting, the decisions made and the actions taken at each meeting, 
including the record of all votes.” G.L. c. 30A, § 22(a). The meeting minutes must be reviewed 
and approved in a timely manner. G.L. c. 30A, § 22(c). “Timely manner” means “within the next 
three public body meetings or within 30 days, whichever is later, unless the public body can 
show good cause for further delay.” See 940 CMR 29.11; OML 2018-48.
4 Whenever possible, 
we recommend that minutes of a meeting be approved at the next meeting. See OML 2018-67; 
OML 2017-133. The Open Meeting Law further requires that “[t]he minutes of an open session, 
if they exist and whether approved or in draft form, shall be made available upon request by any 
person within 10 days.” G.L. c. 30A, § 22(c). If minutes do not yet exist at the time of a request, 
the public body is still required to respond to the request within 10 days with an explanation of 
whether the minutes do or do not exist in either approved or draft form. See OML 2017-50; 
OML 2016-71; OML 2015-173. 
 
The first complaint alleges that the Committees failed to timely approve ten sets of 
meeting minutes for meetings held in 2023 and 2024. The Committees do not dispute, and we 
find, that the Committees failed to timely approve these sets of minutes. 
 
The first complaint further alleges, and the Committees do not dispute, that the 
Committees failed to adequately respond to the complainant’s July 15, 2024, request for meeting 
minutes. The Committees also acknowledge they had fallen behind with creation and approval of 
meeting minutes. In response to the complaint, the Committees represented that they had 
recently approved most of the requested meeting minutes and that the remaining outstanding 
minutes were drafted and awaiting approval at the next meeting. The Committees also 
represented that they had appointed a Secretary/Clerk to be responsible for timely preparing 
meeting minutes for the Committees’ approval. We commend the Committees for taking these 
steps. 
 
The second complaint alleges that the Committees failed to timely approve minutes for 
meetings held on August 20, 2024, and November 20, 2024. We find that the Committees timely 
approved minutes for its meetings on August 20, 2024, and November 20, 2024. The Budget 
Advisory Committee approved minutes for its August 20, 2024, meeting on February 12, 2025, 
which was its next meeting thereafter. Similarly, the Finance Committee approved minutes for 
its November 20, 2024, meeting on February 26, 2025, which was its next meeting thereafter.

Thus, we find that the minutes for these two meetings were timely approved, as they were 
approved “within the next three public body meetings.” See 940 CMR 29.11. 
 
The second complaint further alleges that the Committees failed to adequately respond to 
the complainant’s March 6, 2025, request for minutes since he received a response on March 14, 
2025, from the Town Administrator rather than a member of the Committee. The Open Meeting 
Law does not require that only a member of a public body respond to a request for meeting 
minutes. Thus, we find that the Committees adequately responded to the complainant’s March 6, 
2025, request for meeting minutes by email on March 14, 2025. 
 
The second complaint further alleges that the Committees posted meeting notices with 
insufficient detail by failing to include the dates of the minutes to be approved. When meeting 
minutes will be approved during a meeting, the meeting notice should identify the dates of the 
specific meeting minutes that will be approved, if anticipated 48 hours in advance of the 
meeting.  See OML 2023-124; OML 2020- 34; OML 2017-92; OML 2014-118. Many of the 
notices posted by the Committees only state “Approval of Minutes” as an item, without 
identifying which meeting minutes were anticipated for approval. Thus, we find the Committees 
violated the Open Meeting Law by posting insufficiently specific meeting notices.  
 
Finally, the second complaint alleges that the Committees violated the Open Meeting 
Law by failing to post its meeting minutes on the Town of Richmond website. The Open 
Meeting Law only requires that “minutes of an open session, if they exist and whether approved 
or in draft form, shall be made available upon request by any person within 10 days.” G.L. c. 
30A, § 22(c). The Open Meeting Law does not require that public bodies post their meeting 
minutes to a website. OML 2020-106; OML 2017-98; OML 2017-25. Accordingly, the 
Committees did not violate the Open Meeting Law by failing to post their meeting minutes on 
the Town of Richmond website. 
 
For the reasons stated above, we find that the Committees violated the Open Meeting 
Law by failing to respond to a request for meeting minutes, failing to timely approve meeting 
minutes, and posting insufficiently specific meeting notices. We order the Committees’ 
immediate and future compliance, and caution that a similar violation in the future may be 
considered evidence of an intent to violate the Law and may result in civil penalties of up to 
$1,000 per violation.  
 
We further order the Committees to approve all open session meeting minutes from 2023, 
2024, and 2025 that have not yet been approved in a timely manner in accordance with the above 
guidance, and to certify to this office within sixty (60) days of the date of this letter that it has 
done so.  See 940 CMR 29.07(4).
