RE: Open Meeting Law Complaint 
Dear Attorney Doyle: 
This office reviewed a complaint dated August 24, 2011, filed by Mr. Robert Columbus, 
alleging that the Templeton Board of Selectmen (the "Board") violated the Open Meeting Law, 
G.L. c. 30A, §§ 18-25. Specifically, the complaint states that at the Board's May 23, 2011 
meeting, "Selectman Jeff Bennett made a motion to hold an executive session meetings at the 
next meeting on June 13, 2011 to discuss the performance and possible dismissal of a town 
employee." The complaint alleges that three members of the Board "had knowledge of this topic 
prior to the open meeting." The complaint was filed with the Board on June 13, 2011. The 
Board responded by letter dated August 1, 2011. 
We find that the Board did not violate the Open Meeting Law. In reaching this 
determination, we reviewed the June 13, 2011 complaint filed with the Board, the Board's 
August 1, 2011 response, and the August 24, 2011 complaint filed with our office. We also 
reviewed the notice for the Board's May 23, 2011 meeting, an amendment to that notice, and the 
minutes of the Board's May 23, 2011 meeting. Finally, we reviewed the draft minutes and 
notice and Board's June 27, 2011 meeting 
===PAGE 2===
FACTS 
The Board posted a notice on May 19, 2011, for a May 23, 2011 meeting. The Board 
chair at the time was the complainant, Mr. Columbus. The notice for the May 23, 2011 meeting 
included topics the chair reasonably anticipated would be discussed at the meeting. During the 
meeting, under the New Business portion, Board member Jeff Bennett moved to reorganize the 
Board. Following the vote, Board member Julie Farrell became the chair. After the vote to 
reorganize the meeting, Mr. Bennett moved to place an executive session item on the upcoming 
May 31, 2011 meeting agenda. The item involved a discussion of the discipline or dismissal of 
an employee. Mr. Columbus asked what the discussion was concerning, but was told by Chair 
Farrell that they could not discuss it publicly. Following discussion, the Board voted 3-2 to place 
the item on the June 13, 2011 meeting agenda. 
DISCUSSION 
The Open Meeting Law, G.L. c. 30A, § 20(b) states in relevant part that, "[e]xcept in an 
emergency, in addition to any notice otherwise required by law, a public body shall post notice 
of every meeting at least 48 hours prior to such meeting, excluding Saturdays, Sundays and legal 
holidays... Notice shall be printed in a legible, easily understandable format and shall contain the 
date, time and place of such meeting and a listing of topics that the chair reasonably anticipates 
will be discussed at the meeting." Any topic discussed at a meeting, which was reasonably 
anticipated by the chair of the public body, must be listed in a meeting notice at least 48 hours in 
advance of the meeting. However, topics which arise within the 48 hours of the meeting, which 
the chair did not reasonably anticipate would be discussed at the meeting, may be added to the 
agenda and considered by the public body. 
Here, Mr. Columbus was responsible for setting the agenda ahead of the meeting. 
Although the topic raised by Mr. Bennett's motion during the May 23, 2011 meeting to discuss a 
disciplinary matter in executive session may have been anticipated more than 48 hours before the 
meeting by Chair Farrell at the time, she did not serve as chair when the agenda was assembled 
and the notice was posted. Furthermore, even if the topic had been anticipated, it was 
appropriate for a Board member raise the topic for the purpose of scheduling a future discussion 
when the topic could be including in a meeting notice. 
The complaint also suggests that a quorum of the Board may have discussed the topic 
outside of a posted meeting. The Board denies this and we have uncovered no evidence to 
suggest that the quorum coordinated the vote. 
CONCLUSION 
For the reasons stated above, we find that the Board did not violate the Open Meeting 
Law.
