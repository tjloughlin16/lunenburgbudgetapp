This office received two complaints from Patrick Brown on October 21, 2025, alleging 
that the West Boylston School Committee (the “Committee”) violated the Open Meeting Law, 
G.L. c. 30A, §§ 18-25.  Both complaints were originally filed with the Committee on September 
5, 2025, and you responded, on behalf of the Board, by email on October 14, 2025.
1  Both 
complaints allege that the Committee violated the Open Meeting Law by meeting in an 
inaccessible location on August 19, 2025.  The second complaint further alleges that the 
Committee posted an amended notice that failed to include the date and time that the notice was 
posted.   
 
We appreciate the patience of the parties while we reviewed these matters.  Following 
our review, we find that the Committee violated the Open Meeting Law in the ways alleged.  In 
reaching this determination, we reviewed the original complaints, the public body’s response to 
the complaints, and the complaints filed with our office requesting further review.  In addition, 
we reviewed the notices and minutes of the Committee meetings held on August 19, 2025.

FACTS 
 
We find the facts as follows.  In September 2023, the Town of West Boylston issued a 
report entitled “Americans with Disabilities Act Self-Evaluation and Transition Plan.”  The 
report raised several issues with exterior access to the West Boylston Middle/High School.  
Specifically, the report stated that most exterior sidewalks, walkways, site arrival points, parking 
areas, and routes connecting site elements were significantly out of compliance with Americans 
with Disabilities Act (“ADA”) Standards.  In addition, the report noted that the interior of the 
building also presented significant challenges as well, including existing ramps connecting 
multiple levels of the school are all sloped in excess of maximum slopes. 
 
The Committee posted notice of a workshop for August 19, 2025, at 9 A.M. in the Media 
Center of the West Boylston Middle/High School.  The notice included two topics—Welcome 
and Team-building exercises.  The Committee also posted notice of a regular meeting to occur 
on August 19, 2025, at 10 A.M. in the Media Center of the West Boylston Middle/High School.  
This notice included nine topics.  On August 15, the Committee amended the notice to include 
one additional topic—ADA compliance discussion. 
 
The notices for each meeting included the following language: “If you need reasonable 
accommodations to participate in a meeting, contact the Superintendent of Schools, Dan Riley, 
Ed.D. at danielriley@wbschools.com or (508) 835-2917 in advance of the meeting.  While the 
municipality will do its best to accommodate you, certain accommodations require the hiring of 
outside contractors who may not be readily available if requested immediately before the 
meeting.”  
 
Both the workshop and regular meeting were held on August 19, 2025, as planned, and 
the Committee met in the Media Center of the West Boylston Middle/High School.  During the 
workshop, the Committee and School Administrative team discussed different leadership styles 
and approaches and then focused on improving teamwork. 
 
During the regular meeting, the Committee approved the 2025-2026 Elementary School’s 
and Middle/High School’s handbooks, approved “payable” and payroll warrants, approved four 
sets of meeting minutes, received a presentation from the Director of Special Education 
regarding Special Education Programming and services, discussed regionalization, reorganized, 
and discussed the cost and “priority of addressing ADA Compliance needs between both school 
buildings.”

DISCUSSION 
 
At the core of the Open Meeting Law is the requirement that meetings of public bodies be 
open and accessible to the public.  G.L. c. 30A, § 20(a) (except when meeting in executive 
session, “all meetings of a public body shall be open to the public”).  Generally, access to a 
meeting must include the opportunity to be physically present as well as to see and hear what is 
being discussed by the members of the public body.  See OML 2020-100; OML 2014-3; OML 
2013-189.3  For a meeting to be truly “open to the public,” it must be accessible to people with 
disabilities.  See G.L. c. 30A, § 20(a); see also OML 2023-43; OML 2020-47; OML 2015-38; 
OML 2012-49.  This means that public bodies must hold public meetings in locations where both 
the building and the meeting room are accessible.  See 28 C.F.R. § 35.150 (2011); Dep’t of 
Justice, Americans with Disabilities Act: Title II Technical Assistance Manual, at 5.1000 (1993); 
see also OML 2019-90.  We have explained, as a baseline, that meetings of public bodies that are 
held in person must be held in ADA-compliant locations, even in the absence of any particular 
request or known accessibility need.  See, e.g., OML 2024-130 (explaining that public body 
violated the Open Meeting Law when meeting was held in a non-ADA compliant location, even 
though there is no evidence any person was unable to attend as a result); OML 2023-43 (same).   
 
Here, the complaints allege that the Committee’s workshop and regular meeting held on 
August 19, 2025, were in a location that was inaccessible to individuals with disabilities.  The 
Committee does not dispute this allegation, instead explaining that “we always add to every 
agenda that we will provide reasonable accommodations to anyone who needs it.”  As stated 
above, every public meeting must be held in a location that is accessible, regardless of whether 
the public body receives a particular request for accessibility.
4  We find that the Committee 
violated the Open Meeting Law by holding the August 19, 2025, meetings in a location that was 
inaccessible to members of the public with disabilities.  See OML 2025-3; OML 2024-130; 
OML 2023-43. 
 
With respect to the allegation in the complaints that the Committee did not also provide 
remote access to the August 19, 2025, meetings, we have consistently stated that a public body is 
not required to provide remote access to its meetings, so long as the meetings are held in-person 
at a location that is open and physically accessible to the public.  See OML 2023-225; OML 
2022-158; OML 2021-79.  We note that, since September 2025, the Committee appears to 
provide both in-person and remote access to its meetings.  Generally, to ensure the greatest 
transparency consistent with the goals of the Open Meeting Law, we encourage public bodies to 
offer the public multiple means of access.  See OML 2026-15; OML 2025-125.   
 
Finally, the second complaint alleges that the amended notice of the August 19, 2025, 
regular Committee meeting failed to include the date and time that the notice was amended, as 
well as the date and time that the notice was originally posted.  The Attorney General’s Open

Meeting Law regulations require that the date and time that a meeting notice is posted be 
conspicuously recorded on or with the notice.  See 940 CMR 29.03(l)(d).  In addition, notices 
that are revised within the 48 hours before the meeting must conspicuously record both the date 
and time that the amended notice was posted and the date and time that the notice was originally 
posted.  See 940 CMR 29.03(l)(d); OML 2020-49; OML 2017-183; OML 2013-44.   
 
Here, the Committee posted notice of the August 19, 2025, meeting on August 13, 2025.  
The Committee then amended the notice on August 15, 2025.  Neither the original or amended 
notices include, either on the face of the notices or on the municipal website, which is the official 
method of posting notice for the Town of West Boylston, the date and time that each notice was 
posted.
5  Recording the date and time enables the public to observe that public bodies are 
complying with the Open Meeting Law’s notice requirements without requiring constant 
vigilance.  See OML 2013-44.  Additionally, it provides this office with evidence of compliance 
with the Open Meeting Law’s notice requirements.  See OML 2014-9.  We find that the 
Committee violated the Open Meeting Law where both the original and amended notices did not 
conspicuously record thereon or therewith the date and time of posting.  See, e.g., OML 2024-6; 
OML 2019-102; OML 2018-53 (explaining that the date and time of posting may be listed either 
on the meeting notice itself or, when a meeting notice is posted to a website pursuant to 940 
CMR 29.03(2)(b), on the website next to the link to the meeting notice).   
 
CONCLUSION 
 
For the reasons stated above, we find that the Committee violated the Open Meeting Law 
by failing to ensure its meetings were accessible to the public and by failing to include the date 
and time that notices were posted.  We order immediate and future compliance with the law’s 
requirements and we caution that similar future violations could be considered evidence of intent 
to violate the law.   
 
We now consider the complaints addressed by this determination to be resolved.  This 
determination does not address any other complaints that may be pending with our office or the 
Committee.  Please feel free to contact our office at (617) 963-2540 if you have any questions 
regarding this letter.
