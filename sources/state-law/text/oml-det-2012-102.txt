that the Chatham Golf Advisory Committee (the "Committee") violated the Open Meeting Law, 
G.L. c. 30A, §§ 18-25. The complaint was originally filed with the Committee on or about

the Committee reschedule a hearing that was not listed on a meeting notice, and that the

FACTS  
Based upon our review of the material listed above, the facts are as follows. The 
complainant, Mr. Geiger, is a homeowner and abutter to the Chatham Seaside Links Golf 
Course. One of the Committee's duties is to arrange for maintenance and aesthetic upkeep, 
including habitat clearing, on the Golf Course. 
On February 8, 2012, the Committee posted notice on the Town website for a February 
16, 2012 meeting, listing three agenda items, including "2. Update on the 8th  hole drainage 
issue." The February 16, 2012 meeting minutes indicate that after the Committee gave a brief 
update of the problems with the eighth hole green, members discussed another abutting 
homeowner's request to "remove invasive ground cover next to 9th [hole] fairway at owner's 
expense, but leave trees," deciding, without taking a vote, that the "[c]onservation commission 
should check it out. Dan [Tobin] will check with [Conservation Agent] Kristen Andres." 
Chair Bohman writes in her March 23, 2012 letter that the clearing of the invasive 
vegetation on one homeowner's land was brought to her attention "2-3 days prior" to the 
February 16, 2012 meeting, and because she was leaving on vacation shortly thereafter, she 
wanted to briefly discuss it with the Committee and have the proper parties look into it during 
her absence. However, Chair Bohman writes in her May 10, 2012 letter that she only heard of 
the invasive vegetation clearing "the day before" the February 16, 2012 meeting. When our 
office interviewed Chair Bohman, she indicated again that she first learned of the request to 
discuss the invasive vegetation on the ninth hole the day prior to the committee's February 16, 
2012 meeting. 
Following the meeting, Landscaper Bill Southwick removed invasive vegetation next to 
the ninth hole fairway, including a few trees, believing that he had permission from Ms. Andres 
and Mr. Tobin. Noticing the new landscape on the ninth hole, Mr. Geiger unsuccessfully 
attempted to obtain the February 16, 2012 meeting minutes sometime in early April 2012 from 
the Town Clerk. Mr. Geiger made a verbal request for the minutes and does not recall the 
specific date he made the request. Chair Bohman explains in her May 10, 2012 letter to our 
office that the minutes were unavailable at the time of Mr. Geiger's request because the 
Committee "did not have another meeting until April 19, 2012."

DISCUSSION 
The Open Meeting Law requires a public body to post notice of every meeting at least 48 
hours in advance, with the notice containing the date, time and place, and a list of topics that the 
chair reasonably anticipates will be discussed at the meeting. G.L. c. 30A, § 20(b). The Open 
Meeting Law also requires public bodies to create and approve accurate minutes of all meetings 
in a timely manner, and make them available upon request by any person within ten days, 
whether in draft or final approved form. G.L. c. 30A, §§ 22(a), (c). 
The Committee posted notice for its February 16, 2012 meeting on February 8, 2012. 
While Chair Bohman states in her March 23, 2012 letter that she learned of a new homeowner's 
request to discuss clearing vegetation "2-3 days prior" to the February 16, 2012 meeting, in a 
subsequent letter she wrote that she only learned of the request one day before the meeting. 
Given the conflicting written facts, we interviewed Chair Bohman via telephone on August 17, 
2012, and she confirmed that she learned of the request one day prior to the meeting. Because 
we have not been presented with any evidence to the contrary, we fmd that Chair Bohman did 
not anticipate the topic more than 48 hours in advance of the Committee's February 16, 2012 
meeting, thus the Committee did not violate the law by discussing removing invasive ground 
cover next to the ninth hole. See G.L. c. 30A, § 20(b); See OML 2012-95; OML 2011-23; OML 
2011-13.4  While it is not unlawful for a public body to consider unanticipated topics that were 
not included in the meeting notice, the Attorney General strongly encourages public bodies to 
postpone discussion of items that do not require immediate action and that were not listed in a 
notice at least 48 hours before a meeting. See OML 2012-85; OML 2012-3; OML 2011-53. 
The complaint also alleges that the Committee (or the Town Clerk acting on the 
Committee's behalf) failed to provide Mr. Geiger with the February 16, 2012 meeting minutes 
following his request for those minutes. While the precise date on which Mr. Geiger requested 
the February 16, 2012 meeting minutes is unclear, it appears the request was made sometime in 
early April 2012. The Committee met on April 19, 2012, approved the minutes, and 
subsequently provided them to Mr. Geiger. Because it is not clear when the request for the 
February 16, 2012 minutes was made, we decline to find that they were not made available to 
Mr. Geiger within ten days of his request. See G.L. c. 30A, § 22(c). However, we remind the 
Committee of the need to provide meeting minutes promptly in response to a request. See id. 
Finally, we address the complainant's allegation that the Committee violated the Open 
Meeting Law by failing to submit meeting minutes to the Town Clerk. The Open Meeting Law 
requires that public bodies "create and maintain accurate minutes of all meetings." G.L. c. 30A, 
§ 22(a). While some public bodies designate their respective town clerks as the official 
custodians for meeting minutes, the Open Meeting Law does not require this practice. 
Therefore, we do not find that the Committee violated the law by failing to submit meeting 
minutes to the Town Clerk.

CONCLUSION 
For the reasons stated above, we find that the Committee did not violate the Open 
Meeting Law.
