RE: Open Meeting Law Complaint 
Dear Mr. Ericson: 
Our office received a complaint filed by Allan Wyman, dated October 16, 2013, alleging 
that the Massachusetts Board of Registration of Hazardous Waste Site Cleanup Professionals 
(the "Board") violated the Open Meeting Law, G.L. c. 30A, §§ 18-25. Specifically, the 
complaint alleges that the Board failed to respond to Mr. Wyman's request for the Board's 
executive session minutes. The complaint was originally filed with the Board on or about 
September 10, 2013. The Board responded by letter dated September 30, 2013. 
Following our review, we find that the Board failed to provide Mr. Wyman with its 
meeting minutes in response to his request within the timeframe required by law. However, the 
Board took appropriate remedial action in response to the complaint by reviewing the requested 
minutes and providing them to Mr. Wyman. In reaching this determination, we reviewed the 
September 10, 2013 complaint; the Board's September 30, 2013 response; and the October 16, 
2013 complaint filed with our office. Additionally, we spoke with both parties by telephone. 
On August 14, 2013, Mr. Wyman sent an email to the Board requesting copies of all 
executive session minutes approved by the Board between March 2012 and August 14, 2013. 
Additionally, he requested the April 2013 and June 2013 "professional conduct and board 
meeting minutes," and the "draft version of the March 2012 executive session minutes provided 
to the Board prior to the May 2012 Board meeting, that they reviewed during the May 2012 
executive session." Additionally, he requested the "draft version of the April 2013 executive 
session minutes provided to the Board prior to the June 2013 Board meeting, that they reviewed 
during the June 2013 executive session." The Board responded to the request by letter dated 
August 22, 2013, stating that the "Public Records Law requires that the LSP Board respond to 
your request within ten days. This acknowledgment satisfies that requirement; although, it

generally takes longer than the ten-day period to review our records to determine whether there 
are responsive documents." The Board provided the requested minutes to Mr. Wyman over a 
period of months, with the final production on January 24, 2014. 
While most requests for government documents are governed by the Public Records Law, 
requests for minutes of a public body's meeting are governed by the Open Meeting Law. See 
G.L. c. 30A, § 22; OML 2013-18.' A public body must respond to a request for executive session 
minutes within 10 days. G.L. c. 30A, § 22(g)(2). If the public body has not performed a review 
of its minutes, the public body shall perform the review and release the non-exempt minutes, or 
any portion thereof, not later than the public body's next meeting or 30 days, whichever first 
occurs. Id 
Here, the Board properly responded to the request for executive session minutes within 
10 days. G.L. c. 3OA, § 22(g)(2); OML 2013-99. However, the Board did not undertake a 
review of its executive session minutes within 30 days, as required by law. Id. Following Mr. 
Wyman's September 10, 2013 complaint, the Board provided some of the requested minutes and 
undertook the process of reviewing its remaining executive session minutes, which took nearly 
four months to complete. This violated the Open Meeting Law. 
We understand that as of January 24, 2014, the Board had completed its review of its 
executive session minutes, both in draft and final form, and provided Mr. Wyman with the 
remaining documents he requested. Accordingly, we order the Board's immediate and future 
compliance with the Open Meeting Law, and caution that future similar violations may be 
considered evidence of intent to violate the law. 
We now consider the complaint addressed by this determination to be resolved. This 
determination does not address any other complaints which may be pending with our office or 
pending with the Board. Please contact our office at (617) 963-2540 if you have any questions.
