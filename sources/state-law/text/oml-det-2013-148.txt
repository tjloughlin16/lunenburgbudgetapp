RE: Open Meeting Law Complaint 
Dear Mr. Frattallone; 
This office received an Open Meeting Law complaint from Christopher Welch on March 
20, 2013, alleging that the Littleton Board of Health (the "Board") violated the Open Meeting 
Law, G.L. c. 30A, §§ 18-25. The complaint specifically alleges that during the Board's January 
22, 2013 meeting. Chair Gino Frattallone1 ended a discussion because Mr. Welch arrived at the 
meeting, and then told Mr. Welch to "[g]et out of here." The complaint was filed with the Board 
on or about February 12, 2013, The Board responded to the complaint by letter dated February 
27, 2013. 
Following our review, we do not find sufficient evidence that the Board violated the 
Open Meeting Law. In reaching this determination, we reviewed the February 12, 2013 
complaint filed with the Board; the Board's February 27, 2013 response; and the March 20, 2013 
complaint filed with our office. Additionally, we reviewed the minutes of the Board's January 
22, 2013 meeting. Finally, we interviewed by telephone Christopher Welch on April 29, 2013, 
Chair Gino Frattallone on May 2, 2013, and Board member William Cole on May 2, 2013.

FACTS 
Chair Frattallone and Mr. Welch disagree as to what occurred during the January 22, 
2013 meeting. It is clear that the Board discussed a property at 15 Crestview Road that abuts Mr. 
Welch's property. According to Mr. Welch, when he arrived at the meeting, the Chair 
immediately stopped this discussion and told Mr. Welch that the Board could not continue the 
discussion with Mr. Welch in attendance. He further asserts that Chair Frattallone then told Mr. 
Welch to "[g]et out of here." The Board disputes this account. In its February 27, 2013 response 
to the complaint, the Board writes that the discussion was reopened when Mr. Welch arrived and 
that Mr. Welch was provided an opportunity to address the Board. The Chair and another Board 
member, William Cole, confirmed with this Office that Mr. Welch arrived at the meeting as the 
Board finished its discussion about the property, and that the Board agreed to reopen the 
discussion when Mr. Welch arrived and allowed Mr. Welch to address the Board. Following Mr. 
Welch's remarks, they claim, the Board closed the discussion. The Chair and Mr. Cole deny that 
Chair Frattallone told Mr. Welch to leave the meeting. 
Neither the Board nor the complainant made an audio or video recording of the meeting 
that would confirm either party's account of what transpired. 
DISCUSSION 
Except for meetings in executive session, all meetings of a public body shall be open to 
the public. G.L. c. 30A, § 20(a). The only exception to the general rule that the public may 
attend and observe open meetings is where a person is disruptive. See OML 2013-135; OML 
2013-128.1 The Open Law states that "[n]o person shall disrupt the proceedings of a meeting of 
a public body. If, after clear warning from the chair, a person continues to disrupt the 
proceedings, the chair may order the person to withdraw from the meeting and if the person does 
not withdraw, the chair may authorize a constable or other officer to remove the person from the 
meeting." G.L. c. 30A, § 20(f). 
There is no evidence that Mr. Welch was being disruptive. However, there is also no 
evidence supporting Mr. Welch's contention that he was asked to leave the Board's January 22, 
2013 meeting. In the absence of such evidence, we find that the Board did not violate the Open 
Meeting Law. However, we remind the Board that a member of the public has a right to attend 
an open meeting of a public body, so long as that person is not being disruptive. 
CONCLUSION 
For the reasons stated above, we find that the Board did not violate the Open Meeting 
Law.
