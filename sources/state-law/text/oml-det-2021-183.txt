 RE: Open Meeting Law Complaint 
 
Dear Attorney Castro: 
 
This office received a complaint from Stephen Morgan on October 8, 2021, alleging that 
the Ashland Conservation Commission (the “Commission”) violated the Open Meeting Law, 
G.L. c. 30A, §§ 18-25.  The complaint was originally filed with the Commission on or about 
September 13, 2021, and you responded to the complaint, on behalf of the Commission, by letter 
dated October 6, 2021, following an extension of time granted by our office.  The complaint 
alleges that the Commission failed to timely approve the minutes of a meeting held on July 26, 
2021.1   
 
We resolve this matter by informal action, in accordance with 940 CMR 29.07(2)(a), 
after reviewing the original complaint, the Commission’s response to the complaint, the 
complaint filed with our office requesting further review, and the notice and minutes of the 
Commission meeting held on October 4, 2021.  We find that the Commission violated the Open 
Meeting Law in the way alleged.

The Open Meeting Law requires that a public body “create and maintain accurate 
minutes of all meetings, including executive sessions, setting forth the date, time and place, the 
members present or absent, a summary of the discussions on each subject, a list of documents 
and other exhibits used at the meeting, the decisions made and the actions taken at each meeting, 
including the record of all votes.”  G.L. c. 30A, § 22(a).  The meeting minutes must be reviewed 
and approved in a timely manner.  G.L. c. 30A, § 22(c).  “Timely manner” means “within the 
next three public body meetings or within 30 days, whichever is later, unless the public body can 
show good cause for further delay.”  See 940 CMR 29.11; OML 2018-48.2  Whenever possible, 
we recommend that minutes of a meeting be approved at the next meeting.  See OML 2018-67; 
OML 2017-133.  
 
The Commission approved the minutes of its July 26, 2021, meeting during its October 4, 
2021, meeting, which was the seventh meeting thereafter.  As such, we find that the Commission 
violated the Open Meeting Law by failing to approve these minutes in a timely manner.   
 
We remind the Commission that the responsibility for complying with the Open Meeting 
Law rests with public bodies and members of such bodies, not with individual public employees, 
such as administrative staff.  See OML 2018-105.  We understand the demands on a public body 
with limited staff; however, the Commission is still obligated to timely approve minutes.  See 
OML 2018-111; OML 2017-115.  If staff resources are not available to create minutes for the 
Commission to approve, then Commission members must take on this responsibility to ensure 
compliance with the law.  See OML 2017-115.   
 
For the reasons stated above, we find that the Commission violated the Open Meeting 
Law by failing to approve minutes of its July 26, 2021, meeting in a timely manner.  We order 
immediate and future compliance with the law’s requirements, and we caution that similar future 
violations could be considered evidence of intent to violate the law.   
 
We now consider the complaint addressed by this determination to be resolved.  This 
determination does not address any other complaints that may be pending with our office or the 
Commission.  Please feel free to contact our office at (617) 963-2540 if you have any questions 
regarding this letter.
