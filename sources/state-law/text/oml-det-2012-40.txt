RE: Open Meeting Law Complaint 
Dear Mr. Stagnone: 
This office received a complaint from Dr. Roberta Camacho, dated July 26, 2011, 
alleging that the Stoughton Board of Selectmen (the "Board") violated the Open Meeting Law, 
G.L. c. 30A, §§ 18-25. Specifically, the complaint alleges that the Board considered and took 
action on a topic at its May 3, 2011 meeting that was anticipated by the Chair more than 48 hours 
before the meeting, but was not listed in the meeting notice. The complaint was first filed with 
the Board on June 1, 2011. The Board responded to the complaint by letter dated June 29, 2011. 
We find that the Board did not violate the Open Meeting Law because the Chair did not 
reasonably anticipate 48 hours before the May 3, 2011 meeting that the Fiscal Year 2012 
("FY12") budget would be discussed. See OML 2011-36; OML 2011-13. 1  In reaching this 
determination, we reviewed the June 1, 2011 complaint filed with the Board; the Board's June 
29, 2011 response to the complaint; and the July 26, 2011 complaint filed with our office. We 
also reviewed the notice and minutes of the Board's May 2, 2011 and May 3, 2011 meetings. 
Finally, we reviewed a video recording of the Board's May 3, 2011 meeting, provided to our 
office by the complainant. 
Open Meeting Law determinations may be found at the Attorney General's website, 
www.mass.gov/ago/openmeeting.

FACTS 
On April 26, 2011, the Board met to discuss the FY12 budget as proposed by the Finance 
Committee. The Board discussed an approximately $900,000 budget cut, but ultimately passed 
the budget for consideration at Town Meeting without the cut. On Ap;ril 29, 2011, the Board 
posted meeting notices for meetings on May 2 and May 3, 2011. The notice for the May 2, 2011 
meeting included the topic "Budget & Warrant [Articles]." The notice for the May 3, 2011 
meeting included the topic "Old/New Business," but did not include a topic regarding the budget 
or warrant articles. 
On May 2, 2011, the Board met and discussed the FY12 budget and warrant articles, and 
again considered an approximately $900,000 cut to the budget. The proposed cut failed to pass. 
The Board met the next night and, when the Board reached the section of the meeting reserved 
for "Old/New Business," Selectman John Anderson motioned to reconsider the budget and the 
proposed $900,000 cut. He explained that he learned new information at the Finance Committee 
meeting the night before regarding additional funds coming to the Town from the 
Commonwealth for school budget purposes. After some discussion, the Board passed the 
revised proposed budget, which included a $900,000 cut to the school budget, for consideration 
at the May 4, 2011 Town Meeting. In the Board's response to this Open Meeting Law 
complaint, Board Chair John Stagnone stated that he was unaware prior to the May 3, 2011 
meeting that Mr. Anderson was planning to raise the issue at that meeting. 
DISCUSSION 
The Open Meeting Law requires that a public body "post notice of every meeting at least 
48 hours prior to such meeting, excluding Saturdays, Sundays and legal holidays," and that the 
"[n]otice shall be printed in a legible, easily understandable format and shall contain the date, 
time and place of such meeting and a listing of topics that the chair reasonably anticipates will be 
discussed at the meeting." G.L. c. 30A, § 20. A public body may consider a topic at a meeting 
that was not listed in the meeting notice if it is a topic that the chair did not reasonably anticipate 
48 hours before the meeting. See  OML 2011-36; OML 2011-13. 
We find that that, here, the Chair did not reasonably anticipate 48 hours before the May 
3, 2011 meeting that the budget topic would be discussed by the Board at that meeting. The 
topic raised by Selectman Anderson was a result of information he discovered the previous night, 
and could not have been reasonably anticipated by the Chair 48 hours before the meeting. We 
recommend, however, that where a public body member anticipates raising a topic at a meeting 
after the notice has been posted but before the meeting date, the public body member should 
notify the chair so that the chair may either update the meeting notice with the topic or schedule 
discussion for a subsequent meeting.2

Q: May a public body consider a topic at a meeting that was not listed in the meeting notice? 
A: Yes, if it is a topic that the chair did not reasonably anticipate 48 hours before the meeting. If a meeting topic is 
proposed after the meeting notice is posted, the public body is encouraged to update its posting to provide the public 
with as much notice as possible of what subjects will be discussed during a meeting.

CONCLUSION 
We find that the Board did not violate the Open Meeting Law because the Chair did not 
reasonably anticipate 48 hours before the May 3, 2011 meeting that the FY12 budget would be 
discussed.
