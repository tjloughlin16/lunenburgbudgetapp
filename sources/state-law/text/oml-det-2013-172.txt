RE: Open Meeting Law Complaint 
Dear Attorney Corbo: 
This office received a complaint from Jeanne Golrick dated August 7, 2013, alleging that 
the Montague Board of Selectmen (the "Board") violated the Open Meeting Law, G.L. c. 30A, 
§§ 18-25. The complaint was originally filed with the Board on or about June 20, 2013, and you 
responded, on behalf of the Board, to the original complaint by letter dated June 27, 2013. In her 
complaint, Ms. Golrick alleges that the Board failed to post notice on the Town website for a 
meeting held on May 21, 2013. 
We reviewed the June 20, 2013 complaint; your June 27, 2013 response; and the August 
7, 2013 complaint filed with our office, requesting further review. We also reviewed the Board's 
May 21, 2013 meeting notice. 
Following our review, we find that the Board did not violate the Open Meeting Law. 
FACTS 
Based upon our review of the material listed above, the facts are as follows. On May 16, 
2013, the Board posted notice of its upcoming meeting, scheduled for May 21, 2013. The 
Town's official method for posting meeting notices is a weather-proof bulletin board attached to 
the outside of the Town Hall, near the main entrance. As a courtesy, Town public bodies, 
including the Board, generally try to post meeting notices on the Town website as well. The 
Board did not post notice for its May 21, 2013 meeting on the website, however. 
DISCUSSION 
The Open Meeting Law requires that "a public body shall post notice of every meeting at 
least 48 hours prior to such meeting, excluding Saturdays, Sundays and legal holidays," and that 
notice for a local public body "shall be filed with the municipal clerk and posted in a manner

conspicuously visible to the public at all hours in or on the municipal building in which the 
clerk's office is located." G.L. c. 30A, §§ 20(b), (c). The Attorney General's regulations 
provide that municipalities may choose an alternate notice posting method, including posting to a 
municipal website. 940 CMR 29.03(2)(b)(a). However, before adopting an alternative notice 
posting method, the municipality "shall file with the Attorney General written notice of adoption 
of the alternative method, including the website address where applicable." 940 CMR 
29.03(l)(c). 
As of the date of this letter, our office has not received written notice of the adoption of 
an alternative notice posting method, such as a Town website, for the Town of Montague. 
Accordingly, the Town's official notice posting method remains the bulletin board outside of 
Town Hall. See G.L. c. 30A, § 20(c); 940 CMR 29.03(2)(a). While it is not required, generally 
the Board additionally posts its notices on the Town's website. Its inability to do so for this 
particular meeting did not violate the law, as this additional notice location is merely a courtesy, 
and not the official posting method. OML 2013-42.1 The Board complied with the Open 
Meeting Law's requirements by posting notice of its May 21, 2013 meeting on the bulletin board 
outside of Town Hall. We therefore find no violation. 
For the reasons stated above, we find that the Board did not violate the Open Meeting 
Law. 
We now consider the complaint addressed by this determination to be resolved. This 
determination does not address any other complaints which may be pending with our office or 
the Board. Please feel free to contact me if you have any questions or believe any facts in this 
letter to be inaccurate.
