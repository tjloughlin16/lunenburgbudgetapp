RE: Open Meeting Law Complaint 
Dear Attorney Randazzo; 
This office received a complaint from Daryl Crossman on March 7, 2016, alleging that 
the Sandwich Board of Selectmen (the Board) violated the Open Meeting Law, G.L. c. 30A, 
§§ 18-25. The complaint was originally filed with the Board on or about January 26, 2016, 
and you responded, on behalf of the Board, to the complaint by letter dated February 16, 
2016. In his complaint, Mr. Crossman alleges that the Board improperly convened its January 
21,2016 meeting in a private, unposted location before entering executive session. 
Following our review, we find that the Board violated the Open Meeting Law in the 
manner alleged. In reaching this determination, we reviewed the original complaint, the 
Board's response to the complaint, and the complaint filed with our office requesting further 
review. We also reviewed the notice and minutes from the Board's January 21, 2016 
meeting. 
We find the facts as follows. During the Board's January 21, 2016 meeting, the first 
item on the agenda was an executive session. The meeting notice, timely posted on January 
19, 2016, listed the following topic: "5:30 P.M. Executive Session- M.G.L. C.30A, 
§21(a)... Purpose #6; Disposition of Real Property- Town SSVC Land RFP- Compliance with 
Purchase & Sales Agreement Requirements." The Board convened the meeting in the Town 
Manager's office at Town Hall1 and voted by roll call to convene in executive session for the 
purpose stated in the notice. The Board remained in this office during the executive session

DISCUSSION 
The Open Meeting Law requires that all meetings of a public body be conducted in an 
open session, with some exceptions. G.L. c. 30A, §§ 20(a), 21(a). A public body may enter 
executive, or closed, session provided that it has first convened in an open session, that a 
majority of members of the body have voted to go into executive session, and that the vote of 
each member is recorded by roll call and entered into the minutes. G.L. c. 30A, § 21(b). 
Before entering the executive session, the chair must state the purpose for the executive 
session, stating all subjects that may be revealed without compromising the pmpose for which 
the executive session was called, and the chair must publicly announce whether the open 
session will reconvene at the conclusion of the executive session. Id. 
Although executive sessions occur behind closed doors, all executive sessions must be 
preceded by an open meeting, and that meeting must be held in a location that is open and 
accessible to the public. See G.L. c. 30A, 20(a); OML 2012-48. By opening the January 21, 
2016 meeting in the Town Manager's office, a location that was not listed on the meeting 
notice, the Board denied the public the opportunity to attend and observe whether the Board 
properly followed the procedural requirements for entering executive session, as outlined 
above. See OML 2013-163. The Board should have identified the specific location of the 
meeting on the meeting notice.2 See G.L. c. 30A, § 20(b); 940 CMR 29.03(b). Additionally, 
the meeting notice should have indicated that the Board planned to convene in open session 
prior to the executive session. See OML 2015-13. Listing "open session" on the meeting 
notice is the only means by which members of the public are informed that a public body will, 
in fact, hold an open meeting that they are permitted to attend. Id. It appears, however, that 
this omission was an oversight for this particular meeting notice and is not the Board's usual 
practice. 
As a result of the Board's failure to convene its meeting in an open, publicly-noticed 
location, the Board may not continue to withhold the minutes of the January 21, 2016 
executive session under the Open Meeting Law. See G.L. c. 30A, § 22(1) (minutes of an 
executive session may be withheld from disclosure to the public as long as publication may 
defeat the lawful purposes of an executive session, provided that the executive session was 
held in compliance with the Open Meeting Law); OML 2014-42; OML 2014-30. The Board 
may continue to withhold the minutes from the general public, in whole or in part, under any 
applicable exemptions to the Public Records Law or the attorney/client privilege. 
CONCLUSION 
For the reasons stated above, the Board violated the Open Meeting Law by failing to 
specifically identify the meeting location in its meeting notice. We order immediate and 
future compliance with the Open Meeting Law and caution that future similar violations may
