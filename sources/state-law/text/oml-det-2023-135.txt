 RE: Open Meeting Law Complaint 
 
Dear Chair Doyle: 
 
This office received a complaint from Peter Hill on April 5, 2023, alleging that the 
Weston Traffic and Sidewalk Committee (the “Committee”) violated the Open Meeting Law, 
G.L. c. 30A, §§ 18-25. We understand that the complaint was originally filed with the 
Committee on or about January 23, 2023, and that the Committee did not respond. We 
understand the complaint to allege that the Committee failed to timely create and approve 
minutes for three meetings; failed to respond to requests for meeting minutes; and failed to post 
meeting minutes to a website.  
 
Following our review, we find that the Committee violated the Open Meeting Law by 
failing to timely approve minutes for three meetings. In reaching this determination, we reviewed 
the Open Meeting Law complaint and video recordings of Committee meetings held from March 
16, 2022, through and including February 15, 2023.
1  
 
The Open Meeting Law requires public bodies to create and approve accurate minutes for 
all meetings in a timely manner. G.L. c. 30A, § 22. “Timely manner” means “within the next 
three public body meetings or within 30 days, whichever is later, unless the public body can 
show good cause for further delay.” 940 CMR 29.11. Whenever possible, we recommend that 
minutes of a meeting be approved at the next meeting. See OML 2014-15; OML 2012-91.

The complaint alleges that the Committee failed to create and approve minutes for 
meetings held on February 16, June 15, and November 16, 2022.3 After reviewing video 
recordings of nine Committee meetings held from March 16, 2022, through February 15, 2023, 
we find that the Committee violated the Open Meeting Law as alleged. We order the Committee 
to approve minutes for its meetings held on February 16, June 15, and November 16 within 30 
days of the date of this determination and to provide these minutes to the Complainant. 
 
Although not raised in the complaint, we remind the Committee that when it anticipates 
discussing or approving a particular set of meeting minutes, it must identify those minutes on the 
meeting notice. See OML 2023-8; OML 2020-34. Additionally, we remind the Committee that 
when substantive edits to meeting minutes will be communicated to a quorum (i.e., simple 
majority) of the Committee, this must be done during a properly noticed and open meeting, not 
via email. See OML 2020-136.  
  
Next, to the extent the complaint alleges that the Committee failed to respond to the 
Complainant’s requests for meeting minutes, we decline to review this allegation where the 
complaint does not identify any particular request for minutes that the Committee failed to 
respond to. See OML Declination 7-19-23 (Fairhaven Historical Commission)(“Complainants 
must allege violations with a degree of specificity, as our office will not conduct broad audits of 
public bodies based on generalized allegations.”). Nonetheless, we remind the Committee that 
“[t]he minutes of an open session, if they exist and whether approved or in draft form, shall be 
made available upon request by any person within 10 days.” G.L. c. 30A, § 22(c) (emphasis 
added). 
 
Additionally, to the extent the complaint alleges that the Committee failed to post its 
meeting minutes to a website, we decline to review this allegation because, even if true, it does 
not constitute a violation of the Open Meeting Law. The Open Meeting Law requires that 
meeting minutes be made available upon request but does not require that minutes be published 
to a website or otherwise disseminated absent a request. See G.L. c. 30A, § 22; OML 2019-52.  
 
Finally, we remind the Committee that within 14 business days of receiving an Open 
Meeting Law complaint, the Committee must meet to review the complaint and respond in 
writing to the complainant, simultaneously providing our office with a copy of the complaint and 
response. See G.L. c. 30A, § 23(b); 940 CMR 29.05(5). 
 
For the reasons stated above, we find that the Committee violated the Open Meeting Law 
by failing to timely approve minutes for its meetings held on February 16, June 15, and 
November 16, 2022. We order the Committee to approve minutes for all three meetings and to 
provide these minutes to the Complainant within 30 days of the date of this letter. Additionally, 
we order the Committee’s immediate and future compliance with the Open Meeting Law and 
caution that similar future violations could be considered evidence of intent to violate the Law. 
 
We now consider the complaint addressed by this determination to be resolved. This 
determination does not address any other complaints that may be pending with the Committee or
