 RE: 
Open Meeting Law Complaint 
 
Dear Attorney Amara: 
This office received a complaint from Christine McCue Potts on April 23, 2020, alleging 
that the Medfield School Committee (the “Committee”) violated the Open Meeting Law, G.L. c. 
30A, §§ 18-25. The complaint was originally filed with the Committee on October 18, 2019, and 
the Committee responded by letter dated April 6, 2020.1 In her complaint, Ms. Potts alleges that 
the Committee’s meeting minutes for several meetings held March, April and June of 2019 are 
insufficiently detailed.2 
Following our review, we resolve this complaint by informal action in accordance with 
940 CMR 29.07(2)(a) and find that the Committee violated the Open Meeting Law. We resolved 
a similar complaint regarding many sets of the Committee’s 2014 through 2018 executive 
session meeting minutes in a prior determination, OML 2020-101,3 and found that the 
Committee violated the Open Meeting Law by failing to approve sufficiently detailed meeting 
minutes. For the same reasons explained therein, we find that the Committee’s March 11, 2019, 
April 1, 2019, and June 3, 2019, executive session meeting minutes are insufficiently detailed, in

 We order the Committee’s immedi ate and future compliance with the Open Meeting Law 
and caution the Committee that a determination by our office of a similar violation in the future 
may be considered evidence of intent to violate the Open Meeting Law. We also order the

and June 3, 2019, executive sessions to the best of its ability. The Committee shall certify its 
compliance with this order with our office within ninety (90) days of the Committee’s receipt of 
this letter. We welcome the Committee or counsel to reach out to our office to discuss this order. 
 We now consider the complaint  addressed by this determination to be resolved. This
