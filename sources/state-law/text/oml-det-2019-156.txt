RE: Open Meeting Law Complaint
Dear Attorney Carboni:
This office received six complaints from Joseph Zora on October 18, 2019, alleging that 
the Marion Board of Assessors (the “Board”) violated the Open Meeting Law, G.L. c. 30A, §§ 
18-25. The complaints were originally filed with the Board on August 19 and you responded, on 
behalf of the Board, by letters dated September 5.1 In his complaints, Mr. Zora alleges that the 
Board discussed an anticipated topic that did not appear on the Board’s meeting notices for six of 
its meetings. In a separate declination letter, we found that the complaints relating to the first five 
meetings were untimely, and therefore declined to review them. In this determination, we 
address the allegation that the notice for the Board’s August 6 meeting failed to list an 
anticipated topic.
We resolve this complaint by informal action in accordance with 940 CMR 29.07(2)(a), 
and find that the Board did not violate the Open Meeting Law as alleged. A public body must 
post notice of every meeting, including “the date, time and place of such meeting and a listing of 
topics that the chair reasonably anticipates will be discussed at the meeting.” G.L. c. 30A,
§ 20(b). Public bodies must list anticipated discussion topics in their meeting notices with 
“sufficient specificity to reasonably advise the public of the issues to be discussed at the 
meeting.” 940 CMR 29.03(l)(b). We generally consider a topic to include sufficient specificity 
when a reasonable member of the public could read the topic and understand the anticipated 
nature of the public body’s discussion. See OML 2018-60; OML 2011-44.
The Board’s August 6 notice included the item: “Executive Session pursuant to G.L. 
c. 30A, Section 21 (a)(7) and G.L. c. 59, Section 60 to discuss Abatements and Exemptions per 
submitted list.”2 Applications for tax abatement or exemption are confidential under G.L. c. 59,

determination does not address any other complaints that may be pending with our office or the
