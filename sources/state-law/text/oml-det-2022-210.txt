RE: Open Meeting Law Complaints 
Dear Mr. Hansen: 
This office received three complaints from Allyson Donovan in December 2021, alleging 
that the North Middlesex Regional School Committee (the “Committee”) violated the Open 
Meeting Law, G.L. c. 30A, §§ 18-25.  All three complaints were originally filed with the 
Committee on September 16, 2021, and you responded, on behalf of the Committee, by separate 
letters dated October 5, 2021.
1  The complaints allege that the Committee failed to properly post 
notice of the meetings that were held on September 10, September 13, and September 16.    
  
We appreciate the patience of the parties while we reviewed these matters.  We resolve 
this ma
tter by informal action, in accordance with 940 CMR 29.07(2)(a), after reviewing the 
original complaints, the Committee’s responses to the complaints, the complaints filed with our 
office requesting further review, and the notices and open session minutes of the Committee 
meetings held on September 10, September 13, and September 16.  In addition, we spoke with 
Attorney Nicholas J. Dominello, legal counsel for the Committee, on September 21, 2022.  We 
find that the Committee did not violate the Open Meeting Law in the ways alleged.   
 
A publi
c body must post notice of every meeting at least 48 hours in advance, not 
including Saturdays, Sundays, and legal holidays.  G.L. c. 30A, § 20(b).  The notice must be 
printed in a legible, easily understandable format and must contain the date, time and place of the 
meeting, as well as a listing of topics that the chair reasonably anticipates will be discussed.  
G.L. c. 30A, § 20(b).  The list of topics shall have “sufficient specificity to reasonably advise the

public of the issues to be discussed at the meeting.”  940 CMR 29.03(l)(b).  We generally 
consi
der a topic to be sufficiently specific when a reasonable member of the public could read 
the topic and understand the anticipated nature of the public body’s discussion.  See OML 2015-

For regional school district committees, noti
 ce shall be filed with the municipal clerk of 
each city and town within such district and each such municipal clerk shall post the notice in the 
manner prescribed for local public bodies in that city or town.  G.L. c. 30A, § 20(c).  A regional 
school district committee may designate the regional school district’s website as an alternative 
notice posting location.  940 CMR 29.03(2)(b).  The North Middlesex Regional School District 
has adopted its website, http://www.nmrsd.org, as the official method of posting notice.  
Although public bodies are welcome to post notice of their meetings in additional locations if 
they choose, a regional public body has one official notice posting location for purposes of 
complying with the Open Meeting Law.  See 940 CMR 29.03(2)(b)(3) & (c); OML 2021-94; 
OML 2020-20.  
 
Here, the Committee poste d notices to its website on September 8 at 9:55 A.M. for a 
meeting to be held on September 10 at 10:00 A.M., on August 9 at 2:45 P.M. for a meeting to be 
held on September 13 at 7:00 P.M, and on September 14 at 10:15 A.M. for a meeting to be held 
on September 16 at 7:30 P.M.  We note that notices were posted in two different locations on the 
regional school district website.  Specifically, the regional school district’s homepage includes a 
tab for “School Committee” in the header menu.  Under this tab are three additional links entitled 
“Members,” “Meetings” and “District Policies.”  The notices were prominently posted on the 
“Meetings” page, which listed the date and time of each meeting and included a copy of the 
actual agenda with the anticipated topics to be discussed during each meeting.  In addition, 
notices were also posted to a “Calendar,” which was a separate link accessible from the 
“Meetings” homepage.  If a regional school district adopts its website as the official method of 
notice posting, that website should include a page where all public body meeting notices can be 
located in order that members of the public can easily access notices.  See 940 C.M.R. 
29.03(2)(b).  Although notices were posted in two different locations, we find that the notices 
here were easily accessible.  Compare OML 2018-53 (noting that notices posted to the City 
website were not easily located as a “Notice” tab within the “Open Meetings Portal” did not 
contain any notices; rather, the “Meetings” tab contained the calendar for all meetings, and 
sometimes included a link to the public body’s agenda).   
 
We acknowledge that the notice for the September 16 meeting did not appear to be 
poste
d on the “Calendar” 48 hours prior to the meeting.  However, notice for this meeting was 
posted on the “Meetings” homepage.  We find that notices were properly posted in compliance 
with the Open Meeting Law where the Committee posted the notices for three meetings - 
September 10, September 13, and September 16 - more than 48 hours in advance in the official 
notice posting location.
