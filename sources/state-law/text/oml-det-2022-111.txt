 RE:  Open Meeting Law Complaint 
 
Dear Attorney Castro: 
 
This office received a complaint from Jack Rybicki on May 3, 2022, alleging that the 
Newbury Council on Aging (the “Council”) violated the Open Meeting Law, G.L. c. 30A, §§ 18-
25.1  The complaint was originally filed with the Council on March 28, and you responded, on 
behalf of the Council, by letter dated April 15.  The complaint alleges that topics listed on the 
notice for the Council meetings held on January 24 and February 28 were insufficiently specific. 
 
We resolve this matter by informal action, in accordance with 940 CMR 29.07(2)(a), 
after reviewing the original complaint, the Council’s response to the complaint, the complaint 
filed with our office requesting further review, and the notice, open session minutes, and a video 
recording of the Council’s February 28 meeting.2  We decline to review the allegation that the 
Council posted an insufficiently specific notice for the meeting held on January 24 because we 
find it to be untimely, as discussed below.  Following our review of the allegation involving the 
meeting held on February 28, we find that the Board did not violate the Open Meeting Law in 
the way alleged.  
 
To be considered timely, an Open Meeting Law complaint must be filed with the public 
body within 30 days of the alleged violation, or, if the alleged violation could not reasonably 
have been known at the time it occurred, then within 30 days of the date it should reasonably 
have been discovered.  G.L. c. 30A, § 23(b); 940 CMR 29.05(4).  When an alleged violation 
occurs during an open meeting, the alleged violation is reasonably discoverable at the time it

that meeting should have been filed within 30 days of the meeting.  Because the allegation at

The Open Meeting Law requires that, except in an emergency, “a public body shall post 
notice of every meeting at least 48 hours prior to such meeting, excluding Saturdays, Sundays 
and legal holidays.”  G.L. c. 30A, § 20(b).  The notice must be printed in a legible, easily 
understandable format and must contain the date, time and place of the meeting, the date and 
time that the notice was posted, as well as a listing of topics that the chair reasonably anticipates 
will be discussed.  Id; see also 940 CMR 29.03(l)(d); OML 2011-45. The list of topics shall have 
“sufficient specificity to reasonably advise the public of the issues to be discussed at the 
meeting.”  940 CMR 29.03(l)(b).  We generally consider a topic to be sufficiently specific when 
a reasonable member of the public could read the topic and understand the anticipated nature of 
the public body’s discussion.  See OML 2015-35.   
 
The Council posted notice for a meeting to be held on February 28.4  The notice listed the 
following seven topics: “Call to order,” “Public Comment,” “Review and approval of January 
24, 2022 Meeting Minutes,” “Council on Aging Director’s report,” “Other business,” “Executive 
Session as needed,” and “Adjourn.”  The complaint does not identify which of these topics are 
insufficient; rather, it suggests that the Council’s notices in general lack sufficient specificity.  
Open Meeting Law complaints must allege violations with a degree of specificity, as our office 
will not conduct broad audits of public bodies based on generalized allegations.  See OML 2016-
57; OML 2014-119; OML 2012-106.  Nevertheless, because the Council did not convene in 
executive session, there are only two topics listed on the February 28 notice that could have been 
insufficient - “Council on Aging Director’s report” and “Other business.”  See OML 2018-11 
(finding no violation with respect to the sufficiency of the executive session topic listed on the 
notice because the public body did not convene in executive session); see also OML 2022-62 
(while the Open Meeting Law requires that the chair list on the meeting notice all topics 
anticipated for discussion 48 hours before the meeting, a public comment period is not itself a 
topic for the public body’s discussion that needs to be listed on the meeting notice).  
 
The February 28 meeting was held as planned, and the Board received a report from the 
Council on Aging Director, who updated the Council on recently held events and future 
programming for seniors.  We find that the topic “Council on Aging Director’s report” is simply 
a time to notify Council members and residents of upcoming events, and that such 
announcements are generally not pieces of business that Council members anticipate discussing.

“Where a public body does not actually engage in any deliberation on a particular topic, it cannot 
violate the Open Meeting Law with respect to the sufficiency of the meeting notice.”  OML 
2016-76; see also OML 2017-33.  Moreover, when a meeting notice includes as a topic a report 
or presentation by somebody who is not a member of the public body, such as “Administrator’s 
Report” or “Superintendent’s Report,” we have declined to find a violation of the Open Meeting 
Law for lack of specificity where the public body chair did not anticipate the specific nature of 
the deliberations to be had, if any.  See OML 2019-94; OML 2018-33; OML 2012-19.  
Nonetheless, we encourage the Chair as a best practice to solicit from the Director and include 
on the meeting notice more detail regarding the precise matters to be discussed.  Id.   
 
With respect to the topic of “Other business,” the Council maintains that this topic is a 
time for members to discuss any business that the Chair “could not have reasonably anticipated.” 
We find that the Board’s use of this language on the meeting notice did not violate the law’s 
requirements.  See OML 2013-13.  While a public body may include this type of item on a 
meeting agenda, as a best practice we recommend that, when including such a topic, public 
bodies indicate explicitly that the time is being reserved for topics not anticipated by the chair.  
Here, we find no evidence that the matters that were raised during the meeting under “Other 
business” were reasonably anticipated by the Chair.  
 
For the reasons stated above, we find that the Council did not violate the Open Meeting 
Law.  We now consider the complaint addressed by this determination to be resolved.  This 
determination does not address any other complaints that may be pending with the Board or with 
our office.  Please feel free to contact our office at (617) 963-2540 if you have any questions 
regarding this letter.
