RE: Open Meeting Law Complaint 
Dear Attorney Giaimo: 
This office received a complaint from Dr. Lydia Rosner, dated October 11, 2014, alleging 
that the Center Pond Restoration and Protection District Prudential Committee (the 
"Committee") violated the Open Meeting Law, G.L. c. 30A, §§ 18-25. The complaint was 
originally filed with the Committee on August 24, 2014, and you responded on behalf of the 
Committee by letter dated September 23, 2014.1 In her complaint, Dr. Rosner alleges that the 
Committee failed to create sufficiently detailed meeting minutes. She also alleges that, on 
certain occasions, the Committee held meetings without creating minutes at all. 
Following our review, we find that, on one occasion, the Committee violated the Open 
Meeting Law by holding a meeting without creating minutes. However, we decline to review the 
complaint's other allegations because they are untimely. We find that the minutes of the 
Committee's August 15, 2014 meeting meet the requirements of the Open Meeting Law. 
In reaching a determination, we reviewed the original complaint; the Committee's 
response; the request for further review filed with our office; e-mail correspondence between Dr. 
Rosner and Committee Chair Anne Shrader; the agenda for a meeting of the Committee held on 
September 16, 2012; and the minutes for a meeting of the Committee held on August 15, 2014. 
Finally, we reviewed a signed statement from Chair Shrader, dated February 4, 2015.

FACTS 
We find the facts as follows. The Committee is a three-member public body created in 
2010, which meets periodically to discuss and act on matters of relevance to the Center Pond 
Restoration and Protection District (the "District"). On July 26, 2014, Dr. Rosner made a request 
by e-mail to Chair Shrader for "all Prudential Committee meeting minutes." The Committee 
provided Dr. Rosner with minutes for fifteen meetings for the period between September 2010 
and August 2014. Although the Committee posted notice for a meeting on September 16, 2012, 
and met on that date, it did not create any minutes for this meeting. 
After it had responded to Dr. Rosner's request, the Committee held a meeting on August 
15, 2014. On this date, the Committee met for about two hours and discussed, among other 
topics, the formation of subcommittees, the role of the Moderator during the upcoming District 
Meeting, and the hiring of an administrative assistant to improve communications with District 
residents. The minutes for the August 15, 2014 meeting contained robust summaries of each 
discussion. 
DISCUSSION 
I. Most of the Complaint's Allegations are Untimely. 
The Open Meeting Law states that all complaints must be filed within 30 days of the 
alleged violation. G.L. c. 30A, § 23(b). If the alleged violation could not reasonably have been 
discovered at the time it occurred, the complaint must be filed with the public body within 30 
days of when it reasonably could have been discovered. 940 CMR 29.05(3). Complaints 
regarding the sufficiency or accuracy of open session meeting minutes must be filed within 30 
days of the date on which those minutes were approved. See OML Declination 5-4-2012 
(Sheffield Planning Board). The Open Meeting Law does not specify in what manner minutes 
must be approved. See OML 2014-126. 
Here, all Committee meetings took place in open session. The minutes contain no 
indication of when each set of minutes was approved but, according to Chair Shrader's written 
statement, "[s]ince 2010, the usual practice with respect to minutes of Prudential Committee 
meetings has been for me to take notes while I am conducting the meeting, type them up after the 
meeting, and circulate them to the other Prudential Committee members in advance of the 
following meeting. The Prudential Committee has not formally voted to adopt each set of 
minutes. I have considered the minutes approved and final because there was no objection from 
the other members." Because this was the Committee's practice, and given that years have 
passed since most of the meetings at issue, we find that all but one of the complainant's 
allegations are untimely because they were filed more than 30 days after the approval of the 
minutes at issue. The only timely allegations concerning existing meeting minutes relate to the 
August 15, 2014 meeting, discussed below. 
II. The Committee's August 15. 2014 Minutes are Sufficient. 
The Open Meeting Law was enacted "to eliminate much of the secrecy surrounding 
deliberations and decisions on which public policy is based." Ghiglione v. School Committee of 
Southbridge. 376 Mass. 70, 72 (1978). Accordingly, the law requires that a public body "create 
and maintain accurate minutes of all meetings, including executive sessions, setting forth the

date, time and place, the members present or absent, a summary of the discussions on each 
subject, a list of documents and other exhibits used at the meeting, the decisions made and the 
actions taken at each meeting, including the record of all votes." G.L. c. 3 OA, § 22(a). Minutes 
must include sufficient detail and accuracy so that a member of the public who did not attend the 
meeting could read the minutes and have a clear understanding of what took place. See OML 
2014-54; OML 2013-157. They do not have to be transcripts, but minutes must reflect the 
discussions that occurred, the actions taken by the body, and the positions taken by the individual 
members. See G.L. c. 30A, § 22(a); OML 2012-41; OML 2011-34. The minutes for the August 
15, 2014 meeting contain this level of detail, thus we find no violation of the Open Meeting Law 
with respect to those minutes. 
We note that Chair Shrader's February 4, 2015 written statement reads, in part, "Starting 
with our next meeting, the Prudential Committee will have someone to take and transcribe the 
minutes of the meetings... The agenda for the meeting will include an item for 'approval of 
minutes' and there will be a vote taken and recorded." We commend the Committee's efforts to 
ensure future compliance with the Open Meeting Law. 
III. On One Occasion, the Committee Held a Meeting Without Creating Minutes. 
The Committee provided our office with a meeting agenda concerning one date, 
September 16, 2012, for which no corresponding meeting minutes were created. Because no 
minutes for this meeting were created, the allegation concerning the September 16, 2012 meeting 
was not reasonably discoverable until Dr. Rosner received the Committee's response to her 
request for minutes, thus we find this allegation is also timely. The Committee's failure to create 
minutes for its September 16, 2012 meeting clearly violated the Open Meeting Law. See G.L. c. 
30A, § 22(a); OML 2014-72. 
For the reasons stated above, we find that the Committee violated the Open Meeting Law. 
We order immediate and future compliance with the Open Meeting Law, and caution that similar 
violations in the future may be considered evidence of intent to violate the law. Further, we 
order the Committee to create minutes for its September 16, 2012 meeting to the best of its 
ability, through whatever means are available, including the memories and individual notations 
of the attendees. Compliance with this order should occur within thirty (30) days of the 
Committee's receipt of this determination. 
We now consider the complaint addressed by this determination to be resolved. This 
determination does not address any other complaints that may be pending with our office or the 
Committee. Please feel free to contact the Division at (617) 963 - 2540 if you have any 
questions.
