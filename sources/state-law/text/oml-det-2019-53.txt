This office received two complaints from Peter Douglas on October 22, 2018, and 
January 9, 2019, alleging that the Marion Zoning Board of Appeals (the “Board”) violated the

Board on or about August 14, 2018, and November 30, 2018, and you responded to the

reviewed the original complaints, the Board’s responses to the complaints, and the complaints 
filed with our office requesting further review. We also reviewed the Board’s November 1, 
2018, meeting notice and spoke with you by telephone on May 2, 2019.

FACTS
We find the facts as follows. On July 23, 2018, the complainant submitted a request to 
the Board for a copy of minutes of meetings held on April 19, May 24, June 28, and July 12, 
2018. At the time of its response to the complaint on September 7, 2018, the Board had not 
responded to the request for minutes nor created and approved minutes for meetings held 
since April 2018.
The Town’s official meeting notice posting location is the calendar page on the Town 
website. For its November 1, 2018, meeting, the Board posted notice of the meeting at that 
location on October 30, 2018. The Board also posted a meeting notice on its own webpage. 
The notice posted on its webpage, however, was actually the notice for a different meeting.
DISCUSSION
I. The Board Failed to Timely Respond to a Request for Meeting Minutes and
Failed to Timely Approve Those Minutes.
The Open Meeting Law requires that “[mjinutes of all open sessions shall be created 
and approved in a timely manner. The minutes of an open session, if they exist and whether 
approved or in draft form, shall be made available upon request by any person within 10 
days.” G.L. c. 30A, § 22(c).
The complainant alleges that the Board failed to respond to his request for certain 
meeting minutes. The Board responds that the minutes did not exist at the time of the 
complainant’s request. We credit the Board’s account that minutes did not exist at the time it 
had received the complainant’s request. However, the Board was still required to respond 
within 10 calendar days of the request with an explanation of whether the minutes did or did 
not exist in either approved or draft form. See OML 2017-50; OML 2016-71; OML 2015- 
173.3 Accordingly, the Board violated the Open Meeting Law by failing to timely respond to 
a request for meeting minutes.
Additionally, although the Open Meeting Law does not require that minutes be 
published to a website or disseminated absent a request,4 see OML 2017-25, it does require 
public bodies to create and approve meetings minutes in a timely manner. See G.L. c. 30A, § 
22(c). “Timely manner” means “within the next three public body meetings or within 30 
days, whichever is later, unless the public body can show good cause for further delay.” See 
940 CMR 29.11; OML 2018-48. Whenever possible, we recommend that minutes of a

II. The Board’s November L 2018, Meeting Notice was Properly Posted.
The Open Meeting Law requires a public body to post notice of a meeting at least 48 
hours in advance of a meeting. G.L. c. 30A, § 20(b). The municipal clerk must post notice 
of meetings in a manner conspicuously visible to the public at all hours in or on the municipal 
building in which the clerk's office is located. G.L. c. 30A, § 20(c). Alternatively, the Chief 
Executive Officer of the municipality may authorize the adoption of the municipal website as 
the official method of posting notice. 940 CMR 29.03(2)(1).
The complainant alleges that the Board failed to post notice of its November 1, 2018, 
meeting notice at least 48 hours in advance. The Board responds that the notice was properly 
posted on the calendar page of the Town’s website, which is the Town’s official notice 
posting method, but concedes that an additional notice posted on the Board’s webpage for this 
meeting was actually the notice for another meeting. Because the notice was properly posted 
to the Town’s official notice posting location at least 48 hours before the meeting, we find no 
violation of the law. See OML 2018-53; OML 2017-99.
CONCLUSION
For the reasons stated above, we find that the Board violated the Open Meeting Law 
by failing to properly respond to a request for meeting minutes within 10 days and by failing 
to create and approve meeting minutes in a timely manner. We order immediate and future 
compliance with the law’s requirements and we caution that similar future violations could be 
considered evidence of intent to violate the law. Additionally, we order the Board to timely 
create and approve its meeting minutes within 30 days of the date of this letter, in accordance 
with the guidance above.
