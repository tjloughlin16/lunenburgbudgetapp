A public body must post notice of every meeting at least 48 hours in advance, not 
including Saturdays, Sundays, and legal holidays.  G.L. c. 30A, § 20(b).  Notices must include “a 
listing of topics that the chair reasonably anticipates will be discussed at the meeting.”  Id.  The 
list of topics must have sufficient specificity to reasonably advise the public of the issues to be 
discussed at the meeting.  940 CMR 29.03(l)(b).  A public body may discuss topics that were not 
included on the meeting notice if those topics truly were not reasonably anticipated 48 hours in 
advance of the meeting.  See OML 2024-74; OML 2019-131; OML 2018-119.5 
 
We generally consider a topic to be sufficiently specific when a reasonable member of 
the public could read the topic and understand the anticipated nature of the public body’s 
discussion.  See OML 2024-74; OML 2019-134; OML 2015- 35.  The Open Meeting Law 
requires that the notice describe topics with sufficient specificity so that the public can make an 
informed decision whether to attend the meeting to observe the discussion regarding a topic of 
interest.  See OML 2023-86; OML 2019-95; OML 2018-22.   
 
In its response, the Board acknowledges that the topics added to the agenda at the 
beginni
ng of the May 13 meeting “could have been reasonably anticipated more than 48 hours in 
advance of the meeting.”  We find, therefore, that the Board violated the Open Meeting Law by 
discussing topics at the May 13 meeting which were reasonably anticipated but were not listed 
on the posted notice, to wit, Open Meeting Law complaints and the topics presented by the 
Police Chief. 
 
In the request to our office for further review of the complaint, the compl
 ainant urges that 
we find the Board’s violation intentional.  See G.L. c. 30A, § 23(c).  An intentional violation is 
an “act or omission by a public body or a member thereof, in knowing violation of [the Open 
Meeting Law].”  940 CMR 29.02.  An intentional violation may be found where the public body 
acted with deliberate ignorance of the Law’s requirements or has previously been advised that 
certain conduct violates the Open Meeting Law.  Id.  In OML 2024-74, issued on April 17, 2024, 
we found the Board in violation of the Open Meeting Law for posting insufficiently specific 
notice of its September 5, 2023, meeting. 
 
The B
oard argues that the violation we found in OML 2024-74 is distinguishable from 
the violation here, in that the previous violation concerned a topic (“Comment of the Chair”)  
that was listed on the notice but was not sufficiently specific as to the content of that topic, 
whereas the violation here concerns late notice of a topic that was not included on the notice at 
all.  Although a close question, we agree that OML 2024-74 concerned a different aspect of the 
Open Meeting Law’s notice requirement.  Therefore, we find that the Board’s violation was not 
intentional.   
 
Although we do not find the violation intentional, we take this opportunity to caution the 
Board about what appears to be a standing item in its notices, “Chairman’s Additions or

Deletions.”  Inclusion of such a placeholder does not immunize the Board against a finding of an 
Open Meeting Law violation if the Board discusses an unnoticed topic that was reasonably 
anticipated 48 hours prior to the meeting.  Boilerplate topics and placeholder topics lack 
sufficient specificity to satisfy the Open Meeting Law.6  See OML 2024-106; 2023-150; OML 
2013-164.  For example, “Chairman’s Report” does not sufficiently describe a topic on a meeting 
notice.  See OML 2023-199; OML 2012-34.  The chair is required to list with specificity those 
topics they reasonably anticipate will be discussed.  G.L. c. 30A, § 20(b). 
 
For the
 reasons stated above, we find that the Board violated the Open Meeting Law by 
posting a notice with insufficient specificity for its May 13 meeting.  We order the Board’s 
immediate and future compliance with the Open Meeting Law.  We now consider the complaint 
addressed by this determination to be resolved.  This determination does not address any other 
complaints that may be pending with the Board or with our office.  Please feel free to contact our
