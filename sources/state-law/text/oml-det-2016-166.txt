RE: Open Meeting Law Complaint 
Dear Chair Deroian: 
This office received a complaint from Karen Hill on April 20, alleging that the 
Townsend Conservation Commission (the "Commission") violated the Open Meeting 
Law, G.L. c. 30A, §§ 18-25.1 The complaint was originally filed with the Commission on 
or about April 7, The Commission never responded to the complaint. In her complaint, 
Ms. Hill alleges that the Commission failed to timely approve its June 10, 2015 meeting 
minutes. 
Following our review, we find that the Commission violated the Open Meeting 
Law in the way alleged. Additionally, we find that the Commission violated the Open 
Meeting Law by failing to respond to this Open Meeting Law complaint. In reaching a 
determination, we reviewed the original complaint and we spoke with the Town 
Administrator on November 9. 
We find the facts as follows. The Commission attempted to approved its June 10, 
2015 executive session minutes on July 22, 2015. The Commission discussed them during 
that meeting, but did not approve them. The complainant discovered this when she 
obtained a copy of the draft minutes in response to a request in March. The Commission

DISCUSSION 
Public bodies have an obligation to "create and maintain accurate minutes of all 
meetings." G.L. c. 30A, § 22(a). These meeting minutes must be reviewed and approved 
in a timely manner, G.L. c. 30A, § 22(c). Although the Open Meeting Law does not define 
"timely manner," we recommend that minutes of a meeting be approved at the next 
meeting, whenever possible. See OML 2014-15; OML 2012-91.3 For bodies that meet 
weekly or bi-monthly, we have concluded that a delay of two to three months in approving 
minutes does not comply with the law. See OML 2016-62. Executive session minutes 
may be withheld from disclosure "as long as publication may defeat the lawful purposes of 
the executive session, but no longer." G.L. c. 30A, § 22(f). 
Although the Commission attempted to timely approve its June 10, 2015 executive 
session minutes a month later, on July 22, 2015, it did not do so. Since that time, the 
Commission has not approved them. Even if the Commission believed that the executive 
session purpose warranted continued non-disclosure, the Board was obligated to approve 
final minutes in a timely manner. See OML 2016-57. This delay violated the Open 
Meeting Law's requirement that minutes be approved in a timely manner. See id.; OML 
2016-122. Furthermore, the Commission never responded to this complaint. The Open 
Meeting Law requires that within 14 business days after receiving a complaint the public 
body shall review the complaint's allegations; take remedial action, if appropriate; and 
send to the Attorney General a copy of the complaint and a description of any remedial 
action taken. G.L. c. 30A, § 23(b); 940 CMR 29.05(5). Accordingly, we find that the 
Commission also violated the law by failing to comply with the law's requirements for 
responding to this complaint. 
CONCLUSION 
We find that the Commission violated the Open Meeting Law by failing to timely 
approve its June 10, 2015 executive session minutes and by failing to respond to an Open 
Meeting Law complaint. We order the Commission's immediate and future compliance 
with the law's requirements, and we caution that similar future violations could be 
considered evidence of intent to violate the law. Additionally, we order the Commission to 
approve its June 10, 2015 executive session minutes within 30 days of its receipt of this 
determination. 
We now consider the complaint addressed by this determination to be resolved.
