RE: Open Meeting Law Complaint 
Dear Attorney Blake: 
This office received a complaint from Ms. Audrey Hayward, filed with our office on 
January 31,2014, alleging that the Hanson Board of Selectmen (the "Board") violated the Open 
Meeting Law, G.L. c. 30A, §§ 18-25. The complaint was originally filed with the Board on 
November 19, 2013. After being granted an extension by this Office, the Board responded to the 
complaint through its Attorney by letter dated December 27, 2013. Specifically, the complaint 
alleges that the Board posted a "misleading" notice for its October 22, 2013 meeting during 
which a "public hearing" took place. 
Following our review, we find that the Board did not violate the Open Meeting Law. In 
reaching this determination, we reviewed the November 19, 2013 complaint filed with the 
Board; the Board's December 27, 2013 response; and the January 31, 2014 complaint filed with 
our office. We also reviewed the notice, minutes, and an audio recording of the Board's October 
22, 2013 meeting.

DISCUSSION 
The complaint alleges that by allowing Mr. Smith to address the Board and discuss the 
state of the property during the meeting, the Board held a public hearing, thus violating state law 
by failing to follow the proper procedure to notice and convene such a hearing. The Open 
Meeting Law establishes the procedures that public bodies are required to follow when 
convening meetings, as that term is defined in the statute.2 Accordingly, our review of the facts 
is limited to whether we are able to find an Open Meeting Law violation. We do not review, and 
offer no opinion on, whether there was a violation of any public hearing law. 
The Open Meeting Law states that, "[n]o person shall address a meeting of a public body 
without permission of the chair." G.L. c. 30A, § 20(f). The Open Meeting Law gives the chair 
of the public body discretion to decide whether or not to permit public comment during a 
meeting, and we encourage public bodies to allow for as much public participation as time 
permits. OML 2014-23; OML 2012-78. Therefore, a public body does not violate the Open 
Meeting Law by allowing public comment on an item that was included on a meeting notice. 
See OML 2011-6. While the Open Meeting Law requires that the chair list on the meeting notice 
all topics anticipated for discussion 48 hours before the meeting, there is no requirement that the 
Chair include a specific notice for public comment on those topics. See G.L. c. 30A, § 20(b). 
Furthermore, it is reasonably inferable that a public body may allow the public to comment on 
items listed on its posted agenda. See OML 2011-6. We therefore find that the Board's notice 
did not violate the Open Meeting Law.
