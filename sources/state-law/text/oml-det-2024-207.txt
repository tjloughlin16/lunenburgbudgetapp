This office received a complaint from Patrick Higgins on August 16, 2024,
1 alleging that 
the Somerset Board of Water and Sewer Commissioners (the “Board”) violated the Open 
Meeting Law, G.L. c. 30A, §§ 18-25. The complaint was originally filed with the Board on or 
about July 22, and, after an extension of time granted by our office, you responded on behalf of 
the Board by letter dated August 15. The complaint alleges that the Board failed to timely 
approve the minutes of its May 8 meeting. 
 
Following our review, we find that the Board violated the Open Meeting Law as alleged. 
In reaching this determination, we reviewed the Open Meeting Law complaint, the Board’s 
response, the request for further review, and the Town of Somerset’s municipal website, 
https://www.townofsomerset.org/, for meeting notices.  
 
The Board held a meeting on May 8. Thereafter, the Board held a joint meeting with the 
Board of Selectmen on May 15, and additional meetings on May 22, June 13, and July 24. 
During the Board’s July 24 meeting it approved the minutes of its May 8 meeting.  
 
The Open Meeting Law requires public bodies to create “and maintain accurate minutes 
of all meetings, including executive sessions.” G.L. c. 30A, § 22(a). The Law further requires 
that minutes of open session meetings “be created and approved in a timely manner.” G.L. 
c. 30A, § 22(c). The Attorney General’s Open Meeting Law regulations provide that “timely

manner” means “within the next three public body meetings or within 30 days, whichever is 
lat
er, unless the public body can show good cause for further delay.” 940 CMR 29.11(2); see 
OML 2018-48.2 Whenever possible, we recommend that minutes of a meeting be approved at the 
next meeting. 940 CMR 29.11(2); see OML 2018-67; OML 2017-133.  
 
The Division of Open Government has long explained that the Open Meeting Law and 
the A
ttorney General’s Open Meeting Law regulations do not distinguish between “regular” 
meetings and other types of meetings—such as joint meetings, workshops, public hearings, or 
“special meetings”—for purposes of calculating the timely approval of minutes. See OML 2019-
115 (no exception for “single topic” meetings or meetings not “normally scheduled”); OML 
2023-129 (no exception for joint meetings with other public bodies). The Board acknowledges 
the Division’s longstanding determination on this point; nonetheless, the Board asserts that it had 
good cause for delay in the approval of its May 8 meeting minutes because its May 15 meeting 
was a joint meeting. Specifically, the Board notes that the May 15 meeting was held to address 
two discrete topics and asserts that “it would [have been] inappropriate and an inefficient use of 
the Select Board’s time and resources to require a discussion and vote on the Board’s May 8 
meeting minutes[, a matter] over which the Select Board has no jurisdiction.” We are not 
persuaded.   
 
First, the Board was not required to approve the minutes of its May 8 meeting during the 
May 15 m
eeting. The May 15 meeting was the next meeting following the May 8 meeting; the 
Board could have approved the minutes during its May 22 or June 13 meetings instead. 940 
CMR 29.11(2). Moreover, the Board was not required to approve the minutes during a meeting 
at all. We have consistently explained that the Open Meeting Law does not govern the method 
for approving meeting minutes and public bodies may choose the method for approval. See OML 
2020-81; OML 2019-107; OML 2019-9. Acceptable methods include, but are not limited to, 
approval by majority vote of the public body during a meeting, approval by the public body chair 
alone or by another designated individual, approval by consent of the body (approved unless 
there is an objection), or approval by a parent public body on behalf of a subcommittee. See 
OML 2024-25; OML 2019-107; OML 2019-25; OML 2017-34. Finally, as noted above, we have 
consistently explained that the Open Meeting Law and the regulations do not distinguish 
between different types of meetings for purposes of calculating the timely approval of meeting 
minutes.  
 
Bec
ause the Board failed to approve the minutes of its May 8 meeting within three 
meetings or 30 days and because the Board has not shown good cause for the delay in approving 
these minutes, we find that the Board violated the Open Meeting Law as alleged. We order the 
Board’s immediate and future compliance with the Open Meeting Law and caution that similar 
future violations could be considered evidence of an intent to violate the Law. Because the 
minutes at issue have been approved, we do not order further remedial action.
