 RE: Open Meeting Law Complaint 
 
Dear Ms. Crispo: 
 
This office received a complaint from Deborah Riley on September 23, 2025, alleging 
that the Quincy Board of Registrars of Voters (the “Board”) violated the Open Meeting Law, 
G.L. c. 30A, §§ 18-25.  The complaint was originally filed with the Board on or about August 
12, 2025.  You
1 responded to the complaint, on behalf of the Board, by email on April 24, 2026.2  
The complaint alleges that the Board failed to timely post notice of a meeting held on July 15, 
2025.    
 
We appreciate the patience of the parties while we resolved this matter.  We resolve this 
complaint by informal action in accordance with 940 CMR 29.07(1), after reviewing the 
original complaint, the Board’s response to the complaint, the complaint filed with our office 
requesting further review, the notice of the Board meeting held on July 15, 2025, and the notice 
and minutes of the Quincy City Council meeting held on July 21, 2025.  Following our review, 
we find that the Board violated the Open Meeting Law in the way alleged.

Except in the case of an emergency, the Open Meeting Law requires that public bodies 
post notice of every meeting “at least 48 hours prior to such meeting, excluding Saturdays, 
Sundays and legal holidays.”  G.L. c. 30A, § 20(b).  “Notice shall be printed in a legible, easily 
understandable format and shall contain the date, time and place of such meeting and a listing of 
topics that the chair reasonably anticipates will be discussed at the meeting.”  Id.   
 
On July 14, 2025, at 4:30 P.M., the Board posted notice of a meeting to be held on 
Tuesday, July 15, 2025, at 5:00 P.M.  The notice included one topic – “Temporary change of 
polling location for Ward 6 Precinct 5 due to construction of Squantum Elementary School.”  
The notice stated that the meeting would be held via Zoom and provided information necessary 
to access the meeting, including a link to the Zoom meeting, as well as the Webinar ID number 
and passcode required to log into the meeting.   
 
The July 15, 2025, meeting was held as planned.  However, the Board explains that it 
“stopped what we were doing immediately” upon realizing that the Zoom link listed on the 
notice was incorrect and did not work.  The Board acknowledges though that Chair Nicole 
Crispo informed the Board of the plan for the temporary change in polling location for Ward 6 
Precinct 5 but notes that the Board did not take any vote.
3    
 
The complaint alleges that the Board failed to timely post notice of the meeting held on 
July 15, 2025.  Because the Board posted notice of the July 15, 2025, meeting on July 14, 2025, 
we find that the Board violated the Open Meeting Law by posting notice of a meeting less than 
48 hours prior to the meeting. 
 
In addition, we fin
 d that the Board violated the Open Meeting Law by failing to record 
the date and time that the notice was posted.  The Open Meeting Law requires that “the date and 
time that the notice is posted shall be conspicuously recorded thereon or therewith.”  940 CMR 
29.03(1)(d); see OML 2011-45.4  The date and time of posting may be listed either on the 
meeting notice itself or on the website next to the link to the meeting notice.  See OML 2019-
102; OML 2018-53.  Recording the date and time enables the public to observe that public 
bodies are complying with the Open Meeting Law’s notice requirements and further provides 
this office with evidence of compliance with the law.  See OML 2013-44.  As evidenced here, 
the failure to record the date and time of posting impeded our and the public’s ability to 
determine whether the notice was timely posted and underscores the importance of this 
requirement.   
 
Finally, although not specifically raised in the complaint, we note our concern that the 
Board posted a notice with an incorrect Zoom link thus making the July 15, 2025, meeting 
inaccessible to the public.  See OML 2024-63 (noting that the public was essentially locked out 
of a meeting where the public body utilized the waiting room function in Zoom but then did not

monitor the waiting room for the duration of the meeting); OML 2022-180 (“ [T]he Commission 
violated the Open Meeting Law when, despite learning that technical problems prevented the 
meeting from being streamed live via the Zoom link listed on the notice, the Commission 
proceeded with the meeting.”).  At the core of the Open Meeting Law is the requirement that 
meetings of public bodies be open and accessible to the public.  G.L. c. 30A, § 20(a) (except 
when meeting in executive session, “all meetings of a public body shall be open to the public.”).  
Generally, access to a meeting must include the opportunity to be physically present as well as to 
see and hear what is being discussed by the members of the public body.  See OML 2020-100; 
OML 2018-75; OML 2016-54.  Public bodies are allowed to provide the public with “adequate, 
alternative” access to meetings through technology that enables the public to clearly follow the 
proceedings of the public body while those activities are occurring instead of providing in-person 
access.  See OML 2025-68; Chapter 20, § 20(b) of the Acts of 2021.  We remind the Board to 
take care to ensure that meeting access information is listed correctly on the meeting notice and 
commend the Board here for adjourning the July 15, 2025, meeting when it learned that the 
Zoom link was incorrect. 
 
For the reasons stated above, we find that the Board violated the Open Meeting Law.  We 
order immediate and future compliance with the law’s requirements, and we caution that similar 
future violations could be considered evidence of intent to violate the law.  In addition, to the 
extent it has not already done so, we order the Board to create minutes of the July 15, 2025, 
meeting to the best of its ability, through whatever means are available, including the memories 
and individual notations of the attendees.  See OML 2020-69; OML 2020-53.   
 
We now consider the complaint addressed by this determination to be resolved.  This 
determination does not address any other complaints that may be pending with our office or the 
Board.  Please feel free to contact our office at (617) 963-2540 if you have any questions 
regarding this letter.
