RE: Open Meeting Law Complaint 
Dear Attorney Bard: 
This office received a complaint from Edmond Neal, dated September 14, 2017, alleging 
that the Sturbridge Community Preservation Committee (the "Committee") violated the Open 
Meeting Law, G.L. c. 30A, §§ 18-25. The complaint was originally filed with the Committee on 
June 28, 2017, and, after an extension of time was granted, the Committee responded by letter 
dated August 3, 2017. In his complaint, Mr. Neal alleges that the Committee violated the Open 
Meeting Law by: (i) failing to provide open session minutes within ten days of request, (ii) 
failing to approve minutes in a timely manner, (iii) failing to create and maintain minutes of all 
meetings, and (iv) creating minutes that lacked sufficient detail. 
We appreciate the patience of the parties while we reviewed this matter. Following our 
review, we find that the Committee violated the Open Meeting Law by failing to provide minutes 
within ten days or request and by failing to approve minutes in a timely manner. However, we 
find that the Committee has since created minutes of all meetings. Finally, we decline to review 
the sufficiency of the minutes referenced in the complaint. In reaching this determination, we 
reviewed the original complaint, the Committee's response, and the request for further review 
filed with our office. We also reviewed supplementary e-mails from the Committee's legal 
counsel dated May 10 and June 13, 2018, as well as a supplementary letter from the complainant 
dated June 5, 2018. Finally, we spoke with the complainant by telephone on March 29, 2018. 
We find the facts as follows. On May 18, 2017, the complainant requested the minutes of 
Committee meetings held between December 5, 2016, and the date of request. On May 31, 
2018, the town clerk provided copies of minutes by e-mail for meetings held on January 9, 
February 6, and April 3, 2017. The April 3, 2017, minutes indicate that the minutes of a March

DISCUSSION 
I, The Committee Failed to Provide Requested Minutes within the Time Frame 
Required by Law. 
Public bodies must create and maintain accurate minutes of all meetings. G.L. c. 3 OA, § 
22(a). The minutes of an open session, if they exist and whether approved or in draft form, shall 
be made available upon request by any person within 10 days. G.L. c. 30A, § 22(c). 
The complaint alleges that the Committee failed to provide requested meeting minutes 
within the time frame required by statute. Indeed, the complainant requested the first batch of 
meeting minutes on May 18, 2017, and they were made available on May 31, 2017. The tenth 
day following the date of request was the Sunday of Memorial Day weekend. See G.L. c. 4, § 9 
("when the day or the last day for the performance of an act... authorized or required by statute 
or by contract, falls on Sunday or a legal holiday, the act may... be performed on the next 
succeeding business day"); see also G.L. c. 41, § 110A ("the provisions of section nine of 
chapter four shall apply in the case of such closing of any such office on any Saturday to the 
same extent as if Saturday were a legal holiday"). If the Committee had provided the requested 
minutes on Tuesday, May 30, 2017, we would conclude that the Committee had met its 
obligations under the Open Meeting Law. See OML 2013-131. However, because the 
Committee provided the minutes the following day—Wednesday, May 31, 2017—we find that 
the Committee failed to provide the requested minutes within the time frame required by law.1 
Of significance, we note that the March 6, 2017, minutes, which were apparently approved 
during the April 13, 2017 meeting, had not been provided with either production of minutes. 
Here, too, the Committee violated the Open Meeting Law. G.L. c. 30A, § 22(c).

II. The Committee Failed to Approve Minutes in a Timely Manner. 
Public bodies must create and approve minutes of all meetings in a timely manner. G.L. 
c. 30A, § 22(c). The Attorney General's updated regulations, which took effect October 6, 2017, 
require public bodies to create and approve minutes within three meetings, or within 30 days, 
whichever is later. 940 CMR 29.11(2). Prior to the effective dates of these updated regulations, 
our guidance was that for bodies that meet weekly or bi-monthly, a delay or two to three months 
in approving meeting minutes was too long. See OML 2016-62. 
The Committee plainly failed to approve several sets of minutes in a timely manner. 
Indeed, the Committee approved minutes during its July 26, 2017, meeting that were, in some 
cases, over two years old. No matter which regulatory standard we apply, such a delay exceeds 
an acceptable time frame to approve meeting minutes. We find that the Committee violated the 
Open Meeting Law with respect to this allegation. Nonetheless, we commend the Committee for 
taking sufficient remedial action by approving the overdue meeting minutes in response to the 
present complaint. 
III, The Committee Created Minutes of All Meetings at Issue. 
The Open Meeting Law requires that a public body "create and maintain accurate 
minutes of all meetings, including executive sessions, setting forth the date, time and place, the 
members present or absent, a summary of the discussions on each subject, a list of documents 
and other exhibits used at the meeting, the decisions made and the actions taken at each meeting, 
including the record of all votes." G.L. c. 30A, § 22(a). 
Here, the complaint alleges that the Committee held meeting for which it failed to create 
minutes. In support of this allegation, the complaint points to several meetings that were 
scheduled on the town calendar for which minutes appear to have not been created. In its 
response, the Committee stated that many of these meetings were cancelled due to weather or 
lack of a quorum. We credit this statement. See OML 2017-169 (absent sufficient evidence to 
the contrary, we credit a public body's version of the facts). We find no violation with respect to 
this allegation. 
IV. Because Meeting Minutes Were Either in Draft Form at the Time the Complaint 
was Filed or Had Been Approved More Than 30 Davs Prior. We Do Not Review 
Them. 
When reviewing minutes for compliance with the Open Meeting Law, we look for 
substantial compliance with the accuracy requirement. See OML 2013-64. By substantial 
compliance, we mean that the minutes should contain enough detail and accuracy so that a 
member of the public who did not attend the meeting could read the minutes and have a clear 
understanding of what occurred. See OML 2012-106. 
Here, the complaint alleges that the Committee has generally created insufficiently 
detailed meeting minutes. We note that we do not conduct broad audits of public bodies based 
on generalized allegations. See OML Declination 4-14-2015 (Fairhaven Historical 
Commission). Furthermore, to be timely, complaints concerning the content of meeting minutes

must be filed within 30 days of the date when those minutes are approved. See OML 2015-26. 
We do not review the sufficiency of minutes that were still in draft form at the time the 
complaint was filed. See OML 2014-7. Because the minutes referenced in the complaint were 
either in draft form at the time the complaint was filed, or had been approved more than 30 days 
prior to the date of filing, we do not review this allegation. However, we remind the Committee 
that minutes must include sufficient detail for a reasonable member of the public who was not 
present at each meeting to read the minutes and understand the discussion that took place. See 
OML 2017-92. 
CONCLUSION 
For the reasons stated above, we find that the Committee violated the Open Meeting Law. 
We order the Committee's immediate and future compliance with the Open Meeting Law, and 
we caution that similar future violations may be considered evidence of intent to violate the law. 
Further, we order the Committee to review its files to ensure that all responsive meeting minutes 
have been provided to the complainant. Within thirty (30) days of receipt of this letter, the 
Committee must certify in writing, pursuant to 940 CMR 29.07(4), that it has complied with this 
order. 
We now consider the complaint addressed by this determination to be resolved. This 
determination does not address any other complaints that may be pending with our office or the 
Committee. Please feel free to contact the Division at (617) 963 - 2540 if you have any 
questions.
