RE: Open Meeting Law Complaint 
Dear Attorney Doneski: 
This office reviewed a complaint filed by Arthur Campbell of the Groton Line (the 
complainant) alleging that the Groton Board of Selectmen (the Board) violated the Open 
Meeting Law, G.L. c. 30A, §§ 18-25. The complaint specifically alleges that the Board 
improperly deliberated outside of a posted meeting via serial communications on April 2, 2014 
and April 4, 2014. The complaint was originally filed with the Board on May 8, 2014, and a 
request for further review was filed with our office June 16, 2014. You responded to the 
complaint, on behalf of the Board, by letter dated May 29, 2014.1 
In reaching a determination, we reviewed the May 8, 2014 complaint filed with the 
Board; the Board's May 29, 2014 response; and the June 16, 2014 request for further review 
filed with our office. Additionally, we reviewed the minutes of the May 12, 2014 meeting of the 
Board; an editorial in the Groton Herald, dated April 23, 2014 titled "A Bad Idea that Needs to 
be Stopped;" and a letter to the editor of The Groton Line, dated April 28, 2014, signed by Board 
Chair Peter Cunningham. Finally, we communicated with the complainant via telephone and 
email in June 2014. Following our review, we have decided to resolve this complaint by 
informal action in accordance with 940 CMR 29.07(2)(a). We find that certain telephone calls 
between Board members regarding a waiver of building fees constituted impermissible 
deliberation outside of a properly noticed open meeting. 
At issue in this complaint are a series of phone calls placed by the Chair to other 
members of the Board regarding the possible waiver of building permit fees for a local business 
that suffered a loss due to a fire. In early April 2014, in an effort to demonstrate community 
support for rebuilding those premises, the Chair called each member of the Board in turn to 
gauge support for his idea to waive the building permit fees. Two of the Board members stated 
during their phone calls that they believed such a conversation was inappropriate outside of a 
posted meeting.

The Chair took no further action to promote his idea, and the Board did not consider it 
during a subsequent meeting. On April 23, 2014, an editorial appeared in the Groton Herald 
opposing the Chair's proposal. Chair Cunningham responded to the editorial by a letter to the 
editor that appeared in the Groton Online on April 28, 2014. In that letter, and during a May 12, 
2014 Board meeting, the Chair disclosed the calls at issue in the present complaint. 
The Board argues in its formal response to the complaint that the conversations at issue 
did not constitute deliberation because: 1) they were "in the nature of distributing information or 
documents for subsequent discussion at a subsequent meeting;" and 2) the waiver of 
administrative fees is not a matter of public business within the Board's jurisdiction. See G.L. 
c. 30A, § 18. We disagree. Gauging members' interest in a proposal prior to its introduction at a 
meeting constitutes active solicitation of an opinion of a Board member. We have previously 
stated that this behavior, when reaching a quorum, is in violation of the Open Meeting Law. See 
OML 2013-76; OML 2013-75. Additionally, the Board is both the appointing authority for the 
Town Manager and the policy-making agency for the Town, according to the Town Charter. 
Therefore, any discussion of whether and how the Board may influence the Town Manager in the 
execution of his duties is a matter of public business within the Board's jurisdiction. See OML 
Accordingly, we find that the Board violated the Open Meeting Law. We acknowledge 
that two of the Board's members did raise concerns at the time the calls occurred. We also find 
that the Chair took appropriate action by disclosing the improper communications in an open 
meeting in an effort to respond to the complaint. 
We order no further action but caution the Board that future similar violations could be 
considered evidence of intent to violate the Law. We now consider this matter closed. This 
determination does not address any other complaints that may be pending before this office or 
the Board. Please feel free to contact this office at (617) 963-2540 if you have any questions 
regarding this letter.
