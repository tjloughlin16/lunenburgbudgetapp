This office received two complaints from Patrick Higgins on August 10, 2021, alleging 
that the Easton Select Board (the “Board”) violated the Open Meeting Law, G.L. c. 30A, §§ 18-
25.  The first complaint was originally filed with the Board on or about June 7, 2021, and the 
second complaint was originally filed with the Board on or about July 5, 2021.  You responded 
to both complaints, on behalf of the Board, by letter dated August 24, 2021.  The first complaint 
alleges that the Board failed to timely approve nine sets of minutes of meetings held between 
December 21, 2020, and May 3, 2021.  The second complaint alleges that the Board failed to 
timely respond to the first Open Meeting Law complaint.  
 
Following our review, we find that the Board violated the Open Meeting Law by failing 
to timely approve seven sets of meeting minutes.  We find that the Board did not violate the 
Open Meeting Law in the other ways alleged.  In reaching this determination, we reviewed the 
original complaints, the Board’s response to the complaints, and the complaints filed with our 
office requesting further review.  In addition, we reviewed the notices and open session minutes 
of the Board meetings held on February 8, 2021; March 8, 2021; April 5, 2021; April 26, 2021; 
and June 7, 2021, meetings.  Finally, we communicated with you on October 14, 2021.

DISCUSSION 
 
I. The Board Violated the Open Meeting Law by Failing to Timely Approve 
Seven Sets of Meeting Minutes. 
 
The Open Meeting Law requires that a public body “create and maintain accurate 
minutes of all meetings, including executive sessions, setting forth the date, time and place, the 
members present or absent, a summary of the discussions on each subject, a list of documents 
and other exhibits used at the meeting, the decisions made and the actions taken at each meeting, 
including the record of all votes.”  G.L. c. 30A, § 22(a).  The meeting minutes must be reviewed 
and approved in a timely manner.  G.L. c. 30A, § 22(c).  “Timely manner” means “within the 
next three public body meetings or within 30 days, whichever is later, unless the public body can 
show good cause for further delay.”  See 940 CMR 29.11; OML 2018-48.1  Whenever possible, 
we recommend that minutes of a meeting be approved at the next meeting.  See OML 2018-67; 
OML 2017-133.  
 
The first complaint alleges that the Board failed to timely approve nine sets of open 
session minutes.  Specifically, the complaint contends that the minutes of Board meetings held 
on December 21, 2020; January 11, 2021; January 12, 2021; January 13, 2021; February 8, 2021; 
March 8, 2021; April 5, 2021; April 26, 2021; and May 3, 2021, were not approved in a timely

manner because they were not approved either within the next three public body meetings or 30 
days.   
 
The Board was required to approve the minutes of its January 13, 2021; February 8, 
2021; April 5, 2021; April 26, 2021; and May 3, 2021, meetings within the next three public 
body meetings as that was the later of the two time periods.  See 940 CMR 29.11; OML 2018-
111.  Since the Board did not approve its January 13, 2021, minutes until June 7, 2021, its 
eleventh meeting thereafter; did not approve its February 8, 2021, minutes until June 7, 2021, its 
ninth meeting thereafter; and did not approve its April 5, 2021, minutes until June 7, 2021, its 
fourth meeting thereafter, we find that the Board violated the Open Meeting Law by failing to 
approve these minutes in a timely manner.   
 
Because the Board approved the minutes of its April 26, 2021, and May 3, 2021, 
meetings during the June 7, 2021, meeting, which was the third and second meeting thereafter, 
respectively, we find that the Board approved these two sets of minutes in a timely manner and 
thus did not violate the Open Meeting Law.   
 
The Board was required to approve the minutes of its December 21, 2020; January 11, 
2021; January 12, 2021; and March 8, 2021, meetings within 30 days as that was the later of the 
two time periods for these minutes because the Board met at least three times within 30 days 
following these four meetings.  See 940 CMR 29.11; OML 2018-152.  Since the Board did not 
approve its December 21, 2020, minutes until 169 days thereafter; did not approve its January 
11, 2021, minutes until 148 days thereafter; did not approve its January 12, 2021, minutes until 
147 days thereafter; and did not approve its March 8, 2021, minutes until 92 days thereafter, we 
find that the Board violated the Open Meeting Law by also failing to approve these minutes in a 
timely manner. 
 
We remind the Board that the responsibility for complying with the Open Meeting Law 
rests with public bodies and members of such bodies, not with individual public employees, such 
as administrative staff.  See OML 2018-105.  We understand the demands on a public body with 
limited staff; however, the Board is still obligated to timely approve minutes.  See OML 2018-
111; OML 2017-115.  If staff resources are not available to create minutes for the Board to 
approve, then Board members must take on this responsibility to ensure compliance with the law.  
See OML 2017-115.   
 
II. The Board Did Not Violate the Open Meeting Law by Failing to Timely 
Respond to an Open Meeting Law Complaint. 
 
The second complaint concerns the Board’s failure to respond to the first Open Meeting 
Law complaint in the timeframe required by the law.  The Open Meeting Law requires that 
within 14 days of receipt of a complaint, the public body review the complaint’s allegations; take 
remedial action, if appropriate; and send to the Attorney General a copy of the complaint and a 
description of any remedial action taken.  G.L.  c. 30A, § 23(b); 940 CMR 29.05(5).  Upon the 
filing of an Open Meeting Law complaint with a public body, the chair “shall disseminate copies 
of the complaint to the members of the public body,” and “the public body shall review the 
complaint’s allegations.”  940 CMR 29.05(3), (5).  Thus, a public body must meet to review the

complaint and formulate a response, or meet to delegate that authority, and respond to the 
complaint within 14 business days.  See G.L. c. 30A, § 23(b); OML 2017-69; OML 2012-90. 
 
Here, the Board did not respond to the first Open Meeting Law complaint until August 
24, 2021, after being notified of it by this office.  The complainant states that he mailed the 
complaint to the Board on or about June 7, 2021; he did not send it by email.  The Board 
contends that it did not receive the complaint either by mail or email.  Following our review, we 
find insufficient evidence to conclude that the Board received the complaint.  See OML 
Declination 4-3-15 (a complaint is considered “filed” when it is received by the specified public 
officials).  Accordingly, we are unable to resolve this allegation and do not find that the Board 
violated the Open Meeting Law by failing to timely respond to an Open Meeting Law complaint.  
See OML 2018-50.    
 
CONCLUSION 
 
For the reasons stated above, we find that the Board violated the Open Meeting Law by 
failing to approve minutes of its December 21, 2020; January 11, 2021; January 12, 2021; 
January 13, 2021; February 8, 2021; March 8, 2021; and April 5, 2021, meetings in a timely 
manner.  We order immediate and future compliance with the law’s requirements, and we 
caution that similar future violations could be considered evidence of intent to violate the law.  
As all of the minutes had been approved before the complaint was filed, we order no further 
remedial action.   
 
We now consider the complaints addressed by this determination to be resolved.  This
