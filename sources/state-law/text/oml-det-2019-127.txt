RE: Open  Meeting Law Complaint
Dear Attorney Lindquist:
This office received a complaint from Gerry Mroz, on June 11, 2019, alleging that the 
Melrose School Committee (the “Committee”) violated the Open Meeting Law, G.L. c. 30A, §§
18-25. The complaint was originally filed with the Committee on April 11, 2019, and you 
responded on behalf of the Committee by letter dated May 9, 2019. In his complaint, Mr. Mroz 
alleges that the Committee entered into executive session on March 12, 2019, without stating the 
purpose for the executive session with sufficient detail. The Committee responds that, although 
the Chair mistakenly did not explain the reason for the executive session during the meeting, that 
information was included in the meeting notice.
We resolve this complaint by informal action in accordance with 940 CMR 29.07(2)(a), 
and find that the Committee violated the Open Meeting Law by failing to announce the reason 
for the executive session at the time of the meeting. See G.L. c. 30A, § 21(b)(3); OML 2015-117; 
OML 2014-97 (public body must identify the specific purpose for the executive session both on 
the meeting notice and in the statement prior to entering executive session).1 However, the 
Committee has already taken corrective action by discussing the deficiency in a later open 
meeting and amending its minutes to explain the reason for the executive session in question. We 
also note that the complainant does not allege that the executive session discussions were 
otherwise improper or failed to fall within a permissible executive session purpose. Therefore, 
we order no further remedial action. See OML 2019-60; OML 2019-109.
The complainant urges us to find an intentional violation of the Open Meeting Law. A 
prior, similar violation of the Open Meeting Law may be considered evidence of an intentional 
violation of the law. In the past eight years, the Committee was twice found in violation of the 
Open Meeting Law for failure to state with sufficient specificity its purpose for entering

executive session, in both its meeting notices and verbal statements at the time it entered those

Here, because the Committee made an effort to comply with the executive session notice 
requirements by describing the purpose for its March 12, 2019, executive session on the meeting 
notice with sufficient particularity (“To conduct collective bargaining session with the MEA”) 
we are persuaded that the failure to announce the purpose at the time of the meeting was an 
oversight, rather than a deliberate attempt to conceal this information from the public. 
Accordingly, we decline to find this violation to be intentional.
We now consider the complaint addressed by this determination to be resolved. This 
determination does not address any other complaints that may be pending with our office or the 
Committee. Please feel free to contact the Division at (617) 963 - 2540 if you have any
