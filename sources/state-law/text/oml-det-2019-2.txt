RE: Open Meeting Law Complaint
Dear Mr. Snow:
This office received a complaint from Lynne Maloney and Jim Spelman on October 18 
alleging that the Scituate Conservation Commission (the “Commission”) violated the Open 
Meeting Law, G.L. c. 30A, §§ 18-25.1 The complaint was originally filed with the Commission 
on August 8; and you responded, on behalf of the Commission, by letter dated September 14, 
following an extension of time granted by our office. The complaint alleges that the 
Commission did not allow public participation at its July 9 meeting prior to approving a 
landscape plan.
We resolve this matter by informal action, in accordance with 940 CMR 29.07(2)(a), 
after reviewing the original complaint, the Commission’s response to the complaint, the 
complaint filed with our office requesting further review, and the open session minutes of the 
Commission’s July 9 meeting. We find that the Commission did not violate the Open Meeting 
Law as alleged.
The Open Meeting Law states that “[n]o person shall address a meeting of a public body 
without permission of the chair, and all persons shall, at the request of the chair, be silent.” G.L. 
c. 30A § 20(f). The law does not require that a public body allow public participation, but rather 
gives the chair of the public body discretion to decide whether to permit public comment during 
a meeting. See OML 2017-189; OML 2014-23; OML 2012-78.2 We do, however, encourage 
public bodies to allow for as much public participation as time and circumstances permit. See 
OML 2015-12; OML 2014-129; OML 2012-59.
1 All dates in this letter refer to the year 2018.
2 Open Meeting Law determinations and declinations may be found at the Attorney General’s website, 
www.mass.gov/ago/openmeeting.

The Commission met on July 9 and conducted several public hearings, including the 
approval of a landscape plan for 53 Border Street. When the Commission moved to approve the 
plan, Mr. Spelman objected, stating that the final plan should be available for review and public 
comment before a vote takes place. The Commission disagreed and unanimously approved the 
landscape plan. It is not a violation of the Open Meeting Law for a public body to vote on a 
topic without first taking comments from the public. See OML 2013-64; OML 2012-59; OML 
2012-23. Moreover, the Commission did not violate the Open Meeting by limiting public 
participation during the meeting, as it is within the Chair’s discretion to do so. See OML 2017- 
181.
The complaint also alleges that the landscape plan was not available for review during the 
meeting. While the Open Meeting Law requires that documents and exhibits used during a 
meeting are public records and that the minutes of all meetings include, “a list of documents and 
other exhibits used at the meeting,” it does not require the publication of minutes or documents 
used during a meeting. See G.L. c. 30A, §§ 22(a), (e); OML 2017-98. Absent a specific request 
for documents used at a meeting, the Commission is not obligated to make such documents 
available at a meeting to those members of the public in attendance. Nevertheless, we 
understand that the plan was available in the Conservation Department prior to the hearing on 
July 9.
For the reasons stated above, we find that the Commission did not violate the Open 
Meeting Law. We now consider the complaint addressed by this determination to be resolved. 
Please feel free to contact the Division at (617) 963-2540 if you have any questions regarding 
this determination.
