Dear Attorney King:  
 
This office received a complaint from Phillip King on April 20, 2021, alleging that the 
Sharon School Committee (the “Committee”) violated the Open Meeting Law, G.L. c. 30A, §§ 
18-25.  The complaint was originally filed with the Committee on March 18, 2021, and you 
responded, on behalf of the Committee, by letter dated April 7, 2021.  The complaint alleges that 
the Committee continued to withhold certain executive session minutes past the time that 
publication would defeat the lawful purpose for the executive session. 
 
We appreciate the patience of the parties while we reviewed this matter.  Following our 
review, we find that the Committee did not violate the Open Meeting Law in the way alleged.  In 
reaching this determination, we reviewed the original complaint, the Committee’s response to the 
complaint, and the complaint filed with our office requesting further review.  We also reviewed 
the notices and open and executive session minutes of Committee meetings held on July 29, 
2020; August 5, 2020; August 12, 2020; August 20, 2020; September 2, 2020; September 9, 
2020; and September 16, 2020, as well as the open session minutes of the Committee meetings 
held on October 28, 2020, and January 13, 2021.   
 
FACTS 
 
We find the facts as follows.  The Committee posted notice for numerous meetings 
between July 29, 2020, and September 16, 2020.  The notices for seven of those meetings - July 
29, 2020; August 5, 2020; August 12, 2020; August 20, 2020; September 2, 2020; September 9,

2020; and September 16, 2020 - included a topic for an executive session under G. L. c. 30A,     
§ 21(a)(2) (“Purpose 2”) “to conduct strategy with respect to collective bargaining for STA Units 
A and B, if an open meeting may have a detrimental effect on the bargaining position of the 
Sharon School Committee and the chair so declares.”  Each of these seven meetings were held as 
planned and the Committee discussed strategy for negotiations with the Sharon Teachers’ 
Association collective bargaining unit over working conditions for the 2020-2021 school year.1 
 
On September 16, 2020, the complainant requested copies of minutes of the executive 
session meetings held between July and September 2020 that related to the collective bargaining 
discussions involving the Sharon Teachers’ Association.  During an October 21, 2020, meeting,  
the Committee reviewed the minutes and determined that the purpose for the executive session 
remained ongoing and the minutes could not be released at that time.   
 
During a January 13, 2021, meeting, the Committee announced that it had reached an 
impasse with respect to a collective bargaining agreement with the Sharon Teachers’ Association 
and “no further progress was possible.”  However, the Committee stated that the “parties 
continue to work together in good faith and will soon be bargaining over a successor collective 
bargaining agreement.”  The Committee continued to convene in executive session over the next 
several months to discuss strategy with respect to collective bargaining with the Sharon 
Teachers’ Association, finally reaching an agreement in June 2021. 
 
On March 11, 2021, the complainant again requested copies of minutes of the executive 
session meetings held between July and September 2020 that related to the collective bargaining 
discussions involving the Sharon Teachers’ Association.  On March 16, 2021, the Committee 
advised the complainant that “the minutes remain exempt because the purpose of the executive 
session remains (i.e. the parties continue to negotiate the impacts of returning to the classroom).”   
 
DISCUSSION 
 
Public bodies may enter a closed, executive session for any of ten enumerated purposes. 
G.L. c. 30A, § 21(a).  One permissible reason to convene in executive session is “[t]o conduct 
strategy sessions in preparation for negotiations with nonunion personnel or to conduct collective 
bargaining sessions or contract negotiations with nonunion personnel.”  G.L. c. 30A, § 21(a)(2) 
(“Purpose 2”).  Another permissible reason to convene in executive session is “[t]o discuss 
strategy with respect to collective bargaining or litigation if an open meeting may have a 
detrimental effect on the bargaining or litigating position of the public body and the chair so 
declares.”  G.L. c. 30A, § 21(a)(3) (“Purpose 3”).   
 
The Open Meeting Law requires that a public body “create and maintain accurate 
minutes of all meetings, including executive sessions, setting forth the date, time and place, the 
members present or absent, a summary of the discussions on each subject, a list of documents

and other exhibits used at the meeting, the decisions made and the actions taken at each meeting, 
including the record of all votes.”  G.L. c. 30A, § 22(a).  Executive session minutes may be 
withheld from disclosure to the public “as long as publication may defeat the lawful purposes of 
the executive session, but no longer.”  G.L. c. 30A, § 22(f).  When the purpose for a valid 
executive session has been served, the minutes and any documents or exhibits used at the session 
must be disclosed, unless the attorney-client privilege or an exemption to the Public Records 
Law applies.  Id.  The burden of justifying continued nondisclosure of executive session minutes 
lies with the public body.  Foudy v. Amherst-Pelham Regional School Committee, 402 Mass. 
179, 184 (1988). 
 
The complaint alleges that the Committee failed to release minutes of the executive 
sessions held to conduct collective bargaining with the Sharon Teachers’ Association.  The 
Committee argues that release of the minutes will have a detrimental effect on its bargaining 
position in the future.  We distinguish the situation where a public body enters executive session 
to conduct actual collective bargaining negotiations under Purpose 2 with one in which it enters 
executive session to discuss strategy with respect to collective bargaining negotiations under 
Purpose 3.  See OML 2018-19.2  We have held that where a public body’s discussions touch 
upon long-term strategy that could harm a future bargaining position with respect to subsequent 
collective bargaining agreements, executive session minutes may be withheld for a period of 
time beyond the conclusion of the collective bargaining or contract negotiation sessions.  See 
OML 2015-52; OML 2012-41.   
 
Here, although the notices for relevant Committee meetings list an executive session 
pursuant to Purpose 2, it is clear that the Committee actually met in executive session under 
Purpose 3.  After reviewing the as-yet-unreleased executive session minutes, we note that the 
discussions related to strategy with respect to collective bargaining negotiations with the Sharon 
Teachers’ Association, rather than actual contract negotiations with the teachers’ collective 
bargaining unit.  A public body is not required to provide the specific statutory citation of the 
executive session purpose on which it relies; rather, the public body must provide a sufficient 
description of the topic to be discussed in executive session from which a member of the public 
could determine that the topic falls within a proper executive session purpose.  See OML 2016-
95.  We find that the executive session topic listed on the relevant notices was sufficiently 
descriptive and described the actual discussions that the Committee engaged in during the 
executive session meetings, which was strategy for negotiations with the Sharon Teachers’ 
Association collective bargaining unit.  Moreover, we find that the discussions by the Committee 
during those executive sessions were appropriate for Purpose 3.  See OML 2017-164; 2014-141; 
2013-84. 
 
Even though negotiations have now concluded, we do not find a violation of the Open 
Meeting Law because, at the time of the request for minutes, the Committee was still engaged in 
an ongoing collective bargaining negotiation.  Therefore, it did not need to publicly release the 
minutes from the executive sessions at that time.  We note that a public body may continue to 
withhold minutes of a Purpose 3 executive session, in whole or in part, even after a final

collective bargaining agreement has been reached, if the public release of those minutes could 
have a detrimental effect on the public body’s collective bargaining position in future 
negotiations.  See OML 2018-19; OML 2012-41.  However, they may not be withheld 
indefinitely.  At some point, the public interest in transparency will outweigh the potential for 
harm to the public body’s future collective bargaining position.  See OML 2020-120; OML 
2015-62.  After each subsequent collective bargaining agreement is reached, therefore, the 
burden on the public body to release the executive session minutes of the older sessions 
increases.  See OML 2015-52; OML 2012-41.  We recommend that the Committee review its 
executive session minutes to determine whether publication of the minutes of the sessions will no 
longer “defeat the lawful purposes of the executive session.”  See G.L. c. 30A, § 22(f).  
  
CONCLUSION 
 
We find that the Committee did not violate the Open Meeting Law when it withheld 
executive session minutes because the purpose for keeping those minutes confidential still 
existed at the time of the request for minutes.
