This office received a complaint from Susan Delmolino on January 9, 2024, alleging that 
the Hadley Select Board (the “Board”) violated the Open Meeting Law, G.L. c. 30A, §§ 18-25.  
The complaint was filed with the Board on November 14, 2023, and the Board’s then-Chair, 
Amy Parsons, responded on behalf of the Board on January 23, 2024.
1  The complaint alleges 
that the notice for the Board’s November 15, 2023, meeting “lacks specificity and detail.”   
 
Following our review, we do not find that the Board violated the Open Meeting Law.  In 
reaching this determination we reviewed the original complaint, the Board’s response, and the 
request for further review of the complaint filed with our office.  In addition, we reviewed the 
notice of the Board’s November 15 meeting. 
 
The Open Meeting Law requires that, except in an emergency, “a public body shall post 
notic

and legal holidays.”  G.L. c. 30A, § 20(b).  The notice must be printed in a legible, easily 
understandable format and must contain the date, time and place of the meeting, as well as a 
listing of topics that the chair reasonably anticipates will be discussed.  Id.  The list of topics 
shall have “sufficient specificity to reasonably advise the public of the issues to be discussed at

2  When reviewing a meeting notice 
for sufficiency, unless it is clearly insufficient on its face, we do not review a meeting notice 
standing alone.  See OML 2015-109; OML 2014-155.  Rather, we review what was actually 
discussed at the meeting to determine if the notice was sufficiently specific to make the public 
aware of the discussion topics.  See OML 2015-101; OML 2015-21. 
 
The complaint alleges that the notice for the Board’s November 15 meeting “lacks 
speci
ficity and detail as to what is discussed.”  The complaint does not identify any particular 
topics on the notice that were insufficient.  Complainants must allege violations with a degree of 
specificity, as our office will not conduct broad audits of public bodies based on generalized 
allegations.  See OML 2014-119; OML 2012-106.  Furthermore, the complaint was filed with 
the Board before the meeting had taken place.  Therefore, we review only whether, on its face, 
the notice for the November 15 meeting was sufficient.  See OML 2015-116.  The notice for the 
Board’s November 15 meeting included the date, time, and place of the meeting as well as a 
listing of topics.  In particular, the notice included 13 topic headings, several of which included 
more specific topics beneath them.
3  We note, after reviewing notices of several other Board 
meetings, that the Board’s meeting notices generally follow a template with the same 13 topic 
headings and include more detail below the headings when specific matters are anticipated for 
discussion; all topic headings remain on the notice even if no specific matters are listed and 
anticipated for discussion.  Although we have explained that boilerplate topics or headings, 
without more detail, are generally insufficiently specific to reasonably advise the public of the 
issues to be discussed, see, e.g., OML 2022-179, OML 2021-121, we find here that the notice of 
the Board’s November 15 meeting included detailed topics beneath many of the headings.  
Where the complaint does not allege that any particular matters were included on the notice with 
insufficient specificity nor that the Board engaged in discussion on any matters that included 
only a generic heading, and we find that the notice was not clearly insufficient on its face, we do 
not find a violation of the Open Meeting Law.
