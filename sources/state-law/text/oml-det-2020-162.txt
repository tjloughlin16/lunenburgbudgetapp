 RE: Open Meeting Law Complaint 
Dear Attorney King: 
This Office received a complaint from George Harris on October 20, 2020, alleging that 
the Wayland High School Council (the “Council”) violated the Open Meeting Law, G.L. c. 30A, 
§§ 18-25. The complaint was originally filed with the Council on July 6 and the Council received 
an extension from our Office to respond to the complaint by August 31.1 You responded on 
behalf of the Council by letter dated August 31. In his complaint, Mr. Harris alleges that the 
Council failed to file meeting notices with the Town Clerk and post meeting minutes for 
meetings held in the 2019-2020 school year.2 Following our review, we resolve this complaint by 
informal action in accordance with 940 CMR 29.07(2)(a) and find that the Council violated the 
Open Meeting Law by failing to post meeting notices in Wayland’s official notice posting 
location and failing to approve meeting minutes in a timely manner.  
The Open Meeting Law requires that a public body post notice of every meeting at least 
48 hours in advance, not including Saturdays, Sundays, and legal holidays. G.L. c. 30A, § 20(b). 
The notice must be printed in a legible, easily understandable format and must contain the date, 
time and place of the meeting, as well as a listing of topics that the chair reasonably anticipates 
will be discussed. Id. For meetings of local public bodies, “notice shall be filed with the 
municipal clerk and posted in a manner conspicuously visible to the public at all hours in or on 
the municipal building in which the clerk’s office is located,” unless the municipality has 
adopted the alternative method of posting notice to a website. G.L. c. 30A, § 20(c); 940 CMR 
29.03. For remote meetings conducted under Governor Baker’s executive order, public bodies

still must post notice of every meeting at least 48 hours in advance of the meeting, not including 
weekends or state holidays, using the official notice posting method (physical notice or website), 
even if the clerk’s office is closed. See OML 2020-102.3 According to our records, Wayland’s 
official notice posting location is a physical notice, as Wayland has not adopted its website as the 
official notice posting location. The Council concedes that it did not post meeting notices at the 
official location through the Town Clerk during the school year commencing July 1, 2019. In 
response to the complaint, the Council began posting notices with the Town Clerk during the 
summer of 2020.  
 The Open Meeting Law requires that a public body “create and maintain accurate 
minutes of all meetings, including executive sessions.” G.L. c. 30A, § 22(a). These meeting 
minutes must be reviewed and approved in a timely manner. G.L. c. 30A, § 22(c). The Open 
Meeting Law itself does not define “timely manner.” However, the Attorney General’s Open 
Meeting Law regulations provide that “timely manner” means “within the next three public body 
meetings or within 30 days, whichever is later, unless the public body can show good cause for 
further delay.” 940 CMR 29.11(2). The Council concedes that it did not create and approve 
meeting minutes for its meetings that took place during the 2019-2020 school year. The Council 
assures us that meeting minutes will be created, to the extent possible based on available notes, 
for the meetings which took place in the 2019-2020 school year.  
 The goal of this Office in enforcing the Open Meeting Law is achieving compliance with 
that Law. See OML 2015-118. Where a body acknowledges it failed to fully comply with the 
Law and takes remedial action to rectify a violation and ensure compliance going forward, we 
may take these efforts into account when deciding whether further relief is warranted. 
Accordingly, we find that the Council plans to take adequate remedial measures and no further 
relief is required.  
 We now consider the complaint addressed by this determination to be resolved. This 
determination does not address any other complaints that may be pending with our office or the 
Council. Please feel free to contact the Division at (617) 963 - 2540 if you have any questions.
