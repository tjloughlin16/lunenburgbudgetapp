RE: Open Meeting Law Complaint 
Dear Mr. Fennessy: 
This office received a complaint from John-Michael Pontes, dated March 3, 2017, 
alleging that the Kingston Board of Selectmen (the "Board") violated the Open Meeting Law, 
G.L. c. 30A, §§ 18-25. The complaint was originally filed with the Board on January 12, 2017, 
and the Board responded by letter dated January 24, 2017. In his complaint, Mr. Pontes alleges 
that the Board violated the Open Meeting Law by: (i) restricting public participation during a 
meeting, and (ii) discussing a topic that was not listed on the meeting notice. 
Following our review, we resolve this matter by informal action, in accordance with 940 
CMR 29.07(2)(a). We find that the Board did not violate the Open Meeting Law. The complaint 
concerns a meeting that took place on December 13, 2016. This meeting included a public 
hearing that had been continued from a November 29, 2016 meeting. This portion of the 
meeting was described on the notice as, "Public Fearing—Use Classification of Property for Tax 
Purposes." During this discussion, the Board chair did not recognize certain members of the 
public who wished to address the Board concerning property valuations. Following the 
discussion, the Board closed the public hearing and voted to adopt certain tax assessments. The 
Board then voted to memorialize these discussions on a form required by the Department of 
Revenue. 
The complaint first alleges that the Board erred when the chair curtailed public discussion 
during the December 13, 2016 meeting. The Open Meeting Law gives the chair of a public body 
the discretion to determine who may be recognized to speak or otherwise participate during a 
meeting and what form such participation may take. See OML 2013-64; OML 2012-59.1

Although public participation is within the chair's discretion, we encourage public bodies to 
allow for as much public participation as is reasonably possible. See OML 2012-23. The 
complaint also alleges that the Board should have indicated on the meeting notice that it would 
be signing the Department of Revenue form memorializing the tax assessments votes. We 
disagree. It is reasonably foreseeable that a public body may take a vote following a discussion 
on any topic. See OML 2015-66. Accordingly, we find that the Board did not violate the Open 
Meeting Law with respect to either of the complaint's allegations. 
For the reasons stated above, we find that the Board did not violate the Open Meeting 
Law. We now consider the complaint addressed by this determination to be resolved. This 
determination does not address any other complaints that may be pending with our office or the 
Board. Please feel free to contact the Division at (617) 963 - 2540 if you have any questions.
