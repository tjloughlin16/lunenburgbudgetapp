This office received a complaint from Lynne Fraker, dated January 13, 2016, alleging 
that the Tisbury Board of Selectmen (the "Board") violated the Open Meeting Law, G.L. c. 30A, 
§§ 18-25. The complaint was originally filed with the Board on December 3, 2015, and the 
Board responded by letter dated December 22, 2015. In her complaint, Ms. Fraker alleges that 
the Board violated the Open Meeting Law by: (i) improperly posting a public hearing notice; and 
(ii) discussing a topic during a meeting that was not listed on the agenda. 
Following our review, we find that the Board did not violate the Open Meeting Law. In 
reaching a determination, we reviewed the original complaint; the Board's response; and the 
request for further review filed with our office. We also reviewed the notice and minutes of a 
Board meeting held on November 3, 2015. Finally, we spoke with Town Administrator John 
Grande by telephone on February 19, 2016.1 
FACTS 
We find the facts as follows. On October 20, 2015, the Board published an 
announcement for a public hearing to take place on November 3, 2015. The notice lists the topic 
to be discussed as, "MORATORIUM ON USE OF SHELLFISH DRAGS W/ TEETH AND 
OTHER EQUIPMENT DAMAGING TO EELGRASS, SEED, BROOD STOCK, AND 
SHELLFISH HABITAT[.]" This announcement was published before the Board took a vote to 
hold the hearing at issue. The Board held a meeting on October 20, 2015, after the 
announcement was published. During this meeting, the Shellfish Constable spoke to the Board 
and recommended that the Board hold a hearing to consider a moratorium on "shellfish drags

On October 30, 2015, the Board posted notice for the November 3, 2015 meeting. After

DISCUSSION 
I. The Notice of the November 3. 2015 Meeting Complied with the Open Meeting 
Law. 
The Open Meeting Law was enacted "to eliminate much of the secrecy surrounding 
deliberations and decisions on which public policy is based." Ghiglione v. School Committee of 
Southbridge. 376 Mass. 70, 72 (1978). The law provides that notice of every meeting must be 
posted "at least 48 hours prior to such meeting, excluding Saturdays, Sundays and legal 
holidays," and that every notice shall include "the date, time and place of such meeting, and a 
listing of topics that the chair reasonably anticipates will be discussed at the meeting." G.L. c. 
30A, § 20(b). 
The complaint alleges that the Board violated the Open Meeting Law by publishing the 
announcement of the November 3, 2015 meeting before officially voting to hold that meeting. 
The Open Meeting Law contains no requirement that bodies vote before posting notice of a 
meeting.2 Indeed, the law is silent with regard to any procedures public bodies must follow in 
this regard. The law simply provides that public bodies shall post notice of all meetings at least 
48 hours in advance. G.L. c. 30A, § 20(b). Here, because the Board posted notice for the 
November 3, 2015 meeting at least 48 hours prior— on October 30, 2015— we find that the 
Board satisfied its obligations with respect to notice posting. Id. The Open Meeting Law does 
not govern the requirements for holding a public hearing; therefore we do not review, and offer 
no opinion on, whether there was a violation of any public hearing law. See OML 2015-39. 
II. The Board Did Not Violate the Open Meeting Law by Discussing the Fishing 
Permit Application. 
The complaint also alleges that the Board violated the Open Meeting Law by discussing a 
commercial fishing permit during the November 3, 2015 meeting, as the topic had not been listed 
on the meeting notice. Because the issue of the commercial fishing permit was not reasonably 
anticipated by the Board at least 48 hours prior to the November 3, 2015 meeting, the Board did 
not violate the Open Meeting Law by discussing this issue. If a discussion topic is proposed 
after a meeting notice is posted, public bodies are encouraged to update their posting to provide 
the public with as much notice as possible of what subjects will be discussed during a meeting. 
See OML 2012- 19. Although a public body may consider an unanticipated topic that was not 
listed in the meeting notice, the Attorney General strongly encourages public bodies to postpone
