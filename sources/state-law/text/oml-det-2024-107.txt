 RE: Open Meeting Law Complaint 
 
Dear Ms. Sewall: 
 
This office received a complaint from Patrick Higgins on March 26, 2024, alleging that 
the Dartmouth Historical Commission (the “Commission”) violated the Open Meeting Law, G.L. 
c. 30A, §§ 18-25.  The complaint was originally filed with the Commission on March 6, and you 
responded on behalf of the Commission by letter dated March 25.
1  The complaint alleges that 
the Commission, and specifically the chairperson, violated the Open Meeting Law by failing to 
announce that the Commission’s March 4 meeting was being recorded. 
 
Following review, we resolve this complaint by informal action, in accordance with 940 
CMR 29.07(2)(a).2  The Commission acknowledges, and we find, that the Commission violated 
the Open Meeting Law by failing to announce that the March 6 meeting was being recorded.    
See G.L. c. 30A, §20(f) (“At the beginning of the meeting, the chair shall inform other attendees 
of any recordings.”).  The Commission has explained that the omission was due to inexperience 
on the part of the new chair and the move to a room that has the capacity to record via DCTV.3  
The Commission has committed itself to making the announcement in the future.  Therefore, we 
decline to order that the Commission attend Open Meeting Law training, as requested by the 
complainant.
