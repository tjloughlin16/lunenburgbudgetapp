I. We decline to review the majority of the allegations raised in the complaints where, 
even if true, they would not constitute violations of the Open Meeting Law. 
 
The Division of Open Government’s statutory authority concerns compliance with the 
Open Meeting Law, G.L. c. 30A, §§ 18-25; we do not review whether a public body has 
complied with other laws or rules, including the body’s own rules. See OML Declination 9-19-
2025; OML 2020-41 (“Because our review concerns only compliance with the Open Meeting 
Law, we decline to review and offer no opinion on allegations that the Committee violated other 
laws or its own policy.”).
5  
 
The Open Meeting Law does not require that a public body allow for public participation 
at its meetings. Rather, the Open Meeting Law states that “[n]o person shall address a meeting of 
a public body without permission of the chair, and all persons shall, at the request of the chair, be 
silent.” G.L. c. 30A § 20(f). Additionally, we have explained that the term “person” in this 
section of the law applies to both members of the public and members of the public body. See 
OML 2013-135; OML 2020-134. Thus, the Open Meeting Law gives discretion to the chair of a 
public body to determine who may speak or otherwise participate at a meeting. See OML 2017-
154; OML 2014-23; OML 2013-64; OML 2012-59.6 Furthermore, the chair of a public body is 
empowered to conduct the meeting. See OML 2023-217. Finally, the law does not require a 
public body to discuss any particular matter or discuss a matter in any particular depth, nor does 
the law prohibit a public body from removing, postponing, declining, or tabling the discussion of 
a topic that is listed on a meeting notice. See OML 2023-37; OML 2022-231; OML 2014-35; 
OML 2012-23.  
 
The allegations that the Board did not allow for public comment during the August 22 
meeting; that the chair refused a Board member’s request to discuss appointments in more detail 
during the meeting; and that the discussion of appointments had been tabled and delayed several 
times since June, even if true, would not constitute violations of the Open Meeting Law.

II. We find that the Board did not violate the Open Meeting Law by scheduling its 
August 22 meeting for a Friday at 2:00 p.m. 
 
The complaint alleges that the Board scheduled the August 22 meeting at the end of the 
summer, on a Friday, at 2:00 p.m. intentionally “to avoid public opinion and comment regarding 
Appointments and reappointments to boards and committees.” The Open Meeting Law does not 
dictate when a public body may hold a meeting. See OML 2026-15; OML Declination 11-26-
2024 (Lawrence Alliance for Education); OML 2018-27. However, a public body should not 
schedule a meeting with the intent of discouraging attendance at the meeting. See OML 2026-15. 
Although the complaint alleges that the meeting was scheduled with the intent of avoiding public 
opinion and comment, no evidence has been presented to support this assertion or otherwise 
support a finding that the Board scheduled the August 22 meeting with the intent of discouraging 
public attendance at the meeting. Therefore, we find no violation with respect to this allegation.
