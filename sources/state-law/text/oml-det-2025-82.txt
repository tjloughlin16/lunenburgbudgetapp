 RE:  Open Meeting Law Complaint 
 
Dear Mr. Aponte: 
 
This office received a complaint from Christopher Kelleher on April 2, 2025, alleging 
that the Brookfield Cable Advisory Committee (the “Committee”) violated the Open Meeting 
Law, G.L. c. 30A, §§ 18-25.  The complaint was originally filed with the Committee on or about 
January 6, 2025, and you responded, on behalf of the Committee, by email dated March 27, 
2025, following extensions of time granted by this office.  The complaint alleges that the 
Committee posted a notice with insufficient specificity for a meeting held on December 11, 
2024.   
 
Following our review, we find that the Committee violated the Open Meeting Law in the 
way alleged.  In reaching this determination, we reviewed the original complaint, the 
Committee’s response to the complaint, and the complaint filed with our office requesting further 
review.  In addition, we reviewed the notice and minutes of the Committee meeting held on 
December 11, 2024.   
 
The Open Meeting Law requires that, except in an emergency, “a public body shall post 
notice of every meeting at least 48 hours prior to such meeting, excluding Saturdays, Sundays 
and legal holidays.”  G.L. c. 30A, § 20(b).  The notice must be printed in a legible, easily 
understandable format and must contain the date, time and place of the meeting, as well as a 
listing of topics that the chair reasonably anticipates will be discussed.  Id.  The list of topics

shall have “sufficient specificity to reasonably advise the public of the issues to be discussed at 
the meeting.”  940 CMR 29.03(l)(b).  We generally consider a topic to be sufficiently specific 
when a reasonable member of the public could read the topic and understand the anticipated 
nature of the public body’s discussion.  See OML 2015-35; OML 2013-39.
1  When reviewing a 
meeting notice for sufficiency, unless it is clearly insufficient on its face, we do not review a 
meeting notice standing alone.  See OML 2022-154; OML 2015-109; OML 2014-155.  Rather, 
we review what was actually discussed at the meeting to determine if the notice was sufficiently 
detailed to make the public aware of the discussion topics.  See OML 2021-22; OML 2020-137; 
OML 2019-64. 
 
Here, the Committee posted notice for a meeting to be held on December 11, 2024, at 
6:00 P.M.  The notice listed the following seven topics: call to order, go over next steps, public 
hearing date, survey information, contract update, set next meeting, and adjourn.  During the 
December 11, 2024, meeting, Committee Chair Sarah Campbell stated that she was waiting for 
Charter to provide a draft version of the cable contract and that a public hearing date would be 
set once a draft contract was provided and reviewed by the Committee.  The Committee then 
discussed sample surveys they found from surrounding communities regarding cable access.  
Committee Member Ryan Servant provided examples of contracts the Town of Brookfield had 
previously entered for comparison. 
 
A public body need not list each and every detail about a topic, nor anticipate on the 
notice the course of deliberation or the outcome of a discussion.  See OML 2022-98; OML 2020- 
132; OML 2019-134.  However, notice topics must be described with sufficient specificity to 
reasonably advise the public of the issues to be discussed at the meeting so that the public can 
make an informed decision about whether to attend the meeting in order to observe an 
anticipated discussion.  See OML 2023-86; OML 2019-95; OML 2018-22; OML 2015-74.  The 
Committee maintains that the notice included sufficient specificity because the only goal of the 
Committee is to make a recommendation to the Select Board on the cable contract with 
Spectrum.  Although the topics, as listed, may have been meaningful to Committee members and 
regular Committee meeting attendees, we find that they were insufficient to inform members of 
the public unfamiliar with the regular matters before the Committee of the anticipated nature of 
the Committee’s discussions.  See OML 2024-32; OML 2020-164; OML 2013-55.  As such, we 
find that the Committee posted a notice with insufficiently specific topics for the December 11, 
2024, meeting. 
 
For the reasons stated above, we find that the Committee violated the Open Meeting Law.  
We order the Committee’s immediate and future compliance with the law’s requirements, and 
we caution that similar future violations could be considered evidence of intent to violate the 
law.
