RE: Open Meeting Law Complaint 
Dear Deputy General Counsel Driscoll: 
The Attorney General's Office reviewed a complaint by Colleen Brayer, dated February 
9, 2012, alleging that the Massachusetts School Building Authority (the "MSBA") violated the 
Open Meeting Law, G.L. c. 30A, §§ 18-25. Specifically, the complaint alleges that the "MSBA 
and its subcommittees have not kept minutes of their meetings," and requests that the MSBA 
"Neconvene the subcommittee where the decision for Duxbury to build a new co-located middle 
high school was made." The complaint was filed with the MSBA on or about January 26, 2012, 
and the MSBA responded by letter on February 2, 2012. 
After reviewing the complaint and the MSBA's response, and speaking with both the 
MSBA and the complainant by telephone, we have decided to resolve this complaint by 
informal action,  in accordance with 940 CMR 29.017(2)(a). We find that the MSBA did not 
violate the Open Meeting Law. 
The MSBA does keep minutes of its meetings and subcommittee meetings. The meetings 
during which the decision was made "to build a new co-located middle high school" were not 
meetings of the MSBA or its subcommittees, however. It appears that members of MSBA staff 
met with Duxbury town officials to discuss Duxbury's new middle/high school project. These 
meetings did not include members of the MSBA or any of its subcommittees. Because these 
meetings did not involve a quorum of a public body, there was no requirement that the 
participants post notice for or make these meetings open to the public. See G. L. c. 30A, § 18;
