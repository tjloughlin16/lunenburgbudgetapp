 RE: Open Meeting Law Complaint 
 
Dear Attorney O’Connor: 
This office received a complaint from Joseph Harter on May 13, 2021, alleging that the 
Boston Zoning Board of Appeals (the “Board”) violated the Open Meeting Law, G.L. c. 30A,   
§§ 18-25.1 The complaint was originally filed with the Board on April 8, and you responded, on 
behalf of the Board, by letter dated May 3. In his complaint, Mr. Harter alleges that he was not 
permitted to speak during a Board meeting held on April 6.   
We resolve this matter by informal action, in accordance with 940 CMR 29.07(2)(a), 
after reviewing the original complaint, the Commission’s response to the complaint, and the 
complaint filed with our office requesting further review. We find that the Board did not violate 
the Open Meeting Law in the way alleged. 
The Open Meeting Law does not require that a public body allow public participation in 
its meetings, but rather provides that “[n]o person shall address a meeting of a public body 
without permission of the chair, and all persons shall, at the request of the chair, be silent.” G.L. 
c. 30A § 20(f); see also OML 2021-27; OML 2020-161; OML 2017-189; OML 2014-23; OML 
2012-78.2 We do, however, encourage public bodies to allow for as much public participation as 
time and circumstances permit. See OML 2015-12; OML 2014-129; OML 2012-59.

 On April 6, the Board met remotely and held several public hearings, one of which 
pertained to a request for a variance for a roof deck at 42 Irving Street. Mr. Harter is an abutter to 
this property and submitted written opposition to the Board with respect to the variance request. 
However, he also wished to speak in opposition to the variance during the April 6 meeting. 
When the Chair sought comments from the public with respect to the 42 Irving Street project 
during the April 6 meeting, Mr. Harter waived his hands in front of his computer screen in an 
attempt to alert the Chair, but she did not recognize Mr. Harter and he was unable to speak 
during the meeting. The Open Meeting Law permits the Chair of the Board to decide who may 
speak at a meeting and for how long. See OML 2013-2; OML 2012-23. Therefore, because it is 
within the chair’s discretion to limit public participation during a meeting, the chair did not 
violate the Open Meeting Law by preventing Mr. Harter from addressing the Board during the 
April 6 meeting.3 See OML 2020-114; OML 2017-189; OML 2014-23; OML 2012-78.  
 
 For the reasons stated above, we find that the Board did not violate the Open Meeting 
Law. We now consider the complaint addressed by this determination to be resolved. This 
determination does not address any other complaints that may be pending with our office or the 
Board. Please feel free to contact the Division at (617) 963-2540 if you have any questions.
