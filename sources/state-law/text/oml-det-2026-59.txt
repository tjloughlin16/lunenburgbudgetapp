This office received a complaint from Patrick Higgins on January 7, 2026, alleging that 
the Bridgewater Town Council (the “Council”) violated the Open Meeting Law, G.L. c. 30A, §§ 
18-25.  The complaint was originally filed with the Council on or about December 18, 2025, and

way alleged.  In reaching this determination, we reviewed the original complaint, the Council’s 
response to the complaint, and the complaint filed with our office requesting further review.  In

DISCUSSION 
 
The Open Meeting Law requires that, except in an emergency, “a public body shall post 
notice of every meeting at least 48 hours prior to such meeting, excluding Saturdays, Sundays 
and legal holidays.”  G.L. c. 30A, § 20(b).  The notice must be printed in a legible, easily 
understandable format and must contain the date, time and place of the meeting, as well as a 
listing of topics that the chair reasonably anticipates will be discussed.  Id.  The list of topics 
shall have “sufficient specificity to reasonably advise the public of the issues to be discussed at 
the meeting.”  940 CMR 29.03(l)(b).  We generally consider a topic to be sufficiently specific 
when a reasonable member of the public could read the topic and understand the anticipated 
nature of the public body’s discussion.  See OML 2019-70; OML 2015-35; OML 2013-39.
2   
 
The complaint alleges that the notice of the Council’s December 2, 2025, meeting lacked 
sufficient specificity because it failed to list the specific businesses seeking renewal of licenses.  
The Council maintains that “each agenda item listed within [the December 2, 2025] notice 
included corresponding documentation to ensure all proper information was provided to the 
general public.”  Since 2018, we have consistently required that, to be sufficiently specific, a 
meeting notice must identify the specific applicants or license holders that will be considered 
during the meeting, as well as the specific type of license renewal to be reviewed.  See OML 
2020-112; OML 2020-20; OML 2017-33; OML 2016-90.  That is, where a topic requires a 
public body’s approval or other specific decision, such as annual license renewals or 
appointment of candidates to public bodies, the specific applicants or candidates should be listed 
on the notice itself; it is not sufficient to reference a separate list that is available elsewhere.  See 
OML 2024-236; OML 2022-68; OML 2020-20; OML 2019-22.  We find here that the Council 
violated the Open Meeting Law by posting an insufficiently specific notice where it failed to list 
the specific businesses the Council was considering during the December 2, 2025, meeting.

CONCLUSION 
 
For the reasons stated above, we find that the Council violated the Open Meeting Law by 
posting an insufficiently specific notice for the December 2, 2025, meeting that failed to list the 
specific businesses seeking renewal of licenses.  We order the Council’s immediate and future 
compliance with the law’s requirements, and we caution that similar future violations could be 
considered evidence of intent to violate the law. 
 
We now consider the complaint addressed by this determination to be resolved.  This 
determination does not address any other complaints that may be pending with our office or the 
Council.  Please feel free to contact our office at (617) 963-2540 if you have any questions 
regarding this letter.
