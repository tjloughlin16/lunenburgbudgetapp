This office received a complaint from Stephen Morgan on September 14, 2022,
1 alleging 
that the Ashland Design Review Committee (the “Committee”) violated the Open Meeting Law, 
G.L. c. 30A, §§ 18-25. The complaint was originally filed with the Committee on or about 
August 23, and you responded on behalf of the Committee by letter dated September 13. The 
complaint alleges that the Committee failed to timely approve several sets of meeting minutes. 
 
Following our review, we find that the Committee violated the Open Meeting Law as 
alleged. In reaching this determination, we reviewed the original complaint, the Committee’s 
response to the complaint, and the complainant’s request for further review. We also reviewed 
Committee meeting notices posted on the Town’s website, the notice and minutes of the 
Committee’s August 18 meeting, and the minutes of the Committee’s September 1 meeting.  
 
The Open Meeting Law requires that a public body “create and maintain accurate 
minutes of all meetings, including executive sessions, setting forth the date, time and place, the 
members present or absent, a summary of the discussions on each subject, a list of documents 
and other exhibits used at the meeting, the decisions made and the actions taken at each meeting, 
including the record of all votes.” G.L. c. 30A, § 22(a). The meeting minutes must be reviewed 
and approved in a timely manner. G.L. c. 30A, § 22(c). “Timely manner” means “within the next 
three public body meetings or within 30 days, whichever is later, unless the public body can

Committee voted to approve the remaining meeting minutes as well as minutes for a meeting

The Committee does not di
 spute that it failed to timely approve meeting minutes. Instead, 
the Committee asserts that the Open Meeting Law complaint was not filed with the Committee in 
a timely manner and therefore the Attorney General should decline to review it. We disagree. 
Complaints alleging violations of the Open Meeting Law generally must be filed with the public 
body within 30 days of the alleged violation. G.L. c. 30A, § 23; 940 CMR 29.05(4). The Open 
Meeting Law imposes a continuing obligation to maintain minutes of all meetings. See G.L. c. 
30A, § 22(a); OML 2020-69 (reviewing a complaint regarding a committee’s failure to approve 
meeting minutes for a three-year period); OML 2019-133; OML 2018-115 (reviewing a 
complaint regarding the failure to create and maintain meeting minutes for meetings held four 
years prior); OML 2013-45. Therefore, we consider the complaint to be timely as the alleged 
violation continued each day until the minutes were approved, and we find that the Committee 
violated the Open Meeting Law by failing to timely approve all but one set of meeting minutes at 
issue in the complaint. 
 
For the
 reasons stated above, we find that the Committee violated the Open Meeting Law 
as alleged. We order the Committee’s immediate and future compliance with the Open Meeting 
Law and caution the Committee that a determination by our office of a similar violation in the 
future may be considered evidence of intent to violate the Open Meeting Law. The Committee 
has since approved all the meeting minutes at issue in the complaint; therefore, we order no 
further remedial action.
