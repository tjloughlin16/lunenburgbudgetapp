RE: Open Meeting Law Complaint 
Dear Ms. Nearen: 
This office received a complaint from David Harris, Jr., dated May 11, 2012, alleging 
that the Randolph School Committee (the "Committee") violated the Open Meeting Law, G.L. c. 
30A, §§ 18-25. Specifically, the complaint alleges that Committee "failed to make available to 
me a copy of the minutes of the Randolph School Committee's executive session of the school 
committee meeting that took place on March 15, 2012," and that there is "no lawful purpose 
remaining that would jeopardize the districts security plans because the plans had already been 
made public." The complaint was filed with the Committee on or about April 10, 2012. The 
Committee responded by letter dated April 27, 2012. 
We find that the Committee did not violate the Open Meeting Law. In reaching this 
determination, we reviewed the April 10, 2012 complaint; the Committee's April 27, 2012 
response; and the May 11, 2012 complaint filed with our office. Additionally, we reviewed the 
notice and executive session minutes of the Committee's March 15, 2012 meeting. Finally, we 
interviewed by telephone Committee Chair Marybeth Nearenl  on June 28, 2012. 
FACTS 
The Committee held a properly posted meeting on March 15, 2012. The notice for the 
meeting listed thirteen topics, including "Executive Session for the purpose of the Deployment of 
Security Personnel or Devices." During the meeting, the Committee entered into executive 
session for approximately an hour. The minutes of the executive session have not yet been 
released, so we do not recount their content here. However, after reviewing the minutes, in 
camera, and interviewing Committee Chair Marybeth Nearen, it is clear that the Committee 
discussed a security plan, focusing specifically on the placement of security personnel and

The complainant states that following the executive session, the Superintendent of

on March 15, 2012 has not expired. For such reason, it is not appropriate to release these 
minutes at this time." 
DISCUSSION 
A public body may enter executive—or closed—session for any of ten enumerated 
purposes. G.L. c. 30A, § 21(a). Among the ten purposes is Purpose 4, which allows a public 
body to enter into executive session to "discuss the deployment of security personnel or devices, 
or strategies with respect thereto." G.L. c. 30A, § 21(a)(4). A public body must take minutes of 
its meetings, including executive sessions. G.L. c. 30A, § 22(a). The minutes of any executive 
session may be withheld from disclosure to the public in their entirety "as long as publication 
may defeat the lawful purposes of the executive session, but no longer; provided, however, that 
the executive session was held in compliance with section 21." G.L. c. 30A, § 22(f). Generally, 
our office will provide some deference to a public body's decision regarding its need for 
continued confidentiality with respect to the deployment of security personnel or devices. 
Here, the Committee met in executive session on March 15, 2012 specifically to discuss 
"the deployment of security personnel or devices, or strategies with respect thereto" as part of a 
security plan. G.L. c. 30A, § 21(a)(4). The discussion specifically focused on the deployment 
and location of security personnel and devices. After speaking with Chair Nearen, we are 
convinced that the Committee's need for confidentiality remains, and that the publication of the 
March 15, 2012 executive session minutes at this time would defeat the lawful purpose of the 
executive session. G.L. c. 30A, § 22(f). We understand that at some time in the future, once the 
Committee has implemented a security program, that the minutes may be publicly released. 
Until that time, the Committee is within its rights under the Open Meeting Law to maintain the 
confidentiality of the minutes. 
In his May 11, 2012 complaint, the complainant points to a number of activities and 
communications the Committee and the District have taken since the March 15, 2012 executive 
session that he argues reveal the decisions made in the executive session and thus defeat the 
purpose for continued non-disclosure. Having reviewed the executive session minutes and 
spoken with Chair Nearen, we disagree with that conclusion. The communications the
