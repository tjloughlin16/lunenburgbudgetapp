This office received a complaint from Martin Beinborn on August 2, 2024, alleging that 
the Boston Zoning Board of Appeals (the “Board”) violated the Open Meeting Law, G.L. c. 30A, 
§§ 18-25.1  The complaint was originally filed with the Board on or about May 24, and you 
responded, on behalf of the Board, by letter dated June 25.2  The complaint alleges that the 
Board posted an insufficiently specific notice for a meeting held on April 30.  
 
Following our review, we find that the Board did not violate the Open Meeting Law in 
the way alleged.  In reaching this determination, we reviewed the original complaint, the Board’s 
response to the complaint, and the complaint filed with our office requesting further review.  In 
addition, we reviewed the notice and minutes of the Board meeting held on April 30, as well as a 
video recording of that meeting.

FACTS 
 
On July 22, 2021, Tremont & Pontiac LLC, a developer, filed a Letter of Intent with the 
Boston Planning and Development Agency for the redevelopment of 1558 Tremont Street in the 
Mission Hill Section of Boston.  The proposed project would demolish an existing three-story 
vacant building, located on the corner of Tremont and Saint Alphonsus Streets, and construct a 
seven-story, mixed-use multi-family building with 139 residential units, approximately 1700 
square feet of first floor retail, and 53 parking spaces.  The Boston Planning and Development 
Agency held numerous public hearings in 2022 and 2023, and during those hearings the project 
was referred to and noticed as 1558 Tremont Street.  In October 2023, the Boston Planning and 
Development Agency approved construction of a six-story, mixed-use multi-family building 
with 95 residential units, approximately 3200 square feet of first floor retail, and 53 parking 
spaces. 
 
Thereafter, the developer filed an application with the Board seeking variances with 
respect to building height, open space, parking, floor area ratio, and yard space.  On April 25, 
2024, at 2:06 P.M., the Board posted notice of a meeting to be held on April 30, 2024, at 9:30 
A.M.  The notice listed numerous hearings, one of which was “Case: BOA-1570022  Address: 
100 Saint Alphonsus Street  Ward 10 Applicant: John Pulgini-ARTICLE 80.”  The notice 
described the project as razing an existing structure to construct a six-story building on a newly 
created lot with 95 residential units, a ground floor restaurant with approximately 3200 square 
feet, and garage parking with 53 spaces.  The notice also referenced the various zoning codes 
that the project was not in compliance with and thus would require a variance.   
 
The April 30 meeting was held as planned.  During the meeting, the Board held a hearing 
on the project and heard from the developer’s attorney, architect of the project, the Boston 
Mayor’s Office of Neighborhood Services, a carpenter’s union, abutters and members of the 
public, including the complainant.  Members of the Board questioned the project’s address 
change from 1558 Tremont Street to 100 Saint Alphonsus Street.  The attorney for the developer 
explained that the vacant building that would be razed faced Tremont Street but the new building 
would face Saint Alphonsus Street.  Thus, the address of the new six-story building would be 
100 Saint Alphonsus Street and that is how the City of Boston’s Inspectional Services 
Department referenced the application when it was submitted.
4  Ultimately, the Board approved 
the project and granted the variances.   
 
DISCUSSION 
 
The Open Meeting Law requires that meetings of a public body be properly noticed and 
open to m
embers of the public, unless an executive session is convened.  See G.L. c. 30A, §§ 
20(a)-(b), 21.  Public bodies must post notice of each meeting “at least 48 hours prior to such 
meeting, excluding Saturdays, Sundays and legal holidays” and every notice must include “the 
date, time and place of such meeting and a listing of topics that the chair reasonably anticipates 
will be discussed at the meeting.”  G.L. c. 30A, § 20(b).  The list of topics shall have “sufficient

The complaint alleges that the notice of the Board’s April 30 meeting “misleadingly” 
ident
ified a public hearing with a project location of 100 Saint Alphonsus Street rather than 1558 
Tremont Street.  We acknowledge that, during meetings held by the Boston Planning and 
Development Agency, the project was referred to and noticed as 1558 Tremont Street.  However, 
once the application was submitted to the Board, the project location was identified with a new 
address of 100 Saint Alphonsus Street.  Although listing additional detail, such as a reference to 
1558 Tremont Street, may have been helpful to the public, we are satisfied that the topic as listed 
was sufficient to reasonably advise the public of the issue to be discussed at the meeting.  See 
OML 2023-136.  Here, the notice contained sufficient specificity where it notified the public that 
the Board was conducting a public hearing with respect to a specifically identified property, and 
then described the project as one that would demolish an existing structure to construct a six-
story building with 95 residential units, a ground floor restaurant with approximately 3200 
square feet, and garage parking with 53 spaces, which was the exact project approved by the 
Boston Planning and Development Agency six months earlier and which was the subject of 
multiple hearings.  A public body need not list each and every detail about a topic in order to 
comply with the law’s requirements.  See OML 2022-98; OML 2020-132; OML 2019-134; 
OML 2014-122.   
 
With respect to the concerns raised in the complaint that the notice “compromised 
comm
unity participation,” the Open Meeting Law does not require that a public body allow 
public participation in its meetings, but rather provides that “[n]o person shall address a meeting 
of a public body without permission of the chair, and all persons shall, at the request of the chair, 
be silent.”  G.L. c. 30A § 20(f); see also OML 2021-27; OML 2020-161; OML 2017-189; OML 
2014-23; OML 2012-78.  Nonetheless, here, the Board’s Chair allowed public comment and we 
encourage public bodies to allow for as much public participation as time and circumstances 
permit.  See OML 2015-12; OML 2014-129; OML 2012-59.  
 
Although the Open Meeting Law does not require that members of the public be 
permitted to speak or otherwise actively participate during a meeting, numerous laws in the 
Commonwealth require that a public hearing be held before the government takes certain action.

requiring the hearing and such law may require opportunity for public comment.  However, the 
Division of Open Government’s review concerns compliance with the Open Meeting Law, G.L. 
c. 30A, §§ 18-25.  The Open Meeting Law does not govern the requirements for conducting a

CONCLUSION 
 
For the reasons stated above, we find that the Board did not violate the Open Meeting 
Law.  We now consider the complaint addressed by this determination to be resolved.  This 
determination does not address any other complaints that may be pending with our office or the 
Board.  Please feel free to contact the Division at (617) 963-2540 if you have any questions.
