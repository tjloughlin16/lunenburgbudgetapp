This office received two related complaints from John Hawkinson, both dated December 
21, 2015, alleging that the Cambridge Board of Zoning Appeals (the "Board") violated the Open 
Meeting Law, G.L. c. 30A, §§ 18-25. The first complaint was originally filed with the Board on 
October 26, 2015, and a second complaint, described as an "addendum" to the first complaint, 
was filed with the Board on November 3, 2015. The Board responded to both complaints by 
letter dated November 13, 2015. In his complaints, Mr. Hawkinson alleges that the Board 
violated the Open Meeting Law by: (i) failing to review and release executive session meeting 
minutes for which the statutory purpose for secrecy had expired; (ii) entering into executive 
session for an improper purpose; (iii) posting an inaccurate meeting notice; (iv) failing to 
respond to an Open Meeting Law complaint within the time frame required by law; (v) creating 
insufficiently specific executive session meeting minutes; and (vi) improperly redacting portions 
of executive session minutes.1 
We appreciate the patience of the parties while we reviewed this matter. Following our 
review, we find that the Board failed to release and approve executive session meeting minutes 
within the time frame required by the law. We also find that the minutes of one executive 
session contain an inaccuracy that violates the Open Meeting Law. However, we decline to 
consider certain other allegations concerning the sufficiency of redacted executive session 
minutes.

In reaching this determination, we reviewed the complaints, the Board's response, and

FACTS 
We find the facts as follows. The Board held a meeting on May 28, 2015, during which 
it entered executive session to discuss a pending litigation matter. The minutes state that six 
Board members were present for this executive session. The minutes state that the vote to enter 
executive session was "unanimous," yet the minutes list only four Board members who voted to 
enter the executive session.2 
On June 11, 2015, Mr. Hawkinson requested a copy of the May 28, 2015 executive 
session minutes. On June 25, 2015, the Board responded to Mr. Hawkinson's request, declining 
to release the executive session minutes and stating, "because the litigation remains ongoing, the 
disclosure of the executive session minutes would defeat the purpose for having gone in 
executive session." On September 16, 2015, the parties to the subject litigation filed a stipulation 
of dismissal, and the Appeals Court dismissed the case on September 18, 2015. 
On September 21, 2015, Mr. Hawkinson sent a second request for the May 28, 2015 
executive session minutes, noting that the litigation matter appeared to have been resolved. The 
Board held a meeting on September 24, 2015, but it did not discuss the May 28, 2015 executive 
session minutes. On October 1, 2015, the Board responded to Mr. Hawkinson's second request 
stating that the Board had not yet reviewed and approved the May 28, 2015 executive session 
minutes. On October 8, 2015, the Board again met without discussing either the May 28, 2015 
executive session minutes or Mr. Hawkinson's requests for those minutes. 
The Board held another meeting on October 22, 2015. The notice for this meeting listed 
three anticipated executive session topics, as follows: 
1. 42 BSR LLC & another v. Zoning Board of Cambridge, Appeals Court Docket No. 
2015-P-1074; minutes of the May 28, 2015 Board of Zoning Appeals executive 
session. 
2. Richard D. Fanning v. Constantine Alexander et ah. Land Court Docket No. 4 MISC 
484762-RBF. 
3. BZA Case No. 007664-2015/T-MohHe Northeast, LLC special permit application. 
The Board held its October 22, 2015 meeting as planned and entered executive session to 
discuss the topics mentioned above. While in executive session, the Board approved the May 28, 
2015 executive session minutes and voted to release them. However, certain portions of the 
minutes were redacted based on attorney-client privilege.

DISCUSSION 
I. The Board Violated the Open Meeting Law by Failing to Respond to a Request 
for Executive Session Minutes with the Time Frame Required by Law, and by 
Failing to Approve and Release the Requested Executive Session Minutes within 
the Time Frame Required by Law. 
Public bodies must create and maintain minutes of all meetings. G.L. c. 30A, § 22(a). 
Minutes of open session meetings must be created and approved in a timely manner. See OML 
2012-91. While the Open Meeting Law does not define "timely manner," we recommend that 
minutes be approved at the next meeting, whenever possible. Id Executive session minutes may 
be withheld from disclosure "as long as publication may defeat the lawful puiposes of the 
executive session, but no longer." G.L. c. 30A, § 22(f). When the purpose for a valid executive 
session has been served, the minutes and any documents or exhibits used at the session must be 
disclosed, unless the attorney-client privilege or an exemption to the public records law applies. 
Id. Public bodies have an obligation to review the minutes of executive sessions at reasonable 
intervals to determine if continued non-disclosure of minutes is warranted, and to announce that 
determination at the next meeting following its review. G.L. c. 30A, § 22(g)(1). The burden of 
justifying continued nondisclosure of executive session minutes lies with the public body. Foudy 
v. Amherst-Pelham Regional School Committee, 402 Mass. 179, 184 (1988). A public body 
must respond to a request for executive session meeting minutes within 10 days. G.L. c. 30A, § 
22(g)(2). Upon receiving the request, the public body must review the executive session minutes 
to determine whether the purpose for the executive session continues to require non-disclosure. 
Id. This review must occur at the public body's next regular meeting, or within 30 days, 
whichever comes first. Id. In such circumstances, the body should respond to the requestor 
within 10 days, notifying him or her that it is conducting this review. See OML 2013-99. 
Here, Mr. Hawkinson first requested a copy of the May 28, 2015 executive session 
minutes on June 11, 2015. The Board responded to the request on June 25, 2015—14 days after 
the request. To be in compliance, the Board should have notified Mr. Hawkinson within ten 
days that it would not be releasing the May 28, 2015 executive session minutes. See OML 2015-
102. Its failure to do so here violated the Open Meeting Law. See OML 2016-42. 
At the time that the Board responded, the litigation matter discussed during the executive 
session at issue was ongoing. Mr. Hawkinson again requested the minutes on September 21, 
2015. Because the litigation matter had been resolved at the time Mr. Hawkinson sent his second 
request, the Board should have reviewed and released the May 28, 2015 executive session 
minutes at its next meeting or within 30 days, whichever was first. G.L. c. 30A, § 22(g)(2). The 
Board did not do so. Instead, the Board held two meetings—on September 24 and October 8, 
2015—before voting to release the minutes during its October 22, 2015. This delay violated the 
Open Meeting Law. See OML 2012-91. Ultimately, the Board did not vote to approve the May 
28, 2015 executive session minutes until the October 22, 2015 meeting, nearly five months after 
the meeting at issue. Even if the Board believed that the executive session purpose warranted 
continued non-disclosure, the Board was obligated to approve final minutes in a timely manner. 
Id. By failing to do so, the Board violated the Open Meeting Law.

II. The Board had a Valid Purpose for Convening in Executive Session on October 
22. 2015. However, the Notice of this Meeting Contains a Minor Typographical 
Error. 
A public body may meet in executive session to discuss the release of past executive 
session minutes, citing the original executive session purpose used to hold the meeting for which 
the minutes are being reviewed. See OML 2015-25; OML 2013-199. When listing such 
discussions on a meeting notice, public bodies must include "sufficient specificity to reasonably 
advise the public of the issues to be discussed at the meeting." 940 CMR 29.03(l)(b). We 
generally consider a topic to be sufficiently specific when a reasonably member of the public 
could read the topic and understand the anticipated nature of the public body's discussion. See 
OML 2011-44. 
Here, the Board met in executive session on October 22, 2015 to discuss, in part, the 
release of the May 28, 2015 executive session minutes. By listing the litigation matter for which 
the May 28, 2015 executive session was convened, the meeting notice complied with the Open 
Meeting Law. See OML 2015-25; OML 2013-199. However, we note that the second 
anticipated executive session topic should have read "14 MISC 484762-RBF," as opposed to "4 
MISC 484762-RBF." We find that this meeting notice, despite this minor typographical error, 
substantially complied with the Open Meeting Law's requirements.3 
III. The May 28. 2015 Executive Session Minutes Contain an Inaccuracv. 
The Open Meeting Law requires public bodies to "create and maintain accurate minutes 
of all meetings, including executive sessions, setting forth the date, time and place, the members 
present or absent, a summary of the discussions on each subject, a list of documents and other 
exhibits used at the meeting, the decisions made and the actions taken at each meetings, 
including the record of all votes." G.L. c. 30A, § 22(a). When reviewing minutes for compliance 
with the Open Meeting Law, we look for substantial compliance with the accuracy requirement. 
See OML 2013-64. The minutes should contain enough detail and accuracy so that a member of 
the public who did not attend the meeting could read the minutes and have a clear understanding 
of what occurred. See id. 
The May 28, 2015 executive session minutes state that six Board members were present. 
The minutes state that the vote to enter executive session was "unanimous," yet the minutes list 
only four Board members who voted to enter executive session. Because of this incongruity, 
members of the public cannot know, for example, if any Board members voted against the 
motion to enter executive session. This omission violated the Open Meeting Law. G.L. c. 30A, 
§ 22(a). 
IV. The Attorney General is Unable to Review the Minutes for Which the Board 
Asserts the Attorney-Client Privilege. 
The Open Meeting Law states that minutes, preparatory materials, documents and 
exhibits of an executive session shall be disclosed "unless the attorney-client privilege or 1 or 
more of the exemptions under [the Public Records Law] apply to withhold these records, or any 
portion thereof, from disclosure." G.L. c. 30A, § 22(f). When investigating a complaint, the

Attorney General cannot "require the disclosure of any documentary material which would be 
privileged, or which for any other reason would not be required by a subpoena duces tecum 
issued by a court of the commonwealth," G.L. c. 30A, § 24(e). Here, significant portions of the 
May 28, 2015 executive session minutes are redacted on the basis of attorney-client privilege and 
we have no reason to challenge the Board's claim of privilege.4 We note that the complainant 
may file an Open Meeting Law complaint in Superior Court to seek a judicial order that the 
documents at issue be revealed in their entirety. See G.L. c. 30A, § 23(f); OML 2012-41.5 The 
complainant may also seek review by the Supervisor of Public Records. 
CONCLUSION 
For the reasons stated above, we find that the Board violated the Open Meeting Law. We 
order the Board's immediate and future compliance with the Open Meeting Law. We caution 
that similar future violations may be considered evidence of intent to violate the law. Further, 
we order the Board to amend, within thirty days, the May 28, 2015 executive session minutes to 
clarify the inaccuracy discussed in Section III of this letter. 
We now consider the complaint addressed by this determination to be resolved. This 
determination does not address any other complaints that may be pending with our office or the 
Board. Please feel free to contact the Division at (617) 963 - 2540 if you have any questions.
