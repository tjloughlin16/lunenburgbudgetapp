This office received a complaint from Collin Dias on January 11, 2019, alleging that the 
Fall River Tax Increment Finance Board (the “Board”) violated the Open Meeting Law, G.L. c. 
30A, §§ 18-25. The complaint was originally filed with the Board on December 3, 2018, and 
you responded, on behalf of the Board, by email dated June 18, 2019.1 In his complaint, Mr. 
Dias alleges that the Board’s November 26, 2018, meeting was not accessible to all persons and 
that the meeting notice did not include “citizens input” on the notice.
Following our review, we find that the Board did not violate the Open Meeting Law. In 
reaching this determination, we reviewed the original complaint, the Board’s various responses 
to our requests for information related to the complaint, and the complaint filed with our office 
requesting further review. We also reviewed the notice and open session minutes of the Board’s 
November 26, 2018 meeting. Finally, we spoke with Mr. Dias by telephone on June 6, 2019.

notice for a meeting to be held on November 26, 2018. The notice listed the location of the 
meeting as the Mayor’s Office Conference Room, One Government Center in Fall River. The 
Mayor’s Office Conference Room is on the sixth floor, which is serviced by an elevator, and the

DISCUSSION
The Open Meeting Law was enacted “to eliminate much of the secrecy surrounding 
deliberations and decisions on which public policy is based.” Ghiglione v. School Committee of 
Southbridge. 376 Mass. 70, 72 (1978). The law requires that meetings of a public body be 
properly noticed and open to members of the public, unless an executive session is convened.
See G.L. c. 30A, §§ 20(a)-(b), 21. Access to a meeting must include the opportunity to be 
physically present, as well as to see and hear what is being discussed by the members of the 
public body. See OML 2017-135; OML 2015-38; OML 2014-3; OML 2013-189.2
The complaint alleges that the Mayor’s Office Conference Room is not handicap 
accessible because it is a small room with a large table and many chairs, thus prohibiting 
wheelchair access. The Board responded that the Mayor’s Office Conference Room has been 
“assessed and is, in fact, handicapped accessible,” with elevator access and a wide doorway to 
accommodate wheelchairs. For a meeting to be truly “open to the public,” it must be held at a 
location that is accessible to persons with disabilities. See G.L. c. 30A; § 20(a); OML 2013-8; 
OML 2012-49 (stating that meetings of public bodies must be held in locations that are 
compliant with the Americans with Disabilities Act). This means that public bodies must hold 
public meetings in a location with accessible parking, access to the building, and access to the 
meeting room. See 28 C.F.R. § 35.150 (2011); Dep’t of Justice, Americans with Disabilities 
Act: Title II Technical Assistance Manual, at 5.1000 (1993). The complaint does not allege, nor 
did our investigation reveal, that the furniture layout in an otherwise accessible room actually 
prevented any member of the public from attending the November 26, 2018, Board meeting. See 
OML 2019-14; OML 2018-138. As such, we find that the Board did not violate the Open 
Meeting Law.
The complaint further alleges that the notice for the November 26, 2018 meeting did not 
include a time for public comment and therefore violated the City Charter. The Open Meeting 
Law does not require that a public body allow public participation in its meetings, but rather 
provides that “[n]o person shall address a meeting of a public body without permission of the 
chair, and all persons shall, at the request of the chair, be silent.” G.L. c. 30A, § 20(g). It is 
within the chair’s discretion to determine who may speak or otherwise participate at a meeting, 
and thus he did not violate the Open Meeting Law by limiting public participation here. See 
OML 2014-23; OML 2013-64; OML 2012-59. Given that our office is charged specifically with
