FACTS 
Complaint #1  
On March 30, 2011, Mr. Heydecker requested the executive session minutes for the 
meetings during which the Board discussed its negotiating strategy with respect to the Police 
Union contract. On April 29, 2011, Mr. Paicos responded on behalf of the Board and stated that 
the minutes had not been publicly released. On July 11, 2011, Mr. Heydecker made a second 
request for executive session minutes concerning the police contract. Mr. Heydecker specifically 
requested the executive session minutes from meetings during which the Board approved a cost 
of living adjustment for the Police Union. Mr. Paicos responded on August 8, 2011, stating that 
there were no records responsive to this request. Mr. Paicos' response followed a search 
conducted by Ms. Shewry. In a December 20, 2011 email to our office, Ms. Shewry indicated 
that, when initially responding to this request on behalf of the Board, she was looking for 
minutes from meetings during which the Board voted on the police contract. She was therefore 
under the impression that no minutes were responsive to this request. After the complaint was 
filed, however, Ms. Shewry discovered that the Board discussed the police contract in executive 
session on December 21, 2010, but did not vote on the contract at that time. The minutes of this 
meeting were approved and released on April 5, 2011. Mr. Heydecker received the December 
21, 2010 executive session minutes from the Board on January 17, 2012.

was made concerning a possible increase to the "license fee schedule." He asserts that during 
this presentation, the Board was handed a document that explained the increase in the Board's 
fees. The complainant states that he received this document during the March 29, 2011 meeting.

DISCUSSION 
The Open Meeting Law states that a public body may enter into a closed, executive 
session for any of ten enumerated purposes. G.L. c. 30A, § 21(a). A public body may withhold 
its executive session minutes from public disclosure if publication will defeat the lawful purpose 
of the executive session. G.L. c. 30A, § 22(f). However, executive session minutes must be 
disclosed when the purpose for the executive session has been served. Id. A public body must 
review the minutes of its executive sessions at reasonable intervals to determine whether 
continued non-disclosure of the minutes is warranted. G.L. c. 30A, § 22(g)(1). Such 
determinations must be announced at the public body's next meeting and included in the open 
session minutes of the meeting. Id. Furthermore, a public body must respond within 10 days of 
receiving a request for executive session minutes, and must release any minutes that are not 
exempt from disclosure. G.L. c. 30A, § 22(g)(2). If the public body has not performed a review 
of the executive session minutes to determine whether continued non-disclosure is warranted, the

In his first complaint, Mr. Heydecker alleges that the Board failed to publicly release, 
within 10 days of request, the executive session minutes of the meetings during which the Board 
discussed its negotiating strategy with the Police Union. Mr. Heydecker's first request was made 
on March 30, 2011. The Board responded 30 days later and stated that the minutes had not been 
released. A second request for minutes was made on July 11, 2011. The Board responded 29 
days later and stated that there were no records responsive to his request. In both instances, the 
Board violated the Open Meeting Law by failing to respond to Mr. Heydecker's request within 
10 days of a request. See OML 2012-17. 6  Additionally, we understand that not only did 
responsive minutes exist, they also were approved for release to the public on April 5, 2011. 
Therefore, the Board should have provided Mr. Heydecker with the requested minutes at the time 
of his second request. We understand that the Board may have had difficulty understanding Mr. 
Heydecker's March 30, 2011 and July 1 1, 2011 requests. However, when a request for minutes 
is unclear, public bodies should contact the requestor promptly for clarification of the request. 
Mr. Heydecker also alleges that the Board failed to provide him with a document that 
contained the "schedule of fee changes" for all Town departments. According to Mr. Heydecker, 
the "schedule of fee changes" was discussed and approved during the Board's March 29, 2011 
meeting. While the Board may have been required to produce this document if it was, in fact, 
used during an open session meeting, we have not been presented with any evidence that this 
document was used during the March 29, 2011 meeting, and Mr. Paicos indicated that no such 
document exists. See OML 2012-42. We have not been presented with any evidence 
contradicting Mr. Paicos' assertion. We therefore find that the Board did not violate the Open 
Meeting Law by failing to provide this document within 10 days of the complainant's request. 
Mr. Heydecker's third complaint alleges that the Board failed to provide executive 
session minutes from meetings held between May 25, 2010 and December 6, 2011 within 10 
days of a request. Mr. Heydecker made this request to the Board on December 9, 2011. The 
Board responded 53 days later, stating that the requested executive session minutes had not been 
released. The Board violated the Open Meeting Law by failing to respond to Mr. Heydecker's 
request within 10 days. Additionally, it appears that many of the executive session minutes 
requested were publicly released prior to the December 9, 2011 request. Therefore, the Board 
should have provided Mr. Heydecker with the released executive session minutes within 10 days 
of his request. 
CONCLUSION 
We find that the Board violated the Open Meeting Law by failing respond to Mr. 
Heydecker's requests for executive session minutes within 10 days, and by failing to provide 
previously released executive session minutes within.10 days of a request. However, we do not 
find that the Board violated the Open Meeting Law by failing to provide Mr. Heydecker with the
