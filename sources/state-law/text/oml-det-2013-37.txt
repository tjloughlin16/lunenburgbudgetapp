Re: Open Meeting Law Complaint 
Dear Mr. Sarkisian: 
This office received a complaint filed by Linda Segal, dated December 26, 2012, alleging 
that the Wayland Town Center Green Design Advisory Committee (the "Committee") violated 
the Open Meeting Law, G.L. c. 30A, §§ 18-25. The complaint alleges that 1) the Committee's 
meeting notices failed to contain a listing of topics that was sufficiently specific to reasonably 
advise members of the public of the anticipated topics for discussion; 2) the Committee failed to 
provide meeting minutes within 10 days of request; and 3) the Committee's August 2, 2012 
meeting minutes are insufficient. The complaint was originally received by the Committee on 
October 11, 2012. On October 31, 2012, this office received your request for an extension of 
time for the Committee to respond to this complaint. Subsequently, this office granted the 
Committee an extension of time until November 30, 2012 to respond to this complaint. 
However, the Committee failed to provide this office or the complainant with a response. 
We reviewed the October 11, 2012 and December 26, 2012 complaints, as well as draft 
minutes of the Committee meetings held on June 21, 2012; June 28, 2012; July 12, 2012; July 
19, 2012; August 2, 2012; August 9, 2012; and August 23, 2013. Following our review, we find 
that the Committee violated the Open Meeting Law by either failing to provide its meeting 
minutes within 10 days of a request, or by failing to create minutes in a timely manner. We also 
find that the Committee violated the Open Meeting Law by failing to respond to this complaint.

We decline to review the allegation that the Committee's meeting notices did not state topics 
with sufficient specificity because it is untimely.1  
FACTS 
On September 14, 2012, the complainant made a written request for all of the 
Committee's meeting minutes. At the time of the request, the Committee had held seven 
meetings: June 21, 2012; June 28, 2012; July 12, 2012; July 19, 2012; August 2, 2012; August 9, 
2012; and August 23, 2012. On September 28, 2012, the complainant received handwritten draft 
minutes of the Committee's June 21, 2012 meeting, as well as draft minutes of its August 2, 
2012 meeting. 
The remaining five sets of minutes were ultimately posted on the Town's website, and 
the complainant obtained copies after our office notified her that they were online. The majority 
of the minutes do not contain the time or location of the meeting, nor do they contain a complete 
summary of the Committee's discussions of each topic. Additionally, the minutes do not contain 
a list of documents used by the public body during the meetings. None of the Committee's 
minutes have been approved as of the date of this decision. The Committee has not met since 
October 24, 2012. 
DISCUSSION 
The Open Meeting Law requires a public body to "create and maintain accurate minutes 
of all meetings . . . setting forth the date, time and place, the members present or absent, a 
summary of the discussions on each subject, a list of documents and other exhibits used at the 
meeting, the decisions made and the actions taken at each meeting, including the record of all 
votes." G.L. c. 30A, § 22(a). Minutes must contain enough detail so that a member of the public 
who was not present at the meeting can read the minutes and have a clear understanding of what 
occurred. See OML 2011-55. 2  Furthermore, the "minutes of an open session, if they exist and 
whether approved or in draft form, shall be made available upon request by any person within 10 
days." G.L. c. 30A, § 22(c). 
Since the Committee did not respond to the complaint, it is unclear whether its draft 
minutes existed at the time of the complainant's request. If the draft minutes were in existence at 
the time of request, then the Committee violated the Open Meeting Law by failing to provide its 
meeting minutes within 10 days of request. If the draft minutes were not in existence at the time 
of request, then the Committee violated the Open Meeting Law by failing to create its meeting 
minutes in a timely manner. In any event, it is apparent that the Committee failed to comply 
with certain requirements of the Open Meeting Law. 
1  The Open Meeting Law requires that complaints be filed with the public body within 30 days of the alleged 
violation. G.L. c. 30A, § 23(b). If the alleged violation could not reasonably have been discovered at the time it 
occurred, then the complaint must be filed with the public body within 30 days of the date on which it could 
reasonably have been discovered. 940 CMR 29.05(3). The complainant alleges that the notices for Committee 
meetings held on June 21, 2012; June 28, 2012; July 12, 2012; July 19, 2012; August 2, 2012; August 9, 2012; and 
August 23, 2012 were insufficient. These alleged violations could reasonably have been discovered at the time 
those meetings occurred. We therefore decline to investigate whether the notices stated topics with sufficient 
specificity because the complaint was filed with the Committee more than 30 days after these meetings.

The complainant further alleges that the Committee's August 2, 2012 meeting minutes 
"are defective because they fail to list the documents used during the meeting." After reviewing 
the draft minutes of all seven Committee meetings, it appears that none of the minutes are 
sufficient to comply with the Open Meeting Law. Specifically, many of the minutes failed to 
indicate the time and location of the meetings and failed to include an adequate summary of the 
discussion of each topic. Additionally, the minutes do not contain a list of documents used by 
the Committee during the meetings. However, we note that these are only draft minutes and 
have not been approved by the Committee. Therefore, we do not find a violation of the Open 
Meeting Law. See OML 2012-111. However, we expect that the minutes will be amended to 
include the missing information before they are finalized and approved.3  
Finally, we find that the Committee violated the Open Meeting Law by failing to respond 
to this complaint, despite being granted additional time to do so. The Open Meeting Law 
complaint process is designed to encourage resolution of complaints at the public body level. In 
order to achieve this goal, it is vital that, upon receiving an Open Meeting Law complaint, public 
bodies review the complainant's allegations, take remedial action if appropriate, and send a 
timely response to the complainant and to the Attorney General. 940 CMR 29.05(5). 
CONCLUSION 
We fmd that the Committee violated the Open Meeting Law by either failing to provide 
its meeting minutes within 10 days of a request, or by failing to create meeting minutes in a 
timely manner. In addition, we find that the Committee violated the Open Meeting Law by 
failing to respond to this complaint. We order immediate and future compliance with the Open 
Meeting Law, and caution that future similar violations may be considered evidence of intent to 
violate the Law. We also order the Committee to view the video of the Open Meeting Law 
training available on the Attorney General's Open Meeting Law website, and certify, in writing 
to this office, within thirty (30) days of receipt of this determination that all members have done 
so. 
We now consider this matter closed. Please be advised that closing this complaint does 
not resolve any other complaints that are currently pending with this office, the Committee, or 
the Town. If you have any questions, regarding this letter, please do not hesitate to contact us at 
617-963-2540.
