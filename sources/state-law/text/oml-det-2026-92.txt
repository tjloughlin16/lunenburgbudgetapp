 RE: Open Meeting Law Complaint 
 
Dear Attorney Goodman: 
 
This office received a complaint from Richard Bell on February 23, 2026, alleging that 
the Richmond Board of Selectmen (the “Board”) violated the Open Meeting Law, G.L. c. 30A, 
§§ 18-25. The complaint was filed with the Board on January 15, 2026. You responded to the 
complaint, on behalf of the Board, by letter dated February 3, 2026. The complaint alleges that 
the Board i) failed to specify on the notice for its December 17, 2025, meeting that it planned to 
hold a public hearing regarding a special permit; ii) called items out of order during the 
December 17, 2025, meeting; and iii) failed to follow proper procedures for holding a public 
hearing. 
 
Following our review, we find that the Board violated the Open Meeting Law by posting 
a notice with insufficient specificity for the meeting held on December 17, 2025. We find that 
the Board did not violate the Open Meeting Law in the other ways alleged. In reaching this 
determination, we reviewed the Open Meeting Law complaint, the Board’s response to the 
complaint, the request for further review, and the notice, minutes and recording of the December 
17, 2025, meeting.

FACTS 
 
We find the facts as follows. On Tuesday, November 24, 2025, the Board mailed notice 
of a public hearing to abutters of 81 State Road in Richmond advising them that the Board would 
hold a public hearing on December 17, 2025, at 6:00 P.M. Specifically, the notice indicated that 
the Board, acting as Special Permit Granting Authority, “will conduct a public hearing… on the 
petition for authorization to allow for a 11.52 sq ft sign identifying the location of Balderdash 
Cellars.” The same notice of the public hearing appeared in the classified section of the 
Berkshire Eagle on December 3 and December 10, 2025.  
 
Separately, on Monday, December 15, 2025, at 3:30 P.M., the Board posted notice of a 
meeting to be held on Wednesday, December 17, 2025, at 6:00 P.M. The notice listed 10 topics, 
the third topic listed was “Special Permit Balderdash Sign.” The notice did not indicate that the 
Board would hold a public hearing with respect to this special permit. 
 
The December 17, 2025, meeting was held as planned. After calling the meeting to order, 
Board Chair Roger Manzolini moved directly to the topic of Balderdash Cellars’ request for a 
special permit. The Board questioned the owner of Balderdash Cellars regarding whether the 
sign that identifies the business had changed since the time it was erected. Owner Christian 
Hanson advised the Board that the sign had not changed since it was erected in 2017 and he had 
no plans to change the sign. Chair Manzolini then made a motion, which was seconded by Board 
member Neal Pilson, to approve the special permit. The Board unanimously voted to approve the 
special permit.  
 
DISCUSSION 
 
The Open Meeting Law was enacted “to eliminate much of the secrecy surrounding 
deliberation and decisions on which public policy is based.” Ghiglione v. School Committee of 
Southbridge, 376 Mass. 70, 72 (1978). Pursuant to this objective, a public body must post notice 
of every meeting at least 48 hours in advance, not including Saturdays, Sundays, and legal 
holidays. G.L. c. 30A, § 20(b). Notices must include “a listing of topics that the chair reasonably 
anticipates will be discussed at the meeting.” Id. The list of topics must have sufficient 
specificity to reasonably advise the public of the issues to be discussed at the meeting. 940 CMR 
29.03(1)(b). We generally consider a topic to be sufficiently specific when a reasonable member 
of the public could read the topic and understand the anticipated nature of the public body’s 
discussion. See OML 2023-199; OML 2019-134; OML 2015-35.

newspaper relative to Balderdash Cellar’s special permit, it omitted the phrase “public hearing” 
from the notice of the December 17, 2025, meeting. A separate notice posted for a public hearing 
does not negate the Board’s responsibility to file a sufficiently specific notice under the Open 
Meeting Law. See OML 2016-60 (holding that the Open Meeting Law’s notice posting 
requirements are separate from any notice requirements set forth in any public hearing law). This 
omission is significant because a public hearing offers members of the public certain rights that 
they may not otherwise be afforded during a meeting of a public body. See OML 2013-187. 
Accordingly, we find that the Board violated the Open Meeting Law by failing to specify in the 
notice for its December 17, 2025, meeting that it planned to hold a public hearing with respect to 
the special permit. Although we find a violation, we note that the Board offered the public an 
opportunity to comment on the special permit request during the meeting. 
 
Next, the complaint alleges that the Board took agenda items out of order by discussing 
the Balderdash Cellars’ special permit matter earlier in the meeting than listed on the meeting 
notice. Once a meeting begins, the public body controls its own agenda. Accordingly, the Open 
Meeting Law does not prohibit a public body from removing, postponing, or declining to discuss 
a topic listed on a meeting notice or taking topics in a different order than listed on the notice. 
See OML 2021-201; OML 2014-35. Therefore, we find that the Board did not violate the Open 
Meeting Law when it discussed items in a different order than that listed in the December 17, 
2025, meeting notice. 
 
Finally, the complaint alleges that the Board failed to comply with G.L. c. 40A, § 9, 
which requires the Board to hold a public hearing on a special permit application. Numerous 
laws in the Commonwealth, such as G.L. c. 40A, require that a public hearing be held before the 
government takes certain action. The specific procedures for notice and conduct of the public 
hearing are governed by those laws, not the Open Meeting Law. See OML Declination 11-12-13 
(Ashfield Board of Health); OML Declination 1-29-15 (Holbrook Planning Board). Because the 
Division of Open Government’s review only concerns compliance with the Open Meeting Law, 
we therefore decline to review, and offer no opinion on whether the Board violated G.L. c. 40A, 
§ 9 with respect to its discussion of the special permit involving Balderdash Cellars. See OML 
Declination 11-12-13 (Ashfield Board of Health); OML Declination 1-29-15 (Holbrook Planning 
Board). 
 
CONCLUSION 
 
For the reasons stated above, we find that the Board violated the Open Meeting Law by 
failing to include in the notice that it would hold a public hearing on a specific topic during the 
December 17, 2025, meeting. We order the Board’s immediate and future compliance with the 
Open Meeting Law, and we caution that similar future violations may be considered evidence of 
intent to violate the law.  
 
We now consider the complaint addressed by this determination to be resolved. This 
determination does not address any other complaints that may be pending with the Board or with 
our office. Please feel free to contact our office at (617) 963-2540 if you have any questions 
regarding this letter.
