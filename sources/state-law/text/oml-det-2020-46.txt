Lamanna Assistant City Solicitor Lynn Law Department 3 City Hall Square, Room 406 Lynn, MA 01901  By e-mail only: jlamanna@lynnma.gov   RE: Open Meeting Law Complaint  Dear Attorney Lamanna:  This office received a complaint from Joanne Lindner on March 6, 2020, alleging that the Lynn City Council (the “Council”) violated the Open Meeting Law, G.L. c. 30A, §§ 18-25.  The complaint was originally filed with the Council on February 3, 2020.  The Council has not responded to the complaint as of the date of this letter.  The complaint alleges that the Committee failed to properly respond to a prior Open Meeting Law complaint, dated December 5, 2019, within 14 business day.   We resolve this matter by informal action, in accordance with 940 CMR 29.07(2)(a), after reviewing the original complaint and the complaint filed with our office requesting further review.    The complainant filed an Open Meeting Law complaint with the Council on December 5, 2019, alleging that notice for a specific December 2019 meeting was not posted to the City’s website and that the Council did not allow public participation during that meeting.  The Council responded to the December complaint on January 27, 2020 – 34 business days after the complaint was filed.  A public body must respond to an Open Meeting Law complaint within 14 business days, unless it secures an extension from the Director of the Division of Open Government.  G.L. c. 30A, § 23(b); 940 CMR 29.05(5).  The Council did not seek an extension of time and therefore violated the Open Meeting Law by failing to respond to an Open Meeting Law complaint within 14 business days.

 We note that the Council also failed to respond to this complaint within 14 business days.  
At the time the complaint was filed with this office on March 6, 2020, 23 business days had 
passed since the complaint was first filed with the Council, without a response from the Council.  
We remind the Council of its obligations under the law to meet to review the complaint’s 
allegations; take remedial action, if appropriate; send to the complainant a response and a 
description of any remedial action taken; and send to the Attorney General a copy of the 
complaint and a description of any remedial action taken, and note our concern that the Council 
has now twice failed to respond to a complaint within the timeframe required by the Open 
Meeting Law.  See G.L. c. 30A, § 23(b); 940 CMR 29.05(5) . 
    
For the reasons stated above, we find that the Council violated the Open Meeting Law by 
failing to respond to an Open Meeting Law complaint within 14 business days.  We order 
immediate and future compliance with the law’s requirements, and we caution that similar future 
violations could be considered evidence of intent to violate the law.  Future intentional violations 
for failure to timely respond to an Open Meeting Law complaint may result in the 
recommendation of a civil penalty. 
  
We now consider the complaint addressed by this determination to be resolved.  This 
determination does not address any other complaints that may be pending with our office or the 
Council.  Please feel free to contact our office at (617) 963-2540 if you have any questions 
regarding this letter.
