FACTS 
In April 2012, the complainants requested copies of all of the Board's executive session 
minutes dating back to October 2010. The Board did not respond to the request at that time, and 
the complainants renewed their request repeatedly over the course of the next year. On April 25, 
2013, in response to this Open Meeting Law complaint, the Board finally provided the minutes 
from five of the executive sessions. In an accompanying letter. Board Secretary Roger Jarrett 
writes that the Board "has audited the past executive session minutes prior to 2012 and has found 
that five sessions in between 2010 and 2011 do not have recorded minutes. After an exhaustive 
search we are declaring these minutes are lost." Although the letter states that five sessions are 
missing minutes, the attachment to the letter identifies eight meeting dates—February 25, 2010; 
March 18, 2010; November 15, 2010; March 24, 2011; April 28, 2011; May 16, 2011; July 13, 
2011; and September 21, 2011—for which the Board has not created minutes. Finally, Mr. 
Jarrett writes that the Board "has taken actions to prevent future occurrences of this issue. These 
include the adoption of a yearly calendar with twice yearly review of executive session minutes 
for release and a policy proposal number A-102 for the handling of executive session minutes. 
This policy will be approved and adopted at the June 2013 Annual AMSA Board of Trustees 
meeting." According to the Board's June 20, 2013 letter to our office, the policy was adopted on 
June 13, 2013. 
DISCUSSION 
Public bodies must create and maintain minutes of all meetings. G.L. c. 30A, § 22(a). 
Executive session minutes may be withheld from disclosure to the public "as long as publication 
may defeat the lawful purposes of the executive session, but no longer." G.L. c. 30A, § 22(f). 
When the purpose for a valid executive session has been served, the minutes and any documents 
or exhibits used at the session must be disclosed, unless the attorney-client privilege or an 
exemption to the public records law applies. Id. Public bodies have an obligation to review the 
minutes of executive sessions at reasonable intervals to determine if continued non-disclosure of 
minutes is warranted, and to announce that determination at the next meeting following its 
review. G.L. c. 30A, § 22(g)(1). The burden of justifying continued nondisclosure of executive 
session minutes lies with the public body. Foudy v. Amherst-Pelham Regional School 
Committee. 402 Mass. 179, 184 (1988). A public body must respond to a request for executive 
session meeting minutes within 10 days. G.L. c. 30A, § 22(g)(2). Upon receiving the request, 
the public body must review the executive session minutes to determine whether the purpose for 
the executive session continues to require non-disclosure. Id. This review must occur at the 
public body's next regular meeting, or within 30 days, whichever comes first. Id 
By its own admission, the Board failed to create or maintain minutes of eight meetings 
held between October 2010 and April 2012. Furthermore, the Board clearly failed to respond to 
the complainants' request for minutes within 10 days and failed to conduct a timely review of its

CONCLUSION 
We find that the Board violated the Open Meeting Law by failing to timely respond to a 
request for executive sessions minutes and by failing to keep minutes of certain meetings. We 
order the Board's immediate and future compliance with the law, and caution the Board that a 
future similar violation may be considered evidence of an intentional violation of the law. 
Additionally, we order the Board to create, to the best of its ability, minutes for the eight 
identified executive sessions for which the Board has either lost or failed to create minutes. 
Upon creation of the minutes, the Board shall review those minutes to determine whether the 
applicable executive session purposes continue to warrant non-disclosure of all or part of those 
minutes. If the executive session purpose has expired, the Board shall release the minutes unless 
exempted from public disclosure by the Public Records Law or the attorney/client privilege. 
This must occur within sixty (60) days of receipt of this letter, and the Board shall certify to this 
office on the form attached that it has complied with the order. 
We now consider the complaint addressed by this determination to be resolved. This 
determination does not address any other complaints which may be pending with our office or
