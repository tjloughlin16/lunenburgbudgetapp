RE: Open Meeting Law Complaint 
Dear Ms. Reed, 
This office has reviewed a complaint filed by Mr. Richard Roy and dated October 21, 
2010, alleging violations of the Open Meeting Law, G.L. c. 30A, §§ 18-25, by the Westfield City 
Council (the "Council"). Specifically, the complaint alleges that the Council violated the Open 
Meeting Law on September 16, 2010 when, after entertaining a motion to enter executive 
session, the Council President allowed an Assistant City Solicitor to address the Council in open 
session, even though the topic had not been listed in the meeting notice. The complaint was 
originally filed with the Council on September 30, 2010. The City's Law Department responded 
to the complaint by letter dated October 1, 2010. 
We appreciate the patience of the parties as we reviewed this matter. Following our 
review, we find that the Council did not violate the Open Meeting Law. In reaching this 
determination, we reviewed the September 30, 2010 complaint; the Law Department's October 
1, 2010 response to that complaint; and the October 21, 2010 complaint filed with our office. 
We also reviewed the minutes and a video recording, provided by the complainant, of the 
Council's September 16, 2010 meeting. 
FACTS 
During the Council's September 16, 2010 meeting, the Council entertained a motion to 
enter into executive session to discuss litigation matters. Before entering executive session, the 
Council President allowed Assistant City Solicitor Susan Phillips to address the Council. The 
minutes reflect that Assistant City Solicitor Phillips "informed the Council that Section 108 loans 
are a public topic and cannot be discussed in Executive Session, therefore, prior to entering into 
===PAGE 2===
Executive Session, she discussed the Section 108 loans publicly." The minutes include a 
summary of Assistant City Solicitor Phillips' presentation on the Section 108 loans. This topic 
was not listed in the Council's notice for its September 16, 2010 meeting. 
According to the Law Department's October 1, 2010 letter, during Assistant City 
Solicitor Phillips' preparation for the Council's anticipated executive session, she discovered that 
the topic of Section 108 loans, originally scheduled for discussion during the executive session, 
could not be discussed in executive session. The Council President was not aware that Assistant 
City Solicitor Phillips would raise the issue during the open session meeting. 
DISCUSSION 
The Open Meeting Law requires that a public body "post notice of every meeting at least 
48 hours prior to such meeting, excluding Saturdays, Sundays and legal holidays," and include in 
that notice "the date, time and place of such meeting and a listing of topics that the chair 
reasonably anticipates will be discussed at the meeting." G.L. c. 30A, § 20(b). A public body 
may consider a topic at a meeting that was not listed in the meeting notice if it is a topic that the 
chair did not reasonably anticipate at least 48 hours before a meeting. See OML 2011-36; OML 
2011-23; OML 2011-13.1  However, public bodies are encouraged not to discuss or vote on 
items that arise within that 48 hour period or during a meeting if the topic can be postponed until 
a later date and added to a properly posted meeting notice. See OML 2011-13. Finally, we note 
that during a meeting, the chair of a public body has discretion over who may address the public 
body. See G.L. c. 30A, § 20(f). The law states that, 1143 person shall address a meeting of a 
public body without permission of the chair, and all persons shall, at the request of the chair, be 
silent." Id. 
Here, the Council President, as the chair of the public body, used his discretion to 
permit Assistant City Solicitor Phillips to address the Council during its September 16, 2010 
open session meeting, prior to the Council entering executive session. Because the Council 
President did not reasonably anticipate that Assistant City Solicitor Phillips would raise the topic 
she discussed in open session, the Council was not required to list the topic in the meeting 
notice.2  See G.L. c. 30A, § 20(b); OML 2011-36; OML 2011-23; OML 2011-13. We therefore 
fmd that the Council did not violate the Open Meeting Law by permitting this discussion to 
occur during its September 16, 2010 open meeting. 
As a final matter, we note that it appears the Open Meeting Law complaint was not 
distributed to members of the Council prior to the Law Department's response. We remind the 
Council that, although a public body may authorize an individual or department of municipal 
government to respond on the public body's behalf, the Open Meeting Law and the Attorney 
General's regulations require that the public body first review the Open Meeting Law complaint. 
See G.L. c. 30A, § 23(b);940 CMR 29.05(3); OML 2011-6. The public body may meet in

CONCLUSION 
We find that the Council did not violate the Open Meeting Law when the Council 
President allowed Assistant City Solicitor Phillips to address the Council during its September 
16, 2010 meeting. We now consider this matter closed.
