Our office has reviewed your complaint dated December 2, 2010 alleging a violation of 
the Open Meeting Law, G.L. c. 30A, §§ 18-25 by Becket Board of Selectmen (the "Board'). 
Specifically, your complaint states that a Board member moved "a [p]osted [w]arrant meeting to 
a different location," and that a "[s]electman acted alone in representing the town." The 
complaint was originally filed with the Board on October 9, 2010. The Board responded to the 
complaint on October 20, 2010. We find that the Board did not violate the Open Meeting Law. 
In making this detell 	 iination, we reviewed the two complaints, the Board's response, and 
the enabling legislation of the Sherwood Forest Lake District, Chapter 107 of the Acts of 2010. 
We also reviewed the minutes from the Board's October 13, 2010 and October 20, 2010 
meetings. 
The Open Meeting Law requires that public bodies provide notice of their meetings and 
that they hold meetings in open session. See G.L. c. 30A, § 20. A "public body" is defined, in 
part, as, "a multiple-member board, commission, committee or subcommittee within the 
executive or legislative branch or within any county, district, city, region or town, however 
created, elected, appointed or otherwise constituted, established to serve a public purpose." G. L. 
c. 30A, §18. 
Your complaint alleges two violations. First, that a warrant meeting, presumably the 
October 9, 2010 Sherwood Forest Lake District initial meeting, was moved without proper 
notification. Second, your complaint alleged that a selectman acted alone in representing the 
town during the meeting, where a quorum of the Board is required to act on behalf of the town. 
Neither of these alleged acts, if true, constitutes a violation of the Open Meeting Law. 
A warrant meeting of the Sherwood Forest Lake District is not governed by the Open

Meeting Law, but rather by the enabling statute, Chapter 107 of the Acts of 2010. Sections 4 and 
7 of the statute establish notice procedures for the initial meeting of the District. Section 4 
establishes rules for calling a meeting, and states that lait the initial meeting of the district, a 
selectman shall preside and shall call the meeting to order." The District is not a public body 
subject to the G.L. c. 30A, §§ 18-25. Because this meeting is governed by Chapter 107 of the 
Acts of 2010, and was not convened by a public body subject to the Open Meeting Law, G.L. c. 
30A, §§18-25, we are without authority to investigate and resolve your complaint with respect to 
the meeting and notice for the meeting. 
The Becket Board of Selectmen is a public body subject to the Open Meeting Law. At 
the warrant meeting, a single Board member acted to convene the warrant meeting, pursuant to 
Chapter 107 of the Acts of 2010. There is no allegation that a quorum of the Board acted in any 
'Timmer which would violate the Open Meeting Law. Although you state that a single Selectman 
does not have authority to represent the town, it appears in this case that a single member of the 
Board may represent the town in the narrow circumstance of calling the initial meeting of the 
Lake District, according to Chapter 107 of the Acts of 2010. 
We find that there were no violations of the Open Meeting Law by the Board of 
Selectmen. We provide no determination as to whether or not proper procedures were followed 
in conducting the initial meeting of the Sherwood Forest Lake District. 
We now consider this matter complaint resolved and this matter closed with our office.
