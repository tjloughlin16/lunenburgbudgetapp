This office received a complaint from Joaquin Soares on August 8, alleging that the 
Stoughton School Committee (the "Committee") violated the Open Meeting Law, G.L. c. 
3OA, §§ 18-25.1 The complaint was originally filed with the Committee on or about June 29, 
and you responded, on behalf of the Committee, to the complaint by letter dated August 5.2 
In his complaint, Mr. Soares alleges that the notice for the Committee's June 21 meeting 
failed to indicate that the Committee anticipated voting on a particular topic. 
Following our review, we find that the Committee did not violate the Open Meeting 
Law. In reaching this determination, we reviewed the original complaint, the Committee's 
response to the complaint, and the complaint filed with our office requesting further review. 
We also reviewed the notice and minutes from the Committee's June 21 meeting. 
We find the facts as follows. The Committee timely posted notice of a meeting 
scheduled for June 21. One of the topics listed was, "Superintendent Goal Setting 
Discussion." During the meeting, the School Superintendent presented her goals for the

DISCUSSION 
A public body must post notice of every meeting at least 48 hours in advance, not 
including Saturdays, Sundays, and legal holidays. G.L. c. 30A, § 20(b). Notices must include 
"a listing of topics that the chair reasonably anticipates will be discussed at the meeting." Id. 
The list of topics shall have sufficient specificity to reasonably advise the public of the issues 
to be discussed at the meeting. 940 CMR 29.03(l)(b). 
The complainant alleges that the Committee should not have voted to approve the 
Superintendent's goals during the June 21 meeting because the notice did not indicate that the 
Committee anticipated such a vote. We have previously found that it is reasonably 
foreseeable that a public body may hold a vote following discussion of a topic listed on a 
meeting notice, even where the notice topic does not indicate the possibility of a vote. See 
OML 2015-66; OML 2012-75.4 In this instance, while the notice did not specify that the 
Committee may take a vote, the vote that occurred flowed naturally from the Committee's 
consideration of a posted agenda topic. See OML 2016-150; 2014-102. Accordingly, we find 
that the posted notice was sufficient to inform the public of the discussion that occurred, and 
the Committee did not violate the Open Meeting Law. See id.; OML 2014-132; OML 2013-
145. 
CONCLUSION 
For the reasons stated above, we find that the Committee did not violate the Open 
Meeting Law. We now consider the complaint addressed by this determination to be 
resolved. This determination does not address any other complaints that may be pending with 
our office or the Committee. Please feel free to contact our office at (617) 963-2540 if you 
have any questions regarding this letter.
