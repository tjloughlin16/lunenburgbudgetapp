 RE:  Open Meeting Law Complaint 
 
Dear Attorney DeFrancisco: 
 
This office received a complaint from Celio Dominatto on November 8, 2022, alleging 
that the Appellate Tax Board (the “Board”) violated the Open Meeting Law, G.L. c. 30A,  §§ 18-
25.  The complaint was originally filed with the Board on or about September 21, 2022, and you 
responded, on behalf of the Board, by letter dated September 26, 2022.  The complaint alleges 
that the Board restricted Mr. Dominatto’s right to record the September 21, 2022, meeting.  
 
We appreciate the patience of the parties while we reviewed this matter.  Following our 
review, we find that the Board did not violate the Open Meeting Law in the way alleged.  In 
reaching this determination, we reviewed the original complaint, the Board’s response to the 
complaint, the complaint filed with our office requesting further review, and the Board’s 
supplemental response dated November 16, 2023.  In addition, we communicated with the 
Board’s Chief Counsel, David Cella, on December 13, 2023.   
 
 On September 21, 2022, Board Chair Mark Defrancisco held a hearing on Mr. 
Dominatto’s appeals of the overvaluation by the Town of Barnstable of real property located at 
134 Pine Street for fiscal years 2020 and 2021.  At the beginning of the hearing, the complainant 
made an oral request to record the proceedings, which was denied. 
 
The Open Meeting Law requires that all meetings of a public body be conducted in an 
open session, with some exceptions.  G.L. c. 30A, §§ 20(a), 21(a).  A “meeting” is defined, in

relevant part, as a “deliberation by a public body with respect to any matter within the body’s 
jurisdiction.”  G.L. c. 30A, § 18.  A “deliberation” is defined as “an oral or written 
communication through any medium, including electronic mail, between or among a quorum of 
a public body on any public business within its jurisdiction.”  Id.  Generally, for the purposes of 
the Open Meeting Law, a “quorum” is a simple majority of the members of the public body.  Id.  
 
The complaint alleges that the Board restricted Mr. Dominatto’s right to record the 
September 21, 2022, meeting.  The Open Meeting Law states that “[a]fter notifying the chair of 
the public body, any person may make a video or audio recording of an open session of a 
meeting of a public body, or may transmit the meeting through any medium, subject to 
reasonable requirements of the chair as to the number, placement and operation of equipment 
used so as not to interfere with the conduct of the meeting.”  G.L. c. 30A, § 20(f) (emphasis 
added).  Although Mr. Dominatto was not allowed to record the hearing held on September 21, 
2022, we do not find that the Board violated the Open Meeting Law because this hearing was not 
a meeting subject to the Open Meeting Law.  Here, only one member of the Board, Chair 
Defrancisco, was present at the September 21, 2022, hearing.  See G.L. c. 58A, § 1A; 831 CMR 
1.20 (a single member of the Board may decide appeals from a decision of a board of assessors 
in which the assessed value of the property does not exceed $500,000, where the assessed value 
of the property does not exceed $750,000 and the parties consent to a decision by a single 
member, or the appeal was filed under the informal or small-claims procedure).  As such, where 
a quorum of Board members was not present at the September 21, 2022, hearing, we find that the 
Board did not hold a “meeting” for purposes of the Open Meeting Law.  See OML 2016-99; 
OML 2013-106.1  Where no meeting occurred, the provision of the Open Meeting Law allowing 
any person to record a meeting of a public body was inapplicable, and the Board could not have 
violated the Open Meeting Law. 
 
Because we find that the September 21, 2022, hearing did not involve a quorum of Board 
members and thus was not a meeting subject to requirements of the Open Meeting Law, we do 
not address the Board’s argument that its meetings generally fall within the exception to the 
Law’s definition of meeting for quasi-judicial public bodies that hold adjudicatory proceedings.  
See G.L. c. 30A, § 18 (meeting does not include a meeting of a quasi-judicial board or 
commission held for the sole purpose of making a decision required in an adjudicatory 
proceeding brought before it).
