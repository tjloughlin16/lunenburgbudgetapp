RE: Open Meeting Law Complaint
Dear Assistant Superintendent Luff:
This office received a complaint from Ronald Alexander, dated July 22, alleging that the 
Natick School Committee (the “Committee”) violated the Open Meeting Law, G.L. c. 30A, §§ 
18-25.1 The complaint was originally filed with the Committee on June 3; and you responded, 
on behalf of the Committee, by email dated June 5.2 The complaint alleges that the Committee 
“held a meeting on May 20, 2019 without posting 48 hours prior notice.”
We resolve this complaint by informal action in accordance with 940 CMR 29.07(2)(a), 
after reviewing the original complaint, the Committee’s response to the complaint, the complaint 
filed with our office requesting further review, and the original and amended notices, as well as 
open session minutes of the Committee meeting held on May 20. We find that the Committee 
did not violate the Open Meeting Law as alleged.
A public body must post notice of every meeting at least 48 hours in advance, not 
including Saturdays, Sundays, and legal holidays. G.L. c. 30A, § 20(b). The notice must be 
printed in a legible, easily understandable format and must contain the date, time and place of the 
meeting, as well as a listing of topics that the chair reasonably anticipates will be discussed. Id. 
Public bodies must list topics for discussion with “sufficient specificity to reasonably advise the 
public of the issues to be discussed at the meeting.” 940 CMR 29.03(l)(b).

On May 15 at 4:10 P.M., the Committee posted notice for a meeting to be held on May 
20 at 6:00 P.M. The notice listed eight main topics, with several subtopics listed under these 
main headings. One of the topics was an executive session to discuss strategy with respect to 
collective bargaining and in preparation for negotiations with non-union personnel. A public 
body may convene in executive session to “conduct strategy sessions in preparation for 
negotiations with nonunion personnel or to conduct collective bargaining sessions or contract 
negotiations with nonunion personnel” (“Purpose 2”); and “to discuss strategy with respect to 
collective bargaining ... if an open meeting may have a detrimental effect on the bargaining . . . 
position of the public body and the chair so declares” (“Purpose 3”). G.L. c. 30A, §§ 21(a)(2) & 
(3). When convening in executive session pursuant to Purpose 2 or Purposed, a public body 
must identify the collective bargaining unit or nonunion personnel with whom it will be 
negotiating, if doing so will not compromise the lawful purpose for secrecy. See OML 2017-53; 
OML 2016-43; OML 2015-87; OML 2013-129.3 Here, the original notice listed the collective 
bargaining units but indicated that a list of non-union personnel could be found under a separate 
link. On May 16 at 6:59 P.M., the Committee revised its notice to add the specific non-union 
personnel with whom it would be negotiating during the May 20 executive session. On May 17 
at 10:18 A.M., the Committee revised its notice again to further clarify the non-union personnel 
with whom it would be negotiating. Because the Committee posted notice of the May 20 
meeting at least 48 hours prior on May 15, we find that the Committee did not violate the Open 
Meeting Law.4
In his request for further review, the complainant suggests that the revised notices for the 
Committee’s May 20 meeting failed to include the date and time that the original notice had been 
posted. We generally decline to review an allegation that was not included within the original 
complaint to the Committee because the Committee has not had an opportunity to respond to it. 
See G.L. c. 30A, § 23(b); 940 CMR 29.05(3). We find that this allegation was not clearly raised 
in the original complaint and thus we do not review its merits. However, we note that the Open 
Meeting Law requires that “the date and time that the notice is posted shall be conspicuously 
recorded thereon or therewith.” 940 CMR 29.03(1 )(d); See OML 2011-45. Notices that are 
revised within the 48 hours before the meeting must conspicuously record both the date and time 
that it was posted and the date and time that the notice was originally posted. See 940 CMR 
29.03(l)(d); OML 2017-183; OML 2013-44. We remind the Committee of its obligations under 
the law.
For the reasons stated above, we find that the Committee did not violate the Open 
Meeting Law. We now consider the complaint addressed by this determination to be resolved. 
This determination does not address any other complaints that may be pending with our office or 
the Committee. Please feel free to contact our office at (617) 963-2540 if you have any 
questions regarding this letter.
