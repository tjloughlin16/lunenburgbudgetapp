 RE: Open Meeting Law Complaints 
 
Dear Mr. Lapp:  
 
On September 19, 2024, this office received a complaint from Mary Parsons, alleging 
that the Rockland Select Board (the “Board”) violated the Open Meeting Law, G.L. c. 30A, §§ 
18-25 at its meeting on August 13.
1  The complaint was originally filed with the Board on 
August 19.  You responded on behalf of the Board by letter dated September 6.  The complainant 
alleges that the Board (1) deliberated prior to commencement of a meeting; and (2) entered 
executive session without stating the purpose for same. 
 
Following review, we resolve this complaint by informal action, in accordance with 940 
CMR 29.07(2)(a).2  The Board acknowledges, and we find, that the Board violated the Open 
Meeting Law when it entered executive session on August 13 without stating the purpose of 
same.3  Without denying responsibility for the violation, the Board has attributed the omission to 
inexperience (the August 13 meeting was Tiffanie Needham’s first meeting as chair) and 
disruption by a member of the public, which distracted the chair.  These circumstances do not

The complainant also alleges that the Board violated the Open Meeting Law when four 
members (a quorum) were sitting together behind closed doors prior to commencement of the 
August 13 meeting.  Ms. Parsons could not hear the content of any conversation.  However, 
because the door was closed, she speculates that the Board deliberated outside of a public 
meeting.  The Board denies that there was any deliberation prior to the commencement of the 
meeting.  As we have previously noted, absent sufficient evidence to the contrary, we credit a 
public body’s account of the facts.  See OML 2023-121; OML 2017-93; OML 2016-141;4 see 
also LaPointe v. License Board of Worcester, 389 Mass. 454 (1983) (“There is every 
presumption in favor of the honesty and sufficiency of the motives actuating public officers in 
actions ostensibly taken for the general welfare.”).  As there is no evidence that members of the 
Board deliberated outside of a meeting, we find no violation on this point.  Nonetheless, we 
remind the Board that discussions of a public body’s operation, including how to run its meetings 
and other organizational matters, may be deliberation that must be reserved for a public meeting.  
See OML 2024-189; OML 2022-161; OML 2016-81.  Furthermore, prohibiting members of the 
public from being present before or after a meeting, when members of the public body are 
already gathered or remain assembled, creates at least an appearance of improper deliberation 
outside of a public meeting and should be avoided.  See OML 2019-165. 
 
We order the Board’s immediate and future compliance with the Open Meeting Law.  We 
now consider the complaint addressed by this determination to be resolved.  This determination 
does not address any other complaints that may be pending with our office or the Board.  Please 
feel free to contact the Division at (617) 963 - 2540 if you have any questions.
