RE: Open Meeting Law Complaint 
Dear Mr. Newlands: 
This office received an Open Meeting Law complaint from Demetri A. Kasperson, dated 
October 23, 2012, alleging that the Southbridge Liquor Licensing Board (the "Board") violated 
the Open Meeting Law, G.L. c. 30A, §§ 18-25. The complaint specifically alleges that the notice 
for the Board's July 26, 2012 meeting failed to list with sufficient specificity the topics the Chair 
anticipated would be discussed. The complaint was filed with the Board on August 23, 2012. 
The Board responded by letter dated September 10, 2012. 
Following our review, we find that the Board did not violate the Open Meeting Law 
because its July 26, 2012 meeting notice listed with sufficient specificity the topics the Chair 
reasonably anticipated discussing. In reaching this determination, we reviewed the August 23, 
2012 complaint filed with the Board; the Board's September 10, 2012 response; and the October 
23, 2012 complaint filed with our office. We also reviewed the notice and minutes of the 
Board's January 26, 2012 and July 26, 2012 meetings. 
On January 12, 2012, the Board sent Mr. Kasperson a letter requesting his presence at a 
January 26, 2012 Board meeting. The letter stated that the issue for discussion would be "the 
non-use of the liquor license held by Sevengaits LLC at 150 Central Street." The letter 
continued that "[b]y not being in compliance with statutes, rules and regulations set forth by the 
state and Alcoholic Beverage Control Commission, your license may be in jeopardy."

During the Board's January 26, 2012 meeting, the Board met with Mr. Kasperson

the Board. In that memorandum, the Town Manager stated that four liquor licenses "continue to 
not be in active use even though numerous and repeated attempts have been made with the 
owners," including Mr. Kasperson. During discussion of topic 9, the Board discussed the status 
of Mr. Kasperson's business and its anticipated date for opening. After significant discussion, 
the Board voted unanimously to "set October 18, 2012 as date for public hearing for Sevengaits 
and also for Maqui, Inc." Following that meeting, the Board scheduled a hearing for October 18, 
2012. 
DISCUSSION 
The Open Meeting Law, G.L. c. 30A, § 20(b), states in relevant part that, "[ejxcept in an 
emergency, in addition to any notice otherwise required by law, a public body shall post notice 
of every meeting at least 48 hours prior to such meeting, excluding Saturdays, Sundays and legal 
holidays . . . Notice shall be printed in a legible, easily understandable format and shall contain 
the date, time and place of such meeting and a listing of topics that the chair reasonably 
anticipates will be discussed at the meeting." Public bodies must list reasonably anticipated 
topics in a meeting notice with "sufficient specificity to reasonably advise the public of the 
issues to be discussed at the meeting." G.L. c. BOA, § 20(b); 940 CMR 29.03(1 )(b). We 
generally consider a topic to include sufficient specificity when a reasonable member of the 
public could read the topic and understand the anticipated nature of the public body's discussion. 
See OML 2013-55; OML 2011-7.1 
In his complaint, Mr. Kasperson alleges that the Board's notice for its July 26, 2012 
meeting was not sufficiently specific to advise the public of the issues it planned to discuss. 
Specifically, Mr. Kasperson protests that the Town Manager "introduced a new and undisclosed 
agenda item, supported by a seventeen page memorandum with the subject: 'Cancellation 
Request of 4 Liquor Licenses'... that did not appear on the notice." The notice for the July 26, 
2012 meeting did not list the Town Manager's memorandum as a separate topic for discussion, 
however we find that the Town Manager's recommendations were related to a properly noticed 
item on the agenda. The notice for the July 26, 2012 meeting listed "9. Report: Demetri 
Kasperson-Sevengaits LLC." A reasonable member of the public could read this topic and 
understand that the Board would hear a report on this specific license, which could include 
discussion about the license status. Where a body anticipates taking a vote on a particular item, 
it should note as much in the notice, however we note that, here the Board did not make a 
decision on the license status. Instead, it voted to hold a future hearing on the matter. We
