 RE:        Open Meeting Law Complaint 
 
Dear Attorney Howayeck: 
 
This office received a complaint from Erin Leary on October 24, 2025, alleging that the 
Fall River Zoning Board of Appeals (the “Board”) violated the Open Meeting Law, G.L. c. 30A, 
§§ 18-25.  The complaint was originally filed with the Board on July 22, 2025, and you 
responded to the complaint, on behalf of the Board, by letter dated August 22, 2025, following an 
extension of time granted by this office.  The complaint alleges that the Board held a meeting on 
July 17, 2025, that was not accessible to the public where the meeting room could not 
accommodate all members of the public in attendance and that certain members of the public 
were unable to hear the discussions that occurred. 
  
Following our review, we find that the Board did not violate the Open Meeting Law in 
the way alleged.  In reaching this determination, we reviewed the original complaint, the 
complaint filed with our office requesting further review, and the Board’s response to the 
complaint.  In addition, we reviewed the notice and video recording of the Board meeting held 
on July 17, 2025, as well as screenshots of the July 17, 2025, meeting provided to this office by 
you and the complainant.

The Open Meeting Law was enacted “to eliminate much of the secrecy surrounding 
deliberations and decisions on which public policy is based.”  Ghiglione v. School Committee of 
Southbridge, 376 Mass. 70, 72 (1978).  The law requires that meetings of a public body be 
properly noticed and open to members of the public, unless an executive session is convened.  
See G.L. c. 30A, §§ 20(a)-(b), 21.  Access to a meeting must include the opportunity to be 
physically present, as well as to see and hear what is being discussed by the members of the 
public body.  See OML 2023-99; OML 2017-135; OML 2015-38; OML 2014-3; OML 2013-
189.2  In order for a meeting to be truly “open to the public,” the meeting venue must be 
accessible to the public for the duration of the meeting.  See G.L. c. 30A, § 20(a); OML 2017-83. 
 
Here, the Board met on July 17, 2025, in the first floor Hearing Room at One 
Government Center in Fall River.  During the meeting, the Board held a public hearing on a 
project located on Rock Street which sought a variance and special permits to convert a former 
convent into 12 residential units.  At the time of this hearing, a video recording of the meeting 
shows a crowded room with many people standing in the back of the room and a few people 
standing in the doorway of the room.  Although a number of people were standing, there were at 
least half a dozen open seats in the meeting room.   
 
For approximately 50 minutes, the Board heard from the owner of the property, as well as 
his attorney, and numerous members of the public.  The first member of the public to speak in 
opposition to the project mentioned that she had difficulty hearing the Board’s discussions.  No 
other member of the public expressed their difficulty in hearing the proceedings.  After hearing 
from members of the public, the Board discussed the best use of the property and then 
unanimously voted to approve the requested variance and special permits.  A large number of 
people then left the meeting room, and the Board took a five minute recess.  When the Board 
reconvened, no individuals were standing and there were numerous open seats available in the 
meeting room.   
 
The complaint alleges that the Board meeting held on July 17, 2025, was not accessible 
to the public where the meeting room could not accommodate all members of the public in 
attendance, and that certain members of the public were unable to hear the discussions that 
occurred.  We find that individuals in attendance had sufficient access to the meeting and could 
see and hear the Board and the discussions that occurred.  The video recording and screen shots 
show a crowded room with many attendees standing at the back of the room but does not clearly 
demonstrate that the meeting was inaccessible to members of the public, or that members of the 
public could not see and hear the proceedings.  In fact, although a number of people were 
standing, there were at least half a dozen open seats in the meeting room.  We find that the July 
17, 2025, meeting was accessible, and thus the Board did not violate the Open Meeting Law.  
See OML 2014-11; OML 2014-3.  Although we do not find a violation, we remind the Board of 
its obligation to hold meetings that are accessible to members of the public and encourage the 
Board to make accommodation for larger than usual crowds, such as by moving its meeting to a 
larger location and ensuring there is adequate amplification.  See OML 2019-157; OML 2017-
135.

For the reasons stated above, we find that the Board did not violate the Open Meeting 
Law.  We now consider the complaint addressed by this determination to be resolved.  This 
determination does not address any other complaints that may be pending with our office or the 
Board.  Please feel free to contact our office at (617) 963-2540 if you have any questions
