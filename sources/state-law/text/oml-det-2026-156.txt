 RE: Open Meeting Law Complaint 
 
Dear Attorney Timmins: 
 
This office received a complaint from Lea DeGloria on April 9, 2026, alleging that the 
Quincy City Council (the “Council”) violated the Open Meeting Law, G.L. c. 30A, §§ 18-25.
1  
The complaint was originally filed with the Council on or about March 5, and you responded on 
behalf of the Council by letter dated March 25.  The complaint alleges that the Council violated 
the Open Meeting Law when a quorum engaged in private electronic communication during a 
meeting the Council held on March 2.   
 
Following our review, we find that the Council did not violate the Open Meeting Law in 
the way alleged.  In reaching this determination, we reviewed the Open Meeting Law complaint, 
the Council’s response, and the request for further review of the complaint.  We also reviewed 
the Council’s response to a public records request made by the complainant.   
 
FACTS 
 
We find the facts as follows.  The Council has nine members; thus, five members 
constitute a quorum.  On March 2, the Council held a posted meeting, during which the 
complainant spoke at open forum.  While the complainant was speaking, Councilor Deborah 
Riley took a photograph of her.  After the photograph was taken, the complainant observed five 
members of the Council “huddled over their personal electronic devices.”

DISCUSSION 
 
The Open Meeting Law was enacted “to eliminate much of the secrecy surrounding 
deliberations and decisions on which public policy is based.”  Ghiglione v. School Committee of 
Southbridge, 376 Mass. 70, 72 (1978).  The Open Meeting Law requires that meetings of a 
public body be noticed and open to the public, unless an executive session is convened. G.L. c. 
30A, §§ 20(a)-(b), 21.  Subject to exceptions not material here, “meeting” is defined, in relevant 
part, as “a deliberation by a public body with respect to any matter within the body’s 
jurisdiction.”  G.L. c 30A, § 18.  The law defines “deliberation” as “an oral or written 
communication through any medium, including electronic mail, between or among a quorum of 
a public body on any public business within its jurisdiction.”  Id.  For purposes of the Open 
Meeting Law, a “quorum” is a simple majority of the members of a public body.  Id.  A quorum 
of a public body may not engage in private communication, such as via texting, during an open 
meeting.  Cf. OML 2021-158.
3 
 
During the Council’s March 2 meeting, Councilor Riley took a photograph of the 
complainant while the latter spoke during the public comment period.  After the photograph was 
taken, the complainant observed five Councilors looking at their personal electronic devices.  
She alleges that the five Councilors were communicating privately about topics that were the 
subject of the meeting, and about her public comment. 
 
The five Councilors referenced by the complainant have denied that they communicated 
electronically during the March 2 meeting.  Moreover, in response to a public records request 
seeking copies of all such communications and the photograph taken by Councilor Riley, four 
Councilors stated that they had no responsive records; Councilor Riley produced only the 
requested photograph.  Absent sufficient evidence to the contrary, we credit the Council’s, and 
individual councilors’, account of the facts.  See OML 2026-128; OML 2019-73; OML 2014-
122.4  We find that a quorum of the Council did not communicate electronically during the 
March 2 meeting and therefore the Council did not violate the Open Meeting Law.   
 
To the extent that the complainant alleges in her request for review that the Council did 
not comply with the Open Meeting Law in its review of her complaint, we decline to review that 
allegation.  We have consistently declined to review allegations that are raised in a request for 
further review or other subsequent communications with the Division of Open Government 
where such allegations were not raised in an original Open Meeting Law complaint first filed 
with the public body.  See OML 2026-49; OML 2023-239; OML 2020-53.  We take this 
opportunity, however, to remind the Council that as to Open Meeting Law complaints filed with 
local public bodies, “the complainant shall file the complaint with the chair of the public body, 
who shall disseminate copies of the complaint to the members of the public body.”  940 CMR 
29.05(3).  Additionally, “[w]ithin 14 business days after receiving the complaint, unless an 
extension has been granted by the Attorney General as provided in 940 CMR 29.05(5)(b), the 
public body shall meet to review the complaint’s allegations; take remedial action, if appropriate; 
and send to the complainant a response and a description of any remedial action taken.”  940

CONCLUSION 
 
For the reasons stated above, we find that the Council did not violate the Open Meeting 
Law during the March 2 meeting as alleged in the complaint.  We now consider the complaint 
addressed by this determination to be resolved.  This determination does not address any other 
complaints that may be pending with the Council or with our office.  Please feel free to contact 
our office at (617) 963-2540 if you have any questions regarding this letter.
