 RE: Open Meeting Law Complaint 
Dear Attorney Harrington: 
This office received two complaints from Thomas Kealy on May 25 and 27, 2021, 
alleging that the Wellesley Council on Aging (the “Council”) violated the Open Meeting Law, 
G.L. c. 30A, §§ 18-25. The complaints were originally filed with the Council on March 22 and 
April 21, and you responded on behalf of the Council by letters dated April 13 and May 24.1 In 
his first complaint, Mr. Kealy alleges that the Council approved minutes of the Council’s 
December 17, 2020, and January 21, 2021, meetings which were insufficiently detailed. In 
response to Mr. Kealy’s first complaint, the Council amended the two sets of meeting minutes to 
include more detailed summaries of the discussions that took place during the meetings. In his 
second complaint, Mr. Kealy alleges that the two sets of amended meeting minutes are still 
insufficiently detailed.  
We resolve this complaint by informal action in accordance with 940 CMR 29.07(2)(a), 
and find that the Council’s amended minutes for the December 17, 2020, and January 21, 2021, 
meetings are sufficiently detailed to comply with the Open Meeting Law. In reaching this 
determination, we reviewed the two complaints, the Council’s response to the complaints, and 
the complainant’s requests for further review. We also reviewed the original and amended 
minutes of the Council’s December 17, 2020, and January 21, 2021, meetings and video 
recordings of the two meetings.   
 The Open Meeting Law requires a public body to “create and maintain accurate minutes 
of all meetings, including executive sessions, setting forth the date, time and place, the members 
present or absent, a summary of the discussions on each subject, a list of documents and other 
exhibits used at the meeting, the decisions made and the actions taken at each meeting, including

the record of all votes.” G.L. c. 30A, § 22(a). When reviewing minutes for compliance with the 
Open Meeting Law, we look for substantial compliance with the accuracy requirement. See 
OML 2013-64; OML 2017-1047.2 By substantial compliance, we mean that the minutes should 
contain enough detail and accuracy so that a member of the public who did not attend the 
meeting could read the minutes and have a clear understanding of what occurred. See OML 
2012-106. Although minutes must include a summary of the discussion on each topic, a 
transcript is not required, and the minutes do not need to include every remark or opinion 
presented. See OML 2012-29; OML 2019-136; 2020-105.  
 Here, we find that the Council substantially complied with the requirement that it provide 
an accurate and sufficiently detailed summary of the discussion that occurred during the 
December 17, 2020, and January 21, 2021, meetings. While we appreciate that Mr. Kealy feels 
that particular statements which were not included in the minutes are important, we find that the 
level of detail Mr. Kealy requests is not required in order to understand the discussion that 
occurred. Furthermore, we commend the Council for amending the two sets of meeting minutes 
in response to Mr. Kealy’s first complaint and soliciting suggestions from him on how the 
minutes should be amended.  
 For the reasons stated above, we find that the Council’s amended minutes for the 
December 17, 2020, and January 21, 2021, meetings are sufficiently detailed, in compliance with 
the Open Meeting Law. We now consider the complaints addressed by this determination to be 
resolved. This determination does not address any other complaints that may be pending with our 
office or the Council. Please feel free to contact the Division at (617) 963 - 2540 if you have any 
questions.
