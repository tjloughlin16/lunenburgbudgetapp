RE: Open Meeting Law Complaint 
Dear Attorney Goldberg: 
This office received a complaint from Patrick Higgins, dated December 3, 2014, alleging 
that the Dedham Board of Selectmen (the "Board") violated the Open Meeting Law, G.L. c. 30A, 
§§ 18-25. The complaint specifically alleges that the notice for the Board's October 30, 2014 
meeting failed to indicate the time the notice was posted, and that the notice failed to include 
sufficient detail about an anticipated executive session discussion. The complaint was filed with 
the Board on November 10, 2014. The Board responded by letter dated December 1, 2014.1 
Following our review, we find that the Board failed to conspicuously record thereon or 
therewith the time and date the notice for its October 30, 2014 meeting was posted. However, 
we note that despite this missing information, it is clear that the notice was posted more than 48 
hours before the Board's October 30, 2014 meeting. The Board did not violate the Open 
Meeting Law by failing to include detail about an anticipated executive session discussion in that 
same notice because no executive session was held during the Board's October 30, 2014 
meeting. In reaching this determination, we reviewed the complaint filed with the Board, the 
Board's response, and the complaint filed with our office. Additionally, we reviewed the notice 
for the Board's October 30, 2014 meeting.

FACTS 
The Board posted notice for an October 30, 2014 meeting on October 24, 2014. The 
notice did not include on its face or with its posting the time and date that it was posted. Among 
the topics listed for discussion in the notice was "Executive Session - (Pending Litigation)." 
During the Board's October 30, 2014 meeting, the Board did not enter into executive session. 
DISCUSSION 
A public body must post notice of every meeting at least 48 hours in advance, not 
including Saturdays, Sundays, and legal holidays. G.L. c. 3OA, § 20(b). The date and time that 
a notice is posted must be conspicuously recorded thereon or therewith. 940 CMR 29.03(l)(b). 
Recording the date and time enables the public to observe that public bodies are complying with 
the Open Meeting Law's notice requirements without requiring constant vigilance. OML 2013-
44. Additionally, it provides this office with evidence of compliance with the Open Meeting 
Law's notice requirements. Id. 
The notice for the Board's October 30, 2014 meeting was posted on October 24, 2014, 
six days prior to the meeting. However, the notice did not include, either on its face or with the 
posting, the time and date that it was posted. While we find that it therefore did not comply with 
the law, it is clear that the notice was posted 48 hours in advance of the meeting. Thus, the harm 
from this violation was minimal. In response to the complaint, the Board writes that the "Town 
Clerk has indicated that he or his staff will mark each meeting notice with the time and date of 
posting, regardless of how significantly in advance of the 48-hour deadline such notice is 
posted." We are satisfied that the Board understands this requirement and is taking steps to 
ensure compliance in the future. 
Meeting notices must also include "a listing of topics that the chair reasonably anticipates 
will be discussed at the meeting." G.L. c. 30A, § 20(b). The listing of topics must have 
sufficient specificity to reasonably advise the public of the issues to be discussed at the meeting. 
940 CMR 29.03(l)(b). When entering into executive session to discuss litigation matters under 
G.L. c. 3OA, § 21(a)(3), public bodies generally must identify the matter that is the subject of an 
executive session. See OML 2014-50; OML 2012-118; OML 2015-14. A public body need not 
identify the litigation matter if publicly disclosing it would compromise the purpose for which 
the executive session was called, such as when the body is considering initiating litigation 
against another party. See G.L. c. 30A, § 21(b)(3), OML 2015-14. 
Here, the notice for the Board's October 30, 2014 meeting included the following topic, 
"Executive Session - (Pending Litigation)." The complaint alleges this was not sufficiently 
specific because the notice failed to include the name of the pending litigation matter. When 
reviewing a meeting notice for sufficiency, unless it is clearly insufficient on its face, we do not 
review a meeting notice standing alone. OML 2014-155. Rather, we review what was actually 
discussed at the meeting to determine if the notice was sufficiently detailed to make the public 
aware of the discussion topics. See id. On its face, the notice for the October 30, 2014 meeting

met the statutory requirements by providing the list of topics the chair reasonably anticipated the 
Board would discuss. Because the Board never entered into executive session during its October 
30, 2014 meeting, and thus there was no discussion requiring more specific notice than what was 
provided, we find that the Board did not violate the Open Meeting Law. 
CONCLUSION 
We find that the Board failed to conspicuously record thereon or therewith the time and 
date the notice for the Board's October 30, 2014 meeting was posted. As the notice was clearly 
posted more than 48 hours before the Board's October 30, 2014 meeting, we find that the harm 
resulting from the violation was minimal, and we note that the Board took appropriate action in 
response to the complaint. We therefore order no additional relief. We find that the Board did 
not violate the Open Meeting Law in the other ways alleged.
