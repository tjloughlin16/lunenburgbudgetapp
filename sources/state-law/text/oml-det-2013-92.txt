RE: Open Meeting Law Complaint 
Dear Mr. Briggs: 
This office received a complaint from Ms. Katina Caraganis, a reporter for the Sentinel & 
Enterprise, dated September 4, 2012, alleging that the Ashby Board of Selectmen (the "Board") 
violated the Open Meeting Law, G.L. c. 30A, §§ 18-25. Specifically, the complaint alleges that 
the Board's emergency meeting on July 20, 2012 did not constitute a lawful emergency. The 
complaint was filed with the Board on July 23, 2012. The Board responded to the complaint by 
letter dated August 7, 2012. 
Following our review, we find that the Board violated the Open Meeting Law by failing 
to post notice at least 48 hours before its July 20, 2012 meeting. In reaching this determination, 
we reviewed the July 23, 2012 complaint; the Board's August 23, 2012 response to that 
complaint; and the September 4, 2012 complaint filed with our office. Additionally, we 
reviewed the notice for the Board's July 18, 2012 meeting and the notice and minutes for the 
Board's July 20, 2012 meeting. 
FACTS 
The Board is a three-member public body. The Board planned to meet at 7:00 PM on 
Wednesday, July 18, 2012. The notice for the meeting, posted on July 16, 2012, listed nine 
topics, but did not include a topic regarding the newly certified budget for the North Middlesex 
Regional School District ("NMRSD"), of which the Town of Ashby is a member. According to 
the August 7, 2012 response to the complaint, the Board planned to discuss setting a special 
Town Meeting and special election to vote on acceptance of the newly certified NMRSD budget 
during the "Any other Business Not Reasonably Anticipated by the Chairman" portion of the 
meeting. The Board was unable to achieve a quorum on July 18, 2012, however, thus the 
meeting was cancelled.

Under G.L. c. 71, § 16B, if towns in a regional school district do not approve the school 
district's budget by a two-thirds vote, the district has 30 days to reconsider, amend, and resubmit 
a certified budget to the towns. Once the district submits a reconsidered budget to the towns, the 
towns have 45 days to act. Since the NMRSD's new budget required a special Town Meeting 
and a special election for a Proposition 2 x/i override, the Board was also required to post notice 
14 days in advance for a special Town Meeting, and provide 35 days written notice to the Town 
Clerk for a ballot vote. By the Town Administrator's calculation, to comply with the various 
deadlines, the Board needed to vote to approve the special Town Meeting and special election for 
the NMRSD budget by Tuesday, July 24, 2012. 
Following cancellation of the July 18, 2012 meeting, the Town Administrator notified the 
Board that it needed to hold a meeting on either Monday, July 23, 2012 or Tuesday, July 24, 
2012 to address the NMRSD budget issue. Because a quorum of the Board's members were not 
available between July 20, 2012 and the July 24, 2012 deadline, the Board decided to hold an 
emergency meeting on July 20, 2012. The Board posted notice on July 19, 2012 at 3:00 PM for 
a meeting on July 20, 2012 at 9:00 AM. This notice included several topics related to the 
NMRSD matter, including "Request for Selectmen to Call a Special Town meeting on August 
14, 2012 and Open the Warrant," "Request for Selectmen to Vote to Place Warrant Articles on 
the Special Town Meeting Warrant," "Request for Selectmen to Vote to Close the Special Town 
Meeting Warrant," "Request for Selectmen to Vote to Call a Special Election on August 28, 
2012 for the NMRSD FY13 Operational Override," and "Request for Selectmen to Sign on 
Special Election Warrant." During the July 20, 2012 meeting, the Board voted on the business 
related to the NMRSD budget, the special Town Meeting, and the special election. 
DISCUSSION 
The Open Meeting Law requires that a public body post a notice at least 48 hours before 
a meeting, not including Saturdays, Sundays, and legal holidays, and include in the notice "a 
listing of topics that the chair reasonably anticipates will be discussed at the meeting." G.L. c. 
30A, § 20(b). In an emergency, a public body shall post notice as soon as reasonably possible 
prior to such meeting. Id. An "emergency" is defined as "a sudden, generally unexpected 
occurrence or set of circumstances demanding immediate action." G.L. c. 30A, § 18. If the 
event precipitating the need for a meeting was anticipated, it may not be considered an 
emergency as that term is defined in the statute and regulations. See OML 2012-7; OML 2011-
45.1 
Here, the Board was notified by the Town Administrator following the cancelled July 18, 
2012 meeting that it would need to hold a meeting by July 24, 2012 to discuss the NMRSD 
budget, the need to call a special Town Meeting, and the special election. That the Board was 
unable to convene a quorum during the posted meeting on July 18, 2012, or unable to schedule a 
meeting for July 23, 2012 or July 24, 2012 (which would have allowed the Board to post a timely 
notice on July 19, 2012 or July 20, 2012), does not qualify as an emergency. The need to vote on 
the NMRSD matters was not "a sudden, generally unexpected occurrence or set of 
circumstances," although the lack of quorum on July 18, 2012 may have been unexpected. The

Board cannot rely on emergency meetings to address scheduling problems. Holding an 
emergency meeting deprives the public of full notice, and is reserved for only those 
circumstances that are unanticipated and "require an immediate response to, for example, protect 
public safety or health." OML 2011-45. Although the Town faced consequences if it did not 
meet the statutory deadlines to hold its Town Meeting and special election, the deadline did not 
represent a sudden or generally unexpected occurrence. 
CONCLUSION 
We find that the Board violated the Open Meeting Law by failing to provide 48 hours' 
notice for its July 20, 2012 meeting. As for remedy, we understand that the Town Meeting and 
special election have already taken place. Thus, it is impossible for us to nullify the Board's 
votes, as the complainant requested. See OML 2012-7. Given our limited options, we order the 
Board's immediate and future compliance with the Open Meeting Law, and caution that future 
similar violations may result in nullification of any action taken at the relevant meeting, and may 
be considered evidence of intent to violate the Law. 
We now consider this matter closed. We appreciate the patience and cooperation of the 
parties during this investigation. If you have any questions regarding this letter, please do not 
hesitate to contact our office at (617) 963-2540.
