This office received a complaint from Shaoqiong (Ada) Zhong on May 29, 2025,
1 
alleging that the Westwood Select Board (the “Board”) violated the Open Meeting Law, G.L. c. 
30A, §§ 18-25. Ms. Zhong originally sent an email to the Board on April 29, styled as an Open 
Meeting Law complaint, outlining alleged violations of the Law and requesting a response within 
14 business days. By letter dated May 14, Town Counsel
2 responded to the email addressing one 
of the allegations and explaining that the complaint had not been filed on the required Open 
Meeting Law complaint form and was untimely. Thereafter, Ms. Zhong filed an Open Meeting 
Law complaint form, dated May 15, realleging one of the allegations raised in the April 29 
email.  
 
The April 29 email alleges that during its March 24 meeting, the Board declined to read 
in full a written comment submitted by Ms. Zhong, while reading in full other comments that had 
been submitted. The April 29 email also alleges that the Board failed to respond in 14 business 
days to an April 1 email Ms. Zhong had sent regarding the failure to read her comment in full 
during the March 24 meeting. The May 15 Open Meeting Law complaint again alleges that the 
Board failed to read Ms. Zhong’s written comment in full during the Board’s March 24 meeting 
and asserts that the reason for doing so was discriminatory in nature.

Following our review, we find that the Board did not violate the Open Meeting Law by 
failing to respond to the April 1 email within 14 business days, and we decline to review the 
remainder of the allegations. In reaching this determination we reviewed the April 1 and 29 
emails, the May 14 letter from Town Counsel, the May 15 complaint form, and the May 29 
request for further review, as well as additional communications from Ms. Zhong. 
 
Open Meeting Law complaints must be made in writing “using the form approved by the 
Att
orney General and available on the Attorney General’s website.” 940 CMR 29.05(1). Open 
Meeting Law complaints must be filed with the public body alleged to have violated the Law 
within 30 days of the alleged violation, or, if the alleged violation could not reasonably have 
been known at the time it occurred, then within 30 days of the date it should reasonably have 
been discovered. G.L. c. 30A, § 23(b); 940 CMR 29.05(4). When reviewing the timeliness of a 
complaint we consider when the action that is alleged to have violated the Open Meeting Law 
was reasonably discoverable, not when an individual complainant became aware of the action or 
of the requirements of the Open Meeting Law. See OML Declination 12-18-20 (Pembroke Board 
of Health); OML Declination 5-21-12 (Montague Board of Selectmen).3 When an alleged 
violation occurs during an open session, the alleged violation is reasonably discoverable at the 
time it occurs. See OML Declination 7-20-17 (Edgartown Planning Board); OML 2014-85, n. 1; 
OML 2012-52. A public body is not required to respond to a complaint that is not filed on the 
Attorney General’s complaint form. 940 CMR 29.05.  
 
Fir
st, with respect to the allegation that the Board failed to respond to the April 1 email 
within 14 business days, we find no violation where a public body is not obligated to address a 
complaint that is not filed using the Attorney General’s Open Meeting Law complaint form. 
Additionally, we note that it was not clear that the April 1 email was intended as an Open 
Meeting Law complaint where it did not claim to be an Open Meeting Law complaint and did 
not mention the Open Meeting Law at any point.  
 
Next, we decline to review the allegation that the Board violated the Open Meeting Law 
by declining to read Ms. Zhong’s comment in full during the Board’s March 24 meeting. To 
begin, the allegation was not formally raised in a properly filed Open Meeting Law complaint 
until May 15, 52 days after the date of the alleged violation. Even if the April 29 email were to 
be construed as an Open Meeting Law complaint, it too was sent to the Board more than 30 days 
after the date of the alleged violation.  
 
Additionally, even if the complaint had been timely filed, we would decline to review the 
merits 
of the complaint because it raises allegations that, even if true, would not constitute 
violations of the Open Meeting Law. The Division of Open Government’s review concerns 
compliance with the Open Meeting Law, G.L. c. 30A, §§ 18-25. The Open Meeting Law gives 
the chair of a public body discretion to decide whether to permit public comment during a 
meeting. See G.L. c. 30A § 20(g) (“[n]o person shall address a meeting of a public body without 
permission of the chair, and all persons shall, at the request of the chair, be silent.”); see also

OML 2020-77; OML 2017-189.4 If public participation will be permitted, the chair may 
determine the method for receiving public comment, may decide who can speak or otherwise 
participate during the meeting, and may set limitations on participation. See OML 2020-114; 
OML 2017-189. The Open Meeting Law does not require a chair to read aloud comments that 
are submitted to the public body in writing. See OML Declination 11-3-23 (Board of Elementary 
and Secondary Education). Furthermore, allegations of bias and discrimination would not, in and 
of themselves, constitute violations of the Open Meeting Law. See OML 2018-25. Likewise, 
allegations regarding an individual’s constitutional rights fall outside the scope of the Open 
Meeting Law, and therefore outside of the Division’s review.5  
 
 Although the Open Meeting Law does not require that public bodies allow public 
commen
t, our office encourages public bodies to allow for as much public participation as time 
and circumstances permit. See OML 2019-120; OML 2015-12; OML 2014-129; OML 2012-59.  
 
For the reasons stated above, we find that the Board did not violate the Open Meeting 
Law by failing to respond to an April 1 email within 14 business days. We decline to review the 
remainder of the allegations. We now consider the complaint addressed by this determination to 
be resolved. This determination does not address any other complaints that may be pending with 
the Board or with our office. Please feel free to contact our office at (617) 963-2540 if you have 
any questions regarding this letter.
