 RE: Open Meeting Law Complaint 
 
Dear Attorney Feodoroff: 
 
This office received a complaint from Eric Rossmeisl on January 21, 2024, alleging that 
the Rehoboth Conservation Commission (the “Commission”) violated the Open Meeting Law, 
G.L. c. 30A, §§ 18-25.  The complaint was originally filed with the Commission on or about 
October 18, 2023, and Town Administrator Deborah Arruda responded to the complaint, on 
behalf of the Commission, by email on November 7, 2023.  You also responded to the complaint, 
on behalf of the Commission, by letter dated February 24, 2024.  The complaint alleges that the 
Commission failed to timely approve minutes of meetings held between September 2022 and 
June 2023.   
 
Following our review, we find that the Commission violated the Open Meeting Law in 
the way alleged.  In reaching this determination, we reviewed the original complaint, the 
Commission’s responses to the complaint, the complaint filed with our office requesting further 
review, and relevant notices and minutes of Commission meetings held between September 13, 
2022, and January 30, 2024. 
 
The Open Meeting Law requires that “[m]inutes of all open sessions shall be created and 
approved in a timely manner.”  G.L. c. 30A, § 22(c).  “Timely manner” means “within the next 
three public body meetings or within 30 days, whichever is later, unless the public body can 
show good cause for further delay.”  940 CMR 29.11; see OML 2018-48.

approved any of the remaining 13 sets of meetings minutes.  We find that the Commission failed 
to timely approve all 17 sets of minutes of meetings, which were held between September 13, 
2022, and June 27, 2023, where the minutes were not approved within the later of the next three 
meetings or 30 days following each meeting.  See OML 2021-155.  As such, we find that the 
Commission violated the Open Meeting Law by failing to approve these minutes in a timely 
manner. 
 
We remind the Commission that the responsibility for complying with the Open Meeting 
Law rests with public bodies and members of such bodies, not with individual public employees, 
such as administrative staff.  See OML 2018-105.  We understand the demands on a public body 
with limited staff; however, the Commission is still obligated to timely approve minutes.  See 
OML 2018-111; OML 2017-115.  If staff resources are not available to create minutes for the 
Commission to approve, then Commission members must take on this responsibility to ensure 
compliance with the law.  See OML 2017-115.   
 
We must determine whether this violation was, as the complainant urges, an intentional 
one.  See G.L. c. 30A, § 23(c).  An intentional violation is an “act or omission by a public body 
or a member thereof, in knowing violation of [the Open Meeting Law].”  940 CMR 29.02.  An 
intentional violation may be found where the public body acted with deliberate ignorance of the 
law’s requirement or has previously been advised that certain conduct violates the Open Meeting 
Law.  Id.  The Commission has not been previously advised of the law’s requirements with 
respect to approval of minutes.  Because we have not advised the Commission of its obligations 
prior to the events addressed in this letter, we find that this violation was not intentional.  
However, we note our concern that the Committee still has not approved minutes of meetings 
held between September 13, 2022, and March 28, 2023, which meetings occurred between 12 
and 19 months ago.  The Commission states that it expects to approve the remaining 13 sets of 
minutes within the next five weeks.    
 
For the reasons stated above, we find that the Commission violated the Open Meeting 
Law.  We order immediate and future compliance with the law’s requirements, and we caution 
that similar future violations could be considered evidence of intent to violate the law.  In 
addition, we order the Commission to approve the 13 sets of minutes of meetings held between 
September 13, 2022, and March 28, 2023, within the next 40 days and to thereafter certify to our 
office that it has done so.
