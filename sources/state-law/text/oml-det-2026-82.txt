 RE: Open Meeting Law Complaint 
 
Dear James Maksimoski: 
 
This office received a complaint from Susan Delmolino on April 2, 2026, alleging that 
the Hadley Planning Board (the “Board”) violated the Open Meeting Law G.L. c. 30A, §§ 18-25. 
The complaint was originally filed with the Board on or about February 27, 2026, and you 
responded to the complaint, on behalf of the Board, on March 18, 2026. The complaint alleges 
that the Board routinely failed to “codify, vote on, or approve meeting minutes” in a timely 
manner from 2019 to February 2026.  
 
We resolve this complaint by informal action, in accordance with 940 CMR 29.07(2)(a), 
after reviewing the original complaint, the Board’s response to the complaint, the complaint filed 
with our office requesting further review, and a video recording of the Board meeting held on 
March 17, 2026.1 We find that the Board violated the Open Meeting Law by failing to timely 
approve minutes of meetings held on January 20 and February 3, 2026. However, we decline to 
review whether the minutes of meetings held during the years 2019 to 2025 were timely 
approved for the reasons discussed below.

The Open Meeting Law requires that a public body creates and maintains accurate 
minutes of all meetings. G.L. c. 30A, § 22(a). The Open Meeting Law further requires that open 
session minutes “be created and approved in a timely manner.” Id. at § 22(c). “Timely manner” 
means “within the next three public body meetings or within 30 days, whichever is later, unless 
the public body can show good cause for further delay.” See 940 CMR 29.11; OML 2018-48.2  
Whenever possible, we recommend that minutes of a meeting be approved at the next meeting.  
See OML 2018-67; OML 2017-133.  
 
The complaint alleges that the Board failed to timely approve minutes of meetings held 
between January 2019 and February 2026. We decline to review whether all minutes of Board 
meetings held during the seven-year period between January 2019 and February 2026 have been 
timely approved as our office will not conduct broad audits of public bodies based on generalized 
allegations. See OML 2013-180; OML 2012-106. Nonetheless, we have reviewed this issue with 
respect to the minutes of the Board meetings that occurred in January and February 2026.  
 
Here, the Board held meetings on January 20, February 3, and February 17, 2026. The 
Board approved the minutes of the February 17, 2026, meeting during a meeting held on April 7, 
2026, which was the third meeting thereafter. As such, we find that the Board approved these 
minutes in a timely manner. However, the Board did not approve the January 20 or February 3, 
2026, minutes until its meeting on April 21, 2026, which was the sixth and fifth meeting 
thereafter, respectively. We find that the Board failed to timely approve these two sets of 
minutes, as the minutes were not approved within the later of the next three meetings or 30 days 
following each meeting.
3 To the extent there are other meeting minutes that the Board has not

For the reasons stated above, we find that the Board violated the Open Meeting Law by 
failing to timely approve minutes of meetings held on January 20 and February 3, 2026. We 
order immediate and future compliance with the law’s requirements, and we caution that similar 
future violations could be considered evidence of intent to violate the law.  
 
We now consider the complaint addressed by this determination to be resolved.  This 
determination does not address any other complaints that may be pending with our office or the 
Board.  Please feel free to contact our office at (617) 963-2540 if you have any questions 
regarding this letter.
