 RE:        Open Meeting Law Complaint 
 
Dear Attorney Sousa:  
 
This office received a complaint from Patrick Higgins on December 5, 2024, alleging that 
the Fall River Housing Authority Board of Commissioners (the “Board”) violated the Open 
Meeting Law, G.L. c. 30A, §§ 18-25.  The complaint was originally filed with the Board on or 
about November 7, 2024, and you1 responded to the complaint by email dated December 5, 
2024.2  The complaint alleges that the Board violated the Open Meeting Law by failing to timely 
respond to a request for meeting minutes.   
 
Following our review, we find that the Board did not violate the Open Meeting Law in 
the way alleged.  In reaching this determination, we reviewed the original complaint, your 
response to the complaint, and the complaint filed with our office requesting further review.  In 
addition, we spoke separately with you and with the complainant by telephone on February 25, 
2025.

The Open Meeting Law requires that a public body “create and maintain accurate 
minutes of all meetings, including executive sessions.”  G.L. c. 30A, § 22(a).  The minutes of an 
open session, if they exist and whether approved or in draft form, shall be made available upon 
request by any person within 10 days.  G.L. c. 30A, § 22(c).  If minutes do not yet exist at the 
time of a request, the public body is still required to respond to the request within 10 days with 
an explanation of whether the minutes do or do not exist in either approved or draft form.  See 
OML 2017-50; OML 2016-71; OML 2015-173.3 
 
Executive session minutes may be withheld from disclosure to the public “as long as 
publication may defeat the lawful purposes of the executive session, but no longer.”  G.L. c. 
30A, § 22(f).  When the purpose for a valid executive session has been served, the minutes and 
any documents or exhibits used at the session must be disclosed unless the attorney-client 
privilege or an exemption to the public records law applies to withhold them, in whole or in part, 
from disclosure.  See id.  Public bodies have an obligation to review the minutes of executive 
sessions at reasonable intervals to determine if continued non-disclosure of minutes is warranted, 
and to announce that determination at the next meeting following its review.  G.L. c. 30A, § 
22(g)(1); see OML 2019- 35; OML 2015-94; OML 2013-56.  Upon request by any person to 
inspect or copy the minutes of an executive session or any portion thereof, the body must 
respond to the request within 10 days following receipt and release any such minutes where 
publication would not defeat the lawful purposes of the executive session; provided, however, 
that if the body has not performed a review, the public body shall perform the review and release 
the non-exempt minutes, or any portion thereof, not later than the body's next meeting or 30 
days, whichever first occurs.  G.L. c. 30A, § 22(g)(2); OML 2012-106; OML 2012-91; OML 
2012-86.  
 
The complaint asserts that on October 13, 2024, the complainant sent an email to the 
Board at board@fallriverha.org, which is the email address that is publicized on the Housing 
Authority’s website as a way of contacting the Board.4  The October email requested a copy of 
all executive session minutes for calendar years 2020 through 2024, and a copy of all open 
session minutes for calendar years 2023 and 2024.  Attorney Sousa maintains that the Board 
never received the email.  We have no reason to doubt the truthfulness of this statement.  See 
LaPointe v. License Board of Worcester, 389 Mass. 454 (1983) (“There is every presumption in 
favor of the honesty and sufficiency of the motives actuating public officers in actions ostensibly 
taken for the general welfare.”).  Moreover, we note that Attorney Sousa had previously 
requested that the complainant send all correspondence directed to the Board to Attorney Sousa, 
and the October 13 request was not sent to Attorney Sousa.  In the circumstances here, we are 
unable to conclude that the Board actually received the request for minutes.  Accordingly, we are 
constrained to find that the Board did not violate the Open Meeting Law when it did not respond 
to the request for minutes within 10 days of October 13, 2024.  See OML 2019-66; OML 2018-
50.   
 
However, we note our concern that the Board learned of the request for minutes on 
December 5, 2024, when our office provided the Board with a copy of the complaint, and yet the

Board still did not respond to the request for minutes.  We take this opportunity to remind the 
Board that requests for meeting minutes may fall under the Public Records Law or the Open 
Meeting Law, depending on how the request is made, and the requirements for responding differ.  
A request for minutes that is directed to a clerk or other municipal employees as Records Access 
Officer will likely fall under the Public Records Law and requires a response in 10 business 
days.  See G.L. c. 66, § 10; OML 2019-126; OML 2019-68.  A request for meeting minutes 
made directly to the public body falls under the Open Meeting Law and requires a response 
within 10 calendar days.  See G.L. c. 30A, § 22(g)(2); OML 2021-134; OML 2019-125.   
  
We encourage the Board to respond to the request, if it has not already done so, and 
either provide copies of the requested minutes or direct the complainant to a website where the 
requested minutes are posted and accessible.  See OML 2024-24 and OML 2022-205 (explaining 
that directing a requester to a website where requested minutes are posted and accessible 
complies with the requirement in Open Meeting Law to make minutes “available”).   
 
For the reasons stated above, we find that the Board did not violate the Open Meeting 
Law.  We now consider the complaint addressed by this determination to be resolved.  This 
determination does not address any other complaints that may be pending with our office or the 
Board.  Please feel free to contact our office at (617) 963-2540 if you have any questions 
regarding this letter.
