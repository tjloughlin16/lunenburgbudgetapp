This office received a complaint from Attorney Margaret Sheehan on behalf of 
Community Land & Water Coalition on April 24, 2023, alleging that the Plymouth Zoning 
Board of Appeals (the “Board”), and specifically the Board’s former chair and current member 
Peter Conner, violated the Open Meeting Law, G.L. c. 30A, §§ 18-25. The complaint was 
originally filed with the Board on or about February 21, 2023, and, after an extension of time 
granted by our office, you responded on behalf of the Board by letter dated March 31, 2023. The 
complaint alleges that the Board failed to timely approve minutes for its meeting held on 
February 17, 2021.
1  
 
We resolve this matter by informal action, in accordance with 940 CMR 29.07(2)(a), and 
find that the Board violated the Open Meeting Law by failing to approve the minutes of its 
February 17, 2021, meeting in a timely manner. In reaching this determination, we reviewed the

The Open Meeting Law requires that a public body create and maintain accurate minutes 
of al
l meetings and that those minutes be approved in a timely manner. G.L. c. 30A, § 22. The 
Open Meeting Law does not define “timely manner”; however, the Attorney General’s Open 
Meeting Law regulations provide that “timely manner” means “within the next three public body 
meetings or within 30 days, whichever is later, unless the public body can show good cause for 
further delay.” 940 C.M.R. 29.11(2). The Board acknowledges that it did not timely approve the 
minutes of its February 17, 2021, meeting. In its request for an extension of time to respond to 
the complaint the Board sets forth the circumstances that resulted in the minutes of the February 
17, 2021, meeting not be approved in a timely manner. We understood this to be shared as 
background information and not as an assertion of good cause for delay in approving the meeting 
minutes. Regardless, we would not find the circumstances described to constitute good cause for 
delay. See OML 2022-184 (explaining that in general staffing issues do not constitute good 
cause for delay in approving meeting minutes because it is the responsibility of the public body 
and its members to take steps to ensure that minutes are created and approved in a timely 
manner). 
 
On Ma
rch 31 the Board responded to the complaint, attaching the final approved minutes 
for the February 17, 2021, meeting. We commend the Board for taking this corrective action 
once the issue was brought to the Board’s attention. We decline to find, as the Complainant 
urges, that this violation was intentional.
2 We have not previously warned the Board against the 
violation found here, nor do we find other evidence that would support a finding of an intentional 
violation by the Board.    
 
For the reasons stated above, we find that the Board violated the Open Meeting Law by 
failing to approve the minutes of its February 17, 2021, meeting in a timely manner. We order 
the Board’s immediate and future compliance with the Open Meeting Law. Because the Board 
has since approved the minutes at issue, we do not order further remedial action.  
  
 
2 An intentional violation is an “act or omission by a public body or a member thereof, in knowing violation of [the 
Open Meeting Law].” 940 CMR 29.02. An intentional violation may be found where the public body acted with 
deliberate ignorance of the law’s requirement or has previously been advised that certain conduct violates the Open 
Meeting Law. Id.
