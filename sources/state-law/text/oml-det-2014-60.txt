RE: Open Meeting Law Complaint 
Dear Attorney Corbo: 
This office received an Open Meeting Law complaint from Claire Stidsen, dated March 
14, 2014, alleging that the Sterling Board of Selectmen (the "Board") violated the Open Meeting 
Law, G.L. c. 30A, §§ 18-25. Specifically, the complaint alleges that the Board failed to provide 
sufficient notice of its November 20, 2013 interviews with the candidates for the position of 
town administrator. The complaint was filed with the Board on or about December 2, 2013. 
Following an extension of time granted by our office, the Board responded through counsel by 
letter dated January 14, 2014. 
Following our review, we find that the Board did not violate the Open Meeting Law. In 
reaching this determination, we reviewed the December 2, 2013 complaint filed with the Board; 
the Board's January 14, 2014 response; and the March 14, 2014 complaint filed with our office. 
We also reviewed the notice and minutes of the Board's November 14, 2013 and November 20, 
2013 meetings. 
During the Board's November 14, 2013 open meeting, the Board interviewed three 
finalists for the position of town administrator. The notice for the Board's November 20, 2013 
meeting listed a number of topics for discussion, including "Discuss/Vote for Town 
Administrator." During that meeting, the Board again interviewed the three finalists for the town 
administrator position. According to the minutes, after speaking with each candidate, "the 
consensus of the Board was that they would like more time to individually delve into the 
candidate's [sic] backgrounds." The Board then tabled the vote to select a candidate until its 
December 4, 2013 meeting.

DISCUSSION 
The Open Meeting Law requires that public bodies post notice 48 hours in advance of a 
meeting and include a "listing of topics that the chair reasonably anticipates will be discussed at 
the meeting." G.L. c. 30A, § 20(b). Public bodies are required to list topics in a meeting notice 
with "sufficient specificity to reasonably advise the public of the issues to be discussed at the 
meeting." 940 CMR 29.03(l)(b). We generally consider a topic to include sufficient specificity 
when a reasonable member of the public could read the topic and understand the anticipated 
nature of the public body's discussion. See OML 2013-157; OML 2011-44.1 
Here, the Board listed a topic in the notice for its November 20, 2013 meeting as 
"Discuss/Vote for Town Administrator." The complainant argues this was not sufficiently 
specific because that notice suggested that the Board would be voting to hire a new town 
administrator, rather than continuing the interview process. We find that the topic was 
sufficiently specific to reasonably advise members of the public that the Board would be 
discussing the town administrator candidates. See 940 CMR 29.03(1 )(b). While additional 
detail, such as the fact that the Board would be conducting interviews and the names of the 
candidates, may have been helpful to the public, the discussion that occurred was within the 
scope of the posted agenda item.2 See OML 2014-44 (public body not required to anticipate 
course of deliberation); OML 2013-152 (notice topic was sufficiently specific even though 
additional detail may have been helpful to the public). Accordingly, we find no violation of the 
Open Meeting Law. See OML 2013-145 (no violation to omit reasonably inferable information 
from notice); OML 2012-75 (same). 
CONCLUSION 
For the reasons stated above, we find that the Board did not violate the Open Meeting 
Law. 
We now consider the complaint addressed by this determination closed. This 
determination does not address any other complaints that may be pending with our office or the 
Board. Please feel free to contact our office at (617) 963-2540 if you have any questions.
