RE: Open Meeting Law Complaint 
Dear Attorney Murray: 
This office received a complaint from Attorney George Harris on March 10, 2017, 
alleging that the Wayland Zoning Board of Appeals (the "Board") violated the Open 
Meeting Law, G.L. c. 30A, §§ 18-25. The complaint was originally filed with the Board 
on or about December 29, 2016, and you responded, on behalf of the Board, by letter dated 
January 19, 2017. In his complaint, Mr. Harris alleges that the Board failed to create and 
approve certain meeting minutes in a timely manner. 
Following our review, we find that the Board violated the Open Meeting Law in the 
manner alleged. In reaching a determination, we reviewed the original complaint, the 
Board's response to the complaint, and the complaint filed with our office requesting 
further review. 
We find the facts as follows. On December 18, 2016, the complainant submitted a 
request by email to the Board chair "for access to the approved minutes" for certain 
meetings that were held in 2015 and 2016.1 The complainant had previously submitted a 
request to the Board on October 26, 2016 for "all approved ZBA minutes for meetings held 
subsequent to June 9, 2015," to which the Board replied that the minutes from three

DISCUSSION 
The Open Meeting Law requires public bodies to "create and maintain accurate 
minutes of all meetings, including executive sessions." G.L. c. 30A, § 22(a). These 
meeting minutes must be reviewed and approved in a timely manner. G.L. c. 30A, § 22(c). 
Although the Open Meeting Law does not define "timely manner," we recommend that 
minutes of a meeting be approved at the next meeting, whenever possible. See OML 
2014-15; OML 2012-91.3 For bodies that meet regularly—that is, either weekly or 
bimonthly—we have concluded that a delay or two to three months in approving minutes 
does not comply with the law. See OML 2016-118. 
The complaint alleges that the Board failed to approve minutes from certain 
meetings held in 2015 and 2016. The Board acknowledged that it indeed failed to approve 
its minutes in a timely manner, attributing the delay to "unforeseen staffing losses." We 
find that, by waiting at times over a year to draft and approve its minutes, the Board 
violated the Open Meeting Law. See OML 2016-17; OML 2013-37. We commend the 
Board for its remedial efforts in creating and approving the backlog of minutes. Despite 
those efforts, we order the Board to approve its March 22, 2016 meeting minutes within 
thirty (30) days of receipt of this letter. 
CONCLUSION 
For the reasons stated above, we find that the Board violated the Open Meeting 
Law by failing to approve meeting minutes in a timely manner. We order the Board's 
immediate and future compliance with the law's requirements, and we caution that similar 
future violations could be considered evidence of intent to violate the law. Additionally, 
we order the Board to approve its March 22, 2016 meeting minutes within thirty (30) days 
of receipt of this letter. 
We now consider the complaint addressed by this determination to be resolved.
