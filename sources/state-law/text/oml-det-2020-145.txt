 RE: Open Meeting Law Complaint 
Dear Attorney Feodoroff: 
This office received a complaint from Stephen Morgan on August 24, 2020, alleging that 
the Ashland Affordable Housing Trust (the “Trust”) violated the Open Meeting Law, G.L. c. 
30A, §§ 18-25. The complaint was originally filed with the Trust on July 2, and the Trust 
responded by letter dated August 20.1 In his complaint, Mr. Morgan alleges that the Trust failed 
to timely respond to a prior Open Meeting Law complaint.  
We resolve this complaint by informal action in accordance with 940 CMR 29.07(2)(a), 
and find that the Trust violated the Open Meeting Law as alleged. A public body must respond to 
an Open Meeting Law complaint within 14 business days, unless the public body requests an 
extension from the Director of the Division of Open Government. G.L. c. 30A, § 23(b); 940 
CMR 29.05(5); see also OML 2017-171.
2 The complaint concerns the Trust’s failure to respond 
to a May 22 Open Meeting Law complaint in the timeframe required by the Law. The Trust 
responded to the May 22 complaint on July 20 – almost two months after the complaint was 
filed. The Board acknowledges that it failed to timely respond to the complaint due to an 
oversight, as the complaint was attached to an email requesting public records and the Town 
Clerk and Chair of the Trust failed to notice the attached Open Meeting Law complaint.  
We must determine whether the Trust’s violation was, as the complainant urges, 
intentional. See G.L. c. 30A, § 23(c). An intentional violation is an “act or omission by a public 
body or a member thereof, in knowing violation of [the Open Meeting Law].” 940 CMR 29.02.

An intentional violation may be found where the public body acted with deliberate ignorance of 
the law's requirement or has previously been advised that certain conduct violates the Open 
Meeting Law. Id. Here, we have not previously warned the Trust against the violation found 
here, nor do we find other evidence that would support a finding of an intentional violation, as 
the Trust. We therefore decline to find that the violation was intentional. 
 W e order the Trust’s immediate and future compliance with the Open Meeting Law and 
caution the Trust that a determination by our office of a similar violation in the future may be 
considered evidence of intent to violate the Open Meeting Law. We now consider the complaint 
addressed by this determination to be resolved. This determination does not address any other 
complaints that may be pending with our office or the Trust. Please feel free to contact the 
Division at (617) 963 - 2540 if you have any questions.
