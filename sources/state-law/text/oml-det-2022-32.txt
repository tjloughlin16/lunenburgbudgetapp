 RE: Open Meeting Law Complaint 
Dear Attorney Corbo: 
This office received a complaint from Patrick Higgins on October 6, 2021, alleging that 
the Carver Select Board (the “Board”) violated the Open Meeting Law, G.L. c. 30A, §§ 18-25. 
The complaint was originally filed with the Board on September 16, and you responded on 
behalf of the Board by letter dated October 6.1 In his complaint, Mr. Higgins alleges that the 
Board failed to approve meeting minutes in a timely manner.2 
Following our review, we find that the Board violated the Open Meeting Law by failing 
to approve several sets of meeting minutes in a timely manner. In reaching this determination, 
we reviewed the original complaint, the Board’s response to the complaint, and the 
complainant’s request for further review. We also reviewed the notices for the Board’s July 12, 
July 20, and 26 meetings.  
 The Open Meeting Law requires that a public body “create and maintain accurate 
minutes of all meetings, including executive sessions, setting forth the date, time and place, the 
members present or absent, a summary of the discussions on each subject, a list of documents 
and other exhibits used at the meeting, the decisions made and the actions taken at each meeting, 
including the record of all votes.” G.L. c. 30A, § 22(a). The meeting minutes must be reviewed 
and approved in a timely manner. G.L. c. 30A, § 22(c). “Timely manner” means “within the next

 During an August 17 meeting, the Board voted to approve open session minutes for 
meetings that took place on June 15, July 12 and July 20 as well as executive session minutes for 
meetings that took place on March 2, April 6 and April 20. In addition to the Board’s regularly 
scheduled summer meetings on June 15, July 20 and August 17, the Board convened “special 
meetings” on July 12 and 26 which the Board states were “limited in scope and duration.” The 
Board argues that these special meetings did not count towards the three-meeting requirement for 
approving meeting minutes. The Open Meeting Law defines “meeting” as “a deliberation by a 
public body with respect to any matter within the body’s jurisdiction.” The Open Meeting Law 
and regulations make no exception for special meetings or meetings not “normally scheduled,” 
for purposes of timely approval of minutes. See OML 2019-115; OML 2019-84; OML 2017-
103. We therefore find that the Board’s approval on August 17 of the June 15 open session 
minutes was not timely. In addition, the Law does not make an exception for the approval of 
executive session minutes only at otherwise scheduled executive sessions. G.L. c. 30A, § 22(a). 
We therefore find that the Board’s August 17 approval of the March 2, April 6 and April 20 
executive session minutes was also not timely.  
 For the reasons stated above, we find that the Board violated the Open Meeting Law by 
failing to approve several sets of meeting minutes in a timely manner. We order the Board’s 
immediate and future compliance with the Open Meeting Law and caution the Board that a 
determination by our office of a similar violation in the future may be considered evidence of 
intent to violate the Open Meeting Law. As the Board has now approved the meeting minutes at 
issue, we order no further relief.   
 We now consider the complaint addressed by this determination to be resolved. This 
determination does not address any other complaints that may be pending with our office or the 
Board. Please feel free to contact the Division at (617) 963 - 2540 if you have any questions.
