 RE: Open Meeting Law Complaint 
 
Dear Attorney Corbo: 
 
This office received a complaint from Cory Bombredi on May 1, 2025, alleging that the 
Barre Council on Aging (the “Council”) violated the Open Meeting Law, G.L. c. 30A, §§ 18-25.  
The complaint was originally filed with the Council on March 26, 2025, and you responded on 
behalf of the Council by letter dated April 16, 2025.  The complaint alleges that the Council 
violated the Open Meeting Law when it failed to create and approve minutes in a timely manner, 
and when it conducted business without a chair, vice chair or secretary.  The complaint further 
alleges that a non-member, Council Director Betty-Jo O’Brien, presided over Council meetings, 
submitted a memorandum of understanding to Elder Services of Worcester Area without formal 
approval by the Council, and acted on Council governance materials without Council approval or 
public votes.   
 
Following our review, we find that the Council violated the Open Meeting Law when it 
failed to approve minutes in a timely manner.  We decline to review the remaining allegations.   
In reaching this determination, we reviewed the complaint, the Council’s response, and the 
complainant’s request for further review filed with our office.   
 
DISCUSSION 
 
I. The Council failed to approve meeting minutes in a timely manner. 
 
The Open Meeting Law was enacted “to eliminate much of the secrecy surrounding the 
deliberations and decisions on which public policy is based.”  Ghiglione v. School Committee of 
Southbridge, 376 Mass. 70, 72 (1978).  The Open Meeting Law requires that a public body 
“create and maintain accurate minutes of all meetings, including executive sessions, setting forth

the date, time and place, the members present or absent, a summary of the discussions on each 
subject, a list of documents and other exhibits used at the meeting, the decisions made and the 
actions taken at each meeting, including the record of all votes.”  G.L. c. 30A, § 22(a).  The Law 
further states that “[m]inutes of all open sessions shall be created and approved in a timely 
manner.”  G.L. c. 30A, § 22(c).  “Timely manner,” for purposes of approval of open session 
meeting minutes, means “within the next three public body meetings or within 30 days, 
whichever is later, unless the public body can show good cause for further delay.”  940 CMR 
29.11.  Whenever possible, we recommend that minutes of a meeting be approved at the next 
meeting.  See OML 2023-179; OML 2018-67; OML 2017-133.

The Council held meetings on November 6, 2024, December 4, 2024, January 8, 2025, 
January 16, 2025, January 23, 2025, and March 5, 2025.  The minutes from these meetings were 
approved on April 3, 2025.  Minutes of November 6, 2024, December 4, 2024, and January 8, 
2025 were not approved within the next three meetings or within 30 days.  Because those 
minutes were not approved in a timely manner, we find that the Council violated the Open 
Meeting Law. 
 
The Council neither acknowledges nor disputes that three sets of minutes were not timely 
approved.  Instead, the Council argues that the complainant’s allegation regarding minutes is not 
timely as to those minutes and therefore should not be reviewed by our office.  The Council 
notes that minutes of the November 6, 2024, and December 4, 2024, meetings were to be 
approved at the January 8, 2025, meeting.  However, the Council announced during the open 
session of the January 8 meeting that review of those minutes would be postponed to a later date.  
At the following meeting, held on January 23, 2025, the Council still did not approve the minutes 
of the November 6, 2024, and December 4, 2024, meetings.  The Council relies on this 
chronology to argue that a reasonable member of the public would have known “by January 23 at 
the latest” that the minutes of the November 6, 2024, and December 4, 2024, meetings still were 
not approved.  The complaint should have been filed within 30 days of January 23, 2025, 
according to the Council. 
 
Pursuant to the Open Meeting Law, a complaint must be filed with the public body within 
30 days of the alleged violation.  G.L. c. 30A, § 23(b).  If the alleged violation could not have 
been known at the time it occurred, then the complaint must be filed with the public body within 
30 days of the date on which the alleged violation could reasonably have been discovered.  940 
CMR 29.05(4).  Complaints alleging violations that take place during an open session can 
reasonably be discovered at the time they occur.  See OML Declination 11-27-2023 (Wellfleet 
Selectboard); OML 2022-128; OML 2012-52.   
 
A complaint alleging untimely approval of minutes, if filed with the public body after the 
minutes have been approved in open session, must be filed within 30 days of the minutes’ 
approval.  See OML 2025-96.  However, the Open Meeting Law imposes a continuing obligation 
to maintain minutes of all meetings.  See G.L. c. 30A, § 22(a); OML 2025-35; OML 2020-69 
(reviewing a complaint regarding a committee’s failure to approve meeting minutes for a three-
year period); OML 2018-115 (reviewing a complaint regarding the failure to create and maintain

II. We decline to review the remaining allegations of the complaint. 
 
The Division of Open Government’s review concerns compliance with the Open Meeting 
Law.  The Open Meeting Law does not require that a public body appoint a member as chair.  
See OML 2024-120; OML 2020-110.  Nor does the Law dictate who has authority to run a 
meeting.  See OML 2020-110.  Therefore, we decline to review allegations that an elected or 
appointed chair, rather than Director O’Brien, should have presided over meetings.   
 
Director O’Brien’s submission of a memorandum of understanding and her action on 
Council governance also do not implicate the Open Meeting Law.  Finally, to the extent that the 
complaint alleges violations by a staff member acting independently of the Council, such 
allegations are not subject to our review, as the Open Meeting Law applies only to public bodies 
and individuals acting as public body members.  See OML 2025-79; OML 2023-174; OML 
Declination 5-16-19 (Bourne Town Clerk).  Because these remaining allegations, even if true, 
would not constitute violations of the Open Meeting Law, we decline to review them.  We offer 
no opinion as to the authority of a staff member to take action on behalf of the Council.  
 
CONCLUSION 
 
 For the reasons stated above, we find that the Council violated the Open Meeting Law by 
failing to create and timely approve meeting minutes.  We order the Council’s immediate and 
future compliance with the Open Meeting Law and caution that similar future violations could be 
considered evidence of intent to violate the law and may result in the imposition of civil penalties 
of up to $1,000 per violation.  Because the subject minutes have now been approved, we order no 
further remedial action in response to this complaint. 
 
We now consider the complaint addressed by this determination to be resolved.  This 
determination does not address any other complaints that may be pending with our office or the 
Council.  Please feel free to contact the Division at (617) 963-2540 if you have any questions.
