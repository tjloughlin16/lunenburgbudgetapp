Arlington Tree Committee (the “Committee”) violated the Open Meeting Law, G.L. c. 30A, §§

in the ways alleged.  Moreover, we decline to review allegations relating to the Committee 
meeting held on October 8, 2025, as the complaint was untimely as to those allegations.  In 
reaching this determination, we reviewed the original complaint, the Board’s response to the 
complaint, and the complaint filed with our office requesting further review.  In addition, we

DISCUSSION 
 
The Open Meeting Law was enacted “to eliminate much of the secrecy surrounding the 
deliberations and decisions on which public policy is based.”  Ghiglione v. Sch. Comm. of 
Southbridge, 376 Mass. 70, 72 (1978).  A public body must post notice of every meeting at least 
48 hours in advance, not including Saturdays, Sundays, and legal holidays.  G.L. c. 30A, § 20(b). 
Notices must include “a listing of topics that the chair reasonably anticipates will be discussed at 
the meeting.  Id.  The listing of topics shall have sufficient specificity to reasonably advise the 
public of the issues to be discussed at the meeting.  940 CMR 29.03(1)(b).  We generally 
consider a topic to be sufficiently specific when a reasonable member of the public could read 
the topic and understand the anticipated nature of the public body’s discussion.  See OML 2023-
114; OML 2022-239.2   
 
The Committee asserts
  that the complaint is not timely as to the allegations regarding the 
October 8, 2025, meeting.  Pursuant to the Open Meeting Law, a complaint must be filed with 
the public body within 30 days of the alleged violation.  G.L. c. 30A, § 23(b).  When reviewing 
the timeliness of a complaint we consider when the action that is alleged to have violated the 
Open Meeting Law was reasonably discoverable, not when an individual complainant became 
aware of the action or of the requirements of the Open Meeting Law.  See OML Declination 9-9-
26 (Winchendon Board of Selectmen); OML Declination 4-17- 24 (Monson Select Board); OML 
Declination 12-18-20 (Pembroke Board of Health).  When an alleged violation occurs during an 
open session, the alleged violation is reasonably discoverable at the time it occurs.  See OML 
2012-52; OML Declination 11-27-23 (Wellfleet Selectboard); OML Declination 7-20-17 
(Edgartown Planning Board); OML Declination 9-30-13 (Boston Zoning Board of Appeal).  
Here, because the October 8, 2025, meeting took place entirely in open session, any allegations 
relating to that meeting should have been filed within 30 days of the meeting, that is, on or 
before November 7, 2025.  Because the complaint was filed with the Committee on December 
18, 2025, more than 30 days after the date of the alleged violations, we find that these allegations 
are untimely and we decline to review them.  See OML 2024-174; OML 2024-26. 
 
With respect to the meeting held on November 19, 2025, we read the complaint to allege 
that the Committee provided insufficient notice to the complainant.  The Open Meeting Law 
does not require additional notice of an open session meeting beyond the meeting notice itself.  
G.L. c. 30A, § 20(b).  Here, the Committee posted notice of the November 19, 2025, meeting. 
Therefore, even if true, an allegation that the Committee did not provide sufficient notice to the 
complainant would not constitute a violation of the Open Meeting Law and we decline to review 
it.  See OML Declination 3-22-21 (Rockport Zoning Board of Appeals).  To the extent that G.L. 
c. 87 or the Arlington Town Bylaws require individual notice, we decline to review such an 
allegation for the same reasons.

In addition, we read the complaint to allege that the Committee violated provisions of 
G.L. c. 87 on several counts, including that the Committee allowed individuals to make 
disparaging and defaming remarks against the complainant.  The Division of Open 
Government’s review concerns compliance only with the Open Meeting Law, G.L. c. 30A, §§ 
18-25.  Even if true, these allegations would not constitute a violation of the Open Meeting Law.  
We have previously declined to review complaints alleging that a public body has violated c. 87, 
or that a public body subjected a complainant to hostile treatment.  See OML 2026-98; OML 
2019-138, n.2.  Likewise, we decline to review such allegations here. 
 
Finally, the complaint appears to allege that the Committee engaged in deliberations 
outside of a meeting.  However, the complaint does not cite to any specific improper 
communication outside of a meeting but merely suggests that Committee members spoke “about 
a written letter sent to them” from the Arlington Tree Warden “on or before or there about” the 
November 19, 2025, meeting.  The Open Meeting Law defines deliberation, in relevant part, as 
“an oral or written communication through any medium, including electronic mail, between or 
among a quorum of a public body on any public business within its jurisdiction.”  G.L. c. 30A, § 
18.  For the purposes of the Open Meeting Law, a quorum is generally a simple majority of the 
members of the public body.  Id.  To the extent the complaint alleges that the Committee 
deliberated outside of a properly noticed meeting via serial communication facilitated by the 
Tree Warden, we find no evidence of such serial deliberation.  In general, a communication from 
the Tree Warden to the Committee would not constitute deliberation in violation of the Open 
Meeting Law as the Tree Warden is an individual public official.  Individual public officials are 
not public bodies and therefore are not subject to the Open Meeting Law.  See OML 2021-77, 
n.2 (finding a Town Administrator not subject to the Open Meeting Law); OML Declination 5-
16-19 (Bourne Town Clerk) (finding Town Clerk not subject to the Open Meeting Law); OML 
Declination 12-15-15 (Lawrence City Clerk) (finding City Clerk not subject to the Open Meeting 
Law).  Further, although a public body may not use a non-member, such as a staff member, to 
facilitate communication on matters that the Committee would otherwise be required to save for 
discussion at an open meeting, we find no indication that occurred here.  See OML 2016-172; 
see also Dist. Atty for the Northern Dist. v. Sch. Comm. of Wayland, 451 Mass. 561, 570-571 
(2009). 
 
CONCLUSION 
 
For the reasons stated above, we find that the Committee did not violate the Open 
Meeting Law.  We now consider the complaint addressed by this determination to be resolved.  
This determination does not address any other complaints that may be pending with the 
Committee or with our office.  Please feel free to contact our office at (617) 963-2540 if you 
have any questions regarding this letter.
