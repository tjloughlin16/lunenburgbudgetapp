Re; Open Meeting Law Complaint 
Dear Attorney Bell: 
This office received a complaint from Michael DiPronio (the complainant), dated 
September 25, 2013, alleging that the Christa McAuliffe Regional Public Charter School Special 
Education Parent Advisory Council (the SEP AC) violated the Open Meeting Law, G.L. c. 30A, 
§§ 18-25. For reasons material to this determination, the Board of Trustees (the Board) received 
the original complaint on September 11, 2013. You responded on behalf of the Board by letter 
dated September 25, 2013. Specifically, the complaint alleges that the Board failed to provide 
minutes of any open session meetings conducted by the SEP AC within 10 days of request. 
Following our review, we find that neither the SEP AC nor the Board acting in the 
SEPAC's stead violated the Open Meeting Law. In reaching this determination, we reviewed the 
September 11, 2013 complaint filed with the Board; your September 25, 2013 response on behalf 
of the Board; and the September 25, 2013 complaint filed with our office. Furthermore, we 
reviewed certain electronic mail correspondence between the complainant and representatives of 
the school. Finally, we spoke via telephone with counsel for the Board in October 2013.1 
FACTS 
On August, 18, 2013, the complainant requested, among other items, "[c]opies of 
meeting minutes and attendance for Special Education Parent Advisory Council meetings for the 
2012-2013 school year." The Board responded on behalf of the SEP AC on August 30, 2013, 
stating it would forward the information in a timely manner. In a subsequent letter from counsel 
to the complainant dated September 25, 2013, the Board stated that it had determined the SEP AC

had not met during the 2012-2013 school year and consequently the requested records did not 
exist. This complaint followed. Our office confirmed the SEP AC did not hold any meetings 
during the 2012-2013 school year because it was not constituted at that time. 
DISCUSSION 
A public body must create and maintain accurate minutes of all meetings. G.L. c. 30A, 
§ 22(a). "The minutes of an open session, if they exist. . ., shall be made available upon request 
by any person within 10 days." G.L. c. 30A, § 22(c) (emphasis added). The complaint alleges 
that, following a request, the SEP AC failed to provide minutes of its meeting for the 2012-2013 
school year. The complaint further alleges that the Board, acting on behalf of the SEP AC, failed 
to respond within 10 days of a request for minutes. 
We find that the SEP AC did not hold any meeting during the 2012-2013 school year. A 
public body which does not meet is under no requirement to keep minutes. It follows, therefore, 
that the Board was not obliged to make minutes available within 10 days because the minutes did 
not exist. Thus neither the Board nor the SEPAC violated the Open Meeting Law by failing to 
make minutes available within 10 days of a request. 
CONCLUSION 
We find that neither the Board nor the SEPAC violated the Open Meeting Law because 
there were no minutes responsive to the complainant's request. 
We now consider the complaint addressed by this determination to be resolved. This 
determination does not address any other complaints which may be pending with our office, the 
SEPAC, or the Board. Please feel free to contact our office at (617) 963-2540 if you have any 
questions regarding this letter.
