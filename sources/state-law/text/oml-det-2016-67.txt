This office received a complaint from Patrick Higgins on April 1, 2016, alleging that the 
Swansea Board of Selectmen (the Board) violated the Open Meeting Law, G.L. c. 30A, §§ 18-
25. The complaint was originally filed with the Board on March 7, 2016, and you responded, on 
behalf of the Board, by letter dated March 21, 2016. The complaint alleges that the Board held a 
meeting on February 9, 2016 in a location that was not accessible to the public because the 
elevator, which provided access to the first floor of the building, had expired on December 31, 
2015. 
Following our review, we resolve this complaint by informal action in accordance with 
940 CMR 29.07(2)(a), and find that the Board did not violate the Open Meeting Law. The Open 
Meeting Law states that all meetings of a public body must be open to the public. G.L. c. 30A, § 
20(a). Access must include the opportunity to be physically present as well as to see and hear 
what is being discussed by the members of the public body. See OML 2016-14; OML 2014-3; 
OML 2013-189.1 In addition. Title II of the Americans with Disabilities Act requires that 
municipal services, including public meetings, be made accessible to people with disabilities. 42 
U.S.C. §§ 12131-12165 (1990); OML 2012-49. According to the Massachusetts Department of 
Public Safety (the Department), although the elevator inspection was expired at the time of the 
meeting, it remained in compliance with state law because the Town had submitted its renewal 
application and fee prior to the expiration date. Here, the Town had submitted its renewal form 
to the Department on November 19, 2015, a full month before the inspection expired. Because 
the elevator remained in compliance at the time of the meeting, members of the public were free
