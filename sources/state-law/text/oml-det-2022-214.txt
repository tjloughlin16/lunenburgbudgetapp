The Open Meeting Law requires that public bodies “create and maintain accurate minutes 
of all meetings.”  G.L. c. 30A, §22(a).  These minutes must be reviewed and approved by the 
public body in a timely manner.  G.L. c. 30A, §22(c).  “Timely manner” means “within the next 
three public body meetings or within 30 days, whichever is later, unless the public body can 
show good cause for further delay.”  940 CMR 29.11; see OML 2018-48.

The 
Board does not dispute that it failed to timely approve the meeting minutes as alleged 
in the complaint, in violation of the Open Meeting Law.  The Town Administrator explained that 
on July 20, the Board approved and thereafter posted online most of the meeting minutes at issue 
in this complaint.  We note, however, that the minutes of the Board’s July 6, 11, and 20 meetings 
were not untimely as of when the complaint was filed with the Board on July 26, as the later of 
the next three meetings or 3 days had not yet passed.   
 
The Town Administrator also explained that minutes of the December 6, 2021, and 
Febr
uary 22, 2022, meetings could not be approved because only one Board member in 
attendance at those meetings remains on the Board.  Therefore, the minutes were posted online 
but not approved.  We take this opportunity to clarify that the Open Meeting Law does not 
govern the method for approving minutes.  Public bodies may choose the method for approving 
their minutes, including approval by the public body chair or another designee alone, by majority 
vote of the public body, or by consent of the body (approved unless there is an objection).  See 
OML 2022-85; OML 2019-107; OML 2019-25; OML 2017-34.  There is no requirement in the 
Open Meeting Law that only public body members in attendance at the meeting at issue vote to 
approve the minutes, nor does a lack of quorum of public body members who were in attendance 
during prior meetings constitute good cause for delay in approving minutes.  See OML 2019-25.

Board of its obligations with regard to meeting minutes.

The Open Meeting Law requires that a public body “create and maintain accurate 
minutes of all meetings, including executive sessions, setting forth the date, time and place, the 
members present or absent, a summary of the discussions on each subject, a list of documents 
and other exhibits used at the meeting, the decisions made and the actions taken at each meeting, 
including the record of all votes.”  G.L. c. 30A, § 22(a).  The minutes should contain enough 
detail and accuracy so that a member of the public who did not attend the meeting could read the 
minutes and have a clear understanding of what occurred.  See OML 2016-105.  Although a 
transcript of the discussions is not required, and the minutes do not need to include every remark 
or opinion presented, minutes must include a substantive summary of the discussion on each 
topic.  See OML 2020-57; OML 2018-8; OML 2012-29.  The Open Meeting Law requires that 
meeting minutes include more than a statement that a public body held a discussion about a 
specified topic; the Law requires that the minutes summarize the discussion that was held.  See 
OML 2020-57; OML 2019-167.  Minutes that merely contain a list of motions made and votes 
taken, without a summary of the discussions that took place, do not comply with the Open 
Meeting Law.  See OML 2022-195; OML 2020-53; OML 2019-157. 
 
For the reasons stated above, we find that the Board violated the Open Meeting Law by 
failing to timely approve meeting minutes.  We order the Board’s immediate and future 
compliance with the Open Meeting Law, and caution that a finding of a similar future violation 
may be considered evidence of an intent to violate the Law.  We also order the Board or a 
designee to approve the minutes of the December 6, 2021, and February 22, 2022, meetings 
within 30 days of the date of this letter, if it has not yet done so. 
 
 We now consider the complaint addressed by this determination to be resolved.  This
