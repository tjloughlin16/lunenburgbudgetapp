FACTS 
  
 We find the facts as follows.  On March 20, 2026, at 10:45 A.M., the Board posted notice 
of a meeting to be held on March 24, 2026, at 7:00 P.M.  The notice listed four main topic 
headings, under which additional specific item descriptions were listed that further advised the 
public of the topics to be discussed.  The notice also included a 15 minute public comment 
period to be held at the beginning of the meeting.   
 
 The March 24, 2026, meeting was held as planned.  After reciting the pledge of 
allegiance, Board Chair Peter Warren invited members of the public to speak.  Four members of 
the public spoke, including Roxanne Gunther.  Ms. Gunther expressed her concerns with the 
Town Administrator and mentioned the Town Administrator’s interactions with the complainant 
and attempts to discredit the complainant in 2022.  Board Members Patricia Oney and John 
Morrell both responded to Ms. Gunther’s comments.  Ms. Oney suggested that Ms. Gunther’s 
comments were disrespectful and inuendo, while Mr. Morrell stated that the “Town is lucky 
because of the way the [complainant] treated the [Town Administrator] that she didn’t sue the 
Town.”  After the conclusion of the public comment period, the Board discussed the topics listed 
on the notice.  
 
DISCUSSION 
 
The Open Meeting Law requires that, except in an emergency, “a public body shall post 
notice of every meeting at least 48 hours prior to such meeting, excluding Saturdays, Sundays 
and legal holidays.”  G.L. c. 30A, § 20(b).  The notice must be printed in a legible, easily 
understandable format and must contain the date, time and place of the meeting, as well as a 
listing of topics that the chair reasonably anticipates will be discussed.  Id.  The list of topics 
shall have “sufficient specificity to reasonably advise the public of the issues to be discussed at 
the meeting.”  940 CMR 29.03(l)(b).  A public body may discuss a topic that was not included in 
the meeting notice if that topic was not reasonably anticipated 48 hours, excluding weekends and 
holidays, prior to the meeting.
2  See OML 2020-172; OML 2019-131; OML 2018-119.3 
 
The complaint alleges that the Board, and specifically Mr. Morrell, violated the Open 
Meeting Law by responding to Ms. Gunther’s comments where there was no indication that the 
Board would respond to comments and therefore the matter was not listed in the meeting notice. 
The Board asserts, and we find no evidence to the contrary, that Ms. Gunther’s comments and 
the discussion that followed were unanticipated by the Chair 48 hours in advance of the March 
24, 2026, meeting.  The Open Meeting Law does not prohibit a public body from discussing or 
taking action on unanticipated topics raised by a person addressing the public body.  See OML 
2015-36.  As the discussion was not anticipated by the Chair, we find that there was no violation 
of the Open Meeting Law when Mr. Morrell responded to Ms. Gunther’s comments made during

the public comment period of the meeting without this topic having been included in the meeting 
notice.  See OML 2024-196 and OML 2022-100 (public body permissibly discussed an 
unanticipated topic that was raised by a member of the public during public comment, despite the 
topic not being included in the meeting notice). 
 
To the extent the complaint alleges that the Board discussed the complainant without 
providing her with specific notice, the Open Meeting Law does not require a public body to 
provide notice to an individual that they will be discussed at a meeting unless the public body 
will discuss the individual in executive session under G.L. c. 30A, § 21(a)(1).  See OML 2012-
85; OML 2017-12.  Here, the discussions involving the complainant occurred in open session. 
 
CONCLUSION 
 
For the reasons stated above, we find that the Board did not violate the Open Meeting 
Law.  We now consider the complaint addressed by this determination to be resolved.  This 
determination does not address any other complaints that may be pending with the Board or with 
our office.  Please feel free to contact our office at (617) 963-2540 if you have any questions 
regarding this letter.
