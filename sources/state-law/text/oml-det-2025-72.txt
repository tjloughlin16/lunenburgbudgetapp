This office received a complaint from Gary Winitzer on April 18, 2025, alleging that the 
Royalston Building Committee (the “Committee”) violated the Open Meeting Law, G.L. c. 30A, 
§§ 18-25.  The complaint was originally filed with the Committee on or about January 11, 2025, 
and you responded, on behalf of the Committee, by letter dated February 6, 2025, following an 
extension of time granted by this office.  The complaint alleges that the Committee violated the 
Open Meeting Law by posting a notice with an inaccurate start time for a meeting held on 
January 9, 2025.   
 
Following our review, we find that the Committee violated the Open Meeting Law in the 
way alleged; we further find that this was an intention violation of the Law.  In reaching this 
determination, we reviewed the original complaint, the Committee’s response to the complaint, 
the complaint filed with our office requesting further review.  In addition, we reviewed the notice 
and minutes of the Committee meeting held on January 9, 2025.   
 
FACTS 
 
We find the facts as follows.  The Committee generally meets the first and third Thursday 
of every month at 10:00 A.M.  On December 6, 2024, the Committee posted notice of a meeting 
to be held on Thursday, January 2, 2025, at 10:00 A.M.  On December 19, 2024, the Committee 
cancelled the meeting.  That same day, the Committee posted notice of a meeting to be held on

Thursday, January 9, 2025, at 7:00 P.M.  The notice listed five topics, including approval of one 
set of meeting minutes; updates on the possible demolition of the Raymond School, a generator 
for the Community School, and a building permit for the Raymond School; and discussion of a 
grant for door openers at the Town Hall. 
 
The Committee met on January 9, 2025, at 10:00 A.M., not at 7:00 P.M.  The Committee 
learned at the January 9, 2025, meeting that the posted time on the notice listed the start time of 
the meeting as 7:00 P.M.  Notwithstanding the incorrect posting, the Committee went ahead with 
the meeting, which lasted one hour.  During the meeting, the Committee approved the minutes of 
a meeting held on November 7, 2024, discussed the demolition and renovation of the Raymond 
School and the need for a building permit, discussed the lease of town land for a photovoltaic 
system, briefly discussed a request for proposals for a grant for door openers at Town Hall, and 
briefly discussed applying for federal funding for a generator at the Community School.  
Approximately ten minutes after the meeting concluded, the Committee posted a revised notice 
correcting the time of the meeting to 10:00 A.M.   
 
DISCUSSION 
 
The Open Meeting Law was enacted “to eliminate much of the secrecy surrounding 
deli
berations and decisions on which public policy is based.” Ghiglione v. School Committee of 
Southbridge, 376 Mass. 70, 72 (1978). The Law provides that notice of every meeting must be 
posted “at least 48 hours prior to such meeting, excluding Saturdays, Sundays and legal 
holidays,” and that every notice shall include “the date, time and place of such meeting, and a 
listing of topics that the chair reasonably anticipates will be discussed at the meeting.”  G.L. c. 
30A, § 20(b).   
 
We unde
rstand that the Committee typically meets on Thursdays at 10:00 A.M.  
However, the notice posted for the January 9, 2025, meeting listed the time of the meeting as 
7:00 P.M.  The Committee explains that in “updating the online posting” from a meeting to be 
held on January 2 to January 9, 2025, “the time field, which typically would not be touched, 
changed from 10 am to 7 pm.”  The Open Meeting Law requires that a public body post notice of 
every meeting in its official notice posting location containing an accurate and clear start time.  
See OML 2016-97; OML 2013-161; OML 2012-59.
1  We express our concern that, despite 
having received clear warning during the meeting that the notice included an inaccurate start 
time, the Committee proceeded with holding the meeting.  Even more troubling is the 
Committee’s effort to update the notice of the January 9, 2025, meeting to reflect the start time 
of the meeting as 10:00 A.M. after the meeting had already concluded.  This retroactive 
amendment is problematic and suggests an effort to conceal the original improper notice.  
Instead of amending the notice after the fact, the Committee should have adjourned and 
rescheduled the January 9, 2025, meeting for a future date when it became aware that the 
meeting notice included an incorrect start time.  See OML 2021-63; OML 2021-17; OML 2020-
107.  As the Committee’s meeting notice contained a start time that was incorrect by a difference 
of nine hours, we find that the Committee failed to post accurate notice of the January 9, 2025,

We must determine whether this violation was an intentional one.  See G.L. c. 30A, § 
23(c).  An intentional violation is an “act or omission by a public body or a member thereof, in 
knowing violation of [the Open Meeting Law].”  940 CMR 29.02.  An intentional violation may 
be found where the public body acted with deliberate ignorance of the law’s requirement or has 
previously been advised that certain conduct violates the Open Meeting Law.  Id.  On September 
27, 2021, the Attorney General found that the Committee violated the Open Law by failing to 
post a meeting notice with an accurate start time.  See OML 2021-135.  Moreover, we 
specifically advised the Committee that similar future violations could be considered evidence of 
intent to violate the law.  In addition, the Committee was advised during the January 9, 2025, 
meeting that the notice included an incorrect start time, but the Committee ignored this warning 
and proceeded with the meeting in deliberate indifference to the Law’s requirements.  Because 
the Committee had previously been advised by the Attorney General that failing to post a 
meeting notice with an accurate start time violates the Open Meeting Law and then ignored this 
requirement, we find that this violation is intentional.  See G.L. c. 30A, § 18; 940 CMR 29.02; 
OML 2021-135; OML 2016-69.   
 
CONCLUSION 
 
For the reasons stated above, we find that the Committee violated the Open Meeting Law 
by posting a meeting notice with an inaccurate start time.  Furthermore, we find this violation to 
be intentional.  We order the Committee’s immediate and future compliance with the Open 
Meeting Law.  Additionally, we refer this matter for a hearing pursuant to 940 CMR 29.07(3) 
and recommend that the Committee be assessed a fine of $500.00 pursuant to G.L. c. 30A, § 
23(c) (“Upon the finding of a violation, the attorney general may issue an order to . . . impose a 
civil penalty upon the public body of not more than $1,000 for each intentional violation”).  We 
invite the Committee to contact this office to discuss the hearing process or a resolution of this 
matter.  
 
Additionally, to remedy the lack of notice to the public, we order the Committee, within 
30 days of receipt of this determination, to ratify the vote to approve the November 7, 2024, 
meeting minutes at a properly posted future meeting.
