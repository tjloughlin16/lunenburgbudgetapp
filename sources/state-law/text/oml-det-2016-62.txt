RE: Open Meeting Law Complaint 
Dear Chairman Boussy: 
This office received a complaint from Susan Keeler, dated February 18, 2016, alleging 
that the Stoneham Board of Selectmen (the "Board") violated the Open Meeting Law, G.L. 
c. 30A, §§ 18-25. The complaint was originally filed with the Board on November 24, 2015, 
and, after an extension of time was granted, the Board responded by letter dated December 23, 
2015. In her complaint, Ms. Keeler alleges that the Board failed to create and approve meeting 
minutes in a timely manner. 
Following our review, we find that the Board violated the Open Meeting Law as alleged. 
In reaching a determination, we reviewed the original complaint, the Board's response, and the 
request for further review filed with our office. We spoke with legal counsel for the Board by 
telephone on April 20, 2016, and we spoke with the complainant by telephone on April 21, 2016. 
We find the facts as follows. On November 9, 2015, the complainant sent a letter to the 
Board that read, in pertinent part, as follows: 
Please have minutes from the following meetings made public: 
1) BOS meeting held on Monday, September 28, 2015 
2) BOS meeting held on Tuesday, October 6, 2015 
I am not requesting the minutes from the executive session meetings that were also held 
on those dates. Please have the open session minutes made public within 10 days of this 
request.

The Board generally meets twice a month, holding meetings on October 13 and 27 and 
November 10 and 17, 2015. The Board approved minutes of several meetings held during the 
September-October 2015 period during these meetings. However, the minutes at issue in this 
complaint were not discussed or approved during any of these meetings. 
This complaint was filed with the Board on November 24, 2015. On December 11, 2015, 
drafts of the requested minutes were sent to the complainant along with a letter stating that the 
minutes would be approved during the Board's December 15, 2015 meeting. The Board held its 
December 15, 2015 meeting as planned and, indeed, voted to approve the minutes at issue. 
Copies of the approved minutes were sent to the complainant on December 22, 2015. 
The Open Meeting Law requires public bodies to create and approve minutes of all 
meetings in a timely manner. G.L. c. 30A, § 22(c). The law does not provide a definition of 
"timely manner," but a public body should approve minutes at the next meeting where possible. 
See OML 2012-91. For bodies that meet regularly, we have concluded that a delay of two to 
three months in approving minutes does not comply with the law. See OML 2013-98; OML 
Here, the complaint concerns minutes of meetings held on September 28 and October 6, 
2015. Both were approved during the Board's December 15, 2015 meeting. Because the Board 
held multiple meetings before approving these minutes, we find that the Board failed to approve 
them in a timely manner. We credit the Board's statement that issues of staff availability made it 
difficult to create and approve minutes. Nevertheless, the Board is obligated to follow the law's 
requirements in this area. See OML 2015-43. 
For the reasons stated above, we find that the Board violated the Open Meeting Law. We 
order the Board's immediate and future compliance with the Open Meeting Law and we caution 
that similar future violations may be considered evidence of intent to violate the law. Because 
the minutes at issue have already been approved and released, we order no further remedial 
action. 
We now consider the complaint addressed by this determination to be resolved. This 
determination does not address any other complaints that may be pending with our office or the 
Board. Please feel free to contact the Division at (617) 963 - 2540 if you have any questions.
