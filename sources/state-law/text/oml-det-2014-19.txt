RE: Open Meeting Law Complaint 
Dear Attorney Riley: 
This office received a complaint from Gretchen Benkert, dated January 21, 2014, alleging 
that the Nahant Board of Selectmen (the Board) violated the Open Meeting Law, G.L. c. 30A, §§ 
18-25. The complaint was originally filed with the Board on or about December 10, 2013, and 
the Board responded to the complaint by letter dated December 19, 2013. In her complaint, Ms. 
Benkert alleges that during its December 5, 2013 meeting, the Board's meeting location did not 
comply with the Americans with Disabilities Act (the ADA). 
We reviewed the December 10, 2013 complaint; the Board's December 19, 2013 
response to the complaint; and the January 21, 2014 complaint filed with our office requesting 
further review. 
Following our review, we find that the Board violated the Open Meeting Law by meeting 
in a location that was not sufficiently accessible to all members of the public. However, we find 
that the Board took appropriate remedial action in response to the complaint. 
FACTS 
We find the facts to be as follows. During its December 5, 2013 meeting, the Board 
convened at its regular meeting location on a stage in an auditorium at Town Hall. The main 
floor of the auditorium is accessible to mobility impaired individuals. Historically, members of 
the public have joined the Board on the stage during its meetings. The stage is elevated 
approximately three feet above the main floor of the auditorium and is accessible by a flight of 
steps that is not wheelchair accessible. The complainant, who uses an electric wheelchair 
scooter, wanted to attend and participate during the meeting, but in order to do so, she had to be 
carried up the steps to a chair on the stage.

In response to this complaint, the Board relocated its regular meeting location to the main 
floor of the auditorium, which is accessible to all attendees. 
DISCUSSION 
The Open Meeting Law states that all meetings of a public body must be open to the 
public. G.L. c. 30A, § 20(a). Access must include the opportunity to be physically present as 
well as to see and hear what is being discussed by the members of the public body. OML 2014-
3; OML 2013-189; OML 2012-66. In addition, for a meeting to be truly "open to the public," it 
must be held at a location that is accessible to persons with disabilities. See G.L. c. 30A, § 20(a); 
see also OML 2012-49 (stating that meetings of public bodies must be held in locations that are 
compliant with the ADA). 
We find that the Board violated the Open Meeting Law because the meeting location was 
not ADA compliant, thereby impeding the public's access to the meeting. See OML 2014-3. 
However, the Board took appropriate action in response to this complaint by relocating its 
regular meeting location to the larger auditorium space below the stage which is accessible to 
everyone, including individuals with mobility impairment. We commend the Board's efforts to 
be responsive to the complainant's concerns. 
CONCLUSION 
For the reasons stated above, we find that the Board violated the Open Meeting Law but 
took appropriate remedial action in response to the complaint. We now consider the complaint 
addressed by this determination to be resolved. This determination does not address any other 
complaints which may be pending with our office or the Board. Please feel free to contact our 
office at (617) 963-2540 if you have any questions regarding this letter.
