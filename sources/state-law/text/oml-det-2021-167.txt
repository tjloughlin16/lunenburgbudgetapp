This office received a complaint from Stephen Morgan on March 22, 2021,1 alleging that 
the Ashland Public Safety Building Committee (the “Committee”) violated the Open Meeting

Following our review, we find that the Committee violated the Open Meeting Law by 
failing to timely respond to a request for meeting minutes and by failing to timely approve 
certain meeting minutes. In reaching this determination we reviewed the original Open Meeting

DISCUSSION 
 
The Open Meeting Law requires that a public body “create and maintain accurate 
minutes of all meetings, including executive sessions, setting forth the date, time and place, the 
members present or absent, a summary of the discussions on each subject, a list of documents 
and other exhibits used at the meeting, the decisions made and the actions taken at each meeting, 
including the record of all votes.” G.L. c. 30A, § 22(a). The meeting minutes must be reviewed 
and approved in a timely manner. G.L. c. 30A, § 22(c). The Open Meeting Law does not define

“timely manner.” According to the Attorney General’s Open Meeting Law regulations, “timely 
manner” means “within the next three public body meetings or within 30 days, whichever is 
later, unless the public body can show good cause for further delay.” 940 CMR 29.11. The Open 
Meeting Law imposes a continuing obligation to maintain minutes of all meetings. See G.L. c. 
30A, § 22(a); OML 2018-115.  
 
The Open Meeting Law requires that “[t]he minutes of an open session, if they exist and 
whether approved or in draft form, shall be made available upon request by any person within 10 
days.” G.L. c. 30A, § 22(c). 
 
I. The Committee Violated the Open Meeting Law By Failing to Timely Respond to a 
Request for Meeting Minutes.    
The complaint alleges that the Committee failed to timely respond to a request for 
meeting minutes made on February 10, 2021. Five out of the eight sets of meeting minutes 
requested were not posted to the Town’s website or otherwise provided to the Complainant until 
after the 10-day period allowed for in the Open Meeting Law. G.L. c. 30A, § 22(c). We remind 
the Committee and the Town that, unlike the Public Records Law which requires a response to a 
request for public records within 10 business days, the Open Meeting Law requires a response to 
a request for meeting minutes within 10 calendar days.  
 
II. We Decline to Review Allegations Related to the Timely Approval or Accuracy of 
the March 12, August 4, October 7, and November 4, 2020, Meeting Minutes.   
We decline to review the allegations related to the timely approval or accuracy of the 
minutes for meetings held on March 12, August 4, October 7, and November 4, 2020, as the 
complaint was not timely filed with respect to those minutes. To be considered timely, an Open 
Meeting Law complaint must be filed with the public body within 30 days of the alleged 
violation, or, if the alleged violation could not reasonably have been known at the time it 
occurred, then within 30 days of the date it should reasonably have been discovered. G.L. c. 30A, 
§ 23(b); 940 CMR 29.05(4). Because the complaint regarding the timely approval and accuracy 
of these minutes was filed more than 30 days after the minutes were approved, we decline to 
review them. See OML Declination 3-25-21 (Westminster Planning Board); OML Declination 
12-18-20 (Pembroke Board of Health); OML Declination 9-4-12 (Manchester-by-the-Sea Zoning 
Board of Appeals). 
 
Although we decline to formally review these allegations, we note that the minutes for 
the meetings held on March 12 and August 4, 2020, were not approved in a timely manner. We 
further note that although approval of minutes took place in open session, the minutes for the 
meetings at which the March 12, August 4, October 7, and November 4, 2020, minutes were 
approved either do not reflect that minutes were approved at all or do not identify the specific 
minutes that were approved. Review and approval of minutes must be accurately reflected in the 
minutes of the meeting at which the review and approval takes place. See OML 2019-115.

III. The Committee Violated the Open Meeting Law By Failing to Timely Approve 
Minutes for Meetings Held on March 20, 2017, and December 3, 2020.  
To the extent the complaint alleges that the minutes for the meetings held on March 20, 
2017; December 3, 2020; and January 29 and February 5, 2021, are inaccurate, we find no 
violation of the Open Meeting Law as the minutes were still in draft form at the time the 
complaint was filed. See OML 2014-62 (“A public body may revise draft minutes at any point 
prior to their approval to correct inaccuracies or add detail”); OML 2019-11; OML Declination 
12-18-2020 (Fall River Housing Authority Board of Commissioners). 
 
Although we do not review the substance of the meeting minutes at issue in this 
complaint for the reasons stated above and therefore find no violations with regard to the 
accuracy of those minutes, we take this opportunity to remind the Committee of its obligation to 
comply with the requirement to “create and maintain accurate minutes of all meetings, including 
executive sessions, setting forth the date, time and place, the members present or absent, a 
summary of the discussions on each subject, a list of documents and other exhibits used at the 
meeting, the decisions made and the actions taken at each meeting, including the record of all 
votes.” G.L. c. 30A, § 22(a). 
 
Next, with respect to the approval of the minutes for the January 29 and February 5, 
2021, meetings, we find that the Committee timely approved the minutes where the minutes were 
approved within the next three meetings. G.L. c. 30A, § 22(c); 940 CMR 29.11. However, with 
respect to the minutes for meetings held on March 20, 2017, and December 3, 2020, which were 
approved on October 26, 2021, we find that the Committee violated the Law where those 
minutes were not approved within 30 days or three meetings. Id.   
 
IV. We Do Not Find the Violations Found Here to be Intentional. 
The Complainant urges us to find that the Committee intentionally violated the Open 
Meeting Law. In particular, the Complainant asserts that the Committee has “the same chair and 
similar membership that has prior similar complaints/violations [as] BOS etc.” and that we 
should on that basis find that the Committee acted to intentionally violate the Law in this 
instance. An intentional violation is an “act or omission by a public body or a member thereof, in 
knowing violation of [the Open Meeting Law].” 940 CMR 29.02. An intentional violation may 
be found where the public body acted with deliberate ignorance of the Law’s requirement or has 
previously been advised that certain conduct violates the Open Meeting Law. Id. Here, we have 
not previously warned the Committee against the specific violations we find here, nor do we find 
other evidence that would support a finding of an intentional violation. We therefore decline to 
find an intentional violation. 
 
CONCLUSION 
 
For the reasons stated above, we find that the Committee violated the Open Meeting Law 
by failing to timely respond to a request for meeting minutes and by failing to timely approve 
certain meeting minutes. We order the Committee’s immediate and future compliance with the 
Open Meeting Law and caution the Committee that a determination by our office of similar 
violations in the future may be considered evidence of intent to violate the Open Meeting Law.
