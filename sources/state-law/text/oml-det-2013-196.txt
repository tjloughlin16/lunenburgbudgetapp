RE: Open Meeting Law Complaint 
Dear Attorney Corbo: 
This office received an Open Meeting Law complaint from Jeanne Golrick, dated July 
29, 2013, alleging that the Montague Board of Selectmen (the "Board") violated the Open 
Meeting Law, G.L. c. 30A, §§ 18-25. Specifically, the complaint alleges that eight sets of 
executive session minutes, publicly released on April 16, 2013, are insufficiently detailed. The 
complaint was filed with the Board on or about May 15, 2013. The Board responded by letter 
dated June 25, 2013, following an extension of time granted by our office. 
After reviewing the complaint and the Board's response, we have decided to resolve this 
complaint by informal action, in accordance with 940 CMR 29.07(2)(a). We find that the 
Board's minutes were insufficiently detailed, but that the Board took appropriate remedial action 
in response to the complaint by approving revised minutes that are sufficiently detailed. We 
therefore order no additional relief. 
The Open Meeting Law requires a public body to "create and maintain accurate minutes 
of all meetings, including executive sessions, setting forth the date, time and place, the members 
present or absent, a summary of the discussions on each subject, a list of documents and other 
exhibits used at the meeting, the decisions made and the actions taken at each meeting, including 
the record of all votes." G.L. c. 30A, § 22(a); OML 2013-47; OML 2013-16; OML 2012-29.1 
The complaint alleged that the minutes of the Board's November 10, 2010; April 25, 2011; May 
17, 2011; May 23, 2011; July 18,2011 and June 18, 2012 executive session meetings were

insufficiently detailed. In response to the complaint, the Board revised its minutes to add 
additional detail. The revised minutes identify the documents used by the Board during its 
executive sessions, but do not separately list those documents. We recommend that, in order to 
create the clearest possible record, minutes include a distinct list of documents and other exhibits 
used at the meeting, either at the end of the minutes or as a separate addendum, even if those 
documents are otherwise referenced in the minutes.
