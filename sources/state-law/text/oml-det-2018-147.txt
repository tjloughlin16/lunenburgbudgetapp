RE: Open Meeting Law Complaint
Dear Attorney Cipriano:
This office received a complaint from Attorney Ginny Sinkel Kremer, dated August 27, 
alleging that the Southborough Board of Selectmen (the “Board”) violated the Open Meeting 
Law, G.L. c. 30A, §§ 18-25.1 The complaint was originally filed with the Board on June 25, and, 
after an extension of time was granted by our office, the Board responded by letter on August 16. 
In her complaint, Attorney Kremer alleges that the Board failed to create and approve meeting 
minutes in a timely manner.
Following our review, we find that the Board violated the Open Meeting Law as alleged 
in the complaint. In reaching a determination, we reviewed the complaint, the Board’s response, 
and the request for further review filed with our office. We also spoke with the complainant by 
telephone on November 2.
FACTS
We find the facts as follows. The complaint concerns the minutes of several meetings 
held in January 2018. The Board held a meeting on January 2 and approved minutes of that 
meeting on January 18. The Board approved the minutes of its January 18 meeting on August 1. 
The Board held a meeting on January 30 and approved minutes of that meeting on July 17. The 
Board posted notice of two additional meetings, to be held on January 22 and 25, but those 
meetings were not held. The Board held numerous additional meetings in February, March, and 
April.
DISCUSSION
The Open Meeting Law requires that a public body “create and maintain accurate 
minutes of all meetings, including executive sessions, setting forth the date, time and place, the 
members present or absent, a summary of the discussions on each subject, a list of documents 
and other exhibits used at the meeting, the decisions made and the actions taken at each meeting,

including the record of all votes.” G.L. c. 30A, § 22(a). The meeting minutes must be reviewed 
and approved in a timely manner. G.L. c. 30A, § 22(c). To be timely, a body must create and 
approve minutes within the next three meetings or within 30 days, whichever is later. 940 CMR 
29.11(2).
Here, the complaint alleges that the Board failed to create and approve the minutes of all 
the meetings it held in January 2018. Because the January 2 meeting minutes were approved at 
the January 18 meeting, we find that the Board did not violate the Open Meeting Law with 
respect to those minutes.2 Next, because the noticed January 22 and 25 meetings did not take 
place, the Board was not required to create minutes, see OML 2018-36, and so did not violate the 
Open Meeting Law with respect to those allegations. However, the Board did not approve 
minutes of the January 18 and 30 meetings until August 1 and July 17, respectively, despite 
holding many meetings in the interim at which it could have approved those minutes. We 
therefore find that the Board failed to approve its January 18 and 30 meeting minutes in a timely 
manner. See 940 CMR 29.11(2) (minutes must be created and approved within three meetings 
or 30 days, whichever is later).
CONCLUSION
For the reasons stated above, we find that the Board failed to approve the minutes of its 
January 18 and 30 meeting minutes in a timely manner. We order the Board’s immediate and 
future compliance with the Open Meeting Law’s requirements and we caution that similar future 
violations may be considered evidence of intent to violate the law.
We now consider the complaint addressed by this determination to be resolved. This 
determination does not address any other complaints that may be pending with our office or the 
Board. Please feel free to contact the Division at (617) 963 - 2540 if you have any questions.
