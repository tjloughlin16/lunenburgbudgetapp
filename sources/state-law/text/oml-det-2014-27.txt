Re: Open Meeting Law Complaint 
Dear Attorney Coyle: 
This office received a complaint from Paul Cesan, dated January 24, 2013, alleging that 
the Southwick Board of Selectmen (the "Board") violated the Open Meeting Law, G.L. c. 3OA, 
§§ 18-25. The complaint specifically alleges that Board's December 6, 2010 and July 18, 2011 
open session minutes are inaccurate because they failed to include important information, 
including votes taken and motions made during the meetings. This complaint was originally 
received by the Board as two complaints, filed on or about November 30, 2012. We received 
your response on behalf of the Board in a letter dated December 20, 2012. 
We appreciate the patience of the parties while we reviewed this complaint. Following 
our review, we have decided to resolve this complaint by informal action, in accordance with 
940 CMR 29.07(2)(a). We find that the Board's December 6, 2010 and July 18, 2011 meeting 
minutes failed to contain an accurate summary of the discussion that occurred.1 The notes taken 
by the Board's recording secretary, and provided to this office by the complainant, show that the 
Board took votes and actions that were not included in the final, approved minutes. See G.L. c. 
30, § 22(a); OML 2011-55.2 For example, the notes from the July 18, 2011 meeting state that 
"DPW Bids except line stripping Approved A/R Unu," which suggests that the "DPW Bids"

were approved unanimously. However, this note about the bids does not appear in the approved 
minutes for the meeting. The same is true for the other information identified in the complaint. 
Although counsel assured our office on September 18, 2013 that the Board was working 
on amending its minutes to add the missing information, we have been unable to confirm that 
this process was completed despite numerous attempts. We therefore order the Board to review, 
amend, and approve the final minutes of its December 6, 2010 and July 18, 2011 meetings to 
include the missing information highlighted in the complaint. This should occur within thirty 
(30) days of receipt of this letter, and the Board shall certify to this office in writing that it has 
complied with the order. 
We now consider this matter closed. If you have any questions, please contact our office
