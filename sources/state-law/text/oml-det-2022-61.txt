 RE: Open Meeting Law Complaint 
 
Dear Mr. Toth: 
 
This office received a complaint from Robert May on December 9, 2021, alleging that the 
Templeton Capital Improvements Committee (the “Committee”) violated the Open Meeting 
Law, G.L. c. 30A, §§ 18-25.  The complaint was originally filed with the Committee on 
November 5, and you responded, on behalf of the Committee, by letter dated November 17. 1  
The complaint alleges that the Committee failed to post meeting minutes online for nine 
meetings held since September 29, 2020.2 
 
Following our review, we find that the Committee did not violate the Open Meeting Law.  
In reaching this this determination, we reviewed the original complaint, the Committee’s 
response to the complaint, and the complainant’s request for review.  We also reviewed several 
sets of the Committee’s meeting minutes for the years 2020 and 2021 available on the Town of 
Templeton’s website. 
 
The Open Meeting Law requires that public bodies “create and maintain accurate minutes 
of all meetings.”  G.L. c. 30A, §22(a).  These minutes must be reviewed and approved by the 
public body in a timely manner.  G.L. c. 30A, §22(c).  “Timely manner” means “within the next

Here, the complaint alleges that the Committee violated the Open Meeting Law by failing 
to post minutes for several meetings held in 2020 and 2021.  The Committee responded that it 
had minutes available for all of the meetings at issue though had not yet posted them online, but 
was in the process of posting them online.  As of the date of this letter, minutes are posted online 
for all of the Committee’s 2020 and 2021 meetings.  Moreover, our review of a sample of the 
meeting minutes found that minutes for each meeting were approved at the Committee’s next or 
second meeting thereafter.  The Open Law Meeting does not require that public bodies post their 
minutes to a website or elsewhere, but only that minutes be created and approved in a timely 
manner, and made available within 10 days of request.  G.L. c. 30A, §§22(a), (c); OML 2020-78; 
OML 2017-98.3  There is no allegation that the complainant requested the minutes and did not 
receive them.  Therefore, we find that the Committee did not violate the Open Meeting Law as 
alleged.  
 
Finally, although not raised in the complaint, upon review of several sets of the 
Committee’s meeting minutes we note concern with the content of the minutes.  The Open 
Meeting Law requires a public body to “create and maintain accurate minutes of all meetings, 
including executive sessions, setting forth the date, time and place, the members present or 
absent, a summary of the discussions on each subject, a list of documents and other exhibits used 
at the meeting, the decisions made and the actions taken at each meeting, including the record of 
all votes.”  G.L. c. 30A, § 22(a).  Although a transcript of the discussions is not required, and the 
minutes do not need to include every remark or opinion presented, minutes must include a 
substantive summary of the discussion on each topic.  See OML 2018-8; OML 2012-29; OML 
2011-55.  The Open Meeting Law requires that meeting minutes include more than a statement 
that a public body held a discussion about a specified topic; the Law requires that the minutes 
summarize the discussion that was held.  See OML 2019-167; OML 2018-8.  As an example of 
the minutes reviewed, the minutes of the Committee’s November 18, 2020, meeting document 
that the Committee approved meeting minutes and appointed the Committee’s clerk, and then for 
the remainder of the 2-hour meeting, “Bob McDonald of the Sewer Department presented his 
department requests and answered questions.  Bob Szocik of the Department of Public Words 
presented his department requests and answered questions.  A short break occurred right before 
Mr. Szocik’s second presentation from 5:43pm until 5:48pm.  Bob Szocik presented for 
Buildings and Grounds his department requests and answered questions.”  However, the minutes 
do not summarize any of those discussions.  We remind the Committee of its obligations in this 
regard.
