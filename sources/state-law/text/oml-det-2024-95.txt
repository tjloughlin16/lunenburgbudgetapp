Dear Angela Dolan: 
 
This office received a complaint from Patrick Higgins on January 14, 2024, alleging that 
the Swansea Library Building Committee (the “Committee”) violated the Open Meeting Law, 
G.L. c. 30A, §§ 18-25. The complaint was originally filed with the Committee on December 28, 
2023,
1 and you responded on behalf of the Committee by letter received in our office on January 
12, 2024. The complaint alleges that the Committee’s December 28 meeting was inaccessible to 
the public where the notice for the meeting included the wrong remote meeting access 
information. 
 
Following our review, we find that the Committee violated the Open Meeting Law as 
alleged. In reaching this determination, we reviewed the Open Meeting Law complaint and 
attachments, including notices for the Committee’s December 28, 2023, and January 8, 2024, 
meetings; the Committee’s response; and the request for further review. 
 
The Open Meeting Law was enacted “to eliminate much of the secrecy surrounding the 
deli
berations and decisions on which public policy is based.” Ghiglione v. Sch. Comm. of 
Southbridge, 376 Mass. 70, 72 (1978). At the core of the Open Meeting Law is the requirement 
that meetings of public bodies be open and accessible to the public. G.L. c. 30A, § 20(a) (except 
when meeting in executive session, “all meetings of a public body shall be open to the public.”). 
Generally, access to a meeting must include the opportunity to be physically present as well as to 
see and hear what is being discussed by the members of the public body. See OML 2020-100;

OML 2018-75; OML 2016-54.2 However, Section 20 of Chapter 20 of the Acts of 2021, An Act 
Relative to Extending Certain COVID-19 Measures Adopted During the State of Emergency, 
allows public bodies to provide the public with “adequate, alternative” access to meetings rather 
than physical, in-person access. See Chapter 20, § 20(b); OML 2021-135.
3 Adequate, alternative 
access may be provided through telephone, internet or satellite enabled audio or video 
conferencing or any other technology that enables the public to clearly follow the proceedings of 
the public body while those activities are occurring. Chapter 20, § 20(b).  
 
The C
ommittee posted notice for a meeting to be held on December 28 at 5:00 p.m. The 
notice stated that the meeting would be held via the Zoom platform and provided a link to access 
the meeting. The Committee has explained that the individual who created the meeting notice 
accidentally included on the notice for the December 28 meeting a Zoom link for a Committee 
meeting scheduled for January 8, 2024. The Committee further explains that the individual 
meeting invitations for the December 28 meeting sent to Committee members included the 
correct link and so Committee members were able to access the December 28 meeting, unaware 
that the link included on the notice was incorrect. The Committee held its December 28 meeting 
as scheduled.  
 
Although we credit the Committee’s assertion that inclusion of the incorrect link on the 
not
ice for the December 28 meeting was accidental and that the Committee was unaware that the 
public could not access the meeting, the fact is that the December 28 meeting was inaccessible to 
the public where the Committee had not provided the correct access information to the public. 
See OML 2024-63 (noting that the public was essentially locked out of a meeting where the 
public body utilized the waiting room function in Zoom but then did not monitor the waiting 
room for the duration of the meeting); OML 2022-180 (“[T]he Commission violated the Open 
Meeting Law when, despite learning that technical problems prevented the meeting from being 
streamed live via the Zoom link listed on the notice, the Commission proceeded with the 
meeting.”); OML 2017-83 (finding the meeting inaccessible where the door to the meeting 
location was locked); OML 2014-118 (finding that that meeting venue was not accessible where 
the main entrance was locked and there was no signage instructing the public how to access the 
meeting).  
 
For t
he reasons stated above, we find that the Committee violated the Open Meeting Law 
where its December 28 meeting was inaccessible to the public. We order the Committee’s 
immediate and future compliance with the Law’s requirements and caution that similar future 
violations could be considered evidence of an intent to violate the Law.
