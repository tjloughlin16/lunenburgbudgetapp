This office received a complaint from Amanda Alix on June 6, 2024, alleging that the 
Shutesbury Select Board (the “Board”) violated the Open Meeting Law, G.L. c. 30A, §§ 18-25.  
The complaint was originally filed with the Board on or about May 8, 2024, and the Board 
responded to the complaint by letter dated June 18, 2024.
1  The complaint alleges that the Board 
posted a notice with insufficient specificity for a meeting held on April 23, 2024, by failing to 
include the names of the individuals to be appointed to the Council on Aging and the 
Conservation Commission. 
 
Following our review, we find that the Board violated the Open Meeting Law in the way 
alleged.  In reaching this determination, we reviewed the original complaint, the Board’s 
response to the complaint, and the complaint filed with our office requesting further review.  In 
addition, we reviewed the notice, minutes and video recording of the Board meeting held on 
April 23, 2024, as well as a video recording of the meeting held on June 17, 2024.
2

The Open Meeting Law requires that, except in an emergency, “a public body shall post 
notic
e of every meeting at least 48 hours prior to such meeting, excluding Saturdays, Sundays 
and legal holidays.”  G.L. c. 30A, § 20(b).  The notice must be printed in a legible, easily 
understandable format and must contain the date, time and place of the meeting, as well as a 
listing of topics that the chair reasonably anticipates will be discussed.  Id.  The list of topics 
shall have “sufficient specificity to reasonably advise the public of the issues to be discussed at 
the meeting.”  940 CMR 29.03(l)(b).  We generally consider a topic to be sufficiently specific 
when a reasonable member of the public could read the topic and understand the anticipated 
nature of the public body’s discussion.  See OML 2019-70; OML 2015-35; OML 2013-39.
3   
 
The Board posted notice for a meeting to be held on April 23, 2024, at 5:30 PM.  The 
notice listed 12 topics, two of which were “Receive Concom member’s resignation and make an 
appointment to the Council on Aging” and “Select Board Interviews and Concom 
Appointments.”  The notice did not include the names of the individuals to be appointed to either 
the Council on Aging or Conservation Commission.   
 
The April 23, 2024, meeting was held as planned.  During the meeting, the Board 
accepted the resignation of a member of the Council on Aging.  Board Chair Rita Farrell stated 
that the Board had received a recommendation for the appointment of a specific individual to the 
Council on Aging, heard from that individual, and then voted to appoint her.  The Board then 
interviewed three individuals for two open positions on the Conservation Commission; all three 
individuals were present at the meeting location.  The Board discussed the terms of each open 
position, which were 14 months and three years, and Board Co-Chair Melissa Makepeace-O’Neil 
made a motion to appoint Jan Rowan to the 14-month position and then made a motion to 
appoint Bob Douglas to the three-year position, which motions were unanimously approved by 
the Board.   
 
We have consistently stated that the names of applicants for appointment or employment 
whic
h are known 48 hours in advance of a meeting should be included on a meeting notice. See 
OML 2022-217; 2019-24; OML 2016-150; OML 2013-163.  In its response, the Board explained 
that it “does not always have the specific information when posting the agenda, or know who 
will be able to attend” but did not specifically state that the names of the applicants were not 
reasonably anticipated 48 hours in advance of the April 23, 2024, meeting.  As such, we find that 
the notice for the Board meeting held on April 23, 2024, should have included the names of the 
individuals being considered for appointment to the Council on Aging and Conservation 
Commission.   
 
For t
he reasons stated above, we find that the Board violated the Open Meeting Law by 
posting an insufficiently specific notice for the April 23, 2024, meeting that failed to identify the 
individual candidates being considered for appointment to the Council on Aging and 
Conservation Commission.  We order the Board’s immediate and future compliance with the 
law’s requirements, and we caution that similar future violations could be considered evidence of 
intent to violate the law.
