Dear Ms. Cofske: 
 
This office received a complaint from Michael Doyle on September 2, 2022, alleging that 
the Brimfield Select Board (the “Board”) violated the Open Meeting Law, G.L. c. 30A, §§ 18-
25.  The complaint was originally filed with the Board on or about August 18, 2022, and you 
responded, on behalf of the Board, by letter dated August 30, 2022.
1  The complaint alleges that 
the Board did not allow individuals who attended the August 15 meeting virtually to participate. 
   
We resolve this matter by informal action, in accordance with 940 CMR 29.07(2)(a), 
after reviewing the original complaint, the Board’s response to the complaint, the complaint filed 
with our office requesting further review, and the notices and minutes of the Board meetings held 
on August 15 and August 29, as well as video recordings of those two meetings.2  We find that 
the Board did not violate the Open Meeting Law in the way alleged. 
 
On August 11 at 2:00 P.M., the Board posted notice of a meeting to held on August 15 at 
6:00 P.M.  The meeting was to be held in person at Town Hall and live streamed on Microsoft 
Teams.  The notice listed twelve topics, including a presentation on the feasibility of a new

Senior Center and “Directives for Building Committee.”  The notice further stated that public

The August 15 meeting was held as planned and Board members met in person at Town 
Hall.  The meeting was also broadcast via Microsoft Teams allowing members of the public to 
hear the discussions that occurred in real time.  During the meeting, the Board received a 
presentation on a new Senior Center, including possible locations, building design plans and 
overall costs.  The Board then discussed the Building Committee, the possibility of forming a 
new and separate Building Committee to review additional sites for a new Senior Center, and 
requiring the Building Committee to suspend further spending on any additional projects.  The 
Board then invited the Police Chief to speak about the Building Committee as he was co-chair of 
the Building Committee and was physically present at the meeting location in Town Hall.  
Michael Miller, another co-chair of the Building Committee, attended the meeting virtually and 
asked for permission to speak.  Although Board Chair Martin Kelly reminded Mr. Miller that 
only members of the public who attended the meeting in person would be allowed to participate, 
Chair Kelly eventually allowed Mr. Martin to speak.  Unfortunately, because of technical 
difficulties, Mr. Miller was not able to speak during the August 15 meeting.  The Board did not 
allow any other individuals, whether attending in person or virtual, to participate on either of 
these two topics – the Senior Center or the Building Committee.  
 
The Open Meeting Law does not require that a public body allow public participation in 
its meetings, but rather provides that “[n]o person shall address a meeting of a public body 
without permission of the chair, and all persons shall, at the request of the chair, be silent.”  G.L. 
c. 30A § 20(f).  As such, the law gives the chair of the public body discretion to decide who may 
speak or otherwise participate during a meeting.  See OML 2017-189; OML 2014-23; OML 
2012-78.3  Because it is within the Chair’s discretion to limit public participation during a 
meeting, he did not violate the Open Meeting Law by restricting individuals from speaking 
during the August 15 meeting even though those individuals may be members of, or acting as 
representatives of, other town boards or committees.  See OML 2021-84.  Moreover, the 
allegation that the Board only allowed public comment by those individuals who attended 
meetings in person would not constitute a violation of the Open Meeting Law.  See OML 2020-
114.  We do, however, encourage public bodies to allow for as much public participation as time 
and circumstances permit.  See OML 2019-120; OML 2015-12; OML 2014-129; OML 2012-59.  
Further, our office encourages civility during meetings of public bodies.  See OML Declination 
6-14-16 (Sterling Board of Selectmen).
