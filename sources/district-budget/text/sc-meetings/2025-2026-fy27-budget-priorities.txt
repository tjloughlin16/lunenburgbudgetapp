 FY27 Strategic Investment and 
 Accountability Plan (2026-2027) 
 Dear Students, Families, Educators, and Community Members, 
 When we look at our annual budget, we're not just looking at a spreadsheet of numbers, we're 
 looking at a plan for our kids' futures. This isn't just about spending; it's a  strategic investment  , 
 guided by our shared commitment to creating schools where joyful learning, equity, and success 
 happen every single day for every student. The priorities and principles below will guide how we 
 invest our resources for the 2026-2027 school year. 
 Part I: Strategic Investment Priorities (The "What") 
 These four core priorities drive our resource allocation decisions, defining the specific areas 
 where we expect to see the greatest return on investment for student success. 
 1. The Reading Superpower: Everyone Reading by Grade 3 
 Our Promise:  We are doubling down on a highly critical  skill: reading. We're committing to 
 ensuring every child develops the foundational literacy skills they need to become a confident, 
 grade-level reader by the end of Grade 3. This achievement isn't just a benchmark; it’s the 
 cornerstone of all future learning. 
 Where We'll Invest: 
 ●  Fully implementing evidence-based, structured literacy curricula and materials (aligned 
 with the Science of Reading). 
 ●  Funding for dedicated MTSS Specialists in every elementary school to provide targeted 
 help. 
 ●  Sustained, high-quality professional development for all K-3 teachers and 
 paraprofessionals in effective literacy instruction. 
 Measurable Goal (Key Metric for Success) 
 ●  Targeting a 10-point increase in the number of students meeting or exceeding 
 grade-level reading benchmarks on MCAS by the end of Grade 3.

 2. Customizing Success: Personalized Support and Rigor 
 Our Promise:  We believe in personalized success. Our  goal is to meet students exactly where 
 they are, providing the right support to overcome any learning hurdle, and the right level of 
 challenge to unlock their highest, unique potential. 
 Where We'll Invest: 
 ●  Allocating funds for specialized software and tools that make personalized learning 
 practical and help us track individual student progress data instantly. 
 ●  Expanding access to advanced coursework like AP, dual enrollment, and vocational 
 pathways for all students. 
 ●  Maintaining strong staffing levels for special education, advanced academics, and 
 academic tutoring to ensure low student-to-support staff ratios. 
 Measurable Goal (Key Metric for Success) 
 ●  Achieving a target of 60th percentile for student academic growth, in all tested 
 MCAS areas and grades, according to the state’s growth model. 
 ●  A 20% increase in the enrollment of historically underrepresented student groups in 
 advanced coursework. 
 3. The Power of Connection: Belonging and Inclusive Climates 
 Our Promise:  When a student feels truly safe, genuinely  valued, and connected to their school 
 community, they don't just attend; they thrive. We are investing in fostering truly inclusive 
 climates and an authentic sense of belonging that is felt by every single person in our district. 
 Where We'll Invest: 
 ●  Funding for our district-wide Anti-Bullying Curriculum and training for all staff members. 
 ●  Funding for an annual survey to measure students' perceptions of social-emotional 
 learning, with belonging being one of the key topics. The surveys provide a holistic view 
 of student well-being beyond academics.

 4. Supporting the Best: Investing in Joyful Educators 
 Our Promise:  Our teachers and staff are the true engine  of our schools—they are our greatest

 infrastructure.

 Maintaining a teacher and instructional staff retention rate of 90% or higher.  (80.2% last year) 
 Increase in student engagement metrics (e.g., attendance rates, participation in optional 
 school activities). 
 Part II: Operational Budget Principles (The "How") 
 These eight principles provide the financial guardrails and accountability necessary to execute 
 our strategic budget plan responsibly (Lunenburg Public Schools FY27 Budget Priorities). 
 1.  Keep the System Stable:  Balance within the appropriation; no new recurring costs 
 without offsetting reductions. Rebuild reserves where possible; avoid using one-time 
 funds for ongoing salaries.

 2.  Protect Core Classroom Experiences:  Maintain reasonable class sizes and preserve 
 student-serving positions first. Deliver consistent MTSS, targeted EL services, and early 
 literacy materials/PD, and support student belonging  . 
 3.  Fund Compliance, Safety, and Reliability Before Enhancements:  Prioritize mandated 
 transportation, health & safety (nursing, crisis response), facilities basics, and 
 cybersecurity. 
 4.  Make Disciplined Reductions & Pauses:  Freeze pilots,  and new programs; 
 consolidate software subscriptions. Hold vacancies not directly student-serving. 
 5.  Use One-Time Funds Only for One-Time Needs:  Target  small capital repairs, 
 curriculum adoptions/start-up materials, limited tech replacement (not base salaries). 
 6.  Maximize External Revenue and Offsets:  Fully claim  Circuit Breaker; align grant 
 spending (Title I/II/IV, IDEA) to core priorities. Strengthen governance of revolving 
 accounts. 
 7.  Plan for Known Risks and Contingencies:  Account for  OOD placements, utility 
 volatility, health-insurance increases, and contract settlements. Maintain a small 
 contingency line. 
 8.  Communicate with Radical Clarity:  Publish a clear budget book and a “Fund / Pause / 
 Plan” list for every major request. Provide predictable updates: “you said / we did,” 
 monthly variance snapshots, and school-level summaries.

 Part IV: Investment Rubric and Accountability 
 All budget requests will be vetted using this rubric to ensure they are necessary, strategic, and 
 fiscally responsible. 
 FY27 Budget Request Rubric 
 For each request, answer all five questions clearly and concisely: 
 Question  Detail 
 1. Is it mandated or 
 safety-critical? 
 Legal compliance (IEPs/504, EL mandates, state/federal 
 requirements) or Health, safety, and security (nursing, facilities, 
 transportation, crisis response). 
 2. Is it 
 student-facing and 
 instruction-critical? 
 Does it directly affect what happens in classrooms or student 
 learning/support? Does it directly support one of our strategic priority 
 areas (MTTS, EL, Literacy, Belonging)? Will students notice the 
 difference if this is funded (or not funded)?

 3. Is it recurring 
 (base) or one-time? 
 Recurring costs must fit within the appropriation. One-time requests
