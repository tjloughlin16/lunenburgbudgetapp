Safety
 
●
Nurse  Katie McGuire will handle  any/all COVID related concerns.
 
●
All PPE protocols and requirements must be observed.
 
●
Masks/face coverings will be required for all staff and students in all

mask breaks.)
 
 
●
There will be signs at entrances to the building reminding students of
 
the safety protocols that are in place to keep everyone safe. There will

between students in order to assist with distancing.
 
●

be monitoring each entrance upon student arrival and departure..
 
Students will use hand sanitizer upon entrance into the classroom.
 
●

Teachers will develop a schedule so only one cohort at a time accesses
 
lockers.
 
●
Bathrooms breaks for classrooms will be developed during common
 
planning first 10 days of school.

school building. Teachers will design a schedule that offers masks
 
breaks approximately every hour.
 
 
●
If a student is attending class and suddenly is in need of an individual
 
mask break, they will be allowed to enter the hallway, stand on one of
 
the blue “six feet apart” stickers, remove their mask for a quick mask

Classrooms
 
●
Students will be assigned to a cohort of learners. For 4th and 5th
 
grade, the first level of  cohort will be the traditional interdisciplinary
 
teaming of students (i.e, 5 Red, 5White).  During hybrid students will
 
further be split into cohorts to keep numbers of students in
 
classrooms small. These will be Cohort A and Cohort B. Cohorts should
 
generally have no more than 12 students.
 
●
Classrooms will have been disinfected before student arrival. Students
 
will have an assigned seat to keep up to six feet of space between
 
student and teacher.
 
 
●
 Upon exiting a classroom, students are dismissed by row, use hand
 
sanitizer, the teacher sprays the row that was occupied by the cohort,
 
row by row. The mask break allows the disinfectant to dry on surfaces.
 
The next cohort comes in and sits at assigned seats.
 
●
No more than two educators should be assigned to a classroom
 
cohort.
 
●
If a student violates the mask or social distancing guidelines, it will be
 
brought to the administration’s attention..
 
●
Students will travel to special areas with their homeroom assigned
 
cohort. Students will attend that special area class for approximately 8
 
days and then transition to a different special area- Ex: PE first 8 weeks,
 
Art8 days, Tech 8 days..
 
●
Physical education classes will have further guidelines in order to keep
 
students and teachers safe. Gym classes will happen outside when
 
weather allows, students will need to be 10 feet from each other if
 
participating in a strenuous activity. Equipment will be disinfected in
 
between cohorts of students. Classes may happen inside with
 
appropriate spacing and activity level for indoor gym.
 
 
 
Arrival &
 
Dismissal
 
Bus
 
●
When students arrive on the bus, they will be directed by an adult to
 
exit their bus two buses at a time.  For example, bus 1 and bus 10 will
 
exit the bus and enter the building. This staggered exit of the bus
 
===PAGE 3===
keeps large amounts of students from forming.  Face coverings must
 
be worn at all times.
 
●
Once off the bus, students will be directed to enter the building
 
through two separate entrances. Grade 4 will enter through the main
 
entrance  on the left, out the gym door, across the quad, up the stairs
 
and into their homeroom classrooms. Grade 5 and 3 will enter
 
traditionally.
 
 
Dismissal
 
●
Bus students will be dismissed first, by bus numbers in a staggered
 
fashion to allow for social distancing. After bus students are dismissed,
 
pickups will be seated in the cafeteria in assigned seats and dismissed
 
individually..
 
 
Pick Up & Drop
 
●
Parent drop will continue at the street corner.  Students will enter the
 
side entrance (by the cafeteria) and, if it’s before first bell, will take an
 
assigned seat in the cafeteria.
 
●
Pickup:  all pickup parents must have a yellow card that displays the
 
student’s last name in large dark lettering.  Staff will read the cards,
 
radio to the staff in the cafeteria, and students will individually walk
 
out to their vehicles. Please be patient as we adjust to this new
 
protocol.
 
Teaching &
 
Learning
 
●
The full scope and sequence of the curriculum will continue to be
 
taught in all subjects.  Social-emotional learning classes will also be
 
added to the specials rotation.
 
●
Hybrid instruction will be rich and rigorous in order for students to
 
engage in the curriculum.  New instruction must be included each

Work/activities/assignments will be graded traditionally, and grades

The Board of Elementary and Secondary Education (Board) on December
 
15, 2020 adopted additional amendments to the Student Learning Time
 
(SLT) regulations, 603 CMR 27.00, on an emergency basis. The
 
amendments establish minimum levels for live instruction and synchronous
 
instruction that districts operating hybrid and remote learning models must
 
provide. The amendments include the following standards, effective January
 
19, 2021:
 
●
 Districts and schools operating a hybrid learning model must
 
provide students with access to at least 35 hours of “live instruction”
 
over a 10-school day period, averaged across the grades in the
 
hybrid model. Live instruction means the combination of in-person
 
and remote synchronous instruction.
 
●
Students must have an opportunity to interact with educators each
 
school day, including a required daily “live” check-in between
 
students and educators
 
 
Synchronous learning, also referred to as synchronous instruction, is
 
defined in the regulations as “learning that is directed by a teacher and that
 
happens in real time with other students, such as during live, whole-class
 
instruction, and small group work.” Below are a few examples of what does
 
and does not count:
 
 
 
 
​
Counts
​
 as synchronous instruction:
 
●
Live online classes
​
 that the teacher leads for the whole class,
 
which may include breakout rooms for students to complete
 
tasks and activities with access to the teacher.
 
 
●
  
​
Livestreaming
​
 of in-person instruction accessed by remote
 
students.
 
●
 
​
Small group instruction
​
 while students are learning remotely.
 
In this model, a teacher is present online with their class,
 
working with small groups of students in turn while the
 
remaining students engage in independent or small group
 
work. Students should be able to signal to the teacher that
 
they need support.

Our intention at THES is for off-cohort students to essentially log on in the
 
morning and follow the regular day’s schedule; when mask/snack/bathroom
 
breaks or lunch/recess breaks are provided in school, students at home will
 
also have screen breaks.  Parents and students will be provided with grade
 
specific schedules; all content area subjects will be live-streamed.
 
Intervention blocks will prioritize in-person students, while at home students
 
will use individualized learning platforms, such as iReady or IXL.
 
 
 
Off-cohort students should be prepared in these ways:
 
●
Students will utilize an appropriate work space (desk or table).
 
●
Students will have headphones with a microphone ready to use.
 
●
Students will, when at all possible, keep their cameras on.  They can
 
blur, but not change, the background.
 
●
Students will use the “chat feature” only for questions and
 
connections to the topic.
 
●
Students will use the “hand raise” feature instead of
 
unmuting/interrupting.
 
●
Students will have all school supplies ready in their work space.
 
●
Students will arrive on time and stay for the duration of the class.
 
●
Students will adhere to the school dress code (pajamas, blankets,
 
hoodies that obscure faces are not appropriate).
 
●
Students will complete their classwork before the end of each class,
 
not “save it” for later in the day.
 
 
 
When students must stay home for quarantine or isolation, it is the school’s
 
duty to provide remote learning for students who cannot be in school for any
 
extended period of time.Students required to quarantine due to travel should
 
be tested upon their return and 
​
will be allowed to access remote learning
 
while awaiting a negative test result.
 
 
Students are still expected to attend school in person on their in-cohort
 
days. But if there is an excusable reason he/she cannot attend, they can be
 
marked as remote present, even on in-cohort days.
