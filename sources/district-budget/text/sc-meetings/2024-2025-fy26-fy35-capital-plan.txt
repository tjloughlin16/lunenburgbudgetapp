Priority 1
Primary School Feasibility Study for Entry & Drive Way Expansion  
FY26  -  $30,000
Project Number LPS23-05
Funding will allow for contracting a designer to review the feasibility of the following 
study areas:
● Massachusetts Avenue entry expansion options
● Current parking capacity during the school day/weekend events
● Expansion of the driveway and driveway parking to the school facility
● Evaluate emergency access Police, Fire and EMT vehicles with various capacity 
levels
● Review any environmental limitations related to wetlands
Benefits: The Primary School has limited parking access for residents during 
events; therefore, cars park on both sides of the driveway.  If an emergency vehicle 
were needed during that event, then the responders would be impacted by the 
significantly narrowed and in some spots impassible roadway.  This presents a 
potentially significant safety issue on the property.

Priority 5
Primary School Window Safety FilmFY26   $26,865Project Number LPS23-07
Funding will allow for installation of security film with anchoring caulk.  
CoolVu-Security 8M(72”x50’) – 8 Mil Clear Security (CV8M) 286 panes 
Benefits: 1) creates a barrier that does not allow the plate glass to immediately 
shatter allowing a person immediate access to the building or potentially 
significant injury; 2) decreases solar glare into the rooms thus decreasing the 
heat load while decreasing cooling costs (where applicable); 3) “tint” on the film 
does not impact line of sight from the exterior.
This technology is a low-E (low emissivity) R-value vs low-E, the two work 
together to create energy savings.  Reduces cooling cost by 30%; reduce up to 
79% of solar heat gain; block up to 99% of harmful UV rays; minimize glare by up 
to 83% (according to the manufacturer, 3M Window Film)
• Windows account for up to 28% of cooling load. - US Dept. of Energy 
• Window films can save as much as 19kwh/sq ft of glass.- CONSOL Energy 
• Window films typically payback in as short as 3 years. - US Dept. of Energy 
• 3M™ Window Film can reduce temperature by 9℉.- 3M studies

Priority 6
Replacement of Security Camera Central Server 
FY26  -  $ 40,000
New Project
Funding would allow replacement of the current box and adding a second box in a separate 
location for redundancy. 
Benefit: if one server goes down, the district still has access to camera footage. We would be 
capable of viewing camera feed in the event of a cyber incident/district-wide outage.  In 
other words - if the district is taken down for one full week, we would still be able to 
monitor camera footage. 
Cloud back-up still requires purchase of an on-premise proprietary box (camera feed does 
not go directly to the internet). There would be the added cost of recurring subscription fees. 
Negatives: 1) year-over-year cloud would be more costly; 2) in the event of an outage there 
would be no recording and no access to previously recorded footage.  
Useful life is approximately 12 years; central server is at end of support

Primary School Classroom Mini Splits (Phase II)
FY27  -  $483,000
Project Number LPS20-07
Funding in Phase II will provide: 1) new installation of ductless split systems for thirteen first floor 
classroom units with new Building Management Systems (BMS) integrated controls; and 2) new 
installation of BMS integrated controls to all Phase I 2nd floor units previously installed in the summer 
of 2024.   Phase I was approved at the November 2023 Special Town Meeting and completed summer 
2024. The BMS integrated controls are significant to Phase I and II because they connect all mini-split 
units through the building network. These BMS controls monitor, control, and optimize heating and 
cooling for the entire building. Without the BMS controllers, the mini-splits previously installed during 
Phase I were limited to the cooling only mode. In Phase 2, the ductless split system means that there 
will be 13 condensing units and 13 associated indoor wall mounted units installed.
Benefit: These units will provide supplemental heat during the shoulder seasons and cooling in the 
warmer months in a more energy efficient way and reduce heat load/stressors on the building. 
These units are eligible for energy rebates from Unitil.  Added electrical costs for operation will be offset 
to some degree by solar net metering credits.
Green Communities Eligible: Yes –submitted Oct 18, 2024 (fall 2024 grant 
round)

Turkey Hill Elementary School Classroom Mini-Splits
FY27  - $630,000 (estimate for 17 classrooms) 
Project Number LPS24-03
 
Funding  will provide: 1) installation of ductless split systems for classrooms and office/work 
spaces and 2) new installation of BMS integrated controls.  The BMS integrated controls are 
significant because they connect all mini-split units through the building network. These BMS 
controls monitor, control, and optimize heating and cooling for the entire building. The entire 
project would provide units in a total of 32 classrooms/work spaces.  This project will likely need 
to be done in Phases if Green Communities grant funding is the only source of funding.  Phase 1 
would prioritize installation of units in 17 classrooms.  An additional 16 classrooms/workspaces 
would be taken up in Phase 2.
Benefit: These units will provide much needed supplemental heat during the shoulder and 
heating seasons and cooling in the warmer months in a more energy efficient way and reduce 
heat load in the building.
These units are eligible for energy rebates from Unitil.  Added electrical costs for operation will 
be offset to some degree by solar net metering credits.
  Green Communities Eligible: We anticipate submitting in the fall 2025 grant round.

LMHS Turf Field, Site Survey & Drainage Improvements 
FY27 – $1.43million
Project Number LPS20-04-C
$920,000 (Hillside Hardscape & Drainage)
$510,000 (Track/Turf Replacement & Drainage)
This is a multiphase project.  Phase I was ARPA funded and is completed. 
Collins Engineering conducted an assessment of the complex (excluding the 
turf and track) in the spring of 2024.  Recommendations and cost estimates 
for Phase II  included in the report.
Benefit: This will result in improved safety for student athletes, spectators, 
and community members using the facility; reduce liability
This will become a safety liability. The turf is a much needed playing field 
and the community uses the track for walking.  Currently we struggle to 
accommodate all scheduling needs of LMHS athletics and youth sports 
organizations with this space as usable; will not be able to accommodate all 
users if this space is taken offline as a result of continued deterioration of 
safety conditions.  Will have to rent field space from an area high school, may 
permanently lose facility renters if they are required to relocate for space.
