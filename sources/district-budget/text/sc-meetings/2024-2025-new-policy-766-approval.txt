In order to educate students of compulsory school age (6-16) in Massachusetts, a 
private school must obtain the approval of the school committee of the city or town in which 
the school is located. The Massachusetts compulsory school attendance law, 766 Mass 
General Laws c. 76, § 1, sets the standard by which school committees must evaluate 
private schools.  
The School Committee may approve a private school when it is satisfied that the 
private school’s instruction complies with all legal requirements. Religious instruction shall 
not be evaluated as part of the approval process.  
Approval requires the following:  
1. The private school submits a letter of application to the Superintendent of Schools; 
2. The private school completes all items on the "Checklist for Approval of a Private 
School” and submits the required documentation;  
3. The Superintendent or designee reviews the submitted materials;  
4. The Superintendent or designee visits the private school;  
5. The private school makes a presentation to the School Committee;  
6. The Superintendent makes a recommendation to the School Committee; 
7. The School Committee takes formal action on the recommendation; and 
8. The private school is notified of the School Committee's decision.  
The private school shall have the burden of production for all documents related to 
approval. Failure to provide documentation shall result in non-approval. If substantial 
changes are made in the private school’s program, the private school must seek renewed 
approval. The District will confirm that it has approved the private school, the date which the 
private school commenced operation, and other basic information, such as the private 
school's street and e-mail addresses.  
Legal references: M.G.L. 40:4E; 71:48; 71:71D; 71B:4; 74:4 through 74:7A; 76:1
