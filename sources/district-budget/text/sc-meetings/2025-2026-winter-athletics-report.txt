Formal Report: Scope of the Athletic Director Role
Lunenburg Public Schools
Submitted by: Yanina Burns, Athletic Director
Program Oversight: Grades 6-12
Student Enrollment: 823 (with projected growth over the next three years)
Seasonal Participation Rates: - Fall 2025: 31% of students - Winter 2025: 29% of students
Date: December 08, 2025
Executive Summary
The purpose of this report is to provide the School Committee with a clear understanding of the scope,
demands, and critical importance of the Athletic Director (AD) position within Lunenburg Public
Schools. As student enrollment continues to rise and athletic participation remains strong, the
responsibilities of the AD have expanded to a level that cannot be reasonably or safely fulfilled in a
part-time or combined role. This report outlines the operational, administrative, supervisory, and
compliance-related duties that are required to maintain a safe, high-quality, and legally compliant
athletic program. Athletic Directors across Massachusetts consistently agree: this role is a full-time
administrative position requiring significant daily oversight, evening and weekend commitments, and
constant availability for emergencies and compliance needs.
1. Program Context and Organizational Needs
Lunenburg Public Schools currently serves 823 students in grades 6-12, with confirmed enrollment
increases anticipated. Athletic participation is already strong, involving hundreds of students across
multiple levels and seasons. The district fields numerous teams requiring year-round scheduling,
supervision, and support. High levels of student involvement combined with multi-sport offerings mean
that the athletic program is a major operational arm of the school system, touching a large portion of the
student body and representing the district in highly visible public contexts.
2. Core Responsibilities of the Athletic Director
A. Operational & Logistical Management
The AD is responsible for the day-to-day operations of all athletic programs, including scheduling
games, practices, officials, and transportation, coordinating dozens of moving parts simultaneously;
managing equipment procurement, inventory, reconditioning, and safety compliance for all teams;
overseeing facility use and resolving conflicts across school and community groups; and responding to
weather-related changes, cancellations, and emergencies in real time. These functions require daily
attention, precise communication, and immediate responsiveness.

H. Strategic Planning & Future Growth
To ensure growth and sustainability, the AD tracks participation and injury trends to inform future
planning; evaluates facility needs and equipment investments; plans and executes fundraising
initiatives; and builds long-term goals for program development and competitive success. Strategic
planning is essential as the student population and program demands continue to increase.
3. The "Invisible Hours" the Public Does Not See
The athletic schedule operates far outside traditional school hours: evening supervision of games
multiple nights per week; weekend events, tournaments, and playoff responsibilities; on-call obligations
for emergencies, transportation issues, weather changes, and safety concerns; constant
communication with officials, coaches, administrators, and families; coverage of practices and games
during holidays and school vacation periods. These expectations make it impossible for the AD to
maintain another major role (e.g., teaching or assistant principal duties) without compromising student
safety, compliance, or educational quality.
4. Statewide Feedback From Athletic Directors
ADs across Massachusetts provided written testimony describing: part-time AD positions consistently
result in burnout and rapid turnover (often every 1-3 years); athletic programs suffer when the AD is
also responsible for teaching or administrative duties, as both roles become compromised; many
districts have eliminated split roles after experiencing safety concerns, compliance issues, or loss of
qualified personnel. The consensus statewide is clear: a part-time AD model is not sustainable, not
safe, and not competitive.
5. Conclusion and Recommendation
The Athletic Director plays a vital role in supporting hundreds of students, supervising dozens of staff
members, and representing Lunenburg Public Schools in a highly visible context. The scope of daily
operations, safety responsibilities, and regulatory compliance demands the full attention of a dedicated
administrator. Based on the documented responsibilities, statewide professional testimony, and the
size and participation levels of Lunenburg's athletic programs, it is the professional recommendation
that the Athletic Director position remain a full-time administrative role. A reduction to part-time or a
combined role would create increased safety risks, threats to compliance with MIAA and state
requirements, declines in program quality and consistency, higher turnover and recruitment challenges,
and reduced student support and program visibility. Maintaining a full-time AD ensures program
stability, student safety, and the continued growth and success of Lunenburg Athletics.

Athletic Administrator Job Description
The role of the athletic administrator is to direct the interscholastic athletics program,
provide information, and serve as a resource to others, reporting to the school principal or
superintendent. The athletic administrator achieves these objectives by planning, evaluating,
developing, implementing, and maintaining services in compliance with established school
guidelines.
LEADERSHIP
• Establishes a vision and culture the athletic department abides by
• Evaluates the development of the education-based athletic program by utilizing local, state,
and national standards
• Supports the superintendent, principal, head of school, and school board goals and priorities
in compliance with all strategies, programs, policies, and procedures
• Develops, models, and enforces professional standards of conduct among staff members
• Coordinates, supervises, and leads staff professional development
• Recommends, informs, and administers policies and regulations pertaining to interscholastic
athletics
• Counsels with internal and external personnel (e.g. administrators, auditors, public agencies,
community members, sponsors, etc.) for the purpose of implementing and/or maintaining
services and programs
• Ensures all athletic department concerns are addressed in a timely manner
• Stays informed of legal standards in all areas under the athletic administrator purview such as
Title IX, sex discrimination and negligence cases
• Adheres to inclusive practices in recruiting, retaining, and hiring a diverse coaching staff
• SUPERVISION
• Oversees all athletic department and field maintenance personnel, including ticket takers,
scorekeepers, security, law enforcement and other personnel for all game events
• Supervises and directs the scheduling and contracting of contests
• Manages the approval, coordination, and hosting of all officials
• Ensures all coaches have completed required state and local requirements, including
certification in CPR/AED, sudden cardiac arrest, and concussion management
• Facilitates required training and mentoring for coaches prior to the season to protect student
athletes
• Ensures all coaches are properly trained in district policy and best practices

14 Legal Duties of Athletic Personnel
• Duty to Plan -written plans must be in place to fulfill legal duties imposed by the courts to
protect student-athletes from harm.
• Duty to Supervise - supervision is critical in both a general and specific manner. General
supervision is the oversight of student athletes before and after athletic activities and specific
supervision is actively watching student athletes while engaged in athletic activity.
• Duty to Assess Athletes' Readiness for Practice and Competition - evaluation of student-
athletes for preparedness to safely participate in a sport or activity requires a valid physical
release form for all athletes prior to sport participation.
• Duty to Maintain Safe Playing Conditions - equipment, facilities and playing surfaces are
inspected prior to participation daily and any deficits are corrected immediately before athletic
activity occurs.
• Duty to Provide Proper Equipment - all equipment should be properly fitted, clean, durable,
and sate. It is important all equipment must carry a National Operating Commission on Safety in
Athletics Equipment (NOCSAE) certification and all athletes must wear proper equipment when
exposed to contact in practices or competitions.
• Duty to Instruct Properly - instructors/coaches must be properly prepared to provide
appropriate and sequential instruction and to identify and avoid dangerous practices or
conditions.
• Duty to Match Athletes - athletes should be evaluated by maturity, skill, age, size, and speed for
participation in athletic programs.
• Duty to Condition Properly - personnel oversees appropriate conditioning levels to prepare
student athletes with progressive cardiovascular and musculo skeletal strength for athletic
competitions. Observing weather conditions and concussion policies are required.
• Duty to Warn - detailing and communicating risks related to participation in athletics to student
athletes and parents are essential. Written approval needs to be provided by parents and student
athlete prior to athletic participation.
• Duty to Ensure Athletes are Covered by Injury Insurance - Student athletes cannot participate
until proof of insurance coverage is provided and appropriately documented.
• Duty to Provide Emergency Care - athletic personnel are to be trained and certified in first aid
procedures, especially in CPR and AED applications.
• Duty to Develop an Emergency Response Plan - site specific plans for managing emergency
situations are required.
• Duty to Provide Proper Transportation - Bonded, licensed commercial carriers are to be used
for transporting student athletes to sporting events outside the school confines
• Duty to Select, Train, and Supervise Coaches - athletic personnel are to be professionally
trained and certified in specific fields.
For more information please view: A Profiles of Athletic Administration and/or Professional
Development Academy Handbook.
The value of NIAAA Certification
Becoming certified through the NIAAA is a valuable asset that schools prefer or require when hiring an athletic
administrator at the middle and/or high school level. Preferred certifications include:
• Registered Athletic Administrator (RAA)
• Registered Middle School Athletic Administrator (RMSAA)
• Certified Athletic Administrator (CAA)
• Certified Masters Athletic Administrator (MAA)
• Registered International Athletic Administrator (RIAA)
• Certified International Athletic Administrator (CIAA)
For more information about NIAAA certification, please visit our website at: niaaa.org

Athletic Trainers: Role, Certification &
Why the Position Cannot Be Combined
With an Athletic Director
Who Are Athletic Trainers?
Athletic Trainers (ATs) are licensed and certified health-care professionals specializing in the
prevention, assessment, treatment, and rehabilitation of injuries and medical conditions
related to physical activity. They serve as the first medical responder for athletics and provide
ongoing care during practices, games, and recovery.
What Athletic Trainers Do (NATA Standards)
• Provide injury prevention programs, conditioning guidance, and wellness education
• Evaluate and diagnose sports injuries and medical concerns
• Deliver immediate first aid and emergency care
• Create and supervise individualized rehabilitation programs
• Monitor progress, recovery timelines, and safe return-to-play
• Maintain medical documentation and injury reports
• Serve as the communication link between physicians, coaches, athletes, and families
• Ensure emergency action plans, safety protocols, and medical readiness of all events
• Oversee environmental safety (heat, weather, hydration, equipment, risk factors)
Required Training & Credentials
===PAGE 14===
1. Conflict Between Medical Responsibilities & Administrative Duties
• The AT must remain on-site for medical coverage at practices and games.
• The AD is required in administrative meetings, supervision tasks, scheduling,
facilities management, and event oversight.
• These obligations frequently occur at the same time, making both roles impossible to
perform safely or effectively.
2. Medical Attention Cannot Be Delayed
Injuries, emergencies, and evaluations require immediate response.
An AD who is in meetings, handling discipline, supervising staff, or managing operations
cannot step away without compromising athlete safety.
3. Legal & Liability Risks
• ATs are licensed medical professionals with mandated responsibilities under
Massachusetts law.
• Combining roles increases the risk of:
Delayed medical care
Incomplete injury documentation
Improper return-to-play decisions
• Violations of state practice laws
• This exposes the school and district to significant liability.
4. Burnout & Unsustainable Workload
• ADs routinely exceed 50-60 hours during active seasons.
• ATs have a similar time demand.
• Combining them would create a 100+ hour workload, resulting in exhaustion,
compromised decision-making, and unsafe conditions.
