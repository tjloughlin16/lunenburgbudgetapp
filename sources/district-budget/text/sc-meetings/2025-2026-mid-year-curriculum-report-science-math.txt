To:  Lunenburg School Committee  
 From:   Dr. Kimberly Sarfde 
 Date:   January 5, 2025 
 Subject:  Curriculum Overview: Mathematics and Science (K–12) 
The purpose of this memo is to provide the School Committee with a brief overview of the district’s 
mathematics and science curriculum across grades K–12. This update highlights current instructional programs, 
alignment to Massachusetts Department of Elementary and Secondary Education (DESE) expectations, and 
areas for future consideration. 
Across all grade levels, the district’s mathematics and science programs are aligned to the Massachusetts 
Curriculum Frameworks and reflect DESE expectations for high-quality curriculum and instruction. 
Instructional materials and practices emphasize coherence, conceptual understanding, reasoning, and 
application, consistent with DESE “look-fors” for content-area instruction. These include a focus on grade-level 
standards, opportunities for student sense-making, and access to rigorous learning with appropriate supports. 
In grades K–2, mathematics instruction is grounded in HMH Into Math. The program emphasizes number 
sense, conceptual understanding, and foundational skills through visual models, hands-on exploration, and 
structured discussion. Instruction focuses on helping students understand mathematical concepts rather than 
memorizing procedures, which is consistent with DESE expectations for early mathematics instruction and 
long-term student success.

Science instruction in grades K–2 is delivered through locally designed units aligned to the Next Generation 
Science Standards (NGSS) and the Massachusetts Science and Technology/Engineering Frameworks. These 
units emphasize curiosity, observation, questioning, and hands-on exploration, supporting early scientific 
thinking and language development. Looking ahead, the district may consider implementing the OpenSciEd K–
2 instructional materials to strengthen coherence and vertical alignment across elementary science, while 
maintaining developmentally appropriate instruction. 
In grades 3–5, mathematics instruction continues with HMH Into Math. At this level, students deepen their 
understanding through problem-solving, reasoning, and application to real-world contexts. Instruction aligns 
with DESE expectations by maintaining a strong focus on grade-level standards, multiple representations, and 
mathematical discourse. 
Science in grades 3–5 uses the OpenSciEd instructional materials, which are fully aligned to the Next 
Generation Science Standards and the Massachusetts Science and Technology/Engineering Frameworks. These 
materials are organized around real-world phenomena and integrate science and engineering practices, 
disciplinary core ideas, and crosscutting concepts. This approach supports DESE science look-fors by engaging 
students in investigation, explanation, and sense-making over time. 
In grades 6–8, mathematics instruction is delivered through EdGems. This inquiry-based program emphasizes 
conceptual understanding, reasoning, and problem-solving. Instruction encourages students to engage 
productively with challenging problems and explain their thinking, aligning with DESE expectations for middle 
grades mathematics. 
Middle school science uses Glencoe/McGraw Hill iScience, which aligns to the Massachusetts Science and 
Technology/Engineering Frameworks. The program incorporates hands-on investigations, lab experiences, 
literacy connections, and assessments to support student understanding and scientific reasoning. 
At the high school level, mathematics instruction follows a coherent sequence aligned to the Massachusetts 
Mathematics Frameworks and college- and career-readiness expectations. Students complete required 
coursework in Algebra, Geometry, and Algebra II, with additional options including Precalculus, Statistics, 
Calculus, Business Math, and Advanced Placement courses. Multiple pathways are available to ensure access to 
rigorous, standards-aligned instruction that supports diverse postsecondary goals.  
High school science offers a comprehensive program aligned to state frameworks and DESE expectations. All 
students complete coursework in Biology, with additional opportunities in Chemistry, Physics, Environmental 
Science, Anatomy and Physiology, Biotechnology, and Advanced Placement sciences. Instruction emphasizes 
laboratory experiences, scientific inquiry, and application of scientific concepts. Detailed course descriptions 
and graduation requirements are outlined in the Lunenburg High School Program of Studies. 
In summary, the district’s mathematics and science programs across all grade spans are consistent with DESE 
expectations for high-quality curriculum and instruction. Current programs support coherence, conceptual 
understanding, and equitable access to grade-level learning. Continued attention to vertical alignment, 
particularly in science, will further strengthen instructional consistency and student outcomes.
