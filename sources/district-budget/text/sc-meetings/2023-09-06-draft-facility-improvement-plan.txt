BACKGROUND 
During the February 15, 2023 School Committee Meeting, we were given notice that the Turkey 
Hill Elementary School was to be kept operational until at least the 2036-2037 school year. We 
have put together what we believe are the improvements that are required to meet that goal.  
 
On October 26, 2022 the MSBA Board of Directors approved to temporarily pause the Accelerated 
Repair Program (ARP) and not accept ARP Statements of Interest in 2023.  The ARP provides 
funding for the repair or replacement of roofs, windows and boilers in schools that are otherwise 
structurally, functionally and educationally sound. MSBA is accepting Statements of Interest for 
their Core Program, which is primarily for projects beyond the scope of the ARP, including 
extensive repairs, renovations, addition/renovations, and new school construction.

Area of Need: Asbestos Abatement 
Priority Need MEDIUM 
 
Current Condition:  
In previous years, there has been a plan to remove asbestos from the building.  Throughout the first 
floor of the building where new 2’ x 2’ suspended ceilings have been installed (classrooms, 
hallways & offices) and both stairwells, all the Asbestos Containing Material (ACM) spray on 
fireproofing has been removed and new fire proofing has been applied.  
 
The following rooms have ACM VCT flooring. Library & Librarian Office. These floors are 
covered with carpet. The cafeteria, cafeteria hallway and the music room are not covered by carpet. 
The VCT is in good condition and is inspected regularly for any deterioration and damage. 
All spaces that have 1’ x 1’ ceiling tiles and Gypsum Wall Board (GWB) ceilings will have ACM 
fire proofing spray above them. This includes all second floor classrooms, first & second floor 
storage rooms, Library & Library office.  Existing GWB ceilings are assumed to have ACM. All 
Pipe insulation is negative for ACM.  
 
 
 
Typical 1’ x 1’ Tile Ceiling     Tile floor between kitchen and cafeteria

Massachusetts Architectural Access Board: 
The degree of accessibility compliance required is established based on the cost of construction in 
relation to the “full and fair cash value” of the building. If construction costs equal or exceed 30% 
of the “full and fair cash value” of the building, the entire building must be brought into compliance 
with the accessibility requirements of the Massachusetts Architectural Access Board. Upgrades to 
parking, building access, elevators, and restroom facilities are among the most common 
requirements. Two key points to keep in mind when determining the degree of compliance required: 
The value of all work completed within a 36 month period must be considered and, depending on 
the municipality, the assessed value of the building may, or may not represent the “full and fair cash 
value” of the building. 
 
Currently, the assessed value of the Turkey Hill building is $6,113,700.  Thirty (30%) percent 
represents $1,834,110.00 over a 36 month period.  The district will be forced to bring the entire 
facility into ADA compliance.  
 
Exception: Whether performed alone or in combination with each other, the following types of 
alterations are not subject to 521 CMR 3.3.1, unless the cost of the work exceeds $500,000 or unless 
work is being performed on the entrance or toilet. (When performing exempted work, a memo 
stating the exempted work and its costs must be filed with the permit application or a separate 
building permit must be obtained.)  
 
1. Alteration work which is limited solely to electrical mechanical, or plumbing systems; to 
abatement of hazardous materials; or retrofit of automatic sprinklers and does not involve 
the alteration of any elements or spaces required to be accessible under 521 CMR. Where 
electrical outlets and controls are altered, they must comply with 521 CMR.  
2. Roof repair or replacement, window repair or replacement, repointing and masonry repair 
work 
 
Massachusetts School Building Authority: 
The MSBA does not limit the number of Statements Of Interest (SOI) that the district can submit; 
however, an SOI should only be filed for a facility where the district has the financial ability to 
proceed with a construction project at the time of submission. The district is only allowed to submit 
one SOI per school facility and should detail all of the facility’s deficiencies in that one SOI. 
An SOI should only be filed for a facility where a district has the ability to fund a schematic design 
within two months of invitation and a total project budget within 12 months of invitation.

with the potential to include additional systems as may be determined by the MSBA contingent 
upon available funding and capacity in the capital pipeline. Currently, the MSBA is not accepting 
submissions for this program until further notice.

CORE Program: 
The Core Program is primarily for projects beyond the scope of the Accelerated Repair Program, 
including extensive repairs, renovations, addition/renovations, and new school construction. 
 
IT Loan Program: 
The MSBA IT Loan Program will provide loans for public schools across the Commonwealth to 
support the student experience through the use of enhanced technology and increased broadband 
access.  The loan program will be working in collaboration with an existing information technology 
grant program, the Digital Connections Partnership Schools Grant ("DCPSG") program, that is 
currently being managed by the Department of Elementary and Secondary Education ("DESE") and 
the Massachusetts Office of Information Technology ("MassIT"). 
 
Through a collaborative effort across the agencies, the MSBA loan program will have $50,000,000 
available over a five year period. Loans will be given to approved districts, with funding up to a 
maximum of $2.5 million per district. Loans will be available to pay for IT infrastructure on the 
basis of need as determined by DESE with the total cost of procuring and installing the equipment 
determined by MassIT.
