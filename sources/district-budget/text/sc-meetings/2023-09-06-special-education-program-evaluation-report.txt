OVERVIEW
• In accordance with the District’s Three Year Program Evaluation Schedule, 
the District wanted to assess the adequacy of  Inclusion Supports offered to 
students, grade levels Kindergarten through Grade 8. 
2
===PAGE 3===
PLANNING THAT OCCURRED
• On 04/28/2023,  the evaluator met with the Director of  Special Services to 
review the Three Year Program Evaluation Schedule.  
• DRAFT Interview Guides for the Special Education Inclusion Teachers were 
developed by the evaluator and shared with the District for final approval. 
Questions were similar to compare and contrast responses, with the 
exception of  an additional question for the K-2 level.

INCLUSION SUPPORT 
Lunenburg Primary School
6
===PAGE 7===
Q1. What inclusion support do you currently 
provide?
• Inclusion support is provided at the Primary School in 2 classrooms at each 
level with the assistance of  a paraprofessional.  The Teacher alternates with 
the paraprofessional in each classroom. Pull out services for applicable 
students are provided by the Special Education Teacher during intervention 
class time.
7
===PAGE 8===
Q2A. What is going well with your delivery of  
inclusion services to students?
• Strong special education team who differentiates and shares resources, 
leading to student success
• New curriculum “HMH” (Houghton Mifflin Harcourt) for literacy and math 
instruction 
8
===PAGE 9===
Q2B. What is not?
• Special Education Teachers are not part of  the common planning time to get support from 
coaches on the new curriculum due to the need to cover classes
• Would like to see flexibility in supplementing instruction with other proven methodologies, i.e. 
Project Read, etc. 
• Paras get pulled to cover classes and thus are not able to meet student needs in inclusion setting
• There is not a consensus of  what specialized instruction is beyond modification of  materials
9
===PAGE 10===
Q3. What specific support and/or training would you like 
to receive from the Inclusion Coach that would assist you 
in your role in providing inclusion services?
• Staff  did not identify any specific support or training, but indicated a 
different point of  view would be welcomed

Q4.   If  you could change only one thing about proving 
inclusionary support, what would that one thing be?
The ONE THING noted by staff  is as follows:
• Common planning time – amongst the Special Education Teachers 
themselves, as well as common planning time with their General Education 
Inclusion Teachers.

Q6. FOR K-2 TEACHERS ONLY
What specific support do you need to help with the 
transition to a looping model?
Staff  identified the following:
• Common planning time
• How to maintain boundaries with having students for 3 years
• Teacher support for dealing with history of  difficult parents
• More hands on behavioral support when needed
• Assistance with developmental level of  Kindergarten since staff  are feeling not as 
competent/comfortable with this level
• Advocacy/periodic visits to classroom by Special Education Director

Turkey Hill Elementary School

===PAGE 15===
Q1. What inclusion support do you currently 
provide?
• For 3rd grade, the Teacher along with 2 paras support 3 classes. 
• The 4th grade Teachers works with 2 paras, across 3 classes with a Math and 
ELA focus.
• For 5th grade, the Teachers supports 2 classes with 1 para.  
15
===PAGE 16===
Q2A. What is going well with your delivery of  
inclusion services to students?
• If  able to plan, can co-teach with General Education Teacher, not just 
provide inclusion support.
• New curriculum “HMH” (Houghton Mifflin Harcourt) offers read aloud as 
an accommodation
16
===PAGE 17===
Q2B. What is not?
• Paras in inclusion classes are pulled to sub for General Education Teacher or 
to assist in the sub-separate program
• When Special Education Teacher is absent, there is no substitute provided
17
===PAGE 18===
Q2B. What is not?
• IEP meeting coverage takes place during science and social studies, resulting 
in lack of  support for students in these subject areas
• No common planning time between Teachers and paras; this year paras have 
gone to specials with students
18
===PAGE 19===
Q3. What specific support and/or training would you like 
to receive from the Inclusion Coach that would assist you 
in your role in providing inclusion services?
• How to juggle role – strategies for meeting student needs who need pull out 
services, while providing inclusion services 
• How to manage co-teaching with current role
• New curriculum – HMH is difficult for students with low reading levels – language 
based/moves fast;  need training and support on how to supplement when needed 
with other curriculum tools

RECOMMENDATIONS
1. Share the findings of  this report with applicable staff  and administrators.
Sharing with the School Committee on 9/6/23, Special Education staff  on 9/6/23, and Administration on 9/6/23. Julianna 
Hanscom can be available for any questions/discussions at Special Services building based meetings, administration meetings, or future School 
Committee meetings if  needed. 
2. Provide training/retraining to applicable staff  on the MA Dyslexia Guidelines
Training will be discussed at building based meetings in September and administration will work with staff  to provide PD as needed.  
This may come in the form of  district wide PD days, small group building based trainings, or staff  attending conferences/workshops. 
3. Following a review of  staff  responses as to what support and/or training they would like to receive from  the Inclusion Coach, he/she may also 
find it beneficial to meet with both the Teachers providing Inclusion Support and their General Education Partners to best determine how to 
provide support in each building.
This year the special education staff  is included in building based common planning times with general education staff. Additionally, 
if  the Inclusion Coach needs more time with special education staff  she can join the Special Services building based meetings that occur the 2nd
and 4th weeks of  the month. 
29
===PAGE 30===
RECOMMENDATIONS
4. Determine if  any of  the staff  identified “ONE THING” to change to improve the provision of  inclusionary services can in fact be changed or 
improved upon.
Primary School – Special Ed. Staff  are included this year in Common Planning Time 
THES – Building administration knows the concerns around pulling paras for coverage and teachers wanting more respect and will continue to 
work towards these goals. 
LMS – Building schedule influences the time factor for teachers, but administration is aware of  request.  More discussions will be had at building 
based meetings to try and trouble-shoot this area.  This year, there will possibly be the availability of  another small room in the guidance suite area 
(former SLP office) for additional small group work.   
5. Since the looping model for K-2 will be a new endeavor with existing concerns, questions, uncertainty etc. on the part of  staff, it is recommended that 
there be established periodic review sessions throughout next school year with the key players.  At these sessions, there would be an opportunity to review 
current status of  looping, what is going well, what changes need to be made, etc.
Special Services staff  and administration meet the 2nd and 4th Tuesdays throughout the year. Ongoing discussions and check-ins are welcome and 
can be added to the agenda.
