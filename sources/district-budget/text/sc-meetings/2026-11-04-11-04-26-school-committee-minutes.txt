8. Superintendent’s Report 
 
Dr. Fortuna reported that district administrators had begun visiting classrooms together to observe the work 
students were asked to complete, the questions teachers posed, and the opportunities students had to explain 
their thinking. Administrators compared observations after each visit to develop consistent expect ations for 
grade-level instruction and provide more useful feedback to teachers. 
Dr. Fortuna reported that five students educated in the district participated in the MCAS Alternate Assessment 
(MCAS-Alt) during the past year. Each student represented 20 percent of the group, so a change in one 
student’s performance could substantially a ffect year-to-year comparisons. Given the small group size and 
the need to protect student confidentiality, Dr. Fortuna did not present a separate districtwide analysis. She

9. New Business  
Action 
a. Public Hearing: Anti Bullying and Prevention Plan 
 
Chair Brzozoski opened the public hearing on the district’s Bullying Prevention and Intervention Plan and 
invited comments from those attending in person and via Zoom. Participants discussed how students and 
families distinguish bullying from peer conflict, how students learn where to report concerns, and why some 
students hesitate to seek help. Comments addressed the value of teaching appropriate behavior beginning in 
the elementary grades, access to social workers and trusted adults, and the importance of following up when 
a family raises a concern. Participants also discussed the effect of staffing reductions on the district’s ability 
to provide preventive instruction and support. 
 
Hearing no further comments, Chair Brzozoski closed the public hearing. 
 
b. Anti-Bullying and Prevention Plan 
 
Dr. Fortuna reviewed the distinction between repeated conduct that may constitute bullying, an isolated 
incident, and mutual conflict. She explained that staff must gather facts and respond to harmful behavior 
even when it falls outside the definition of bullying. The presentation also addressed cyberbullying, 
reporting options, family feedback, and steps to prevent retaliation. In response to a question about high 
school students, Dr. Fortuna described the use of student check-ins, attention to the times and locations of 
prior incidents, and no-contact directives communicated to staff. 
Members discussed the status of preventive instruction across grade levels and the relationship between 
students’ social and emotional well-being and their ability to learn. The administration would incorporate 
family feedback and update the plan to identify the district’s reporting application. The Committee deferred 
action until its next meeting to allow time for review of the proposed revisions.

e. Consideration of Special Education Reserve Fund Policy 
 
The Committee discussed how a reserve fund could help the district meet unforeseen special education 
expenses. Members considered the need for a written policy specifying how the fund would be financed, a 
target balance, the circumstances under which funds could be used, and the process for requesting a 
withdrawal. They reviewed another community’s policy as an example and discussed seeking input from 
the incoming finance director before proposing a funding amount. 
 
A motion was made and seconded to pass over the special education reserve fund article at 
Town Meeting. The motion passed by roll call, 5–0. Chair Brzozoski agreed to notify the Select 
Board of the Committee’s decision. 
 
f. FY28 Budget Priorities 
 
Dr. Fortuna presented a framework for developing the FY28 budget. She recommended protecting the 
district’s investment in instructional materials, allowing teachers time to plan together, and using literacy 
screenings, student work, and classroom observations to identify students who need additional support. She 
also distinguished ongoing operating needs from capital projects, including track and field improvements, 
primary school site improvements, and long-range planning for Turkey Hill Elementary School. 
Members requested that the priorities address social and emotional supports across grade levels, 
extracurricular activities, and athletics. They discussed the cumulative effect of prior reductions and 
expressed concern that another round of cuts would further limit student opportunities. Members favored 
presenting the cost of meeting students’ educational needs and explaining to residents what would be lost 
under continued funding constraints. The discussion was preliminary. The Committee took no vote on an 
FY28 budget amount. Chair Brzozoski supported the Superintendent’s emphasis on sustainable working 
conditions for staff and expressed concern that current conditions could not continue for another year.
