Lunenburg Public Schools

Project Title: 
Lunenburg Districtwide Instructional Implementation System Development Initiative: 
Institutionalizing Evidence-Based Literacy and Mathematics Instructional Infrastructure 
Anticipated Project Period: 
January 2027 – December 2028 (or upon release of federal funds) 
Funding Request: 
$300,000 
PROJECT DESCRIPTION 
Lunenburg Public Schools is facing significant fiscal constraints while simultaneously working 
to address persistent gaps in literacy and mathematics achievement. The district is projected to 
lose approximately $130,000 in Title I funding in FY2027, which will result in the reduction or 
elimination of intervention services and instructional supports for students most in need. Without 
strategic investment, the district will be forced to scale back instructional capacity while demand 
for academic intervention services continues to increase. 
Lunenburg Public Schools serves approximately 1,568 students (2025–26), of whom 25.2% are 
low-income and 12.5% identify as Hispanic or Latino. District assessment data—including 
mCLASS DIBELS 8 screening results and local accountability measures—indicate that a 
significant number of students require additional support in foundational literacy skills, 
comprehension, and mathematical reasoning to access grade-level content. On the Spring 2024 
MCAS, 39% of students in Grades 3–8 met or exceeded expectations in English Language Arts 
and 46% in Mathematics. 
Without a codified, districtwide instructional system to ensure consistent Tier 1 access to grade-
level academic content, variability in instruction increases the likelihood that students will 
require federally funded intervention services or be referred for special education evaluation. 
Strengthening Tier 1 instruction is therefore both an instructional priority and a fiscal protection 
strategy, designed to reduce long-term reliance on Title I and IDEA-funded services. 
Based on current trends, the district projects that strengthening Tier 1 instruction through a 
standardized implementation system could reduce the percentage of students requiring Tier 2 and 
Tier 3 interventions by 10–15% over three years, offsetting a significant portion of the district’s 
projected $130,000 loss in Title I funding while reducing long-term federal expenditures. 
In addition, strengthening Tier 1 instruction is expected to reduce inappropriate or premature 
referrals to special education by ensuring that students receive consistent, high-quality core 
instruction prior to evaluation. By improving Tier 1 access and reducing instructional variability, 
the district anticipates a measurable decrease in new special education identifications over time.

Even modest reductions in identification rates—particularly in high-incidence disability 
categories—can yield substantial long-term cost avoidance, as fewer students require extended 
specialized services, out-of-district placements, or intensive supports. Based on current district 
trends, this approach could result in avoided costs of up to $1,000,000 over time. 
This initiative represents a one-time, system-building federal investment to develop permanent 
instructional infrastructure—including fidelity monitoring tools, internal certification processes, 
and implementation protocols—that will be formally adopted and embedded within district 
curriculum and instructional systems. 
This investment is time-sensitive. Absent federal funding, the district will be required to make 
reductions to instructional staffing and intervention services for the 2026–2027 school year. 
Without this investment, reductions in intervention capacity will increase the number of students 
requiring federally funded services, further straining limited resources and increasing long-term 
costs. 
This project is not included in the President’s budget request and would not otherwise receive 
federal funding. 
No CDS funds will be used to procure curriculum materials, software licenses, or instructional 
products, and CDS funding will not support ongoing operational or administrative costs. This 
request does not fund recurring expenses and does not supplant existing Title II-funded 
professional development. All activities supported through this proposal are tied to system 
development and implementation and do not constitute general professional development. 
FEDERAL INTEREST AND NEXUS 
This project directly advances federal education priorities by protecting prior federal investments 
in Title I, Title II, and IDEA-funded programs; reducing long-term federal expenditures by 
decreasing reliance on intervention and remediation services; improving access to grade-level 
academic content for all students; and strengthening workforce readiness through improved 
literacy and mathematics outcomes. 
The project is aligned with the Massachusetts Department of Elementary and Secondary 
Education’s priorities related to high-quality instructional materials, evidence-based literacy 
practices, and equitable access to grade-level content. It is also highly relevant to similarly sized 
districts across Massachusetts facing declining federal allocations and increasing instructional 
demands. 
By strengthening Tier 1 instruction, this initiative reduces downstream federal costs associated 
with remediation, special education identification, and workforce retraining—ensuring more 
efficient use of federal education funding. 
PROGRAM DESIGN AND IMPLEMENTATION 
Through this initiative, Lunenburg Public Schools will develop and formally adopt the following 
standardized instructional systems: 
● Districtwide instructional framework aligned to the Massachusetts Curriculum

● Curriculum rollout protocols and implementation procedures 
● Internal trainer certification processes to build sustainable local capacity 
● Fidelity monitoring tools aligned to instructional expectations 
● Implementation guidance documents 
● Instructional alignment protocols across grade spans and content areas 
● Designation and certification of Standards Institute Implementation Fellows responsible 
for system development and sustainability 
To support the development of these systems, the district will engage an implementation partner 
with demonstrated expertise in evidence-based instructional systems (e.g., UnboundEd). External 
expertise is required due to limited internal capacity to design, codify, and scale districtwide 
instructional systems at the level of coherence and precision necessary for sustained 
implementation. 
The partner will provide targeted technical assistance directly tied to the creation of district 
deliverables, including system design, fidelity tools, and implementation protocols. This 
engagement is not stand-alone professional development, but rather structured, deliverable-based 
system development support necessary to build permanent district infrastructure.

TRANSFORMATIVE IMPACT AND PROJECT GOALS 
This initiative will: 
● Increase the percentage of Tier 1 instruction meeting “High Quality” criteria from 
baseline to at least 80% districtwide 
● Reduce the percentage of students requiring Tier 2 and Tier 3 interventions by 10–15% 
over three years 
● Improve alignment between instruction and state standards across all grade levels 
● Increase access to grade-level content for multilingual learners and students requiring 
additional support 
● Strengthen long-term instructional consistency and system coherence 
EVALUATION AND ACCOUNTABILITY 
Project success will be evaluated using: 
● Baseline-to-endpoint comparison of Tier 1 instructional quality 
● Reduction in instructional variability across classrooms 
● mCLASS DIBELS 8 benchmark growth 
● Reduction in students requiring Tier 2 and Tier 3 interventions 
● Trends in local literacy and mathematics performance data 
These indicators will directly measure the extent to which strengthened Tier 1 instruction 
reduces reliance on federally funded intervention services. 
SUSTAINABILITY 
All instructional systems developed through this initiative will be institutionalized within the 
district’s curriculum adoption and instructional review processes and maintained through 
existing local and Title II-supported structures. 
The district has demonstrated a strong track record of managing federal funds in compliance with 
Title I, Title II, and IDEA requirements and will ensure full fiscal accountability and timely 
obligation of funds. The district will obligate 100% of awarded funds within 12 months of 
receipt. 
No additional federal funding will be required beyond the project period. 
COMMUNITY AND REGIONAL BENEFIT 
This initiative will serve as a scalable model for districts across Massachusetts facing similar 
fiscal and instructional challenges. Lunenburg Public Schools will share implementation 
frameworks, tools, and processes with regional districts to support broader improvement in 
instructional quality and maximize the impact of federal education investments. 
FINAL STATEMENT 
This proposal represents a high-impact, fiscally responsible federal investment that strengthens 
instructional systems, protects prior federal investments, reduces long-term federal costs, and 
directly improves student outcomes at scale.
