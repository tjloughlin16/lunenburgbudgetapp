Activation Fund Concept Paper 
Lunenburg Public Schools 
 
Organizational Mission & Population Served 
Lunenburg Public Schools (LPS) serves approximately 700 PreK–Grade 5 students in Worcester 
County and is committed to ensuring equitable access to foundational literacy skills that are 
directly associated with long-term health, economic stability, and life expectancy. Increasingly, 
literacy acquisition must be understood not only as an educational priority, but as a critical social 
determinant of health. 
Children who are unable to read proficiently in the early grades are at significantly greater risk 
for long-term academic disengagement, reduced access to postsecondary opportunity, 
unemployment, and poorer physical and mental health outcomes in adulthood. Early literacy is 
directly linked to health-protective factors including access to preventative care, medication 
adherence, employment stability, and reduced risk of chronic disease. 
At present, the district is critically under-resourced to respond to this escalating need. While LPS 
continues to allocate limited local funds toward discrete literacy supports, these piecemeal 
investments do not address the underlying workforce capacity required to implement consistent, 
evidence-based foundational reading instruction across classrooms. The district does not have the 
available funding required to implement the professional learning and instructional coaching 
necessary to address this crisis independently. 
Project Description: Responding to the Public Health Crisis of Early Illiteracy 
LPS seeks $125,000 in Activation Fund support to partner with a vetted external organization 
specializing in early literacy professional development and instructional coaching to build 
educator capacity across our PreK–Grade 5 schools to address what is now widely recognized as 
a public health crisis: early childhood illiteracy. 
Research from pediatrician and former California Surgeon General Dr. Nadine Burke Harris 
demonstrates that exposure to early adversity and toxic stress disrupts the development of brain 
architecture responsible for language processing, attention, memory, and executive functioning. 
These neurological systems are essential for literacy acquisition. When these systems are 
compromised, children experience increased risk for cognitive impairment, reduced educational 
attainment, and lifelong health inequities, including elevated risk for cardiovascular disease, 
depression and anxiety disorders, substance use disorders, reduced life expectancy, and barriers 
to accessing preventative healthcare. 
Without structured, explicit, evidence-based literacy instruction, children exposed to adversity 
are more likely to experience persistent educational and economic instability that contributes to 
long-term negative population health outcomes. This research underscores the need for a 
coordinated public health response across sectors, including education, to address upstream 
determinants of lifelong health. Schools therefore function as one of the most critical

intervention points available to buffer the effects of toxic stress through structured learning 
environments that promote language development, cognitive regulation, and access to 
foundational literacy. 
In response, LPS proposes a one-year, capacity-building initiative in which an external literacy 
partner will provide targeted professional development for PreK–Grade 5 educators, embedded 
instructional coaching cycles, Tier 1 instructional strengthening for students identified as at 
elevated risk for reading difficulty, development of internal literacy leadership teams to sustain 
implementation, and the establishment of standardized instructional and progress monitoring 
protocols. This initiative is designed not to provide direct services alone, but to strengthen the 
district’s workforce capacity to deliver preventative, health-protective literacy instruction at 
scale. 
Impact on Organizational Capacity & Sustainability 
Activation Fund support will enable LPS to build sustainable internal instructional expertise 
necessary to respond to the public health implications of early illiteracy beyond the 12-month 
grant period. Grant-funded professional development and coaching will strengthen educator 
knowledge, leadership capacity, and districtwide instructional infrastructure to ensure that 
literacy supports continue without ongoing reliance on external intervention services or 
subscription-based platforms subject to cyclical funding uncertainty. 
Rather than continuing to rely on fragmented purchases of materials or environmental supports, 
this one-time investment will develop the internal workforce capacity necessary to implement 
preventative literacy instruction consistently across classrooms. This approach aligns directly 
with Activation Fund priorities related to discrete workforce capacity-building initiatives 
designed to improve organizational effectiveness and produce sustained impact beyond the grant 
period. 
Estimated Project Costs & Grant Request 
Total Project Cost: $125,000 
Activation Fund Request: $125,000 
Project Purpose: External partner–provided professional development and instructional 
coaching for PreK–Grade 5 educators to address early literacy as an upstream determinant of 
health. 
By treating foundational literacy as a modifiable upstream determinant of health and equipping 
PreK–Grade 5 educators with the tools necessary to mitigate the neurocognitive impacts of toxic 
stress, this initiative positions Lunenburg Public Schools to address early illiteracy as a public 
health crisis while advancing educational equity and long-term community health outcomes for 
children across Central Massachusetts in the absence of sufficient local funding to meet this 
urgent need.
