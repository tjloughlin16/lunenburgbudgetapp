LPS  Special  Education  Grants  are  for  supporting  Special  Ed.  Most  of  those  grants  are  yearly  and  recurring.  Do  we  use  those  grants  to  pay  for  staff?  If  not,  why  wouldn’t  we  use  some  of  those  grants  for  staff  since  staff  is  the  most  expensive  item  for  special  ed?  
The  decision  to  keep  staff  salaries  out  of  Special  Education  grants  is  a  strategic  fiscal  move  to  maximize  the  "buying  power"  of  every  dollar:  The  MTRS  Penalty:  If  the  district  pays  a  teacher  or  licensed  staff  member  via  a  federal  or  state  grant,  the  district  is  often  required  to  pay  an  additional  11%  of  that  salary  into  the  Massachusetts  Teachers’  Retirement  System.  Resource  Allocation:  By  keeping  staff  on  the  general  fund  (the  local  budget),  the  district  avoids  that  11%  surcharge.  This  allows  the  grant  money  to  be  fully  utilized  for  equipment,  specialized  technology,  or  contracted  services  that  don’t  carry  the  retirement  penalty,  effectively  keeping  more  money  in  the  district  for  the  students.  LPS   Should  we  join  the  procurement  of  custodial  contract  for  town  and  school?  As  part  of  our  ongoing  responsibility  to  manage  the  district’s  resources  efficiently  and  maximize  funding  for  direct  classroom  instruction,  the  administration  has  conducted  a  preliminary  review  regarding  the  potential  outsourcing  of  our  custodial  services.  While  fiscal  prudence  is  our  obligation,  we  must  weigh  financial  data  against  the  operational  realities  and  the  cultural  fabric  of  the  Lunenburg  Public  Schools.  The  contracted  custodial  industry  notoriously  struggles  with  high  employee  attrition  rates.  In  an  outsourced  model,  this  results  in  a  constant  influx  of  new,  unfamiliar  workers  entering  our  schools.  This  "revolving  door"  undermines  our  layered  security  approach.  Our  custodians  have  established  trust  and  rapport  with  our  student  body  and  educators.  They  are  familiar  faces  who  contribute  to  a  sense  of  safety  and  stability  in  our  buildings;  whether  they  do  this  by  greeting  each  student  personally  in  the  morning  or  playing  ukulele  for  the  students  at  lunch  time  or  creating  toilet  paper  snowmen  to  make  employees  smile.   District  employees  generally  hold  a  higher  degree  of  ownership  over  the  facilities  than  contracted  workers.  Our  staff  is  "part  of  the  fabric"  of  our  learning  community,  often  going  above  and  beyond  the  strict  definition  of  their  job  descriptions  to  support  school  events  and  student  needs.  A  potential  implication  of  this  process  is  the  disruption  of  ongoing  efforts  to  restore  trust  and
