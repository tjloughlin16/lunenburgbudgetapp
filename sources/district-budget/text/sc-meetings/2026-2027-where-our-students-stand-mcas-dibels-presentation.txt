Evidence-based reading is now the law
Evidence-based K–3 curricula
Districts must select K–3 reading curricula backed by scientific research 
and proven to improve reading, vocabulary, oral language, and 
comprehension.
Screening at least twice a year
Schools must assess K–3 reading at least twice a year, follow screening 
protocols for dyslexia, and report screening practices annually.
A free state K–3 curriculum
DESE must develop a free, high-quality K–3 literacy curriculum 
available to every district in Massachusetts.
Stronger educator preparation
The state will expedite reviews of teacher preparation programs for 
evidence-based literacy training and expand paid teaching 
apprenticeships.
Why it matters: Students who are not reading proficiently by the end of Grade 3 are four times more likely to leave high school without a 
diploma. In Lunenburg, more than half of K–2 students began this year below benchmark.
Sources: Office of Governor Maura Healey, June 26, 2026; Hernandez (2011), Annie E. Casey Foundation; DIBELS 8 BOY , 2026–27.

Starting early: implementing Focus on PreK
Strong evidence of impact
In a study of 2,018 children, Boston's pre-K program had moderate-to-large impacts on 
language, literacy, and math skills.
Greatest benefits for students who need it most
The largest gains were for students from low-income families, Latinx students, and students 
with disabilities.
Aligned with the grades that follow
Boston research links strong, aligned instruction from PreK into Grade 1 to gains in literacy 
and math that last.
