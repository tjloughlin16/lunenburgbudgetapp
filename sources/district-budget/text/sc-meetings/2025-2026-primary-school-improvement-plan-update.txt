Lunenburg Primary School (Pre-K–Grade 2) 
Continuous School Improvement Plan (2024–2026) 
Mid-Year Update 
 
This report provides the Lunenburg School Committee with a comprehensive mid-year update 
on progress toward Building Strategic Goals #1 (Literacy) and #2 (Mathematics) in the 
Lunenburg Primary School Continuous School Improvement Plan. It documents current literacy 
and math systems, instructional practices, assessment results, and targeted supports, and 
explains how mid-year data are being used to guide instructional decision-making for the 
remainder of the school year. 
 
School Improvement Plan Literacy Goal 
The Primary School’s literacy goal is to promote deeper learning of the HMH literacy curriculum 
and increase the number of students on the pathway to literacy proficiency through 
implementation of MTSS, with particular focus on students with IEPs and students scoring 
significantly below benchmark on literacy assessments. 
 
Core Literacy Instruction (Tier 1) 
Lunenburg Primary School prioritizes high-quality Tier 1 literacy instruction as the foundation for 
improved reading outcomes. Across Grades K–2, HMH Into Reading is implemented as the 
core literacy curriculum, aligned to research on early reading development. Instruction includes 
explicit, systematic instruction in phonological awareness, phonics/decoding, spelling, and early 
fluency; carefully sequenced lessons that build skills cumulatively; daily guided and independent 
practice; immediate corrective feedback; integration of reading and writing; and embedded oral 
language, vocabulary, and knowledge-building to support comprehension. These practices 
ensure all students receive consistent access to grade-level literacy instruction while 
establishing the foundational skills necessary for later reading comprehension. Mid-year 
implementation evidence indicates these Tier 1 systems are fully in place and operating as 
intended. 
 
Assessment and Progress Monitoring (Literacy) 
In alignment with the School Improvement Plan, mid-year (MOY) DIBELS benchmarking has 
been completed for all students in Grades K–2. DIBELS composite scores reflect overall 
reading risk based on multiple grade-appropriate literacy measures and are used instructionally 
to categorize students as Well Below Benchmark (high risk), Below Benchmark (some risk), At 
Benchmark (on track), or Above Benchmark (exceeds benchmark). Movement from red or 
yellow into green indicates an increased likelihood of meeting grade-level reading expectations 
when effective instruction and supports continue. Completed MOY benchmarking and nearly 
weekly PLC collaboration demonstrate intentional use of data to guide instruction and MTSS 
supports.

Mid-Year DIBELS Composite Results 
Kindergarten demonstrated strong positive movement from the beginning of the year to mid-
year: the percentage of students in the highest-risk category decreased from 39% to 30%, while 
the percentage meeting benchmark increased from 18% to 33%, consistent with expected gains 
from systematic early literacy instruction. Grade 1 showed an increase in the percentage 
meeting benchmark from 34% to 39%; mid-year benchmarks reflect increased rigor (particularly 
in decoding accuracy and automaticity), and these results are being used to refine instructional 
groupings and prioritize Grade 1 students for targeted support. Grade 2 results were largely 
stable BOY-to-MOY, with a modest increase in students performing Above Benchmark from 
24% to 27%; stability at this grade level is expected as literacy demands increase and 
instruction emphasizes accuracy, fluency, and comprehension of more complex text. These 
mid-year results are guiding targeted instructional adjustments for students who remain below 
benchmark. 
 
Use of Data to Guide Instruction and MTSS (Literacy) 
Assessment data are used instructionally rather than diagnostically. Decisions regarding student 
grouping, intervention intensity, and instructional focus are made collaboratively by classroom 
teachers, literacy coaches, administrators, and the Director of Teaching & Learning. 
Professional Learning Communities at the Primary School have met almost weekly to analyze 
literacy data, refine instructional groupings, and align small-group instruction to student needs. 
A structured deep data dive with grade-level teams is scheduled for the February 3 half-day 
professional development session to analyze BOY-to-MOY trends, evaluate effectiveness of 
current practices, and make targeted adjustments for the remainder of the school year; findings 
from that dive will further inform MTSS decisions and professional learning needs. 
 
Targeted Literacy Supports and Intervention 
When students require additional support beyond Tier 1 instruction, Kindergarten students may 
receive approximately 15 minutes of targeted literacy instruction, and Grade 1 and Grade 2 
students may receive up to 30 minutes of additional instruction. The frequency and intensity of 
support are determined by assessment data and progress monitoring, and these supports are 
aligned to the core literacy curriculum to ensure coherence and maximize instructional impact.

These targeted supports, combined with completed MOY benchmarking and PLC review, are 
contributing to the observed mid-year improvements, particularly in Kindergarten and Grade 1. 
 
Early Literacy Tutoring Partnership (Winter–Spring 2026) 
To further support students with the greatest needs, Lunenburg Primary School will participate 
in a state-supported Early Literacy Tutoring program. The school will receive 117 tutoring seats 
for students in Grades K–3, with Grade 1 prioritized. Students will receive short, daily, one-on-
one tutoring during the school day, delivered by Ignite, a state-approved provider. This tutoring 
is designed to complement, not replace, core classroom instruction, and student progress will 
be monitored to ensure alignment with classroom instruction and MTSS goals. 
 
Professional Learning and Instructional Feedback (Literacy) 
Ongoing professional learning supports consistent implementation of high-quality literacy 
instruction through literacy-focused PLCs, instructional coaching, classroom-based feedback, 
learning walks, and instructional sweeps. A job-embedded partnership with the HILL for Literacy 
beginning in February 2026 through June 2026 will further strengthen instructional capacity 
through in-class modeling and real-time feedback. These efforts are intended to sustain and 
deepen the gains seen in mid-year literacy data. 
 
Instructional Support Stipend (Literacy) 
To strengthen implementation of evidence-based instructional practices, the district proposes 
providing an instructional support stipend to a highly skilled interventionist with demonstrated 
expertise in literacy. All work funded through this stipend will be completed outside of 
contractual hours. The scope of work includes planning and facilitating PLCs focused on 
evidence-based literacy and math instruction; developing and sharing lesson plans aligned to 
district curriculum; modeling instructional strategies during scheduled prep periods; and 
collaborating with the Director of Teaching & Learning to plan and deliver professional 
development before school, after school, and during district professional development days. 
This role is intended to strengthen instructional coherence, improve educator practice, and 
reinforce alignment between curriculum, instruction, and assessment. 
 
Family Communication (Literacy) 
Families receive DIBELS reports following benchmark assessments, along with explanatory 
materials to support understanding of results and next steps. Additional communication is 
provided when assessment data indicates characteristics associated with dyslexia risk. 
 
School Improvement Plan Math Goal 
Deepen implementation of HMH Into Math and increase the number of K–2 students 
progressing toward proficiency, with priority given to students with IEPs and those who scored 
well below benchmark on fall i‑ Ready/Forefront USNS. 
 
Core Math Instruction (Tier 1) 
K–2 instruction is aligned to the MA Frameworks and HMH Into Math. Classroom practice 
emphasizes number sense, visual models, concrete manipulatives, hands-on work, and

structured mathematical discourse to build conceptual understanding and procedural skill. 
These Tier 1 practices are in place to provide consistent, coherent math instruction across 
grades K–2. 
 
Assessment and Progress Monitoring (Mathematics) 
All K–2 students were screened with USNS. MTSS deep data dives are scheduled three times 
per year; the next deep data dive is scheduled for Feb 3 during a half‑ day professional 
development session. Screening and progress-monitoring data are shared regularly to inform 
intervention groupings and instructional adjustments. Progress monitoring is used to guide 
interventions rather than to guarantee specific outcomes; ongoing monitoring and collaborative 
review are central to measuring short- and mid-term impact. 
 
Intervention and Staffing (Mathematics) 
A newly hired intervention teacher is delivering standards-aligned small-group instruction, 
co‑ planning with classroom teachers, and overseeing progress monitoring protocols. Small-
group interventions are aligned to core instruction to maintain coherence across Tier 1 and 
targeted supports. This staffing supports the early positive trends observed in short-cycle 
probes. 
 
Early Outcomes (Mathematics) 
Short-cycle probes indicate upward trends for many targeted Tier 2 and Tier 3 students, while 
overall K–2 readiness remains strong. These early trends are encouraging but do not yet 
constitute evidence of long-term proficiency gains; continued progress monitoring, coaching, 
and instructional adjustments will determine sustained impact. The combination of curriculum 
alignment, universal screening, and targeted intervention supports is producing promising early 
indicators while remaining appropriately cautious about projecting long-term outcomes. 
 
Use of Data to Guide Instruction and MTSS (Mathematics) 
Assessment data are reviewed collaboratively by classroom teachers, instructional coaches, 
intervention staff, and administrators to make decisions about grouping, intervention intensity, 
and instructional focus. Monthly PLCs and the scheduled Feb 3 data dive are used to analyze 
BOY-to-MOY trends, finalize small-group rosters, and set immediate instructional priorities 
based on evidence; these processes parallel literacy review structures and support coordinated 
MTSS decision-making across content areas. 
 
Targeted Math Supports and Intervention 
Targeted Tier 2/3 math supports are provided through standards-aligned small-group 
instruction. Intervention minutes and frequency are determined by assessment data and 
progress monitoring; groupings are adjusted based on short-cycle probe results to better meet 
student needs. These targeted supports, together with aligned Tier 1 instruction and intervention 
staffing, are contributing to the observed short-cycle gains.

Combined observations and next steps 
Implementation structures for K–2 literacy and mathematics are in place, with curriculum 
alignment, universal screening, and targeted intervention supports active and monitored. Mid-
year literacy data show clear improvement in Kindergarten, growth in Grade 1, and stable Grade 
2 performance as demands increase; early short-cycle math probes show positive trends for 
targeted students while overall readiness remains strong. Data are being used collaboratively to 
guide MTSS decisions and refine instructional groupings in both areas. It is too early to promise 
long-term proficiency gains; the Feb 3 deep data dive will provide updated screening and 
progress-monitoring results and inform any recommended adjustments to interventions, staffing, 
and professional learning. We will report updated screening and progress-monitoring results 
and any recommended adjustments following that review. 
 
School Culture and Climate Goal 
Building Strategic Goal #3:  Improve students, staff and families' sense of belonging by 
strengthening school culture and climate, implementing a social/emotional curriculum and 
learning about inclusive and culturally responsive instruction. 
Weekly communication continues to be shared with families every Friday. Parent/teacher 
conferences were recently held, strengthening family-school partnerships. Monthly school 
assemblies are conducted with a focus on a PBIS theme aligned to each month. In addition, the 
district continues its partnership with the Lunenburg Police Department through the Hanks 
Helper Awards program. 
Two principals and the SOR have completed ALICE training for staff at Lunenburg Middle High 
School, Lunenburg High School, and the Primary School. A training for THES staff is scheduled 
for February 3. Additionally, the Primary School partners with the Fire Department to provide fire 
safety training for all students from the ELC through 2nd grade.
