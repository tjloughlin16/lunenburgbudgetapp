Accountability, Growth, and Achievement  
Overview 
The new accountability system uses indicators that provide more information about the school and the 
opportunities for students.   Indicators include: achievement, growth, high school graduation, English 
proficiency (ELLs), and chronic absenteeism.  Information will be reported on each school’s performance 
against targets and other schools across Massachusetts.  The system will focus on raising the performance of 
each school’s lowest performing students rather than closing the gap.  Schools will be reported in two 
categories: (1) Schools without required assistance or intervention; and (2) Schools requiring assistance or 
intervention. 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
Student Growth Percentile (SGP) measures a student’s progress on MCAS compared to the progress of 
other students with similar MCAS performance histories.  SGPs range from 1 to 99, where higher numbers 
represent higher relative growth and lower numbers represent lower growth.  A student who grew at the 70th 
percentile had a higher MCAS score than 70% of the students who performed like him or her in the past. 
 
Since the introduction of student growth as an assessment metric in 2008, median SGPs have been the main 
statistic used for these summaries representing the middle value of each group.  However, recent research1 has 
shown that means are more sensitive and representative than medians in describing group performance, and 
align better with the DESE’s guiding philosophy that all students contribute to accountability results.  As a 
result, this year the first SGPs based on both baseline and comparison scores from the next-generation tests 
introduced in 2017 will be reported for students in grades 4 through 8.   
 
Achievement is scored in four categories. Grades 3-8 have taken the Next-Generation MCAS since 2017.  The 
performance categories of the Next-Generation MCAS test are: 
• Not Meeting Standards (440) 
• Partially Meeting Standards (470) 
• Meeting Standards (500) 
• Exceeding Standards (530-560) 
 
Grade 10 students continue to take the MCAS ELA and Math Legacy tests.  The Science MCAS test is also the 
Legacy test and is administered in grades 5, 8, and high school.  The Legacy test has four performance 
categories: 
• Failing (200) 
• Needs Improvement (220) 
• Proficient (240) 
• Advanced (260-280)

Growth & Achievement 
 
Growth and Achievement Data can be reported on the same chart.   The graphs that follow are a 
representation of two measures:  mean student growth on the horizontal (x) axis and student 
achievement based on % Meeting/Exceeding Standards (% Proficient/Advanced) on the vertical (y) 
axis.  This information is provided for ELA and Mathematics at each of the tested grades  and is 
compared to the State (represented by an “X”).  A mean SGP between 40 -60% is considered within 
the average range and is framed on the graphs  below by two vertical dashed-lines.   Student 
achievement is also shown on this graph based on the vertical placement on the grade- level dots.  
Grade 3 does not appear on this graph because students must have two consecutive testing years to be 
assigned a student growth percentile (SGP).  Similarly, there are no graph s for Science, Technology, 
and Engineering.  As this test is not administered in consecutive grades, there is not student growth 
percentile (SGP).

2018 MCAS-AL T Assessment        
Based on Every Student Succeeds Act (ESSA), beginning in 2016-17, the number of students participating in 
MCAS-Alt assessments can not exceed 1% of all students.  In Spring 2017, Lunenburg had 10 students 
participating in MCAS-Alt ELA and 12 students participating in MCAS-Alt Math assessments.  In Spring 2018, 
Lunenburg had 4 students participating in MCAS-Alt ELA and 4 students participating in MCAS-Alt Math 
assessments. 
Lunenburg students’ 2018 MCAS-ALT portfolios were scored in the following categories: 
• Awareness: The student demonstrates very little understanding of learning standards in the 
Massachusetts Curriculum Frameworks in the content area. [0 students] 
• Emerging: The student demonstrates simple understanding of a limited number of learning standards 
in the Frameworks in the content area, below grade-level expectations.  [3 students]     
• Progressing: The student demonstrates partial understanding of a limited number of learning 
standards in the Frameworks in the content area, below grade-level expectations. [4 students] 
• Needs Improvement: The student demonstrates partial understanding of subject matter in the 
Frameworks at grade-level expectations (This performance level is considered the minimum passing 
score on the MCAS) [0 students] 
• Incomplete: assessment was not submitted in the portfolio; or, two or more of the three required 
strands in a content area did not contain sufficient information to determine a score in Demonstration 
of Skills and Concepts and Independence.  [1 student]

Next Steps 
 
Principals have provided MCAS data to staff for additional in-depth analysis.  Data is available for teachers to 
examine results reported by curriculum standards, question type, and by test questions (if released by DESE).  
Teachers can look at data by school, grade, class, and individual student level. 
 
The data analysis conducted at the district leadership level indicates that there is much work to do to continue 
working to increase the achievement and growth of our Students with Disabilities subgroup.  In grade 4 and 5 
ELA, we need to focus resources on essay writing, specifically on conventions, text types and purposes.  In 
Math, we need to focus instruction in grades 3 and 4 on multiplication and fractions; and in grade 6 on 
equations and inequalities.  
 
The data will be used to measure progress on educator and school goals, inform decision-making on 
curriculum material, professional development, and staffing needs.  The data will be used to justify and 
prioritize FY20 budget requests.
