MEMORIAM
NORMAN R. PAGE
10/28/29 - 2/13/98
Norman Page was born on October 28, 1929 and was a lifelong resident of
Lunenburg. He was the son of retired Police Chief Chester W. Page and his
wife Evelyn. Norman was a 49-year member of the Lunenburg Fire
Department, retiring in 1993. He also was an active member of the
Lunenburg Firefighters' Association. Norman also worked at Simonds Saw
and Steel for 34 years as a cost estimator before retirement. In addition to
his firefighting duties, he maintained a position of Emergency Services
Dispatcher for the Fire and Police Departments. He held this position for 40 years. He was an
Army veteran serving in Korea and held membership in various other civic and fraternal
organizations in the area.
THOMAS E. MORTIS
11/23/16 - 3/21/98
Thomas E. Mortis lived in Lunenburg for most of his life and owned and
operated the former Thomas E. Mortis Plumbing and Heating from 1954 to
1979. He was a member of the Plumbers Union, Local No. 4 in Worcester
for 42 years. He served as plumbing and gas inspector for the Town of
Lunenburg for 36 years, and had been the plumbing inspector for the Town
of Shirley for the past 17 years, and had assisted the plumbing inspectors in
Leominster for 26 years. Tom also served on the Advisory Plumbing Board
of Montachusett Regional Vocational Technical High School in Fitchburg.

BOARD OF SELECTMEN/SEWER COMMISSIONERS
The Board of Selectmen acting in their capacity as Sewer Commissioners attended numerous
meetings with the Leominster City Council throughout the year and expect that an
Intermunicipal Agreement between the two communities will be signed in the Spring of 1999.
The sewer design phase began in November 1998 with surveys, borings and wetland flagging
being done along specified routes. The community should be aware that a number of
subcontractors will be out along the roadways and may request access to private property during
this design phase. Should you have any questions on the status of the project please contact
this office at 582-4144. The Board also anticipates that a vote on the construction phase will
occur sometime in 1999.
In an effort to maintain the Towns aging facilities and to keep up with the ever changing field of
technology, the Board of Selectmen entered into a Memorandum of Understanding with the
School Committee to hire a Director of Facilities and Maintenance, John Londa and a
Technology Coordinator, Richard Mobley. This measure has resulted in a consolidation of
services between all Town Departments and the School Department. The Board expects to
realize the cost effectiveness of this endeavor over the long term, and would like to welcome
John and Rick on board.
The Board has been advised from the Massachusetts Highway Department that the Route 13
widening and reconstruction project from the Leominster/Lunenburg town line to the
intersection of Routes 2A/13 has been approved and placed on the Transportation Improvement
Program for FY 01. It is anticipated that the Signalization of Whalom Road and Route 13
(Electric Ave.) will be completed in 1999 subject to federal/state funding.
In an effort to keep the lines of communication open among all Departments, Boards,
Committees and Commissions, the Board of Selectmen have been scheduling monthly
department head meetings. In conjunction with these meetings, the Town has hosted seminars
on the Conflict of Interest Law and Open Meeting Law, which were presented by the Inspector
Generals Office and District Attorneys Office.
On Monday, November 16th the "retired" bandstand gracefully moved from the Lower Common
to its new site on 94 Main Street. Members of the Gazebo Committee, as well as others, joined
in the parade up Main Street to it's new home. The bandstand was purchased by Robert Ebersole
and will complement the gracious Ebersole home as it is a perfect match for the old 1905
bandstand with the dignified house built prior to the Civil War. The Gazebo Committee worked
very hard with the local builder, Richard L. Paquette, in designing the architectural structure of
the new Gazebo. The Gazebo Committee is proud of the fact that this project is being
completely funded through private donations. The Committee is now in the final phase which is
the landscape design phase. In an effort to raise money for the design, the Committee is
sponsoring an Engraved Brick Sale. Purchasing a brick is an opportunity to remember loved
ones, or honor significant persons, as you contribute a lasting gift for future generations. The
members of the committee are: Norma B. Frost, Lisa A. Normandin, Linda H. Douglas, Kathryn
Herrick, Ruth L. Anderson, Louise A. Paquette and Gregory Bingham.
In conclusion, this Board would like to thank the Lunenburg Grange for maintaining the window
boxes, Newcomers Club for the outside Christmas Decorations and Rotary Club for donating
daffodils bulbs for the Lower Common. The Board would also like to recognize all the individuals
who work so hard for the Town and those who volunteer their time and commitment on behalf of
the Town by serving on the many Boards, Committees, and Commissions.

ANIMAL CONTROL OFFICER
1998 has been very busy year in my office. We have had 240 stray dogs from all different areas
of Town. Sixty of these dogs have returned home to their original owners while others were
adopted. About thirty of these dogs were euthanized due to the fact they were aggressive, too
old or very sick and untreatable. I am also on a "Get Your Dogs Licensed" trail again. We sent
240 fines for unlicensed dogs, and took those who chose to ignore the fine to court. If people
would just license their dogs, we would have very few strays, and occasional dog bites as we
would have all the information on the dogs made available a lot sooner for the parties involved
and the hospitals.
I hope all the animals and their owners have a wonderful, healthy and safe 1999. Please
remember that the wild life population has become a real problem and small animals are not safe
out at night.
Respectfully submitted,
Kathleen Comeau
BUILDING DEPARTMENT/ZONING OFFICIAL
The Building Inspectors office is located on the second floor of the Town Hall with regular office
hours Monday through Thursday 8:00 AM - 3:30 PM and Friday 9:00 AM to 12:00 noon. The
Inspector is available in the office daily and inspections are by appointment only. Roger
Williams is the Building Inspector and Zoning Officer for the Town of Lunenburg. Lisa
Normandin is the Building Department Secretary.
Building activity in Lunenburg has been steady in the residential districts. The following figures
indicate the number and type of permits issued from this office in 1998: single family homes 46,
one commercial building (Mr. Mikes on Massachusetts Avenue) 46 additions, 31 barns and or
garages, 33 porches & decks, 25 renovations, 47 repairs/maintenance, 14 pools, 6 use and
accessory permits, 9 permits for signs, 10 demolition's and 3 woodstove permits. Permit fees in
1998 totaled $25,548.00
Residents should be aware that Massachusetts General Law requires that all persons,
partnerships and corporations who bid or perform residential contracting (reconstruction,
alterations, additions etc.) for work exceeding $1,000.00 be registered with the Commonwealth.
This office will enforce this provision, however, you should ask your contractor about his/her
registration. Please contact this office with any questions about this requirement or call for any
other information you may need on Zoning requirements or Building Code issues.
Recent news in building effective March 1, 1998 is the Energy Conservation Requirements for
Low-Rise Residential New Construction. Builders must satisfy two different parts of the energy
code. General Requirements applies to all low-rise residential buildings and includes:
where and how vapor barriers and insulation must be installed
what must be done to minimize air leakage in buildings and ducts
sizing and controls for heating and cooling systems
requirements for domestic hot water systems
For more information regarding the energy code, contact the Building Inspectors office for a
brochure explaining all the options.
CAPITAL PLANNING
The FY'99 Capital Plan was approved in FY'98 for $458,659.00 for Capital Expenditures,
Improvements and Maintenance. The items in these articles included the second half of the
8
===PAGE 12===
Rescue/Ambulance, One Ton Dump Truck with Plow, a Police Cruiser, Generator, Voting
Equipment and others. Capital School Improvements and Maintenance consisted of fire alarm
upgrades at the High School and fire suppression system at the Primary School, handicap access
at Thomas C. Passios Elementary School, replacement of gym windows at Thomas C. Passios,
replacement of gym roof at Thomas C. Passios, and the Cook property. To date $340,427.74 has
been spent.
At the Special Town Meeting on August 17, 1998, the Town voted to purchase 100 + acres of the
Woodruff Property at 107 Fairview Road for the sum of $700,000.00.
To recap Capital Spending, monies passed for the FY 98 were $389,585.00. Monies spent to date
are $335,067.27. The FY97 Plan was for $509,450.00. Monies spent to date from this
allotment are $465,565.24. Monies for the FY96 Plan were $287,140.00. The amount spent to
date is $287,097.57. Monies designated for the FY95 Plan were $633.200.00. Monies spent to
date are $629,830.42. All monies allocated but not spent, have been or will be, part of the
funding for the FY Plans.
The FY Plans have continued the Vehicle Rotation Plan to date. This plan supports vehicle
purchases such as police cruisers, one ton dump trucks and etc. The rotation becomes effective
when one truck from first priority department is passed to departments with less use. This is
cost effective.
The Capital Planning Committee supported a coordination of services for cost effectiveness
between all Town Department and the School Department which has resulted in a Director of
Facilities and Maintenance and a Technology Coordinator. The Committee has seen effective
results from this approach.
This past year the Capital Planning Committee raised the level for requests for Capital Planning
and Improvement Maintenance budget to a $10,000.00 base. We still need to advance to a
higher levy so we can place Lunenburg on a long range Capital Plan. We need to project out at
least five years.
The Capital Planning Committee is part of the Planning Board's facility planning which will be
conducted within the "Envisioning" process. The capital facility needs are many, including
school, fire/police, library and municipal. Sewers are a priority for most of the above.
However, Municipal Planning cannot be accomplished and put into reality until the Big Man -
Little Man analogy for Operating and Capital Budgeting is put into place. In this past year, and
as in other years, this Committee has been trying to achieve the balance of proportion. That is,
five (5) percent of the total available revenue should be set aside for Capital Expenditures,
Improvement and Maintenance Plans. Fifty (50) percent, or half of the amount of those monies
should be for FY plans and fifty (50) percent for major items such as facilities. As the
Committee works through the FY2000 Plan at the time of this writing, it is trying to prioritize
to the 2 1/2 percent. Funding is questionable. What about the other 2 1/2 percent for Facility
Planning?
The Capital Planning Committee realizes funding operating budgets are the basis of Town
Government and Services but the Committee is also aware that without FY Capital Plans, the
needed expenditures, improvements and maintenance items are not done because they are not
funded. This is not cost effective. Lack of capital spending in these areas brings forth safety
issues. In addition, when the needs are ignored, crises are born. Taking the scenario one step
further notes that the Capital Facility needs are not being addressed. Our buildings are growing
older, becoming too small and not being used as effective space. To continue in this vein will be
to the detriment of our Town. Steps must be taken to insure that Capital Planning is, as noted
in the Little Man - Big Man theory, as important as operating budgets.

COMPUTER ADVISORY COMMITTEE
The Computer Advisory Committee is pleased to submit this annual report. During this past year
the Computer Advisory Committee has accomplished a great deal. Creating a new Technology
Department. Hiring a Technology Director. Preparing the Town systems and software for the
year 2000 (Y2k). Continued planning for the installation of an I-Net (Institutional Network).
The Town of Lunenburg has created a new Technology Department and hired a Technology
Director, Richard Mobley. Rick is responsible for the computer systems in the Town and in the
Schools. He works very closely with the Computer Advisory Committee to assure that all Town
and School departments have the proper technological tools to do their jobs as quickly and
efficiently as possible.
The Computer Advisory Committee has taken on the task of making sure the Town and School
computer systems will transition smoothly from 1999 to the year 2000. The committee has
taken the following steps:
1. Town departments have requested Y2k updates from all of their major vendors.
Vendors were asked specific questions about their efforts to bring their products
and/or services into Y2k compliance.
2. Hardware and software inventories of Town equipment have been completed.
3. Hardware is being tested to be sure that it is Y2k compliant. Those systems that fail
the tests are being upgraded to bring them into compliance.
4. We have created a list of all software used within the Town and Schools. Most software
packages were found to be Y2k compliant. Software that is not Y2k compliant will be
upgraded or replaced, with comparable software that is Y2k compliant.
5. It is our plan to have all systems Y2k compliant by September 1, 1999.
Another exciting project that the Computer Advisory Committee has been working on is the
installation of an I-Net (Institutional Network). This system will connect all of the Town and
School buildings together electronically. Administrative functions can be decentralized which
will increase efficiency and reduce paperwork. Departments will be able to communicate
electronically, sharing information, files and data quickly and easily. Planning for this project
has been completed and implementation has started. We anticipate the I-Net to be up and
running in the spring of 1999.
The Computer Advisory Committee is blessed with members that are very talented and
energetic. Through their hard work and expert guidance, technology is being integrated into the
daily routines of all Town and School departments. The Town of Lunenburg is very fortunate to
have this outstanding technical expertise available.
CONSERVATION COMMISSION
The Lunenburg Conservation Commission has seven members appointed by the Town Selectmen.
The Commissioners are charged with administering the Massachusetts Wetlands Protection Act.
and the Lunenburg Wetlands Bylaw. The Commission is also responsible for management and
acquisition of Conservation Lands and assisting the public with wetland and ecological issues
and concerns.
1998 was an active year for the Commission. There were 44 Notice of Intents and 26 Requests
for Determination of Applicability reviewed by the Commission. The Commission issued 43
Orders of Condition, 25 Determinations of Applicability, 21 Requests for Certificates of
Compliance (7 issued) and 4 Enforcement Orders.
The Commission received 34 complaints of violations in 1998. Some of the complaints involved
numerous calls from abutters. Not all complaints result in an issuance of Enforcement Orders.
These Orders are only issued after all other reasonable attempts for compliance have failed.

COUNCIL ON AGING
The Council on Aging proudly opened the 4000 sq. ft. addition to the Eagle House on February
28, 1998. Town officials and state dignitaries participated in the program with over 300
attendees. An American flag given in memory of Bernadette Holman was raised at Veteran's

The mission of the Council on Aging is to advocate for improved quality of life for all seniors
through supportive services, programs and education. The Council on Aging achieves this
mission by providing a gathering place for area elders to enjoy social and recreational activities,
to receive educational opportunities such as the computer classes, creative writing classes and
speakers on varied topics throughout the year. We produce a monthly newsletter bringing up to
date information to elders. Health screenings are conducted monthly and nutritious meals are
served daily both at the center and through the Meals on Wheels Program. Exercise classes such
as line dancing, chair aerobics and Tai'Chi offer further opportunities to stay healthy. We
sponsor intergenerational programs with the Lunenburg Schools. Our Secret Pen Pal writing
program is beginning its 8th year.
A handicap accessible van is available Monday through Friday to residents 60 years or older and
eligible non-elder disabled residents providing transportation to shopping, medical
appointments, part time jobs, visits to nursing homes and of course to all activities at the Eagle
House. A part time Outreach worker is assigned to visit frailer elders in their homes and assist
with any services we can provide or to make appropriate referrals. The center is actively

BOARD OF HEALTH
The Board of Health continues to deal with matters relating to the public health including the
review and approval of replacement septic systems for existing structures that have failed the
Title 5 inspection criteria. In order to minimize the impact of replacement systems, the Board
utilizes maximum feasible compliance, a less stringent section of Title 5, whenever possible.
The failure rate in Lunenburg remains at approximately 20%.
Under the State Betterment Program, low interest loans have been granted to replace eight (8)
failing septic systems located in environmentally sensitive areas. Following completion of
these eight systems, if sufficient funds remain applications will be accepted to select additional
properties for the loan program. Through this program the Board of Health received grant funds
to implement a computerized tracking system utilizing GIS (Geographic Information System)
Technology. This new system offers the Board the ability to generate maps as well as allow the
Board to better track permits, well test results, soil data and to identify environmentally
sensitive areas. This is the second low interest loan program that the Board of Health has
administered to assist homeowners in replacing failed septic systems. Under the last program
five systems were repaired. A revolving fund was established under the first loan program,
which will be used to assist additional homeowners in the future, the current balance in this
account is approximately $7,000.
The Board, through its agent, the Nashoba Associated Boards of Health, continues to provide
dental examinations and fluoride treatments in our schools as well as numerous clinics that are
held at various times during the year. These are advertised in local newspapers or you may
contact our office for information.

This Historical Commission met with a few volunteers on a Saturday morning in February '98 in
the Town Clerk's Office under the direction of our then Chair Deborahanne Mayer. Our goal was to
begin a listing of all the Local Government Records presently stored in the town vault. We were
able to accumulate a list of 128 items, a small amount considering the extent of records gathered

continued handling and possible destruction of our original documents.
Provide the Town with an accurate resource for genealogists, researchers, historians

RITTER MEMORIAL LIBRARY
Ritter Memorial Library is a small library with a big heart. The staff here dedicates itself to the
promotion of reading and lifelong learning for everyone. 1998 was another banner year. We lent
out 83,400 items. Our overall lending was up 16.6% over the previous year. We have 6237
registered borrowers.
17
===PAGE 21===
Ritter Library offered over 170 programs in 1998. "Book Time with Your Baby" is a new venture
for infants and toddlers. Children and their caregivers learn fingerplays, songs, and simple
games to enhance playtime. The library also offers a large collection of sturdy board books for
babies to encourage a love for books and reading. Current research reveals that eighty percent of
a child's lifelong intellectual capacity is determined by the age of four. We feel the public library
can play a powerful role supporting families and young children with great resources and
stimulating activities to help them learn.
The trustees have renewed their commitment to serving teens, an often over-looked population.
They promoted Kate McCarron to coordinator for teen programming. "Read it and Reap" the
summer reading program for teens encourages them to read during the summer by offering
incentives and programs targeted at diverse interests. Summer 1998 saw participation double
when we awarded movie passes, gift certificates, and a mountain bike. "Read it and Reap" has
been an effective way to reconnect teens to the joys and benefits of reading.
The townsfolk are asking for more materials, more hours, and more parking. Our space, inside
and out, is limited. The current Ritter Library was already inadequate by the mid-1980's.
Lunenburg rejected an expansion proposal in 1988 saying the school and public safety building
needs should take precedence. As we approach the Millennium, the situation is worse. Nearly
10,000 children and adults are underserved by a cramped, outdated, 4100 square foot structure.
Our mission is clear but frustrated by size and the inexcusable lack of handicapped access to the
meeting room and children's library. Whenever we hold a program some part of our collection
becomes inaccessible. Expanding the current building would not solve the parking problem, so
the trustees are actively seeking another central location for a new public library. There is State
support available for up to 40% of the cost.
The nearly 1000 members of The Friends of the Ritter Memorial Library enjoyed passes to
eleven of the area's best museums. New passes this year include the New England Aquarium and
the Isabella Stewart Gardner Museum. Friends also benefited from a 10% discount on auto
insurance from Foster Insurance. In addition to museum passes, the Friends invested in reading
incentives and programming materials for the children, funded a new display case and lovely
new library shelving. The first sixty CD-ROM's available for free loan were purchased by the
Friends. To list all their contributions is impossible. The Friends pay for all the extras at Ritter
that help us promote life-enhancing connections to books, culture, and community.

INSPECTOR OF PLUMBING AND GAS FITTINGS
I hereby submit my annual report for the year ending December 31, 1998. There were 115
Plumbing and 62 Gas permits issued; resulting in approximately 237 field or on-site inspections.

All installation of plumbing and gas must follow the State Uniform Code under Chapter 142 of
the Massachusetts General Laws and Titles of the Environmental Code. The Plumbing and Gas
Code is founded upon certain principles of environmental sanitation and safety through properly
designed, acceptable installed and adequately maintained plumbing and gas. Construction may
vary, but the basic sanitary and safety principles are desirable and necessary to protect the

All plumbing and gas must be done by a licensed plumber or licensed gas fitter and subject to

Measures
MCAS Results
The first administration of the Massachusetts Comprehensive Assessment System (MCAS) took
place in the spring of 1998. Results from these assessments were received by the District the
first week of December. MCAS is a critical part of education reform in the state and is one of
the most demanding assessments ever administered to our students. The results serve as a
baseline by which to measure future improvement. The results are not a measure of the past.
Now, more than ever students, parents, and schools will need to work together in partnership to
improve student performance and prepare students for the 21st century.
In Lunenburg all means ALL and 100% of the students enrolled in 8th and 10th grades
participated in the assessment. Ninety-nine percent (99%) of the students in 4th grade
participated. One percent of our student population was provided an alternate assessment.
Overall, the performance of Lunenburg students on the MCAS was comparable to the average
performance of students across the state. At grade 4 our strongest performance was in Science
& Technology. Our strongest performance in grade 8 was in English Language Arts. At grade 10
our strongest performance was in English Language Arts and Science & Technology. Lunenburg
students' performance was weakest in grades 8 and 10 in Mathematics.
On the MCAS, student performance is reported by level (Advanced, Proficient, Needs
Improvement, and Failing). Students at the Advanced Level demonstrate a comprehensive and
in-depth understanding of rigorous subject matter and provide sophisticated solutions to
complex problems. Students at the Proficient Level demonstrate a solid understanding of
challenging subject matter and solve a wide variety of problems. Students at the Needs
Improvement Level demonstrate partial understanding of subject matter and solve some simple
problems. Students at the Failing Level demonstrate minimal understanding of subject matter
and do not solve even simple problems.

ranged from 0% to 7% across the three grades and three areas assessed. Seven percent (7%) of
students in grade 8 and 6% of grade 4 students in Lunenburg performed at the Advanced level in
Mathematics. Five percent (5%) of students in grade 8 and grade 10 in Lunenburg scored at the
Advanced level in English Language Arts, and 5% of students in grade 4 scored at this level in
Science & Technology.

IOWA Results
In the spring of 1998 students in grades three, seven, and nine also were administered the Iowa
Tests in the spring of 1998. The Iowa Tests are standardized, norm-referenced tests. This
means that the tests were given using specified directions and under specific conditions to a
representative group of students nationwide. Scores from this national sample become the
"norms" that permit local schools to compare the performance of their students with this
national group. Scores from norm-referenced tests do not tell what students know and what
they don't know. They tell only how a student's or group's performance compares with that of
others in the norm group.
The scores reported are national percentile rank scores. Percentile ranks are reported in units
that range from 1 to 99. A percentile rank score of 67 means that the group earned a raw score
that was higher than 67 percent of the students in the norm group. It does not mean that the
local group scored as well or better than 67 percent of districts nationwide.

Special Services
A committed and well-qualified staff of special education teachers and related service personnel
work in concert with other professionals in the district to provide a quality program for each
student. Related service staff include four speech/language therapists, an occupational
therapist, a school psychologist, and a social worker. Additionally, a dedicated group of parents
involved with the parent Advisory Council-Special Achievers of Lunenburg (PAC-SAL) provide
support and assistance for students, parents and professionals.
In each of our schools we continue to align our special education services to ensure that
students with disabilities have access to the general education curricula and high expectations.
Currently, approximately two hundred students in the district are identified with a disability
and receiving special education and related services. This figure represents 11% of the total
school population. The percentage is consistent with the national average, but well below the
state average. Nine students receive services in out-of-district placements.
During the summer of 1998 the district developed and provided a summer school program for
seventeen students. A dedicated staff provided services to students for a period of five weeks.
The goal of summer school services is to support the maintenance of skills for students who in
the absence of such services would demonstrate significant regression.

TURKEY HILL MIDDLE SCHOOL
Turkey Hill Middle School considers the whole student in its implementation of appropriate
programs. Educational decisions for early adolescents are based on the students' emotional,
physical, social and intellectual development. We believe that each child will become a self-
motivated, creative, life-long learner in a safe, stimulating environment that encourages
diversity and honest, open communication. Principal Mary E. Cringan and assistant principal
Thomas Rousseau along with the entire staff recognize the responsibilities in preparing young
adolescents to be successful, productive and contributing members of a changing global society.

Academic standards and expectations are a high priority at THMS. The entire staff strives hard
to provide numerous opportunities for all students to learn. A positive response from students
and families is expected and encouraged. The staff recognizes the challenge of helping all
students reach their academic potential. The four basic curricular areas in grades five through
eight are; English language arts, math, science and social studies. All eighth grade students
have the opportunity to study French, Spanish or Latin. Special area subjects (art, music,
family and consumer science, technology education, health, guidance, library skills, world
languages, and physical education) offer a unique element to the middle school curriculum.
New curriculum has been written and has been implemented in all subjects except history social
science and foreign languages. These two areas are currently under study and new curriculum
guides will be written and implemented for the 1999-2000 school year. All subjects will then be
in compliance with the frameworks from the Massachusetts Department of Education. All
students in grades four, eight and ten will be required to take the MCAS tests. These tests will
be based on the frameworks.
THMS was fortunate to receive a twelve thousand dollar grant from the Department of Education
for after school activities. This grant has enabled us to offer free programs for all students.
Students are able to extend their school day and participate in floor hockey, chorus,
environmental club, drama, homework help, weightlifting, cross-country running and spring
track. We are also fortunate to have a late bus provide transportation two days per week. This
helps working families and provides the students with the opportunity to extend their school
day.

improvement plan is available in the school office.
