MEMORIAM
PETER GRENIER
1928 - 1991
Peter Grenier taught at the Lunenburg
High School and Middle School for a
total of thirty-two years. A Maine
native, he earned his Bachelor's Degree
at the University of Maine, and his
Master's Degree at Fitchburg State
College. He was married to Paulette
(Fluet) Grenier and had three sons,
Peter, David and Michael. He gave of
himself as a teacher and as a coach. He
was a man of energy and was excited
about all he did. He was well loved in
the community and is missed by all his
many friends and colleagues. He brought
dignity to an already noble profession.

BOARD OF SELECTMEN
The Board of Selectmen bid farewell to Paula Fletcher and Janet
Goodrich this year. Their seats were filled by Michael Sauvageau and
Steven deBettencourt. It is always difficult when two members of any
three member Board change in one year. However, the transition went
very smoothly. In other personnel changes, 1991 also brought with it a
new Town Counsel. With Tim Hillman resigning to accept a judgeship,
the Board of Selectmen hired the firm of Kopelman & Paige to handle
Lunenburg's legal needs. Rebecca Whitten resigned as Town Accountant
and Karen Rameau filled that position in September of 1991. One other
change of note was the departure of Mary Padula as our State Senator.
The Board wishes all the recently transitional people the best of luck.
The Lunenburg Housing Partnership was officially formed early in 1991.
Their task is to advance affordable housing alternatives within
Lunenburg's borders.
Town Hall finally received some much needed repairs -- a drop in the
bucket. However, bit by bit the Board hopes to be able to bring Town
Hall back into use as a viable spot for many of the community's
functions. It is the Board's hope that in turn this will help to
instill pride in Lunenburg's heritage and sense of community spirit.
Community was also the key to the Board's initiation of the All-Town
Board Meetings. The purpose of these meetings is to advance the
implementation of innovative and cooperative ideas, as well as to
strengthen communication within the Town and especially within Town
government.
One such cooperative step was achieved with the installation of Centrex
by all Town offices. While this necessitated changes in all the Town's
municipal telephone numbers, over the next ten years, there will be a
significant savings to the Town.
The next several years will provide Lunenburg with major changes in the
disposal of the Town's solid waste. One such change is the recycling
program's growth. By way of a grant, the State provided two large
roll-off containers. Every item that is recycled is saving Lunenburg
landfill space.
Thank you for allowing the Board to continue serving you, the citizens,
as best as we can, for the good of Lunenburg.
BOARD OF HEALTH
The year 1991 marked the first full year of operation for the newly
elected Board of Health. During this time we have familiarized
ourselves with both the State and local sanitary codes, and in spite of
the continued budgetary crunch, have been able to provide the community
with fair and timely decisions regarding health issues.
It is evident that health and environmental problems are growing at an
alarming rate and that the potential cost of dealing with these
- 4 -
===PAGE 7===
problems will have a significant effect on the citizens of our
community.
Nowhere are these issues more important than they are at our existing
landfill. With the help and cooperation of the Board of Selectmen and
the Recycling Committee, the Town will be submitting an application for
the repermitting of our existing facility prior to July 1, 1992. At
this writing, many decisions are yet to be made. Recycling, curbside
collection, lined or unlined cells, capping, short term and long term
disposal plans, are but some examples of the issues that must be
resolved over the next year. No matter what we do, it will cost the
community deeply over the next decade.
We encourage your participation in these decisions. The Board meets
twice a month throughout the year and welcomes any comments you may
have.

BOARD OF ASSESSORS
The Board of Assessors and its staff completed a third year of property
valuation using the CAMA software program. Paul Gagliardi and Lisa
Normandin both completed the Department of Revenue's Assessment and
Administration Tax Law course. The Board members and Judy Holden
continue to attend seminars and workshops in order to keep abreast of
changes in the law and updates and enhancements to the computer system.
Marsha Metzler, formerly an Assistant Assessor in Billerica, has been
doing field inspections in conjunction with our staff, as part of an
ongoing annual process of inspecting portions of the Town.
The Assessors' office is currently working on the implementation of a
quarterly tax billing system as voted at the May 1991 Annual Town
Meeting. This will go into effect for FY1993. Four tax bills will be
mailed to all property owners. The first two bills are estimated or
preliminary bills. They are based on the prior year's tax and will be
due in two equal installments on August 1st and November 1st. After
the Town sets its tax rate, the actual bills are mailed. They will be
due on February 1st and May 1st. The preliminary tax does not
represent an estimate of the actual tax due for FY1993. Abatements for
overvaluation can only be filed after the actual tax bills are mailed.
The Board of Assessors and its staff look forward to working with
taxpayers in resolving questions they may have about their assessments
or quarterly tax bills.
There are various tax exemptions available to qualifying taxpayers.
Complete information may be obtained at the Assessors' office in Town
Hall or by phone at 582-4145. A summary of these exemptions is as
follows:

PLANNING BOARD
The Planning Board processed 15 ANR plans for calendar year 1991, and
initiated closure on the Ecological Fibers industrial project, and the
Fairlane Mobile Home Park commercial project. The modification of the
Villages at Blueberry Hill was permitted pending final condition
completion. This modification, which has less housing, retains three
golf holes on the south side of Northfield Road as part of the proposed
18-hole course. Submission of a Development Plan Review for the
Maplewood Golf Course is expected in the early months of 1992.
The Gitto Corporation industrial project is well into construction.
Project meetings are held bimonthly and inspection procedures are
carried out as needed. Gitto Corporation is scheduled to be in
production by mid 1992. The Board has been approached for conceptual
commercial projects on Massachusetts Avenue, and housing projects on
Gilchrest Street and Flat Hill Road. There is no formal status change
of The Woodlands at Hickory Hills.
The Planning Board has continued to implement action plans designated
by the Growth Improvement and Strategic Plans. The Board is also
anticipating completion of the Open Space Plan in early 1992. There is
preliminary work being done on the Master Plan update.
The Sewer Master Plan Committee, a sub-committee of the Planning Board,
reports they are reviewing a Master Plan Model and are developing a
questionnaire for citizen input. The Innovative Housing Committee and
the Planning Board are seeking direction from EOCD for the approval of
Lunenburg's Housing Action Plan.

RITTER MEMORIAL LIBRARY
Community generosity has been a key factor in the successful operation
of the Library in 1991. This generosity has taken several forms. Gift
funds actually received in 1990 were used to replace old, sagging
shelves in the room housing children's fiction. All walls in the
children's section were freshly painted and new carpet was laid. Irene
Proctor contributed funds in memory of her two nephews, Lee and Dana
Woodward. Thus, the fiction room has now been designated the "Woodward
Room". Other funds for the renovations were received as gifts from the
Lunenburg Rotary Club and a Town family. Painting and carpentry was
done by inmates of MCI Lancaster, with volunteers and Library staff
also painting and doing much moving and cleaning. An oil painting on
permanent loan from Sylvia Loiselle added the perfect finishing touch.
General contributions of time by the Library corps of volunteers,
organized by the staff last March, have greatly enhanced service.
These dedicated people sort book donations, file cards, type, process
books and magazines, deliver materials to Pearl Brook Housing, work on
press releases, shelve books, generate computerized lists and records,
weed out old materials, assist with youth services, mend books, and
pitch in wherever there is a need. They have become an indispensable
asset for the work they accomplish and for the assurance they provide
that the community is fully behind its Library.
As always, the Friends of the Library have given their vital support in
fund raising for many of the summer programs and year-round craft
sessions, expensive museum passes, Christmas decorations, the
Children's Art Festival and, with assistance from the Arts Lottery,
concerts of quality. In addition, they are the mainstay of Library
hospitality and can be depended on for unending batches of cookies.
Their efforts were appreciated Town-wide at the Ice Cream Social and
the community Christmas Tree Lighting Ceremony.
The Central Massachusetts Regional Library System loaned us a CD-ROM
for three months. After only a few weeks, enthusiasm for this advanced
technology was running high and a drive to raise funds to buy one was
started. At year's end, the goal of $2,500 had been reached through
pledges of $500 each by the Friends and LEAF, and donations of over
$1,500 in response to an appeal to individuals by the Trustees. The
equipment soon to be purchased will make more complete research service
readily available.
A handsome painting of the Cook Farm now enhances the Library -- a gift
of the artist Carol Shiel through a grant from the Arts Lottery. This
donation provides a record of a part of Town history for the future.
Books and other materials have been given in memory of several
individuals: Ralph Benjamin, Fred Browne, Dr. James Congdon, James
Hardman, Walter Jeffko, Jr., and Bernard Quinlan. Other contributions
- 8 -
===PAGE 11===
include card covers from Hometown Real Estate, bookmarks from Foster
Insurance Agency, funds for books from the Lunenburg Memorial Fund, and
many donations of used materials.
All of these voluntary contributions convey a clear sense of the
positive attitude of the community towards the Library. This in turn
is perceived by the Trustees and staff as an endorsement of their own
efforts to provide the best service possible under the circumstances.
Although budget limitations necessitated the loss of three hours of
operation per week, the public adjusted and checked out more materials
than at any time since 1985, while the staff has shown more motivation
than ever in accommodating the increased demand for services, in spite
of a ten percent cut in their working hours.
The total number of materials circulated was 56,000, with 20,844 from
the adult section, 1,702 from the young adult section, and 33,454 from
the children's section. Books, magazines, video and audiocassettes,
passes and pictures were checked out year round at the average rate of
32 per open hour.
It has been said that of all municipal services, libraries are used
voluntarily by more people than any other. In 1991, Ritter Memorial
Library provided the services that this use entails for just one
percent of the Town's budget. Does anyone know of a better return for
such a relatively small investment?
HOUSING AUTHORITY
As 1991 came to a close, it ended a year which began with anxious
waiting of impending budget cuts and their impact on the Authority's
operation, and ended with the Authority awarding the fire alarm update
contract.
The Housing Authority did see anticipated savings from the retrofitting
of the incandescent lighting with fluorescent lighting.
The
Authority's electric utility bills were reduced by $3,500. As part of
our scheduled equipment upgrade, the Authority is replacing
refrigerators and electric ranges in our elderly housing development.
We will continue this practice as long as our budget permits. We were
able to repave some areas of sidewalk at the Pearl Brook Apartments.
The Authority received 29 new applications for housing during 1991,
consisting of 10 elderly and 19 family. The current waiting lists as
of December 31, 1991 have 4 elderly applicants and 29 family
applicants.
The Housing Authority will continue to work with the Town of Lunenburg
and its Boards to meet the needs of its tenants and citizenry. The
Board of Commissioners remain committed to provide decent, safe
affordable housing within its budget constraints, and to promote new
housing opportunities within the community.

POLICE DEPARTMENT
In 1991, the Lunenburg Police Department, along with the City of
Fitchburg and the Town of Ashby, again applied for the Drug Task Force
Grant and were successful in obtaining it. We received full funding,
approximately $30,000, and the Town did not have to put up any matching
funds. This allowed us to keep Patrolman Sean Connery on our force to
fill the vacancy left by elevating Patrolman James Marino to a
detective/drug officer position. We will be putting in a drug hotline
at the station for anyone who wants help or assistance with drug
problems, or wants to call with information that will help us in the
fight against drugs in our community. I would like to thank the
citizens of our community for their support at Town Meeting and also
our fund raisers. Through your efforts, money has been raised and an
Article passed at Town Meeting which will allow us to keep our D.A.R.E.
program going for the next two years.
Domestic crimes have shown a significant increase in Lunenburg, as well
as other acts of violence and vandalism to property. Crimes involving
alcohol and substance abuse continue to rise in our community as
unemployment rises. Traffic congestion continues to be a problem in
our Town, however, motor vehicle accidents are down approximately 10%.
Of "alarming" concern is the increase in calls for alarms to which we
are dispatched. They have increased from 401 in 1990 to 611 in 1991.
We never charged a fee for this service in the past, but as our
workload increases, along with our calls for assistance, we may have
to. Some people may not like this, but our resources are limited and
we can only do what we have the manpower and money to do.
This past year, in order to keep our Safe Boating Program going, the
citizens who live on Lake Shirley chipped in and donated funds of their
own. It is unfortunate that the Safe Boating Program budget had to be
supplemented this way, but I applaud them for their zeal and enthusiasm
in supporting this worthwhile program and their community.
In today's tough times, it is refreshing to see a Town such as ours
still working together with its Department Heads and leaders to
maintain its character and dignity. We have a Police Department that
is second to none in the Commonwealth, staffed by highly motivated
professionals. It makes me extremely proud to be the Chief of such a
Department and allows us to protect and serve you, the citizens of our
community.

vehicle accidents, 611 alarms, and took 42 persons into protective
custody. The following 931 court cases were processed through the

CONSERVATION COMMISSION
The Conservation Commission continues to enforce the Massachusetts
Wetlands Protection Act and the Town of Lunenburg's Wetlands Protection
By-Law. In 1991, the Commission issued 26 Order of Conditions, 7
Wetlands Determinations, 2 Enforcement Orders, and conducted many on-
site inspections.
The Conservation Commission office is presently located in the
Selectmen's Conference Room along with the Board of Health. In 1990,
the Commission secured permanent access to the third floor of the Eagle
House Senior Center. Relocating the office still remains contingent
upon finishing off the third floor.
The Commission is also responsible for the acquisition and management
of Conservation owned land. This year the Commission swapped 2.025
acres of land located on Mulpus Road in return for 15.077 acres of land
on Howard Street previously owned by Harold and Karen Alatalo to
connect Conservation land to the Town Forest land. This land swap was
approved at the 1988 Annual Town Meeting.
The Conservation Commission repeats their request to urge the Town to
hire a part-time professional Conservation Officer possibly to be
shared with the Board of Health and the Planning Board for the purpose
of enforcing the Wetlands Protection Act and protecting the
environmental interest of the community. We would like this agent to
work towards public education and reach out to the students in the
school system.
The Commission has agreed to allow the Highway Department to remove
approximately 20,000 yards of sand and gravel from Conservation
Commission owned property known as Laurel Bank/Brown Property off
Pleasant Street. The purpose of the excavation is to sand and pave
- 15 -
===PAGE 18===
roads for the 1991/1992 winter season. It would cost the Town $7.00 -
$8.00 per yard for sand and gravel if purchased elsewhere. The Board
of Selectmen, Highway Department and Conservation Commission are very
interested in a possible land swap. The Commission is particularly
interested in the West Street property owned by the Town under the
School Department jurisdiction. The Commission considers this land to
have great habitat value, which also contains wetlands.
Long standing Conservation Commission member Tom Lawson continues his
dedication to the maintenance and well being of several Conservation
owned properties. The Commission continues to report vandalism at the
Cowdry Lot.
David Holtz has performed maintenance on the dam on Reservoir Road. He
also removed a willow tree which had been badly damaged in Hurricane
Bob. Several other trees were trimmed so as not to inhibit the ball
field. The Commission wishes to thank David for his effort and
concerns.
The Commission also thanks Bob Gniot for the volunteer work of
photographing the shoreline of Hickory Hills Lake for the purpose of
documenting the existing conditions of the lake. The Commission is
looking for volunteer photographers for similar work from Lake Shirley
and Lake Whalom.
The Conservation Commission appreciates the work of former members
Michael Sauvageau and Robert Kreger for their efforts in Conservation
practices. The Commission welcomes David Anshus as a new member.
CAPITAL PLANNING COMMITTEE
Article 3 of the 1991 Annual Town Meeting mandated formation of a
Capital Planning Committee. The Committee was formed in September of
1991. Committee members are Michael Sauvageau, Board of Selectmen;
Jane Hall, School Committee; Edward Hubbard, Finance Committee; Marion
Benson, Planning Board; and Elaine Rae Murphy, Member at Large. The
Committee's mission statement reads:
"Our goal is to help local government form a financial structure by
knowing the community's worth, planning for necessary capital
expenditures, and maintaining its present assets thus creating a
climate of shared information for sound planning decisions for
preserving the characteristics and quality of life in this community."
The first task of the Capital Planning Committee is to establish a
complete inventory of Town property, roads, rolling stock and all other
values in unit form of over $250.00. Inventory form requests require
condition and obsoleteness rating. Replacement time line, cost and
improvement plans are also noted. As of this writing, the inventory is
60% complete. The inventory will be kept up-to-date, making available
the value, condition and needs of Town operation.
Concurrently, the Committee, after studying contingent towns'
procedures, formulated a one year survey form for each department's
- 16 -
===PAGE 19===
capital expenditure request. Future plans include the development of a
system where each Department will generate a five year Capital Plan of
need.
The Capital Planning Committee's mandate is to inform the budget
working committees of overall long-range capital improvement and
expenditures, enabling them to reflect these costs in sound budget
planning. This will also enable voters to make informed decisions.
The Committee's consensus is that the procedure will take about two
years to be effective.
COUNCIL ON AGING
The Eagle House Senior Center completed its first full year of
operation in 1991, functioning as a Monday through Friday congregate
meal site and distribution point for Meals On Wheels. The Wednesday
Drop-In, with coffee and donuts on tap, is a popular gathering spot for
socializing, cards and games. Other programs are biweekly Cribbage
Tournaments and weekly sessions of Open Bridge, Craft Classes, Bingo,
Walking Club, and Gentle Exercise Program. Interspersed are special
programs, such as day trips, overnight trips, a Volunteer Tea, a First
Anniversary Celebration, a Spaghetti Supper and various lectures and
informational presentations.
Counseling and referral service is provided for elders and their
families. A newsletter is published each month, with three issues
mailed and other issues distributed at various local establishments.
The Eagle House Senior Center and Council on Aging have assumed the
responsibility for the distribution of surplus food, and the Center
also serves as a collection point for non-perishable food for needy of
all ages. The Eagle House provides meeting space for various Town
Boards and Committees when space at the Town Hall is not available.
American Legion Post #283 has an office at the Center and holds their
monthly meetings in the social hall.
Perhaps the greatest achievement of 1991 was the institution of a much-
needed Monday through Friday elderly transportation service through an
agreement with Montachusett Area Regional Transit (MART) at full
reimbursement to the Town. This is the same service that is offered in
11 of the 14 communities that are members of MART. Ridership has more
than doubled for twice weekly shopping trips, area medical
appointments, participation in the noon meal and programs at the Eagle
House.
The Council on Aging in 1991 changed from a volunteer Board with
minimal secretarial help, offering limited programs due to space
constraints, to a volunteer Board in charge of a building with programs
in operation five days a week for 52 weeks a year, employing a
Director, part-time secretary, part-time custodian, a meal site manager
and two van drivers.
There were other changes! Longtime Council member and former
Chairperson Elvira May resigned in January, but continues as Volunteer
Trip Coordinator. Former Vice Chairman and Chairman Mario Patacchiola
- 17 -
===PAGE 20===
chose not to be reappointed, as did veteran member Chester Pliska.
Also the Council accepted the resignation of Elizabeth Donovan. All
will be missed, but were ably replaced by Jean Hobbs, Raymond Delisle,
Nicholas Rimpas and Christine Lattanzi. The reorganization of the
Council on Aging in June elected Jean Hobbs as Chairperson and Freida
Jacobs as Vice Chairperson. Among staff changes was the resignation of
Director Dianne Harrington who was replaced in May by Jeanne DeBruin.
Also, former Secretary Priscilla Kresser was replaced in October by

During 1991, 186 volunteers contributed a total of 4,572 hours of their
time, worth some $60,000, to assist the Council on Aging in providing
programs and services to the 1,467 residents of Lunenburg 60 years of
age and over. Without this assistance, none of these things would be
possible. The Council on Aging and the Eagle House staff and
volunteers are dedicated to serving the elderly of the Town of
Lunenburg. All programs at the Eagle House Senior Center and any
services provided by the Council on Aging through their Director are
open to all elders in the community, regardless of income.
Anyone interested in any of the programs or in need of elder services,
are asked to contact the Center at 582-4166, Monday thru Friday from
8:00 A.M. to 3:00 P.M. Volunteers are always needed and welcome!

by one full time employee by comparing a variety of statistics; in my "for what it's

IT DOESN'T LOOK TO ME LIKE WE'VE COME A LONG WAY!! If this little look back
has done anything for me, it has increased my respect for the work my staff is doing.
Many changes have taken place in the offices of Treasurer, Tax Collector and Town
Clerk. Lisa Wanamaker replaces Karen Rameau as Assistant Treasurer. Karen has assumed
the position of Town Accountant; we all wish her well in her new post and look forward
to working with her in that capacity. Lisa has been trying to do the job of two people
while at the same time training a part time replacement for her full time job. You are
appreciated, Lisa!
All Town employees are being paid on a bi-weekly basis now and the payroll is being
done in house. Many thanks to Laura Dunbar for service beyond the call of duty in
bringing this change about. While this has saved the Town money overall, it has
created additional expense in the Treasurer's administration, not to mention the drain
on availability of computer time.
Another area of savings this year is the in-house preparation of the excise, personal
property and real estate tax bills. We will be issuing Quarterly tax bills in FY93 and
the in- house operation will reduce costs significantly; however, there is still the
expense of additional hours needed to prepare them.
Thanks to the efforts of my staff, we were able to obtain 100% collection rate of FY91
Real Estate taxes, This means that we either collected the taxes or placed a tax lien
on the property and recorded it with the Registry of Deeds to protect the Towns
interest.
A milestone has been reached this year. Everyone on my staff will soon be computer
literate and while I'm delighted with their sincere efforts in using computers, I
can't help but wonder if I haven't created a monster or two While at their computer
training, my staff have been exposed to newer, more advanced equipment and have begun
to realize just how slow and old our system really is. It is my hope that in the
coming year we will be able to update and expand our computer equipment in these three
administrations in order to use the ever decreasing staff hours more efficiently.

The Meeting was called to order at 9:08 A.M. by Moderator Richard J. Bishop, a quorum
having been declared present.
The meeting pledged allegiance to the United States Flag.
David W. Rodgers, 82 Highland Street was recognized and asked that the Town recognize the
years of service of the following persons who have retired from employment with the School
Department: M. Donald Piermarini 29 years; Richard F. Mulligan 37 years; and Jean M.
Killeen 33 years.
Proclamations were presented to Joseph Giovino and James Mann in recognition of their
years of service to the Town.
SPECIAL TOWN MEETING
ARTICLE 1. To see if the Town will vote to raise by taxation and appropriate, or
appropriate from available funds, the sum of $90, or some other sum or sums, for payment
of a medical expense incurred by a firefighter a previous fiscal year, or act anything
thereon. (Submitted by Selectmen) Approved by Finance Committee with appropriation to
come from Free Cash. VOTED UNANIMOUSLY
ARTICLE 2. To see if the Town will vote to accept and improve Sunset Lane under the
Betterment Provisions of Massachusetts General Laws, Chapter 80, and to authorize the
Selectmen to do all things necessary to effectuate the same, and to appropriate by
borrowing the sum of $640,180. to do same, and to assess said owners at a rate of interest
on said betterment assessment equal to 2% above the rate of interest chargeable to the
Town for the betterment project in MGLA Chapter 80, Section 13, or act anything thereon.
(Submitted by Selectmen) Finance Committee recommends approval. VOTED YES 125, NO 10 (83
needed for 2/3rds)
ARTICLE 3. To see if the Town will vote to transfer $19,300, or some other sum or sums,
from Article 2 of this Special Town Meeting Warrant to the Reserve Fund for the purpose of
replenishing the equivalent amount spent on engineering for the Sunset Lane betterment
project, or act anything thereon. (Submitted by Selectmen) Finance Committee recommends
approval. VOTED UNANIMOUSLY
ARTICLE 4. To see if the Town will vote to petition the Legislature to file a Special Act
to approve the exemption of certain boiler repairs at the T.C. Passios Elementary School
from Chapter 30B of the Massachusetts General Laws, the Procurement Act, or act anything
thereon. (Submitted by School Department) Finance Committee recommends approval. VOTED
UNANIMOUSLY
ANNUAL TOWN MEETING
ARTICLE 1. To see if the Town will vote to accept the regular written reports of the Town
Officers and Committees, or act anything thereon. (Submitted by Selectmen) VOTED
UNANIMOUSLY
ARTICLE 2. To see if the Town will vote to demand that its Senator and Representatives do
everything necessary for the establishment of a reasonable state revenue sharing program
for cities and towns as required by Question 5 which was approved overwhelmingly by the
voters at the 1990 biennial state election, to provide for sufficient state revenues to
help fund local services, or act anything thereon. (Submitted by Selectmen). VOTED
UNANIMOUSLY

SUPERINTENDENT'S REPORT - CONCETTA A. VERGE
Once again it is necessary to report to the community that the
schools are grappling, as are all other Town Departments with a very
dismal fiscal situation.
During the past two years a significant amount of money has been
brought into the School District to supplement the depleted General
Appropriations (town portion of the School Department Budget) in order
to maintain a quality comprehensive school system. Through active
recruitment efforts, Lunenburg is a school system that continues to
attract students who live in other communities. During the past two
(2) years approximately over $440,000 was generated through the
administrative efforts to attract more and more Non-resident students
to the Lunenburg Schools. These funds have in turn allowed Lunenburg
Schools to continue to offer a quality educational experience for all
students. Aggressive pursuit of grant funds has also supplemented our
underfunded School District. Increases in users fees, fundraising
efforts of parents and students and generous contributions from local
business people have helped the School District retain SO much.
Lunenburg has financial hope for the future. Currently a town
appointed committee is studying the feasibility of regionalization
which, if positive, could bring significant additional funds to the
schools which; and in turn reduce the financial burden to the town of
the schools.
Lunenburg has also adopted School Choice which will allow
additional students from other school districts to attend the
Lunenburg Public Schools at no expense to them or their families.
Sending municipalities will bear the burden of Choice tuition.
Although the Choice legislation is philosophically non-palatable for
many of us, it may be a means to make our schools even more effective
in meeting the challenge of educating our students for the 21st
century. Choice will also reduce the financial burden the schools
place on the town.
The Lunenburg Administrators and Staff have been proactive in
pursuit of the continuation of excellence in the Lunenburg Schools.
This administration pledges to continue that effort.
In the interim strides are being made in the area of curriculum
development and revision, innovative teaching strategies and
methodologies shared decision making and planning and professional
development. Our test scores remain high as does our college
admission rate while our dropout rate is virtually below 1%.
We are fortunate in Lunenburg to have such dedicated professionals
working with our students. We are fortunate to have parent interest,
commitment and support. We are fortunate to have a community that
demands quality education for the youth of Lunenburg. Together, these
groups have made it work for Lunenburg and will continue to do SO.

Elementary School Principal's Report
(William F. McSheehy)
This year, the Thomas C. Passios Elementary School would like to
acknowledge the deep loss which the school and community have suffered
in the deaths of fourth grader Kim VanHorne and third grader Billy
Proctor. The memory of these two special children will live forever
in our hearts. We are fortunate to have had the Good Grief Program to
support the needs of our children and families during these times of
loss.
The T.C. Passios Elementary School is dedicated to educating the
total child with a very special emphasis on the principles of dignity,
self worth, and integrity. We stress the importance of acceptance of
differences; we encourage cooperative learning situations; we foster a
genuine concern for others; and we encourage a sense of commitment
toward the larger community. In response to parent requests, we have
expanded our Before and After School Extended Day Program to include a
kindergarten alternate session. Our After School Activity Program,
remains a popular option for many of our children.
We have established a rigorous curriculum with flexibility to meet
the needs of each student. We believe to be effective, education must
be founded on a secure mastery of the basic skills taught not only in
isolation but also in integration with one another. We place a strong
emphasis on literature in our reading program. Through our
Professional Development system this year, we have provided our
teachers with a year-long training course in the Whole Language
Approach to the teaching of reading and language. They have
implemented within their classrooms the strategies and techniques
consistent with this philosophy. A writing program that nurtures a
personal expression of ideas is in place in each classroom. The
mastery of computational and problem solving skills is an inherent
part of our math program. Through the Math Their Way Program,
children at the kindergarten level experience math using
manipulatives. We stress the use of the scientific method in an
activity based science exploration program, a creative music and art
program and a developmental physical education program. In every area
of the school day we stress independent thinking and creative problem
solving balanced by receptivity to the ideas of others. We were
fortunate this year to receive a grant from the newly established
Lunenburg Education and Achievement Foundation (LEAF) supporting an
in-class thinking skills program for high achievers.
Our school atmosphere is informal and friendly. Although styles
of teaching vary, we recognize and respect different learning styles
and believe that we are capable of reaching every child. Our faculty
is excited about the new directions in education, and they are
constantly learning and growing. This year we have piloted a
heterogeneously grouped, self-contained classroom at each grade level.
Our staff continues to demonstrate to the children a caring school
community by working together and respecting one another. In

academic tutors, teacher aides, and fundraisers. Through the efforts

We believe in the excellence of the individual child and hope to

Turkey Hill Middle School Principal's Report
(Mary E. Cringan)
Turkey Hill Middle School focuses on an educational process that
recognizes and provides for the uniqueness of the individual. This is
accompanied by a program that is intellectually challenging and
recognizes the students academic, emotional, physical, aesthetic, and
social needs. In a time of transition between elementary and high
school, our students will be encouraged and guided to discover not
only self-worth and potential, but sensitivity toward human
relationships, respect for the abilities, values, and attitudes of
others, and a sense of responsibility to the entire community.
Turkey Hill Middle School is home to 484 students in grades five
through eight. Our curriculum in grades five, six and seven include
reading, english, math, science and social studies. In grade eight,
all students take a foreign language of their choice; Spanish, French
or Latin and english, science, geography and either algebra,
pre-algebra or general math. Our unified arts program consists of
home economics, industrial arts, instrumental music, art, health and
physical education.
Our students continue to score well above the State average on the
Massachusetts Educational Assessment Test and the Massachusetts Basic
Skills Test.
Suspensions and truancies are extremely low. We look at each
student as an individual when dealing with discipline and we have very
close contact with parents and social agencies when needed.
Our Special Education percentage is 13%. We integrate and
mainstream as many students as we can. Our Special Education teachers
consult, team teach and support our regular education teachers.

High School Principal's Report
(Frank A. Sambuceti)
Lunenburg High School offers a comprehensive program of studies
for students in grades 9-12. Since almost 75 percent of the graduates
of Lunenburg High School typically pursue some form of higher
education our curriculum provides a strong program of college
preparatory courses in English, mathematics, science, social studies
and foreign language. Complementing this academic core are quality
offerings in business education, art, music, industrial technology and
physical education.
Lunenburg High School also provides an extensive co-curricular and
extra curricular program and our students are encouraged to
participate in the many clubs, organizations, events or
interscholastic athletic teams which are available. Among our
numerous student clubs and activities are: Debate Club, Youth and
Government Club, Yearbook, Student Council, International Club, Math
Team, National Honor Society, Nature/Environment Club, S.A.D.D.,
Academic Decathlon Team, Latin Club, Ski Club, National Art Honor
Society and Future Business Leaders of America. Additionally
Lunenburg High School sponsors a wide variety of interscholastic
sports at the various levels of boys' and girls' competition. Our
athletic program includes baseball, basketball, cross country,
football, golf, soccer, field hockey, tennis, track and softball.
Lunenburg High School has a long tradition of academic excellence
and scholastic achievement. There are many criteria which can be used
to demonstrate this but college acceptance rates are surely one of the
most significant. While statistics in Massachusetts last year
indicate that approximately 63% of graduating high school seniors went
on to some form of higher education, LHS's class of 1991 distinguished
itself with a college acceptance rate of almost 85%. We believe this
speaks powerfully to the quality of our academic programs and
dedicated staff as well as the calibre of our student body.
Lunenburg High School's proud tradition of athletic achievement
was demonstrated in singular fashion in 1991. LHS was honored as
having the most successful sports program in its division of
Midland-Wachusett Athletic League. Lunenburg was the winner of the
prestigious Richard Mulligan "All Sports Cup" which is annually
awarded to the school which achieves the best cumulative record for
all its sports teams throughout the year.
Thomas Jefferson observed that citizens have the right to expect
efficiency and economy from their public institutions We believe
Jefferson would be very pleased with Lunenburg High School's dedicated
and enlightened efforts to serve the needs of its students and the
larger community. Despite significant financial impediments LHS
continues to distinguish itself for its rigorous and engaging academic
offerings and successful athletic programs. In addition, the high

organizations have been commended by the New England Association of

Lunenburg High School remains committed to providing its students

contributing to the overall quality of life in the community.
