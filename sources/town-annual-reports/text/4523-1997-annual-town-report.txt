MEMORIAM
HENRY D. KELLEY JR.
1925 - 1997
Henry D. Kelley was born in Lunenburg on January 30, 1925. He lived all his life in
Lunenburg and graduated from Lunenburg High School. Mr. Kelley was a self-employed
plumber for many years before he retired. He was a Navy veteran of World War II.
Henry was a longtime member of the Lunenburg Grange and the Lunenburg
Sportsmen's Club. He had also been an auxiliary member of the Lunenburg Police
Department and the Lunenburg Fire Department, serving as assistant chief for the
latter 42 years.
NICHOLAS MINA
1909 - 1997
Nick Mina was born in Boston on July 7, 1909. He lived in Naples, Italy from the age
of two to fifteen and returned to the United States in 1924, living most of his life in
Lunenburg. Mr. Mina owned and operated the former Mina's Barber Shop on lower
Main Street in Fitchburg. Upon his retirement in 1971, he passed the shop on to his
son and became a self-employed landscaper.
He was a charter member of Our Lady of the Lake Church in Leominster. Active in
Town affairs, he served as registrar of voters and census taker for 28 years. He was a
board member of the Lunenburg Memorial Fund, a past president of the Whalom
Recreation Association, and a member of Civic Interest of Lunenburg and the
Lunenburg Friendly Seniors.

BOARD OF SELECTMEN/SEWER COMMISSIONERS
1997 has proven to be a year of many changes for the residents and property owners of the
Town.
The Board of Selectmen acting in their capacity of Sewer Commissioners continued discussions
with the City of Leominster throughout the year and anticipate a memorandum of
understanding to be developed in the Spring of '98. The Facilities Planning Study is nearly
completed and the final hearing necessary to submit this study to the State will also be held in
the Spring. This study must be completed in order for the town to access State funds.
The Special Town Meeting of October 21, 1997 authorized the Town to apply for $400,000 in
zero interest loans through the Commonwealth's Sewer Relief Fund. This funding will enable
the Town to proceed with the design phase of the first priority area identified in the Facilities
Plan as being the Town Center and Whalom Area. This phase is also anticipated to commence in
the Spring, however, it is contingent upon the availability of State funds. After the design is
complete, the Town will be asked to vote for low interest loans from the State to begin the
construction.
This Board has been pleased to have been a part of the addition and renovation to the Eagle
House Senior Center. As the project is nearly complete, we would like to acknowledge all the
members of the Eagle House Building Committee for their diligence and commitment in seeing
this endeavor through. Soon we will be able to step back and reflect with pride on all that was
learned through this long project. The Board, on behalf of the Town, would also like to express
their sincere appreciation to Mary L. Padula, for her contribution while serving with the
Department of Housing and Community Development (DHCD), formerly Executive office of
Community Development (EOCD).
The signalization of Whalom Road and Route 13 (Electric Avenue) has been scheduled with the
State TIP program for 1999. However, as the Town has fulfilled our requirement with the 25%
design submittal, this Board has requested the State place this project as a priority should any
of their previously scheduled projects fail.
In an effort to keep the lines of communication open among all Departments, Boards,
Commissions and Committees, the Board of Selectmen has been scheduling quarterly
department head meetings. Also, separate monthly meetings are held with the Chairs of the
Board of Selectmen, School Committee, Finance Committee and School Superintendent. A
number of accomplishments have been realized as a result of these meetings. It is this Board's
belief that for the good of the Town this communication between all departments must
continue.
In conclusion, this Board would like to thank all of the individuals who work for the Town and
those who volunteer their time and commitment on behalf of the Town by serving on the many
Board's, Committees and Commissions.

BOARD OF ASSESSORS
Lunenburg has now completed its third full year under the regional assessing agreement with
Ashby and Townsend. The arrangement we have with our neighboring communities calls for the
shared expense of retaining a professional, full-time assessor to administer the town's property
valuation and taxation function. We are happy to report that the tri-town agreement has been
both cost effective and well received by the taxpaying public. For example, Lunenburg's
budgeted account for exemptions and overvaluation abatements has been in steady decline. We
have reduced these charges to the town budget from $63,108 in 1994 to $17,601 for Fiscal Year
1997. Additionally, lost revenue and short-term borrowing costs have been significantly reduced
because we now have a consistent track record for the timely billing of property and excise bills.
Part of the success of regionalization can be attributed to a very capable staff. Norma Frost and
Dorothy Musgrove, respectively our administrative assessor and clerk handle day-to-day office
operations - insuring that vital property records are properly maintained, public information
requests satisfied, numerous reports completed and filed, and Board activities supported.
Harald Scheid, our regional assessor, is responsible for all property assessment functions
including market research, valuation model development, tax assessment preparation, valuation
defense, tax forecasting, and Department of Revenue reporting. He is also auditing all forestry,
agricultural, and recreational land exemption accounts to insure their proper assessment. This
year saw Paul Dickey run for successful re-election to a third term. Our chairman, Chris
Comeau with assessors Paul Dickey and Ernie Sund serve in a policy-making and assessment
review capacity.
Our office has successfully implemented a program of annual assessment reviews and valuation
updates. This practice, recommended by the Department of Revenue, insures that assessed
valuations track with real estate market conditions. Though the Town does not realize a tax
windfall from increased valuations, property owners benefit by having a better sense of whether
their properties area being fairly valued. This year, valuations were increased an average of 3-5
percent. This general increase, along with higher than anticipated new construction revenues,
allowed us to reduce the tax rate from $18.46 to $18.16 per $1,000 valuation.
BUILDING DEPARTMENT/ZONING OFFICIAL
The Building Inspectors office is now located on the second floor of the Town Hall with regular
hours on Monday through Thursday 8:00 AM until 3:30 PM and Friday 9:00 AM to 2:00 PM. The
Inspector is available in the office daily and inspections are by appointment. Roger Williams is
the Building Inspector/Zoning Officer for Lunenburg. Lisa Normandin is the Building
Department Secretary.
Building activity in Lunenburg has been steady in the residential districts. The following figures
indicate the number and type of permits issued from this office in 1997: single family homes 38,
commercial additions 2, commercial renovations 2, additions 89, garages 15, renovations 33,
repairs/maintenance 51, pools 22, use and accessory permits 7, signs 19, demolitions 4 and
woodstoves 13. Permit fees in 1997 totaled $20,790.
Residents should be aware that Massachusetts Law requires that all persons, partnerships and
corporations who bid or perform residential contracting (reconstruction, alterations, additions
etc.) for work exceeding $1000.00 be registered with the Commonwealth. This office will
enforce this provision, however you should ask your contractor about his/her registration.
Please contact this office with any questions about this requirement or call for any other
information you may need on Zoning requirements or Building Code issues.

CAPITAL PLANNING
The FY'98 Capital Plan was approved at the Annual 1997 Town Meeting for $381,420.00 for
Capital Expenditures, Improvements and Maintenance. The items in these Articles consisted of
portable classrooms, police cruisers, computer purchases for all Departments, road drainage, a
generator, an irrigation system at Marshall Park and the rebuilding of the Oak Avenue soccer
field.
There are also two items that we committed funding as savings for one half of the cost, the
second half to be placed on the FY'99 Plan. These items are the ambulance and a body mounted
sander, $64,000.00.
Also approved was a Capital Land Purchase of 40+ acres of property in Chapter 61A bordering
Pleasant St. for the amount of $450,000.00
At the Special Town Meeting, October 21, 1997, Capital Expenditures passed in the amount of
$48,814.00 for a Fire Suppression Hood and Sprinkler System for the Eagle House, and
supplements to previous articles for the portable classrooms, and retrofitting of the Police and
Fire Departments' vehicles.
Monies for the FY'97 Plan passed at the Town Meeting were $ 509,450.00. Monies spent to date
from this allocation are $452,526.19. $56,923.81 is still held for ongoing projects. Monies
allocated for the FY'96 Plan were $287,140.00. The amount spend is $278,880.45. Monies still
held for ongoing projects are $8,259.55. Monies allocated for the FY'95 Plan were $633,200.00.
Monies spent are $628,830.42 and monies held for ongoing projects are $4,369.58.
The Vehicle Rotation Plan, which is a rolling document and revised on a yearly basis, will be
presented at the Town Meeting. Mainly, the Plan reflects purchases of utility vehicles such as
one ton dump trucks w/plow to be used primarily by the Highway Department and then passed
down to a Department with a reduced level of use. The plan to incorporate other Departments
in a snow plowing schedule has been worked out by the Board of Selectmen with the Cemetery
and Park Departments.
The Capital Planning Committee has been encouraging a coordination of services for cost
effectiveness, between all Town Departments and the School Department. At the time of this
writing, there are two areas that are being studied; an All Town Director of Facilities and
Maintenance and a Technology Coordinator.
Meetings are being held with the Board of Selectmen, Planning Board, Finance Committee,
Capital Planning Committee and School Representatives to establish a Town Center Plan. This
plan will enable the Capital Planning Committee to proceed with a long range priority plan. The
Long Range Plan will encompass plans for future Fire and Police, Library, School and Town
Government space.
The Committee also established several procedures in tracking the bidding and contract process
for Capital Expenditures. The Town Departments' budgets were presented in both Operating and
Capital bottom lines. This enables the Capital Planning Committee to have a clear picture of the
needs and priorities. Further, the Capital Planning Committee has requested Five Year Plans for
Capital Improvements and Maintenance.
The Committee feels that the Town is making progress using the Criteria of Mandates, Safety,
Need, Crisis, Cost Effectiveness and Ability to Fund for Capital Spending. However, the
Committee also feels that we have a long way to go to achieve cost effectiveness. Cost
effectiveness will aid the Town's ability to fund Capital Expenditures. There is also a need to
remember the Big Man-Little Man analogy for Operating and Capital Budgeting.

CENTRAL MASS MOSQUITO CONTROL PROJECT
The Central Massachusetts Mosquito Control Project (CMMCP) currently provides its services to
28 cities and towns throughout Middlesex and Worcester Counties.
The project's headquarters is located at 111 Otis Street, Northboro, MA. Tours of the
headquarters or visits to field worksites may be arranged by calling the office in advance.
The CMMCP practices Integrated Mosquito Management (IMM), blending state of the art methods
and techniques with expertise, experience, and scientific research to provide our member
communities with modern, environmentally sound, cost effective mosquito control.
The Mosquito Awareness program which we offer to elementary schools in our district has
become very popular. Project staff meet with students and teachers to discuss mosquito
biology, mosquito habitat, and control procedures. Much of the presentation is directed towards
what the children and their families can do to prevent mosquitoes from breeding around their
homes. Live samples of mosquito larvae are included with the presentation, and are left in the
classrooms so that students can watch them develop. Slides, videos, handouts, and coloring
books help to make this an interesting program.
As part of our effort to reduce the need for pesticides we continue to expand our water
management program. By cleaning clogged and overgrown waterways, mosquito breeding can be
reduced, wetlands are restored, and water quality is improved.
Bti mosquito larvacide is used to treat areas where mosquito larvae are found. We routinely
check known breeding sites, but also encourage the public to notify us of any areas they suspect
could breed mosquitoes. Our field crews will investigate all such sites and treat if needed.
Our goal is to handle all mosquito problems with water management or larviciding but we
recognize that there are times when adult mosquito spraying is the only viable solution. In such
cases residential and recreational areas are treated with either hand-held or pick-up mounted
sprayers.
The project's surveillance program monitors adult mosquito and larval population density and is
the backbone for prescribing various control techniques. Rain gauges are set out and data
collected by our surveillance crews in an effort to predict when mosquito breeding will occur.
The project's video "Working for You" is available to anyone interested in learning about
mosquito control and the services provided by the Central Massachusetts Mosquito Control
Project.
CHARTER COMMISSION
At the Town election in May '97 the voters chose to establish a Charter Commission and elected
nine citizens to serve on the Commission.
The Commission developed a questionnaire and sent it to the chairpersons of all committees,
commissions and boards. Nearly all responded. During the first months of meetings the

COMPUTER ADVISORY COMMITTEE
The Computer Advisory Committee continues to try to address the technological needs of the
Town. Over the past year it has become apparent that a Management Information Services
Department is needed. As the Town continues to expand, so does the need for on staff
technological support.
An area of critical importance being faced by the Town is the ability for all departments to
communicate electronically. Therefore, the Committee recommended that the Town implement
an institutional network, commonly referred to as I-Net. Partial I-Net funding was appropriated
at the last Town meeting. Additional funding is requested this year to get the project underway.
The Committee is awaiting additional information from the Cable Company regarding the
services to be provided as stated in the Cable Contract. Various other means of communication
are also being investigated.
It is the position of the Committee that inter-departmental communication and cooperation
between all Town departments is vital to the success of technology within the Town. Currently
the Committee is in discussions with the School Committee regarding the creation of an IS
Coordinator Position to address the needs for both the Town and the School. It is anticipated
that this individual would address areas such as network deployment, purchasing, creation of
desktop/client standards, internet access, training coordination, investigation of grant options
and many other issues. It is the belief of the Committee that the proposed Coordinator is the
first step towards addressing the entire Town's technological needs. It is important for the
Town citizens to realize that the continued financial support of its investment in technology is
imperative.
The Town purchased a software package for payroll, financial management and tax collection.
One of the major objectives of this software was to eliminate the current duplication of work
being performed. The Committee anticipates that remote access to this software will be
available to Departments this year. It is expected that the first department to utilize this
service will be the school.
Members of the Committee continue to donate numerous hours to the Town Department and the
School. Without the volunteer efforts of the members the Committee, the establishment of the
NT network would not have been completed, nor would several of the work stations within the
Town Hall be operating. The Town is very fortunate to have this outstanding technical expertise
available.

BOARD OF HEALTH
The new "Title V" code became effective in September, 1994. As part of the new code, homes
which are being sold must have their septic systems inspected. In Lunenburg the failure rate has
averaged approximately 21%. Much of the work performed by the Board of Health since
September, 1994 has involved review and approval of replacement septic systems for those that
failed.
The new Title V code requires that septic systems with a potential flow of at least 10,000 gallons
per day (gpd) be inspected. The septage lines for the Lunenburg school system interconnect,
resulting in a total potential flow exceeding 10,000 gpd. The tanks and leaching area for the
Lunenburg school system were inspected in 1997. The entire system passed, thus not requiring
any further action. However, if a public sewer system is proposed for the center of Lunenburg, the
Board of Health strongly recommends that the schools be connected to the sewer. Should the
school buildings be reconfigured, the septic system must be required to be in full compliance with
the new Title V code. This means that the septic system may have to be replaced.
Under a State program, low interest loans to replace five failing septic systems were approved by
the Board of Health in 1996 and early 1997. These systems, all located near environmentally
sensitive areas, were installed in 1997. A new State funded program was started in 1997 which
will provide additional funds to repair more failing septic systems. Under this program, the Board
of Health also received funds to develop a database tracking system. The Board of Health is
currently investigating implementing a Geographic Information System which will be utilized to
identify environmentally sensitive areas.

POLICE DEPARTMENT
On August 25, 1997, Lunenburg recorded its fourth murder in 23 years. Our last reported
murder occurred on April 13, 1995. This violent crime was given top priority by your police
department and the C-Pac unit out of the Worcester District Attorney's office. Through hard
work and support from members of our community, we were able to detect and apprehend the
perpetrator of this heinous crime. Sadly, this is a wake up call to all of us, that no matter how
big or small a community might be, this type of crime can and does happen anywhere and all too
frequently.
Most murders start out as a lesser crime and escalate to that degree. Be aware of your
surroundings and take the necessary steps to avoid from looking like or becoming an easy target
for a perpetrator of a violent crime that can escalate to murder. I urge you, the citizens of our
community, to call the police station and report any and all unusual happenings to yourself and
in your neighborhood. Speak with Lt. Lambert, our community policing officer, who will give
you some good advice and information on how to decrease the odds on becoming a victim of a
violent crime. Only through public awareness and community involvement can we reduce these
type of violent crimes. We have a Web-site set up where you can e-mail us with any questions
you might have and we will answer them for you. We also invite you to access our Web-site and
look it over. You can view our Web-site www.net1plus.com/users/lunpd.

MONTACHUSETT REGIONAL VOCATIONAL TECHNICAL SCHOOL
Mony Tech began its twenty-seventh year of operation on August 26 with an enrollment of 1055,
its highest in recent years. The Class of 2001 numbered 330, another recent high. Enrollment
has been increasing steadily the past few years due to a renewed interest in vocational-technical
education and an awareness of its numerous and diverse educational and career opportunities.
Predictions indicate enrollment will continue to increase annually as the global job market
continues to focus on school-to-work preparedness.
A total of 250 incoming students participated this past summer in Monty Tech's first-ever
Freshman Summer Orientation Program. The event was designed to help new students make a
smooth transition from middle school to high school. Participants were able to tour the school,
meet staff and form early friendships in a relaxed setting. They also enjoyed a cookout, games
and special activities.
Increasing enrollment and the need for more space is one of a number of current and long-range
building needs school officials intend to address through a modernization/expansion plan, the
school's first major construction project since the building opened in the fall of 1971. After
receiving the green light last spring from member cities and towns, school officials are currently
finalizing plans, which will be going out to bid in late winter of 1998. Construction is scheduled
to begin in the summer of 1998. The state has approved the project and will reimburse 76
percent of the cost.
At the annual Senior Awards program, held Tuesday, June 3, members of the graduating class
were presented awards and scholarships in recognition of their many accomplishments in the
areas of academics, vocational-technical skills, athletics, student government and citizenship.
On Friday, June 6, members of the Class of 1997 were awarded diplomas and trade certificates
at the annual Commencement Exercises held in the Vietnam Veterans' Memorial Physical
Education Complex at Mount Wachusett Community College, Gardner. Keynote speaker was
former Massachusetts Governor and 1988 Democratic presidential candidate Michael S.
Dukakis.
25
===PAGE 30===
It has been two years since Monty Tech established its innovative and very successful student
motivational program known as "Bulldog Pride". This program was designed to increase school
attendance, decrease discipline problems and create a better school atmosphere. Students who
attend school regularly, stay out of trouble, get good marks and serve as role models qualify to
win prizes at quarterly drawings. In addition, pre-owned, reconditioned cars are awarded at a
grand prize drawing held at a schoolwide assembly the last day of school. School officials say
the program has been successful in meeting their expectations.
This year, Monty Tech's House Building Program entered its third decade. Begun during the
1976-77 school year, the program was established for the primary purpose of providing students
in each building trades with valuable hands-on experience in all phases of residential building
construction. Each year, a complete house is built in one of the district communities. The
lucky home owner is chosen by a lottery-style drawing. All residents of the school district are
eligible to apply. Currently, students and their instructors are working on the program's twenty-
second project, a 2,300 square foot, contemporary-style structure going up in Gardner.
Again during 1997, Monty Tech offered a variety of programs designed to increase awareness
among the region's middle and junior high students about the numerous and diverse career
opportunities currently available through vocational-technical education. These unique
programs include Monty Tech's annual Tour Day Program, whereby prospective students have
the opportunity to learn firsthand what Monty Tech is all about and what it has to offer. This
year, more than 2,600 students participated in the event. "Project VIP" (Vocational Interest
Program), initiated through a School-To-Work grant a few years ago, gives middle school
students a head start on an exciting and interesting career through participation in an after-
school exploratory program. This past summer marked the tenth consecutive year that "Project
Crossroad", another grant funded program, has been providing local disadvantaged youth the
opportunity to earn a paycheck while exploring career programs, learning basic job skills and
receiving an introduction to the world of work.
Other new programs for 1997 worth noting include a collaborative pilot program between Monty
Tech and the Ashburnham-Westminster Regional School District which provides students from
Oakmont Regional School District with advanced technical training not currently available at
their school. Last spring, officials for both school districts signed an agreement making it
possible for Oakmont students to spend part of their school day receiving training in a number
of technical career fields offered at Month Tech.
Another new and innovative program, implemented this fall, addresses the need to expand
remedial services in reading, math and language arts. Known as the SuccessMaker Program, this
comprehensive courseware was developed by Computer Curriculum Corporation and is
correlated to the Massachusetts Curriculum Frameworks set forth by the Education Reform Law.
The program has received much acclaim from participating schools for its success in bringing
students' basic skills up to appropriate grade levels.
Monty-Tech continues to be very proud of its outstanding chapter of the Vocational Industrial
Clubs of America. This past year, students have brought pride and recognition to themselves
and their school by competing for medals at local, district, state and national trade and
leadership skills competitions. In addition, VICA members sponsored a number of fund raising
events and activities which benefited local charitable, veterans' and health organizations. VICA
students also served as tour guides for major school events.
Stratos G. Dukakis, Monty Tech Superintendent-Director since 1986, was selected by Mount
Wachusett Community College, Gardner, as a recipient of its "Service Above Self" Award. It was
presented at the college's second annual convocation ceremony held September 2, in
recognition of Dukakis'; "significant contribution to the education of the citizens of the area."

Monty Tech's Co-Operative Education and Job Placement Program continued its outstanding

regional companies. Through the program, students were able to secure co-op, part-time and
full-time employment during their high school years and/or following graduation. The list of
employers who consistently look to Monty Tech as their primary resource for highly successful
employees grows annually.
Superintendent Dukakis is pleased to report that Monty Tech continues to be very successful in
securing grant monies to fund or defray costs for programs and activities not funded through
the regular school budget. Some of the areas earmarked for these funds include technology,

training and skills development.
Monty Tech's Fine Arts department undertook a number of exciting and creative projects this
past year. They include the completion of a 10-foot by 100-foot mural, depicting life and
commerce in the Twin City area at the turn of the century, painted by students and their
instructors on the side of a commercial building located in Fitchburg. Monty Tech was
commissioned by a local businessman to undertake this challenging project. In addition,
through a grant provided by the Massachusetts Cultural Council, Monty Tech art students, under
the director of an art-in-residence, participated in a unique entitled "Multi-Media Installation-

The school's Head Start Plus Program is making its tenth year of providing local residents with
the skills and occupational training needed to get off the welfare system and gain financial
independence. The program has had many success stories over the years. Through the grant-
funded program, qualified parents of Head Start Preschoolers are able to earn a GED and acquire
marketable job skills. Many are currently working full time for area businesses.
The tenth annual Superintendent's Dinner, the school's major fund raiser, was held April 8 in
the school cafetorium. Over 230 guests, representing area business and industry, attended the
gala event, which is sponsored by Superintendent Dukakis and the school's Business Education
Enrichment Fund (B.E.E.F.), a group of area business and industry executives working to
enhance vocational-technical education through successful school/business partnerships.
Highlight of the evening is a multi-course, gourmet dinner prepared and served by the school's
Culinary Arts department. In addition to raising funds for scholarships and tools-of-the-trade,
the event also provides funding for enrichment and career preparedness programs.
Monty Tech's third annual Scholarship Golf Tournament, held September 18 at the Westminster
Country Club, was a huge success. A total of 144 golfers, the highest number to date,
participated in the event which raised $9,500 to be used for scholarships and start-up funds for
students starting their own business.

participated in the Iowa Tests in the spring of 1997. The Iowa Tests are standardized, norm-

specific conditions to a representative group of students nationwide. Scores from this national
sample become the "norms" that permit local schools to compare the performance of their
students with this national group. Scores from norm-referenced tests do not tell what students
know and what they don't know. They tell only how a student's performance compares with

SPECIAL EDUCATION DEPARTMENT
The energy, enthusiasm, and the uncertainty that accompany educational reform efforts across
the district are also evident in our special education department. Local school improvement
efforts are designed to ensure the inclusion of ALL students in educational reform including
students with disabilities. By aligning our special education program with local, state, and
federal reform initiatives, we are working to ensure that students with disabilities may not only
access, but also participate in the quality education program available to the students of this
community.
Three major principles guide our improvement efforts for these students. First, we must have
high expectations for ALL our students. Second, students with disabilities must have access to
the general curriculum. Finally, there must be a system of accountability. It is not enough that
students with disabilities are in school. It is also important that these students have access to
the same curricular expectations. There is a significant research base that supports a need for
higher expectations for ALL students.
In contrast to past state testing policy, virtually all students will participate in the new
Massachusetts Comprehensive Assessment System (MCAS). Full participation is important
because all students will eventually have to earn a competency determination in order to
graduate from high school. Additionally, there is a need to measure the academic achievement
and progress of ALL students in order to serve them effectively. Toward this end the Lunenburg
Public Schools has made an up-front commitment to include all students in the district's system
of accountability for outcomes and student performance.
In Lunenburg, special and general educators are working to link the Individual Education Plans
(IEP) of students with disabilities to the general education curricula. Educators and parents of
these students are responsible for establishing high expectations, considering how students will
access the general curriculum, and ensuring the student's participation in state and district-
wide assessments. If it is determined that the student is unable to participate in the MCAS an
alternative assessment mechanism must be developed to ensure accountability for that
student's program.
Approximately two hundred students in the district are currently identified with a disability and
receiving special education and related services. This figure represents 11% of the total school
population. This percentage is consistent with the national average, but well below the state
average. Eleven students receive services in out-of-district placements.
A committed and well-qualified staff of special education teachers and related service personnel
work in concert with other professionals in the district to provide a quality program for each
student. Related service staff include four speech/language therapists, an occupational
therapist, a school psychologist, and a social worker. Additionally, a dedicated group of parents
involved with the Parent Advisory Council-Special Achievers of Lunenburg (PAC-SAL) provide
support and assistance for students, parents, and professionals.
For additional information about special education programs in the district contact a building
principal or the Director of Instruction.
CURRICULUM AND INSTRUCTION
Lunenburg Public Schools continue to aggressively pursue educational reform at the local level.
The administration in conjunction with the School Committee has developed a framework for
reform that builds upon the state's initiatives. The framework also serves to focus efforts
toward improving student learning. Under this framework key areas of school operation are

focused upon this outcome. The key areas are: (1) Learning and Performance Standards, (2)
Assessment, (3) School Improvement Planning, (4) Staff Development, (5) Parent/Community
Involvement, (6) Services to Students At Risk, and (7) Facilities and Budget. Following is a
summary of efforts in each of these areas to date.
Learning and Performance Standards. Learning standards are statements of what students will
do. A performance standard indicates how well students are expected to perform relative to the
learning standard. In Massachusetts learning and performance standards have been identified
and are articulated within the curriculum frameworks and curriculum guides. Districts have
been encouraged to look at these frameworks and implement them as much as possible.
Lunenburg began the process of ensuring that local curricula were aligned with the state
frameworks shortly after their release. The district is in its third year of a five-year curricula
update cycle. Mathematics and Science/Technology curricula have been revised and adopted.
English/Language Arts and Integrated Health area are being revised this year. World Language,
History/Social Sciences, and Business/Computers areas will be articulated for adoption spring,
1999.
Assessment. An assessment task force will begin work in the spring of this year to develop a
comprehensive assessment plan for the district. The task force will approach its task viewing
assessment not only from a testing and performance validating perspective, but all from a
feedback perspective. Assessment as feedback is directly connected to performance standards.
This enables classroom instruction to be directly linked with assessment.
This spring will mark the first administration of the new Massachusetts Comprehensive
Assessment System (MCAS). Students in 4th, 8th and 10th grade will be administered
performance assessments in core academic areas. The MCAS is considered a high-stakes, on-
demand assessment process. Beginning with the Class of 2003 (this year's seventh grade) the
awarding of a diploma will be contingent in part upon performance upon a student's
performance on the MCAS at the 10th grade level. The administration, faculty, and staff are
engaged in numerous activities to prepare students for this extensive assessment and to inform
parents and the public about the system.
The district will continue to utilize standardized assessment instruments (Iowa Test of Basic
Skills and the Iowa Tests of Educational Development) to evaluate student learning.
Additionally, the task force will establish an assessment policy to ensure that classroom
assessments are valid and reliable.
School Improvement Planning. In January a new model for school improvement planning model
was adopted. The goal of the model is to develop a learning organization that will help students
learn what they need to know in order to function as productive citizens in the future. The
school improvement process provides for the development of school improvement plans that are
(1) based upon a school's profile, (2) establish challenging student centered goals for all
students, (3) make explicit how goals will be measured, and (4) articulate clear action plans for
accomplishing the selected goals.
Staff Development. Staff development in the district is focused upon student performance
standards, assessed student results, and individual professional development needs. Our staff
development practices are based upon a continuous improvement philosophy and are a visible
district priority.
Parent/Community Involvement. Many opportunities for program planning are provided to
parents and members of the community including membership on standing committees that
make decisions about programs and occasional memberships on committees for a specific
purpose.
