It is once again my privilege to present the Fiscal Year 2021 Budget Message for the Preliminary 
Budget, which is an overview of the operating and capital budget, identifies major variations from 
the current operating budget, and explains the Town Manager’s rationale for budget 
recommendations.  The budget recommendation includes the most up to date revenue projections 
and expenditure requests. The Preliminary Budget, as presented, provides for funding operating 
budgets at a reasonable level and continues to focus on addressing the needs of the Town while 
balancing the cost to taxpayers and maintaining fiscal sustainability.   
 
The proposed budget is balanced in two respects:  it provides a balanced approach to the needs of 
all the departments of the Town and is also balanced based upon the use of reoccurring revenues 
for operating costs and does not use free cash or stabilization towards the operating budget. Fiscal 
sustainability is a theme throughout by using conservative revenue estimates that are not one time 
revenues to support current expenditures and adheres to the town’s financial policies to maintain a 
certain level in the town’s reserve accounts.  The preliminary budget uses consistent revenue 
percentage increases over the current fiscal year, with the exception of projected New Growth.  
When the target budget was created, New Growth was projected at $350,000, based on the recent 
trend of declining New Growth.  Just this past week, this figure was even further reduced to 
$234,000 based on new information provided by the town’s assessing service firm.  
 
In the process of developing the preliminary budget, departments were given target budgets to 
meet that included known personnel costs for existing staff, known contractual increases, level 
funded expense line items and included conservative estimates for the “unknowns”, such as health

Other variations from the current operating budget include: 
 Addressing an identified need by the Board of Health and the Town Clerk to increase 
hours for staff in those offices.  This will provide more coverage in those offices during 
current office hours and more appropriately aligns the hours needed for those positions. 
 Inclusion of a new position that would be entirely funded with grant funds for an

requirements.  This continues the Town’s goal to improve recycling within the Town. 
 Elimination of the Assistant Town Mechanic position.  This position was added to the 
budget in FY19 but not filled due to the abilities of the Town Mechanic.  Due to other 
growing needs in that department, these funds are being proposed to be reallocated to 
address ADA compliance and additional funding for Town Facilities. 
 
Reserves and FY 2021 Capital Plan: 
The budget proposes to use free cash, non-reoccurring revenue, to fund a portion of the FY21

(Other Post-Employment Benefits) Trust Fund.  By setting aside these funds in the town’s 
stabilization account and OPEB Trust Fund, it will ensure the town is financially secure and is 
planning for their known future capital needs and future retiree health benefits.

Budget Process: 
  
The budget process for Fiscal Year 2021 began not long after the start of Fiscal Year 2020.  The 
Finance Committee began discussing the budget process in July and the Capital Planning 
Committee began their process in September.  The Capital Planning Committee continued utilizing 
a new data accumulation process using Google Sheets and this year added additional criteria in 
which to evaluate projects including categories such as effectiveness of government, effects on 
operating costs, effects on revenues, and how the project contributes to such things as economic 
growth, public safety, public health, and education.  Based on responses to these categories, a 
project received a composite score.  The CPC used this information as a starting point for 
prioritizing projects and provided a prioritized list to the Town Manager on January 7th. 
 
During the week of January 20th, the Town Manager presented a Five Year Financial Forecast and 
a Capital Plan to the Finance Committee and Board of Selectmen.  The capital plan presented 
made no changes from the recommendation the Town Manager received from the Capital Planning 
Committee.  The Five Year Financial Forecast projects revenues and expenditures based on 
historical trends, a variety of assumptions, and known future expenditures such as contractual 
increases and large capital projects. These projections are a tool in order to assist town officials to 
prepare the ensuing years budgets to maintain fiscal sustainability and how expenditures will be 
addressed if a deficit is imminent.  Here is a link to the Five Year Financial Forecast presentation 
for FY21-FY25: https://www.lunenburgma.gov/sites/default/files/field/files-docs/fy21-
fy25_financial_forecast_presentation_0.pdf . 
 
In mid-February the Town Manager prepares a preliminary budget recommendation that is 
presented to the Finance Committee.  The framework for the budget begins with looking at the 
levy capacity, estimated revenues, contractual increases, and using reasonable estimates for 
expenditures.  This year departments were asked to meet a target budget that included contractual 
increases and level funded most expense categories.  Certain departments had target budgets that 
included added personnel and certain expenditure increases including the costs to add an additional 
full time police officer and the costs to add an additional full time firefighter.  On the expenditure 
side, the target budget included an additional $50,000 for pavement management per an 
established past practice in order to achieve a total of $1,000,000 in the operating budget for 
pavement management by 2032.  For any requests above the target budget, departments were 
directed to provide a justification such as the effect on town services, if the increase would 
generate revenues, or address a need for increased service levels.  After meeting with each 
department and updating the budget with any new information, such as the Worcester County 
Retirement assessment, and an updated maximum increase for health insurance, the FY2021 
preliminary proposal was drafted.

taxes are levied on real property (land and buildings) and personal property (equipment) used by

state law now allows this to be performed every five years.  Under the provisions of Proposition 2 
1/2, property taxes, in the aggregate, may not exceed 2 ½ percent of their full and fair cash value.

Local Receipts 
 
Local Receipts are made up of Motor Vehicle Excise taxes, fees, fines, permits, licenses, and other 
departmental revenue or charges for services.  These are often labeled “elastic revenues” as they 
may fluctuate with the economy.  Local Receipts account for 7% of the town’s revenues.  The 
estimate for Local Receipts for the FY21 budget assumes an overall increase of 2.27% or $68,450 
over the FY2020 Local Receipts estimate.  One of the FY2021 budget goals is to maintain a 
conservative Local Receipts estimate in order to create a healthy Free Cash figure when actual 
Local Receipts exceed the Local Receipts estimate.  Used for non-operating expenditures, Free 
Cash allows the Town to address their capital needs and put funds into reserves.

The FY2021 Preliminary Budget includes all contractual increases including agreed upon COLA 
increases for non-union employees under the Salary Administration Plan, all salary and related 
expenses per Collective Bargaining Units, and increases for employees with an Employment 
Agreement.  The Town has begun contract negotiations with the Fire and Clerical Unions and if 
not settled by the May 2, 2020 Annual Town Meeting will request a transfer of funds at a future 
town meeting from the Salary Reserve Account.   
 
Outside of allocating funding for the long term staffing plans for the Police and Fire Departments 
and $50,000 for pavement management, this year’s budget proposal addresses needs expressed an 
increase in staffing hours for the Town Clerk and Board of Health offices.  The hours for both of 
these positions were cut some years back and each department has expressed that the current hours 
are insufficient to address the workload.  Other priorities in this proposed budget address the 
hourly rate of the registration and election workers, the need for new voting booths, additional IT 
software costs, and reallocating funds from the elimination of the Assistant Mechanic position to 
address ADA public rights-of-way compliance and maintenance of town facilities.

General Government 
 
The General Government category includes the Administrative, Financial, and Planning Offices of 
the town.  Total spending in this category is recommended at $1,617,795, which is an increase of 
5.81% or $88,773.   The proposal includes all contractual increases, audit costs, legal expenses, an 
increase in hours for the Assistant Town Clerk from 24 to 32 hours a week, an increase in salary 
costs for election workers, an increase in software maintenance costs, voting booths for the Town 
Clerk’s office, and expenditures to provide general government services.  The Town Clerk 
expressed during the budget process that the requests in the Town Clerk’s budget are a reflection 
of ensuring that office can function in the event of the current Town Clerk’s eventual retirement.

Police Department 
 
The Lunenburg Police Department employs 17 full time Police Officers, 9 Reserve Intermittent 
Police Officers, a Public Safety Coordinator (also counted as a Reserve Officer), and a Desk 
Officer (also counted as a Reserve Officer).  The Police Department is in operation 24 hours a day, 
7 days a week.  For many years the Chief had requested additional staffing based upon the 
department’s level of activity.  During the FY19 budget development process, the Police Chief 
proposed a 10 year plan of adding one additional full time officer each fiscal year to reach 
adequate staffing levels and making these changes incrementally.  The last two fiscal years, an 
additional police officer was added each year.  Adding one additional officer last year does not 
resolve the Police Department’s need for additional officers.  Based on state and national data on 
the number of officers per capita in communities of similar size, Lunenburg falls below the state 
average.  There has been significant growth in housing units over the years and the Police 
Department’s response to calls for service correlates to the growth in the community.  The increase 
in the FY21 Police Department budget reflects one additional Police Officer and related expenses.  
The preliminary budget includes the associated costs for an additional FT police officer but also 
reduces the Shift Differential line and Payroll for Lock-Up by a total of $22,000.  The total 
increase over the previous fiscal year is $108,335, which is a 6.6% increase. 
 
Fire Department 
 
The Fire Department is comprised of a full time Fire Chief, 6 full time Firefighter/EMTs, an EMS 
Coordinator, and on call reserve firefighters and on call EMT’s. The Fire Department faces similar 
challenges as the Police Department in an increase in service calls due to the growing population, 
as well as the need for growing the Department to be able to run at the paramedic level in order to 
provide a higher level of medical care alongside a growing percentage of residents in their senior 
years.  The Fire Chief proposed a five year plan to add one firefighter/paramedic each fiscal year 
to reach adequate staffing to operate a 24/7 Paramedic Level Service. This plan was accelerated at 
the May 2018 Annual Town Meeting through a citizen’s petition to hire two firefighter/EMT’s 
instead of one firefighter/Paramedic in FY19 and in FY20 a seventh full time firefighter was not 
included in the budget due to a need to address a significant rise in overtime costs . The 
preliminary budget for the Fire Department includes the costs in the projected five year plan to add 
a seventh full time Firefighter/Paramedic.  The Fire Department currently has 24/7 coverage at the 
Advanced Level, with Sundays covered by two per diem firefighter/EMT’s and one per diem and 
two full time firefighter/EMT’s covering Monday thru Saturday shifts. The total increase over the 
previous fiscal year is $86,855, which is a 7.6% increase.

The Department of Public Works preliminary budget is slightly less than the target budget they 
were asked to meet and the Town Buildings and Facilities preliminary budget is slightly higher 
than the target budget. This was achieved by the elimination of the Assistant Mechanic position 
and reallocating those funds to both the Facilities budget and a new account under Highway to 
address Americans Disabilities Act (ADA) compliance in the public right-of-way.

Solid Waste/Recycling Program 
 
In Fiscal Year 2020 there was a substantial increase in the Solid Waste/Recycling costs due to the 
global market changes in recycling stemming from China’s National Sword Policy.  The Town 
began paying for its recyclables on July 1, 2019, adding approximately $62,000 to the cost of that 
program and an increase in Pay-As-You-Throw (PAYT) bags.  This program is self-sustaining and 
any surplus in revenues from the PAYT bags goes towards funding the next fiscal year.  We are 
projecting no cost increase for this program in FY 2021.

Unclassified General Government 
 
The Unclassified category of the operational budget includes Health Insurance, Workers 
Compensation Insurance, Liability Insurance, Police/Fire Injured on Duty Insurance, and Reserves.   
 
The Town purchases Health Insurance for all active and retired employees through the 
Massachusetts Inter-local Insurance Association (MIIA).  By purchasing insurance through this 
cooperative, we are able to receive the benefit of being a part of the largest municipal joint 
purchasing group.  As a result of our membership in MIIA and due to the fact that we have 
favorable claims history, our rates have also been favorable. MIIA provided an estimate of a 6.5% 
increase for Health Insurance renewal rates for FY21 in December and released their maximum 
rate range increases to members in January of a low of -1.5%, an average of 3.7%, and a maximum 
of 8%.  Based on the town’s loss ratios, our insurance representative confirmed we could adjust 
our maximum health insurance for active employees to 3.7% and 5% for retirees.  We will receive 
our actual renewal rates by the end of February and hope to see that figure decrease again. 
 
Another noteworthy point is that 38% of the Health Insurance budget is health insurance for active 
town employees and 62% is retiree health insurance for both town and school employees.  The 
active health insurance portion of the FY21 budget is approximately $966,000 out of the 
$2,489,643.  The estimate for FY21 School retirees health insurance is approximately $1,088,0000 
and $413,000 for Town retiree health insurance. 
 
The estimates for Workers Compensation, Liability, and Police/Fire IOD insurances have not been 
received but the budget level funds these budgets based on current year’s expenditures and the 
reward and participation credits the town receives for performing best practices in risk 
management and credits for attending trainings and webinars.

Retirement Assessment 
 
The Worcester Regional Retirement System annually provides each member Town an assessment 
based upon anticipated retirement benefits to be paid out.  The Town’s FY2021 assessment has 
been certified at $1,502,144, which includes an early payment discount of approximately $28,028.  
This is an overall increase of $147,791 or a 10.9% increase from last fiscal year.   
 
Non-Appropriated Expenditures 
Cherry Sheet Charges, Cherry Sheet Offsets, funds for the Overlay account, and Tax Title make up 
the non-appropriated expenditures that are raised on the tax recapitulation sheet that is submitted to 
the Department of Revenue to set the tax rate.  The Cherry Sheet Charges and Cherry Sheet 
Offsets are based on the Governor’s House 1 Budget Proposal and indicate a decrease in the 
State/County Charges and Choice/Charter Tuition Assessments but an increase in Cherry Sheet 
Offsets.  We will watch these closely in the House and Senate budget proposals to see if they 
fluctuate.  For the preliminary budget proposal, the Tax Title Account is recommended to be level 
funded at $24,000 to address additional tax title properties and the funds required to go through the 
tax title process, however, the amount the Tax Collector requested be appropriated for tax title is 
$26,400.  As the unknown costs become known, the goal would be to revisit this estimate. 
 
 
 
 
Conclusion 
 
The preliminary budget presented provides for a balanced budget that funds certain increases to 
address the town’s need for increased public safety services, the necessity for certain staff 
increases, funds contractual increases, and adheres to the Town’s financial policies.  The target 
budget of a 2.5% increase for the Lunenburg Public Schools will be revisited once the Town 
receives their health insurance renewal rates at the end of February and the assessment from 
Montachusett Regional Vocational School in early March.  Given the information known at this 
point in time in regards to revenue projections and known expenses, the Town Manager’s proposed 
FY21 budget aims to address the needs of the community while balancing the effect on taxpayers.
