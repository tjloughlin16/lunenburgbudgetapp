Property Taxes
Property taxes are the largest, single revenue source for the Town. Property taxes are levied on real property (land and buildings) and
personal property (equipment) used by non-manufacturing business firms. In accordance with State Law, the Board of Assessors
revalues all taxable real property at fair market value every three- (3) years. FY2015 was our last triennial revaluation year. In
addition, the Town conducts annual interim adjustments in order to reflect the changes in market conditions. The next full revaluation
will be conducted in FY2018. Under the provisions of Proposition 2 ½, property taxes, in the aggregate, may not exceed 2 ½% of
their "full and fair cash value." This limit is known as the "levy ceiling." Annual levy increases may not exceed 2 ½% more than the
previous year's levy plus the taxes added from any new properties- New Growth- added to the tax rolls. Any Proposition 2 ½ override
and/ or debt exclusion amounts voted are added to the levy limit.

Aid. In his short time in office, Governor Baker has shown himself to be an advocate for cities and towns. He released Chapter 90
Funds allocated but held by former Governor Patrick and has vowed not to resolve the current budget deficit by reducing Local Aid.
Although FY2016 appears that it will be as difficult for the State as it is for localities, it does not appear that the Governor will
propose, not with the Legislature support, any decreases in State Aid, however, until the release of the Governor's Budget, one can't be
completely sure. I have proposed modest increases in State Aid in some categories, while level funding others. The total preliminary
estimate for F 12016 IS $1,126,144, which includes a 2% increase, or $112,117, in Chapter 70 Education Aid. I have not projected an
increase in Unrestricted General Government Aid, which still remains just below what was received in FY2005 ($941,205) and a full
30% lower ($400k) than what was received in FY2008 & 2009. Adjusted for inflation, we are approximately $600k below where we
could expect to be had UGGA aid been increased 2.5% per year.
Given the late start on the State Budget this year, it is likely that we will not see a Joint Aid Resolution until the very end of our local
budgeting process. Consequently, we may be required to have a Special Town Meeting in the Fall to re-balance the budget prior to
setting the FY2016 Tax Rate.
Below is a chart comparing projected FY2016 State Aid to prior fiscal years. The chart shows that we will expect to receive less in
State Aid in FY16 than in FY15, however, FY15 includes an amount of $183,618 in Smart Growth 40S, which was somewhat
unanticipated and not actually received until October 2014. The Town may receive an additional 40S payment in FY2016, but again
not until October 2015, and not unless appropriated through the State Budget.

Available Funds
Available Funds is really a "catch all" category. Many Available Funds are one-time revenues, which cause a wide variance from the
total amount available from one year to the next. Other sources of Available Funds are transfers from Enterprise Funds or Betterment
Accounts to fund charges within the General Fund.
A total of $35,064 in Unexpended Capital Articles funding will be used to support this year's CIP.
As of July 1, 2014, we had certified Free Cash in the amount of $484,117. I have included an appropriation of $183,618 from Free
Cash. This amount is equivalent to the amount received in FY2015 from the State for the Smart Growth 40S payment. These funds are
paid by the State to help offset additional costs of Educating children residing in a Smart Growth District. Application for payment is
made in September with a State award coming at the end of October. FY15 was the first year that the Town received an award by the
State. Future awards are subject to successful documentation of additional Education expenses and State appropriation. Because of
the timing of receipt of the funding, I recommend that we appropriate the prior year's receipts in the upcoming budget. This way, we
will know exactly what will be available.
After appropriating $183,618 from Free Cash, we have a balance of $300,499. At the time the 5-Year Financial Forecast was
developed, I had hoped to use much of this balance to partially offset the FY16 Capital Plan and to fund the additional deposit to the
Stabilization Fund which is needed to maintain the balance at 5% of the Operating Budget. Unfortunately, due to the projected
overrun in the FY2015 Snow & Ice Account, I do not currently anticipate that any of this remaining balance will be available. Another

Police Department
The Lunenburg Police Department budget supports FT and 16 Reserve Officers and a Public Safety Coordinator. The Department
is broken down into three divisions: Administrative; Operations; and Patrol, which are further divided into the subdivisions of:
Criminal Investigations; Traffic Control; and Community Outreach. The Chief of Police is responsible for managing the day-to-day
operations of the all divisions. The Public Safety Coordinator provides secretarial and administrative support to the Chief. The
Lieutenant serves as the Operations Commander, in charge of the Crinimal Investigations Division, Traffic Control Officer, Patrol
Division and Reserve Force. Additionally, the Lieutenant is the department's Public Information Officer, Court Liaison, Internal
Affairs Investigator and responsible for Firearms Licensing.
Patrol is assigned town-wide, around the clock, 7 days per week, 24 hours per day, 365 days per year. In addition to Officers, Patrol
includes 2 Street Supervisors (Sergeants) and a Community Outreach Officer: One Detective/ Patrolman is assigned to Criminal
Investigations. This individual acts as the Domestic Violence Advocate, Drug Enforcement Officer assigned to the North Worcester
County Drug Task Force, and works in collaboration with State and Federal agencies to investigate drug crimes. The Detective is a
qualified crime scene examiner and is trainted to investigate a range of crimes, including: sexual assault, robbery, burglary, identity
theft, and credit card fraud.
One Patrolman is assigned to Traffic Enforcement for 2 day and 2 evening shifts per week. The Traffic Officer focuses on the
enforcement of all traffic laws, with an emphasis on speed enforcement, and is trained in crash analysis and reconstruction. The
Officer is also responsible for investigating all motor vehicle crashes. As such, the Officer is on-call for any motor vehicle crash which
results in serious bodily injury or death. Other responsibilities include identifying and resolving potential road hazards and dangerous
road conditions.
One Patrolman is assigned to Community Outreach for 2 day and 2 evening shifts per week. This Community Outreach Officer
ocuses on providing personal customer service to the community to combat persistent complaints. This Officer provides support to
he Patrol and Detective divisions, acts as the Sex Offender Registrar, represents the Department at School programs and communit
events, and conducts firearms licensing during the evening hours and on weekends for the convenience of citizens.
The department maintains a six- (6) cell lock up detainment area for arrestees not released from custody.

Fire Department
This category of spending covers the Fire Department and both Hydrant and Radio Maintenance expenses. The Department provides
Fire, Rescue and Emergency Medical Services at the BLS (Basic Life Support) level services for the entire Town. Additionally, the
department provides many preventative services, fire code inspections, training and specialized response such as trench and water
rescue.
The department operates twenty-four- (24) hours per day, seven- (7) days per week, year round, with a full-time/ career staff of five-
(5) and forty-one (41) Call Personnel, either Firefighter, EMT or Firefighter-EMT.
The career personnel staff the Station Monday
through Friday, 6AM to 6PM and respond to all calls, along with available call personnel as needed. The Station is unmanned 6PM to
6AM on weeknights and 6PM Friday to 6AM Monday. All personnel carry radio pagers and are "toned out" over the radio when
needed. Appropriate personnel then respond to the Station and pick up needed equipment, or respond directly to the incident location
if they are nearby. The number of personnel dispatched varies depending on the type of incident, from 1 to 3 personnel, to the entire
department if the incident warrants that level of response. Career personnel are expected to respond to off hours calls as needed.
During hours that the Station is unmanned, 2 EMT's are paid a "hard call" stipend to be available for EMS calls to guarantee a
response. In addition, each Fire Officer takes a turn in rotation to be available as a "Duty Officer" on weekends.

Radio Watch/ Dispatch
In previous years, the cost associated with providing Emergency 911 Dispatch was included within the Fire Department Budget. In
FY2012, the Town decided to join the Nashoba Regional Dispatch District, along with the towns of Harvard and Lancaster and
Devens. Special legislation has been sought, and finally approved in January 2015, to allow for the creation of the district and all 4
communities have signed an Intermunicipal Agreement. Funding for all capital aspects of this endeavor have been funded through
State and Federal Grants. The 4 members of the District will be responsible for the Annual Operations, which will be divided equally
and assessed. The district officially began operations as of July 1, 2013. Lunenburg was the first to transition, in June 2013. The
other 3 communities migrated soon thereafter. There is already significant interest from other communities to join, though the District
has agreed that there will be no new members until there is a solid 2-year track record.
Saving money was never a stated goal of this transition. The driving force was cost avoidance and providing a high level of service.
To partially offset the void created by the loss of 24/7 coverage at the Station with Dispatch Personnel, a Desk Officer position was
created. This position also provides coverage/ monitoring of the Lock Up Facility, Monday - Friday, 7:00 AM - 3:00 PM. This
position also provides valuable assistance to both the Fire and Police Departments by routing inquires or complaints to the appropriate
personnel, routing requests for service to the appropriate personnel, issuing and logging permits, and/ or writing reports and taking
witness statements.
The total recommended appropriation for Radio Watch/ Dispatch for FY2016 is $257,293. The FY16 Assessment for the district will
remain the same as the prior year.

For the first time in FY2014, all costs associated with public buildings were removed from individual department budgets and into a
central location- the Public Buildings appropriation. This realignment was made as there had been no consistency in the appropriation
and payment of utility bills. For instance, all utility charges for the public safety building are funded in the Police Department Budget.

been said that there is an interest in representing the true cost of departments, which is why funding is provided in some. However, as
demonstrated, this is not the case universally. Further, by having a decentralized operation, more individuals are involved, decreasing
efficiency. The appropriation for Utilities for the Public Library will stay with that department for now, to insure the Town meets its
Minimum Appropriation Requirement (only direct appropriations are counted toward the MAR).

This budget provides for the repayment of principal and interest on the Town's long-term General Fund debt. The Town issues debt
pursuant to votes of Town Meeting to provide funding for major projects, in accordance with Massachusetts General Law, Chapter 44,
Sections 7 and 8. The maximum amount of debt for each project is authorized by Town Meeting, and then the Town Treasurer issues
the bond after working with the Town Manager and the Town's Financial Advisor to design and structure the bond, and with the
approval of the board of Selectmen.
The General Debt Limit of a city or town consists of a Normal Debt Limit and a Double Debt Limit. The Normal Debt Limit is five-
(5) percent of the valuation of taxable property as last equalized by the State Department of Revenue. According to the most recent
statistics, the Town of Lunenburg's EQV for 2012 was $1,220,468,700. The Normal Debt Limit (of 5% EQV) is therefore,
$61,023,435. As of June 30, 2014, the Town's permanent outstanding debt was $22, 140,230.
A city or town can authorize debt up to this amount without State approval. It can authorize debt up to twice this amount (the Double
Debt Limit) with the approval of the state Municipal Oversight Board, which is composed of the State Treasurer, the State Auditor, the
Attorney General and the Director of Accounts.
There are many categories of General Obligation debt which are exempt from and do not count against the General Debt Limit.
Among others, these exempt categories include revenue anticipation notes, grant anticipation notes, emergency loans, loans exempted
ax perimen finanain scA a 0, 2 ba, the Tow had 4209 in one bonds outside the debt limit for andrill and
Sewer Construction projects.
All debt obligations carry a bond rating. In FY2014, the Town's bond rating was increased two notches, from "AA-" to "AA+." This
increase means that future bond issues will carry lower interest rates, saving the Town money. It is important to note that the Town's
bond rating has increased 4 grades since FY2009, when we were listed as a A rating (A to At to AA- to AA to AA+). This most

Cherry Sheet Charges & Underestimates are certain charges that are levied on the Town by State Law and are not subject to Town
Meeting appropriation. The principal items are Mosquito Control District, MBTA Assessment and Regional Transit. Other charges
are for the Air Pollution Control District (Ch. 111), Special Education (Ch. 71B), and the RMV Non-Renewall Surcharge (Ch.90; Ch.
60A).
Cherry Sheet Offsets are amounts that are included on the Budget Summary to now reflect only two state revenue programs that are to
be spent without being part of the appropriated budget. These small grants programs are for the public library and the school lunch
program. We show these as "offsets" in the Non-Appropriated Expenditures category because the revenue for them is included in the
total Cherry Sheet Revenues, and must be used for direct expenditures to support the grant program.
Cherry Sheet Assessments are for tuition payments for Lunenburg students attending school outside of the district, either at a public
school in another town or at a charter school.
