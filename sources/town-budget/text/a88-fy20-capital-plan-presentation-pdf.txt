What qualifies as a 
Capital Project? 
 
 
The Town’s Policy on Capital Expenditures (Financial 
Policy Section 3.02) defines capital projects as items that 
cost $10,000 or more and have a useful life of three years or 
longer. 
===PAGE 3===
Capital Planning Process 
• The Capital Planning Committee (CPC) creates a Capital Plan that 
consists of all capital requests made by each town department for the 
next five fiscal years.   
• The CPC’s first meeting to begin the FY20 Capital Planning process was 
on October 2nd, the Capital Planning documents were sent to 
departments on November 1st and the Capital Planning Committee met 
with departments throughout November to review all the capital 
project requests as a group. 
• Based on the meetings with the Departments and the budget provided 
by the Town Manager, the CPC prioritized all the capital project 
requests for FY20 and provided the prioritized list to the Town 
Manager.  
• Per the Town Charter, the Town Manager may modify and/or add to 
this list of capital projects as part of the proposed budget for the next 
fiscal year. This capital budget is presented at the Annual Town 
Meeting, amended as necessary to reflect the will of Town Meeting, and 
voted on. This plan reflects the same plan that the CPC provided the 
Town Manager.

• Replacement of 1995 Pumper (Engine 4): 
o Has served beyond its expected life span;  
o Third year that it has been requested for replacement 
o Has had approx. $65,000 in repairs in last 4-5 years, most recently 
with pump replacement.   
o Replacement of unit would see increased reliability, better safety 
for personnel and citizens, and decreased maintenance costs. 
o Two unsuccessful attempts were made through federal grants to 
replace the Ladder Truck but due to the competitiveness of the 
grant and that the federal benchmark for eligibility for this grant is 
based on having an average age of the fleet 20 years, 
replacement of Engine 4 through this grant would have the same 
result.  
o Replacement will lower annual maintenance costs for next 5-10 
years.  
o Capital Request for $685,000.

FY2020 Fire Dept. Capital 
Requests 
• Replacement of 2008 Ambulance (Rescue 2): 
o  Vehicle at the end of its life cycle; 
o Second year requested for replacement.   
o Current unit serves as back-up for other ambulance but is not 
strictly reserve unit.   
o Unit experiencing increased maintenance costs and out of 
service for repairs.  Stress cracks noted in annual State 
Ambulance Inspection.   
o New unit would become primary and 2014 reserved for back-up  
unit.  The 2008 would then be used as the Water Rescue unit, 
used less frequently.  
o No grant funding for ambulances.   
o Replacement would equate to decreased maintenance costs for 
next 4-5 years and increases reliability.  
o Capital Request for $310,000.
