FY2021 Budget Timeline to Date 
• Finance Committee began budget discussions in July 2019 
• Capital Planning Committee began in September 2019, met with 
departments in November and December 2019 
• Town Manager sent target budgets to departments in December 
2019 due back on January 2, 2020 
• The Capital Planning Committee finalized prioritization of projects 
for the FY21 Plan on January 7, 2020 
• Town Manager presented FY21 Capital Plan and FY21-FY25 Five Year 
Financial Forecast at the end of January 2020 
• Town Manager presented a Preliminary FY21 Budget in mid-
February 
• Due to the economic impact as a result of the COVID-19 pandemic, 
the FY21 Recommended Budget had to be revamped beginning in 
mid-March until now.

Preparing the FY2021 Recommendation 
• Before COVID-19, the goal was to create a balanced budget that 
funded operating budgets using recurring revenues at current 
service levels with some exceptions. 
• When developing a budget in a stable economic environment, these 
three phrases are appropriate: 
1. Expenditures are a reflection of the cost of providing a level of services 
to residents of all ages in our community, to visitors traveling or doing 
business in Lunenburg, and maintaining or improving our assets. 
2. As a town, we provide services to students, we provide health and 
human services to seniors and veterans, recreational services, public 
safety to all 
3. The balance is to provide the best services possible given the available 
resources, maintain our assets, and put aside funding for anticipated 
capital and into our “rainy day fund”. 
 
 
===PAGE 4===
Impact of COVID-19 on Revised 
Recommended Budget: 
• In the current environment, in order to address the economic 
uncertainties and the impact on our revenues, the effect of not 
being able to include all the priority needs of various departments 
will have an impact on the level of services. 
• Current recommendation creates an excess levy capacity of 
$1,055,950 by postponing new positions and certain expenditures in 
order to absorb a decline in revenues beyond level funding local 
receipts and State Aid.  The current recommendation does not 
represent the needs of various departments. 
• Current recommendation  does not fully meet the Town’s Financial 
Policies. 
• Does not use Certified Free Cash on operating budget, which should 
be a cardinal rule when balancing a budget.

FY 2021 Recommended Budget 
Conclusions 
 
• Public Hearing recommended budget is radically different than 
recommended budget from the beginning of March. 
• Postpones decisions on new public safety positions and added hours for TC 
and BOH, Pavement Management,  and ADA funds until the Fall Special 
Town Meeting. 
• Defers Capital Projects originally in FY21 Capital Plan and deposit into OPEB 
until the Fall Special Town Meeting. 
• Funds existing staff contractual obligations  and non-union salary increases. 
• Uses recurring revenues for operating costs; no draw on Stabilization Fund 
or Free Cash for operating expenses 
• Net loss of one town employee position 
• Includes a 1.58% increase for LPS that includes two new positions 
• Recommended budget does not  meet the needs of the town 
• Recommended budget leaves an excess levy capacity of $1,055,950 that will 
insulate any revenue decline beyond the level funded local receipt and State 
Aid figures. 
===PAGE 18===
FY 2021 Recommended Budget 
Conclusions 
COVID-19 doesn’t change the fact that: 
This budget does not address the staffing needs for our Public 
Safety Departments, which during times like these we rely on 
more heavily and there will be an increased need in services 
for; 
Although it defers such things like adding the planned $50K 
into Pavement Management and $27K into ADA, these 
infrastructure needs do not go away; 
The postponement of Capital projects does not mean those 
projects are still not necessary; 
The postponement of putting money into OPEB doesn’t 
address our growing liability and that bond rating companies 
include not funding this liability as a weakness when reviewing 
the Town’s debt. 
===PAGE 19===
Looking ahead… 
• There are still a number of uncertainties related to State revenues 
and the impact on State Aid and the long term impact of COVID-19 
on the overall budget and how is will impact the need for additional 
services such as public safety, health services, food insecurity for 
vulnerable populations, outreach to seniors, and educational needs. 
• State officials have given no indication of what the State consensus 
budget will look like and have said that it could drag out past July 1st 
to see what that represents for State Aid; 
• State officials have relayed that towns will be able to apply for FEMA 
and CARES Act funding for any deficits related to COVID-19 that are 
not budgeted for in both FY20 and FY21. 
• We will continue to pay close attention to our actual-estimated 
revenues and expenditures and make the necessary adjustments at 
the Fall Special Town Meeting.
