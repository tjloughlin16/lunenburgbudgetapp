LUNENBURG PUBLIC LIBRARY DIRECTOR’S REPORT 
   FY25 Budget Update 
 
   FY26 Budget Update  
I discussed the library budget a number of times with ITM Carter Terenzini, and he made a final 
recommendation of $625,000 for the library budget. This recommendation included funding for 
100 floating hours to help us cover staff illnesses and still meet our Operation Safety Policy to 
remain open. It also includes a 10% increase to staff salaries as required by recent negotiations 
and updates to the Collective Bargaining Agreement and Salary Admin Plan. 
   Staff Update 
Deb Goodsell, Angela Clement, Michelle Belleza and I are planning to have monthly meetings to 
discuss collaborations and increase communication and coordination between departments.  
The renovation is well on its way toward completion. We have set up temporary shelving in the Main 
Hall, and stored a small number of books from the Teen Room. Work has been only minimally 
disruptive, with relatively little noise and mess. I anticipate moving to the new office within the 
week. The Story/Craft Room floor should be done when we return on Tuesday morning this week. 
We anticipate delivery of furniture on March 4. 
My presentation to the Finance Committee will be the night of our Trustee meeting. This will wrap up 
a very budget focused stretch of time. 
The Children’s Room, in particular, continues to be very busy, especially on Tuesday and Friday 
afternoons. The sheer volume of programming, though, also contributes to lots of activity. Our

February 2025 
Summary of Last Month’s Programs & Events 
➢ January started with another music clinic and performance workshop with local music teacher Jason 
Graham on Saturday, January 4th. We had 6 people attend. 
➢ Our launch of a new Saturday monthly series also started last month with the first Board Game 
Brunch happening on Saturday January 11th. There were 7 of us in attendance to play a variety of 
board games while sipping on coffee and eating snacks.  
➢ There were 20 people who came out on a Wednesday evening to make all natural DIY household 
cleaners using easily accessible materials. We made spray cleaners, fizzy toilet cleaner tablets, and 
a laundry scent booster. 
➢ Also in that week, we hosted the monthly Craft & Chill program for teens and adults. Participants 
made new year resolution bracelets with symbolic colors, patterns, beads and other supplies to 
remind them of a mantra or wish for 2025. Despite the weather concerns, we had 12 people attend. 
➢ Our Monthly Music Concert was on a Saturday afternoon in January. Marc Berger returned for a third 
time to the library and played his original Americana/folk music for 12 people in the community room. 
➢ Melanie led a workshop for patrons to learn more about Hoopla on a Wednesday morning. There 
were 4 people who came and left with some helpful information about this new digital resource. 
➢ Our first Sew Simple Session, a new open 2-hour time frame for patrons to come sew together, was 
at the end of January. There were 5 of us in attendance and the program was positively reviewed 
and appreciated by everyone there.

Recurring Program Update 
➢ Book clubs are going well with attendance continuing to grow. 
o The Baking Book Club, which meets every first Wednesday, has not generated much 
growth in attendance, but there are 3 of us who faithfully attend each month and are 
building strong bonds together. One of our members is determined to get new attendees 
so she talks it up at other town events. We had one new face this month so perhaps it’s 
working! We are also discussing options to generate more interest, such as moving the 
date and time or changing to a general cookbook club with other dishes welcome. 
o The 100 Books Bucket List book club meets every first Tuesday morning. We averaged 9 
attendees at each meeting last year, and we continue to get new members each month.  
o The Brighter Days book club meets every second Wednesday evening. There are 8 
consistent members each month and we are building bonds of friendship after each book 
discussion. This month we had 2 new members join us and are discussing new ideas for 
next year’s theme. We may draw titles from a pool of reader generated recommendations. 
The current books on our list may miss the mark for “uplifting reads” but they generate 
deep and meaningful conversations among our readers.  
➢ Stitchcraft knitting and crochet group attendance has bloomed in recent months with some weekly 
meetings having nearly a dozen crafters! There are consistently at least 4-5 people here every 
Tuesday, but most weeks we will have 6-7 people with us for the drop-in program. Quite a hit!
