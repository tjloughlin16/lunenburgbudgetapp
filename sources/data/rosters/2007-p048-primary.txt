   1| LUNENBURG PRIMARY SCHOOL
   2| The Lunenburg Primary School continues to strive toward educating the total child. We believe that the success of our
   3| programs is dependent upon a healthy and productive partnership between home and school. Toward that end, parents
   4| must play an active role in the daily programs and routines of their child's educational program.
   5| With an enrollment of four hundred twenty-six students, class sizes range from eighteen to twenty-three. Because research
   6| indicates that class sizes of twenty and under in the early years provide lasting educational benefits to young children, we
   7| strive to keep our kindergarten through grade 2 classes smaller.
   8| During the summer of 2007 summer school funding was limited. Through fundraising efforts, which included two book
   9| fairs, we were able to provide the funding for a limited number of kindergarten and first grade children to receive instruction
  10| for three weeks in July. Mrs. Lisa Stone, kindergarten teacher, and Mrs. Barbara Nelson, grade 1 teacher, were excited
  11| about the progress that all children made. This progress was further noted in our grade one and two fall reading testing.
  12| Through the support of parents and to meet the needs of parents and children, three full day, tuition-based kindergarten
  13| programs were created this fall. We are excited that parent interest in the full day program continues to increase, and look
  14| forward to when we can offer this program to all five year olds. The State Quality Full Day Kindergarten Grant supports
  15| $37,000 in faculty salaries in these full day programs. We realize that this State money will continue to diminish as our
  16| Chapter 70 funds increase with more students enrolled in a full day program.
  17| The Primary School continues to strive toward academic excellence. Reading testing was completed this fall and again in
  18| January on every child in kindergarten, grade 1, and grade 2. The mid-winter testing shows significant improvements in all
  19| areas of reading (phonemic segmentation, fluency, letter identification, sound symbol). We are hopeful that our end-of-year
  20| testing will validate that all children are reading on grade level. According to our School Improvement Plan, it is our goal
  21| that 80 percent of our students at each grade level will read on grade level in June.
  22| In the area of math, the faculty administers quarterly math assessments to monitor children's progress. Teachers use the
  23| Results model to discuss their individual classroom results as well as to plan the needed interventions for those children
  24| not meeting the standard. Our School Improvement Plan's goal in math is that at least 80 percent of our students will attain
  25| 80 percent of the standards.
  26| Our School Improvement Plan focuses on the district goals: To foster the talents, skills, and potential of all students, to
  27| ensure that staff and programs are conducive to instructional excellence, to use technology as an integral component of
  28| teaching, learning, and management, and to provide facilities that meet staff, program, and community needs. We are very
  29| proud that two of the Primary teachers attended a week-long training for Responsive Classroom. Both Ms. Chapdelaine and
  30| Ms. Stone will provide some Responsive Classroom training for staff on release days.
  31| The Primary School Council meets the first Thursday of each month to discuss school issues and to develop its annual
  32| school Improvement Plan. Parents and community members are welcome to attend. Members of the Council for the 2007-
  33| 2008 school year are:
  34| Principal: Cyndy Daukantas
  35| Teachers: Shella Bilotta, Nancy Chapdelaine, and Vicki Barbier

  39| We would like to recognize Ms. Debra Colello-Cleaves for her the thirty-three years of dedication to the young children of
  40| Lunenburg. Ms. Cleaves retired in early December after having taught grade 6, kindergarten, and for the last twenty some-
  41| odd years, grade 2. Her dedication to young children, the total school, and the staff has earned Ms. Cleaves both respect
  42| and admiration. We will miss Deb next year, but wish her a long, healthy, and busy retirement.
  43| In closing, the faculty and staff of the Primary School is extremely proud of the work they do with young children. We are
  44| deeply committed to student learning and to our own professional growth. Only through working with parents are we able
  45| to accomplish the goals set forth by the State and district.
