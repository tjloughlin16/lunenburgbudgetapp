   6| Lunenburg High School has a long tradition of academic excellence and scholastic achievement. 1992 was a
   7| year which saw our students distinguish themselves in academic competitions at both local and state wide
   8| levels. In April a team of LHS pupils competed against high schools from across Northern Worcester
   9| County in a scholastic contest held at Mt. Wachusett Community College called "Head to Head". Only a
  10| narrow overtime loss to Leominster High School in the dramatic final round denied Lunenburg a first place
  11| finish. As runner up school we were awarded a full scholarship to Mr. Wachusett which was subsequently
  12| presented to a deserving member of the graduating class.
  13| In November of 1992 Lunenburg High School was proud and privileged to host the Small School Division of
  14| the prestigious Massachusetts Academic Decathlon. This well known Scholastic Competition matches high
  15| school teams from across Massachusetts in a series of religious academic events which cover the
  16| curriculum. Of the 22 high schools which competed the LHS team finished 7th, with a number of our
  17| students earning medals for individual excellence.
  18| LHS' proud tradition of athletic achievement was again demonstrated in 1992. Our Basketball team enjoyed
  19| what was perhaps its most outstanding season in the school's history. Undefeated during regular season
  20| play (17-0) the team won the Small School Division of the Clark Tournament, the St. Bernard;s Christmas
  21| Tournament, was Mid-Wach Division C Champion and ended post season play with a remarkable 10-1 record.
  22| The trend toward increased student enrollment at the high school first noted in 1991 has not only
  23| continued by has in fact accelerated. Our present enrollment of 475 students represents an increase of
  24| almost 20% over last year and projections strongly suggest that this steady growth in our pupil
  25| population will likely continue.
  26| In the Fall of 1992 the High School submitted to the New England Association of Schools and Colleges its
  27| "Two Year Progress Report". This important document represents the first of the major, comprehensive
  28| Reports which must be submitted to NEASC detailing the implementation status of the Recommendations
  29| contained in LHS' 1990 Evaluation. Copies of the complete Report are available at the Ritter Memorial
  30| and High School Libraries, Town Hall, the Superintendent's office and from members of the School
  31| Committee and High School Parent Advisory Council. The Report documents that over 70% of the
  32| Recommendations contained in our 1990 NEASC Evaluation have either been completed or are currently in
  33| progress. This is by any standard a commendable implementation rate and a clear and powerful
  34| demonstration of Lunenburg High School's commitment to the pursuit of excellence. While much still
  35| remains to be done, significant deficiencies in our educational facilities and physical plant remain as
  36| yet unresolved, we are confident that with the continued support and good will of our community no
  37| obstacle is too great to overcome.
  38| Lunenburg High School remains committed to providing its students with every possible opportunity to
  39| learn and grow as well as to contributing to the overall quality of life in the community.
