   1| THOMAS C. PASSIOS ELMENTARY SCHOOL
   2| Cynthia Daukantas, Principal
   3| \The Thomas C. Passios Elementary School continues to strive toward educating the total child. We utilize a variety of
   4| instructional methods and modalities in our approach to meet the individual needs of every child. We believe that the success of
   5| our programs is dependent upon a healthy and productive partnership between home and school. Parents play an active role in
   6| many of the daily programs and routines of the school.
   7| This year continues to be a challenging and exciting year for the students, faculty, and staff of the Elementary and Primary
   8| Schools. With a combined enrollment of seven hundred students, class sizes continue in the twenty to twenty-four range, with
   9| the younger children benefiting from the smaller class sizes.
  10| The summer of 2001 proved to be very beneficial to many children in Lunenburg. A number of academic programs were offered
  11| to the children: a five week program for children with special needs in grades preschool through grade 4, and a five week
  12| program for children in Grades 1-4 who were experiencing difficulty in the areas of reading and math. Funding for the Grades 1
  13| and 2 programs was provided through School Linked Services; State MCAS Grant monies supported the Grade 3 and 4 program.
  14| As the Elementary School continues to analyze our students' results on the Massachusetts Comprehensive Assessment System
  15| (MCAS) and revise curricula and strategies, we continue to grow as an excellent educational facility. We are extremely proud of
  16| the work the faculty continues to undertake each year. As teachers continue to reflect on their teaching practice, students
  17| continue to benefit.
  18| Our results on the State Grade 4 MCAS Tests Indicate that in the areas of both English/Language Arts and Math, the number of
  19| Grade 4 students performing in the "failing" category continues to decline. Children in Grade 3 were administered their first
  20| MCAS Test last spring in Reading. We are proud to say that no child failed the Grade 3 Reading Test. Individual Success Plans
  21| were developed on those children who scored in the lower end of the 'needs improvement' category with the intent that
  22| additional time/teaching would make a difference.
  23| This year the Elementary School Improvement Plan focused on the areas of reading comprehension and writing across all
  24| curricula areas. At this point in the year, the School Council is proud that we have accomplished many of our goals. This fall we
  25| implemented a writing assessment schedule/practice in which all students in grades kindergarten through four were asked to
  26| write on a given topic. Students' writing was evaluated using a rubric (scoring guide). All students were assessed again in
  27| March and again in May/June. By implementing this program, teachers and parents will be able to document individual student
  28| writing progress and re-teach as needed.
  29| We are pleased to report that our Reading Assessment practice has also been re-evaluated and revised. Each child in grades 1-
  30| 4 will be evaluated twice each year in reading; in October and March. New individual reading folders have been implemented to
  31| document student progress as well as strategies for improvement.
  32| The Passios Elementary School Council meets the first Wednesday of every month to discuss school issues and to develop its
  33| annual School Improvement Plan. Parents and community members are welcome to attend. Members of the Council for the
  34| 2001 - 2002 school year are:

  38| Parents:    Brent Benson, Debra Chapdelaine, Lynn Boucher-Cooley, and Sandra Sheehy
  39| Community Representative:    Elizabeth Lakso
  40| School Committee Rep:    Dr. Greg Berthiaume
  41| In conclusion, we would like to recognize the years of service to young children that Mrs. Joyce Wass has given. At the end of
  42| this school year, Mrs. Wass will retire with thirty-eight years in education, thirty-seven of which were devoted to the children of
  43| Lunenburg. After teaching second grade for thirty-four years, Mrs. Wass has been a Reading Recovery teacher to first grade
  44| children for the last three years. Her dedication to young children, the total school, and the staff has earned Mrs. Wass the
  45| respect and admiration she deserves. We will miss Joyce next year, but wish her a long, healthy, and busy retirement.
