   1| PRIMARY SCHOOL
   2| Cynthia Daukantas, Principal
   3| July 1 and the move into the new Primary School was both exciting and exhausting. Preparing the building for four hundred
   4| little faces in September proved a daunting task. Hundreds upon hundreds of boxes, equipment, and supplies needed to be
   5| unpacked and stored. Many parents visited the school during the closing weeks of the summer to prepare their child for a
   6| successful entrance to the new school year.
   7| The Lunenburg Primary School continues to strive toward educating the total child. We utilize a variety of instructional methods
   8| and modalities in our approach to meet the individual needs of every child. We believe that the success of our programs is
   9| dependent upon a healthy and productive partnership between home and school. Toward that end, parents must play an active
  10| role in many of the daily programs and routines of their child's educational program.
  11| With an enrollment of four hundred two students, class sizes range from eighteen to twenty-two. Because research indicates
  12| that class sizes of twenty and under in the early years provide lasting educational benefits to young children, we strive to keep
  13| our kindergarten through grade 2 classes smaller.
  14| During the summer of 2005 summer school funding was limited. Through fundraising efforts, School Linked Services was able
  15| to provide the funding for kindergarten and first grade children to receive instruction for the month of July. Mrs. Lisa Stone,
  16| kindergarten teacher, and Mrs. Barbara Nelson, grade 1 teacher, were excited about the progress that all twenty children made.
  17| This progress was further noted in our grade one and two fall reading testing. I am hopeful that through continuing fundraising
  18| efforts, we will be able to offer support to both kindergarten and grade one children this summer. Through the support of
  19| parents and to meet the needs of parents, two full-day kindergarten programs were created this fall. We are excited about
  20| offering a third full-day program to kindergarteners, and look forward to when we can offer this program to all five year olds.
  21| There are several projects underway at the Primary School in this, its opening year. One of our major undertakings this year was
  22| to permanently recognize every child and faculty member In the school in its opening year. Early in January Mrs. Linda
  23| Widstrand, Lunenburg parent and artist, led a project in which over the course of four days, every child and adult in the school
  24| painted three tiles which would comprise a rainbow. Children returned from February vacation excited to see their artwork
  25| permanently installed on the atrium wall. A second project will add books to our library. Our goal will be that each student will
  26| have a book in the library with his or her name in it. Our final project will involve the community. In an effort to open the Primary
  27| School to parents and the community, several special 'events' were scheduled throughout the year. A puppet show in February
  28| and a book night with parents and young children were among several of the events planned.
  29| The Primary School continues to strive toward academic excellence. Reading testing was completed this fall on all children in
  30| kindergarten, grade 1, and grade 2. The mid-winter testing has just been completed and shows significant improvements in all
  31| areas of reading. We are hopeful that our end-of-year testing will validate that all children are reading on grade level. This year,
  32| the Primary School Improvement Plan focuses on the areas of reading and math, including those children with disabilities and
  33| receiving Title I support. We continue to work on these areas, using the diagnostic support of DIBELS testing, Results, Lexia
  34| Learning System, and the Everyday Math Program, as well as our revised delivery model in Title I. Other goals of the
  35| Improvement Plan include pursuing accreditation with NAEYC (National Association for the Education of Young Children),
  36| monitoring our transition plan to a new Pre-K to grade 2 facility, and the enhancement of our Extended Day Program. We
  37| continue to move forward on each of these goals.
  38| The Primary School Council meets the first Thursday of each month to discuss school issues and to develop its annual School
  39| Improvement Plan. Parents and community members are welcome to attend. Members of the Council for the 2005 # 2006 school
  40| year are:
  41| Teachers:    Sheila Bilotta, Nancy Chapdelaine, and Lisa Stone
  42| Parents:    Carla Cappucci, Elaine Carroll, Karin Menard, and Jason Rowley
  43| Community Rep:    Sharon Cronin
  44| School Committee Rep: Jennifer Benson
  45| In closing, the faculty, staff, and students of the Primary School wish to express our appreciation to the Lunenburg community
  46| for their beautiful new building.
