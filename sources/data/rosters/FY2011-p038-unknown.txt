  16|                 The 2011-2012 Thomas C. Passios Elementary School year began with 358 students in 15 classrooms for an average class size of approximately 24 students.
  17|                        The school year began with great news. TCP made Adequate Yearly Progress (AYP) in student growth in the aggregate
  18|                as well as in all of the subgroups and was designated a Commendation School by the Massachusetts Department of Elementary
  19|                and Secondary Education (DESE).
  20|                         The wonderful achievement of the students was a testament to all of the hard work that had been undertaken
  21|                throughout the school year by all constituencies in the school community to find better ways to meet the collective as well as
  22|                individual needs of all of our students. Monumental student growth and improvement was attributed to many different initiatives
  23|                that contributed to the success of the students including the establishment of a school mantra: "Good Enough Is NOT Good
  24|                Enough; Strive for Excellence" - as well as the launch of the Trifecta, a three-prong program that was designed to support
  25|                traditional classroom teaching by tailoring ancillary programs to meet the dynamic learning needs of all students.
  26|                         While all of the staff, parents, and students of TCP celebrated and were credited in making AYP, the celebration was
  27|                tempered with the knowledge that regardless of the positive inroads achieved by the students, the school is not out of the woods yet. No Child Left Behind mandates that a school must continue to demonstrate growth for two years in a row to rid itself
  28|                of the needs improvement and corrective action status. Furthermore, the deliberate commitment to continuous improvement
  29|                cannot diminish because despite making significant strides in growth and gains in achievement, the school's performance
  30|                targets were not met. Rather, the TCP MCAS scores demonstrate that the work has just begun and that everyone needs to stay the course.
  31|                        Improved MCAS scores at TCP demonstrate that education is a journey and like any worthwhile endeavor there are
  32|                always obstacles in the road to success that must be surmounted. Change is difficult and takes time and commitment as well as
  33|                effort. For something to work, every member of the school community must be dedicated to working together to make it work
  34|                100 percent of the time. Every decision, every action, every result must be aligned with a shared vision that is consistent and
  35|                driven by the pledge to do the right things for students. High expectations must be made clear and individuals must buy into the
  36|                understanding that success is predicated upon everyone contributing. People must be given the knowledge, resources, and time
  37|                to accomplish meaningful goals. All constituencies must be cognizant of the path as well as the destination.
  38|                        Accordingly, during the 2011-12 school year, programs like the Trifecta were tweaked and enhanced by a longer school
  39|                day, a modified 45-minute block schedule that supported common planning time, a revitalized Power Block that was more data
  40|                driven, and the development and deployment of a Research Skills class using the Independent Investigation Method (IMM) that
  41|                supports the growth mindset as well as the tenants of the School-wide Enrichment Model (SEM). The staff is also using Teach
  42|                Like a Champion by Doug Lemov as an anchor book to examine best practices and share ideas at staff and common planning
