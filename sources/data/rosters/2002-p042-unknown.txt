  20| THOMAS C. PASSIOS ELMENTARY SCHOOL
  21| The Thomas C. Passios Elementary School continues to strive toward educating the total child. We utilize a variety of
  22| instructional methods and modalities in our approach to meet the individual needs of every child. We believe that the success of
  23| our programs is dependent upon a healthy and productive partnership between home and school. Toward that end, parents play
  24| an active role in many of the daily programs and routines of their child's educational program.
  25| This year continues to be a challenging and exciting year for the students, faculty, and staff of the Elementary and Primary
  26| Schools. With a combined enrollment of seven hundred students, class sizes continue in the twenty to twenty-four range, with
  27| the younger children benefiting from smaller class sizes.
  28| The summer of 2002 proved to be very beneficial to many children in Lunenburg. Again we were able to offer a number of
  29| extended learning opportunities to children: a five week program for children with special needs in grades preschool through
  30| grade 4, and a five week program for children in Grades 1-4 who were experiencing difficulty in the areas of reading and math.
  31| Funding for the Grades 1 and 2 programs was provided through School Linked Services; State Academic Support grant monies
  32| supported the Grade 3 and 4 program.
  33| As the Elementary School continues to analyze our students' results on the Massachusetts Comprehensive Assessment System
  34| (MCAS) and revise curricula and instructional strategies, we continue to grow as an excellent educational facility. We are
  35| extremely proud of the work the faculty continues to undertake each year. As teachers reflect on their teaching practice,
  36| students benefit.
  37| We are pleased to report that the Thomas C. Passios Elementary School met its targeted Improvement goal as determined by the
  38| Department of Education. Results on the State Grade 4 MCAS Tests indicate that in the areas of both English/Language Arts and
  39| Math, the percentage of Grade 4 students performing in the "failing" category continues to decline, while those performing In the
  40| proficient and advanced categories increases.
  41| This year the Elementary School Improvement Plan focused on the areas of open response questions and math, particularly
  42| geometry and measurement. At this point in the year, the School Council is proud that we have accomplished many of our
  43| goals. On all professional development half days, Ms. Maria Marolda, educational consultant at Children's Hospital, has provided
  44| additional math training to all teachers and support staff in grades K-4.
  45| We are pleased to report that our Reading Assessment practice has also been re-evaluated and revised. Each child in grades 1-
  46| 4 will be evaluated twice each year in reading; in October and March. New Individual reading folders have been Implemented to
  47| document student progress as well as strategies for improvement.
