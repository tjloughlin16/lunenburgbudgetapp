   8| TURKEY HILL MIDDLE SCHOOL
   9| The 1995-1996 school year was a busy, academically rewarding and productive year for
  10| students, staff and administration. At Turkey Hill Middle School, young adolescents
  11| learn and grow in a safe, nurturing and intellectually stimulating environment. Our
  12| beliefs include the following key components:
  13| Our curriculum is an up to date academic program that challenges students and
  14| promotes excellence in the areas of mathematics, social studies, English, science and
  15| world languages. Art, music, physical education, health, family and consumer science and technology
  16| education enhances the core curriculum. All areas of instruction provide each student with the
  17| opportunity to pursue and explore different areas of interest, while encouraging them to be creative,
  18| motivated learners.
  19| We strive to encourage a school climate in which all human beings will experience a warm, respectful,
  20| caring atmosphere.
  21| Academic standards and expectations continue to be a high priority. The entire staff strives to provide
  22| the maximum number of opportunities for all students to learn.
  23| Assessment will be multifaceted. Strategies such as portfolios, report cards, demonstrations, parent
  24| conferences, interviews and narratives reports are used at various levels in each grade level.
  25| The School Advisory Council worked extremely hard to create the second annual School Improvement
  26| Plan. Council members Pam Trainque, Irene Masciarelli, Dan Evers, Tom Sugrue, Elizabeth Burnap,
  27| Sharon Kimball, Kerry McCarthy, Sharon McCullah and Mary Cringan are proud of their
  28| accomplishments and believe that THMS is a fine educational institution. The five objectives in the
  29| School Improvement Plan are: to improve and increase communication with parents, staff, students,
  30| community, established committees, District Advisory Council, School Committee and other School
  31| Advisory Councils; to enhance the use of computer technology as a tool to assist student learning; to
  32| update curriculum in conjunction with the curriculum frameworks established by the Massachusetts
  33| Education Reform Act and follow the timeline established by the Lunenburg Public School Curriculum
  34| Advisory Committee, incorporating appropriate professional development and revision of instructional
  35| and assessment methodologies; to increase student time on learning and to maintain a safe, comfortable
  36| learning environment. A copy of the complete School Improvement Plan is available at the THMS office,
  37| Brooks House or the Ritter Memorial Library.
  38| Several new courses and staff were added to our program for the 1996-1997 school year. We are
  39| pleased to be able to provide sixth and seventh graders with an introduction to Spanish, French and
  40| Latin and to also return music to the course of study for students in grades five through seven. The
  41| addition of a part time library/media specialist, part time guidance counselor, and reading tutor has had
  42| a profound impact on all students and staff. With an increase in our enrollment, services, programs and
  43| support has also increased. Class size averages between 27-28 in academic classes and we are full to
  44| capacity. Space needs and class size must be addressed before the start of the 1997-1998 school year.
  45| THMS considers the whole student in its implementation of appropriate programs. Educational
  46| decisions for early adolescents are based on the students' emotional, physical, social and intellectual
  47| development. We believe that each child will become a self-motivated, creative, life-long learner in a
  48| safe, stimulating environment which encourages diversity and honest, open communication.
