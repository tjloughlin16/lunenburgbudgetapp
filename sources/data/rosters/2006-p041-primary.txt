   1| LUNENBURG PRIMARY SCHOOL
   2| Cynthia Daukantas, Principal
   3| The Lunenburg Primary School continues to strive toward educating the total child. We utilize a variety of Instructional methods
   4| and modalities in our approach to meet the Individual needs of every child. We believe that the success of our programs is
   5| dependent upon a healthy and productive partnership between home and school. Toward that end, parents must play an active
   6| role In many of the dally programs and routines of their child's educational program.
   7| With an enrollment of four hundred twenty-four students, class sizes range from eighteen to twenty-four. Because research
   8| indicates that class sizes of twenty and under in the early years provide lasting educational benefits to young children, we strive to
   9| keep our kindergarten through grade 2 classes smaller.
  10| During the summer of 2006 summer school funding was limited. Through fundraising efforts, which Included two book fairs,
  11| School Linked Services was able to provide the funding for kindergarten and first grade children to receive Instruction for the
  12| month of July. Mrs. Lisa Stone, kindergarten teacher, and Mrs. Barbara Nelson, grade 1 teacher, were excited about the progress
  13| that all twenty children made. This progress was further noted in our grade one and two fall reading testing. I am hopeful that
  14| through our continuing fundraising efforts, we will-be able to offer support to both kindergarten and grade one children during the
  15| summer of '07.
  16| Through the support of parents and to meet the needs of parents and children, two full day tuition based kindergarten programs
  17| were created again this fall. We are excited about offering a second full day program to kindergarteners, and look forward to
  18| when we can offer this program to all five year olds. We are extremely pleased and proud that our efforts in securing grant funds
  19| to begin the process of planning for full day kindergarten for all students has resulted in a State Transition Planning Grant of
  20| $30,000. This money will allow us to begin the process of a self-study through NAEYC In anticipation of national accreditation, as
  21| well as to begin purchasing some much needed materials/equipment. We are hopeful that Lunenburg will be able to secure some
  22| portion of full day kindergarten implementation funding for next fall.
  23| The Primary School continues to strive toward academic excellence. Reading testing was completed this fall and again In January
  24| on every child in kindergarten, grade 1, and grade 2. The mid-winter testing shows significant Improvements In all areas of
  25| reading (phonemic segmentation, fluency, letter Identification, sound symbol). We are hopeful that our end of year testing will
  26| validate that all children are reading on grade level. According to our School Improvement Plan, it is our goal that 80% of our
  27| students at each grade level will read on grade level In June.
  28| This year the faculty has worked to create quarterly assessments in math to monitor children's progress. Each administration of
  29| this assessment includes an open response problem. Teachers have met to discuss their Individual classroom results as well as
  30| to plan the needed Interventions for those children not meeting the standard. Our School Improvement Plan identifies the goal
  31| to be 80% of students at each grade level will attain 80% of the standards in math.
  32| The Primary School Improvement Plan focuses upon reading, writing, and math, as well as revising / updating our report cards and
  33| writing exemplars. As the curriculum changes, our report cards needs to reflect the current standards so that we can
  34| communicate student progress to parents. At this point in the year, report cards have been updated at all grade levels and will be
  35| submitted to the School Committee for their approval In May as part of our School Improvement Plan update.
  36| The Primary School Council meets the first Thursday of each month to discuss school Issues and to develop its annual School
  37| Improvement Plan. Parents and community members are welcome to attend. Members of the Council for the 2006 . 2007 school
  38| year are:
  39| Principal:    Cyndy Daukantas
  40| Teachers:    Shella Bilotta, Nancy Chapdelaine, and Lisa Stone
  41| Parents:    Carla Cappucci, Karin Menard, Jason Rowley, and Heather Swift
  42| Community Representative    Pamela Penna
  43| School Committee Rep:    Michael Clark
  44| In closing the faculty and staff of the Primary School is extremely proud of the work they do with young children. We are deeply
  45| committed to our own professional growth and to student learning. Only through working with parents are we able to accomplish
  46| the goals set forth by the State and district.
