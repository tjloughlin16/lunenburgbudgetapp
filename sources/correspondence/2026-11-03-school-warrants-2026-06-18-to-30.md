# The school warrant batches of 18 to 30 June 2026, redacted by the district, 3 November 2026

Not off a website. The district shared a Google Drive folder in answer to a records request,
so the email and the share are the address (rule 12).

## How it reached us

**Emailed to TJ Loughlin by Liz Petersen <lpetersen@lunenburgschools.net>, Executive
Assistant to the Superintendent, Lunenburg Public Schools, on 3 November 2026**, as a Google
Drive folder share. The message reads, verbatim:

> *"In regards to your public records request about warrants presented at the 7-15-26
> meeting, here is the folder with the redacted warrants."*

The request it answers is `2026-10-26-sc-warrant-june-18-30` in
`notes/reference/records-requests.csv`: the warrant for 6/18 to 6/30/2026 that the 15 July
2026 School Committee agenda lists. The same folder was first shared unredacted on 27 October
2026 and revoked nine minutes later over personal information in the bills
(`2026-10-27-warrants-folder-superintendent.txt`). This is the redacted folder the district
promised then.

TJ downloaded the folder as one zip, `drive-download-20261103T215419Z-1-001.zip`
(34,052,807 bytes uncompressed, nine PDFs). Three of the publisher's filenames carry a byte
that is not valid UTF-8 (`0xFF`), written below as `<0xFF>`; the file system refused it, so
the files were taken out of the zip under our names and the publisher's names are recorded
here and in `sources/data/redactions.csv` (`delivered_as`).

## What the batches are

Each PDF is one accounts-payable batch from the school department. It opens with an
**FY26 BATCH COVERSHEET** (batch number, voucher range, date, a printed grand total and the
certification to the Town Accountant), then the MUNIS **Invoice Entry Proof List** for the
batch, which prints a total of its own, then the invoices, purchase orders and
reimbursement forms behind each voucher. Every cover sheet prints its grand total, so a later
extract can reconcile each batch to it (rule 13).

The district redacted them before sending, by its own judgement. Nothing here has been
extracted into a dataset.

## Whether the district's redaction redacts

Checked 3 November 2026, file by file, before anything was published. A black box drawn over
a page that still carries the words beneath it hides nothing from anybody who selects the
text, so each redaction was tested against what the file actually holds:

- **the picture under every box** was read from the page's image object, not from the
  rendered page. In all nine files the pixels under each box are blacked or whited out in the
  image itself; nothing of the original survives in the picture;
- **whole pages the district blacked out** carry nothing else: no image, no text;
- **the file's structure**: no unused image objects, no annotations, no form-field values and
  no earlier revision of any page (the files are linearized, one revision each);
- **the text layer**: eight pages across five files carry an OCR text layer over the scan.
  On those pages, every character whose position falls under a black region was read out.

**Three files fail the last test.** On one page of each, the district redacted the picture but
left the OCR text layer that had been made from the unredacted page, so the words under the
boxes can still be selected and copied: batch 5811 (page 9), batch 5818 (page 21) and batch
5823 (page 8). Those three raws are **held in the private bucket and not published**, by TJ's
instruction of 3 November 2026; our redacted copies, below, are published instead. What they cover
is not repeated here. 

On the other text-layer pages nothing readable was under a box: one page of batch 5780
carries a few characters of OCR noise from handwriting under its boxes, not words.

**State:** four of the district's files are public, whole, on TJ's decision of 3 November 2026
(*"these are all public. they have been redacted by the school body already."*), recorded in
`sources/data/redactions.csv`: 5765, 5780 and 5809. (5808 was among them until TJ's later decision the same day; see below.)

## Our redacted copies, 3 November 2026

TJ decided the same day that six batches are published only as OUR redacted copies, built
by `scripts/redact.py`: every page rendered and rebuilt as an image, so no text layer survives;
the district's own boxes burned in; our notice page first (*UNOFFICIAL DOCUMENT, REDACTION DONE BY
LUNENBURGBUDGETPROJECT.ORG*, *Official document available upon request*). The boxes are declared
in the register's `rule`, and `redact.py --check` rebuilds each copy byte for byte.

- **5811, 5818, 5823**: the district's redaction left the covered words in the OCR text layer.
  Rebuilding as images removes the layer; the district's boxes are burned in where they were.
- **5806, 5810**: these were public for some hours on 3 November 2026. A family receiving
  school services could still be identified from payee numbers beside the redacted names, and
  on 5806 from initials and a supporting page. DATA-PRIVACY.md masks families receiving services,
  so the district's files were taken down (`sources/data/takedowns.csv`, `scripts/takedown.py`)
  and replaced by our copies, which mask those lines as well.
- **5808**: the same, decided later that day. A supporting page names a parent on a
  special-education transport mileage log, and the proof list carries the same family payee
  as 5806. Taken down and replaced by our copy.

Our first copy of 5806 missed payee numbers printed with leading zeros on two ledger pages; our
own check of its Textract reading found them the same day. That copy is taken down too and the
corrected one, with its sha256 in its name, replaces it.

Every figure, account, vendor that is a business, staff name and batch total is kept, and each
copy's printed batch total was checked legible after the rebuild.
