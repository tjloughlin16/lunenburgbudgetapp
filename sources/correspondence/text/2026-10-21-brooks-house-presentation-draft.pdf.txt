Brooks House – a Brief 
History
• The Brooks house was built in 
1835 by Samuel Bailey and 
included 7 acres.
• Fernando Brooks purchased the 
house in 1870, and it remained in 
the Brooks family until the Town 
purchased it (via eminent domain) 
to build TC Passios Elementary 
(and much later, the current 
Library).

===PAGE 3===
• It was used as a hostel for a time, and meetings were 
held in the living room for the 4-H club and the 
Grange.
Under School Committee and Select Board custody, it has 
served as: 
• A classroom 
• School District/Superintendent Office 
• Planning Board Office 
• School Facility Director’s Office 
• Home of the Lunenburg Ledger 
• Municipal Record Storage 

===PAGE 4===
• The Municipal Building Design Committee has 
identified the Brooks House as having limited 
municipal value, consistent with assessments by Town 
Managers, DPW Directors, Facility Directors, and 
School Superintendents for the past 15 years.
• Requested maintenance has often been deprioritized 
due to pressing capital demands that more directly 
affect Town operations.
• The building is in a salvageable condition, but in need 
of significant capital investment.

Article 10 - Special Town Meeting – November 17, 2024
The general intent of the warrant article:
• Can the Town split off the Brooks House into its own 
stand-alone parcel that will conform with our Zoning 
Bylaw’s dimensional requirements?
• If yes, what steps will the Town have to take to sell or 
lease the property to an entity that will restore the 
Brooks House and put it back into productive use?
===PAGE 9===
Property Details:
• Currently part of the larger School property.
• Approximately 160 feet of frontage on Mass Ave.
• Building is 191 years old and has 3,137 square feet of 
living area.
• 19 parking spaces – used as overflow parking for 
library.
• Located in the Village Center zoning district.
• Also falls within the Architectural Preservation District. 
===PAGE 10===
Current Condition and Needed Repairs:
 Last major renovation occurred in 1963.
 The building has been winterized for 2 years. Gas is off, 
water has been drained.
 Needs a new roof, has multiple areas of damaged roof 
visible from the ground.
 Needs a new fire escape. The upper floors are off limits 
due to ne 2nd means of egress.
 Asbestos status is unknown.
===PAGE 11===
Current Condition and Needed Repairs:
 The fieldstone foundation needs repairs especially 
towards the back of the structure.
 There appears to still be some knob and tube wiring left 
in the house (lighting circuits).
 There are partially finished spaces on the first floor.
 All the doorways are very narrow and would not support 
ADA in its current condition.
 The staircases are very steep, and hand -rails are not to 
code.
===PAGE 12===
Current Condition and Needed Repairs:
 The interior finishes are all dated and in need of 
renovation.
 The porch and entry stairs are in serious structural 
disrepair.
 Doors and windows appear operable but look to be 
older and not energy efficient.
 There is some exterior siding damage.
 Exterior trim needs to be painted.
===PAGE 13===
Question #1: Can the Town split off the Brooks House 
into its own stand-alone parcel that will conform with our 
Zoning Bylaw’s dimensional requirements?
Answer: Yes, it will be relatively easy to split off the 
Brooks House as its own parcel by going through the 
Approval Not Required (ANR) process with the Planning 
Board.
The Village Center District does not have a minimum lot 
size requirement, and the property would have more than 
double the District’s minimum frontage requirement.

What steps will the Town have to take to sell or lease 
the property?
Once split off from the larger School property, the Town 
can issue a Request for Proposals (RFP) to sell or lease the 
Brooks House parcel to an entity that will restore the house 
and put it back into productive use. 
Is it really just as simple as that?
===PAGE 16===
No, of course not.
Challenges to Redevelopment: Access 
is THE issue
• The big issue for using the property is 
access from Mass Avenue, i.e. getting cars 
from the road to the parking in back.
• Parking for the Brooks House is currently 
accessed via the Library’s driveway. The 
Library Trustees would have to agree to 
legalizing the current access arrangement.
• The Trustees are not willing to let a private 
business use their driveway for access.

===PAGE 17===
Challenges to Redevelopment: Access is THE issue
• Another option would be accessing the Brooks House property 
from the School’s driveway to the right of the building.
• The School Committee has said they are not willing to let a 
private business use their driveway for access.
• Even if either entity was willing to let the new owner use their 
driveway for access, this would require a V ariance from the 
Zoning Board because our Zoning Bylaw requires that lots be 
accessed via their street frontage.
===PAGE 18===
Challenges to Redevelopment: Access is THE issue
• To use Mass Ave for access, the new owner would need to secure a 
Driveway Permit from the MA Department of Transportation 
(MassDOT).
• The MassDOT driveway standards are more stringent than the 
Town’s driveway standards. 
• A single-lane driveway would need to be 16-feet wide from Mass 
Ave to the parking in back.
• A two-lane driveway would need to be 26-feet wide from Mass Ave 
to the parking in back.

What Happens Next?
• The Lunenburg Select Board will decide if, when and how to 
dispose of the Brooks House and under what conditions.
• If they decide to sell or lease the property, they will first go 
through the ANR process for the land division.
• Then they would work with Town Counsel to draft a Request 
for Proposals  that would sell the property to an entity that 
would restore the House and put it back into productive use. 
This will include special conditions to protect the Town’s 
interests.
