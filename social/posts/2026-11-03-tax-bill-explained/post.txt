Last time we showed Lunenburg's average single-family tax bill: $1,637 in FY1991, $7,444 in FY2026. That average overstates what happened to a home that has been here the whole time.

The average is taken over every single-family home in town today, including newer and bigger ones that weren't here in FY1991. A new home pays its own tax and nobody else's bill goes up because of it. But it usually pays more than the average, so it pulls the average up.

So we followed one home instead: one that didn't change, with its value moving like the town's. Its bill went from $1,637 to about $5,210. Newer, bigger homes account for about $2,230 of the average.

Where did that home's rise come from? Our arithmetic on the state's levy files:
• Prop 2½'s 2.5% a year: 63%. It needs no vote. On its own it would have taken the bill to $3,885.
• Overrides and debt exclusions, everything voters approved: 16%.
• The rest: home values gaining on businesses and other property, so homes carry more of the total, plus whatever our method misses.

Prop 2½ caps the town's total, not anyone's bill. The page shows both lines, how to read each, and what does and doesn't change a bill: why a new business doesn't lower anyone's taxes, and why a business closing raises them.
