Lunenburg has written itself a report every single year since 1959, and we've now gone through every one of them. 📚

67 annual town reports, 1959 to 2025: 8,997 pages in all. Each one is the town's own account of its year: the tax rate, the Assessors' counts, the Treasurer's books, the school rosters, the election results. Every section was written by the people doing the work that year: the Town Clerk, the Assessors, the Treasurer, the school department, every board and committee. The town printed them year after year, for more than six decades, and they are a gift to anyone who wants to know how we got here. Thank you to everyone who wrote their part.

Of those 8,997 pages, 2,332 hold financial tables. We read every one of them, and 2,158 check out against totals the town itself printed on the page.

Some things only reading every report can show:

💸 Across all 67 reports, the highest tax rate printed is $133.00 per $1,000, in 1969. Today's, in the FY2025 report, is $14.36. The two are not comparable: the rate fell by more than a third 3 times, each in a year the town revalued ("In 1970 Lunenburg went to full and fair valuation."). A rate only compares within one assessment, so neither number tells you what a household paid.

📋 The school rosters printed 84 names in 1959 and 232 in 2025. That counts names, not a staffing level: a roster says nothing about hours or who pays.

🧑‍🏫 The first teacher aide appears on the 1970 roster: Dorothy Hillman, "Teacher Aide for Perceptually Handicapped Program". The 2025 roster prints 52 paraprofessionals, the second most common job after classroom teacher.

🏛️ A study committee recommended a town manager in 1966, a second committee in 1983, and the charter passed in 1999, 422 to 150: 33 years from first study to charter.

🏡 The fact pages count 5,282 residents in 1955 and 10,280 in 2010.
